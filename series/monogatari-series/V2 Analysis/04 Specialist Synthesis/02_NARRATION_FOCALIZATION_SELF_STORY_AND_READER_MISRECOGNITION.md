# 〈物語〉シリーズ V2 — Narration, Focalization, Self-Story, and Reader Misrecognition

## Who gets to tell the monster?

## 0. Purpose, governing question, and evidentiary boundary

This document treats narration not as a transparent delivery system for the story but as one of the story's central ethical and formal mechanisms.

The governing question is:

> **How does the series make a person's manner of narration part of the wound, defense, desire, misrecognition, or recovery being narrated?**

The answer cannot be reduced to the familiar observation that *Monogatari* has "unreliable narrators." That label is too blunt for the supplied V2 corpus. It treats reliability as a switch: a narrator is either trustworthy or not. The actual series repeatedly distributes accuracy unevenly across domains.

A narrator can be:

- excellent at reporting bodily sequence;
- poor at identifying another person's motive;
- unusually honest about embarrassment;
- wrong about causal explanation;
- right about a symptom but wrong about the whole person;
- self-critical in a way that itself becomes distortion;
- a privileged witness to one relationship and an interested witness to another;
- highly reflective at narration-time while reconstructing an earlier self who did not possess that reflection;
- capable of explicitly marking speculation while still inviting the reader to overvalue the speculation because it is elegant.

The V2 model therefore replaces generic unreliability with **multidimensional corrigibility**.

The primary evidence base is `L02_NARRATOR_AND_FOCALIZATION_LEDGER.md` through the V30 + Hyaku + Mijikana boundary. `21_VOLUME_AND_ARC_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md` supplies the locator-grade routing layer. `L04`, `L05`, and `L09` are used where narration becomes inseparable from self-story, relationship state, or first-pass revision. Document 01 supplies chronology locks and is not rebuilt here.

This artifact does **not** attempt to become:

- the definitive Araragi character monograph;
- the definitive Hanekawa, Nadeko, Kanbaru, Kaiki, or Ougi study;
- the oddity-metaphysics reference;
- a complete Japanese stylistics atlas;
- a complete ethics-of-rescue synthesis.

Those belong in later specialist homes.

The present task is narrower:

> **to show how the series produces knowledge through interested voices, then repeatedly teaches both characters and readers to distinguish access from authority.**

---

# 1. Core thesis: narration is situated access, not a truth hierarchy

The strongest corpus-wide formulation is:

> **Monogatari does not progress from "unreliable narration" to reliable narration. It progresses from an initially dominant situated narrator to a plural system in which every perspective gains authority by domain, loses authority by overreach, and remains open to correction.**

That structure matters ethically.

A person's story about:

- why they help,
- why they suffer,
- what another person wants,
- which self is "real,"
- whether a relationship was necessary,
- whether a wound made them stronger,
- whether they caused another person's fate,

can become a practical force. Self-story guides action. It allocates guilt. It defines who is allowed to speak. It can make rescue possible or make the rescued person disappear behind a role.

The series therefore distinguishes several layers that must remain separate in V2 synthesis.

| Layer | Meaning | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|
| **event report** | what a narrator says happened | omission, memory error, reconstruction |
| **perception report** | what the narrator saw/felt at the time | treating personal perception as shared fact |
| **motive attribution** | what the narrator thinks someone wanted | intimacy mistaken for interior access |
| **self-story** | explanatory identity the narrator gives themselves | guilt, innocence, heroism, victimhood, purity, fraudulence |
| **specialist model** | diegetic theory offered by an expert | domain overreach, rhetorical metaphor treated as law |
| **embedded testimony** | another speaker's account inside a narrator's text | relational access mistaken for neutrality |
| **internalized voice** | imagined, remembered, dreamt, or modeled speech | quoted wording treated as literal historical testimony |
| **retrospective revision** | later narration changes present understanding of earlier material | later knowledge back-projected into earlier consciousness |
| **paratextual framing** | afterword or production note | authorial comment treated as diegetic omniscience |

This distinction prevents two opposite errors.

The first is credulity: assuming first-person access grants final truth.

The second is nihilism: treating all first-person narration as equally suspect and therefore analytically useless.

Neither fits the corpus.

The series instead rewards a more demanding reader:

> **believe locally, classify carefully, preserve correction history, and never turn access into sovereignty.**

# 2. Araragi's first-person baseline: sharp observation, oblique emotion, corrigible interpretation

## 2.1 The opening narrator is already a performed narrator

V01 establishes nearly every problem that later narrator substitutions will formalize.

Araragi is observant. He notices that Hitagi's socially assigned role as `病弱な女の子` does not fit the literal fact of her weight. He notices inconsistencies in behavior, social routines, and bodily evidence. He is good at recognizing that something is wrong.

But he immediately narrativizes what he sees.

People become:

- sick girl;
- class-president ideal;
- lost child;
- star athlete;
- shy childhood acquaintance;
- little sister;
- vampire burden.

These labels are not arbitrary lies. They are efficient social models. The problem is that Araragi often mistakes **a useful first description for a sufficiently complete person**.

V01 therefore establishes a correction pattern rather than a simple reliability failure:

- Hitagi exceeds the sick-girl role.
- Hanekawa says `何でもは知らない`, undercutting Araragi's near-omniscient class-president ideal.
- Hachikuji is visible to Araragi but not to Hitagi, demonstrating that personal perception is not automatically shared reality.
- Hitagi identifies Araragi's general helping impulse as unusual when he treats it as ordinary.
- The Mayoi case reveals that the helper is also part of the condition: Araragi's own reluctance to go home matters.

The correct V2 description of early Araragi is therefore:

> **high-observation, high-rhetoric, emotionally oblique, motive-limited, self-correcting, and dependent on others for ontology.**

That is more precise than "unreliable."

## 2.2 Araragi's largest early blind spot is relational, not sensory

V02 and V03 make this especially clear.

Araragi frequently sees the relevant physical or behavioral evidence. What he misses is the **relationship structure that gives the evidence meaning**.

With Kanbaru, he initially assumes that because he does not perceive a meaningful relation, she cannot possess one-sided knowledge or interest in him.

With Nadeko, the opening childhood model is explicitly low-confidence. Araragi admits his memory may be mixed, incomplete, or wrong. Yet the familiar "shy childhood girl" image remains narratively powerful because it is easy for both narrator and reader to use.

With Hanekawa, the distortion becomes much more consequential.

Araragi notices:

- gauze;
- headaches;
- strange routes;
- inefficient requests;
- family anomalies;
- behavioral changes.

He still misses:

- courtship;
- jealousy;
- fear;
- desire for his presence;
- the emotional meaning of being asked rather than simply informed.

The failure is not lack of care.

It is care filtered through a role.

## 2.3 Positive idealization is one of the series' most important forms of misrecognition

V03 and V07 establish a principle that governs the entire V2 reread:

> **Idealization can dehumanize without hostility.**

Araragi misrecognizes Hanekawa partly because he thinks too highly of her.

The `委員長の中の委員長` image places Hanekawa above ordinary need. Gratitude turns her into:

- benefactor;
- saint;
- perfect student;
- moral reference point;
- person who knows everything;
- person who must therefore already know how to live.

That makes several ordinary possibilities harder for Araragi to perceive:

- she can want him;
- she can be jealous;
- she can need shelter;
- she can be frightened;
- she can make an inefficient request because she wants company;
- she can resent;
- she can desire care without ceasing to be good.

This is a crucial reader problem as well.

Early *Monogatari* gives the audience enough access to enjoy Araragi's admiration. The later corpus does not simply announce that the admiration was false. It reveals that admiration itself can become a **perceptual filter**.

The error is not:

> "Hanekawa was secretly bad."

The better formulation is:

> **Hanekawa's virtues were real; Araragi's demand that they form an exhaustive model of her was not.**

# 3. Comedy as camouflage, affect regulation, and relationship infrastructure

## 3.1 Serious emotion is frequently displaced into comic form

Araragi's narration often handles high-pressure feeling indirectly.

He converts:

- fear into argument;
- shame into self-deprecation;
- attraction into erotic commentary;
- vulnerability into tsukkomi;
- intimacy into ritualized insult;
- uncertainty into elaborate analogy;
- guilt into hyperbolic self-condemnation.

This means comedy cannot be treated as analytically empty.

At the same time, comedy does not create automatic moral immunity.

A joke can be:

- a bonding ritual;
- a defense;
- an attempt to regulate another person's panic;
- a way of keeping unbearable feeling speakable;
- a means of distributing power inside a relationship;
- a means of avoiding direct accountability.

The function must be determined scene by scene.

## 3.2 Hyakumonogatari makes comic procedure unusually visible

`佰物語` is especially useful because plot pressure is low. The source isolates conversational grammar.

Araragi remains recognizably Araragi, but his discourse changes by partner.

| Pair | Recurring interaction procedure | Narrational consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Araragi ↔ Hitagi | Hitagi controls premise; Araragi reacts, protests, renegotiates | romantic familiarity is procedural, not proof of total access |
| Araragi ↔ Hanekawa | explanation/correction meets admiring inquiry | admiration and epistemic dependence remain visible |
| Araragi ↔ Hachikuji | polite mock attack meets theatrical reciprocity | formality can carry intimacy rather than distance |
| Araragi ↔ Kanbaru | formal transgression meets boundary-setting tsukkomi | indecorous content cannot be modeled apart from disciplined delivery |
| Araragi ↔ Nadeko | short/literal speech meets softened elder-brother mode | quietness coexists with refusal and selective agency |
| Araragi ↔ Fire Sisters | older-brother authority is repeatedly punctured | family role is a contested performance |
| Araragi ↔ Meme | junior inquiry meets aphorism and undercutting | quotability is not doctrine |

The methodological result is important:

> **voice is not a bag of lexical markers. It is a relationship-indexed procedure for taking, yielding, contesting, or repairing conversational control.**

This will matter later for D18, but D02 establishes the epistemic consequence: who appears authoritative in a conversation partly depends on the interaction choreography.

## 3.3 Comedy can protect a relation without solving its problem

The Hachikuji exchanges are the clearest case.

Polite morphology, insults, puns, mock violence, correction, and companionship coexist. The comic routine is not simply decoration placed over a "real" relationship. It is one of the relationship's real forms.

Likewise Kanbaru's provocative formality and Araragi's tsukkomi create a boundary system. The humor lets both characters stage transgression and refusal without requiring every line to become a solemn negotiation.

That does not mean all comic boundary crossings are ethically erased.

The V2 rule is narrower:

> **comic form is evidence about relational procedure; it is not a waiver of consent, harm, or power analysis.**

# 4. Eroticization and attention: narration can critique a gaze while still participating in it

Araragi's first-person narration repeatedly sexualizes bodies.

This is not merely an external feature of presentation. It is part of focalization.

The analytical danger is to choose one of two simplifications:

1. the series is "really" critiquing the gaze, so the sexualization does not count;
2. the sexualization proves that every serious scene involving those bodies is invalidated.

The V2 evidence supports neither shortcut.

V01–V07 show Araragi's attention constantly sorting people through gendered, erotic, bodily, and archetypal cues. V02's Nadeko case can be diagnostically necessary while still being narratively sexualized. V03/V07 can expose Hanekawa's suffering while Araragi continues to eroticize her. The narration can recognize its own excess without thereby ceasing to perform it.

This produces a recurrent formal split:

> **self-awareness is not the same thing as transcendence.**

Araragi may know that he is performing a sexualizing joke. The reader may know that the joke is a joke. The body is still being selected and described through that gaze.

The same principle later applies to other narratorial habits:

- Kaiki knows he lies.
- Nadeko knows she avoids.
- Araragi knows he catastrophizes.
- Ougi knows she is pressuring him.
- Ononoki knows her tool-self presentation is constructed.

Awareness improves classification. It does not automatically dissolve the behavior.

Erotic focalization therefore belongs beside, not outside, the series' larger epistemic problem:

> **what a narrator repeatedly notices is part of what the narrator makes available to the reader.**

Reader knowledge is shaped not only by false statements but by **attention allocation**.

# 5. Hanekawa: from object of idealizing narration to narrator of integration

## 5.1 V03 → V07: retrospective Araragi cannot fully narrate the person he reveres

The Hanekawa sequence provides the clearest demonstration of why narrator substitution matters.

V03 gives Araragi's retrospective summary of Golden Week and his current interpretation of Hanekawa.

V07 returns to Golden Week in much greater detail, but it remains Araragi's retrospective narration. It is more candid, more precise about memory gaps, and more explicit about the limits of truth reproduction. It is still a construction.

V07 is especially valuable because Araragi tells the reader when some apparently quoted material is not literal testimony.

The imagined Shinobu rebuke, for example, has interpretive value as evidence of Araragi's internal model of their bond. It does not become direct Shinobu dialogue simply because it appears in quotation-like form.

This distinction will later become essential for:

- V19 Ougi-as-internal-critic logic;
- V30 `内なる羽川翼`;
- Mijikana dream/remembered voices.

## 5.2 V08 does not reveal the "true Hanekawa"; it redistributes authority

When Hanekawa narrates V08, the series performs a moral and epistemic decentering.

The reader receives direct access to a person whom Araragi had repeatedly explained from outside.

That direct access corrects major distortions:

- the perfect saint image;
- the idea that her self-control is effortless;
- the assumption that having "friends" means she can practice dependence;
- the assumption that she has transparent access to her own jealousy.

Yet Hanekawa's narration is not omniscient.

She revises herself inside the volume.

She can say:

- the old household is not "home," then feel its loss as `私の家`;
- that she had tried to approach her parents, then recognize that this account was incomplete;
- that she has friends, then discover she cannot ask them for shelter;
- that she does not understand the tiger, then acquire the vocabulary of jealousy through other people.

The narrative correction therefore occurs twice.

First:

> Araragi's model of Hanekawa is corrected by Hanekawa's first person.

Then:

> Hanekawa's first-person self-model is corrected by social interaction.

This is one of the series' deepest formal rules:

> **self-narration improves access without abolishing the need for other people.**

## 5.3 Black Hanekawa is not a hidden truth oracle

The same sequence prevents a "dark side = real self" reading.

Black Hanekawa possesses:

- shared knowledge;
- distinctive goals;
- different practical reasoning;
- partial access to stress;
- separate oddity mechanics;
- discontinuous memory conditions.

She can expose things Hanekawa suppresses.

She does not therefore become the one true Hanekawa speaking from beneath a mask.

V08's integrated model is plural:

- Hanekawa's goodness is real;
- anger is real;
- jealousy is real;
- Black Hanekawa is causally real;
- the tiger is causally real;
- none gains sole authenticity.

Narrative plurality and psychological plurality reinforce each other.

## 5.4 Later Hanekawa proves that integration is not transparency

V21's `つばさスリーピング` shows a more integrated Hanekawa who can directly own:

- anger;
- jealousy/rivalry;
- mixed motive;
- helper desire.

She is still not epistemically complete.

Her transformed-vampire interval includes a genuine memory blank. Some events must be reconstructed from aftermath and specialist inference.

V23 then supplies an external corrective from adult Araragi himself: his old knowledge of Hanekawa may now be noise. History gives him standing and memory, not permanent access to current Hanekawa.

V30 pushes the distinction further.

Araragi's `内なる羽川翼` is explicitly an internalized model. It may be useful. It is not current Hanekawa testimony.

The mature rule is:

> **the better Araragi knows that he is modeling Hanekawa, the more responsibly the model can be used—but it remains a model.**

# 6. Second Season narrator substitution: moral decentering through interested first persons

Second Season changes the reader's contract with the series.

The important formal development is not merely "other characters narrate."

It is:

> **the reader can no longer mistake Araragi's habitual access for the natural shape of reality.**

## 6.1 Kanbaru: confession without self-purity

V10's Kanbaru narration is unusually direct about:

- guilt;
- envy;
- sexuality;
- loneliness;
- self-punishment;
- bodily history.

This makes her seem more "honest" than early Araragi in some domains.

But Kanbaru still discovers motives during narration.

Her explicit admission that she envied Numachi comes late. Earlier interpretations centered other emotions. Her attempt to carry permanent bodily punishment as atonement is later corrected toward a future-directed responsibility model: `自分を責めることは反省じゃない`.

The narratorial importance is that confession itself can become another performance of morality.

A person can be brutally self-accusatory and still be wrong about what accountability requires.

Kanbaru therefore provides a second anti-hierarchy rule:

> **self-indictment is not automatically more truthful than self-excuse.**

## 6.2 Nadeko: selective self-lucidity and causal self-obscuring

V11 is the series' most overt experiment in narration as self-construction.

Nadeko begins with a character-sheet mode that foregrounds roles:

- cute;
- shy;
- victim;
- class representative;
- admirer;
- childlike relation to `暦お兄ちゃん`.

Her dominant self-name `撫子` makes self-reference itself part of the performance.

The Kuchinawa `俺様` register then appears to permit a rougher, more aggressive voice.

A simplistic reading would create an authenticity ladder:

> `撫子` = fake → `俺様` = true → later `私` = mature.

The V2 corpus rejects that.

The rough register carries thoughts Nadeko was already capable of thinking. It is still a constructed/disinhibiting role.

Likewise Nadeko can be acutely self-aware about some avoidance behaviors while hiding the central causal desire from herself. She understands that `ごめんなさい` can terminate conversation. She understands the appeal of rescue. Yet she constructs a supernatural adventure around a motive she refuses to name directly.

This is why "unreliable narrator" remains too general.

Nadeko is better described as:

> **selectively self-lucid, narratively inventive, role-conscious, and causally self-obscuring.**

Most importantly, she eventually marks her own fabrication boundary.

The narration can distinguish:

- event;
- admitted lie;
- retrospective realization;
- wished-for preview.

The distinction arrives late enough that fabrication has already shaped both plot and reader understanding.

## 6.3 Kaiki: declared lying does not make truth impossible

V13 creates the opposite reader temptation.

Kaiki openly warns that written language lies and promises concealment/fabrication. The reader is invited to become suspicious of everything.

That invitation itself becomes a manipulation.

If every statement is discarded, the reader becomes less rigorous, not more.

The correct method is claim-specific:

- physical travel and injury gain support through consequence;
- meetings are constrained by other speakers;
- self-description remains unstable;
- psychological diagnosis remains interested;
- professional claims require corroboration;
- apparent death is publication-locally available but later corrected by direct appearance.

Kaiki's most revealing reliability event is not when he boasts about lying.

It is when the detached-fraudster story can no longer explain his own continued action and he admits:

`俺はもう自分を騙せなかった`.

The key problem is self-deception.

Kaiki therefore reverses the usual logic.

The narrator who declares himself a liar may be easier to audit than the narrator whose role appears morally pure.

# 7. Ougi and Final Season: the narrator becomes an object of interrogation

Final Season does not simply return to Araragi's first person.

It returns to Araragi **after the reader has learned not to treat his viewpoint as the default ontology**.

Ougi becomes the formal mechanism that exploits this new condition.

## 7.1 Ougi's method is interrogative, accusatory, and dramaturgical

Ougi is not a neutral Socratic questioner.

V16 establishes:

- pressure on language;
- attention to counts and omissions;
- refusal of convenient closure;
- deliberate "casting";
- observational interest in how Araragi behaves.

Ougi's questions often produce real correction.

That does not make every implication true.

This is the same claim-specific rule applied to specialists and narrators:

> **a method can expose a real problem without making every theory generated by the method correct.**

## 7.2 Memory taxonomy: forgetting, misremembering, reconstruction, retained effect

V16 is the central memory text for D02 because it separates phenomena that ordinary discussion often collapses.

### Forgotten

An event is not narratively available as episodic recall.

Araragi's childhood shelter history is the clearest case.

External corroboration can establish the event without restoring subjective memory.

### Misremembered / misclassified

A remembered event survives under the wrong category.

Araragi vividly remembers Oikura's abusive home but classifies it as an abandoned house.

This is more dangerous than a blank because confidence attaches to the wrong model.

### Reconstructed

Later evidence permits an account of earlier events that the narrator did not originally possess.

This can be strong while remaining inferential.

### Retained effect

A forgotten event can continue shaping behavior, aversion, expectation, or relationship without being consciously recallable.

The formal consequence is profound:

> **the absence of narrative memory is not the absence of causal history.**

## 7.3 Self-indictment becomes another object of suspicion

Araragi repeatedly responds to correction by swinging toward total guilt.

Final Season refuses to treat this as moral enlightenment.

V14's `報い → 対価` correction is emblematic.

A bodily consequence can be real without being cosmic punishment.

V16 and V17 similarly resist:

- "I failed to recognize something" → "therefore I caused everything";
- "others could have lived without me" → "therefore I never mattered";
- "I was wrong" → "therefore self-erasure is the correct response."

This matters narratively because self-accusation is rhetorically persuasive. Readers often grant more truth to a harsh confession than to a self-defense.

The series repeatedly teaches the opposite discipline:

> **severity is not evidence.**

## 7.4 Ougi as continuity editor: useful correction and over-coherence risk

V17 makes Ougi's relation to continuity especially explicit.

Ougi enjoys reconciling contradictions. Hidden narrative becomes a source of perceived fraying. This can identify real gaps.

It can also incentivize totalization.

The March armor example is especially valuable: Araragi initially speaks as though he remembers Shinobu consuming the armor, then recognizes that he does not actually remember.

Narrative coherence had briefly substituted for episodic memory.

This produces a standing V2 rule:

> **a smooth story can be epistemically weaker than a visibly incomplete one.**

## 7.5 V15's anti-foreshadowing rule protects the entire V2 project

`暦物語` provides a methodological warning against retrospective overfitting.

Humans can connect past events until everything appears to have been foreshadowing.

The V2 consequence is not that later connections are invalid.

It is:

- later evidence may revise interpretation;
- later recurrence may establish thematic continuity;
- later text may deliberately backfill an earlier gap;

without proving:

- original authorial intention at every earlier publication point;
- character foresight;
- destiny;
- one master causal plan.

This rule is indispensable once Mijikana's later-authored spring-break insertions enter the corpus.

# 8. Ougi after diagnosis: self-criticism becomes relational rather than eliminative

V18 and V19 alter Ougi's function.

If Ougi were merely "the truth inside Araragi," the obvious endpoint would be elimination after self-recognition.

That is not what the later corpus supports.

Ougi becomes:

- critic;
- safety valve;
- partner in interpretation;
- relationally misaligned extension;
- later an independently continuing school figure.

V19 gives the clearest formulation.

Araragi and Ougi overlap but do not coincide. The `ズレ` is productive. Difference permits criticism.

This matters for narration because perfect self-transparency is not the goal.

A person does not become epistemically mature by eliminating every internal contradiction.

A more mature system may require:

- internalized countervoices;
- external friends;
- specialists;
- institutional procedures;
- remembered objections;
- people who know enough to challenge but not enough to possess.

The same principle appears later in V30's internal Hanekawa model, but with a crucial caution:

> **an internal critic is useful precisely when the narrator remembers that it is an internal critic.**

# 9. Off Season: leaving Araragi does not reveal a final truth

V20 is a formal break because three narrators occupy the first person in one volume.

The reader receives:

- Oikura;
- Kanbaru;
- Ononoki.

The result is not objective access.

It is three new performances.

## 9.1 Oikura: recovery narrated without personality conversion

Oikura's first person is abrasive, analytical, angry, and materially attentive.

Her narration makes visible things early Araragi-centered storytelling could not center as effectively:

- welfare;
- housing;
- scholarship;
- school norms;
- technology;
- outsider status;
- ordinary infrastructural recovery.

She can also overreach.

Her privacy violation can contribute to a useful outcome without becoming ethically clean.

Her reconstruction of other classmates remains inferential.

Most importantly, Oikura's growth does not require a softer narrator voice.

The series therefore refuses a common narrative shorthand:

> kinder tone = recovered person.

Oikura can become more agentic without becoming serene.

## 9.2 Kanbaru: inherited voices without inherited destiny

V20 Kanbaru openly narrates:

- body;
- desire;
- room disorder;
- dependency;
- shame;
- aspiration.

She also hears Tooe in a self-labeled daydream/internal voice.

That voice matters.

It does not become literal deposition from the dead.

The formal distinction allows Kanbaru to inherit:

- language;
- memory;
- values;
- warning;
- aspiration;

without surrendering authorship.

Narration becomes a space where inheritance can be present without becoming command.

## 9.3 Ononoki: the tool-self narrates evidence against its own totality

Ononoki repeatedly describes herself as:

- constructed;
- professional;
- tool-like;
- emotionally imitative.

Yet her narration records:

- embarrassment;
- fear;
- preference;
- concealment;
- gratitude pressure;
- attachment;
- conflict of interest.

The V2 mistake would be to conclude:

> "She says she is a tool, but secretly she is a normal human girl."

That would reproduce the hidden-true-self hierarchy the series repeatedly rejects.

The stronger formulation is:

> **the professional-tool self-story is real, and acquired affect is also real; neither alone exhausts the constructed person.**

V20 therefore demonstrates the general Off Season principle:

> **decentering Araragi does not uncover essences. It multiplies situated authorship.**

# 10. V21–V23: narrative access after the "main story"

## 10.1 Legendary narration is not forensic narration

V21's Acerola material includes a deliberately legendary/fairy-tale voice.

This mode compresses catastrophe into moral image.

It should not be flattened into the same evidence class as:

- Araragi's day-by-day report;
- Nadeko's case narration;
- police investigation;
- specialist testimony.

Formal genre is part of evidentiary handling.

The later Suicide-Master first person also proves that self-narration can omit material facts about the self. The age-regression evidence exists despite not being narratively foregrounded.

Again:

> **first-person access is selective access.**

## 10.2 Hanekawa's later self-access remains incomplete

V21's Hanekawa demonstrates real integration.

She can name mixed motive and anger without outsourcing them into the old purity structure.

But integration does not eliminate:

- risk appetite;
- self-expenditure;
- memory gaps;
- specialist dependence.

Recovery increases the range of narratable feeling.

It does not produce omniscience.

## 10.3 V22 Nadeko: present self authors past selves

V22 turns self-story into literal causal craft.

Current Nadeko draws prior Nadeko roles.

This is not an archival retrieval of objective past selves.

It is present authorship.

The distinction matters because V22 explicitly preserves continuity:

- past Nadekos remain hers;
- they are not disowned as unrelated persons;
- they do not govern her future;
- the drawn versions are stylized representations.

Nadeko's manga vocabulary—`キャラ`, `作者`, `モブ`, `NPC`, `描き直す`—becomes both:

- an analytical instrument;
- a possible source of distortion.

She understands herself through representation because she is now a creator.

The risk is to over-literalize the metaphor and treat life as though every person were a character under one author's total control.

The mature V22 rule is:

> **self-authorship is revision under constraint, not unilateral authorship of reality.**

## 10.4 V23 adult Araragi: better epistemic limits, persistent narrator residue

Adult institutional life improves Araragi's procedure.

He more readily distinguishes:

- rumor;
- evidence;
- victim protection;
- public fear;
- specialist uncertainty;
- institutional remit.

He also more readily delegates.

Yet his narration remains:

- self-deprecating;
- sexually comic;
- nostalgic;
- relationally invested;
- vulnerable to personal moral drama.

This is an important longitudinal result.

Adulthood is not a genre switch from unreliable adolescent to reliable professional.

It is:

> **improved procedure operating through the same recognizable person.**

The clearest adult epistemic proposition arrives through Hanekawa:

`お前がわからないことが…`

Old intimacy does not create permanent knowledge rights.

Araragi can care and still not know.

# 11. Monster Season: procedural epistemology becomes explicit

Monster Season increasingly turns narratorial problems into institutional ones.

The question is no longer merely:

> "Did Araragi read someone correctly?"

It becomes:

> "What counts as proof, who has standing to interpret, how should uncertainty be represented, and what procedures remain legitimate when the answer may still be wrong?"

## 11.1 V24: the right answer can come from a wrong proof

`証明は間違えたけど解答はあっていた` is one of the most important epistemic formulations in the supplied corpus.

A conclusion can be correct while the reasoning that produced it is not.

This blocks a common retrospective mistake:

> outcome truth does not retroactively validate the path.

The implication extends beyond mystery-solving.

It applies to:

- rescue;
- specialist intervention;
- coercion;
- self-story;
- moral blame;
- later authorial backfill.

Success and justification must be audited separately.

## 11.2 V26: plural narrators improve by admitting partiality

Araragi and Nadeko provide paired focalization.

Nadeko's casework is especially mature because she can separate:

- desire;
- means;
- victim;
- discoverer;
- client;
- provider;
- property incentive;
- domain uncertainty.

She still engages in self-blame.

The narration itself can then correct rhetorical moral identification away from causal perpetration.

This is a major V2 development:

> **epistemic maturity is not neutrality; it is better partitioning.**

## 11.3 V27: title association is not narrator evidence

The anonymous feminine interludes of `おうぎライト` provide a direct formal trap.

The title invites Ougi attribution.

Section 030 reveals Hitagi as the speaker.

This should remain a permanent warning against metadata-driven narrator assignment.

Do not infer speaker identity from:

- title;
- arc association;
- thematic fit;
- expected cast.

Wait for textual evidence.

The same volume also preserves multiple Ougi hypotheses after a literal mechanism is established.

Those hypotheses do not become worthless merely because they are not the final mechanism.

They remain psychologically diagnostic possibilities.

Thus:

> **literal answer and interpretive usefulness can coexist without collapsing into each other.**

## 11.4 V28: the narrator can openly construct the future he wants

V28's graduation epilogue briefly offers an implausibly neat future, then marks it with `そういうことに、しておこう`.

This is a narratorial honesty event.

The future fantasy is not simply a lie to be removed from analysis.

It reveals:

- desire;
- emotional closure;
- preferred order;
- the wish to narrate uncertainty as stability.

The correct classification is not TF.

It is narratively meaningful construction.

## 11.5 V29: relational proximity raises relevance, not neutrality

Uroko's testimony about Gaen-family history comes from a high-access position.

That access matters.

So does investment.

The V2 rule is:

> **closeness increases evidentiary relevance without guaranteeing neutrality.**

This is the mature version of an early problem.

Araragi's intimacy with Hanekawa did not grant transparent access.

Uroko's daughter-position does not either.

## 11.6 V30: mature narration can know that it is modeling

V30 offers two opposite signs of adult improvement.

First, `内なる羽川翼` explicitly stages an internal model. Araragi can use the remembered/cognitive Hanekawa as a tool while recognizing that the voice is internal.

Second, §§022–024 preserve his capacity to withhold and mislead.

The narrator has improved.

He has not become purified.

The V30 coda is equally important because it underdescribes a crisis. Underdescription is a source boundary.

The analytical discipline is:

> **when the narrator does not provide the mechanism, synthesis must not reward itself for inventing one.**

# 12. Self-story versus event fact

The phrase "self-story" can become vague unless the corpus distinguishes what sort of thing is being claimed.

The V2 evidence supports at least eight recurrent self-story operations.

## 12.1 Role adoption

A person organizes behavior around a social identity:

- class president;
- sick girl;
- justice fighter;
- shy/cute girl;
- fraudster;
- monster;
- tool;
- specialist;
- rescuer.

The role can be:

- imposed;
- chosen;
- useful;
- protective;
- coercive;
- incomplete.

Its incompleteness does not make it fake.

## 12.2 Retrospective moralization

A person tells the past as proof of:

- guilt;
- punishment;
- destiny;
- worthlessness;
- heroism;
- corruption.

Araragi and Shinobu are especially prone to self-condemning versions.

The reader must not assume harsher = truer.

## 12.3 Hidden-self essentialism

The narrator discovers:

- repressed desire;
- angry voice;
- monstrous body;
- fabricated origin;
- hidden memory;

and then concludes that the hidden layer is the "real" person.

The series repeatedly rejects this inference.

## 12.4 Benefactor or victim totalization

A relation becomes an explanatory identity.

Examples include:

- Hanekawa as benefactor;
- Nadeko as victim;
- Araragi as rescuer;
- Oikura as harmed person;
- Tsukihi as created/false being.

These statuses can be real while failing to exhaust the person.

## 12.5 Professional self-story

Later characters narrate themselves through work:

- specialist;
- police officer;
- creator;
- mediator;
- constructed professional tool.

Professional identity can create real competence and real duty.

It does not create total personhood or total jurisdiction.

## 12.6 Origin story

A person or relation is explained by:

- family;
- oddity creation;
- trauma;
- first rescue;
- vampirism;
- creator;
- naming.

Origins matter causally.

The V2 corpus strongly rejects the move from:

> "this helped form you"

to:

> "therefore this owns what you must remain."

## 12.7 Counterstory

A person deliberately constructs a new account against an earlier one.

This can be liberating.

It can also become another simplification.

Nadeko's later authorial self, Ougi's critique, and Kanbaru's inheritance refusal all show why counterstory must remain corrigible.

## 12.8 Plural story

The most mature state is often not one corrected master narrative.

It is the ability to preserve multiple true statements:

- I was a victim and later exercised harmful agency.
- I loved someone and no longer organize my future around that love.
- I am constructed and my acquired feelings matter.
- I was helped and my helper does not own my life.
- I harmed someone and am not therefore the sole author of everything that followed.
- I inherited this identity and can refuse its command.

This is where narration becomes a model of adult selfhood.

# 13. Memory versus retelling: a working taxonomy

The corpus requires a stronger vocabulary than "memory."

| Evidence state | Definition | High-value example | What must not be inferred |
|---|---|---|---|
| **episodic recall** | narrator claims direct remembered experience | many immediate Araragi sequences | perfect accuracy |
| **forgotten event** | event established without available episodic recall | V16 childhood shelter history | causal irrelevance |
| **misclassified memory** | event remembered under wrong category | V16 inhabited abusive home as `廃屋` | confidence = correctness |
| **reconstruction** | narrator combines later evidence into past model | Route X; adult motive chains | camera-level event fact |
| **embedded testimony** | another speaker supplies history | Shinobu, Uroko, Oshino letters | neutrality |
| **dream/internal voice** | psyche stages another person's voice | V20 Tooe; V30 inner Hanekawa | literal speech |
| **prospective invention** | narrator stages possible/preferred future | V11 preview; V28 neat future | event occurrence |
| **represented past self** | present narrator constructs an earlier self-image | V22 drawn Nadekos | archival authenticity |
| **later-authored early insertion** | later publication revisits earlier internal time | Mijikana SHORT-028–032 | earlier-character knowledge |
| **meta-memory montage** | recollection organized for thematic/provenance function | SHORT-026 `こよみヒストリー` | new hard chronology |

This taxonomy should be reused in later specialist documents.

It is especially important because *Monogatari* often makes **retelling itself** part of the event.

Hachikuji's farewell is a clean example.

The August separation, later narration, and eventual ability to say `さようなら` do not occur at the same time.

Experience, memory, telling, and closure have separate chronologies.

# 14. Reader complicity: character types are deliberately useful before they become insufficient

The series does not merely trick the reader with false archetypes.

It does something more interesting.

It gives the reader **useful low-resolution models**.

Early characters are legible through compressed social forms:

- Hitagi: sick girl / sharp-tongued beauty;
- Hanekawa: ideal class president;
- Kanbaru: athletic star / pervert;
- Nadeko: shy cute younger girl;
- Kaiki: liar/fraud;
- Ononoki: artificial tool;
- Ougi: suspicious mystery figure;
- Araragi: self-sacrificing helper.

These models work.

That is why they are dangerous.

If they were obviously false, correction would be easy.

Instead, later narration shows that the reader often participates in the same cognitive process as the characters:

1. notice a stable pattern;
2. assign a type;
3. use the type to predict behavior;
4. stop noticing evidence that does not fit;
5. experience correction as a "reveal";
6. risk replacing the old type with an equally totalizing new type.

The last step is the most important.

Examples:

- Hanekawa is not "secretly dark."
- Nadeko is not "secretly manipulative."
- Kaiki is not "actually a good man" merely because the fraudster model is incomplete.
- Ononoki is not "really a normal emotional girl."
- Ougi is not simply "the true conscience."
- Araragi is not "actually selfish" in a way that nullifies genuine care.

The mature reader must resist replacement essentialism.

The correct revision grammar is the project's standard:

- PRESERVE;
- STRENGTHEN;
- REVISE;
- DOWNGRADE;
- REJECT;
- OPEN.

Narrative correction should update the model rather than invert it.

# 15. Narrator substitution as moral decentering

The strongest formal consequence of narrator substitution is ethical.

When Araragi is the default first person, other people frequently enter the text as:

- person to rescue;
- person to interpret;
- person to desire;
- person to protect;
- person to feel guilty about.

When those people narrate, Araragi can become:

- offstage influence;
- mistaken memory;
- former crush;
- helper among several;
- source of frustration;
- absent relation;
- one model among others.

This does not diminish his causal importance.

It limits his interpretive jurisdiction.

The season progression can be summarized as follows.

| Phase | Narratorial structure | Ethical consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **First Season** | Araragi-dominant first person | sympathetic rescue centrality; reader learns corrigibility |
| **Second Season** | Hanekawa/Kanbaru/Nadeko/Kaiki plurality | other people organize evidence; Araragi loses default epistemic privilege |
| **Final Season** | Araragi returns under Ougi/self-judgment pressure | the former center becomes an object of analysis |
| **Off Season** | distributed first persons and offstage Araragi | people continue outside the role "person Araragi helped" |
| **Monster Season** | alternating narrators + institutional evidence problems | knowledge becomes procedural, domain-bounded, publicly consequential |
| **supplied Family boundary** | adult Araragi remains narrator inside shared governance | intimacy increases standing while narration remains interested |
| **supplementary corpus** | dialogue ensemble + historical local-state voices | ordinary interaction and prior selves gain resolution without new master authority |

This is why narrator change should be understood as more than stylistic variety.

It is one of the mechanisms by which the series refuses ownership.

# 16. Hyakumonogatari: direct dialogue and the limits of narrator language

Hyaku complicates D02 because much of the source is not prose narration.

Araragi is the interaction spine across 100 topic units, but direct dialogue changes the evidence problem.

There is less room for prose focalization to tell the reader:

- what someone "really" felt;
- what an expression "meant";
- what the narrator inferred in retrospect.

The source therefore becomes unusually valuable for **interaction grammar**.

This helps separate two concepts often conflated as "voice."

### Lexical voice

- pronouns;
- endings;
- catchphrases;
- register;
- vocabulary;
- speech quirks.

### Procedural voice

- who sets the premise;
- who corrects;
- who yields;
- who escalates;
- who turns seriousness into a joke;
- who softens disagreement;
- who asks for explanation;
- who refuses a frame;
- who repairs after transgression.

Procedural voice is epistemically important because it reveals where authority is negotiated in real time.

Hyaku also carries two source cautions.

First, the afterword references Shinobu's finished-audio narrator performance, but the supplied scenario does not provide enough recoverable narrator lines to reconstruct that prosody.

Second, speaker-label anomalies exist. The V2 analysis therefore refuses silent emendation.

This is a methodological virtue rather than a defect:

> **the source is allowed to remain imperfectly recoverable where the evidence is imperfect.**

# 17. Mijikanamonogatari: local-state narration and the dual-horizon rule

Mijikana expands the narrator archive far beyond Araragi.

The 39 stories include first-person/focalized prose associated with:

- Hanekawa;
- Hachikuji;
- Nadeko;
- Shinobu/Kiss-shot;
- Kaiki;
- Kanbaru;
- Ougi;
- Black Hanekawa;
- Meme;
- Suicide-Master;
- Ononoki;
- Karen;
- Tsukihi;
- plus external hunter-side narration.

Its largest narratological value is not simply "more narrators."

It is the ability to preserve **historical local states**.

A short may be written much later than the event it depicts.

That creates two coordinates:

1. first-release horizon;
2. internal-story horizon.

They must never be collapsed.

## 17.1 Later composition can revise the reader without revising the earlier character

SHORT-028–032 revisit spring break from later publication horizons.

These stories can legitimately change the present reader's model of:

- motives;
- constraints;
- hunter-side understanding;
- Meme's action space;
- Kiss-shot's history.

They do not make those later publication insights available to V04 Araragi at event-time.

This is the narratorial version of the project's chronology rule:

> **retrospective interpretation can travel backward; character knowledge cannot travel backward without textual evidence.**

## 17.2 Internalized and remembered voices remain source-labeled

SHORT-021's Tooe material is dream/internal maternal voice.

SHORT-026 is meta-memory montage.

These forms may reveal:

- present organization of memory;
- inherited values;
- unresolved affect;
- current self-story.

They do not automatically preserve literal historical wording.

## 17.3 Ougi interiority remains bounded

SHORT-035 gives a particularly clean epistemic limit.

Araragi can know:

- Ougi's origin history;
- long relational context;
- recurrent behavior;
- causal connection.

He still cannot possess Ougi's inner state.

That strengthens a corpus-wide rule:

> **provenance knowledge is not interior ownership.**

# 19. The reader's changing epistemic task across the series

The V2 corpus suggests that the series trains the reader in stages.

## Stage 1 — trust the sympathetic observer enough to enter the world

Early Araragi must be readable.

If he were merely deceptive, the series would not function.

The reader learns characters through his attention.

## Stage 2 — notice correction inside the same narration

Other characters tell Araragi:

- you are unusual;
- your model is incomplete;
- you are overvaluing one interpretation;
- you do not know this motive;
- your moral impulse is outrunning the case.

## Stage 3 — experience another person's first person

Hanekawa, Kanbaru, Nadeko, Kaiki, and others reorganize previously known figures.

The reader learns that a changed focalizer changes not just tone but evidence.

## Stage 4 — distrust the desire for a final correction

The new narrator is not "the truth."

Self-narration has its own role systems.

## Stage 5 — distinguish memory, model, testimony, and invention

Final Season makes these categories explicit.

## Stage 6 — accept distributed adulthood

Off Season removes the expectation that one protagonist must mediate every meaningful life.

## Stage 7 — require procedure

Monster Season increasingly asks whether evidence, authority, institutional standing, and intervention are properly separated.

## Stage 8 — preserve intimate interest without granting epistemic sovereignty

V30's adult/family position is not neutral because it is intimate.

Its maturity lies in consultation and explicit standing, not in omniscient love.

This progression parallels the series' ethical architecture:

> **reader maturity and character maturity both require learning that caring about someone does not mean possessing the final interpretation of them.**

# 22. What D02 establishes for later character monographs

Later character documents should inherit several mandatory rules.

## 22.1 Do not reconstruct voice from catchphrases alone

Use:

- lexical register;
- syntax;
- discourse control;
- partner-conditioned changes;
- self-correction patterns;
- humor procedure;
- role performance;
- what the character repeatedly notices.

## 22.2 Preserve historical narrator states

A mature monograph must show:

- what the character could narrate at each stage;
- what they could not yet narrate;
- what later correction changes;
- which earlier self-story remains historically real.

## 22.3 Separate self-report from project judgment

A character saying:

- "I am a monster";
- "I am fake";
- "I caused this";
- "I do not have feelings";
- "I was only a victim";
- "I am a liar";

is evidence about the character.

It is not automatically the final analytical conclusion.

## 22.4 Use other narrators as counterperspectives, not replacement owners

Araragi on Hanekawa, Hanekawa on Araragi, Nadeko on Ougi, Ougi on Araragi, Kaiki on Nadeko, Uroko on Gaen: all are valuable.

None should become the final proprietor of the other person's identity.

# 24. Mature synthesis

The supplied corpus supports a stronger final formulation than "everyone is an unreliable narrator."

*Monogatari* repeatedly places a person inside a story about themselves or someone they love.

Araragi tells himself that helping is ordinary, then that repayment should be bodily, then that guilt means he caused everything, then that maturity may require not being central.

Hanekawa tells herself that goodness and normality can protect her from family definition, then discovers that suppression has become another prison.

Kanbaru tells herself that punishment is responsibility, then learns that self-blame can avoid the harder work of future action.

Nadeko occupies the shy/cute/victim story, then the disinhibited voice, then godhood, then manga authorship and specialist practice—without any one stage becoming the secret authentic answer to all previous stages.

Kaiki announces that he lies and then becomes most legible when he can no longer preserve his lie about his own motives.

Ougi pressures stories until their contradictions become visible, yet Ougi's own interpretive pressure can also overfit coherence.

Oikura narrates recovery without becoming pleasant.

Ononoki narrates a constructed-tool identity that remains real even as her own prose records affect the identity cannot fully contain.

Adult Araragi becomes more procedural without becoming neutral.

The formal lesson is therefore inseparable from the ethical one.

> **No narrator owns the final meaning of another person merely because they care, suffer, remember, created, rescued, diagnosed, loved, opposed, or originated that person.**

The same is true of the narrator's relation to the self.

> **A person has privileged access to their own experience without receiving unlimited authority to convert one self-description into a permanent ontology.**

This is why self-story matters so much in *Monogatari*.

Stories are not just falsehoods to be stripped away.

They are:

- coping structures;
- moral technologies;
- relationship scripts;
- identity containers;
- ways of allocating blame;
- ways of making desire speakable;
- ways of hiding desire;
- ways of carrying memory;
- ways of revising memory;
- ways of constructing futures.

A good story can become a prison.

A false story can produce real consequences.

A later story can correct an earlier one without making the earlier life unreal.

A narrator can tell the truth and still misrecognize the person.

A liar can reveal something true.

A critic can be useful without being omniscient.

A loved person can remain partly unknown.

And a reader can become more responsible by learning not to demand that the series resolve all of those tensions into one final voice.

The most compact D02 thesis is therefore:

> **Monogatari treats narration as situated agency: people make themselves and others legible through stories that are causally real, ethically consequential, and always corrigible. Narrative maturity does not mean reaching a final true story. It means preserving evidence, accepting correction, distinguishing access from authority, and allowing both self and other to remain revisable.**

The reader's corresponding obligation is:

> **do not confuse intimacy with omniscience, confession with truth, severity with accuracy, coherence with memory, origin with essence, or later understanding with earlier knowledge.**
