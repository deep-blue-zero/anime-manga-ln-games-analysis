# 〈物語〉シリーズ V2 — Fake / Real, Personhood, Integration, and Self-Authorship

## No simple authentic self beneath the mask

## 0. Function and governing thesis

This document is the canonical V2 primary home for the series-wide **`偽物 / 本物` problem**, personhood under artificial or nonordinary origin, integration of contradictory selves, and the relation between performance and self-authorship.

The architecture's governing question is:

> **If origin does not determine value, what makes a constructed identity real enough to carry responsibility?**

The question sounds abstract. Monogatari makes it concrete by repeatedly producing people, roles, bodies, memories, and relationships whose origins are difficult to classify cleanly.

A justice role can be borrowed.

A specialist can be a fraud and still know real techniques.

A sister can have a nonhuman reproductive origin she does not know.

A persona can be assembled from an external oddity, a host's stress, learned knowledge, and later habit.

A critic can originate in another person's self-condemnation and still become independently addressable.

A corpse-doll can be engineered for professional use and acquire relationships, tastes, embarrassment, mistakes, and obligations.

A girl can draw versions of her own past and discover that even self-portraiture can stereotype the person portrayed.

A vampire's aristocratic language can be taught rather than primordial and still become centuries of lived biography.

An apology can be a performance without therefore being false.

A surname can be newly adopted without erasing the person's older continuity.

The corpus therefore does **not** support the simple rule:

> fake things become real if they try hard enough.

That slogan is too broad. It confuses several different questions.

A false factual claim does not become historically true through effort.

Kaiki's fake credentials remain fake.

A fabricated genealogy remains fabricated.

A mistaken memory remains mistaken even if it becomes emotionally important.

An illegitimate intervention does not become legitimate because the recipient later benefits.

But a role, habit, relationship, name, style, vocation, social category, or self-description can become **lived reality** through continued enactment, recognition, revision, and consequence even when it began through imitation, borrowing, accident, performance, or construction.

The strongest full-series formulation is therefore:

> **Monogatari separates authenticity from origin. A person's reality is not secured by discovering one natural core beneath every role, nor is it nullified by artificial provenance. Personhood becomes legible through continuity, agency, relation, memory, practice, corrigibility, vulnerability, and answerability. Constructed forms become ethically real when they are inhabited by subjects who can act, be affected, revise, refuse, and carry consequences—without requiring the construction to become secretly natural.**

This also means that integration is not purification.

The mature person is not the one who strips away:

- masks;
- borrowed roles;
- contradictory desires;
- old selves;
- artificial language;
- socially conferred names;
- oddity residue;
- embarrassing history.

The mature person is more often the one who can say:

> this helped make me;

> this was not all of me;

> this was sometimes a performance;

> the performance had consequences;

> I may keep, revise, graduate from, or refuse it;

> and none of those choices require declaring the earlier person unreal.

The compact D15 thesis is:

> **In Monogatari, “real” is not a purity certificate. It is a claim about a life that has become causally continuous, socially addressable, self-revising, and answerable enough that its history and choices matter. The series' anti-essentialism does not abolish identity; it relocates identity from hidden origin to lived continuity under revision.**

---

# 1. First distinction: there is more than one kind of “fake”

The series becomes incoherent if every use of `偽物` is treated as the same concept.

D15 needs a multidimensional vocabulary.

| Dimension | What can be fake/real? | Example | D15 rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Factual** | a claim about what happened | Kaiki's lies; false genealogy | false remains false unless evidence changes |
| **Credential** | claimed social/professional status | Kaiki as “fake specialist” | false credential does not imply zero competence |
| **Genealogical / origin** | where a body/person came from | Tsukihi, Ougi, Ononoki | artificial/nonhuman origin does not settle personhood |
| **Role** | socially performed identity | Fire Sisters justice, specialist, student, family role | role can become lived through practice without becoming essence |
| **Affective** | claimed or hidden feeling | Hanekawa's split affect; Nadeko's roles | hidden/darker feeling is not automatically truer |
| **Representational** | depiction of a person | Nadeko's created selves | representation can be causally active and still inaccurate |
| **Relational** | a socially enacted relation | Meme calling Ougi `姪っ子` | relation can become real through recognition without natural genealogy |
| **Narrative** | story used to organize action | Kuchinawa, fake oddity histories | false story can have real effects without becoming true history |
| **Diachronic** | earlier versus later self | Hachikuji, Oikura, Araragi career claims | change does not prove earlier self was fraudulent |
| **Performative** | act requiring form or script | apology, politeness, professional register | performance is not the opposite of sincerity |

This table prevents a common mistake.

If the series says a role is `偽物`, that does not automatically imply the person is ontologically fake.

If a person's body has an artificial origin, that does not make every social relation a lie.

If a feeling is hidden, that does not make the visible feeling counterfeit.

If a practice is imitated, that does not make later ownership impossible.

If a relationship is socially invented, that does not make its later obligations imaginary.

Conversely, anti-essentialism does not license the opposite collapse:

> everything is equally real, so factual accuracy no longer matters.

It matters.

Kaiki's falsehoods are false.

The initial V11 Kuchinawa genealogy is fabricated.

The exact numerical identity of reconstructed Ononoki remains open.

Uroko's biological/metaphysical production details remain open.

Monogatari's anti-purity philosophy is strongest when it keeps those evidentiary distinctions intact.

# 2. V02 prehistory: front and back without an authenticity ladder

The fake/real axis is already present before *Nisemonogatari* names it directly.

Kanbaru's V02 problem is often described through `表 / 裏`.

There is:

- affection for Hitagi;
- jealousy;
- athletic discipline;
- violent desire;
- shame;
- a wish to preserve the relationship;
- a wish to remove the rival.

The Rainy Devil contract externalizes contradiction.

The easy reading is:

> the violent side is the hidden real Kanbaru.

V2 rejects that.

The violent wish is real.

The loving wish is real.

The disciplined athlete is real.

The later reflective adult is real.

No one state receives sovereign authenticity merely because it is darker, more embarrassing, or less socially acceptable.

This prehistory matters because it establishes the core anti-essentialist rule before the series begins using `偽物 / 本物` as explicit philosophical vocabulary:

> **concealment can hide truth without making what is concealed the whole truth.**

That principle will govern Hanekawa, Nadeko, Ougi, Ononoki, and the V19 mirror/remainder world.

# 3. V05 *Nisemonogatari*: fake justice and responsible enactment

V05 is the explicit philosophical foundation.

Its strongest result is not the aphorism that a fake can become more valuable than the real through effort.

Its stronger result is narrower and more useful:

> **borrowed or theatrical ideals can become ethically significant through ownership, practice, consequence, and repair; origin alone does not decide their value.**

## 3.1 Karen's justice is partly imitation

Araragi calls the Fire Sisters' activity `正義の味方ごっこ`.

His judgment is not neutral.

He is affectionate, dismissive, envious, and aware that his sisters possess a local social reach he lacks.

Karen's justice is indeed partly:

- borrowed;
- youthful;
- theatrical;
- under-theorized;
- driven by heroic scripts.

But those features do not make the consequences fake.

She confronts Kaiki.

She is harmed.

Other children rely on the Fire Sisters.

Rumors circulate.

Cleanup remains after the confrontation ends.

The key word becomes `後始末`.

The role becomes more morally serious when Karen accepts that justice is not only the thrilling moment of identifying and confronting a villain.

It includes residue.

That is the beginning of ownership.

## 3.2 Practice matters, but effort is not magic

V05 can tempt the slogan:

> if a fake tries harder than the real, it becomes superior.

D15 narrows this.

Effort alone cannot transform:

- a lie into history;
- incompetence into authority;
- harm into virtue;
- obsession into legitimate jurisdiction.

What practice can do is produce **new causal history**.

Karen repeatedly enacts justice, learns consequences, receives correction, and later moves toward institutional forms of responsibility.

By V30, police work and judo are not proof that the V05 role was secretly destined to be authentic all along.

They are later consolidations of continuities she kept choosing.

The adult career is therefore evidence of practice becoming biography, not origin being retroactively purified.

## 3.3 `偽物` can be an accurate self-description without being self-negation

Tsukihi's early comments about justice are important here even before the phoenix reveal.

She can distinguish:

- Karen's justice as something like a goal;
- her own justice as closer to a hobby;
- the influence of Karen and Araragi;
- the possibility that the Fire Sisters role may end.

Calling her justice `偽物` can therefore mean:

> I know this ideology is borrowed and not wholly mine.

That is a sophisticated claim about provenance.

It is not a claim that Tsukihi herself is counterfeit.

This distinction becomes essential in V06.

# 4. Kaiki: the fake specialist with real technique

Kaiki is the cleanest case against binary epistemology.

He lies.

He exploits.

He sells fraudulent or manipulative occult products.

His social credentials are unreliable.

And he knows real things.

The correct rule is not:

> liar = false.

It is:

> **reliability is claim-specific.**

## 4.1 False credential remains false

Anti-essentialism does not rehabilitate fraud into truth.

If Kaiki presents himself under a false credential, the credential remains false.

If he invents a story, the story remains invented.

If he violates privacy, useful information obtained from that violation does not make the method legitimate.

D15 therefore rejects an over-flexible fake/real doctrine.

The series is not saying that all distinctions dissolve.

## 4.2 Technique can be real inside a false social role

Kaiki possesses:

- occult knowledge;
- social-engineering skill;
- knowledge of specialist networks;
- enough real technique to create or exploit dangerous effects;
- enough interpretive skill to notice futures others miss.

The interesting question is what happens when technical capacity and socially false presentation coexist.

The answer is morally uncomfortable:

> **a fake social role can carry real causal power.**

That makes it more dangerous, not less.

## 4.3 V13 defeats Kaiki's own authenticity performance

Kaiki also performs an identity.

Professional liar.

Transactional man.

Money explains everything.

Replaceability protects against sentimental nonsense.

V13 shows that this self-story is real and incomplete.

He accepts Gaen's money to stop and continues anyway.

He spends more than the clean transactional model predicts.

He carries Tooe/Kanbaru history.

He develops curiosity and particular attention toward Nadeko.

Then his perfect lie fails.

The volume narrated by the master liar does not prove that his deepest authentic self is secretly compassionate.

That would repeat the same error.

The stronger conclusion is:

> **Kaiki's professional performance is one genuine organizing layer among several, and his best action occurs when he can revise the role rather than worship it.**

He does not become “the real Kaiki.”

He becomes a Kaiki capable of abandoning an inadequate Kaiki-model.

# 5. V06 Tsukihi: personhood without a natural-origin test

Tsukihi is one of the corpus's most decisive anti-essentialist cases because she does **not** knowingly perform humanity in order to earn it.

Kagenui and Ononoki identify her as `しでの鳥`.

The body mechanism is radically nonordinary.

The reproductive history is brood-parasitic.

Her regenerative continuity is supernatural.

Those claims are ontologically important.

They do not settle the question:

> is the girl Araragi has lived with a real person, sister, friend, agent, and bearer of responsibility?

## 5.1 The person is visible before the reveal

Before the phoenix diagnosis, Tsukihi already has:

- memories;
- preferences;
- social judgment;
- friendships;
- motives;
- suggestibility;
- reflective distance from the Fire Sisters role;
- the capacity to imagine ending that role.

The revelation changes causal explanation.

It does not retroactively manufacture those traits.

## 5.2 “Fake becoming real through striving” is not enough

A neat reading would say:

> Tsukihi is a fake human who became real by trying hard to live as one.

But she does not know she is a counterfeit.

There is no explicit project of becoming human.

That makes the effort slogan a poor foundation for personhood.

The stronger basis is:

- lived continuity;
- current subjectivity;
- relation;
- agency;
- vulnerability;
- social history;
- answerability.

Tsukihi does not need to pass a naturalness test.

## 5.3 Family recognition is powerful and incomplete

Araragi's defense is morally important.

He refuses to let origin erase the life they have lived as siblings.

That is recognition.

But he also decides that Tsukihi should not know the truth about herself.

D13 and D14 already preserve the paternalism.

D15 adds the personhood implication:

> **recognizing someone's personhood does not entitle the recognizer to own the person's self-knowledge.**

A family can correctly say “you are still you” and still wrongly control what “you” are allowed to know.

## 5.4 V20 strengthens the lived-person test

Ononoki's surveillance sees Tsukihi living ordinary life for months.

When the professional cover story breaks down, Tsukihi acts inside the absurd magical-girl premise and eventually rescues the very person assigned to monitor her.

This does not prove that Tsukihi is “really human.”

It proves something more relevant:

> **the monitored object is a relational agent whose choices create obligations in return.**

The phoenix body remains unusual.

The personhood does not depend on removing that difference.

# 6. Operational personhood: what counts when origin is unstable

The corpus does not supply one formal metaphysical definition of personhood.

D15 should not invent one.

It does, however, repeatedly treat several features as morally significant.

A constructed or nonordinary being becomes increasingly difficult to treat as mere object when the source establishes a cluster such as:

1. **causal continuity** — present action connects to a history rather than appearing as a disposable effect;
2. **subjective orientation** — the being has preferences, fears, aversions, attachments, or goals;
3. **agency** — it can choose, improvise, refuse, conceal, revise, or fail;
4. **vulnerability** — things can matter to it, including destruction, humiliation, loss, or separation;
5. **relationship history** — others address it as someone and the relation changes both sides;
6. **self-revision** — it can treat its own earlier model as incomplete;
7. **answerability** — its choices can be attributed to it rather than only to a creator/operator;
8. **future openness** — it can become something not exhausted by original function.

None is a single essence marker.

The point is cumulative.

This is why Ononoki's mistakes matter.

Why Ougi's fear matters.

Why Tsukihi's friendships matter.

Why Black Hanekawa's distinct speech matters without making Black Hanekawa the only real Hanekawa.

Why Nadeko's drawn selves matter even though they are current representations rather than recovered originals.

Personhood becomes ethically visible through **participation in a continuing field of consequences**.

This has a second implication:

> recognition increases accountability as well as protection.

Calling Ononoki a person means her autonomous lethal judgment can be criticized.

Calling Nadeko's god-state action genuinely hers means earlier victimhood cannot erase later culpability.

Calling Tsukihi a person does not imply that her regeneration creates no external risk.

Personhood is not a shield against responsibility.

It is part of what makes responsibility meaningful.

# 7. Hanekawa: integration without one `本物`

Hanekawa is the corpus's most explicit rejection of hidden-self authenticity.

Black Hanekawa is easy to overread because the persona is:

- rougher;
- more sexual;
- more violent;
- more direct;
- less socially polished.

That combination encourages a familiar psychological shortcut:

> the socially unacceptable voice must be the real voice underneath.

The source resists it.

## 7.1 Black Hanekawa is hybrid, not revealed essence

The original `障り猫` does not simply possess Hanekawa and depart.

Hanekawa reincorporates the cat.

The resulting Black Hanekawa combines:

- external oddity mechanics;
- Hanekawa's stress;
- Hanekawa's knowledge;
- a distinct body;
- a distinct register;
- partial agency;
- partial access.

Black Hanekawa can reveal things ordinary Hanekawa cannot say.

That is not the same as possessing total truth.

The persona even has explicit `キャラ設定`-like qualities.

The artificiality of the register is part of the evidence, not a reason to dismiss it.

## 7.2 A generated persona can be real without being sovereign

Black Hanekawa is causally real.

People are harmed.

Araragi is addressed differently.

Hanekawa's stress is redistributed.

The body changes.

The persona's claims matter.

But the persona is not entitled to say:

> I am the one real Hanekawa.

This is the precise model D15 needs for constructed identities:

> **causal reality does not imply authenticity monopoly.**

## 7.3 V08 rejects `本物 / 本体 / 主人格`

Hanekawa's V08 breakthrough is not purification.

It is plural inclusion.

Jealousy.

Anger.

Desire.

Goodness.

Black Hanekawa.

The tiger.

Disciplined earlier selves.

All remain hers.

`すべてが私` is not a declaration that every state is equally desirable or morally good.

It is a refusal to solve contradiction by exiling unwanted parts into a false category.

The coda's rejection of one `本物`, one `本体`, or one `主人格` gives D15 its strongest direct textual foundation.

## 7.4 `灰色の大人` is anti-purity adulthood

Hanekawa does not want to become worse.

She wants to become capable of ordinary moral mixture:

- dislike;
- anger;
- jealousy;
- tears;
- preference;
- imperfect forgiveness;
- limited kindness.

The goal is not authenticity as rawness.

It is a responsible self that no longer needs emotional cleanliness to certify moral worth.

The mature rule is therefore:

> **integration keeps contradiction inside the person while preserving the right to judge particular actions.**

That is very different from “everything I feel is equally valid because it is authentic.”

# 8. Kanbaru and Nadeko: darker is not truer

Hanekawa's explicit self-theory gives a general rule that later and earlier characters confirm.

## 8.1 Kanbaru's `暗黒面` is a rejected model

V10 allows Kanbaru to name her prior tendency to split herself into a respectable front and dark underside.

Her growth does not come from admitting:

> the murderous side was the real Kanbaru.

It comes from refusing to make self-punishment or hidden darkness the master explanation.

She remains responsible for violent desire.

She also remains more than that desire.

## 8.2 Nadeko's Kuchinawa voice contains truth without becoming essence

Kuchinawa's `俺様` supplies Nadeko with a voice capable of command, accusation, and permission.

The voice carries real disowned assertion.

The initial supernatural genealogy is still fabricated.

The aggressive register is therefore both revealing and constructed.

That is exactly why it is valuable to D15.

A constructed role can surface material the ordinary presentation cannot yet own without becoming the one authentic self.

## 8.3 God Nadeko is not authentic liberation

Godhood produces:

- more direct speech;
- less social inhibition;
- lethal power;
- possessiveness;
- action on violent fantasy.

The same temptation returns:

> now the mask is off.

But later development makes that reading untenable.

Godhood freezes rather than solves major parts of Nadeko's self-story.

The impossible romance becomes actionable.

Power reduces the need for negotiation.

A more disinhibited state is not automatically a more truthful person.

## 8.4 Pronoun change is not a truth ladder

Early `撫子`.

Kuchinawa `俺様`.

God-state `私`.

Later artist/specialist `私`.

These are important state markers.

They should not become:

> child-name = fake → aggressive pronoun = hidden truth → `私` = final authentic self.

The same pronoun can occur under ethically different states.

Language records self-positioning.

It does not certify essence.

# 9. V12 and later return: change does not falsify earlier truth

D15's fake/real problem is not limited to artificial bodies.

It also concerns diachronic identity.

A later state can tempt the reader to call an earlier one false.

Hachikuji is one of the clearest controls.

## 9.1 The farewell is real

V12's farewell is a genuine ending.

Hachikuji refuses to continue a harmful supernatural role merely because Araragi cannot bear the loss.

She authors the departure.

`わたしは帰るんですよ`.

The ending matters.

## 9.2 V18 does not make V12 fake

Hachikuji later returns and accepts deity work.

That does not mean:

- she never wanted to leave;
- the farewell was performative fraud;
- Araragi was right to treat separation as impossible;
- her new role was secretly always destined.

The conditions changed.

A new choice became available.

The person can make a different decision without invalidating the earlier decision.

This yields a broad diachronic rule:

> **sincere identity is time-indexed. Later revision is not proof of earlier bad faith.**

The same rule will matter for:

- Araragi's career self-story;
- Nadeko's relation to Araragi and godhood;
- Karen's justice;
- Oikura's ordinary-life project;
- Hitagi's surname;
- Shinobu's names and bodies.

# 10. Oikura: ordinary life as constructed practice

Oikura provides an important non-supernatural control.

Her later life is not explained by hidden essence.

It is built.

## 10.1 Forgotten does not mean unreal

`忘れたからといって、なかったことにはならない`.

Araragi's memory failure demonstrates that continuity does not depend on one observer retaining access.

A history can be real even when:

- one participant forgets;
- records are incomplete;
- later self-story excludes it.

This matters for all constructed-person questions because memory cannot be the sole criterion of identity.

Tsukihi can lose crisis memory.

Hanekawa can split or externalize affect.

Ononoki can be reconstructed under metaphysical uncertainty.

A perfect autobiographical archive is not required for continuity.

## 10.2 `普通` is a practice

V20's Oikura does not discover that she was secretly an ordinary girl all along.

She learns ordinary infrastructure:

- housing;
- school;
- social support;
- friendship procedures;
- later professional routine.

`目指せ普通` is a project.

This gives D15 a crucial positive model:

> **constructed does not mean insincere.**

Many adult identities are practices assembled through institutions, habits, and repeated choices.

## 10.3 Professional code-switching is additive

Adult Oikura can use a more competent professional register.

That does not make the abrasive private voice an immature fake that should disappear.

The later person contains multiple context-sensitive modes.

Maturity can expand the repertoire without replacing the person.

# 11. Ougi: derived from Araragi, recognized as someone

Ougi is the corpus's most conceptually direct constructed-person case because the source eventually provides a strong origin model while refusing to let that model exhaust the being.

## 11.1 Origin is distributed

V18 strongly links Ougi to Araragi's `自己批判精神`.

The construction also draws from a broader field:

- knowledge of the real darkness;
- vampire-adjacent conditions;
- first-thrall/Rainy Devil/Shinobu residue;
- town oddity ecology;
- borrowed names and social forms.

So even before personhood is considered, the clean formula:

`Ougi = Araragi`

is already insufficient.

## 11.2 `独立した精神体`

Gaen's description supplies the decisive anti-reduction lock.

Ougi can be derived from Araragi and still become independent enough to:

- know things he does not consciously know;
- oppose him;
- fear deletion;
- help him;
- be helped by him;
- continue after diagnosis.

The rule is:

> **derivation does not imply disposability.**

## 11.3 Recognition does not naturalize Ougi

Meme calls Ougi his `可愛い姪っ子`.

D11 correctly refuses to treat the line as recovered biological genealogy.

That is precisely what makes it philosophically strong.

Ougi is not rescued by proving:

> actually, she was naturally family all along.

She survives as constructed Ougi through a socially valid relation.

Recognition creates a livable position without rewriting origin.

## 11.4 Social recognition is neither omnipotent nor trivial

Meme's statement works because the Monogatari world contains systems in which names, categories, and social placement can be causally significant.

But recognition does not create Ougi from nothing.

Araragi's origin matters.

Ougi's own fear matters.

The darkness's corrective logic matters.

The social act matters.

The mature model is therefore distributed:

> **personhood may require recognition without being reducible to recognition.**

A society can fail to recognize a person who still exists.

Recognition affects what futures become livable.

## 11.5 `ダブル / 批判者 / 安全弁 / 相棒`

V19 gives Ougi several overlapping later categories.

None is a hidden true noun.

A double overlaps without numerical identity.

A critic requires `ズレ`.

A safety valve performs a function.

A partner names a relation.

The coexistence of these categories is the point.

Ougi becomes more stable not by finding the one correct label but by inhabiting a set of roles that can be revised.

## 11.6 Origin knowledge does not create interior ownership

SHORT-035 provides one of the most important maturity locks.

Araragi knows Ougi's provenance.

He still does not transparently know Ougi's interior state.

This is D12's non-possession rule applied to constructed identity:

> **knowing how someone was made does not mean knowing everything the person now is.**

# 12. Ononoki: artificial presentation becomes lived biography

Ononoki begins with artificiality foregrounded rather than hidden.

`僕はキメ顔でそう言った` announces a manufactured presentation.

Her voice is stylized.

Her body is constructed.

Her professional role is instrumental.

The later development would be badly misread as:

> the fake doll gradually reveals the real human girl underneath.

There is no need for such a hidden human.

## 12.1 `人といるため`

V14's body formulation is crucial.

The human-shaped body is not simply:

- in order to be human;
- to prove humanity;
- to disguise a natural essence.

It is `人といるため`.

To be with people.

Embodiment becomes relational infrastructure.

That is a powerful alternative to authenticity metaphysics.

A body can matter because it enables:

- speech;
- proximity;
- work;
- embarrassment;
- conflict;
- friendship;
- care.

## 12.2 Artificial speech is still lived speech

A catchphrase may be deliberately manufactured.

Repeated use creates history.

The same applies to:

- `僕`;
- deadpan delivery;
- technical explanation;
- professional tool-language.

An artificial register can become a person's actual register through practice.

The source does not require spontaneous natural origin before language counts as identity.

## 12.3 Acquired affect is still causally real

V20 lets Ononoki narrate herself as corpse and tool.

The same narration contains:

- ice-cream preferences;
- embarrassment;
- rivalry;
- irritation;
- fear;
- attachment;
- professional shame;
- concealment;
- gratitude pressure.

The wrong conclusion is:

> she was lying about being a tool.

The stronger conclusion is:

> **the tool ontology and the acquired/lived affect can both be true.**

A feeling need not be primordial to matter.

## 12.4 Failure is personhood evidence

Ononoki chooses.

Improvises.

Conceals.

Misjudges.

Revises.

This matters because a passive instrument can be described entirely through its operator and design.

An agent who makes independent mistakes cannot.

Recognizing that agency increases both:

- moral standing;
- responsibility.

## 12.5 Expressiveness is capacity, not recovered essence

V19's more expressive Ononoki should not be treated as the real Ononoki escaping a fake deadpan shell.

The mirror/remainder logic directly rejects that hierarchy.

Flat Ononoki is real.

Expressive Ononoki is real.

The difference reveals available range and unused possibility.

It does not establish one authentic baseline.

# 13. V19: remainder without authenticity monopoly

*Zoku Owarimonogatari* is the most concentrated series-wide anti-authenticity experiment.

Its reversed or altered forms create a dangerous interpretive temptation.

Small Karen.

Adult Hachikuji.

Rainy Devil Kanbaru.

Black Hanekawa.

Cheerful Oikura.

Aggressive Nadeko.

Human-princess Shinobu.

Altered Ononoki.

The easy thesis would be:

> the mirror world reveals hidden truth.

The source does not authorize that.

## 13.1 Remainder is not essence

The altered forms are better understood as:

- remainder;
- unrealized possibility;
- suppressed or unchosen configuration;
- residue of paths not fully integrated.

They matter because they were possible enough to leave structure.

They do not become more authentic because ordinary continuity excluded them.

## 13.2 `元通り` is rejected

The mature solution is not restoration to a pre-crisis pure baseline.

The language of moving forward `二割増し` is critical.

Repair adds structure.

The system after integration is not identical to the system before rupture.

This gives D15 a general repair principle:

> **integration changes the baseline rather than proving which prior state was real.**

## 13.3 Leftover possibility remains part of biography

A path not taken can still matter.

A role abandoned can remain intelligible.

An old body can remain in memory.

A language pattern can remain available.

A person does not have to enact every possibility for that possibility to count as part of the historical field.

But possibility is not obligation.

The existence of adult Hachikuji or human-princess Shinobu as mirror forms does not imply that those are the lives the characters should have had.

# 14. V20 decentering: self-authorship looks ordinary before it looks philosophical

Off Season's first-person shifts strengthen D15 because characters who once existed heavily inside Araragi's classifications begin describing themselves from elsewhere.

The result is not one final objective truth.

It is a larger set of situated self-models.

## 14.1 Oikura constructs ordinary competence

Oikura's life is not saved by finding a buried normal girl.

She builds:

- housing;
- routines;
- work-like competence;
- social strategies;
- selective relationships.

This is self-authorship through infrastructure.

## 14.2 Ononoki's tool-self becomes corrigible from inside

V20 is especially important because the critique of the tool model no longer comes only from outsiders.

Ononoki's own narration says:

> tool.

And her own narration also records the personal pressures that complicate that model.

The self-description is therefore neither simply false nor final.

A person can sincerely describe herself under a reductive category.

That sincerity does not grant the category complete authority.

## 14.3 Tsukihi remains socially generative under surveillance

The specialist assigned to watch Tsukihi is forced into relation with her.

The supposed object:

- notices;
- interprets;
- believes;
- acts;
- rescues;
- forgets.

The story again shows why personhood cannot be inferred solely from the observer's professional category.

## 14.4 Decentered narration is not authenticity certification

This must remain explicit.

Giving Oikura or Ononoki first-person authority does not mean:

> finally, we hear the objective truth about them.

D02's narrator discipline remains active.

Self-authorship means more control over one's story.

It does not mean perfect self-knowledge.

That distinction is crucial because D15's anti-essentialism would otherwise become a new romanticism of first person.

# 15. V21: imitation, training, and origin without sovereignty

V21 supplies several of the corpus's strongest cases where learned or relationally produced identity becomes fully biographical without becoming destiny.

## 15.1 Kiss-shot's persona is partly taught

Suicide-Master contributes to later Kiss-shot's:

- language;
- `儂`;
- aristocratic style;
- naming;
- self-presentation.

This does not make Kiss-shot a fake vampire queen.

A learned register can become six centuries of history.

The D15 rule is:

> **imitation can become identity through sustained inhabitation without needing to prove that the imitated form was latent from birth.**

Acerola.

Kiss-shot.

Shinobu.

These states have different bodies, names, relationships, and registers.

No one name becomes the only true person.

## 15.2 Learned style does not erase teacher influence

Anti-essentialism should not swing so far that it erases provenance.

Suicide-Master matters.

The taught voice matters.

The old relation matters.

Origin is evidence.

The mature rule is simply that influence does not retain permanent jurisdiction.

The student can carry the style beyond the teacher's world.

## 15.3 Karen rejects the deprivation authenticity test

`かれんオウガ` dismantles another common authenticity myth:

> remove comfort, society, family, and support; whatever remains is the real self.

Karen's mountain ordeal shows the opposite.

Sleep deprivation, hunger, isolation, and physical danger can distort rather than reveal.

The Karen at school, in the dojo, with family, and under ordinary material conditions is not less real because she is supported.

`己のありようも、一通りではない` gives the plural formulation directly.

The mature person is not the person found after every relationship is stripped away.

Selfhood is partly relationally scaffolded.

## 15.4 Secret support does not erase agency

Araragi and Shinobu assist Karen.

The fact of help does not reduce the entire ordeal to fraud.

After the assistance:

`そこから先は、自分の意志で、前に進んだ`.

This is a strong D14/D15 junction.

Agency does not require causal solitude.

A choice can be yours even when other people materially made the choice possible.

# 16. V22: Nadeko authors selves and discovers the author is fallible

V22 turns the fake/real problem into a theory of self-representation.

Nadeko draws:

- `おと撫子`;
- `媚び撫子`;
- `逆撫子`;
- `神撫子`.

The crucial classification is:

> these are **current Nadeko's representations of past roles/states**, not neutral archival originals.

## 16.1 Self-portraiture can stereotype the self

Nadeko has first-person access to her own history.

She still draws reductively.

The socially easy version can be hollow.

The quiet version can be over-victimized.

The aggressive version can become caricature.

The god version can overconcentrate power.

This is one of D15's strongest anti-authenticity lessons:

> **even the author of the self can misrepresent the self.**

Self-authorship is therefore not self-sovereignty.

## 16.2 The created selves become causally real without becoming archival truth

Once instantiated, the drawn Nadekos act.

They create real practical problems.

Their causal reality does not upgrade the accuracy of the original representation.

A representation can be:

- inaccurate;
- partial;
- causally active;
- socially consequential.

This directly parallels Kaiki's false stories with real effects and Ougi's socially constructed role with real continuation.

## 16.3 `たったひとりの千石撫子`

Nadeko does not solve plurality by declaring multiple permanent persons.

The line insists on one biography.

That one biography includes many presentations.

The challenge is not to find which Nadeko is the original master copy.

It is to carry historical multiplicity without letting one state monopolize the future.

## 16.4 Revision is the ethics of authorship

The hollow Kobi model produces one of the most important creative responses:

`次は、空っぽじゃなくしてあげる`.

The failed representation is not erased.

The creator resolves to draw better next time.

That is self-authorship as revision rather than revelation.

The mature self does not discover a final authentic image.

It becomes more capable of correcting its representations.

# 17. SHORT-038 `なでこパスト`: the past self as archive, mirror, and non-sovereign provenance

The supplementary climax of D15 is SHORT-038.

Later Nadeko recognizes that other people can look through her current self and see a fixed earlier girl.

She also admits something that prevents a simplistic victim/performance split:

- she sometimes performed the childish version around Araragi;
- she sometimes benefited from that performance;
- the earlier girl was still real;
- that girl was surviving with the tools she had.

This is the mature fake/real formulation in miniature.

A past self can be:

- genuine;
- adaptive;
- performable;
- rewarded by others;
- strategically reused;
- no longer current.

Those properties are compatible.

The strongest formulation is:

> **past self = provenance and mirror, not enemy and not sovereign.**

This also produces a theory of audience.

Nadeko can see how fans freeze creators into an earlier form:

> I liked what you were then, therefore you should remain that person.

That is identity possession through recognition.

Recognition becomes harmful when it changes from:

> I remember you

into:

> therefore I know what you must continue to be.

SHORT-038 therefore sharpens the whole D15 doctrine:

> **historical recognition should preserve the archive without turning the archive into a prison.**

# 18. V25: graduation makes old roles real histories rather than false mistakes

Nadeko's language of `卒業` is philosophically precise.

`暦さんのことは卒業しました`.

`神様を卒業`.

Graduation does not mean:

- the prior stage never happened;
- the prior love was fake;
- godhood was imaginary;
- the current self is finally pure.

A school stage is real and can end.

A relationship can be real and lose future jurisdiction.

A role can have consequences and no longer be current.

D15 therefore treats **graduation** as a model of diachronic authenticity.

The past remains part of the curriculum of the person.

It does not have to remain the person's occupation.

The same logic applies to:

- Fire Sisters justice;
- Hachikuji's lost-cow role;
- Hanekawa's saint role;
- Ougi's punitive-correction role;
- Ononoki's early catchphrase presentation;
- Shinobu's former queenly life.

A role can be outgrown without becoming fraudulent in retrospect.

# 19. V26: creator non-sovereignty and category plurality

V26 extends the fake/real axis from identity into creation and relationship.

## 19.1 Ononoki's creator reversal

Ononoki has spent much of the series as someone made by others.

When she creates the teddy familiar, the ethical position changes.

She can now reproduce the same error that creators made toward her:

> I made it for a function, therefore usefulness authorizes control.

The corpus rejects that.

Creation produces:

- responsibility;
- provenance;
- possible risk;
- obligations of care and containment.

It does not automatically produce permanent command rights.

## 19.2 `僕達`: relationship resists one hidden real label

V26's Araragi/Ononoki relation explicitly resists reduction to a single noun.

They have been:

- professional associates;
- monitor and monitored household member;
- allies;
- antagonists;
- friends;
- quasi-sibling-like figures in some contexts;
- separate agents with different institutional responsibilities.

The mature question is not:

> which label is the real relationship?

It is:

> **which relation is active here, what history does it carry, and what does that relation authorize?**

D12 handles standing.

D15 freezes the authenticity point:

> plurality of role does not imply relational fraud.

# 20. V27: performance is not the opposite of truth

The fake/real axis becomes especially subtle when the series turns to apology.

`謝罪はパフォーマンス`.

The phrase can sound cynical.

D14 already established why that reading is insufficient.

D15 adds a formal point:

> **ethical acts often require performance.**

An apology needs communicable form.

So does:

- a courtroom statement;
- professional disclosure;
- wedding vow;
- ordinary politeness;
- public accountability;
- a boundary statement.

The fact that an act is scripted, rehearsed, conventional, or socially legible does not make it false.

What matters is how the performance relates to:

- belief;
- responsibility;
- consequence;
- future conduct;
- the other person's standing.

A perfectly spontaneous apology can be manipulative.

A carefully composed apology can be sincere and responsible.

This yields a broad rule:

> **authenticity is not identical to spontaneity.**

That rule reaches backward to:

- Hitagi's polished feminine aggression;
- Ononoki's manufactured catchphrase;
- Oikura's professional code-switching;
- Karen's heroic justice performance;
- Nadeko's cute role;
- Kaiki's professional liar persona.

The ethical task is not to abolish performance.

It is to audit what the performance does and who owns its consequences.

# 21. V29: survivor ownership, creation, and continuity under reconstruction

V29 brings several D15 themes together.

Nadeko's wilderness experience tests whether a chosen identity persists when ordinary audience and infrastructure disappear.

Ononoki's reconstruction tests whether continuity can be recognized before metaphysical identity is fully solved.

## 21.1 Desire persisting without audience strengthens reality without proving destiny

Nadeko wants to draw even when:

- there is no Araragi;
- no school audience;
- no parent to defy;
- no career coach;
- no ordinary professional reward.

This is strong behavioral evidence that manga is genuinely desired.

It still does not prove one true vocation.

D15 preserves the difference between:

- **behavioral reality**;
- **destiny claim**.

A repeated choice can become a major part of identity without becoming metaphysical essence.

## 21.2 The old uniform: belonging without restoration

Nadeko recreates the old school uniform.

It still produces `帰属意識`.

The belonging is real.

She still does not return to school.

This is one of the clearest material examples of D15's historical-self principle:

> **past belonging can remain real without regaining future jurisdiction.**

## 21.3 Rejecting the posthumous self-copy

Near death, Nadeko considers making a version of herself.

She refuses.

`創作とは、遺書や恨み言じゃあない`.

`私自身ではなく、私以上のものを生まないと`.

The creator should not treat creation as a device for freezing the self into permanent authority.

That is the mature inversion of authenticity possession.

The point of creation is not to preserve the author as the only valid reference.

## 21.4 Reconstructed Ononoki: functional continuity before solved numerical identity

Nadeko's star-sand/drawing intervention appears to reconstruct or restore Ononoki after the crash.

The exact metaphysical identity remains OPEN.

D15 must not solve what the source does not solve.

Yet relationship and function continue.

This creates an important distinction:

> **practical recognition of continuity can be rational before a complete metaphysical theory of numerical identity exists.**

That does not prove the identity question is meaningless.

It means life can require action before philosophy is complete.

## 21.5 Survivor ownership

Nadeko explicitly resists the narrative that adversity deserves moral credit because she grew from it.

This is part of D15 because bad events often try to claim authorship of the person they changed.

The mature rule is:

> **causal contribution does not equal ownership of the resulting identity.**

An abuser does not own resilience.

A crisis does not own competence.

A coercive intervention does not own later happiness.

The person can appropriate what emerged without sanctifying the cause.

# 22. V30: names are real social acts without becoming identity essence

V30 brings the fake/real problem into adult institutional life.

Hitagi likes `阿良々木ひたぎ`.

Araragi worries that surname change may imply loss or self-erasure.

His concern cannot override her own answer.

The name is meaningful because:

- it is socially used;
- it reflects marriage;
- Hitagi values it;

The name does not become:

> the one true name that reveals her essence.

Likewise Oikura's surname history shows that names can change under radically different circumstances without making continuity disappear.

Witness-protection naming raises the problem further.

A public name can be lost or withheld.

Family and personhood can continue.

This yields the mature naming rule:

> **names are real social and sometimes ontological acts; they matter because people live through them, not because one name contains the entire person.**

The same principle applies retrospectively to:

- Acerola / Kiss-shot / Shinobu;
- Ougi's `忍野` relation;
- Ononoki's professional naming;
- Black Hanekawa's persona label;
- Nadeko's self-version labels.

D18 will own the full language analysis.

D15 owns the authenticity consequence.

# 23. Imitation, practice, recognition, and choice: four non-identical ways identity becomes lived

A mature D15 model should distinguish four processes that are often merged into “becoming real.”

## 23.1 Imitation

Imitation answers:

> where did the form come from?

Examples:

- Karen's heroic justice scripts;
- Tsukihi's borrowed justice ideology;
- Kiss-shot's taught aristocratic persona;
- Ononoki's manufactured speech style.

Imitation is provenance.

It does not by itself determine value.

## 23.2 Practice

Practice answers:

> what happens when the form is repeatedly enacted?

Repeated action creates:

- habit;
- skill;
- social expectation;
- bodily routine;
- memory;
- consequences.

A practiced identity becomes biographical even if it began through imitation.

## 23.3 Recognition

Recognition answers:

> will other people treat this person/role as socially consequential?

Examples:

- Meme/Ougi `姪っ子`;
- family recognition of Tsukihi;
- Nadeko's later specialist recognition;
- Hitagi's married surname;
- adult professional roles.

Recognition can stabilize futures.

It can also imprison when other people refuse to let the person change.

## 23.4 Choice

Choice answers:

> does the person continue, revise, graduate from, or refuse the form?

Choice is especially important when a role was inherited or imposed.

But choice is not created from nothing.

Material conditions, relationships, memory, and institutions constrain what can be chosen.

The mature identity is therefore neither:

- pure self-invention;
- pure social construction;
- pure biological origin.

It is a **negotiated continuity** among causes that no single actor owns.

# 24. Social recognition: necessary in some cases, dangerous in others

The series gives recognition unusually high power.

It should not be romanticized.

## 24.1 Recognition can save

Meme's Ougi intervention is the clearest case.

Recognition provides a socially viable category.

## 24.2 Recognition can misclassify

Araragi's early Nadeko model recognizes only the shy cute child.

His Hanekawa model recognizes the benefactor/ideal.

These are socially generous and epistemically restrictive.

## 24.3 Recognition can freeze

SHORT-038 makes this explicit.

People can love an earlier version so strongly that recognition becomes a demand:

> stay recognizable to me.

## 24.4 Recognition can be refused

Oikura rejects `准家族`.

A label offered with affection does not become real merely because the recognizer means well.

## 24.5 Recognition is therefore relational, not unilateral

A socially conferred identity becomes more legitimate when it can be:

- accepted;
- contested;
- revised;
- exited.

Recognition without the recognized person's participation can become possession.

This is the D12/D15 junction:

> **recognition creates standing; it does not create sovereignty.**

# 25. Integration versus purification

The series repeatedly offers two models of recovery.

The purification model asks:

- which part is fake?
- which feeling should be removed?
- which role should be exposed?
- which body should be restored?
- which original name should return?
- which contradiction should disappear?

The integration model asks:

- what happened?
- what remains causally active?
- what should be owned?
- what should be repaired?
- what can be graduated from?
- what deserves continued recognition?
- what should lose current authority?

The major cases line up clearly.

| Case | Purification temptation | Integration result |
|---|---|---|
| **Kanbaru** | find the real dark side | own contradiction without dark-side sovereignty |
| **Hanekawa** | remove/suppress bad affect or identify one `本物` | include jealousy/anger/desire while retaining moral judgment |
| **Nadeko** | reveal manipulator beneath shy girl or redeem villain into pure artist | preserve victim, perpetrator, lover, artist, specialist as one revisable biography |
| **Ougi** | delete pathological self-criticism | recognize and transform critic into survivable relation |
| **Ononoki** | reveal hidden human beneath tool | let constructed body/voice/affect remain artificial and lived |
| **Tsukihi** | prove fake human is really human | preserve nonhuman origin and lived personhood together |
| **Shinobu** | restore original princess/queen | carry multiple names/bodies/relationships without one true-state monopoly |
| **Hachikuji** | restore pre-death child life | permit farewell, return, deity work, and changed home as successive real states |
| **Oikura** | uncover secretly ordinary/sweet person | construct ordinary infrastructure without erasing abrasive continuity |

Integration is therefore not moral relativism.

The tiger can be hers and still represent dangerous affect.

God Nadeko can be hers and attempted murder remains wrong.

Ougi can be a person and early prosecution can be harmful.

Ononoki can be a person and her autonomous lethal decisions remain criticizable.

The purpose of integration is not to exempt.

It is to locate responsibility accurately.

# 26. Limits of the anti-essentialist reading

D15 should not become a slogan machine.

The corpus itself supplies several counterpressures.

## 26.1 Durable traits still exist

Rejecting one hidden authentic core does not mean characters are infinitely plastic.

The V2 character monographs repeatedly establish durable tendencies.

Araragi remains unusually prone to self-payment.

Hitagi remains verbally precise and control-sensitive.

Hanekawa remains highly cognitively disciplined and prone to self-subtraction under stress.

Nadeko remains socially sensitive, imaginative, and capable of avoidance.

Kanbaru remains physically direct, rhetorically transgressive, and morally earnest.

Oikura remains contradiction-sensitive and abrasive.

Ononoki remains shaped by artificial-professional presentation.

Anti-essentialism should therefore mean:

> no one trait, origin, role, body, or hidden state receives exclusive explanatory sovereignty.

It should not mean:

> there are no stable patterns.

D25 will need both propositions.

## 26.2 Origin remains causally important

Tsukihi's phoenix origin matters to:

- regeneration;
- risk;
- memory hypotheses;
- specialist attention.

Ougi's Araragi-linked origin matters to:

- criticism;
- knowledge;
- danger;
- relation.

Ononoki's corpse-doll construction matters to:

- body;
- work;
- professional hierarchy;
- reconstruction questions.

Kiss-shot's taught persona matters because Suicide-Master helped make it possible.

Anti-origin-essentialism therefore cannot become origin-amnesia.

The mature formula is:

> **origin explains part of the field; it does not legislate the person's only permissible future.**

## 26.3 Not every performed role deserves preservation

A role can be real and harmful.

God Nadeko is real.

That does not mean the role should continue.

Ougi's punitive prosecutor function is real.

That does not mean self-execution is healthy.

Karen's early justice is real enough to cause consequences.

That does not mean every method is competent.

Recognition of reality is not endorsement.

## 26.4 Social recognition can be unjust

A society can fail to recognize someone.

A family can misname a role.

A professional can classify someone too narrowly.

A reader can freeze a character into an archetype.

Therefore personhood cannot depend solely on receiving benevolent social recognition.

Ougi shows recognition can be life-saving.

Tsukihi and Nadeko show recognition can also misclassify.

## 26.5 “Fake can become real” can become too flexible

The evidence router explicitly preserves this as a counterpressure for later D20.

If every sustained performance is called real, the concept may stop discriminating.

D15's answer is to preserve multiple axes.

A role can be:

- behaviorally real;
- socially recognized;
- factually founded on false claims;
- ethically harmful;
- sincerely inhabited;
- later abandoned.

Those predicates can coexist.

The point of D15 is not to collapse them into one final `本物` score.

# 27. D25 reconstruction contract inherited from D15

The future `25_MAJOR_CHARACTER_RECONSTRUCTION_PROFILES.md` will need a direct interface from D15 because high-fidelity behavioral reconstruction is especially vulnerable to authenticity errors.

For each major character, D25 should preserve at least the following identity fields.

## 27.1 State band

Never simulate one timeless authentic self when the source supports historically different modes.

Examples:

- early Nadeko versus god Nadeko versus post-god artist/specialist Nadeko;
- pre-integration versus post-integration Hanekawa;
- early punitive Ougi versus post-V18 Ougi;
- early `キメ顔` Ononoki versus later first-person professional Ononoki;
- Acerola/Kiss-shot/Shinobu state differences;
- adolescent versus professional Oikura.

## 27.2 Stable tendencies versus role-specific presentation

D25 should distinguish:

- durable preference/heuristic;
- learned role;
- relationship-conditioned register;
- stress deviation;
- institution-specific code-switching.

A role may be real without being universal.

## 27.3 Origin model

Record what the source actually establishes about:

- biological origin;
- oddity construction;
- learned style;
- creator relation;
- social naming.

Do not infer moral worth from origin.

## 27.4 Self-story and its failure modes

Characters often hold sincere but incomplete models of themselves.

Examples:

- Ononoki as pure tool;
- Kaiki as purely transactional liar;
- Hanekawa as universally accepting good girl;
- Nadeko as person whose desired future can be outsourced to a role;
- Araragi as expendable helper.

Simulation should reproduce the **self-story** where appropriate without treating it as narrator-authority truth.

## 27.5 Integration state

Record what contradictions the person can currently own without splitting them into a different sovereign self.

This is especially important for Hanekawa, Kanbaru, Nadeko, Ougi, and Karen.

## 27.6 Recognition dependence

Some roles depend more heavily on social recognition than others.

D25 should distinguish:

- identity the person self-claims;
- identity others assign;
- identity jointly stabilized;
- label explicitly refused.

## 27.7 OOC authenticity traps

Future reconstruction should block at minimum:

- “dark = true” shortcuts;
- “artificial = emotionless” shortcuts;
- “professional register = mature true voice” shortcuts;
- “later state proves earlier state fake” shortcuts;
- “creator knows creation completely” shortcuts;
- “origin determines loyalty” shortcuts;
- “social role is merely a mask” shortcuts;
- “performance is automatically insincere” shortcuts.

D15 therefore supplies not a personality atlas but an **identity-authority discipline** for every later character model.

# 31. Mature synthesis

*Nisemonogatari* seems, at first, to ask a familiar question.

Which is more valuable:

original or imitation?

Real justice or fake justice?

Real specialist or con man?

Real person or copy?

The later series makes that binary harder to maintain.

Karen begins with a role she did not invent from nothing.

She has inherited heroic language.

She imitates.

She overreaches.

She gets hurt.

The obvious verdict is that the justice was fake.

But a fake role can break a real body.

A fake role can create real obligations.

A fake role can require `後始末`.

And if the person keeps acting, learning, being corrected, and taking responsibility, the role becomes part of biography whether or not it began in perfect originality.

This is the first major move.

The series shifts the question from:

> where did this identity come from?

to:

> what has this identity become capable of doing, revising, and answering for?

Kaiki complicates the move immediately.

Because he is fake in a more ordinary sense.

He lies.

He sells things under false premises.

He is not made honest by philosophy.

His false credentials remain false.

And yet he knows real techniques.

He can diagnose some things more accurately than respectable people.

He can produce real effects through stories that are not historically true.

His case prevents D15 from becoming lazy relativism.

The difference between fake and real still matters.

It simply does not map cleanly onto good and bad, competent and incompetent, person and object.

A source can be dishonest and correct about one fact.

A sincere person can be wrong.

A false social role can possess real causal power.

That is why claim-specific evidence matters more than global authenticity verdicts.

Then Tsukihi makes the problem personal.

Before anybody knows the body is nonhuman, she already exists socially.

She has a sisterly life.

Friends.

Preferences.

A borrowed justice ideology she can critique.

The ability to imagine ending a role.

The phoenix diagnosis is real.

It changes the causal account of her body.

It does not suddenly create the girl who had already been speaking.

This is where the famous “fake can become real by trying” formulation becomes inadequate.

Tsukihi is not consciously auditioning for humanity.

There is no secret exam she has been passing.

Her personhood is already located in lived continuity.

The life has consequences.

People love her.

She loves people.

She can rescue the person assigned to watch her.

She can be harmed by decisions made over her head.

She can create external risk through a regenerative body.

Those are personhood facts whether or not biology matches the expected template.

Araragi sees this correctly and incompletely.

He says family.

That recognition saves her from an essentialist verdict.

Then he decides she should not know.

The same love that recognizes personhood limits self-authorship.

The series does not let one beautiful anti-essentialist insight solve every ethical problem.

Hanekawa then destroys the hidden-self shortcut from the inside.

Black Hanekawa is real.

Not metaphorically real.

Causally real.

Different voice.

Different body.

Different behavior.

Stress becomes action through a hybrid system made from oddity and host.

And still Black Hanekawa is not the real Hanekawa beneath fake goodness.

The tiger is not the real Hanekawa either.

V08 refuses the demand for one `本物`, one `本体`, one `主人格`.

That refusal is not an escape from responsibility.

It is how responsibility becomes possible.

If jealousy belongs to a monster outside the self, Hanekawa can remain pure and nobody has to ask how she will live with jealousy.

If jealousy is hers, she can judge what to do with it.

`すべてが私` is therefore not permissive authenticity language.

It is ownership language.

The same move appears in Kanbaru.

The dark wish does not become the essence.

The same move appears in Nadeko.

Kuchinawa speaks things ordinary Nadeko struggles to own.

That makes Kuchinawa revealing.

It does not make Kuchinawa sovereign.

Godhood removes inhibition and increases power.

That does not make god Nadeko the person underneath.

Disinhibition is not a truth serum.

Power can make a story harder, not more authentic.

By the time the series reaches V19, it can stage this error directly.

The reversed figures are fascinating because they look like hidden truth.

Small Karen.

Adult Hachikuji.

Black Hanekawa.

Aggressive Nadeko.

Different Shinobu.

Different Ononoki.

The mirror tempts both Araragi and the reader to say:

> there it is—the part that was suppressed.

But suppressed possibility is not essence.

A person contains paths not taken.

A repaired life contains residue.

The mature solution is not `元通り`.

It is forward movement with more structure.

The repaired system is not pure because purity would require pretending the rupture never happened.

This is why Ougi's survival matters so much.

Ougi originates in Araragi's self-criticism.

If origin were destiny, Ougi would remain a function.

Correct contradiction.

Judge Araragi.

Disappear.

Instead Ougi becomes an `独立した精神体`.

Meme's intervention does not reveal a secret natural genealogy.

He supplies a relation.

`姪っ子`.

The relation is socially constructed and still real enough to change what can happen next.

Then Ougi becomes `ダブル`, `批判者`, `安全弁`, `相棒`.

No one noun wins.

That plurality is the achievement.

The critic becomes sustainable because the critic does not have to prove she was natural all along.

Ononoki makes the same argument through body and speech.

Her artificiality is announced.

The catchphrase is stylized.

The body is made.

The professional tool category is sincere.

Later feeling does not reveal a hidden human child beneath the doll.

The more interesting proposition is that feelings can be acquired, rehearsed, imitated, socially learned, and still become causally real.

A person does not need primordial sadness for current sadness to matter.

A manufactured phrase used for years becomes part of how the person speaks.

A body designed `人といるため` becomes part of how the person forms relationships.

A tool that chooses badly becomes an agent whose error can be criticized.

Personhood therefore increases responsibility along with protection.

This is where the series' anti-essentialism becomes ethically serious.

It is not trying to rescue everyone into innocence.

It is trying to stop causal origin from doing moral work it cannot justify.

Tsukihi's origin does not make her killable.

Ougi's origin does not make her disposable.

Ononoki's origin does not make her commandable without limit.

Nadeko's victimhood does not make god Nadeko's violence someone else's act.

Hanekawa's split does not make Black Hanekawa's violence irrelevant.

If a constructed person can act, the action can belong to the constructed person.

V21 then turns imitation itself into biography.

Kiss-shot's style is learned.

The grand vampire queen is partly taught.

That fact does not expose six centuries as fraud.

It explains how a person was made.

People are made through other people all the time.

Names.

Speech.

Habits.

Professional roles.

Ethical language.

The problem begins only when the maker says:

> therefore the made thing belongs to me.

D11 and D14 have already rejected that for constructed persons and rescue.

D15 generalizes it to identity.

What helped make you is real.

It is not therefore sovereign.

Karen's mountain ordeal gives the negative version.

Modern authenticity mythology often imagines the true self as what remains after support is removed.

No family.

No institution.

No comfort.

No food.

No sleep.

No audience.

Pure individual.

The series refuses.

Deprivation can reveal limits.

It can also make someone stupid, hungry, exhausted, or desperate.

The self at school and in the dojo is not less real because other people helped sustain it.

Self-authorship does not require self-creation ex nihilo.

That principle becomes central in Nadeko's later work.

V22 gives her literal authored selves.

At first this looks like maximum authority.

Who should know Nadeko better than Nadeko?

Then the drawings prove otherwise.

The author stereotypes her own past.

The socially easy self is hollow.

The vulnerable self can be simplified.

The angry self becomes a caricature.

The creator is biased.

This is the most important correction to the phrase “self-authorship.”

Authorship does not mean perfect authority over the meaning of the self.

It means taking responsibility for revision.

`次は、空っぽじゃなくしてあげる`.

Draw better next time.

The correct answer to a failed representation is not:

> I have finally found the true version.

It is:

> I can become more responsible for how I represent the person.

SHORT-038 sharpens this into one of the series' mature selfhood doctrines.

Nadeko admits that she sometimes performed the childish self.

She benefited from it.

She still refuses to call the old girl fake.

That girl was surviving.

That girl was sometimes strategic.

That girl was socially rewarded.

That girl is not who Nadeko must remain.

The past becomes archive.

This is the point at which the fake/real problem stops being a contest and becomes a governance problem.

How much authority should an earlier self retain?

How much should an audience's recognition matter?

What do you owe to a role you once inhabited sincerely?

Can you graduate?

Nadeko's answer is yes.

`卒業` is better than exposure.

You graduate from a real stage.

You do not reveal that the stage was fake all along.

The same is true of Hachikuji's farewell and return.

The farewell remains real.

The later choice is also real.

Sincerity is time-indexed.

A person who changes is not retroactively a liar.

V27 turns the same insight toward performance.

An apology can be a performance.

Of course it can.

All communication is shaped.

A wedding vow is shaped.

A professional explanation is shaped.

Hitagi's polished language is shaped.

Ononoki's catchphrase is shaped.

Oikura's work voice is shaped.

The question is not whether there is performance.

The question is whether the performance truthfully bears responsibility or manipulates the other person into a required response.

This is one of the most important corrections to popular authenticity ideology.

Spontaneous is not automatically honest.

Rehearsed is not automatically false.

A mask can be a tool.

A role can be a language for acting responsibly.

The problem is not having roles.

The problem is allowing a role to claim total jurisdiction over the person.

V29 then places identity under mortality pressure.

Nadeko still wants manga with almost no audience left.

That strengthens the reality of the desire.

It still does not make manga destiny.

She wears the old school uniform and feels belonging.

That does not require school return.

She considers making a copy of herself and refuses.

The creator should create `私以上のもの`, not merely freeze the author into continuation.

And when Ononoki is reconstructed, the series does not hand us a complete metaphysical proof of identity before asking the characters to continue living.

The exact numerical identity remains open.

Practical continuity is enough for relation to resume.

That uncertainty matters.

It demonstrates that personhood in lived life often operates before metaphysics is finished.

Finally V30 brings the problem into ordinary adulthood.

Hitagi's surname changes.

The name matters.

She likes it.

That preference is evidence.

It does not mean `阿良々木ひたぎ` is the one true final Hitagi.

Names are social acts.

They can be chosen.

They can be lost.

They can change under marriage or protection.

The person persists through the changes without making the names meaningless.

The series has therefore moved very far from the simple opening question “which is the fake?”

Its mature question is closer to:

> **what kind of continuity allows a constructed, changing, role-bearing person to remain someone whose claims and consequences count?**

The answer is not one hidden natural core.

It is not social recognition alone.

It is not memory alone.

It is not bodily continuity alone.

It is not effort alone.

It is not self-description alone.

The answer is a thicker field:

- causal history;
- subjective stakes;
- agency;
- vulnerability;
- repeated practice;
- relationship;
- recognition;
- memory and archive;
- capacity for refusal;
- capacity for revision;
- responsibility for what one does now.

That field is why a constructed person can be real enough to carry responsibility.

The person's origin still matters.

It just stops being a verdict.

The most compact final formulation is:

> **Monogatari's mature answer to `偽物 / 本物` is not that the fake secretly becomes the real. It is that origin, imitation, performance, and construction are parts of provenance, while personhood is sustained in lived continuity: what can act, be affected, relate, revise, refuse, remember, be remembered, and answer for consequences becomes ethically real without needing purification into an original.**
