# 〈物語〉シリーズ V2 — Comparative Reference Matrices, Counterreadings, and Open Questions

## Reusable analytical interface

## 0. Function, authority, and the no-new-claim rule

This document is the compact comparative interface for the mature V2 specialist corpus.

Its job is not to discover a twentieth theory of Monogatari.

Its job is to make Documents 01–19 usable together.

The governing architecture is explicit:

> **No new load-bearing claim should appear here without a primary home in Documents 01–19.**

D20 therefore operates under a strict routing discipline.

Every compact statement below is one of four things:

1. a direct compression of an already canonical specialist conclusion;
2. a cross-document comparison whose components already have canonical primary homes;
3. a counterreading whose affirmative and adverse cases are already present in the specialist corpus;
4. an OPEN question already preserved by one or more primary documents.

If a matrix exposes a genuinely new interpretation, this document is **not** the place to promote it. The appropriate upstream specialist home must be revised first.

The purpose of D20 is practical.

A future reader should be able to answer questions such as:

- How do Araragi and Meme differ as helpers?
- Why are Hitagi and Hanekawa both “controlled” speakers without being behaviorally interchangeable?
- Which oddity cases are heavily social and which are relatively autonomous?
- What forms of relationship generate standing without ownership?
- Which characters become more autonomous by reducing another person's centrality?
- Where does the series genuinely critique harem logic, and where does it continue to participate in it?
- Does later institutionalization solve the rescue ethic, or merely move its risks?
- Which claims are stable enough for a full-series synthesis?
- Which questions must remain open at the supplied V30 boundary?

The compact answer to what D20 is for is:

> **comparison without flattening.**

Monogatari repeatedly punishes analytical systems that become too total.

D20 therefore compares across dimensions while preserving:

- publication state;
- narrator state;
- relationship state;
- source class;
- claim authority;
- unresolved contradiction.

# 1. How to read the matrices

The tables below are retrieval tools rather than substitute monographs.

Each row should be read with three cautions.

## 1.1 “Mature form” does not mean final essence

A mature form is the strongest current formulation available by the supplied boundary.

It is not:

- a permanent personality;
- a post-V30 prediction;
- a claim that earlier states were false;
- a claim that later development cannot regress.

D19's historical-state discipline remains active.

## 1.2 “Blind spot” is not a moral essence

A recurring failure mode can be:

- defensive;
- relational;
- professional;
- situational.

The matrix does not convert it into a character's one defining flaw.

## 1.3 Comparison is dimensional, not scalar

No table below creates one ladder from:

- immature → mature;
- selfish → selfless;
- false → authentic;
- monster → human;
- private → institutional.

The V2 corpus repeatedly rejects those one-axis models.

The matrix yields several already-canonical comparisons.

First, **social usefulness often begins as a trap**.

The competence that helps others can make vulnerability harder to see:

- Hanekawa's knowledge;
- Hitagi's control;
- Araragi's rescue;
- Kanbaru's athletic usefulness;
- Ononoki's professional durability;
- Ougi's criticism.

Second, later maturity generally does not destroy the useful capacity.

It changes its jurisdiction.

Hanekawa remains intelligent.

Hitagi remains sharp.

Araragi remains intervention-prone.

Shinobu remains dangerous.

Nadeko remains representationally powerful.

Ougi remains critical.

The specialist corpus therefore rejects the model:

> development = removal of defining trait.

A more stable cross-document formulation is:

> **development changes what a trait is allowed to govern.**

That formulation is a compression of D04–D18, not a new primary claim.

The intervention matrix prevents two common reductions.

The first is:

> Meme = correct ethic; everyone else = deviation.

D10 rejects that.

Meme sees important things about:

- agency;
- balance;
- limits.

He also withholds, leaves, and can fail through distance.

The second is:

> later institution = superior version of all earlier help.

D17 rejects that.

Institutions can:

- preserve memory;
- distribute workload;
- permit oversight;

and also:

- surveil;
- erase;
- normalize bad precedent;
- scale error.

The most stable shared principle remains D10/D14's:

> **knowledge, capacity, and good outcome do not self-authenticate jurisdiction.**

# 24. Productive contradictions the full synthesis must not smooth away

A continuous literary argument will be tempted to make Monogatari more coherent than the corpus is.

D20 therefore freezes several productive tensions.

## 24.1 Self-saving versus indispensable help

Meme's aphorism should not become causal isolation.

Many people survive because someone else acts.

Agency and dependence coexist.

## 24.2 Non-possession versus obligation

Relationships do not own people.

They still create real duties and standing.

## 24.3 Anti-essentialism versus literal embodiment

Bodies do not define identity.

Bodies still impose hunger, pain, risk and power.

## 24.4 Institutionalization versus freedom

Institutions can reduce heroic monopoly.

They can also scale coercion.

## 24.5 Comedy versus harm

A scene can be funny and boundary-crossing.

The joke's function does not erase the ethical direction of the act.

## 24.6 Retrospective revision versus historical uncertainty

Later evidence can change the current best model.

Earlier readers and characters still lacked that later evidence.

## 24.7 Family inclusion versus refusal

Family can be meaningful.

A person may still refuse a family label.

## 24.8 Creation versus control

Art, naming, rescue, mentorship and supernatural creation may create causal responsibility.

They do not create sovereignty.

## 24.9 Recovery versus residue

A body or crisis can improve.

History remains.

## 24.10 Decentering versus erasure

Araragi becomes less central to several lives.

His influence does not become false.

These tensions are not problems to solve before the full synthesis.

They are part of what the synthesis must explain.

have material consequences.
