## A funeral performed by the surviving friend

The recollection explicitly places the book burning on the day the girls swallowed the decisive tablets. It is not an unsituated emblem inserted by the analyst. Miaha has gathered books by the river and says she will burn them all. Tuan cannot verify the inventory because she has not visited Miaha’s home; she thinks Miaha does not look as though she is lying. The scope of the destruction therefore remains a claim about all her novels, with an actual assembled pile and an actual later fire. E001.

Tuan asks the obvious question partly because she thinks it is the role Miaha expects her to play. Her participation is already shaped by anticipation of the other girl’s performance. She enjoys agreeing, feeling as if Miaha writes something into her body. That is a stated metaphor for receptivity, not proof of childhood molecular programming. Its later relation to Miaha’s claimed code-writing is nevertheless striking: the unit places a felt inscription and a technical inscription in one sequence while giving them different causal levels. E001,E007.

The books are not simply rejected as worthless. Miaha calls them sources of strength and fears they could bind her to life, preventing departure to the beyond or nothingness. She wants to take with her what empowered her. Her reported difficulty is practical as well as symbolic: waiting until she is weaker could make transporting them impossible. Destroying an attachment is presented as part of preparing to leave, without proving that the books possess a literal power to prevent death. E001.

Miaha prepares the fire but gives the igniter to Tuan, places her cold hand over Tuan’s and says lighting it herself would be too painful. Tuan actually ignites the pile. The transferred act differentiates two kinds of participation: Miaha designs and requests a ritual, while Tuan performs the decisive movement. Her calm stance beside the flames does not erase the difficulty she has just confessed. It would be a mistake to treat this scene as proof that books never mattered to her or that her visible composure makes her emotionally empty. E003.

## The body that might become a book

Miaha’s explanation expands the book from an object into an image of a fully described world. Her account of CT distinguishes direct photography from computer-mediated reconstruction through equations; WatchMe becomes the supposed limit of bodily description. The source retains WatchMe and the variant WacthMe in this passage. The variation is not silently repaired or made into a second technology. Her medical account remains her explanation inside the fiction, not an imported scientific proof of the relation between description and identity. E002.

Her objection concerns becoming the description itself. She wants to die before the device is placed in her body, while still a girl, to assert that her body is not a book. This is a claim about bodily ownership and the cost of making life legible; it does not demonstrate that a medical description literally replaces every property of its referent. The sensuous hand, unpleasant oil and light of the burning pile keep a material body present while she theorizes its conversion into writing. E001–E003.

Writing also promises duration. Miaha cites the Bible, pyramids and an alleged practice of burning historical scrolls when Chinese rulers changed; these are her analogies and historical claims, not independently authenticated histories. She treats the era’s protection from disease as an illusion of bodily eternity, with aging a remaining trace of nature. Calling the Maelstrom a possible return to natural conditions is likewise her provocative evaluation. The analysis need not endorse catastrophe as rehabilitation or reduce the actual care previously shown to a false benefit. E002.

The fire is then named a cremation and Miaha’s funeral. Her account of former funerary possessions and current protein-based corpse disposal explains why the books cannot enter a coffin with her. They sit by the river until the burning ends. What is enacted is a funeral for a living person’s attachments, beside a city she calls the castle of presumed eternity. When Tuan asks whether their three deaths will change the world, Miaha answers that everything will change for them. That answer turns from public effect to the participants’ own boundary; it does not establish that the earlier pact had already become a workable strategy of global reform. E003.

The present ending will return Tuan’s hands to Miaha’s death. The parallel is strong, but its difference matters: a request to light books is not advance consent to the later shooting. The first ritual recruits a friend to an act Miaha finds painful; the later revenge denies Miaha a future she wants. Formal recurrence cannot make the two acts ethically interchangeable.

## Hatred becomes an inadequate present explanation

Tuan asks whether their society was worse than this bunker. Miaha does not provide a simple ranking. She says she did not know what to do, then recalls the death of a neighboring boy when she was twelve. Twelve is Miaha’s age; the boy’s exact age is not supplied. His reported statements of having no place and hating the world enter her account of learning that suppressing the natural within oneself could also destroy people. They do not supply a complete independent explanation of his suicide. E006.

Miaha describes her former conclusion as simple: the life-government system must be wrong. Tuan recalls how this view gave her and Kian a particular gaze on compulsory health and self-discipline. Her own speech slips back into the voice of the girl who ate lunch with them. This is not just a plot-information transfer. Adult grief and the present gunpoint conversation are being spoken through a remembered mode of belonging. E006.

Miaha then says the place she went with Tuan’s father taught her that humans could change beyond the limit of being conscious. Asked whether hatred caused the present chaos, she instead professes wholehearted love for this world and a wish to save it from the invading I. That is direct evidence against retaining Tuan’s earlier conditional hatred hypothesis as the sole current motive account. It is not omniscient evidence of pure benevolence. Her trauma, expressed wish to prevent suicides, later qualified agreement about returning to an earlier condition and the violent means remain material to reading what this love entails. E006–E007,E012.

The transformation preserves a disturbing consistency: the institution’s demand is no longer rejected for the sake of the unadapted person. The person is to be changed so completely that the demand ceases to hurt. Miaha’s old bodily refusal and present commitment to social completion are therefore related through a changed answer to the same conflict, rather than made identical by the analyst.

## The code she claims, and the code she cannot command

Miaha says she wrote most of the source code for the molecular neural network that the experimenters, including Tuan’s father, placed in WatchMe users without consent. Most is not all. She claims backdoors in several life-governments’ control systems, not unlimited access to every government. She describes giving the desire for death an overwhelming current value; Tuan’s explanatory continuation treats the resulting attractiveness of death as something the affected people could not evade. These are a current insider admission and narrated causal account, not inspected code, server logs or an external model of actual human motivation. E007.

The distinction between valuing death and removing consciousness is decisive. Miaha’s side can manipulate values within the installed system; it cannot itself authorize the final transformation. She identifies the elders with those controlling the working group and describes Tuan’s father as a key ideologue. The mainstream regards the mirroring I as dignity; her minority regards consciousness as a source of suffering left behind by a completed social system. She names herself leader of that minority and says she fled to former Chechen rescuers. These are more specific present claims than the earlier button image, not a retroactive correction to an analyst who had certified a physical interface. E008–E009.

She expressly corrects the shorthand: several codes known only to the elders, rather than a literal button. The minority’s inability to obtain this authority explains the pressure strategy. Catastrophe is staged so the reluctant holders will choose the action they otherwise prohibit. That is a coherent account of a power limit, not evidence that her side secretly has the same final permission anyway. It also does not show an actual code entered, a complete roster, universal activation or success. E009.

Miaha’s defense of removal depends on mature mutual aid, guidance software and extensive outsourcing. She argues that only the painful demand to will conformity remains, while the old faculty is no longer needed. The remembered injunction to fit a body to a shoe makes the direction of adaptation explicit. Its apparent convenience is the ethical problem: a social order’s completion becomes a reason to eliminate the part of a person that cannot fit. Her claims of widespread suicide motivate her proposed rescue; they are not a verified census or a proof that every possible social remedy has failed. E008.

## Assent to a world, refusal of a beneficiary

After wind and pauses, Tuan raises the gun again and aligns it with Miaha’s heart. She accuses Miaha of killing Kian and her father. Miaha nods and invokes necessity and random selection; Tuan distinguishes her father, and Miaha says he died for his belief. The random-selection explanation does not make father’s death another demonstrated random target. Her acknowledgment strongly supports responsibility for the project and its costs without reconstructing every individual instruction, phone call or physical killing. E011.

Tuan then imagines the future without an inner voice: shopping, work, laughter and tears might continue as self-evident reactions. The repeated yesterday phrases turn a radical alteration into outward continuity. This is her prospective conception of such a world, not a view of an already activated population. Her qualified probably and no objection register assent to the proposed outcome at this moment. They should not be softened into simple opposition to harmonics, nor expanded into a complete proof that all consequences are known. E011.

Her decision concerns who will enter that future. She asks whether Miaha wanted to return to her people’s supposedly unconscious condition; Miaha gives a qualified agreement. Tuan then says taking that away might be her revenge. Miaha’s surprise makes Tuan laugh and feel relieved by recognition of a familiar self-centeredness. That is Tuan’s interpretation of the response, not exhaustive access to what Miaha had imagined about the journey. E012.

Tuan’s accusation about the earlier Kian contact is equally bounded. She says Miaha needed conscious self-justification for an event already decided. Miaha answers with uncertain questions. This is a current interpretation met by qualified responses, not a new recorded call or a complete confession of an unconscious mechanism. The project’s admitted manipulation gives the accusation weight, while its exact personal and psychological details remain less secure than the broad admission. E012.

Tuan promises to realize the desired world while withholding it from Miaha. Her revenge is thus not announced as saving humanity from the program. It is excluding one person from its benefit, whatever that benefit may be. The withholding depends on a continued capacity to choose a particular act against a particular person. Even as she accepts the removal of choosing, she claims a final choice that the other cannot share. No future promise is treated as an accomplished implementation. E012.

## Two bullets and an answer to the dying person

The trigger is pulled and Miaha falls. The initial compact action is later specified as two bullets through her chest, one assigned to Kian and one to father. The analysis retains that explicit count without inventing two separately displayed trigger movements; the earlier cheek graze belongs to the preceding unit. The distributive accounting makes the deaths the measure of Tuan’s chosen act. This is a performed fatal attack, not an unrealized threat or a harmless warning. E013.

Miaha still speaks after falling. She asks whether she will now be forgiven; Tuan clarifies that she means Kian and father; Miaha confirms, and Tuan declares her revenge ended. That exchange is substantial evidence of Tuan’s stated personal closure. It is not a neutral absolution of every death or proof of the act’s justice. Tuan strokes her hair, and Miaha asks to be taken where she can see the mountains. A person denied the desired future is still given a concrete last request. The care does not cancel the shooting, and the shooting does not make the care unreal. E013–E014.

Tuan carries her toward the snow and mountain view. She says the world does not matter to her at this instant. Possible crowds, violence and an elder about to enter the last code occur in an even-if sequence, subordinated to that personal attention. They are not the narrator independently showing an actual global input. The insistence that she herself fired, by no one else’s will, then repeats the I in short isolated statements. She claims the act’s ownership at the very point where she has agreed that such an owner might disappear. E014.

Miaha asks her to watch the end of her consciousness. White breath ceases; body and brain lose heat; the narration explicitly says consciousness disappears through ordinary death. This establishes Miaha’s death here, rather than leaving the scene at an unconfirmed fall. Tuan adds that even if the consciousness was a limbic emulation, there is no great difference between it and theirs. That qualification is not a verified brain scan, but it is a clear refusal to make the reported difference a lesser form of personhood. The person who theorized removing consciousness is given a watched, particular end. E014.
