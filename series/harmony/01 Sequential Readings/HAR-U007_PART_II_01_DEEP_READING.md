# HAR-U007 — A crime named before its cause

The institutions preparing to investigate are therefore also deciding what sort of actors these people were: agents of a shameful act, victims of an influence, or participants in an attack on life itself. 霧慧トァン brings her friend's death into this uncertain public account, then obtains permission to investigate by threatening her own institution with disclosure. The permission lasts within a five-day therapy constraint. Seven bounded claims HAR-CL039–HAR-CL045 follow the meeting, its remembered negotiation and the formal replay of 零下堂キアン's last words.

## Counting deaths while withholding a verdict on agency

The liaison's report distinguishes confirmed deaths from attempts and marks its own time: eight hours have elapsed, and some people remain between life and death. Tuan subtracts 2,796 from 6,582 to obtain 3,786. That remainder is not a count of recovered or safe people. The later statement that surviving participants are in deep coma explains why no interview can yet supply a motive (E004/E007).

`当事者` keeps the question of agency open. The narration says these people apparently decided to take their lives—`決意したらしい`—then considers that such simultaneity may instead indicate an influence or injury suffered by the actors. The acts look self-initiated, yet the relation between action, decision and victimhood has become uncertain. The report gives a real commonality: every known participant belonged to スクナビコナ医療合意共同体 (Sukunabikona medical agreement community, analyst rendering), across twenty-five countries. The objects used varied and were close to hand. Abandoning a search for a pattern in methods does not erase the shared membership or establish the cause (E004/E006; HAR-CL040).

The inventory of objects is followed by a dialogue-list that imagines people discovering their available means. Those lines are a rhetorical reconstruction, not collected survivor testimony: the same meeting says nobody can presently answer questions. Their grotesque ease exposes the difficulty of explaining synchronized acts by the ordinary availability of objects alone.

## The common judgment and its marked exception

The `〈commonsense〉` enclosure contains three itemized public condemnations, followed by an `〈ex:…〉` exception for Tuan's bodily-ownership view. Its structure gives the norm and the dissenting position different fields rather than letting every statement become a neutral narrator's judgment. Tuan recognizes the grief of friends and acquaintances while objecting to strangers' resource-based contempt (E005).

She initially says 御冷ミァハ (Miaha) agreed, then corrects herself with `というより`: the thought was Miaha's. The correction complicates her claim to speak as an independent dissenter. It places the present objection within an inherited rhetoric, without proving that she merely repeats every Miaha judgment. Her remembered account of burial customs and her imagined gravediggers dramatize social condemnation. They are attributed and rhetorical layers; they are not a newly observed legal proceeding. The text expressly separates a socially despised act from a legally punishable crime (HAR-CL041).

The institutions' urgency develops in that atmosphere. The inspectors have been summoned because the office judges the event an intervention-worthy crime against the highest value of life, while Tuan says they do not yet know what the crime is. An unnamed inspector declares terrorism. The source thus shows a criminal frame being chosen ahead of a demonstrated mechanism. It also leaves a grave shared emergency that warrants inquiry; uncertainty about classification does not make the deaths or need for investigation unreal (E005/E007/E009).

## What the watchers cannot yet see

The scene keeps virtual placement separate from physical presence. The colleague sits “beside” Tuan only in augmented reality; she is alone on her hotel bed. Oscar is expected to be in Sahara. Tuan's boredom enclosure exposes her inward response to the colleague's emphatic intervention, while her account of adult harmony explains the outward courtesy expected at the meeting. The ruby of `調和` here reads `ハーモニクス`. Neither displayed agreement nor the common office establishes identical private judgments (E007).

When the colleague asks about WatchMe, the liaison states that it does not monitor the brain. Tuan supplies the technical account, corrects a simple mesh analogy and qualifies the conclusion: most of the brain is inaccessible, with named endocrine exceptions. The specialist detail gives her an effective public role inside the institution she resents. Senior rank has not guaranteed that colleague's knowledge of this particular limit (E008; HAR-CL042).

The liaison also reports ongoing external scans. Those cannot provide nanoscale forensics, and no report of a brain abnormality has yet arrived after eight hours. This distinguishes internal monitoring, external observation, resolution and current reporting time. A diagnostic gap remains a gap; it neither proves an undetectable attack nor rules out abnormality. The reader receives an investigatory constraint, not the hidden cause.

Oscar announces that headquarters will request an emergency WHO assembly two hours later and publicly frame the event as an attack on life rights. Treaty authority, she says, permits inspectors to join investigations in their assigned areas. These are a stated jurisdiction and planned response; the assembly, announcement and subsequent investigations have not yet been shown (E009; HAR-CL043).

## Permission within an unremoved constraint

Tuan invokes the inspectors' professional five-day grace before therapy. Oscar eventually grants participation but refuses an extension of that limit. The unit distinguishes **120 hours of prescribed therapy** from **five days of grace before it**. They are different constraints even though their numerical durations match (E010–E012).

Tuan interprets Oscar's response as fear of the concealed subordinate and of a Miaha-like shadow whose name the chief does not know. Her own feeling that she speaks as a living Miaha would have spoken is explicit self-interpretation, not a present action by Miaha or direct access to Oscar's mind. Here the Miaha-like performance becomes spoken action. In HAR-U006 the imagined DummyMe lesson to Kian had remained private; she now gives Oscar that information as one part of a different, threatening bargain.

The bargain changes an actual order: Tuan receives permission to participate. It leaves the ordinary member's therapy obligation in place, a rule both women acknowledge. Her anticipation of confinement is shaped by her judgment of imposed kindness; Oscar's concern also has the stated purpose of treating traumatic exposure. The heading's warmth therefore meets a concrete tension between care and the recipient's freedom. The unit closes on a practical uncertainty—whether five days are enough to explain her friend's death—rather than on a solved case or a completed therapy episode (HAR-CL045).
