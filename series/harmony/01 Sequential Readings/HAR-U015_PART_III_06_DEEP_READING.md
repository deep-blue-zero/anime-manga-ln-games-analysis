## The answer becomes an experiment report

Nuaza’s surprise gives Tuan’s unfinished U014 inference a new status: he explicitly confirms it, then says experiments disclosed that perfect coordination of rationally discounting reward systems eliminates the need for the meeting itself. The previous unit ended before this answer. Its frozen uncertainty was appropriate; the present reader now has a participant’s confirmation and an asserted experimental result. Neither should be erased in favor of permanent uncertainty or retrospectively placed inside the earlier frontier. E001.

The result is not a claim that a person sits blankly in a chair. Nuaza says shopping, meals, entertainment and ordinary social life continue, with behavior outwardly indistinguishable from conscious behavior. His distinction concerns whether an act requires experienced choice or is self-evident within a harmonized value system. He also claims reduced self-killing and the disappearance of the society’s stress. These are substantial claims about the program’s effects, with no test record or live demonstration supplied to this scene. His account of external indistinguishability makes a casual observer especially unable to settle the matter merely by watching ordinary activity. E001–E002.

He supplies a more specific report about Miaha: during the intervention she ate, studied and conversed; after the experimenters restored consciousness she reported no memory of the interval and only a vague happy ecstasy. The reported restoration and subsequent speech positively place her as a living experimental participant, extending the previous donated-body and limit-trial accounts. They still do not supply the complete history by which her earlier reported death, custody and later activity relate. The account reaches the reader through Nuaza; Miaha is not now present to give her own testimony. E002.

The reported ecstasy also resists a simple statement that nothing whatever was experienced. Nuaza speaks of lost consciousness, while the remembered report uses language of a happy state without episodic recall. The text gives those descriptions together. The analyst need not silently harmonize them into a completed theory of memory, phenomenology or neurological measurement. Tuan’s evolutionary and animal comparisons remain her attempts to understand this account, including a speculative explanation of what Miaha might have felt. E002.

## An unchosen installation, restrained by an unchosen authority

Tuan accuses her father’s group of intending to impose this condition on humanity, or at least everyone connected to WatchMe. He denies that formulation and describes his own fear of losing the awareness of being himself, comparing it in one sense to death. His appeal to the need for a decision about billions of people acknowledges the magnitude of the ethical problem. It does not yet describe an inclusive public decision. E003.

The compromise he reports is covert installation without activation. He explicitly says that molecular networks already exist in his brain and Tuan’s brain, ready for emergency harmonics if catastrophe returns. The new finding is stronger than research proximity or a hypothetical technology: it is a direct insider claim of dormant bodily implementation, including two named recipients. It is still distinct from activation, universal active monitoring or an inspected implant in the current scene. E003.

Tuan’s anger turns that hidden readiness into an “automatic hallelujah” apparatus. The literal music field is named `Messier` with its original identifier; five enclosed hallelujah lines, the field closure and a later hallelujah remain in their witnessed arrangement. No external musical work is identified by silently correcting the name. The praise is bitterly framed by anger: an unsolicited system promises to remove the troublesome self that might resist its happiness. The literary music field is not evidence that an audible playback has begun or that a technical emotion command is executing. E004.

Nuaza then says WHO and frightened life-government elders avoided discussion and ethics sessions because most people would refuse to lose this function. Thus their refusal to activate coexists with exclusion of people from the decision to install. His insistence that they currently have no intention of pressing the last button is explicit and repeated. Their belief that social remedies should still be possible is a material limit in his account, rather than proof of total indifference. It leaves the political asymmetry intact: undisclosed authorities claim the power to decide whether the crisis has become sufficient. E004.

Tuan’s retort names Miaha as a possible bearer of the intention her father disclaims. At this point she is asking; she has not independently inspected a captured control system. The unit will add a faction member’s account, but the difference between installed capacity, claimed restraint and a rival’s desired outcome remains essential.

## A normality analogy with a visible fault

Holding a pot in a hardware shop, Nuaza changes the subject to Martha’s Vineyard and signing. His history, inheritance figures and account of a community’s norms are attributed exposition, not independently researched history here. Its argumentative role is clear: a capacity treated as normal elsewhere need not be necessary to a functioning culture, while a minority elsewhere can become ordinary within a different environment. E005.

There is also an internal fault the reading must preserve. He first calls sign language the island’s basic language, then says the signing island needed no “language” when drawing the later analogy. The second shorthand cannot become the analyst’s assertion that signing is language-free. Recognizing the discrepancy requires no imported source: his own earlier wording supplies it. Nor does the analogy itself establish an equivalence between deafness and absent consciousness. Tuan directly resists such a claim about Miaha; Nuaza explicitly answers that she has consciousness, then describes it as acquired. E005–E006.

His subsequent minority-group history offers tests, genetic inheritance and imaging as a proposed foundation for that distinction. He says an isolated community near Chechnya lived culturally and rationally without the consciousness his model recognizes. No underlying test records, images or genetic records are displayed. These claims should be read as the character’s scientific and ethnographic account within the fiction, with its classificatory interests visible, rather than adopted as real biology or an analyst’s judgment that an actual population lacks an inner life. The source does not name the minority; it must not be merged with every Chechen or Russian person. E006.

## Trauma reported through the researcher’s categories

Nuaza reports that Miaha belonged to this group and was apparently abducted at eight, taken into a trafficking camp and subjected to repeated sexual violence. He explains her acquired consciousness as an adaptation to immediate terror, with another brain region emulating the function he assigns to the usual reward systems. The abduction channel is qualified in his wording; the neural explanation is his theory, not Miaha’s own current account or an independently validated clinical history. E007.

Tuan takes up the explanation in language of simulation and imitation, then realizes that extreme despair was not the only reason for selecting Miaha. This extends the previous unit’s “one reason” without making it retrospectively exhaustive. The new account puts heredity and a claimed unusual experimental condition inside the research motive. Its language can also reduce a traumatized person to a function that interests researchers. The reading should not repeat “imitation consciousness” as a verdict that Miaha is less fully a person. It is a revealing classification used by Nuaza and absorbed by Tuan. E006–E007.

Her reported rescue and move to Japan connect two forms of domination in Tuan’s interpretation: explicit physical abuse and a society whose coercive kindness strangled her. She asks whether Miaha sought a return to original harmony through the shared attempt to die. The interrogative remains a conjecture about motive, not the recovered private reason for every adolescent act. Her researcher father’s response combines fascination and disappointment at what self-killing implies about a conflicted will. That use of the victim’s history as an experimental problem is ethically consequential even while its neurological truth remains unverified. E007.

Tuan first denies that he is Interpol, then makes the more precise distinction: the ID is Interpol, but he acts for the Miaha faction opposing her father. His eventual admission confirms that loyalty. The scene supports a definite new judgment about his current alignment without requiring the analyst to discard the credential or merge him with the earlier unnamed liaison. E008–E009.

Her explanation that the investigation was a lure receives further support from his later admission that her visits brought Nuaza before him. He says capture would weaken a rival mainstream faction by removing its leading figure for the decisive next day or two. This revises the practical meaning of his earlier “chemical reaction” admission. The earlier inference that he used Tuan was supported already; the new source strengthens it and identifies a more specific hostile-faction purpose. Purpose and fallback outcome are disclosed separately. E009, E012.

## Two shots, then the names of the bodies

The tension and nested silence fields frame simultaneous attempts to raise weapons. One shot belongs to Tuan and one to Vashilov; the initial “each other” construction seems to invite a simple reciprocal wound. Only after the literal closures does the text specify Vashilov’s chest and her father’s chest. Tuan is not the second wounded target. Nuaza has put himself in the line of fire to shield her. The disclosure order makes the correction of the expected pair part of the event’s force. E011.

His death is definite in the narration, reinforced by her unsuccessful pulse check. The simile that he tried to fulfill thirteen years of paternal duty in an instant is Tuan’s interpretation, not access to his final intention. She finds her grief quiet and her tears absent, in contrast with Kian’s death and the first reported “death” of Miaha. Thirteen years have made her father almost a stranger to her. This particular comparison must not become a general diagnosis that she cannot grieve. E011.

She reads his action as continuing love, then offers alternatives: his body might have moved before conscious deliberation, or kinship might have operated as an inherited reflex. The act of protection is observed; its exact neural and emotional cause is not. The uncertainty matters in a chapter that has just separated effective conduct from conscious choice. Her earlier childhood rescue and this final shielding give a bounded recurrent paternal action under immediate danger, while prolonged absence and unequal attachment remain countercases to a complete story of dependable fatherhood. E011; earlier HAR-U002-E015 remains at its own boundary.

Tuan thanks him and closes his eyes. Gratitude here follows a life-saving bodily act without manufacturing forgiveness, matching grief or complete ideological agreement. The response is especially consequential because the caring body she has resisted can still demand a concrete acknowledgment from her. The scene retains both the death’s asymmetry and the act’s value.

## Peace as the explanation of manufactured chaos

Vashilov is still breathing after Tuan’s chest shot. His account treats Nuaza’s death as a success despite the preference for capture, then gives chaos the job of weakening the opposing faction. His declared purpose is a new harmonious world, with Miaha as ideologue and guide. The contradiction between that promise and the deaths and ultimatum is explicitly challenged by Tuan. His response invokes her childhood experience of charisma and a future vision; it does not supply a justification she accepts. E012.

Tuan’s rounded reference to six thousand people and his assent belong to this argument. They do not replace the earlier exact distinctions among simultaneous attempts, confirmed deaths and provisional reporting times. His subsequent description of self-killing and threats as catalysts claims a political strategy, not a finished technical account of every death. His assertion that it is already too late also remains a faction member’s prognosis; the reader has not watched the global outcome it predicts. E012–E013.

The strategy makes the two factions’ uses of catastrophe legible together without making them identical. Nuaza’s group claims to reserve a covert dormant intervention for a future disaster; Vashilov’s group says the current chaos advances harmony. One restraint may become another faction’s condition to be produced. The reading can identify that supported political relation while leaving the precise activation route, control of devices and actual future decision unresolved. E003–E004, E009, E012–E013.

## A bargain made inside pain

The wounded man offers information in exchange for a shot to the forehead. He says he was allowed to disclose the location to Tuan, but the source of that permission is not identified. He names Chechnya and contact with the anti-Russian freedom front, then tells her to verify the matter by going there. This is a route to investigate, not a confirmed exact present location or a witnessed meeting with Miaha. E013.

His pain transforms the register of the card-waving investigator. He expresses astonishment at a sensation WatchMe and medical care had concealed from him, and pleads for relief. The pain is bodily and particular; it does not prove a clinical history of never having felt any pain before. His account supplies a countercase to his composure and a narrow basis for understanding this appeal, without turning his distressed request into a generally uncoerced contract. Tuan inflicted the chest wound, wants the information and retains the weapon. Their exchange has a severe asymmetry even though he explicitly asks for the final shot. E013.

She promises, presses the gun to his forehead and cocks it. The weapon and his visible relief make the prospective act concrete before the last hesitation. The source does not let the informational result stand alone as a clean investigative success: it attaches the cost of the promise to the person receiving it.

## Choosing while disliking the choice

Tuan describes this as the first time she is about to kill a person. That is her distinction at this moment, despite the immediately preceding potentially fatal chest shot. The reading should preserve it rather than inventing an earlier criminal history or treating the return fire as causally irrelevant. She connects the act to the decision imposed on millions by the ultimatum, then recognizes an unfair convenience: a pleading victim, revenge and release from the deadline seem to supply too many justifications. E014.

Her self-loathing interrupts any easy formula of mercy, revenge or coerced compliance. The expected release from the ultimatum is her appraisal; no server acknowledgment or verified exemption is shown, especially after the offline boundary. She also asks why an evolutionary system would contain this uncomfortable emotion. That question returns the molecular discussion to a lived act: several reasons compete, the decision is painful and the deciding subject can observe her own response. In a chapter about eliminating the need for experienced choice, she remains sharply conscious of having to choose. The source’s final movement is action, not the disappearance of conflict: she pulls the trigger with the loaded gun against the man’s forehead, fulfilling the promised shooting. There is no separate post-shot examination before the frame closes. E013–E014.

The literal body and ETML closures follow that act. They close the inherited body/ETML frame here, at the end of Part III, not the novel’s natural epilogue. The continued source stays unopened; no later travel, verification, activation or retrospective resolution is supplied to this unit.
