# HAR-U002 — Owning a body, accepting a hand

Numbered 02 gives the declaration of the opening a social and bodily setting. The first-person teller is identified as 霧慧トァン, a schoolgirl in the remembered scenes, while 御冷ミァハ supplies a language of resistance to a society organized around health and mutual care. Their affinity becomes credible through specific recognition and pleasure. It also becomes troubling through a concrete contradiction: Miaha continues painful bodily contact after Tuan asks her to stop, while arguing that a body must belong to its owner. That contradiction is stronger evidence than a general label such as rebellion or liberation. The unit also depicts successful care, so the pressure exerted by kindness cannot be reduced to medicine being useless or simply malevolent. Seven bounded claims HAR-CL005–HAR-CL011 own this reading; no novel-wide verdict follows.

## From becoming to being required

The opening formally corrects itself. A theorem-like pair first makes growing up and dying sound like transformations; the narrator then replaces it with a rule-like pair and explicitly calls for the language of prohibition. The child body must not become words before adulthood; the dead adult must be reduced to foam (E001). The load-bearing modalities are `してはならない` (must not) and `なくてはならない` (must). Prohibition and obligation replace apparently descriptive becoming. The visible switch of label and Japanese modality makes this normative reconstruction high-confidence (HAR-CL005). Its rhetorical force does not require an executable language. The material process behind death/foam remains unexplained; the assertion of a rule does not yet depict its enforcement or justify it.

The next explanation locates the distinction in homeostasis. The narrator asserts that rapidly growing children cannot receive WatchMe, whereas its entry marks adulthood (E002). This is a fictional technical account supplied by the teller, not independently tested physiology. The schoolgirl refuses the adult condition before the reader knows precisely what its word-making accomplishes. Miaha's invitation then recasts the boundary as an announcement of bodily ownership, using another visible list inside her speech (E003). The apparently private refusal therefore becomes a project offered to another person. Its first practical medium is friendship and persuasion, rather than an institution openly issuing commands.

The literary notation now organizes several kinds of utterance: narrator explanation, reported or direct speech, anger, maxims and embedded references (E001, E003, E005, E010, E016–E017). These differences matter more than a blanket claim that all tags are a machine's output. The theorem/rule correction is argumentative; the anger declaration categorizes an affect; the textbook and thesis blocks attribute knowledge. Their formatting makes categorization and authority legible, while no causal implementation of the outer protocol has been demonstrated. HAR-CL011 preserves that distinction. The arithmetic construction of HAR-U001 remains deliberate; these new forms expand its context without retroactively proving what the earlier first-person labels meant.

## The price of care, and the care that works

Tuan describes a social order whose kindness demands kindness in return. The declaration later isolates this reciprocity as an explicit price (E010). It is reinforced by concrete ordinary behavior: groups approach the solitary Miaha out of concern; Tuan joins a group and volunteers while trying to reduce her own visibility; she experiences teachers' and parents' attention as suffocating (E009–E010). The pressure is not that she receives no care. It is that receiving and circulating care appear to be conditions of acceptable membership. Children are called scarce social resources and public bodies in the post-catastrophe order. Her counterfactual wish to have been bullied belongs to this discontent; the unit does not depict her being bullied or depict her bullying anyone.

The earlier conversation about paid sex and unhealthy pleasures supplies a deliberately perverse imagined exit (E004). Miaha introduces a mediated historical account, and Tuan imagines a district in which a girl could ruin her life, if such a place still existed. Disease, drink and tobacco acquire appeal as unavailable ways of disposing of one's own body. These are adolescent fantasies in a health-governed world, not completed conduct or reliable evidence about the historical prevalence of exploitation. The scene gives felt content to refusing safe adulthood; it does not turn exploitation into an endorsed alternative.

A particularly strong countercase arrives in the childhood fall. Tuan remembers injury, her father rescuing her, prompt wound repair and protection against infection (E015). The medical molecules described by Miaha as potentially murderous are the same technology that actually helps her here. The scene's unusual remembered center is the visual movement of blood, not pain or fear: danger has an aesthetically vivid residue even though treatment succeeds. This does not establish a wish to die. It complicates the opposition between a protected body and a personally experienced body. A child can also have a medical port without possessing the adult WatchMe implant; conflating those devices would falsify the age boundary.

Miaha herself says society did well in eliminating most disease, even while criticizing the loss of felt knowledge of illness (E016). HAR-CL006 therefore holds together effective protection and the constraints of making health a social obligation. A flat anti-medicine reading fails on both demonstrated rescue and this explicit concession. Conversely, those benefits do not answer Tuan's complaint about obligatory reciprocal feeling. The ethical conflict is specific: what choices remain to the beneficiary when care becomes a claim upon her body, attention and conduct? The unit raises that conflict without showing an exhaustive catalogue of laws or allowing the narrator's hostile generalization to stand as a neutral census of everyone in the society.

## Knowing the past, being recognized

Miaha's influence depends partly on an asymmetry of knowledge. The older narrator remembers her as knowing everything, but the scene provides specific channels and limits rather than omniscience. Miaha is a strong student, a reader of physical books and a source of stories about dangerous past practices. Tuan lacks the remembered experience of ordinary illness and initially does not recognize a book (E003–E006, E011–E016). Historical visual information is legally restricted. The narrator explicitly distinguishes the schoolgirl without a qualification from her narrating present, when she has acquired the qualification for professional reasons (E011). An occupation, date and current age remain unspecified; this later access cannot be credited to the girl at the first meeting.

The book's materiality is chosen. Miaha downloads from a network library and pays for printing/binding, then uses a heavy, awkward object to sustain solitude (E006, E013). Her practice is neither simple technological ignorance nor complete escape from the network. It makes a way of attending that the immediate social environment treats as a problem. The bending playground equipment gives that environment a tangible counterpart: the girl's movement is surrounded by responsive devices designed to prevent injury (E012). Miaha describes the lethal risks of earlier rigid equipment; Tuan has not witnessed those deaths. Her own later-recounted camp accident is not proof that current playgrounds are dangerous or that all present childhood injury is impossible.

Miaha accurately identifies a more local fact: Tuan's public sociability conceals self-concern and fatigue with harmony. Tuan confirms that the assessment struck home (`図星だった`) and remembers the pleasure of having otherwise inarticulate feelings articulated (E013). That is evidence of successful recognition and persuasive intimacy (HAR-CL007). It does not establish that every diagnosis Miaha makes is true. Her proposal that people forget danger develops through questions about falling; Tuan initially appeals to instinct, then supplies a memory in which the fall was too quick for a distinct fear response (E014–E016). Miaha's point eventually concerns forgetting, so treating her as simply asserting that fear never has an innate component would flatten the exchange.

The historical and technical references also have uneven status. The catastrophe narrative is a school passage recited from memory and includes a hedge about nuclear mutation as a possible source of unknown viruses (E016). The medical-misuse exposition is framed through Miaha's explanations, not a demonstrated successful attack (E005). The capacity she claims is extraordinary; no operational detail is needed to establish its argumentative role, which is to detach physical capacity from benevolent software restrictions. Neither assertion becomes real-world technical knowledge in this analysis. The thesis is an embedded fictional bibliographic object, with named researcher 霧慧ヌァザ and co-researcher 冴紀ケイタ (E017). Its appearance attributes a scientific lineage within the telling; it supplies no external verification of priority.

Tuan privately knows that her father is implicated in the technological world Miaha hates. She wonders whether Miaha knows, fears rejection and does not volunteer the connection (E017). The asymmetry thus runs in both directions: Miaha can recognize Tuan's alienation without the text establishing that she knows this parentage. Shared hatred may be, in Tuan's anxious formulation, an excuse; the analysis does not assign inherited guilt. The concealment shows what belonging with Miaha costs Tuan emotionally and informationally (HAR-CL009). The paper's thirty-five-year interval is relative to this account, not a license to derive an absolute year or the father's age.

## An autonomy lesson that overrides refusal

Miaha's most decisive contradiction is enacted at the desk. She takes hold of Tuan's breast and continues despite pain, explicit requests to stop and Tuan's statement that what her hand is doing is itself unbearable (E008). Her brief refusal is `や、やめてよミァハ`; the narration twice uses `いつものように` (as usual) in describing Miaha’s disregard/persistence. While performing that disregard, Miaha describes the prospective medical appropriation of the body into other people's words. Bodily self-ownership is the stated principle; the recipient's immediate refusal does not govern the lesson. HAR-CL008 is grounded in that conduct, without a psychiatric explanation or a claim about Miaha's hidden intentions.

One could read the scene as provocative intimacy between familiar friends, or as a didactic performance intended to make abstract medical power palpable. Those functions are plausible and can coexist with the argument. They do not remove the explicit refusal or transform persistence into consent. Tuan's later remembered pleasure in an earlier hand kiss also cannot supply consent for this later, painful contact. Keeping that distinction permits an affirmative account of coercion while leaving motive and the whole relationship open.

Kian offers a second friction point. She says mass harm must not be done; Tuan initially experiences the objection as hollow. The older narrator then questions her own unexamined response (E007). Calling Kian thoughtless would repeat Tuan's dismissive characterization as objective fact. Her spoken refusal is actual evidence of an ethical limit, while her inner reasoning remains unavailable. The scene exposes the gap between rejecting imposed feeling and having worked out a reason for one's own judgments. Sympathy with Miaha is not equivalent to an examined ethics, even in Tuan's later account.

## Disclosure after the contradiction

The unit tells the desk conversation first, then returns to the park encounter that began the relationship. This order matters (HAR-CL010). The reader has already seen Miaha disregard refusal before seeing her original power to make Tuan feel understood. Recruitment is not introduced as a neutral charm subsequently qualified by facts we have not yet read; the qualification is already present in the unit's disclosure order. The two scenes nevertheless remain chronologically distinct. A childhood fall is nested further back, while the adult teller's occupational qualification and her self-questioning belong to later reflection. No actual scene of their separation is supplied.

At the crossroads Miaha takes Tuan's hand, gives a speech about recovering ownership of health, life and possibly thought, and kisses the back of the hand (E018–E019). The possible outsourcing of thought is Miaha's qualified extension, not proof that a technology already reads minds. Tuan withdraws her hand and remembers the cold contact as pleasantly lingering. Miaha's assertion that they are made of the same material is a claim of affinity. It gives no warrant to erase the different experiences and knowledge just disclosed. The quasi-courtly gesture, suddenness and pleasure allow an inference of charged intimacy; they do not establish a sexual identity, an explicit mutual commitment or a complete account of desire.

The final narrator calls the relationship brief and life-changing (E019). This is a proleptic assessment from the telling, not an outcome the reader has yet witnessed. The earlier opening's defeat/flight labels still have no securely identified incident. The unit ends with an origin made consequential by that promised future, and with a relationship already capable of both recognition and disregard. Its local explanatory gain is large, but neither a reconstruction of either girl nor a settled judgment about all care follows from it.
