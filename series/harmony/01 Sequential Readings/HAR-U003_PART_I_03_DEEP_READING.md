Her adulthood turns rebellion into a repeated bargain: medical programs go outward and cigars come back. ## An announced death, a deferred reunion

The first sentence promises separation and reunion with Miaha, then substitutes Kian’s death as the necessary starting point (E001). Its chronology is unusually explicit: thirteen years after the three met, Kian will die at a meal; the Sahara action begins forty-eight hours before that event. The caprese’s red/white ingredients are isolated as a list before the death sentence completes. The ordinary meal is thus made visually legible before its lethal future is explained. Calling the death `自死` is the teller’s attribution. This unit does not depict it, establish its method or let Sahara-time Tuan know it in advance.

Later the teller calls Miaha’s life what she lost, and the final reflection says Miaha went alone while Tuan and Kian remained (E005/E013). These are significant narrative assertions, not merely analyst guesses. Yet the opening has also announced reunion. Neither a resurrection nor mistaken identity nor a metaphorical reunion is established. The productive tension is the narration’s: it confidently tells an outcome while withholding the circumstances that would reconcile its own promises. A source-bounded account must retain both statements instead of solving them with later plot knowledge (HAR-CL012).

The unit moves Sahara → childhood classroom → Sahara. The childhood account itself ends with Tuan’s unequivocal survival before returning to the adult body smoking in the desert. Disclosure therefore supplies the adult consequence before the earlier choice, and survival before its explanation. The temporal cuts are not evidence of an omniscient narrator: the telling has later outcome knowledge, while the actors retain their own limited scene-time access.

## A landscape of compensation and contested names

At first Sahara is a two-color abstraction. Landscape notation separates blue sky from yellow earth, while the Rothko comparison and the lips’ tactile attention to a cigar give Tuan an aesthetic mode of noticing (E002). The flowers then acquire a history: engineered sunflowers have been made to absorb contamination, clean the soil and end their lives. `償いのひまわり` is the narrator’s phrase—an analyst’s gloss is “sunflowers of compensation/atonement.” The diction gives the beautiful surface a burden and a cost. It does not identify each beneficiary or establish the flowers as conscious sufferers.

The dictionary block gives dates, nuclear losses and an IAEA attribution within the fiction. Its reference style is an attribution device, not an external historical authority. The narrator also compresses former states and conflicts into historical episodes and comments on the blanket term terrorism. These judgments belong to this represented history and speaker. No assertion here about actual-world RRW history, ethnic etymology or nuclear remediation is licensed by a fictional dictionary.

The warrior immediately contests Tuan’s exonym and supplies a self-designation defined by language (E003). Tuan keeps thinking in the disputed name for much of the scene, then uses ケル・タマシェク at farewell. That sequence is observable; complete ideological conversion is not. Her thought that the abandoned-by-god label sounds cooler exposes an aesthetic attraction to abandonment, alongside an institution that, in her metaphor, will never abandon its medical people. The warrior’s complaint makes that apparently reassuring inclusion political: a server connection can be presented as imposed faith or imperial infrastructure.

Tuan distinguishes her WHO/Geneva Treaty Organization monitoring role from the Niger government; the warrior says the difference is immaterial to his people’s experience. Their positions are not merged into neutral world truth. She has a politically consequential job but calls political discussion boring (E004). This makes her participation in the bargain more revealing than a declaration of impartiality alone. Her national stereotype about Etienne’s taste and her claim to see the warrior’s understanding likewise remain character judgments, not analyst-certified qualities.

## The adolescent “only way” and its older correction

The classroom scene shows Miaha recruiting through the very public-body premise she opposes. She argues that distributed medical consensus has removed a concentrated political target; since the girls themselves are valuable infrastructure, taking their bodies away will attack that system (E006–E007). This is Miaha’s explanation and justification, with Tuan’s remembered agreement, not the reading’s moral endorsement or proof that death is the only available political act. The argument transforms a demand for ownership into a command felt by the girls: they *must* die because life is cared for too much.

Kian’s question about death as an attack is an actual hesitation. Tuan then describes Kian as fearful and compliant, but this is the same narrator who treated her earlier ethical objection dismissively. Kian’s later endorsement of Miaha’s technical ability and her tentative assent do not give independent access to her whole motive hierarchy (E008). Her recognition record must preserve the objection and the uncertainty, rather than reducing her to a follower.

Tuan supplies a clear retrospective correction: `あの選択が自由意志だったなんて、さすがに今は思ってない` (analyst’s gloss: she no longer thinks that choice was free will). Her reason is not simply that the plan failed. She identifies the pair’s dependence on Miaha’s charisma and her own expectation that Miaha always has a reliable method. The repeated `今しかない` (“only now,” analyst’s gloss) narrows the adolescent’s perceived possibilities. The scene portrays assent and ingestion; it also supplies a later account of how that assent was shaped. Agency and influence coexist, and the older judgment should not be backdated into the girl’s conscious reasoning (HAR-CL015).

The means are presented through Miaha’s claim, her displayed tablets, Tuan’s trust and Kian’s reinforcing assertion. Tuan expressly rejects the imagined exploitative acquisition story, but her certainty that Miaha manufactured them is still not a witnessed manufacturing process. What is depicted is all three taking them, followed by Tuan’s survival. This closes some uncertainty about action while preserving causal uncertainty about efficacy, intervention and the others’ immediate outcomes. The exact mechanism is unnecessary to repeat in order to assess the literary choice.

## A monitored body and unequal access to care

Adult Tuan’s small escape requires pretending to accept adulthood and continuing to deceive the system about it (E005). Smoking now needs a battlefield’s distance rather than the school hiding places Miaha described. The list turns adulthood from a natural destination into a performance whose acceptance can be tactical (HAR-CL013). Her WHO position, armored transport, accomplices, stolen server program and regular customers make this escape dependent on an institution and repeated social cooperation. It is not pure solitary autonomy.

The warrior’s use of obsolete-looking communications at first surprises her. The ISS report then exposes a surveillance arrangement she had not understood: transaction times/places follow an orbit, and the remote young associate supplies warning of a possible armed reconnaissance aircraft (E009). The warning is received report, not a verified image of weapons. Tuan’s claim to political sophistication does not make her all-knowing about the conditions enabling her own trade.

The strongest disclosure correction comes when Tuan admits that the Kel Tamasheq also use WatchMe (E010). Her address to a reader who may have romanticized an unmedicalized people deliberately retracts an outside/inside binary that her prior staging helped invite. The problem is now precise: surveillance can identify disease while the group cannot afford the medical intellectual property, and its server is disconnected from the other networks (E011). Monitoring capacity, treatment access and institutional membership are different variables. The question/answer frame asks what the monitored person can do, then answers with practical incapacity. This is a materially grounded access problem, not an argument that WatchMe never helps anyone (HAR-CL016).

Tuan calls the exchange a respectable act of assistance, then lists both saved people and her cigar reward. The list prevents altruism and personal advantage from being exclusive explanations. Her moral self-defense is credible in its stated benefit and interested in its presentation. The existence of unpaid illicit aid does not establish the complete distribution or law of medical IP. Likewise her dismissal of the crystal medium as meaningless fetishism is her interpretation; the scene’s secrecy and mistrust leave a material reason for demanding a visible transferable object. No hidden security property is demonstrated.

## The offer she does not take

The warrior offers a place among his people; Tuan declines because she is `臆病者` (coward, analyst’s gloss), then explains that losing every gaze upon her would leave her unable to manage (E012). Her dependence is not just fear of disease. She hates the system that regards her yet fears the loss of that regard. This gives her earlier charged self-labels a concrete neighboring vocabulary without proving that the opening’s defeat/desertion labels refer to this specific transaction.

The connection to the suicide attempt is carefully qualified: `なんだろう、たぶん` makes fear a retrospective possible explanation for why Miaha alone went and the other two stayed (E013). The actual failure/survival sequence has not been told. It would be an attribution error to turn this tentative self-account into a settled cause, or to call Miaha objectively stronger because Tuan says she lacked that strength. Her self-condemnation belongs alongside the earlier explicit rejection of a free-choice account.

The ending gives kindness a local alternative. The warrior’s invitation remains available after her refusal; Tuan says merely being told there is somewhere to flee is enough. She values its lack of the obligation she associates with compulsory care (HAR-CL017). The invitation also invokes women’s scarcity and value during struggle, so it is no proof of a universally unconstrained haven. A real difference in this encounter survives that limit: the represented recipient can refuse the offer and still keep an exit available. The departure returns her to her companions and the driving seat, not to the new community.

Form carries these distinctions throughout. Lists isolate ingredients, landscape, tactical rules, benefits and survival outcomes; a dictionary attributes history; a question/answer exposes the gap between monitoring and available action (HAR-CL018). The same visible family of notation accommodates description, perspective and argument. Its use is deliberate and highly certain. The status of the outer ETML as a diegetic mechanism remains unproved; none of these literary operations licenses a simulated consciousness or a technical account of the narrative’s production.
