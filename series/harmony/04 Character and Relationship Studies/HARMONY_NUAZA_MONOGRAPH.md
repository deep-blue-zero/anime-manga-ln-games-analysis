# Nuaza and the limits he preserves and crosses

Nuaza's central contradiction concerns who is allowed to remain uncertain. In Tuan's childhood memory he defends moderate use and describes science as ambiguous, ordinary and repeatedly testable. His later research group protects its work through managed ignorance, secret experiments and unrequested bodily installation. He finally refuses to abolish consciousness, the contested faculty through which an individual can be troubled by an apparently completed social order. His resistance matters; so does the fact that he has helped place that faculty at the discretion of a small group. Harmony makes him intelligible through these connected commitments rather than through a final verdict that the caring father either excuses or exposes the scientist.

Its DOMAIN_BOUNDED assessment supports a substantial monograph while restricting any later reconstruction. The [current claims](../03%20Longitudinal%20Ledgers/HARMONY_CLAIMS_PREDICTIONS_AND_REVISIONS_LEDGER.md#g2-current-arguments--complete-admitted-novel-2026-10-17) retain the versioned institutional and motive qualifications. Nuaza is mostly seen by an estranged daughter, heard in his own interested explanation and reinterpreted through Miaha. Those channels provide strong evidence of particular conduct, sparse evidence of ordinary life during his long absence and no transparent final thought.

## The father before his explanation

Nuaza's earliest personally diagnostic action is rescue. Tuan remembers slipping from rocks, seeing blood in water and being pulled out by her father. He treats the injury and connects the medical protection. His own scientific legacy and his immediate paternal labor occupy the same remembered scene: medicine works because someone uses it to help this child. The image gives care bodily specificity before the later researcher can argue about whole populations. Tuan's sensory memory centers on the blood's movement rather than fear or pain; the difference belongs to her experience of the rescue, not to proof that his action was unnecessary. [HAR-U002-E015](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U002_PART_I_02_DEEP_READING.md#har-u002-e015); CL006.

Her admiration subsequently makes the ethics session hurt. The father who helped make WatchMe and changed the world is unable to secure a modest exception in an ordinary public discussion. Nuaza argues that caffeine has professional uses and can reduce stress. The unnamed woman's decorous certainty overwhelms him; he cannot finish 適度に. His account afterward describes scientific truth as banal, ambiguous and open to repeated verification. The daughter agrees with his reasonableness and finds his defeat humiliating. Her revulsion toward the social order therefore has a history tied to care for him. It precedes her meeting with Miaha. [HAR-U011-E001–E003](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U011_PART_III_02_DEEP_READING.md#har-u011-e001); CL072–CL073.

The scene does not establish Nuaza as an unrestricted dissenter. His defense remains professional and moderated. He neither announces bodily sovereignty nor rejects the life government's basic aims. His frustration also carries a scientist's self-defense: the man who has lost the discussion explains why people prefer categorical speech over the sort of truth his profession supplies. Tuan's later recollection is sympathetic to that account. Its bounded force is enough. Nuaza can resist excessive certainty and still belong to the medical project, just as a public intellectual authority can be socially defeated in a forum whose relevant currency is moral confidence.

That public weakness acquires a second meaning when Tuan discovers his private authority. The text eventually places the supposedly helpless father among people with power above the visible institutional hierarchy. These settings do not cancel one another. Having access to research resources and covert influence does not guarantee rhetorical command in an ethics session. The discrepancy shows how Tuan misreads his overall capacity from one painful scene, and how the novel withholds the institutional setting that would qualify her picture.

## An absence that becomes an investigative route

For much of the novel Nuaza is a name arriving through other people's information. The early thesis gives Tuan's concealed parentage political weight: her admired friend's critique of medical power touches her father's work. The girl fears rejection, and later disclosure establishes that Miaha did not reject her. Keita and Reiko eventually turn that hidden family relation into a route toward Baghdad. Scientific lineage, personal concealment and actual experimental custody remain different matters. A founding credit does not establish every later decision; a reported body transfer does not supply informed consent to every experiment. [HAR-U002-E017](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U002_PART_I_02_DEEP_READING.md#har-u002-e017); [HAR-U008-E007/E013–E016](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U008_PART_II_02_DEEP_READING.md#har-u008-e007); CL009v2/CL053v2.

Keita distinguishes understanding Nuaza's attraction to the science from approving his departure from his family. Tuan insists the father's guilt belongs to him. This is useful ethical work performed before he appears to offer his defense: scientific explanation cannot make an abandoned spouse and child disappear from the account. Keita's sympathetic curiosity and guarded information are evidence of a relationship to Nuaza; they do not authenticate the absent man's complete motives. Gabriel later confirms early consortium participation and current absence, while disclaiming knowledge of parallel work. The accumulating route makes Nuaza consequential without pretending anyone encountered so far has narrated his thirteen years. [HAR-U009-E009–E013](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U009_PART_II_03_DEEP_READING.md#har-u009-e009); [HAR-U012-E005](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U012_PART_III_03_DEEP_READING.md#har-u012-e005).

At the river he remains recognizable despite aging. The recognition is immediate; reconciliation is absent. Tuan's accusation that he told Miaha to kill receives a denial. He describes travel under supplied identities, a disclosure that sharpens the paternal injury. His absence was not simple physical confinement somewhere he could never leave. He reports being able to reach much of the world while remaining unavailable to his daughter. Yet the source does not tell us which specific family contact opportunities he refused or why he made every earlier choice. His means of mobility strengthens the question of abandonment without completing its biography. [HAR-U013-E011](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U013_PART_III_04_DEEP_READING.md#har-u013-e011); [HAR-U014-E001–E002](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U014_PART_III_05_DEEP_READING.md#har-u014-e001).

The encounter's posture complicates the accusation. They walk shoulder to shoulder and she tries to miss none of his words. Paternal instruction becomes available again through intimacy that estrangement has not erased. Her cold hands and sensible jacket keep this father and daughter in a vulnerable evening body even as he describes controlling will. He is neither only an abstract lecturer nor simply the father restored to his old place. Recognition gives his explanation access to her attention; it grants no acquittal.

## The custodian's modesty and the scale of his power

Nuaza corrects Tuan's picture of an ongoing research enterprise. Most research, he says, is completed; the group exists to prepare. 我々は、備える組織だ identifies its professed function: preserve a guarded technology, await a possible collapse and distribute it if the feared time arrives. He wishes that time never to come. Fear of a second catastrophe gives the project an intelligible protective rationale. The speech is serious positive evidence of the group's self-description, later strengthened by Oscar's and Miaha's different positions. It is also a participant's account of authority, not a neutral demonstration that this was the sole possible safeguard. [HAR-U014-E002–E004](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U014_PART_III_05_DEEP_READING.md#har-u014-e002); CL066v2/CL097–CL099.

His explanatory register often qualifies a proposition while claiming a vast jurisdiction. He grants Tuan's broad paraphrase, distinguishes current readiness from research and admits problems in the work's direction. At the same time he calls his group uniquely capable of controlling the world. This combination matters to his characterization. He need not be a ranting dogmatist to participate in exceptional power. His composure and distinctions can make the custodial claim more persuasive precisely because he sounds capable of identifying limits.

The information-management admission is the sharpest reversal of his earlier account of scientific humility. He says passage across the brain barrier was quickly achieved, and that the belief in a protected brain was a deliberate misunderstanding. Relevant papers remained publicly available while the group adjusted their visibility. The scientist who once suffered because a modest proposition could not be heard now belongs to an organization that decides which propositions others will effectively hear. The reversal is institutional and ethical; it need not be a consciously planned personal revenge for the session. No passage supplies that motive. [HAR-U014-E004](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U014_PART_III_05_DEEP_READING.md#har-u014-e004).

A charitable defense is that dissemination of a dangerous capacity might itself produce catastrophe. That fits the protective rationale he states, but it does not resolve the authority problem. Keeping knowledge under specialized custody also keeps affected people from knowing which boundary has already been breached. The public can reasonably use an older technical explanation while the custodians quietly prepare an intervention. Nuaza's earlier belief in repeated testing is not simply exposed as false. Its political reach is restricted: others cannot test the premises of the decision from the same effective information position.

## Rescue, experiment and the unchosen daughter

Nuaza's account of Miaha's selection concentrates this tension in a person. He says that if the program could subdue the strong will toward death gathered in her, it could control anyone. He calls the experimental interventions 限界検証試験, limit-verification trials. The children are in danger; they also supply extreme test conditions. His claim that his group took them in and treated them thus carries two purposes that cannot be collapsed. The source establishes a substantial participant account of experiment. It leaves the full custody history, each recipient's consent and individual outcomes incompletely supplied. [HAR-U014-E009](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U014_PART_III_05_DEEP_READING.md#har-u014-e009); CL103/CL053v2.

Tuan's immediate jealousy makes the scene unusually resistant to a flattering moral division. She first wonders why she was not selected. Her father's involvement with Miaha feels like another preference for a person whose despair was more interesting than hers. The imagined recruitment networks and her explanation of why he left remain Tuan's conjectures. Her jealousy is nevertheless explicit, and it neither cancels nor proves the ethical objection she then speaks. Nuaza has to answer an injured daughter who also asks a valid question about experimental purpose. [HAR-U014-E010–E011](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U014_PART_III_05_DEEP_READING.md#har-u014-e010).

His answer is a counterfactual rescue argument: without the intervention the children might have continued toward death. Tuan calls the substitution of result for purpose deliberate. His そうなのだろうな is a qualified concession, followed by acknowledgment that the work was imperfect. The precise exchange supports something stronger than either pure self-exoneration or a complete confession of evil. Nuaza can recognize force in the criticism and continue to understand the work through danger averted. Effective aid does not establish that rescue was its governing purpose; experimental purpose does not prove that nobody was helped. [HAR-U014-E011](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U014_PART_III_05_DEEP_READING.md#har-u014-e011); CL104.

His interest in Miaha has an additional classificatory dimension. He describes a minority population living rationally without the consciousness his model measures, her acquired consciousness through traumatic adaptation and her unusual experimental suitability. This is his scientific and ethnographic account; the underlying records are not opened. Miaha later supplies her own survivor testimony, which strengthens the reality of her suffering without independently validating his neural explanation. The monograph must therefore preserve his framework as evidence of how he approaches her, while refusing to adopt imitation or acquired consciousness as a verdict on lesser personhood. [HAR-U015-E005–E007](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U015_PART_III_06_DEEP_READING.md#har-u015-e005); [HAR-U019-E004–E005](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U019_PART_IV_04_DEEP_READING.md#har-u019-e004); CL110v2.

The signing analogy shows both explanatory ambition and a visible fault. Nuaza first calls signing a basic language, then uses a shorthand implying that this community needed no language. His own sequence prevents adopting that shorthand as fact. The further analogy between the normality of a capacity and its necessity cannot establish that absent consciousness and deafness are ethically interchangeable. He does explicitly affirm Miaha's consciousness when Tuan objects. His mixture of classificatory reduction and recognition remains more revealing than a reading that assigns him one consistent attitude toward her personhood.

## A final boundary defended after earlier boundaries were crossed

Nuaza confirms the disappearance of consciousness under perfect harmonization. He reports outwardly ordinary behavior and Miaha's subsequent restoration, absent episodic memory and vague happy ecstasy. These claims establish meaningful fictional experimental history through a participant channel. The apparent tension between consciousness loss and reported ecstasy remains a problem of phenomenology and memory; it cannot be erased by declaring either that nothing occurred or that every ordinary feature of experience continued. [HAR-U015-E001–E002](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U015_PART_III_06_DEEP_READING.md#har-u015-e001); CL105v2/CL106.

He is frightened by what he has helped make. He calls loss of self-awareness in one sense equivalent to death and says a decision affecting billions cannot simply be settled by the researchers. That statement supports a real limit in his position. The arrangement he then describes gives that limit its disturbing scope: dormant networks are already installed in himself and Tuan, while selected WHO and life-government authorities keep the activation decision. He is included as a potential recipient, so this is not a simple exemption of the scientist's own body. He still participates in imposing exposure without asking the others who must live with it. [HAR-U015-E003](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U015_PART_III_06_DEEP_READING.md#har-u015-e003); CL107v2.

His repeated 我々には、その気はない declares that his side presently has no intention to activate. He says social remedies should remain possible despite hidden oppression and self-killing. This is meaningful restraint with a reason attached. It is not a promise that the capacity cannot be used or that its existence has been democratically authorized. The group avoids open ethical sessions because most people would resist losing themselves. Nuaza thus recognizes a likely refusal and helps exclude it from deliberation. He preserves the future possibility of conscious objection while denying its present exercise over the installation. [HAR-U015-E004](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U015_PART_III_06_DEEP_READING.md#har-u015-e004).

Miaha later calls him stubborn and identifies him as the ideologue holding the last boundary in the name of dignity. This comes from his opponent and has weight beyond his own professed restraint, though it remains testimony. His death deprives the mainstream of an influential figure at the very moment her faction wants to make the emergency unbearable. Vashilov's stated preference is capture; death is subsequently accepted as a successful fallback. No specific Miaha order to kill Nuaza is supplied. Ideological opposition, operational objective and individual shooting responsibility stay distinct. [HAR-U015-E009/E012](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U015_PART_III_06_DEEP_READING.md#har-u015-e009); [HAR-U019-E009–E010](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U019_PART_IV_04_DEEP_READING.md#har-u019-e009).

## The body answers before the theory is settled

Nuaza's final action supplies the strongest counterevidence to a portrait of complete paternal indifference. He moves between Vashilov's gun and Tuan. The prose initially encourages an expectation of reciprocal wounds, then identifies the two wounded chests as Vashilov's and Nuaza's. This delayed identification makes Tuan's survival legible through an action she did not first see as her father's choice. His death is definite, and her pulse check fails. The act repeats the childhood rescue under immediate danger, giving the father a bounded continuity across the long gap. [HAR-U015-E011](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U015_PART_III_06_DEEP_READING.md#har-u015-e011); CL113–CL114.

Tuan interprets the movement as love, then considers bodily reflex and inherited kinship. These alternatives matter after the exposition about conduct without experienced choice. The action is observable; its exact private cause remains unavailable. If conscious deliberation cannot be established, its protective value does not disappear. Equally, an effective bodily response does not make thirteen years of absence good parenting. Tuan experiences quiet grief and almost-strangerhood, compared with her stronger reactions to Kian and the earlier reported loss of Miaha. She thanks him and closes his eyes. Gratitude acknowledges what he did without manufacturing matching attachment or forgiveness.

His later memorial authority is relationally specific. Oscar's condolence seems sincere to Tuan and prompts an inference that he really was respected as a leader. Miaha's account of his stubborn defense then lets Tuan reread the humiliated man in the caffeine session. His personal dignity had been injured, yet he continued to believe in conscious dignity. The event has not changed; Tuan's interpretation of it has. Her sadness becomes a ground she finds sufficient for revenge. That sufficiency is a character judgment, not a settled ethical warrant for the subsequent shooting. [HAR-U016-E012](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U016_PART_IV_01_DEEP_READING.md#har-u016-e012); [HAR-U019-E009–E011](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U019_PART_IV_04_DEEP_READING.md#har-u019-e009); CL156–CL157.

Nuaza's death cannot be made the single technical cause of the global ending. His removal helps the factional strategy in Vashilov's account, while the epilogue separately narrates the elders' actual authorization. No complete intermediate deliberation or code transfer is shown. The man who resisted the last step has gone; the capacity he helped build remains. This is a warranted tragic relation without an invented deterministic counterfactual that a living Nuaza certainly would have prevented activation. [HAR-U020-E004](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/HAR-U020_INTERSTICE_AND_EPILOGUE_DEEP_READING.md#har-u020-e004); CL164.

## A character whose contradictions retain their consequence

Nuaza gives Harmony a scientifically articulate defender of limits who helps make an extraordinary power possible. He can see why losing the I is frightening, recognize the experimental work's imperfection and put his body in danger for his daughter. He can also turn endangered children into limit tests, obscure the actual permeability of a brain and accept installation over anticipated public refusal. These acts belong to one person. The last rescue gives the earlier failures emotional weight precisely because it cannot undo them.

The reading's strongest alternative is that catastrophe prevention and adolescent rescue make his choices tragic necessities. The text supports the feared catastrophe and endangered children, and his reluctance distinguishes him from Miaha's desired acceleration. It does not show that the particular secrecy, recruitment and installation were unavoidable, nor that social remedies had been exhausted. A rival reading of pure scientific domination fails against his actual concessions, nonactivation and protection. The more adequate account is of a custodian who draws a morally serious final boundary after helping to move several earlier boundaries beyond other people's reach.

This monograph does not reconstruct a complete Nuaza mind. Ordinary parenting, his marriage from his wife's perspective, everyday collegial practice, the full disappearance chronology and his last intention remain sparse or unavailable. His scientific claims belong to the fictional witness and attributed reports, with no external validation supplied. The substantial evidence supports development in the daughter's understanding and a few repeated paternal actions; it cannot supply a continuous biography or general behavior model.
