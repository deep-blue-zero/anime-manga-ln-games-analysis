# 『変身』 / *Henshin*
## Pre-Finale Audit After Chapter 6
### What futures remain plausible before the ending is allowed to exist

# 0. Purpose

The V2 method requires a **pre-finale audit** after Chapter 6.

Its function is anti-teleological.

The Finale is expected to have enormous retrospective emotional power.

That power must not be allowed to rewrite the state Chapter 6 actually establishes.

The audit therefore asks:

> **Without knowledge of the Finale, where exactly is Saki now, and what futures remain textually plausible?**

This document is not a prediction contest.

It is a frozen record of:

- material state;
- bodily state;
- linguistic state;
- drug state;
- agency;
- relationships;
- institutional attachment;
- future horizon;
- open alternatives;
- and the strongest competing interpretations.

After the Finale is read, this checkpoint remains immutable.

---

# 1. Executive pre-finale finding

The single most important fact is:

> **Chapter 6 ends with a genuine reopening of strategic futurity.**

Saki is in an extraordinarily bad material position.

But she is not at:

> no articulated future.

She has moved from Chapter 5's:

> **「もうずっと此処に居たい」**

an indefinite chemically suspended present,

to Chapter 6's:

> **「産みます」**

> **「クスリも もうしない」**

> **「この子のために…変わってみせる」**

These are future-directed commitments.

Therefore the Finale must inherit:

> **a precarious but real alternative future.**

It may later close that future.

It may validate it.

It may complicate it.

It may reveal obstacles already present.

But it cannot retroactively prove that Chapter 6 never opened it.

---

# 2. Exact material state

At the Chapter 6 endpoint Saki has:

## Housing

- no family home;
- no Hayato apartment;
- no independent tenancy;
- temporary access to an informal encampment/tent settlement.

The camp provides:

- somewhere to sleep;
- some food.

But access exists inside an exploitative sexual economy.

Recommended classification:

> **severe housing insecurity with temporary survival shelter.**

## Money

- no stable cash reserve shown;
- the week's earnings were stolen;
- Hayato expelled her after the payment failure;
- no independent legal/ordinary income stream is established.

## Food

- intermittent food from encampment members.

## Work

- no stable independent employment.
- sexual/survival exchange remains the only currently depicted income/resource mechanism.

## Transportation

- ordinary urban infrastructure exists, but no independent transportation plan is established.

## Phone

- Saki had phone access during Hayato expulsion, but the chapter endpoint does not build a new support network through it.

## Healthcare

- no active prenatal or addiction-related healthcare connection is established by Chapter 6.

Material verdict:

> **Saki has regained a goal before regaining the infrastructure necessary to realize it.**

---

# 3. Exact bodily state

Saki remains visibly continuous with the transformed Chapter 4–5 body.

She remains:

- light-haired;
- tattooed;
- pierced;
- visibly marked by the `HAYATO` inscription;
- physically worn by recent drug/sexual exploitation.

No visual "purity reset" occurs.

Pregnancy status must be recorded precisely:

> **Saki strongly believes she is pregnant based on a bodily sensation she recognizes from the prior pregnancy.**

The Chapter 6 pages do not show:

- a pregnancy test;
- medical confirmation;
- gestational age.

Therefore:

> **pregnancy = `CB-HIGH` / strong character belief, not yet medically verified `TF`.**

This distinction must survive into Finale analysis.

---

# 4. Exact substance-use state

The drug evidence is severe.

By Chapter 6 Saki has:

- repeatedly used unidentified substances;
- moved from oral use to powdered use to injection exposure;
- experienced the injected state as `居場所`;
- chosen drugs over desperately needed cash;
- used drugs to make commercial work easier;
- struggled to flee danger because intoxication felt overwhelmingly rewarding;
- experienced memory loss/severe impairment.

This is strong evidence for:

- escalating use;
- prioritization;
- powerful reward;
- severe impairment;
- dependence-like narrowing.

It remains methodologically inappropriate to assign a formal clinical diagnosis solely from the manga.

At the Chapter 6 endpoint Saki says:

> **「クスリも もうしない」**

This is:

> **an intention to stop.**

It is not:

> evidence that cessation has already succeeded.

The Finale must test, not assume, whether this intention can be realized.

# 5. Exact social state

## Hayato

Materially severed.

He:

- expels Saki;
- blocks her;
- redirects access to his apartment toward another woman.

Saki's psychological attachment may persist as residue, but Chapter 6's final future is not Hayato-authored.

## Family

Not active.

No reconciliation occurs in Chapter 6.

## School

Not active.

No return or intervention occurs.

## Female peers

Not active.

## Obata

Explicitly transactional.

Saki herself recognizes the relationship as a sex/drug exchange.

## Encampment

Provides:

- food;
- shelter;
- social contact.

Also exploits Saki.

This is **care/extraction fusion**, not stable protective care.

## Believed unborn child

Becomes Saki's primary future-oriented relation.

The child is not yet a social actor, but the relationship reorganizes Saki's decisions prospectively.

---

# 6. Exact agency state

Agency must not be summarized globally.

## Strategic agency

**Restored meaningfully at the endpoint.**

Saki decides:

- continue the pregnancy she believes exists;
- refuse repetition of the prior abortion;
- stop drugs;
- raise and care for the child;
- change her life around that objective.

## Tactical agency

Mixed.

Saki can:

- seek resources;
- communicate;
- accept food;
- make decisions.

But severe material insecurity and drug impairment continue to narrow local capacity.

## Exit agency

Very weak materially.

She has no established independent:

- housing;
- income;
- protective adult;
- institution.

Yet the new future gives her a reason to search for exits that previously had little subjective salience.

## Interpretive agency

Meaningfully stronger at the endpoint.

Hayato no longer defines:

> what must happen next.

The encampment men suggest abortion.

Saki rejects their expected interpretation.

This is a significant recovery of narrative authority over her own future.

# 7. Exact linguistic state

Saki remains recognizably continuous with earlier Saki.

She still uses:

- `私`;
- ellipsis;
- self-blame;
- gratitude;
- relational language;
- direct refusal when decision crystallizes.

The key Chapter 6 lexical chains are:

## Disposal

> `捨てられてた`
>
> `捨てられちゃった`
>
> `見捨てたりなんか…しない`

This changes Saki from:

> one who is discarded

to:

> one who refuses to discard another.

## Transformation

> Ch1 `変わりたい`
>
> Ch4 `ハヤトのためなら…いくらでも変われる`
>
> Ch6 `この子のために…変わってみせる`

This is the most important title-level linguistic sequence before the Finale.

## Futurity

> `産む`
>
> `育てる`
>
> `もうしない`
>
> `変わってみせる`

All are prospective.

The Japanese voice therefore provides direct evidence that the future horizon has reopened.

# 8. Exact future horizon

At Chapter 5:

> `F0_NO_ARTICULATED_FUTURE`

Saki wanted the drug state to continue indefinitely.

At Chapter 6 midpoint:

> `F2_IMMEDIATE_SURVIVAL`

She had no home, money, or destination.

At Chapter 6 endpoint:

> **`FR_RESTORED_OR_IMAGINED_FUTURE`**

This is stronger than a vague fantasy.

It has components:

- pregnancy;
- childbirth;
- parenting;
- sobriety;
- self-change;
- non-abandonment.

The future remains materially underdeveloped.

But temporally it is long.

The Finale must therefore begin from:

> **long-horizon intention + low material capacity.**

---

# 9. Which alternatives are objectively open?

The manga does not establish accessibility in detail, but a reader can still identify possible routes:

- prenatal healthcare;
- shelter/social services;
- school or another educational route;
- independent work;
- abuse-support services;
- reconnecting with a trustworthy adult;
- family contact under changed circumstances;
- leaving the exploitative encampment;
- reducing/ending commercial sexual activity;
- drug cessation/treatment.

The text does not show Saki actively accessing these.

Therefore:

> objective possibility ≠ realistic accessibility.

But the existence of unshown institutions cannot be denied simply because the narrative omits them.

---

# 10. Which alternatives are psychologically live for Saki?

The endpoint gives us only one explicit strategic program:

> **the child.**

Saki does not yet articulate:

- school;
- career;
- independent housing;
- friendship;
- medical support.

She articulates:

- give birth;
- care;
- stop drugs;
- change.

Therefore the future is:

> **restored but narrow.**

This is not the expansive Chapter 1 future.

It is a purpose-centered recovery horizon.

---

# 11. Is this self-authorship or transferred telos?

Both readings remain live.

## Strong self-authorship reading

Saki:

- makes the decision herself;
- rejects the advice around her;
- defines the behavior required;
- reintroduces future responsibility;
- rejects drugs as incompatible with that future.

That is real strategic authorship.

## Transferred-telos counterreading

Saki still says:

> change **for the child**.

She does not say:

> I am valuable independent of any relationship.

Therefore the baby may function as another external object around which Saki organizes self-worth.

## Current verdict

> **restored authorship is real at the decision level; the deeper source of self-worth remains unresolved.**

Do not collapse these.

---

# 12. Is the maternal-redemption reading sufficient?

No.

It is relevant.

The chapter clearly uses motherhood/pregnancy to restore:

- responsibility;
- future;
- moral direction.

But Saki does not become:

- visually pure;
- socially reintegrated;
- materially safe.

The tattooed, pierced, homeless Saki makes the decision.

So the ending cannot be reduced to:

> fallen girl becomes pure through motherhood.

A stronger hostile reading is:

> the work may still require motherhood as the first relationship capable of making Saki value a future.

That remains serious.

---

# 13. Is recovery plausible?

**Plausible enough that the Finale must earn any later closure.**

Recovery is not likely in a simple probabilistic sense given Saki's conditions.

But literary plausibility is different from ease.

The chapter provides:

- a goal;
- motivation;
- explicit drug refusal;
- long-term temporal imagination;
- a caregiving principle.

Those are meaningful ingredients.

It does not provide:

- money;
- housing;
- treatment;
- stable support.

So the correct pre-finale formulation is:

> **psychological/strategic turning point without material stabilization.**

---

# 14. Is catastrophe now inevitable?

**No.**

This is the most important freeze.

The Chapter 6 state permits:

- success;
- partial success;
- relapse;
- institutional rescue;
- continued exploitation;
- family reconnection;
- medical complication;
- another form of failure.

Multiple futures remain textually compatible.

Therefore later catastrophe—if any—must be analyzed as:

> **something that happens after a real turning point, not proof that no turning point occurred.**

---

# 15. Misery-machine pressure before the Finale

The strongest current objection is institutional.

The causal chain through Chapters 4–6 is comparatively tight.

But the story continues to depict almost no effective intersection with:

- school authorities;
- social workers;
- shelters;
- law enforcement;
- sustained healthcare;
- extended family.

The Finale must therefore be scrutinized for whether it:

- develops existing constraints;

or

- introduces new catastrophe mainly to prevent the reopened future from becoming viable.

This is not a minor concern.

It is one of the major tests of the ending.

---

# 16. Reader-position state before the Finale

The reader has just been moved from:

> eroticized destitution

to:

> Saki's future-oriented interiority.

At the endpoint the reader is strongly positioned:

> **WITH_SAKI**
>
> and
>
> **CONFRONTING_CONSEQUENCE.**

The Finale will therefore inherit a reader who has been asked to take Saki's plan seriously.

If the Finale later destroys that future, its emotional force will partly depend upon this positioning.

That later force cannot be used backward to deny the authenticity of the current state.

# 18. What the Finale is not allowed to prove retroactively

The Finale may not retroactively prove that:

- Saki never genuinely wanted to stop drugs.
- Saki never genuinely wanted the child.
- Saki's Chapter 6 choice was fake.
- recovery was already impossible.
- the original Chapter 1 makeover was always a mistake.
- Saki never retained agency.
- Hayato was the only person she could ever orient toward.
- motherhood had already "redeemed" Saki.
- the pregnancy was medically confirmed before the Finale unless the Finale itself confirms it.
- Chapter 6 was merely foreshadowing for later tragedy.

Later evidence may show:

- relapse;
- failure;
- new constraints;
- new acts of agency;
- new catastrophe.

Those become **later truths**.

They do not erase Chapter 6 truth.

# 20. Pre-finale freeze thesis

> **Immediately before the Finale, Saki is materially more vulnerable than at any earlier chapter boundary but psychologically more future-oriented than she has been since the early work. Hayato has expelled and blocked her; she has no stable money, no school, no active family, no independent housing, and severe substance-use risk. Her immediate shelter is an exploitative informal encampment. Nothing about the material state resembles recovery.**
>
> **Yet Chapter 6 ends with an unmistakable strategic reversal. Saki believes she is pregnant again, rejects the surrounding expectation of another abortion, says `産みます`, promises `クスリも もうしない`, and defines a new transformation through `この子のために…変わってみせる`. The disposal vocabulary of the chapter also reverses ethically: after being repeatedly `捨てられる`, Saki promises not to `見捨てる` the child. The abandoned person chooses non-abandonment as a future principle.**
>
> **That choice remains ethically and psychologically ambiguous in one respect. Saki still organizes transformation around another person rather than explicitly grounding her own worth in herself. A maternal-redemption reading and a transferred-telos reading therefore remain live. But the child-oriented project is not equivalent to Hayato's captured project: it demands care, sobriety, endurance, and long-term responsibility rather than obedience, commodification, and extraction.**
>
> **The pre-finale state must therefore be frozen as: real strategic authorship + extremely weak material capacity. A genuine alternative future has reopened. If the Finale closes that future, the ending must be judged by how it closes it. It is not permitted to claim, after the fact, that the future was never open.**
