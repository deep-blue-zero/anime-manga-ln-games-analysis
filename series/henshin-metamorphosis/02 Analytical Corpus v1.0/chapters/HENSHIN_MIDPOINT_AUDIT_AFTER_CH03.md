# 『変身』 / *Henshin*
## Midpoint Audit After Chapter 3
### Prospective checkpoint before Chapter 4

# 1. Purpose

The V2 method requires a checkpoint after Chapter 3 so that the first half of the work can be frozen **before** Chapter 4 changes the interpretive horizon.

This document therefore uses only evidence from Chapters 1–3.

Its purpose is not to summarize those chapters again.

It asks:

- which hypotheses have survived;
- which have become stronger;
- which require revision;
- what Saki's current state actually is;
- what futures remain possible;
- where the "misery machine" objection currently stands;
- and what must not be imported backward from later chapters.

---

# 2. Midpoint structural thesis

The first three chapters produce a coherent three-stage movement:

## Chapter 1 — Transformation opens the world

Saki changes herself.

The transformation produces:

- family warmth;
- female friendship;
- social possibility;
- romantic/sexual attention.

Risk enters.

Fate does not.

## Chapter 2 — Maintaining the transformed self acquires hidden costs

Recognition becomes economic.

Saki begins using:

- money;
- status goods;
- concealed sexual economy;
- Hayato's drug-linked sexual environment

to maintain her place in the visible social world.

The visible and hidden selves remain separate.

## Chapter 3 — Hidden causes become public character judgments

The hotel photograph crosses the boundary.

Blackmail creates coerced behavior.

Coerced behavior becomes:

- rumor;
- reputation;
- new recordings;
- peer hostility.

Family abuse then repeats the same epistemic structure.

The first-half thesis is therefore:

> **Saki's initial problem was invisibility. By the midpoint, her problem is that she has become hyper-visible in forms she cannot control.**

She wanted:

> to be seen.

She now lives among:

> photographs;
> recordings;
> rumors;
> body judgments;
> family classifications;
> and relationship categories

produced by other people.

---

# 3. Is recognition still the central driver?

**Yes, but its function has changed.**

## Chapter 1

Recognition is desired.

It tells Saki:

> maybe I can like myself.

## Chapter 2

Recognition becomes conditional and expensive.

It appears to require:

- correct appearance;
- consumer fluency;
- money.

## Chapter 3

Recognition becomes punitive.

Others recognize Saki through:

- images;
- rumor;
- coerced appearances;
- sexual categories.

The midpoint formulation should therefore be revised from:

> Saki wants recognition.

to:

> **Saki wants recognition she can trust, while the social world increasingly recognizes versions of her produced without her authorship.**

That distinction should govern the second half.

---

# 4. Is self-authorship narrowing?

**Sharply.**

The first-half movement is:

> **self-authored transformation**
>
> → **co-authored/performed identity**
>
> → **externally authored social ontology.**

Saki still authors:

- concealment;
- bargaining;
- help-seeking;
- whom she contacts.

But other people increasingly author:

- what her body means;
- what her social behavior means;
- why she is sexually active;
- whether she is victim or initiator;
- which relationships remain available.

The strongest first-half agency finding is:

> **choice survives longer than authorship.**

---

# 5. Are substances changing agency?

**Yes, but Chapter 3 does not materially advance the pharmacological evidence.**

Through Chapter 2 we have:

- repeated Hayato-supplied use;
- active seeking;
- altered-self language;
- situation-specific craving.

Chapter 3 adds little direct substance evidence.

Therefore the midpoint must not exaggerate.

What changes is **contextual risk**.

At Chapter 3's endpoint:

- Hayato is sole perceived refuge;
- Hayato is also the person associated with drug supply.

The concentration of relationship, shelter, and substance access around one person is prospectively dangerous.

But any stronger claim belongs to later evidence if it appears.

---

# 6. What kind of power does Hayato currently possess?

At the midpoint Hayato's power is primarily:

## Emotional

Saki believes she loves him.

## Interpretive

Earlier he repeatedly defined:

- risk;
- sexual response;
- what kind of girl Saki is.

## Chemical

He is the established source associated with the repeated drug-sex routine.

## Prospective material

He has just offered refuge after Saki loses home.

This is the first moment when housing may become attached to the same relationship.

## What Hayato does **not** control

Chapter 3 does not show him causing:

- school blackmail;
- female-peer hostility;
- paternal abuse;
- maternal rejection;
- father's unemployment.

The dependency is being concentrated around him by **distributed failure**, not proven mastermind design.

---

# 7. Has the social network contracted?

**Yes. This is a major revision from Chapter 2.**

After Chapter 2:

> numerical network broad / disclosure network fragmented.

After Chapter 3:

> actual network contraction.

## Lost or hostile

- female peers;
- many male peers;
- father;
- mother;
- home.

## Still potentially present but not psychologically live

- school adults;
- outside institutions.

## Perceived remaining attachment

- Hayato.

This is the first true social-network collapse.

---

# 8. Is "taxonomy becomes chronology" holding?

**Strongly.**

The evidence through Chapter 3 is now substantial.

Chapter 1's drug persists into Chapter 2.

Chapter 2's photograph creates Chapter 3 blackmail.

Blackmail creates new recordings.

Blackmail also changes Saki's availability to friends.

That changes reputation.

The father's abuse becomes repeated domestic infrastructure rather than reset.

Maternal discovery becomes housing loss.

Housing loss creates the appeal to Hayato.

This is more than repeated erotic novelty.

It is causal residue.

The hypothesis should therefore be upgraded:

> **`HIGH` confidence through Chapter 3.**

---

# 9. But has the misery-machine objection strengthened?

**Yes—dramatically.**

The strongest causally persuasive sequence is:

> photograph → blackmail → recording → rumor → peer collapse.

The weaker area is:

> father suddenly becomes incestuous abuser.

The mother's reversal is emotionally coherent as betrayal but psychologically abrupt.

Therefore the midpoint judgment should be:

> **The work is currently strongest at accumulating consequences after a scenario is introduced, and weaker at making every new catastrophic scenario equally organic in its introduction.**

That distinction should be preserved.

---

# 10. What has Japanese voice newly revealed?

Three major gains.

## A. Visual change does not equal linguistic replacement

Through Chapter 2, sober Saki's language remains recognizably continuous.

## B. Kinship vocabulary survives abuse

In Chapter 3, `お父さん`, `お母さん`, and `家族` remain central.

Saki's language continues to insist on expected relational categories.

## C. Option collapse becomes grammatical

`もうハヤトしかいないの` is the strongest first-half linguistic marker of narrowed alternatives.

It states not:

> I like Hayato most,

but:

> **there is nobody except Hayato.**

The Japanese pass has therefore materially strengthened the self-authorship analysis rather than overturning it.

---

# 11. Can Saki still exit the trajectory?

## Objectively

**Yes.**

Possible external routes remain untested.

## Subjectively

**Much less.**

Saki has just tried the healthiest route she knows:

> mother.

It failed catastrophically.

That matters.

A later analyst must not say:

> she never sought help.

Chapter 3 proves the opposite.

At the midpoint the correct formulation is:

> **Saki can still act, but her evidence about whom she can trust has become catastrophically distorted by actual betrayal.**

# 14. Prospective hypotheses carried into Chapter 4

The following may be carried forward.

They must remain hypotheses.

1. **Hayato dependency concentration**
   - shelter may join drugs/romance as another dependency.

2. **Financial exploitation risk**
   - debt + `金づる` + Saki's vulnerability create strong warning.

3. **Decision-environment capture**
   - Saki may continue choosing locally while Hayato increasingly authors the larger conditions.

4. **Image/reputation residue**
   - school recording/photo system may remain active even if Saki leaves school.

5. **Family rupture**
   - home is presently unavailable; reconciliation is not established.

6. **Linguistic continuity**
   - Saki may continue sounding recognizably like herself even as circumstances worsen.

7. **Misery-machine pressure**
   - if every new chapter adds another independent catastrophe without causal integration, the tragedy reading should weaken.

8. **Pornographic reset refusal**
   - if Chapter 4 begins from housing loss, the no-reset hypothesis strengthens further.

# 15. What Chapter 4 is not allowed to rewrite

The following are frozen.

- Chapter 1's makeover was genuinely self-authored.
- Chapter 1's female-friendship pathway was genuinely healthy at that horizon.
- Chapter 2's mother was genuinely a plausible support figure.
- Chapter 2 ended with multiple live alternatives.
- Saki did not choose school blackmail.
- Saki did not consent to paternal assault.
- Saki actively sought her mother's help before turning to Hayato.
- The mother refused Saki's attempted explanation.
- Saki was expelled from home.
- Hayato did not cause every Chapter 3 catastrophe.
- At Chapter 3's end, Saki **perceives** Hayato as the only remaining person.
- The reader knows Hayato categorizes her as `セフレ`, not girlfriend.
- Hayato's specific future plan for Saki is not yet established.

---

# 16. Midpoint freeze thesis

> **Through three chapters, 『変身』 has moved from self-authored visibility to externally authored legibility. Saki's makeover works: it produces friends, family warmth, social opportunity, and the possibility of a new life. The tragedy begins when recognition becomes a system in which other people increasingly decide what Saki's body and behavior mean. By Chapter 3, blackmail can produce sexual behavior, photographs and recordings can remove the coercive context from that behavior, peers can turn the resulting appearance into a reputation, a father can violate Saki while judging her sexual morality, and a mother can mistake paternal abuse for filial seduction.**
>
> **The midpoint therefore supports the proposition that Saki loses strategic authorship before she loses the capacity to act. She continues bargaining, refusing, helping, escaping, and seeking care. The most important fact is that she does seek care: when school collapses, she runs to the mother who had promised to be on her side. Only after that route fails does she say `もうハヤトしかいないの`.**
>
> **The causal-tragedy reading is now strong, especially in the photograph → blackmail → recording → rumor → ostracism chain. But the misery-machine objection has also become serious because the father's incestuous assault and the mother's abrupt victim-blaming reversal are much less patiently prepared. The second half must therefore test two competing possibilities at once: that the work is successfully converting pornographic scenario residue into tragedy, and that the need for escalating pornographic scenarios is beginning to overdetermine the tragedy itself.**
