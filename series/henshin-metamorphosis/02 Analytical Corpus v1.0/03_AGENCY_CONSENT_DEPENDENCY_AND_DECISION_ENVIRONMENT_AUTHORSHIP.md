# 『変身』 / *Henshin*
## Agency, Consent, Dependency, and Decision-Environment Authorship

# 1. Executive thesis

The central autonomy problem in 『変身』 is not whether Saki ever chooses.

She chooses constantly.

She chooses:

- to change her appearance;
- to accept friendship;
- to see Hayato;
- to enter compensated dating;
- to conceal information;
- to help her family;
- to contact Hayato after expulsion;
- to assume debt;
- to recruit clients;
- to disclose pregnancy;
- to reject another abortion;
- to save money for a child.

The deeper question is:

> **How much of the life in which those choices become necessary, rewarding, or apparently unavoidable is still authored by Saki?**

The full-work answer is:

> **Saki's tactical agency survives and often becomes more sophisticated while strategic authorship collapses.**

This is why the work cannot be accurately described either as:

> a girl who simply keeps choosing badly,

or:

> a girl with no agency.

Both erase the most important structure.

# 2. Action is not authorship

A person may physically initiate an act without authoring:

- the location;
- the information;
- the dependency;
- the threat;
- the price;
- the drug state;
- the meaning assigned afterward.

This distinction becomes increasingly important.

## Chapter 2

Saki voluntarily enters a compensated-dating meeting.

But the adult later authors:

- transport;
- hotel destination;
- changed bargain;
- cash visibility;
- escalation beyond an explicit boundary.

## Chapter 4

Saki volunteers to help Hayato repay debt.

But Hayato and Obata author:

- the drug environment;
- the debt framing;
- the sex-work mechanism;
- the quota;
- the client system;
- the body modifications;
- the work schedule.

## Chapter 5

Saki actively recruits and bargains with clients.

But the larger purpose remains:

- Hayato's debt;
- Hayato's approval;
- housing dependency;
- abortion cost;
- lack of ordinary alternatives.

Therefore:

> **initiative is not a reliable proxy for autonomy.**

# 3. Strategic agency versus tactical agency

The most useful full-work distinction is between:

## Strategic agency

Can Saki decide what kind of life she is building and what her labor, body, money, and future are for?

## Tactical agency

Can Saki make local choices inside the already existing environment?

The trajectory is:

| Phase | Strategic agency | Tactical agency |
|---|---|---|
| Chapter 1 | high | high/mixed |
| Chapter 2 | substantial but conditional | substantial |
| Chapter 3 | sharply reduced | active through bargaining, escape, help-seeking |
| Chapter 4 | low | increasingly competent |
| Chapter 5 | very low | highly self-regulating |
| Chapter 6 | restored at endpoint | materially constrained |
| Finale | sustained partially, then destroyed by attack | partial under dependence; absent during assault |

The paradox is clearest in Chapter 4.

Saki becomes capable of:

- quoting prices;
- managing clients;
- communicating schedules;
- collecting payment.

Yet the strategic objective is no longer independently examined.

She works:

> for Hayato's debt.

She changes:

> for Hayato's preference.

Competence has become a more efficient form of dependency.

# 4. Refusal is not one capacity

The Japanese reread reveals a developmental history of refusal.

## Internal refusal

> **「今断りたかったのに…出来なかった」**

Saki knows she wants to refuse but cannot produce effective action.

## Procedural knowledge without speech

In the client's car she knows:

> this is when I should say something firmly.

She cannot.

## Explicit boundary

Later she says:

> **「入れるのはダメだって」**

The client understands and ignores it.

## Kinship refusal

During paternal assault:

> `お父さん`;
> `家族`;
> stop.

She uses relationship language as resistance.

## Attempted testimony

Saki begins:

> `お父さんが—`

The mother terminates the account.

## Reproductive refusal

At the Chapter 6 endpoint:

> **「絶対イヤ」**

becomes:

> **「産む」**

Here refusal successfully opens a strategic future.

The full-work conclusion is:

> **Saki's problem is not simply that she cannot say no. It is that her internal state repeatedly fails to become socially authoritative.**

# 5. Bodily response and consent

The work repeatedly places direct refusal beside stylized bodily response.

Other men then interpret physiological reaction as:

- proof of consent;
- proof of sexual identity;
- proof that prior refusal does not matter.

The manga itself sometimes eroticizes the same contradiction.

The evidence hierarchy must remain:

1. expressed will;
2. decision environment;
3. impairment;
4. behavior;
5. bodily response.

A bodily response can be narratively real without being consent.

This is especially important in:

- Chapter 2's boundary violation;
- Chapter 3's paternal assault;
- Chapter 5's network coercion;
- the Finale's attack.

# 6. Impairment as environmental authorship

The substances are not passive background.

They are repeatedly introduced or supplied by actors who benefit from the resulting state.

## Hayato

- introduces first pill;
- normalizes risk;
- repeatedly pairs drug and sex;
- brings Saki to stronger-drug network;
- facilitates escalation after warning.

## Obata/network

- controls powder/injection;
- trades access;
- continues sexual use under severe impairment.

The full-work development is:

> imposed impairment → repeated reward → work technology → chemical belonging → prioritization over money → inability to function without use → final self-destructive use.

The drug state changes not only what Saki does but what she experiences as desirable.

That is recursive decision-environment authorship:

> environment creates altered preference; altered preference reproduces environment.

# 7. Dependency is plural

Saki's constraints cannot be reduced to romance or addiction alone.

Her later choices are shaped by overlapping dependencies:

- **recognition** — praise can make self-liking possible;
- **romantic attachment** — Hayato becomes sole perceived person;
- **housing** — Hayato's apartment resolves homelessness conditionally;
- **money** — status, debt, abortion, survival, child savings;
- **work** — client system becomes only represented income;
- **drugs** — reward, work coping, belonging;
- **future** — child becomes reason for sobriety and saving.

Dependency concentration is most severe when multiple needs route through Hayato.

Leaving him would threaten:

- relationship;
- shelter;
- drug access;
- work;
- debt meaning;
- identity.

That is why the same outward act—contacting or obeying Hayato—has a different autonomy profile in Chapter 1 and Chapter 4.

# 8. Hayato as decision-environment author

Hayato's role develops from interpretation to administration.

## Early

He defines:

- alcohol as harmless;
- drugs as normal;
- bodily response as identity;
- attention as affection.

## Middle

He controls:

- housing;
- drug access;
- debt purpose;
- client management;
- body modifications;
- ownership meaning.

## Late

He no longer needs continuous presence.

Saki anticipates:

- his anger;
- his price expectations;
- his definition of good performance.

She defends him from outsiders.

The system has become internal.

But Hayato does not author every catastrophe.

School blackmail, father, mother, and final attackers have independent causal roles.

The most accurate formulation is:

> **distributed failure concentrates dependency around Hayato; Hayato then actively administers the dependency he did not solely create.**

# 9. Reproductive autonomy

The two pregnancies create a useful comparison.

## First pregnancy

Saki discloses voluntarily.

Hayato immediately says:

> abort tomorrow.

He romanticizes the directive by promising that he wants Saki eventually to bear **his** child.

Saki assents:

> **「ハヤトの言うとおりにする」**

This is not physical force.

It is not independent reproductive deliberation either.

Classification:

> **pressured + dependency-shaped.**

## Second believed pregnancy

Hayato is absent.

Men around Saki assume abortion.

Saki rejects them:

> `絶対イヤ`;
> `産む`;
> `育てる`;
> `クスリももうしない`.

This is the strongest strategic act since Chapter 1.

Classification at preference level:

> **substantially autonomous.**

Material feasibility remains extremely weak.

The distinction demonstrates that relationally organized purpose is not automatically captured purpose.

The ethical content of the telos matters.

# 10. Restored agency and its limits

The Chapter 6 child-oriented project restores:

- long time horizon;
- self-authored refusal;
- care obligation;
- sobriety intention;
- non-abandonment principle.

The Finale proves that it changes behavior:

- advanced pregnancy;
- saved money;
- reduced/rationed drugs;
- changed spending priorities.

Therefore the project is real.

But purpose is not treatment.

Saki cannot will away:

- homelessness;
- dependence-like substance use;
- unsafe labor;
- absent institutions.

The recovery branch is strategically genuine and materially underpowered.

# 11. Final drug use

After the final attack, Saki:

- believes the baby is dying/lost;
- loses the savings;
- is severely injured;
- is alone;
- calls herself weak;
- says she could not become a mother;
- decides to use all remaining drug.

The narrative strongly implies a deliberate self-destructive and likely lethal overdose.

But Saki never explicitly says:

> I want to die.

The correct classification is:

> **behaviorally initiated by Saki under an extremely narrowed, impaired, injured, dependent, and horizonless decision environment; lethal intent `SI-HIGH`, not narrative `TF`.**

The author afterword later confirms death as intended outcome.

That retrospective intent does not simplify the diegetic agency problem.

# 12. Strongest defense of Saki's remaining agency

Saki is never merely an inert object.

She:

- initiates change;
- learns social roles;
- notices manipulation;
- states causal truth;
- sets boundaries;
- bargains;
- seeks help;
- works;
- saves;
- forms future commitments.

These facts matter because sympathy should not require erasing agency.

# 13. Strongest argument for autonomy collapse

Her local actions increasingly serve systems whose larger purposes she did not independently choose.

- images define reputation;
- Hayato defines debt and body;
- markets define value;
- drugs define belonging;
- homelessness defines exchange;
- stigma defines deservingness.

The fact that Saki can function inside these systems does not mean she authors them.

# 14. Final autonomy thesis

> **『変身』 distinguishes freedom from motion. Saki remains active almost until the end, but activity occurs inside increasingly authored environments. Her most tragic competence is the ability to adapt to each new constraint so successfully that adaptation itself becomes evidence that she chose the constraint. The late pregnancy project matters because it breaks that pattern: Saki defines a purpose against the expectations around her and partially acts on it. The Finale's new violence then closes the branch materially, not logically. Saki's final self-blame is therefore an interpretation the full causal record does not support.**
