# 『変身』 / *Henshin*
## Saki State Ledger
### What changes, what remains, and who increasingly authors the meaning of change

# 1. Purpose

This ledger reconstructs Yoshida Saki's state at every chapter boundary using the immutable Japanese-primary chapter freezes.

It is not a plot summary.

Its job is to preserve the differences among:

- outward appearance;
- inward continuity;
- self-authored change;
- socially conditioned performance;
- externally imposed classification;
- material security;
- substance exposure;
- sexual and reproductive autonomy;
- relationship dependency;
- institutional attachment;
- Japanese voice;
- and the distance into the future Saki can still imagine herself existing.

The governing longitudinal finding is:

> **Saki's capacity to act, adapt, learn, and transform survives far longer than her ability to choose the strategic purpose of those actions.**

Her tragedy is not best described as a linear movement from innocence to passivity. It is a movement from **strategic authorship** to **tactical competence inside externally authored systems**, followed by a late, materially precarious return of strategic purpose.

# 3. Chapter 1 — self-authored visibility

## Opening state

Saki begins as a socially isolated middle-school graduate who has converted fear of ridicule into withdrawal and then withdrawal into defensive contempt. Her first decisive act is not sexual. It is self-reflective:

> **「変わりたい」** — I want to change.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0008 / printed p.005`

She asks her mother for help with makeup. The first bodily metamorphosis is therefore:

- initiated by Saki;
- collaborative rather than coercive;
- oriented toward a desired social future;
- materially supported by family.

## End state

The makeover genuinely works. Saki obtains:

- renewed maternal intimacy;
- positive paternal recognition;
- female-peer invitations;
- ordinary plans for weekends and shopping;
- proof that she can speak with other people.

But she experiences the new persona as partly worn rather than fully integrated:

> **「普通の人を装っている」** — presenting/disguising herself as an ordinary person.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0010 / p.007`

Recognition becomes a regulator of self-worth:

> **「なんか、自分が好きになれそう」** — somehow I feel I might be able to like myself.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0013 / p.010`

The chapter ends with serious predatory risk, first substance exposure, and degraded local agency, but not fate. Saki still has a wide social and institutional field.

## State verdict

> **Outward transformation is rapid; inward linguistic and relational continuity remains strong.**

# 4. Chapter 2 — maintaining the transformed self becomes expensive

Saki remains socially successful. Her female friendships have not yet failed. The problem changes from access to maintenance.

She worries about:

- conversational fluency;
- fashion knowledge;
- sounding poor;
- possessing the visible objects that allow her to blend into the group.

The line:

> **「これで女子の中に溶け込める…かな」** — maybe now I can blend in with the girls.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0063 / p.060`

shows that money and consumer goods have become technologies of social camouflage.

At the same time, the drug relationship with Hayato becomes recurrent. Saki describes the altered state as frightening:

> **「自分が自分じゃなくなるようで…怖い」** — it feels as though I am no longer myself.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0035 / p.032`

Yet she actively seeks more. This is the beginning of state-specific linguistic metamorphosis: intoxicated Saki sounds more disinhibited, while sober school Saki remains recognizably hesitant and socially vigilant.

The compensated-dating encounter produces a crucial agency distinction. Saki enters the meeting voluntarily and recognizes the broad category, but the adult changes:

- the concrete bargain;
- the destination;
- the isolation;
- the monetary incentive.

Saki eventually states an explicit sexual boundary, and the client violates it. Her problem is no longer only that refusal fails to become speech. It is that speech fails to govern another person's conduct.

The chapter ends with:

- a hidden sexual/economic world;
- shame-driven non-disclosure;
- status goods acquired from that hidden world;
- photographic evidence crossing into school.

## State verdict

> **Saki's visible life is still functional, but a hidden economy has formed to finance and chemically regulate her place within it.**

# 5. Chapter 3 — externally authored social ontology

Chapter 3 shifts the primary site of metamorphosis away from appearance.

Other people now transform what Saki's behavior means.

- blackmailers: sexually available;
- female peers: conceited/promiscuous;
- father: adult woman / younger version of mother;
- mother: initiator/seducer;
- class: publicly writable sexual identity;
- Hayato: `セフレ`.

The key mechanism is **interpretive dispossession**:

> coercion produces behavior → behavior circulates without context → observers infer character → character judgment destroys support.

Saki's own voice remains causally precise. She knows she is appeasing blackmail:

> **「言うこと聞いてれば上手くやり過ごせそうだ」** — if I do what they say, maybe I can get through it.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0064 / p.061`

She repeatedly preserves kinship categories during paternal assault:

> **「家族でそんな事しちゃダメ」** — family must not do this.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0074 / p.071`

She actively seeks her mother as the healthiest support route available. The mother refuses the attempted account and expels her.

At the endpoint Saki says:

> **「もうハヤトしかいないの」** — there is nobody except Hayato.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0093 / p.090`

This is the first grammatical map of perceived option collapse.

## State verdict

> **Saki retains tactical agency, causal knowledge, and relational continuity while losing school safety, family safety, housing, and confidence that anyone except Hayato will receive her account.**

# 6. Chapter 4 — captured metamorphosis

Chapter 4 converts Chapter 3's narrowed attachment into an economic and bodily system.

Saki lives with Hayato and no longer attends school. Her waking reflex still expects school, but she narrates the new arrangement as:

> **「大人の世界に入っているんだ」** — I have entered the adult world.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0095 / p.092`

Adulthood becomes self-disciplinary language:

> **「大人になれ」** — become an adult.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0098 / p.095`

The debt scene creates a second `しかない` contraction:

> **「私が出来る事…これくらいしかない」** — this is about the only thing I can do.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0099 / p.096`

Saki's adaptability is redirected toward:

- Hayato's debt;
- client work;
- drug use;
- Hayato-directed appearance changes;
- proprietary marking.

Her body becomes a dual asset:

- romantic property within Hayato's ownership story;
- income-producing commodity within the client market.

The final title-level line is:

> **「ハヤトのためなら…いくらでも変われる」** — for Hayato, I can change as much as necessary.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0128 / p.125`

## State verdict

> **The capacity for transformation survives, but its telos has been captured. Tactical competence rises as strategic autonomy falls.**

# 7. Chapter 5 — self-regulating exploitation and chemical belonging

Chapter 5 shows that Hayato's system can operate in his absence.

Saki:

- recruits clients;
- monitors prices;
- anticipates Hayato's anger;
- hides poor performance;
- defends him against an outsider's exploitation diagnosis;
- generalizes obedience into commercial language.

The reproductive state becomes causally important. Saki becomes pregnant, cannot identify paternity, and terminates the pregnancy after Hayato immediately supplies the expected outcome and romanticizes it through a promised future child of his.

The first abortion then becomes another cost of the labor system.

A returning client tells Saki she has lowered her own:

> **「価値」** — value.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0139 / p.136`

Saki eventually promises:

> **「何でも言うこと聞くから」** — I'll do anything you say.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0140 / p.137`

The obedience grammar has become portable.

The chapter ends after a new injection produces severe impairment and an unusually spacious subjective sequence. Saki experiences:

> **「心地良さ」** — comfort;

> **「もうずっと此処に居たい」** — I want to stay here forever;

> **「私が求めていた居場所」** — the place where I had been seeking to belong.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0167-0168 / pp.164-165`

## State verdict

> **The social project of belonging has been replaced by chemically simulated belonging, while Saki's body becomes transferable value within a network larger than Hayato.**

# 8. Chapter 6 — disposal and restored strategic futurity

Chapter 6 begins with Saki literally discarded among garbage:

> **「またこんな所に捨てられてた」** — I was dumped somewhere like this again.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0170 / p.167`

She is robbed of money and drugs. Obata offers cash or drugs. Saki knows she needs cash for Hayato but chooses the drug.

This demonstrates that chemical reward can now outrank the Hayato-centered economic objective.

Hayato then claims Saki's earnings as:

> **「俺の金」** — my money.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0179 / p.176`

When Saki's independent drug use competes with his financial interests, he calls her a junkie, expels her, and blocks her.

Saki interprets the expulsion as:

> **「捨てられちゃった」** — I was discarded.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0183 / p.180`

The HAYATO mark remains after the relationship's material meaning collapses.

Homelessness reduces Saki's horizon to survival. An encampment offers food and shelter through another care/extraction arrangement.

Then pregnancy recognition interrupts the survival routine.

The people around Saki assume another abortion. Saki rejects it:

> **「絶対イヤ」** — absolutely not.

> **「産む」** — I will give birth.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0204 / p.201`

She says:

> **「見捨てたりなんか…しない」** — I will not abandon the child.

> **「クスリも もうしない」** — I will not use drugs anymore.

> **「この子のために…変わってみせる」** — for this child, I will change.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0205 / p.202`

## State verdict

> **The abandoned person constructs a future around non-abandonment. Strategic authorship returns before material capacity does.**

# 9. Finale — partial recovery, contingent destruction, imagined rebirth

The Finale proves that the Chapter 6 project was behaviorally real.

Saki:

- carries the pregnancy into an advanced state;
- saves money for the child;
- tries to reduce drug exposure;
- rations supply;
- treats earnings as her money for a future she chose.

The relevant economic language is:

> **「お金を無駄に使えない」** — I cannot waste money.

> **「この子のために…使うんだ」** — I will use it for this child.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0222 / p.219`

When strangers take the money, Saki says:

> **「私のお金なの」** — it is my money.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0224 / p.221`

This is a partial recovery of economic authorship after Hayato's `俺の金`.

The new attackers then impose another externally authored biography:

- homeless appearance = thief;
- sexualized appearance = social trash;
- marginality = unfit mother;
- pregnancy = disposable future.

After they steal the savings and violently target the pregnancy, Saki internalizes the world's causal model:

> **「私…弱い人間だから」** — because I am a weak person.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0238 / p.235`

The environment disappears from the explanation.

Her final all-at-once use of the remaining drug is a likely intentional self-destructive overdose, though the narrative pages alone stop short of medical confirmation.

The imagined Hana future gives Saki a different temporal state:

- suffering can be called `昔` — the past;
- self-hatred can be remembered rather than inhabited;
- care is non-transactional;
- transformation becomes:

> **「生まれ変われた」** — I was able to be reborn.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0244 / p.241`

## State verdict

> **Saki's future project partially works before new violence destroys its material basis. The final imagined metamorphosis is survival long enough for pain to become biography.**

# 10. Longitudinal continuity

The most important continuity is not hairstyle, clothing, or sexual conduct.

Across every state, Saki remains oriented toward:

- belonging;
- recognition;
- usefulness;
- care;
- a relationship in which she is not abandoned;
- the hope that change can make life livable.

Her adaptability is morally neutral and narratively central.

It permits:

- the Chapter 1 makeover;
- social learning;
- client competence;
- survival bargaining;
- attempts at motherhood;
- imagined adult integration.

The same capacity can be:

- self-authored;
- socially conditioned;
- captured;
- economically exploited;
- redirected toward care.

The tragedy is therefore not that Saki becomes a wholly different person.

It is that:

> **the same durable needs and capacities are repeatedly given environments that convert them into instruments of somebody else's purpose.**

# 11. Longitudinal discontinuities

Several changes are nonetheless qualitative.

## A. Social field

> expansion → fragmentation → contraction → exploitative repopulation.

## B. Agency scale

> strategic authorship → constrained strategy → tactical survival → captured tactical competence → restored strategic intention.

## C. Money

> status tool → hidden income → Hayato debt → abortion/debt cost → stolen recovery savings.

## D. Drugs

> imposed experiment → repeated sexual reward → adult-world membership → chemical `居場所` → work coping/prioritization → dependence-like obstacle → apparent final escape.

## E. Body

> self-authored presentation → socially maintained image → externally interpreted evidence → Hayato-marked property → market asset → discarded/homeless body → pregnant future body → injured body → imagined adult body.

## F. Future

> ordinary adolescence → conditional social future → immediate survival → outsourced near future → timeless intoxicated present → child-oriented restoration → contingent destruction → imagined counterfactual rebirth.

# 12. Final Saki-state thesis

> **Saki's full-work metamorphosis is not one fall from purity. It is a sequence of authorship regimes. She first changes herself to enter society; then changes to maintain recognition; then is classified by images and rumors; then changes for Hayato; then self-regulates inside his economy; then loses every material support; then tries to change for a child; and finally imagines the rebirth that survival might have made possible.**
>
> **The Japanese voice is the strongest evidence that these are states of one continuous person. `私` remains. Hesitation remains. The need to belong remains. The ability to care remains. What changes most radically is not whether Saki has desires, but who supplies the environment in which those desires become actionable and what social meaning is assigned to the resulting behavior.**
