# 『変身』 / *Henshin*
## Agency, Consent, and Decision-Environment Ledger

# 1. Governing distinctions

The work repeatedly defeats binary descriptions such as:

> Saki chose it.

or:

> Saki had no agency.

The required distinctions are:

- **action** — what Saki physically does;
- **expressed preference** — what she says she wants;
- **experienced desire** — what she positively values or enjoys;
- **consent** — what she agrees to in the relevant moment;
- **impairment** — whether intoxication or incapacity degrades decision-making;
- **perceived alternatives** — what Saki believes she can do;
- **actual alternatives** — what objectively may exist;
- **material accessibility** — whether an alternative is realistically usable;
- **decision-environment authorship** — who created the location, incentives, information, threats, dependencies, and meanings that shape the choice;
- **strategic authorship** — who determines the larger purpose of action;
- **tactical agency** — what Saki can still negotiate within that purpose.

The most important full-work finding is:

> **Saki's local initiative often increases while her strategic authorship decreases.**

This is why client recruitment, price negotiation, self-management, and even active requests cannot be interpreted without the surrounding system.

# 2. Classification key

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `A1_AUTONOMOUS` | substantially self-authored choice with meaningful knowledge and accessible alternatives |
| `A2_UNEQUAL_BUT_VOLUNTARY` | voluntary action inside a real but non-determinative power asymmetry |
| `A3_PRESSURED` | social or interpersonal pressure materially affects assent |
| `A4_DEPENDENCY_SHAPED` | emotional, relational, chemical, or institutional dependency strongly structures preference |
| `A5_IMPAIRED` | intoxication or incapacity materially degrades decision-making |
| `A6_BARGAINING_WITHIN_COERCION` | local negotiation inside a non-voluntary larger arrangement |
| `A7_MATERIALLY_CONSTRAINED` | survival, housing, money, food, or similar necessities sharply narrow alternatives |
| `A8_MANIPULATIVELY_ENGINEERED` | another actor controls information, location, sequencing, or incentives to manufacture assent |
| `A9_EXPLICITLY_COERCED` | threat, blackmail, or disregard of explicit refusal structures the act |
| `A10_NON_CHOICE` | force, incapacity, or total control removes meaningful choice |
| `UA_UNRESOLVED` | available evidence cannot sustain a single classification |

These are analytical categories, not jurisdiction-specific legal conclusions.

# 4. Longitudinal agency patterns

## 4.1 Strategic authorship contracts before behavior stops

The sequence is:

> **Chapter 1:** choose a life project.  
> **Chapter 2:** choose means increasingly shaped by status and dependency.  
> **Chapter 3:** bargain to survive others' systems.  
> **Chapter 4:** become competent at a Hayato-authored objective.  
> **Chapter 5:** self-regulate that objective without Hayato present.  
> **Chapter 6:** lose the system, then choose a new future.  
> **Finale:** partially enact that future before external violence destroys it.

This is why high initiative does not prove high autonomy.

## 4.2 Refusal has a developmental trajectory

Saki's refusal history is not one trait.

1. **Internal refusal that fails to become speech**  
   `今断りたかったのに…出来なかった`.

2. **Knowledge that she should object, but speech still fails**  
   car sequence.

3. **Clear spoken boundary that is understood and violated**  
   Chapter 2 client.

4. **Repeated kinship-based refusal under force**  
   father.

5. **Attempted disclosure that listener terminates**  
   mother.

6. **Attempted exit from network environment that men ignore**  
   Chapter 5.

7. **Reproductive refusal that successfully becomes a strategic decision**  
   `絶対イヤ` → `産む`.

The governing conclusion is:

> **Saki's problem is not simply an inability to say no. It is the repeated failure of her internal state to become socially effective.**

## 4.3 Bodily response never supersedes expressed will

The work repeatedly juxtaposes:

- direct refusal;
- involuntary or stylized bodily response;
- other characters interpreting response as identity or consent.

The evidence hierarchy must remain:

> **expressed will and environmental context govern consent analysis.**

Pornographic response conventions are formal evidence, not retroactive authorization.

## 4.4 Dependence is multi-dimensional

Saki's choices are shaped by overlapping dependencies:

- recognition dependency;
- romantic attachment;
- housing;
- money;
- drug reward;
- work network;
- pregnancy/future responsibility.

These dependencies do not arrive all at once. Their convergence around Hayato in Chapter 4 is especially important because leaving one relationship would then threaten multiple life functions simultaneously.

## 4.5 Decision environments become internal

Hayato eventually does not need to stand in the room.

Saki:

- anticipates his anger;
- recruits clients;
- discounts herself;
- conceals failure;
- defends his character;
- interprets his managerial concern as love.

The externally authored system becomes a preference and self-evaluation system.

## 4.6 Reproductive agency is not one repeated event

### First pregnancy

- paternity unknown;
- Hayato immediately supplies abortion as outcome;
- future exclusive child with him romanticizes compliance;
- Saki assents under dependency.

Classification:

> `A3 + A4`, not `A10` and not clean `A1`.

### Second believed pregnancy

- no Hayato present;
- surrounding people assume abortion;
- Saki rejects them;
- chooses birth, care, non-abandonment, drug cessation.

Classification:

> `A1` at preference/strategic-intention level, while material feasibility remains extremely constrained.

# 6. Strong conclusions

1. Saki never becomes wholly passive.
2. Remaining activity does not negate exploitation.
3. Tactical competence often grows inside strategic dependence.
4. Formal agreement and substantive authorship repeatedly diverge.
5. Explicit refusal is repeatedly ignored.
6. Impairment is often actively produced by others.
7. Material survival increasingly determines what options are usable.
8. The Chapter 6 future decision is genuine strategic authorship.
9. The Finale shows that decision partially changing behavior.
10. Saki's final self-blame is causally incomplete.

# 7. Residual ambiguities

- Exact pharmacological identities remain unknown.
- Some intoxicated expressions of desire cannot be cleanly separated into conditioned preference, immediate pleasure, and impairment.
- The final all-at-once drug use is strongly self-destructive and likely intended as lethal, but explicit suicidal language is absent.
- Objective alternatives such as social services may exist outside the depicted narrative; their realistic accessibility to Saki is not established.
- The child-oriented telos restores strategic authorship but may not restore self-worth independent of another person.

# 8. Final agency thesis

> **『変身』 is most precise when read as a story about the shrinking distance between choosing an act and authoring the life in which the act becomes necessary. Saki can desire, agree, initiate, bargain, and work while still living inside environments whose locations, risks, prices, information, dependencies, and meanings were supplied by others. Her late strategic return matters because it proves agency was never simply extinguished. The tragedy is that it returns after the material world has become too weak to support it—and that the final external violence closes a future Saki had already begun to enact.**
