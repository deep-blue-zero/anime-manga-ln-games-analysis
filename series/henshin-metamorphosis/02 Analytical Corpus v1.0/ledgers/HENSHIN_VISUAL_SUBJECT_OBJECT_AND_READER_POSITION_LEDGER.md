# 『変身』 / *Henshin*
## Visual Subject/Object and Reader-Position Ledger

# 1. Governing question

The work repeatedly asks the reader to perform two incompatible acts:

1. encounter Saki as a continuous subject with fear, hope, memory, refusal, and future;
2. consume Saki's body through commercial eromanga grammar.

The ledger therefore tracks not only:

> what happens to Saki,

but:

> **where the page places the reader while it happens.**

The strongest full-work formal conclusion is:

> **Saki's narrative subjecthood is never completely erased, but the manga repeatedly makes the destruction of her autonomy available as erotic spectacle.**

That contradiction is formal fact.

Whether it constitutes deliberate reader indictment remains underdetermined.

# 2. Chapter-by-chapter reader-position matrix

| Unit | Dominant early position | Dominant explicit position | Return to subjectivity | Formal contradiction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ch1 | `WITH_SAKI` — isolation, tears, makeover, peer anxiety | `DESIRING_SAKI + WITH_SAKI` | final phone/boyfriend interpretation | interior uncertainty remains during erotic objectification |
| Ch2 | `WITH_SAKI` — school/status anxiety, meeting uncertainty | increasingly `OBSERVING/DESIRING_SAKI` | mother scene and final photo shock | direct refusal coexists with eroticized bodily response |
| Ch3 | `WITH_SAKI` — blackmail strategy, kinship refusal, help-seeking | prolonged coercive/incest spectacle | school breakdown, maternal betrayal, Hayato message | narrator labels repeated rape while visual grammar eroticizes it |
| Ch4 | `OBSERVING/DESIRING_SAKI` inside explicit commodification | body modification, group drug scene, client work | black title page isolates Saki's Hayato-directed thought | the commodity is priced and aesthetically consumed simultaneously |
| Ch5 | crowded commodity gaze | client degradation, network coercion, severe intoxication | floating white-space subjective sequence | most whole/serene Saki appears when bodily autonomy is lowest |
| Ch6 | discarded body and public vulnerability | destitution/homelessness eroticized | pregnancy decision restores `WITH_SAKI` | survival collapse becomes fetish scenario before strategic voice returns |
| Finale | pregnant body as erotic/commercial spectacle | extreme stigma/violence | mirror, self-blame, park/Hana future | the work asks grief for the person while still consuming her catastrophe erotically |

# 3. Face, eyes, and body fragmentation

## Early work

Chapter 1 often preserves Saki's face and inner narration even after the makeover.

The reader sees:

- transformed body;
- insecure old voice.

This creates:

> outward discontinuity + inward continuity.

## Explicit sequences

As erotic intensity rises, the body is increasingly fragmented:

- face omitted;
- body parts isolated;
- other men's reactions privileged;
- bodily response enlarged.

Fragmentation converts Saki from:

> perceiving subject

into:

> visible evidence.

## Returns to face

The manga repeatedly restores Saki's face after spectacle:

- end of Chapter 1;
- mother scene in Chapter 2;
- school breakdown in Chapter 3;
- black-page interiority in Chapter 4;
- floating sequence in Chapter 5;
- pregnancy pledge in Chapter 6;
- mirror and Hana sequence in Finale.

This recurring re-subjectivization is one reason the tragedy remains legible.

# 4. Glasses

The glasses are among the most polyvalent motifs.

## Chapter 1

Part of the isolated middle-school presentation.

## Chapter 3

Return in Saki's private home appearance before paternal abuse.

This weakens any moralistic claim that glamorous presentation caused the danger.

## Finale

Saki puts on glasses and reconstructs an early-Saki-like appearance before the mirror.

Possible meanings:

- attempted return;
- continuity;
- confrontation with the self before transformation;
- failed restoration;
- identity no longer inhabitable.

The mirror is broken.

The final page leaves the glasses broken beside drug paraphernalia.

No single symbolism should be forced.

# 5. Makeup, hair, clothing, and body modification

## Chapter 1 — self-authored

Makeup and hair are tools of agency.

They create real social possibility.

## Chapter 2 — socially maintained

Accessories and fashion become status infrastructure.

## Chapter 4 — Hayato-authored

Hair, tanning, piercings, and tattoos are shaped around:

- Hayato's taste;
- ownership;
- marketability.

The body is constrained by two viewers:

> Hayato wants visible possession;

> the market wants sellable access.

## Finale — imagined normalization

Adult-future Saki appears with darker hair and conventional styling.

This may function as visual shorthand for ordinary adulthood.

It also risks implying recovery through normalization.

That counterreading should remain active.

# 6. The HAYATO mark

The tattoo is a dense formal object.

It functions as:

- love token for Saki;
- property mark for Hayato;
- signal to clients/other men;
- historical residue after rejection.

Chapter 4 Saki says the mark means she will always belong to Hayato.

Chapter 6 she tells strangers it means:

> nothing.

The body preserves an earlier authorship regime after its relational contract collapses.

# 7. Phones, photographs, recordings, and portable Saki

The phone/image chain is one of the strongest formal systems.

## Chapter 1

Hayato photographs impaired Saki.

## Chapter 2

A hotel photograph crosses into school.

## Chapter 3

Photo becomes blackmail; blackmail creates new recordings.

## Chapter 4

Phone becomes remote labor management.

## Chapter 5

Phone carries pricing anxiety and Hayato's imagined judgment.

The governing property of the image is:

> **behavior remains visible; decision environment disappears.**

Each image creates a portable Saki that others can:

- own;
- circulate;
- interpret;
- weaponize.

The manga itself also presents Saki as portable erotic image.

This formal rhyme is strong.

Deliberate self-indictment remains unproven.

# 8. Money as visual object

Money is repeatedly given panel salience.

- Chapter 2 cash stack changes decision environment.
- Status goods show conversion of hidden transaction into visible belonging.
- Chapter 4 rates and quota render body as economic instrument.
- Finale cash roll becomes material future.

The theft of savings is especially important because the reader has seen the money's meaning before attackers redefine it as stolen.

The visual evidence allows the reader to know Saki's claim is true while the characters refuse it.

# 9. Drugs and paraphernalia

The route progression is visually legible:

> pill → powder → syringe.

The objects increasingly mediate access to Saki's interior state.

The Chapter 5 injection sequence then produces a formal inversion:

- objective room is crowded and exploitative;
- subjective field is pale, open, warm, whole-body.

The final restroom page returns to:

- syringe;
- broken glasses;
- bodily residue.

Chemical escape and old identity become debris on the same floor.

# 10. Rooms, doors, and public/private space

## Karaoke/hotel rooms

Private spaces selected by adult men.

## Family bedroom

Nominal private safety violated by father.

## Door with mother nearby

Potential protection is physically close but informationally blocked.

## Hayato apartment

Shelter becomes conditional property.

## Public city

Crowded but institutionally unavailable.

## Tent village

Minimal shelter without privacy/autonomy.

## Public restroom

Final private space inside public infrastructure.

The spatial movement is:

> home → controlled private rooms → unsafe home → conditional apartment → public homelessness → improvised shelter → public restroom.

Privacy progressively loses its protective meaning.

# 11. Mirrors and self-recognition

The Finale mirror scene is the culmination of the visual identity problem.

Saki has been seen by:

- family;
- peers;
- clients;
- blackmail images;
- Hayato;
- markets;
- attackers.

The mirror offers:

> Saki looking at Saki.

She reconstructs an early visual identity and cries.

Then breaks the mirror.

Possible readings include:

- rejection of the old self;
- recognition that return is impossible;
- rage at self-image;
- refusal of appearance as answer;
- final collapse of self-recognition.

The text does not fix one.

# 12. Pregnancy and fetal imagery

Pregnancy gives the body future meaning.

Chapter 5's first pregnancy is:

- uncertain paternity;
- Hayato-authored abortion;
- medical procedure;
- financial cost.

Chapter 6/Finale pregnancy is:

- self-authored future;
- sobriety motive;
- saving motive;
- target of stigma and violence.

Fetal imagery is juxtaposed with ongoing exploitation.

This has two effects:

1. it makes consequence materially present;
2. it risks converting pregnancy vulnerability into erotic intensification.

Both should remain in the final formal judgment.

# 13. The floating Saki and the imagined adult Saki

These are the two most visually whole late-stage Sakis.

## Floating Saki

- whole body;
- pale open space;
- no men in subjective field;
- comfort and belonging;
- objective incapacity.

## Adult imagined Saki

- ordinary park;
- whole body;
- daughter;
- calm speech;
- past integrated as past.

The first is chemical escape from the social world.

The second is counterfactual survival within ordinary social time.

Their contrast is central:

> **one removes the world from consciousness; the other imagines a world in which Saki can remain conscious and live.**

# 14. Reader-position progression

The reader's dominant relation changes:

> empathic proximity → mixed erotic access → knowledge of coercion during erotic access → explicit knowledge of commodification → witness to subjective escape → witness to destitution → witness to lost future.

The work repeatedly gives the reader information that in-story observers lack.

We know:

- why Saki entered the hotel;
- why she is around boys;
- why she uses drugs;
- what the savings are for.

That privileged knowledge makes the reader a witness to causal truth.

But the reader is also the consumer of erotic images.

This is the work's unresolved ethical position.

# 15. Critique versus exploitation

## Evidence for critical function

- consequences persist;
- direct refusal is preserved;
- narrator names paternal violation;
- subjective interiority returns;
- image/context gap is structurally emphasized;
- final Hana sequence values ordinary personhood.

## Evidence for exploitative function

- coercion and destitution remain extensively eroticized;
- bodily response conventions can work against expressed refusal;
- escalating catastrophe maps onto escalating fetish novelty;
- afterword confirms favored pornographic attributes helped generate the work;
- the heroine's death was decided early.

## Current verdict

> **The manga critiques and participates in objectification, but the degree of deliberate self-critique cannot be established from form alone.**

# 16. Final visual thesis

> **The formal history of Saki is a history of who can look at her and what the look is allowed to prove. She first changes her appearance to make approach possible. Photographs then detach appearance from causality. Men and peers turn the body into evidence of identity; Hayato turns it into property; markets turn it into price; drugs briefly return it to Saki as a whole floating subject; the Finale turns pregnancy into future and then lets strangers destroy that future through a stigmatizing gaze. The imagined Hana park is the only late image in which being seen does not require being used.**
