# 『変身』 / *Henshin*
## Substance, Money, and Material-Security Ledger

# 1. Governing thesis

The material architecture of 『変身』 can be summarized as a series of conversions:

> **recognition becomes consumption; consumption becomes money pressure; money pressure becomes sexual labor; sexual labor produces bodily and medical consequences; those consequences create more money pressure; drugs make the labor tolerable; drug use undermines earnings; loss of earnings destroys housing; homelessness turns bodily access into survival barter.**

This closed-loop structure is one of the strongest reasons the work exceeds a sequence of isolated pornographic scenarios.

The ledger also preserves an essential caution:

> **The substances are never pharmacologically identified with sufficient certainty to name them as textual fact.**

The manga depicts:

- an unidentified pill;
- an unidentified powdered substance used nasally;
- an unidentified injected substance.

Their effects and narrative functions can be analyzed. Exact drug names remain speculation unless external paratext explicitly identifies them.

# 2. Material-security matrix

| Phase | Housing | Money | Work | Food | Healthcare | Main dependency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chapter 1 | stable family home | no precarity | none needed | ordinary family security | not relevant | recognition only |
| Chapter 2 | stable family home | status/consumer pressure | one compensated-dating encounter | secure | none | recognition + Hayato |
| Chapter 3 | expelled from home | family unemployment introduces pressure | blackmail-controlled sexual access; offer to help family | not yet insecure | none | immediate shelter + Hayato |
| Chapter 4 | Hayato apartment | earnings directed to Hayato debt | organized commercial sexual labor | dependent on Hayato household/network | none | Hayato for housing, drugs, work |
| Chapter 5 | Hayato-linked housing | debt + abortion cost + unstable prices | self-recruited clients; network exploitation | no explicit food crisis | one abortion procedure | Hayato system + drug reward |
| Chapter 6 | Hayato expels her; homeless; encampment | robbed, no money | survival exchange / irregular exploitation | warm food from encampment | none | drugs + exploitative shelter |
| Finale | homeless/unstable | saves substantial money for child, then robbed | continued survival sex | unstable | no sustained care | child future vs dependence and stigma |

# 3. Substance timeline

## Chapter 1 — introduction and impairment

Hayato introduces:

- alcohol;
- an unidentified pill.

Saki's internal line is decisive:

> **「今断りたかったのに…出来なかった」**  
> I wanted to refuse just now, but I couldn't.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0016 / p.013`

The chapter shows acute impairment.

What is **not** established:

- repeated use;
- tolerance;
- dependence;
- exact identity.

## Chapter 2 — repeated reinforcement and altered-self awareness

Saki now:

- meets Hayato two or three times per week;
- asks for another dose;
- says she has come to like sex in that state;
- says the drug makes her feel unlike herself;
- wishes for it during an unpleasant encounter without it.

Key language:

> **「自分が自分じゃなくなるようで…怖い」**  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0035 / p.032`

> **「アレが無いとこんなに苦しいんだ」**  
> It is this hard without it.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0046 / p.043`

Status:

> repeated reinforced use + active seeking + situation-specific craving; clinical dependence not yet established.

## Chapter 3 — relational concentration without new pharmacology

Chapter 3 adds little direct substance evidence.

Its importance is structural:

- Hayato becomes Saki's sole perceived refuge;
- Hayato is already the person associated with drug access.

Housing, attachment, and substances are therefore beginning to converge around one relationship.

## Chapter 4 — stronger environment and chemical authorship

Hayato brings Saki into Obata's network.

A powdered drug is used nasally.

Saki explicitly identifies the social pressure:

> **「怖いけど…断れない空気だよね」**  
> It is scary, but this is an atmosphere where I cannot refuse.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0097 / p.094`

She then commands herself:

> **「大人になれ」** — become an adult.

The drug becomes part of:

- adult-world membership;
- debt negotiation;
- group sexual exploitation;
- body modification.

An explicit warning about excessive quantity is ignored.

Status:

> stronger/different route + severe dependency environment + active external authorship of chemical state.

## Chapter 5 — injection and chemical `居場所`

The network introduces an injectable substance.

Saki becomes severely impaired and experiences:

- floating;
- warmth;
- cleansing;
- comfort;
- a desire to remain indefinitely;
- the conviction that the state is the `居場所` she had sought.

Key lines:

> **「心と体の汚れが洗い流されている」**  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0168 / p.165`

> **「もうずっと此処に居たい」**

> **「私が求めていた居場所」**

Status:

> powerful reward + severe incapacity + drug state acquiring existential belonging function.

## Chapter 6 — prioritization and work coping

Obata offers cash or drugs.

Saki knows Hayato needs money and thinks she should take cash.

She chooses the drug.

She later says:

> **「これがイイ」** — this is what I want.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0177 / p.174`

She also explains independent use to Hayato:

> **「仕事がキツくて…少しでも楽にできたらと思って」**  
> Work is hard; I thought it might make it a little easier.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0180 / p.177`

Public intoxication then weakens her ability to flee danger, and she has a memory gap regarding arrival at the encampment.

Status:

> severe prioritization, work-coping function, impaired exit capacity, dependence-like narrowing.

Saki's Chapter 6 endpoint promise:

> **「クスリも もうしない」**

is an intention, not proof of successful cessation.

## Finale — harm reduction, withdrawal-like distress, final use

Saki:

- knows use may harm the fetus;
- tries to delay/reduce it;
- describes functioning without it as impossible;
- is described by another character as showing `禁断症状`;
- rations a small supply;
- ultimately decides to use all remaining drugs after severe injury and apparent pregnancy loss.

Key lines:

> **「赤ちゃんに…障るから…だめ…なのに」**  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0216 / p.213`

> **「コレ無いと…無理…無理なの」**  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0217 / p.214`

> **「もう…全部使っちゃえ」**  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0239 / p.236`

Final status:

> **severe dependence-like use with compulsive prioritization, withdrawal-like distress, failed abstinence but real harm-reduction effort, and a final likely self-destructive overdose.**

# 4. Money timeline

## Chapter 1 — money is not yet a problem

Saki's social transformation occurs inside stable family conditions.

This is essential.

The makeover is not initially driven by poverty or survival.

## Chapter 2 — social-legibility money

Consumer vocabulary makes Saki fear sounding poor.

Compensated dating appears as rapid access to:

- bag;
- pendant;
- status fluency;
- peer blending.

Money's first major function is:

> **maintain the recognized transformed self.**

## Chapter 3 — family-help possibility

Father's unemployment creates a new possible moral use for hidden income.

Saki volunteers to work and help.

The chapter does not show her actually funding the family through sex work, but it expands the moral availability of money:

> status → family responsibility.

## Chapter 4 — Hayato-debt money

Hayato's debt becomes the governing objective.

Saki describes existing money as `バイト` earnings and volunteers future work.

Rates, quotas, clients, and overnight prices turn sex into occupation.

Hayato manages the system.

Money now serves:

> **Hayato's survival and debt.**

## Chapter 5 — recursive cost

Pregnancy leads to abortion.

The abortion costs money.

Saki later cites that expense while begging a client not to leave.

The loop is:

> sex → pregnancy → abortion → expense → more sex.

A client also defines Saki's `価値`—market value—and lowers his offer.

Saki discounts herself and worries Hayato will be angry.

## Chapter 6 — theft, property, and drug competition

Saki is robbed of money earned for Hayato.

Hayato calls her earnings:

> **「俺の金」** — my money.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0179 / p.176`

Obata offers cash or drugs.

Saki chooses drugs.

The economic mission loses to chemical reward.

After expulsion, the state is:

> **「金もない」** — no money.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0184 / p.181`

## Finale — recovery savings

Saki saves money for the child:

> **「お金を無駄に使えない」**

> **「この子のために…使うんだ」**  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0222 / p.219`

When robbed she says:

> **「私のお金なの」** — it is my money.  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0224 / p.221`

This is a meaningful recovery of economic authorship.

Its theft removes the principal material artifact of Saki's new future.

# 5. Work timeline

| Phase | Work form | Who sets purpose | Saki's local role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ch2 | one compensated-dating encounter | Saki seeks status money; client engineers actual sexual terms | reluctant transactional participant |
| Ch3 | blackmail sexual demands | blackmailers | appeasement and bargaining |
| Ch4 | organized sex work | Hayato debt + Obata quota | increasingly skilled worker |
| Ch5 | self-recruited clients | internalized Hayato objective | worker + marketer + price negotiator |
| Ch6 | irregular drug/sex exchanges and survival barter | drugs, homelessness, Hayato residue | severely constrained participant |
| Finale | pregnancy-limited survival sex | child savings + survival | future-oriented earner within exploitative system |

The central labor paradox is:

> **Saki becomes professionally more capable as the work becomes less self-authored.**

# 6. Housing timeline

1. **Family home** — stable and initially supportive.
2. **Family home made unsafe** — paternal abuse.
3. **Expulsion** — mother removes housing.
4. **Hayato apartment** — immediate stability, no independence.
5. **Conditional housing** — tied to usefulness, obedience, and money.
6. **Hayato expulsion** — housing loss imposed.
7. **Street/public vulnerability** — no destination.
8. **Encampment** — food and bed through exploitative barter.
9. **Finale** — continued homelessness/unstable shelter.

The crucial distinction is:

> **Immediate material stability can improve while material autonomy worsens.**

Hayato's apartment resolves literal homelessness while concentrating:

- housing;
- love;
- drug access;
- work;
- money;
- body authorship

inside one relationship.

# 7. Food, warmth, and bodily survival

Food appears late because early Saki is secure.

Once homeless, ordinary necessities become visible.

The encampment offers:

- warm food;
- bed;
- inclusion.

Customers sometimes provide:

- bodily warmth;
- a place to sleep;
- money.

These resources are real.

They are also bundled with access claims.

The repeated structure is:

> **care is available, but not free of extraction.**

# 8. Healthcare

The first pregnancy receives a medical procedure.

The narrative shows no durable healthcare connection for:

- substance use;
- assault;
- prenatal care;
- homelessness;
- safeguarding.

This is narrative absence, not evidence that such real-world services do not exist.

Its formal effect is that bodily consequences are treated episodically while environmental causes continue.

# 9. Closed-loop exploitation model

```text
recognition need
    ↓
status performance
    ↓
need for money
    ↓
sexual transaction / labor
    ↓
trauma, pregnancy, medical cost, market devaluation
    ↓
more need for money
    ↓
drug use to endure labor / feel belonging
    ↓
impairment, robbery, reduced earnings
    ↓
housing loss and homelessness
    ↓
sexual access exchanged for survival
    ↓
continued drug exposure and pregnancy risk
```

This is the work's strongest material-causal structure.

# 10. Final material thesis

> **『変身』 does not merely depict Saki making repeated dangerous choices. It shows an exploitative economy in which each consequence becomes a new input. Money moves from possibility to status, debt, medical necessity, managerial control, and finally a future Saki briefly owns. Drugs move from imposed impairment to pleasure, work technology, belonging, priority, and apparent final escape. Housing moves from ordinary family security to conditional relational property and then survival barter. By the Finale, Saki has recovered a reason to save but not a social structure capable of protecting the savings—or the future they represent.**
