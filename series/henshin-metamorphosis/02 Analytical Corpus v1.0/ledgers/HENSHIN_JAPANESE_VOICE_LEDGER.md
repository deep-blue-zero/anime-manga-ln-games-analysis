# 『変身』 / *Henshin*
## Japanese Voice Ledger

# 1. Governing thesis

The largest new evidentiary gain from the Japanese second pass is not that Saki becomes a completely different character from the English reading.

It is that the original language makes the **continuity and capture of Saki's selfhood** much more precise.

The governing finding is:

> **Saki's visual, sexual, social, and material states change more quickly than her core linguistic identity.**

She continues to use ordinary `私`.

Her speech repeatedly preserves:

- hesitation;
- ellipsis;
- self-correction;
- fear of burdening others;
- desire for recognition;
- kinship categories;
- care language;
- future-oriented promises.

The deepest changes occur in the semantic systems surrounding that same voice:

- normality;
- adulthood;
- obedience;
- value;
- ownership;
- belonging;
- disposal;
- motherhood;
- rebirth.

# 2. Longitudinal voice matrix

| Phase | Surface register | Primary speech task | Key language |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ch1 | hesitant, self-correcting young woman; polite to adult stranger | social presentation and verification of change | `変わりたい`, `普通の人を装っている`, `自分が好きになれそう`, `変われたのかな` |
| Ch2 | sober continuity + disinhibited drug register | maintain peer persona, set/attempt boundaries, hide shame | `自分が自分じゃなくなる`, `所為`, `何も言えない`, `入れるのはダメ`, `溶け込める` |
| Ch3 | appeasement, kinship refusal, attempted testimony, exclusive attachment | preserve causal truth under coercion | `やり過ごせそう`, `家族`, `お父さん`, `味方だって言った`, `ハヤトしかいない` |
| Ch4 | adult/work/ownership register overlays continuity | become useful and legible to Hayato's world | `大人の世界`, `断れない空気`, `大人になれ`, `これくらいしかない`, `ハヤトのモノ`, `変われる` |
| Ch5 | commercial obedience, value, chemical comfort | self-manage labor and defend Hayato narrative | `言うとおり`, `価値`, `何でも言うこと聞く`, `心地いい`, `居場所` |
| Ch6 | self-blame, disposal, non-abandonment, future pledge | survive abandonment and author child-oriented future | `捨てられた`, `見捨てない`, `産む`, `育てる`, `もうしない`, `変わってみせる` |
| Finale | maternal conflict, dependence, property, self-condemnation, counterfactual retrospect | protect future, then narrate failure and imagined rebirth | `私のお金`, `弱い人間`, `ママになれなくて`, `昔`, `自分の事が嫌いだった`, `生まれ変われた` |

# 3. First person and linguistic continuity

Saki's first person remains:

> **私 / watashi**

The manga does not give her a new performative pronoun when she changes appearance, enters sex work, or adopts a gyaru-coded style.

This matters because visual transformation is not accompanied by a simple first-person identity replacement.

The same `私` says:

- I want to change;
- I cannot refuse;
- I am Hayato's;
- my value;
- I will give birth;
- I am weak;
- I was able to be reborn.

The continuity of subject grammar does not prove complete psychological stability.

It does make the work's identity question harder than:

> old Saki disappears and another girl replaces her.

# 4. Ellipsis, hesitation, and self-correction

Early Saki's dialogue is full of:

- `……`;
- interrupted words;
- self-correction;
- questions to herself.

The clearest example is her high-school self-introduction:

> **「趣味は……読書とゲー……えっと……買い物です」**

She begins to name reading and games, then replaces them with shopping.

This is not merely nervous speech.

It shows social self-editing in real time.

The pattern persists later:

- `私…`;
- `あれ…`;
- `どうして…`;
- `ごめん…`;
- `もう…私には…`.

Late Saki is more commercially fluent but still reverts to hesitation when confronting identity, shame, abandonment, or the future.

# 5. Normality and disguise

> **「普通の人を装っている」**  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0010 / p.007`

`装う` suggests presenting, putting on, or disguising.

The line establishes that Saki initially experiences ordinary social identity as something worn.

Translation that reduces this to:

> trying to act normal

preserves broad meaning but may flatten the stronger appearance/disguise resonance.

Recommended audit class:

> `JP_REGISTER_OR_IMAGE_FLATTENED`.

# 6. Recognition and self-worth

> **「カワイイって言われるの気持ちいいし……なんか、自分が好きになれそう」**  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0013 / p.010`

The crucial form is:

> `好きになれそう`

not:

> I love myself now.

It is tentative possibility.

Recognition may make self-liking possible.

The English first pass captured the broad idea, but the Japanese secures the epistemic mechanism.

# 7. Causal authorship: `所為`

Saki says to Hayato:

> **「ハヤトの所為だからね」**  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0034-0035 / pp.031-032`

She preserves causal history:

> the drug-sex pattern developed because of Hayato.

Hayato answers:

> Saki is simply naturally lewd.

This is the clearest early linguistic contest over environmental versus intrinsic explanation.

# 8. Altered-self language

> **「自分が自分じゃなくなるようで…怖い」**  
> `JP_CBZ_IMG_0035 / p.032`

This should be translated phenomenologically:

> it feels as though I cease to be myself.

It should not be upgraded to a clinical diagnosis.

The line proves Saki herself perceives state-specific discontinuity.

# 9. Refusal language

Saki's Japanese distinguishes several stages.

## Internal refusal

> **「今断りたかったのに…出来なかった」**

## Knowledge without speech

> **「強く言わなきゃ…何も言えない」**

## Explicit boundary

> **「入れるのはダメだって」**

> **「入れないって言ったのに」**

## Kinship refusal

> **「家族でそんな事しちゃダメ」**

## Attempted testimony

> **「お父さんが—」**

## Reproductive refusal

> **「絶対イヤ」**

These cannot be collapsed into:

> Saki cannot say no.

The language shows speech capacity developing while social efficacy repeatedly fails.

# 10. `しか…ない` — exclusion grammar

This is one of the strongest longitudinal discoveries.

## People

> **「もうハヤトしかいない」**  
> no one except Hayato.

## Useful action

> **「これくらいしかない」**  
> this is about the only thing I can do.

## Resource route

> **「小幡さんにお願いするしかない」**  
> there is no option except asking Obata.

## Drug dependence/self-blame

> **「私が弱いから…こうするしかない」**  
> because I am weak, this is the only thing I can do.

The syntax maps shrinking perceived option sets.

It is stronger than generic words such as:

> only / have to

when the English rendering does not preserve the repeated grammatical pattern.

# 11. Adulthood vocabulary

Chapter 4 uses adulthood as ideology.

> **「大人の世界に入っている」**

> **「大人になれ」**

Later Obata says:

> **「大人の世界はビジネス」**

The concept changes function.

Saki uses adulthood to legitimate risk and institutional loss.

Obata uses adulthood to deny care outside transaction.

This should be preserved as a lexical chain.

# 12. Obedience grammar

The `言うこと / 言うとおり` chain tracks internalization.

## Hayato predicts

> **「俺の言うこと何でも聞くから」**  
> she will do anything I say.

## Saki accepts reproductive decision

> **「ハヤトの言うとおりにする」**  
> I will do what Hayato says.

## Saki markets compliance

> **「何でも言うこと聞くから」**  
> I will do anything you say.

## Saki pleads for housing

> **「なんでもハヤトの言う事聞くから」**

The phrase moves from:

> external prediction

through:

> relationship pledge

into:

> commercial offer and housing plea.

This is one of the clearest linguistic traces of captured agency.

# 13. Ownership language

Chapter 4's proprietary vocabulary includes:

- `俺のモノ`;
- `俺の女`;
- `私をハヤトのモノにして`;
- `私ハヤトのモノだもんね`.

The language matters because ownership is not only imposed.

Saki adopts it as security and love.

Later, after expulsion, the HAYATO tattoo remains but Saki says it is:

> **「なんでも…ない」**

The linguistic meaning disappears before the physical mark does.

# 14. `価値` — market value enters self-conception

The returning client says:

> **「自分で自分の価値を下げている」**

Saki repeats internally:

> **「私の…価値」**

The term converts self-worth into price.

This is the market endpoint of Chapter 1's recognition mechanism.

# 15. `心地いい`, `心地良さ`, and `居場所`

A non-boyfriend client's embrace feels:

> **「心地いい」**

The injected state produces:

> **「心地良さ」**

Then becomes:

> **「私が求めていた居場所」**

The lexical movement is:

> bodily comfort → total comfort → existential belonging.

The drug is not merely pleasure.

It becomes a substitute social place.

# 16. Disposal and non-abandonment

Chapter 6's lexical architecture is:

> **「捨てられてた」** — I had been dumped.

> **「捨てられちゃった」** — I was discarded.

> **「見捨てたりなんかしない」** — I will not abandon the child.

The abandoned person reverses the verb's moral direction.

This is one of the strongest examples of Japanese wording sharpening the ethical structure.

# 17. Transformation verbs

## `変わりたい`

Self-authored wish for another life.

## `変われたのかな`

Question whether social evidence proves change.

## `ハヤトのためなら…いくらでも変われる`

Limitless adaptability captured by Hayato's telos.

## `この子のために…変わってみせる`

Future-directed caregiving project.

## `生まれ変われた`

Rebirth in the counterfactual adult future.

The final phrase is stronger than simple change.

It names:

> survival long enough for a former self to become past.

# 18. Self-blame language

The Finale's:

> **「私…弱い人間だから」**

is the completed internalization of causal laundering.

Saki has previously accurately named:

- coercion;
- Hayato's causal role;
- refusal;
- exploitation.

At the endpoint, she reduces:

- assault;
- robbery;
- homelessness;
- dependence;
- institutional absence

into:

> weak person.

This is not merely low self-esteem.

It is a causal theory.

# 19. Translation-audit priorities

A targeted English comparison should prioritize passages where translation could shift:

| Japanese feature | Risk in English |
|---|---|
| `装う` | disguise/performance flattened to generic nervousness |
| `好きになれそう` | tentative possibility upgraded to stable self-love |
| `所為` | causal accusation softened |
| `断りたかったのに出来なかった` | failed refusal made ambiguous |
| `しか…ない` chains | repeated option-exclusion pattern lost |
| `大人になれ` | self-command reduced to generic bravery |
| `言うこと / 言うとおり` | obedience chain not visible across scenes |
| `価値` | market/self-worth resonance softened |
| `居場所` | belonging reduced to location |
| `捨てる / 見捨てる` | disposal/non-abandonment lexical reversal lost |
| `生まれ変わる` | rebirth reduced to change |

# 20. Final Japanese-voice thesis

> **The Japanese second pass confirms that late Saki remains recognizably continuous with early Saki even as her register expands into intoxicated speech, commercial speech, ownership language, maternal speech, and self-condemnation. Her voice does change—but mainly by acquiring the vocabularies of the systems governing her: normality, adulthood, obedience, value, property, business, drugs. The final counterfactual voice is powerful because it recovers temporal and relational language from those systems: `昔`, what mattered to me, Hana, and `生まれ変われた`.**
