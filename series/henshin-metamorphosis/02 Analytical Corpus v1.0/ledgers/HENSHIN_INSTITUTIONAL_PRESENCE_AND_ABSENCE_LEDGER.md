# 『変身』 / *Henshin*
## Institutional Presence and Absence Ledger

# 1. Purpose and caution

This ledger distinguishes:

> **institution absent from Saki's life**

from:

> **institution absent from the depicted narrative.**

The manga cannot establish that no school official, physician, shelter, police officer, relative, or social worker could ever have helped Saki merely because such a figure is not shown.

What it can establish is:

- which systems are present;
- what information they possess;
- what they actually do;
- whether Saki experiences them as usable;
- how their failure changes the causal chain.

The strongest full-work counterreading is:

> **The story is often causally coherent inside the exploitative systems it depicts, but the terminal tragedy depends upon a suspiciously thorough evacuation of protective institutions.**

# 3. Family as first institution

The family is not initially absent.

It supplies:

- housing;
- mother's practical help;
- father's praise;
- ordinary material security.

This matters because the early work is not set in total abandonment.

The collapse occurs in Chapter 3.

## Father's institutional betrayal

The father uses:

- bedroom access;
- parental hierarchy;
- domestic proximity;
- moral authority

for repeated assault.

## Mother's institutional betrayal

The mother controls:

- whether Saki's account is heard;
- physical safety;
- housing.

She terminates Saki's attempt to identify the father as actor and expels her.

## Result

The family does not merely fail to rescue Saki.

It transforms from protective institution into:

- perpetrator;
- misinterpreter;
- expeller.

The abruptness of this reversal is one of the work's strongest misery-machine vulnerabilities.

# 4. School

The school passes through three states.

## State 1 — social opening

Female classmates provide a real healthy route.

## State 2 — fragmented knowledge

Saki's hidden world becomes visible through photography, but context does not.

## State 3 — coercive/hostile field

- blackmail occurs in school;
- new recordings are made;
- girls coordinate harassment;
- desk vandalism becomes public inscription;
- teacher proximity interrupts one act but produces no durable protection.

The school therefore exists institutionally but fails to connect adult authority to the actual abuse.

The key distinction is:

> **adult authority is physically present, informationally ineffective.**

# 5. Healthcare

The clinic in Chapter 5 is the only conventional professional institution that directly treats Saki.

Its depicted function is technical:

> pregnancy termination.

The story does not show:

- coercion screening;
- substance assessment;
- abuse inquiry;
- safeguarding referral;
- follow-up;
- contraception planning.

No inference should be made about real-world standard practice from this omission.

Narratively, however, the narrowness matters:

> **the institution changes the body while leaving the environment untouched.**

The Finale's advanced pregnancy, dependence-like use, homelessness, and injury still produce no durable healthcare contact.

# 6. Police and law

The story contains conduct that could plausibly implicate:

- sexual assault;
- blackmail;
- recording/image abuse;
- child sexual exploitation;
- drug distribution;
- robbery;
- severe assault.

Yet police/legal structures do not enter the narrative.

This should not be translated into:

> law could not help.

The valid claim is:

> **legal institutions are narratively evacuated.**

That evacuation increases both:

- the closed-system intensity of the tragedy;
- the plausibility of the misery-machine objection.

# 7. Social welfare and shelter

Once Saki is homeless, no conventional shelter or social-service route appears.

Instead the story supplies:

- street men;
- tent encampment;
- transactional food and bed.

The informal encampment functions as substitute welfare.

It provides real resources but does not preserve bodily autonomy.

This is thematically coherent with the work's care/extraction pattern.

It is also a selective representation of available social worlds.

# 8. Exploitative networks as functioning institutions

The most organizationally competent systems in Saki's late life are exploitative.

Hayato/Obata's network possesses:

- drug supply;
- debt enforcement;
- prices;
- quotas;
- client acquisition;
- scheduling;
- body-market knowledge;
- informal housing link.

This matters.

The story does not depict a world without institutions.

It depicts a world in which:

> **extractive institutions reach Saki more effectively than protective institutions.**

That is a stronger and more defensible thematic claim.

# 9. Public space and non-intervention

The final passerby is important because the scene occurs in ordinary public space.

Saki is not hidden in a sealed underworld.

A suited adult sees an injured, bleeding young woman and continues.

The image condenses the work's institutional thesis:

> visibility does not guarantee recognition as somebody to whom obligations are owed.

But one passerby cannot prove universal social indifference.

He is a formal emblem.

# 10. Institutional-causality verdict

## Strongly supported

- family failure materially causes housing loss;
- school failure permits blackmail/rumor to become social identity;
- healthcare is procedural rather than continuous in the depicted text;
- exploitative networks are more present and responsive than protectors;
- no conventional institution interrupts homelessness, addiction, or final violence.

## Strong inference

- institutional evacuation is load-bearing to Saki's death;
- access to even one durable protective adult/institution could plausibly have altered the trajectory.

## Not supported as fact

- no help objectively existed;
- Japanese institutions generally behave this way;
- Saki could easily have accessed support if she had simply tried;
- institutional rescue would certainly have succeeded.

# 11. Misery-machine assessment

The story's strongest causal sequences are internal:

> image → blackmail → recording → rumor → ostracism;

> housing dependency → debt labor → drug coping → lost earnings → expulsion;

> pregnancy → abortion cost → more work;

> Chapter 6 future → savings → robbery/attack → collapse.

The weakest explanatory area is why protective systems never acquire comparable causal density.

The afterword's confirmation that Saki's death was planned early intensifies the question:

> were institutions excluded because Saki's social world plausibly closed, or because the terminal design required no durable rescue route?

The final synthesis should answer:

> both mechanisms are probably operating.

# 12. Final institutional thesis

> **『変身』 does not portray a literally institutionless society. It begins with functioning family and school relationships, briefly shows healthcare, and keeps Saki physically adjacent to ordinary urban life. Its tragedy lies partly in how protective systems fail to receive or believe the causal truth of her situation—and partly in how the narrative increasingly withholds those systems altogether. The result is a world where exploitative networks possess logistics, money, drugs, housing, prices, and continuity, while care appears episodically, informally, or too late. That asymmetry is thematically powerful and artistically suspect in equal measure.**
