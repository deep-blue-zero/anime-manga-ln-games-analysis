## Causal synopsis and governing argument

The Norton household has collectively defeated a detective's inquiry by staging the very evidence he believes he found independently. That opening offers a small version of the volume's larger problem: an apparently secure conclusion may be produced by other people's coordinated omissions. At the New Year celebration, Monica's successful chime ends in a faint when public attention returns to her awareness. The other sages conceal the failure and Raul's absence. Encounters with Raul and Ray introduce incompatible kinds of genius, friendliness and political resentment. Monica wants familiar Cyril rather than the rank-conscious deference he gives the witch; she sees the school group's gathering from outside her other identity. Clockford offers advancement while claiming mastery over Felix. She refuses his claim on her through a frightening display of mental-magic butterflies. No dream is imposed, no bargain is accepted, and her body still shakes.

Back at school, Barney's warning actually arrives, Hubard transfers and Glen's earlier expulsion becomes newly intelligible. Huge reserves, weak foundations, class contempt and a hunger for praise made Glen vulnerable to Hubard's apparently safe magic practice. The barrier prevents wounds but leaves pain and depletes reserves with each hit. Glen wishes his power would run out, but his exceptional reserves keep the assault going. Still holding abundant power, he concentrates it in fear and anger and pours all he has into an incoherent formula, forming a fireball before losing consciousness; the remembered scene does not show the resulting damage. In the present Monica helps him develop a practical tracking spell and learns something for her own research. Albert's imposed transfer brings a separate desire for friendship mixed with opposition to Felix. Robert pursues chess through an unwanted marriage proposal and ill-fitting family advice. Lana's birthday coffee and planned trading future allow Monica to feel needed while increasing guilt over an impossible shared Norton future.

Hubard abducts Monica to summon the witch whose earlier panicked victory made him desire a stronger opponent. Knowing the family name, he makes disclosure a weapon. The student men accept a duel over her future while she refuses to leave the council; formal safety cannot make her a willing prize. Before the match, her friends make a place for rest, food and skipped obligations. Their imperfect, familiar conduct and refusal to demand confession let her say that she wants to remain. During the match Cyril and Glen learn real cooperation, but Hubard's prepared devices let him overwhelm them. Felix secretly orders Will to kill him as an apparent accident while going to find Monica. Monica independently intervenes. Anger at her friends' suffering coexists with fear; tactical understanding and speed let her turn Hubard's devices against him. Victory gratifies rather than reforms him, and her exhaustion prevents her from managing everything afterward.

The official draw and hospital assurances conceal another abduction. Spirits take Cyril and Glen to seek help against an ancient flute's enslavement. Cyril separates the right to ask from the wrong of taking them without consent, consults Glen and agrees to hear and assist. He thanks the particular spirits who bring food; the weak ice boy refuses to demand payment and asks for a future frozen flower only when invited. Louis meanwhile discloses the sages' covert operation against Darwin. The flute threatens war, but destroying a conscious witness also protects their corporate independence from parliament and Clockford. Monica joins a rescue whose secrecy she finds troubling.

The first armor reveals spirits being consumed as fuel; Monica then identifies a way to sever the armor's synchronization without destroying the next five cores. Raul and Ray reach the endangered boys after Felix has drawn attention toward them, but the controlled fire spirit escapes. Bartholomeus reports Peter's earlier employment by Clockford, strengthening Monica's suspicion without proving her father's case or Felix's involvement. He refuses the investigative fee and tells her that a child can ask an adult for help. His genuine kindness retains a matchmaking interest and a mistaken interpretation. Controlled Lin attacks before safety is achieved. The secret episode identifies Darwin with the flute and shows Mary and Kara preparing further intervention, while keeping the sponsor's operational chain and the crisis's outcome unresolved.

The volume's decisive achievement is to make help ethically and causally differentiated. Some protect by withholding, some demand dependence, some preserve a room in which no explanation is required, and some ask consent after having violated it. Monica's anger makes attachment actionable without making force automatically right. Cyril's gratitude refuses to treat a species as one debtor. The sages' rescue places actual care beside deliberate suppression of testimony. Magic remains a set of particular affordances and costs—barriers that prevent wounds but not pain, devices that bypass one constraint, spirits whose consent can be removed—rather than a single ladder of worth. The recurring interruptions after apparent closure give that argument narrative form.

**E011 — p-006:P140–272.** Ordinary conversation has become an epistemic hazard. **E013 — p-007:P147–219.** Eliane engineers an apparently casual inquiry about Glen's health. Seeing Claudia beside him converts the planned words into threatened comparison before she notices Neil. **E014 — p-007:P223–354.** Robert's transfer pursues repeated chess and imagined marriage access despite an earlier refusal. **E015 — chapter five 第三王子アルバートの、お友達大作戦, p-008:P4–116/P190–248.** Library refuge serves actual study: Monica retrieves biology and cited papers to understand her father's book, while assuming the Black Grail belongs to that field because of its placement. That assumption is prospective, not a discovered meaning. Hand pain changes where/how she reads. Glen's wish for shortened chants is partly defeat resentment and partly admiration of Everett. Her specific counsel replaces resemblance to a famous technique with his next practical need: tracking increases hit probability across spells, whereas shortening must be learned formula by formula. Narrator exposition supports the tactical point while the belittling “only makes casting fast” remains Monica's undervaluation of her own specialty. She falsely credits Cyril to preserve cover. Teaching also prompts her own tracking research and desire for scholarly feedback; giving help is intellectually generative.

His request to be asked for help offers a real means to reduce Robert's pressure while making dependence itself desired. Her protective formal refusal, painful clenched hands and uncertainty whether “prince” or “Ike” is speaking resist a simple authentic-mask division. The same face can combine wanting, care and control. The text does not grant her his unspoken response beyond observable disappointment.

**E016 — p-008:P119–186/P252–400.** Patrick's inquiry finds public praise partly because this is Clockford's school; an uncritical report is not independent innocence evidence. His inclusive friendship declaration changes Albert's immediate desire without erasing rivalry: he relishes being a friend where Felix is only called a senior. Monica is relieved her cover survives and eats only at the end. Connection occurs through different motives and mistaken accounts; the remaining pie joke materially limits Albert's expected reward.

### A person made a prize, and friends who make time

**E017 — chapter six モニカ・ノートン拉致事件, p-009:P4–45/P108–245.** Byron's defeat is not an anonymous demonstration of Hubard's power: wounded pride in the school makes him persist and name stronger peers. Hubard's “monster” standard seeks a particular kind of overwhelming opponent, rather than simply any available victim. Cyril prioritizes hearing the alleged offender's account over correcting dress, apologizes for cold and tries to regulate anger before Hubard seizes the medical brooch and exposes a bodily insecurity. Expert recognition becomes intrusive handling. Elliott supplies practical interruption and physical cover; Monica selects his larger back, then Cyril's coat as a desperate curtain. Those comic maneuvers are failed attempts to avoid exposure, not flirtation. Cyril's concern becomes visible to the reader while hidden from her. Her hand reaches after him during abduction, but “help” remains unsaid. The earlier offer to protect her from Robert matters precisely because another senior's pattern taught her to expect unanswerable coercion.

**E018 — p-009:P49–105.** Lana's birthday gives a fulfilled small prospective opportunity from AFTER04. Monica practices the congratulation, prepares requested coffee and makes a rose sachet with Raul's materials; Lana receives scent and handmade care without knowing the special roses' origin. Claudia arranges the room and food while minimizing the labor in speech; Lana can accept the familiar manner without erasing the benefit. The conversation preserves Lana's business aim, actual port reconnaissance and commercial policy, which Monica does not fully follow. Her job offer values skill and council credibility as well as friendship; Claudia specifies the institutional advantage. Monica's pleasure at being needed immediately meets guilt about a school future Lana believes they share. She cannot accept under her current mission assumption, but lets the offer remain live to Lana. Friendship's care is real and its future is unequally understood. No promised shared graduation, job or disclosure has occurred.

**E019 — p-009:P249–371.** Glen's flight coaching to Albert is bodily, ordinary practice before he sees an abduction and acts. Hubard explicitly recognizes Everett privately and correctly infers a restricted political assignment, then trades concealment for leverage. Her hope that he has mellowed lasts only until the demanded benefit. Cyril's binding and Glen's aerial retrieval actually free her body, but the secret makes testimony costly. Felix can plausibly impose sanctions, yet Monica anticipates retaliatory disclosure. The proposed duel transfers this pressure into a rule-governed spectacle; she explicitly says she does not want to leave council, not that she consents to becoming anyone's property. Glen's personal score, Cyril's duty and Felix's command create different reasons for assent. Elliott's prize joke is answered by her interior 「人権が欲しい」 (P371, “I want human rights”), a pointed refusal of the comic exchange's premise. Heroic concern does not make the wager an informed choice by its supposed beneficiary.

**E020 — chapter seven 決闘と狩り, p-010:P4–121.** MacLagan's three-day preparation requirement makes safety a labor/resource problem. It also leaves Monica hungry, sleepless and self-blaming while her familiar sleeps and Isabelle blames herself. The improvised skipping party supplies what she cannot ask for: a safe time/place, water, adapted food and sleep. Claudia initiates it, but her theatrical-prize consolation worsens guilt; Neil notices and repairs the interpretation. Glen's food departs from his own meat preference to suit the recipient. Lana forcefully assigns blame to Hubard instead of accepting Monica's apology. Felix's accepted “bribe” gives institutional permission while Cyril's rule objections remain characteristic. These are different forms of care, some imperfect, rather than a seamless benevolent group mind.

Monica explicitly locates their value in **unchanged conduct without demanded confession**, contrasting past neighbors' stones and talent-linked social reversal. She does not yet test how they respond to full identity disclosure. Her thanks and wish to remain are actual speech. Cyril's habitual harsh promise is received as continuity; her 「いつものシリル様だぁ」 (P119) returns the earlier castle longing in a safer audience. That relief neither removes the literal coercive idiom nor means she would welcome every punishment. Ordinary relation supports action before the crisis, rather than being a reward added after victory.

**E021 — p-010:P125–324.** The three champions initially compete over finishing rights: command prestige, a needed personal victory and chess access prevent cooperation. Cyril and Glen learn to combine different strengths after a real aerial failure. The barrier protects from magical wounds, not the pain of attack or accidental fatal falling; Cyril's ice ramp supplies a distinct rescue. Glen's new tracking works and forces Hubard's first alarm, so defeat does not negate learning. Robert's hidden waiting then exposes them to the prepared ring system. Hubard's hunting focalization values strong prey and uses cheap repeated attacks plus deception; its apparent unchanted casting is manufactured. The reader sees tactical competence and deliberate degradation together, rather than ability proving honor. The silent transmitted image lets viewers perceive suffering without hearing exact speech, a limit that later determines what Cyril alone overhears.

**E022 — p-010:P328–357; chapter eight ガツン, p-011:P4–168 and p-013:P1–47.** Felix's private contingency and actual order are explicit: Will is to intervene and kill Hubard disguised as an accident, by any means (p-010:P352). Concern for a collapsing Monica is real alongside the contemplated secret killing; it neither sanitizes the means nor is proved a pretext. Will's assignment is not yet an accomplished death. Monica independently leaves the screen, makes inefficient straight flight with stop/reorient/recast turns, and maintains concealing mist. Her anger is named as the same previously unrecognized feeling Clockford provoked. Fear persists beneath deliberate posture and provocation. Cyril notices the missing rings and hears “Silent Witch” before unconsciousness; this is evidence acquired, not a complete narrated identity conclusion.

Hubard's retrospective pleasure in defeat changes the meaning of her panicked first victory to him without changing her experience. His Barney-substitute theory is expressly refused: Cyril is a respected senior, not an interchangeable rescuer. She exploits the two-spell constraint through a long-tracking ice spear and a rewritten linked ring network. The device is an external channel, not an arbitrary third simultaneous human spell; speed, precision and recognizing a limited firing pattern matter together. She even compares the weak anti-rewriting defense with Louis's earlier much harder design. Yet defeating him gratifies the very violent recognition she rejects. When asked for a queenly command she returns to awkward requests for secrecy; “ガツン” becomes a literal repeated device attack. His satisfaction blocks a simple lesson-that-punishment-reforms reading. She expressly prefers the skipped afternoon to glory, then cannot stay to move the injured without risking cover. Exhaustion, omitted efficiency formula and collapse prevent the battle from certifying general composure or sufficient care for every aftermath.

**E023 — p-013:P51–200.** Felix recognizes his projection of the apologizing, unreliable-to-him friend onto Monica. 「君が僕を頼ってくれないから、いけないんだ」 (P71, “It's because you won't rely on me”) makes desired dependence an attributable grievance; it is not the narrative's blame assignment. Will, meanwhile, encounters actual secondary abduction, weighs combat weakness and disclosure risk, and follows the wolf rather than executing the original order. The boy's apology and exceptional-capacity perception complicate a simple malicious-monster picture without erasing coercion. Their weight limit, low reserves and uncertainty determine which students they take.

Louis and Kara, the helpers maintaining the match barrier, have a different view of closure. They know Monica intervened and plan a device-malfunction/draw account to preserve her mission; they do not yet know where Glen and Cyril went. Kara declines unnecessary private knowledge and supplies sensing/communication, independent professional restraint beside Louis's pleased interpretation of forced silence. Lin's failed summons is distinguished from her familiar selective obedience: power fails to reach her, and the contract link is actually unavailable. The following unnamed old craftsman's scene confirms Lin immobilized under a speaking ancient flute and a hero-making ambition. Its promise that disaster creates heroes and his self-validation through repair are new evidence, not a confirmed complete identity/political chain at this point. The narrative replaces an apparently finished rescue with another vulnerability generated by the exhausted bodies it left behind.

### The rescue after the rescue: withheld facts and qualified help

**E024 — chapter nine 真夜中の訪問者の物騒なお誘い, p-014:P4–130.** MacLagan calls the patient Everett (P10) and says that he recognized her through the coincidence of her placement (P14). This is the explicit knowledge join the earlier clue did not license. His watching without prying supplies another institutional adult's discretion, not proof that the entire teaching staff knows. His immediate use of sage rank to discourage Hubard is sensible deterrence with a narrow limit: Hubard remains absorbed by amusement, not another person's suffering.

Isabelle reaches that limit from the other direction. Her removal of bystanders had made Monica's intervention possible (P41), but she judges her protection inadequate. The reader sees Agatha physically stop a chant before Isabelle's social threat can operate. Isabelle then makes the cost personally inconvenient: countly connections can have Hubard expelled from the country (P75–76), and she deliberately draws his hostility toward herself. The narrator confirms the family's capacity; no subsequent deportation or durable obedience is shown. This is neither powerless performance nor a demonstrated cure of him. Its mechanism is precisely that his pleasure defeats a warning about pain, whereas exclusion would remove opportunities for pleasure. The mother invoked here is a practical connection, not evidence that Isabelle herself possesses every power she threatens.

Meanwhile, the hospital physician's statement that all students returned to their dormitories (P107) and Lana's repetition of Felix's assurance (P120) cover an actual disappearance. Lana is not knowingly lying on the evidence available. A lie may protect the speaker's operational project while depriving Monica of the information needed to understand her unease. The scene repeats the volume's separation between apparent settled safety and what other focalizers know.

**E025 — p-014:P134–210; chapter ten 精霊の供物, p-015:P4–153.** Louis's midnight visit offers both more truth and another assignment. He credits MacLagan's readiness to involve a former teacher, admits the broken spirit link and supplies the facts of the missing students. His praise for silencing Hubard does not initially match Monica's reason for acting. She corrects it: she was angry because her friends were being hurt, and identifies the Clockford encounter as the same feeling. The narrator's account of her previous indifference to things outside numbers and magic (p-015:P29–30) gives this new self-recognition weight. Yet that local generalization should not erase the prior volumes' already enacted gratitude, worry and protective acts. The change is a newly grasped affect and willingness to oppose a person, not the first possible care in an otherwise empty life. Louis's joke that annoying people should be hit gives her affirmation from someone whose own anger is not a sufficient moral guide.

The danger has an institutional second layer. Privately holding an ancient device is unlawful under the stated procedure. Nevertheless the sages plan to conceal their colleague's possession: scandal could move their current direct royal subordination under parliamentary control, where Clockford has the strongest voice (P41–57). Louis explicitly imagines the loss of the discretion Monica just used to refuse him. Protection of that freedom and protection of a corporate reputation are joined interests. Mary leads the operation and moderates his preferred killing into confiscation and painful humiliation (P66–69); this is a consequential difference in means, not evidence that her whole plan is transparent or benign.

The flute must be destroyed because of war-making danger **and** because it can speak as an inconvenient witness (P83–87). Those are separable reasons. Monica's subsequent sense that she sees neither justice nor a great cause here (P153) belongs to her appraisal; the preceding disclosures provide real grounds for it without proving every participant acts from only one selfish motive. Students are to be saved both as persons and before they learn the compromising fact (P106). Louis is not indifferent to all pupils, but neither is rescue unconditionally open information. His claim that he would accept Lin's death in self-defense because she undertook the task herself is his stated rule of mutual responsibility (P128–132). It does not establish that he would feel no loss. Their broken link also makes blame for simple disobedience factually inappropriate.

Professional limits remain specific. Louis acknowledges Darwin's real device-making ability while distinguishing it from inferior direct combat, identifies the importance of rich magical resources and sponsorship, and allocates pairs with different strengths. Flight and communication have depleted him by the secret episode. The embarrassment of needing help survives his authoritative manner.

### A stolen choice, a proper thank-you, and a winter flower

**E026 — p-014:P214–310; p-015:P157–256.** The spirits' abduction is an attempted rescue of their own community. That aim explains rather than annuls its wrong to Cyril and Glen. The nameless ice boy is weak enough to have forgotten his name; his limited ability explains whom he could carry, while Sezdio's greater power supplies threats and species-wide blame. The boy has resisted the flute to save weaker spirits. Neither his childish appearance nor the wolf's contempt settles his moral or personal worth.

Cyril is angry, prepares to protect Glen at his own expense and insists on hearing enough to decide. At p-014:P298 he expressly separates asking for help from taking people without consent. His permission to be heard is not a retroactive agreement to kidnapping. At P302 he asks Glen before committing their help; Glen's trust is itself a response by a second affected person. The scene makes the ethical distinction operational through turn-taking, proposed action and an actual opportunity for refusal. The strongest rival is simply that a conscientious senior follows his duty regardless of who asks. His explicit consultation and discrimination between need and method support the narrower agency-sensitive reading.

That discrimination continues in apparently small acts. Cyril sings for winter spirits who supply nuts despite scarcity (p-015:P175–178). Glen explicitly marvels that he thanks them even after being kidnapped. The song belongs to these providers, not to a blanket pardon for all spirits or a transaction with their captor. The boy brings water but says that someone seeking help cannot demand an offering (P187, P217–220); Sezdio urges the opposite. Cyril's gratitude solicits the recipient's wish and produces a request for a frozen flower (P234–235). The flower is **promised**, not delivered in this unit. The text has room for an obligation freely undertaken after wrongful compulsion without making that earlier compulsion right.

The return of a gentle, chime-like song carries the previous volume's public miracle into work too small for an audience. Here no one earns a national title: provisions, shelter and warm behavior matter. Cyril's insistence that dawn is too early to visit even the enemy (P245) also exposes courtesy to comic excess. Glen's admiration is not an independent guarantee that this timing is strategically best. Their continuing belief that they lost the match and endangered Monica matters: both act without knowing her intervention or the official draw. Their conduct cannot be credited to a conclusion they have not received.

### The man who asks to be hired changes the request

**E027 — chapter eleven 通りすがりの男達, p-016:P4–96; epilogue 立ち塞がる風, p-017:P4–109.** Monica's forest diversion avoids fire because the place lacks the duel's protection. Bartholomeus arrives for a different reason: he saw Lin and followed to help her. His first account gives a labor history under Darwin, including resentment of devices sold under someone else's signature (p-016:P64), but remains his report. Monica's association with Cyril's defective brooch is a possibility (P67), not confirmation that this craftsman made it or that Darwin deliberately caused its failure. He does not yet know about the flute (P85–86), and Monica does not disclose the sages' covert punishment. Their useful cooperation is consequently not full informed participation in the same project.

The first armor's emptied core makes the cost of fighting newly legible. The spirit's light dies as it is consumed. This is discovered through the encounter, not knowledge Monica secretly possessed when she first attacked. The epilogue then revises the intervention. The components synchronize through a vulnerable connective formula; she freezes the next five armors' legs to force exposed arm positions, locates the formula and threads lightning from the right shoulder toward the left clavicle (p-017:P30–59). Disabling the connection arrests consumption and leaves the gems intact. She keeps the spirits temporarily sealed because release would expose them to renewed capture by the still-functioning flute (P68–91).

“Cannot rewrite” therefore does not entail “cannot stop.” This is a precise alternative obtained by understanding the device's organization, rather than by possessing the largest reserve or breaking a general magical law. It is also a morally consequential revision after a death she did not avert. A rival heroic reading would treat the display as triumphant proof that the witch saves everyone; the dead first core, temporarily confined survivors, unresolved flute and her fall onto her face all resist it. Bartholomeus's astonishment makes the achievement visible from outside; his smaller walking stride afterward answers the exhausted seventeen-year-old whom that achievement has not made invulnerable.

**E028 — p-017:P111–178.** Bartholomeus has actual investigative news: Peter was in Clockford's employment eight years earlier, while Monica dates her father's execution seven years earlier. The interval strengthens **her suspicion** without establishing what work he did, a trial chain or Felix's culpability. She herself recognizes the lack of evidence (P135). Her wish to obtain Darwin's information could make a useful exchange possible, but none has occurred. The Grail remains unknown.

He now refuses the information-gathering fee she offers and changes the request from 「雇われてください」 to 「力を貸してください」 (P155). A child may ask an adult for help without manufacturing an employment bargain. This corrects the specific obstacle through which she has tried to secure reliable assistance. It does not invalidate paid professional work, nor does it make him free of interest: the narration preserves his hope for Lin-related matchmaking payment (P165) and his mistaken romantic interpretation of Monica's guarded interest in Felix (P167–168). His kind intervention and partial misunderstanding coexist. The hair-ruffling feels warm to her, unlike an abstract declaration of benevolence; she receives an alternative way to request help before demonstrating that she can use it under all conditions.

Lin's lethal wind then interrupts them. Monica defends; no liberation, flute destruction or final settlement follows inside V06. The cut makes the last apparent movement toward safety provisional in the most literal bodily sense.

### Useful concealment, elective power, and the unfinished war device

**E029 — p-014:P314–333; p-016:P100–124 and P127–273.** Will reports his altered action, and Felix explicitly praises the judgment rather than punishing disobedience (p-014:P320). The spirit's concealment skill and river return are distinct from flight or exceptional fighting. His private recognition that Felix may choose an unpopular necessity, and uncertainty about the master's hollow smile, remain his own interpretation. Felix's “incidental” concern for future attendants should likewise not be treated as transparent motive when he rides alone, displays the uniform to draw the sages toward the endangered students and remains hidden to ensure their meeting with help. Actual protection coexists with the secret killing order and desire for dependency. The capacity to approve Will's improvisation is meaningful counterevidence to a model of absolute, mechanically enforced control.

Cyril and Glen then make useful tactical cooperation under conditions where it still cannot secure victory. Their attacks, ice restraint, riding and escape are not erased by needing stronger rescuers. Reruva's controlled appearance exposes an impersonation limit—eye color—and the fire threatens both them and the resisting wolf. Raul's wet vines, roots and manipulation of water-rich plants answer the fire in an environment-sensitive way. His ancestors' reputed massacre had made Cyril wary of the same sort of power; actual use saves him here without falsifying the historical danger. Raul also remembers Cyril's criticism of the hat and left it behind (P245). This tiny recipient-specific adjustment gives firmer evidence of listening than a general claim that friendliness means he understands everyone. Ray complains but secretly sends a familiar bat carrying the report that the missing people are protected (P258–259). Their “passing gardener/poet” cover is transparently awkward, and Cyril suspects a reason for concealment rather than simply believing it.

Raul's continuing picnic enthusiasm and Ray's apprehension keep their partnership from becoming a smoothly coordinated rescue machine. Reruva escapes: stopping this attack is not freeing her, ending control or proving forest-wide safety. Felix's satisfaction concerns a result he really helped produce, while his desire to see Everett again remains separately active.

**E030 — secret episode 星詠みと星槍, p-019:P4–66.** The narrator now identifies Darwin with the flute. His ability to control Reruva but not spirit kings is a limit, not a hypothetical liberty to make every spirit obey. Garanis flatters him as the hero who will master the “monsters,” aggravating the inadequacy that helps make his power attractive. Darwin wants the sages captured but Louis killed (P18). Darwin believes a sponsor wants war (P21), and the narrator marks the flute's own terrible ambition (P24). These are not a complete explicit identification of that sponsor with Clockford, nor proof that every actor shares a single fully informed aim.

Mary and Kara watch from outside. Mary's stars and illusions do not mean that she can screen the whole forest; she states that limit (P39–40). Her estimates that Raul might kill Darwin or Ray destroy the forest are anticipations of risk, not events. Mira recognizes a nearby loved presence through the box, but its referent is not identified. Kara's sensing, farsight and cigarette fire visibly require three simultaneous spells (P48); the narrated former sage can maintain seven (P64–65). This is an explicitly introduced exception to the ordinary two-spell account, not retrospective permission to invent such a capacity for Monica or anyone else.

Kara's professional restraint changes under a concrete relational obligation: she chooses to assist a junior fellow disciple and his peer, and asks Mary to coordinate. Her previous refusal of unnecessary private knowledge is not indifference. The final perspective widens the network of care and danger while withholding the action needed to settle it. It also reveals Louis's depleted reserves after communications and flight. Knowledge of weakness circulates among allies even while they hide their common institutional embarrassment.
