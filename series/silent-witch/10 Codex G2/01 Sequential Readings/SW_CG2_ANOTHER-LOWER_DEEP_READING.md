This is a useful limit on later interpretive shortcuts. Barney's actual patience cannot be reclassified as entirely sham because the run knows his later controlling conduct. Nor does a pleasant early exchange cancel that conduct. Monica's absorption is similarly irreducible to defensive symptom or gratitude performance. The object asks for no public speech, permits a satisfying structure to be seen and gives calculation an immediate material result. Her fear and her pleasure are both observed under different conditions. The prequel supplies a prior instance of a process already known in the adult books, not a new present-day recovery.

## Love that withholds its own explanation

<a id="e008"></a>
**E008 — The house exists before its shared use.** Chapter three, p-006:P4–78. Louis's preparations make his desire concrete: warmth, bathing, furniture, shelves, paper and a carefully rewritten invitation. He remembers Rosalie's discomfort with cold and plans an environment for her. These are consequential acts rather than a generic declaration that he loves his wife. But ownership of the setting is not shared understanding of its purpose. His unanswered letters lead him to blame Birdland; Rosalie's later viewpoint has a different explanation for her own unanswered letters. At this stage neither possesses proof of the postal mechanism.

Lin's contract supplies another asymmetry. It enables her proximity to Kara and gives Louis an upper spirit's prestige; its parties pursue different wants. Refining her wind for something as small as drying hair is difficult work, while her great force can easily move bodies. Glen's accidental arrival ruins the letter; Louis's violent expulsion and his self-justifying recollection of having survived a higher fall do not prove the pupil safe. Lin catches him to protect the plants. The joke refuses to let useful intervention certify benevolent intention. Birdland's invitation then transforms Louis's mood so sharply that irritations no longer hold him. The capacity to be patient varies with the desired news; it has not become a settled trait.

<a id="e009"></a>
**E009 — Rosalie chooses her work and refuses another man's use of her.** Chapter three, p-006:P82–155. Rosalie has accelerated her medical studies and trained in a hospital's mana-related work. She volunteers for corps employment; it is both a professional choice and a way to care for the person she loves. Her earlier expectation that Louis would freelance remains understandable and obsolete. She does not secretly possess the reader's explanation of sage ambition. The anticipated rough smile would have pleased her. That wish will make the actual polished arrival troubling without turning her into an enemy of his learning.

Adolf offers practical accommodation through a relative's hospital, so his proposal initially has the language of fitting a career. Rosalie suspects status-seeking and resentment of Louis. She nevertheless considers that some sincere affection might deserve a sincere answer. Her ethical hesitation governs how she refuses, not whether she owes him assent. He grips her wrist after the refusal; she names her lasting love and retracts her arm when the grip loosens. The bodily sequence is important: honest consideration does not convert persistence into courtship accepted. His own viewpoint then turns Louis's attention and success into intolerable injustice. His desired peace requires removing a rival. This discloses a motive, not a justified description of what Louis has done to him.

<a id="e010"></a>
**E010 — Birdland can recognize an injury and continue its mechanism.** Chapter three, p-006:P159–244. Louis interprets Rosalie's shock as charming embarrassment. Narration explicitly gives the reader the missing alternative: he does not understand how stunned she is. Birdland notices her stiffness, remembers her initial joy about the engagement and tries to ask what is wrong. She says there is no problem. His internal acknowledgment—he has made her swallow complaints—is substantial self-knowledge; he still does not know how to invite an answer. Delegating concern to the steward repeats the displacement of direct conversation.

The steward's question then makes silence itself formally diagnostic. Birdland's silence does not mean assent; the narrator says it comes from not remembering the order. The old instruction suppressed **both** Louis-to-Rosalie and Rosalie-to-Louis correspondence (P238–244). The drunkenness supplies a comic cause of forgetting, not cancellation of the lasting effects. Ordering future delivery permits contact; it does not disclose to the lovers what happened or apologize to the daughter. The father keeps his severe exterior and privately resolves to avoid drink. This is a limited administrative correction inside a still-unrepaired relation. Q067's entering question about received communication therefore survives even after a practical obstruction is lifted.

<a id="e011"></a>
**E011 — The same words do different work inside and outside a mind.** Chapter three, p-006:P248–281; chapter four, p-007:P4–183; repeated listener scene p-012:P91–135. The soldiers know Louis's ambition but invert its means and end. Their certainty draws on real conduct: a commander who beats them, inhibits visits to the infirmary and seems able to love only drink and jam. Rosalie demands professional address while he uses a tiny abrasion to gain time with her after dismissing a subordinate's stomach pain. The double standard gives gossip an evidential surface even where its explanation is false. Glen's gratitude makes the apparent transaction more painful because he does not want to hate his teacher.

When Trajett assumes political engagement, Louis suppresses anger partly to protect an important working connection and partly to spare a man unhappy in his own arranged marriage. The voiced result is **「彼女が〈治水の魔術師〉殿の娘でなければ、婚約なんてしませんよ」** (p-007:P182). His unspoken gloss means he would already have married without that obstacle. Rosalie and Glen hear only the sentence. The later replay does not reveal a different utterance; it removes privileged access to intention. A reader who excuses him because the inward sense was available misses the very mechanism of injury. A reader who treats the literal sentence as his sincere status calculation ignores narrated access in the other direction. The two routes must be held together.

<a id="e012"></a>
**E012 — Rutherford protects a talent by making it public; Kara's warning loses force without office.** Chapter four, p-007:P87–249. Louis has reasonable respect for Monica's paper and unreasonable confidence about what practical magic she could possibly cast. Rutherford's nomination enrages him because the teacher does not explain its politics. In Kara's conversation Rutherford says Clockford would crush a nomination from him, whereas another sage's backing can stand; he also wants Monica publicly known and protected against foreign recruitment. Winning is not essential to that publicity strategy. This is a real protective rationale, and a strategic treatment of a frightened student as national talent. The teachers' search for her in curtains and capture by specialists in living creatures turns the institutional handling into comedy without supplying her agreement.

Kara can see both the reason and the needless provocation to Louis. She also arrives with her own work: dangerous concentrations at Arsune workshop. Its management favors aristocratic investors and wider distribution over maintenance. Her former office might have made the warning harder to dismiss; the family scandal has reduced her leverage despite unchanged expertise. Allison's pragmatic explanation does not deny the danger. The independent investigator's work thus connects the political selection story to a preventable physical catastrophe. Later rescue will not make the warning unimportant, nor prove the factory could only have failed through an individual worker's error. Expertise needs a route through authority before it can prevent harm.
## What remains when recognition fails

<a id="e013"></a>
**E013 — Caring for the injured person does not entitle the carer to define her story.** Chapter five, p-008:P4–90/P125–178/P224–301. Louis's immediate calculation of the fastest route and his eventual tears are compatible. The leap from his office is not a loss of reason: the narration explains its practical route. His fear is then exposed by seeing Rosalie breathe and by her failure to recognize him. Her calm request for medical assessment is neither proof of indifference nor a joke about his feelings; he knows she would not joke in that situation. The accustomed order of care reverses, and he cannot obtain the response he expected by presenting the ideal fiancé.

Rosalie worries about work and inconvenience before her own needs. Louis recognizes this as continuous with her usual conduct; that recognition is affectionate and also describes a pressure the novel has not cured. He kneels to approach her level and arranges a comfortable carriage, cushions, household help and slow travel. Anderson's quick transfer of duties and suitable vehicle are independently useful contributions. Yet Louis suppresses details of the roof fall and the prior professional address, kicks Glen out of sight and controls the public account. These actions have several purposes: reducing alarm, improving security, protecting an investigation, and getting a wanted relationship recognized. They cannot all be assimilated to one selfless intention or one fraudulent cover.

The chapter title distinguishes loss from retention. Rosalie deduces class and education from what she knows, how she lives and even the quality of her underwear. The comic collision between medical practicality and Louis's embarrassment reveals a priority structure, not an absence of modesty. Technical knowledge and acquired reasoning survive autobiographical recognition. A hair color feels welcome before the person is identified. Her headache and the imperative not to remember later acquire a magical explanation; they should not be retrospectively diagnosed as a clinical mechanism the text has proved independently of that spell.

<a id="e014"></a>
**E014 — Knowing a friend supplies evidence; failing to notice him supplies a limit.** Chapter five, p-008:P91–124; chapter six, p-009:P24–124. Louis initially judges intact rails and a missing Owen as evidence of an attack. Those are investigative inferences, not a logical proof supplied by the rails alone. His confidence that Owen is not a jealous murderer rests on lived acquaintance. The supposedly explanatory note is troubling precisely because it makes a ready-made story fit the observable sequence. Louis refuses to confuse coherent narrative with true causation.

Owen's room then turns ordinary intimacy into detective evidence. The bedroom is characteristically chaotic; only the room for receiving someone is orderly. Coffee equipment is put away, and poisoned tea is a guest drink. These differences permit a hypothesis about a visitor that an impersonal inventory would miss. They do not at this moment independently prove every later reconstructed act. Louis's discovery that Owen's father has been hospitalized also exposes an ignorance within friendship. He had been busy and had not learned what Owen was carrying. His thought that the friend should have spoken is plausible, yet risks treating disclosure as the other's sole responsibility. The later repair must change the material leverage, not merely praise Louis's perceptiveness.

The childhood memory woven into the inquiry explains the intensity of his anger. Wages and dinner once had to be hidden or consumed before another worker took them. Beating the thief established a precarious peace. That history makes the present attack on a wanted life legible to Louis as renewed dispossession. The text offers a causal vocabulary for violence—security achieved through overwhelming deterrence—without demonstrating that this vocabulary is ethically adequate to every present situation. An explanation of how he learned it is not a universal necessity claim.

<a id="e015"></a>
**E015 — The old books and the friend who risks accusing him.** Chapter six, p-009:P128–360. Rosalie does not remember the exercises by reading a synopsis of herself. Her own name in the battered books, their preservation as a first gift, sunlight and a familiar voice make a different access route. The competent elegant man also creates an inferiority she cannot resolve by looking at his beauty: why would such a person choose someone she sees as severe and uncharming? Her conclusion is a self-appraisal, not the narrator's verdict. Memory fragments retain an attachment that the available public biography cannot explain.

Lionel's visit initially makes the patient more anxious. Lin's description of the guest as a golden gorilla is concise but omits the socially decisive fact that he is a prince. Lionel's dramatic medical concern could send the whole city into confusion. Warm friendship therefore does not guarantee a fitting visit. He nonetheless notices the lovers' problem, risks an accusation and raises mental interference as a possibility. Louis's denial draws on his own anguish at being seen as a stranger by Rosalie; he candidly says he would use the magic on some other people if necessary (P296–300). This is a recipient-specific refusal, not a general principle against altering minds.

Lionel then apologizes explicitly. His **「政治ではない。友人だからこそ、謝るのだ」** (P339) rejects Louis's suggestion that fault can be covered by political evasion. It is a small received correction in their friendship. The suspicion, though misdirected, has changed the available investigation; Louis goes to the library with a hypothesis he had not conceived. Another person's uncomfortable error can produce useful inquiry, and the acknowledgement of the error can matter independently of the useful result. Neither needs to be made a secret benevolent lesson Louis already understood.

<a id="e016"></a>
**E016 — Ordinary help has a provider's body and a recipient's terms.** Chapter seven, p-010:P4–134; earlier door scene p-008:P181–220. Lin's vegetables are exact, though she cannot taste the soup. Rosalie can praise an actual contribution without pretending the spirit has acquired human sensation. The neck-turning body, literal timings and interest in being a maid preserve a different mode of attention. At the door, a schedule-based inference first makes Lin reject Louis's unexpected return; mana and his abuse then identify him. Like Sally's recognition, the joke makes habitual speech diagnostic without making it the whole person. Her contract increases her mobility toward a loved person; it is not simply loyalty transferred into Louis's household. Facts she supplies about his desire for sagehood are accurate but insufficient to explain its purpose. Her literal truth can increase Rosalie's mistaken certainty.

Glen brings freshly killed chicken and offers to cook because Rosalie has repeatedly treated him. The gift comes from a butcher's son's own skill and value system. It is funny in a sickroom without becoming valueless or automatically ideal. Rosalie responds to his head injury with practical instructions despite her own pain; he recognizes a continuity of care. His gratitude toward Louis and his wish to repay Rosalie coexist, so he is not merely an unwitting romantic rival. Her relief in his unguarded manner is a recipient's response to this particular exchange. It cannot be transferred to Louis's manner or to every effort to help her. Later attempts to lift her for a better parade view will show the same generous boy overriding a different preference.

## Unequal powers, unequal reasons to seek a seat

<a id="e017"></a>
**E017 — A good candidate can decline the supposed prize.** Chapter seven, p-010:P137–229, with chapter four p-007:P253–269. The sages' meeting stages independent valuations. Darwin promotes his faction's candidate while disliking the proliferation of young colleagues; Ray exposes the contradiction without becoming enthusiastic about his office. Bradford wants strength, Raul an interesting near-age friend. Raul does not defend his youngest-record distinction as an ego possession. Birdland professes fairness despite personal interest. Mary manages the competing voices; Graham's apparently irrelevant question grounds the heroic body in ordinary need. Their differences prevent the institution from serving as one transparent voice of merit.

Winston makes the alternative substantive. His swift message helped make the opening rescue possible, yet public memory privileges the visible defenders. He likes the smaller acknowledgment of people who know how things worked. He is not merely bitter at being overlooked or too weak to compete. Free flight is a positive aim, and he honestly dislikes magical combat. His withdrawal benefits Louis without validating Louis's goal as universally desirable. Louis can understand that preference and choose another burden for himself. The scene is a counterexample to an automatic prestige hierarchy and makes the eventual appointment dependent on another person's life choice.

<a id="e018"></a>
**E018 — The contest revises a model rather than merely reversing a ranking.** Chapters seven–eight, p-010:P232–300; p-011:P4–191. Louis's introduction to Monica fails despite a smile he has trained to reassure most recipients. Her terrified sounds become cicada, small-animal and fish comparisons through his perspective and the narration's comic register. Calling her a monster later is not a revelation that the frightened girl was acting. Her fear of people, clumsy motion and painful tongue-bite remain while she makes exact calculations. The false inference was that these signs predicted what she could cast.

Louis's practical account of the duel is initially strong. He reads terrain, conserves magic, takes cover, avoids a height that exposes him to Adolf and chooses an inward-reflecting barrier. The defeat is not produced by absence of all preparation. It emerges through repeated correction. Instant spirit-king summoning overturns his expected casting time. High-power area spells seem wasteful until reserves fail to run down as predicted. A precise remote spell defeats Adolf and bypasses the supposed enclosure's practical protection. Restraint formulae then explain why his resource calculation is wrong: Monica can economically cast what he would need weeks of desk work to prepare. His money-derived skill in managing scarcity encounters another way of reducing cost.

He initially excludes barrier punches and loopholes because Birdland is watching, so his choices include a social constraint. That does not establish the counterfactual that abandoning appearance would have won. He suffers the actual damage, recognizes the possibilities too late and loses by the bracelet's depletion rule. The protected contest permits recovery from a first attack he believes would kill in a real fight; it does not make that tactical difference irrelevant. Rutherford's labels, effort genius and number genius (p-011:P25), distinguish different bases of exceptional power, but remain a teacher's appraisals rather than exhaustive accounts of their selves.

A small textual problem remains local: **p-011:P141 says Adolf was selected as a sage**, while the surrounding contest, later appointment and p303 reference consistently retain him as a candidate. I preserve the wording as an apparent local inconsistency and do not invent a prior appointment to reconcile it. It changes no observed outcome of this selection.

<a id="e019"></a>
**E019 — Admitting defeat does not settle the claims of others.** Chapter eight, p-011:P195–289. Louis tells Glen that Monica should become a sage and that he would select her. This is real recognition against his own hoped-for result. The subsequent suggestion of attacking an existing sage or intimidating Monica into withdrawal is called a joke, but the expression gives Glen reason to fear it. Neither a literal performed attack nor completely harmless banter is established. Louis's inability to return home, his mechanical work and use of matches to save depleted mana make shame bodily and ordinary rather than a single grand speech.

Glen's question about the engagement is heard through jealousy and insubordination before its content is understood. Louis's staff silences him. Rosalie arrives and intervenes, then offers to dissolve the engagement after Louis obtains the supposed benefit. Her act is intended to relieve a burden, not an absence of desire or a free choice based on full facts. His immediate declaration of love does not persuade her. When Glen restrains the pursuit, Louis first demands sympathy for himself; noticing the boy's conflict finally makes him lower the raised fist and ask what he saw. Inquiry begins where the assumption of transparent motive fails. That is a genuine changed action in this exchange, not proof that inquiry has replaced threats in his method.

<a id="e020"></a>
**E020 — Glen's protective error requires several distinctions.** Chapter nine, p-012:P4–240. Library records establish sustained study of mental interference, not intent by themselves. Glen's explanation establishes the missing causal sequence: he heard the remark, watched Rosalie cry, wanted that specific pain forgotten, then decided not to use the spell because altering another's memory was wrong. Witnessing the fall revives the plan under a mistaken belief that she has tried to kill herself and will do so again. He intends to erase a short scene; excessive power and incomplete understanding seal all her personal memories. This is compassionate intention producing unconsented injury, not a successful healing whose only cost is inconvenience. The earlier teachers' failure to develop his foundations remains consequential beyond an examination grade.

His silence also protects Louis. If he explains why he acted, he exposes the teacher he still wants to respect. Louis's assumption that he merely wants Rosalie for himself misses that loyalty. Lionel and Nate physically block violence; Lionel asks whom he is protecting and speaks as a friend. This intervention elicits an account threats did not. Glen's mixed affection, gratitude and anger are not reduced to one secret romantic cause. Nor does the narration's designation of him as good erase the danger of his act.

**「順番が逆です」** (P169) names the means/end reversal: Louis seeks sagehood for marriage, not marriage for sagehood. The reversal answers the factual suspicion, while Nate makes Louis face why it was plausible. The performance looked alien to Rosalie, and nearly everyone but Owen repeated the status explanation. Louis had assumed his feelings unmistakable and the gossip too trivial to correct. His apology here, **「反省してますごめんなさい」** (P159), is addressed to Lionel and immediately redirected toward Rosalie; it must not be recorded as a received apology to her. Lin's literal termination of protection when the selection ended adds a further, different failure of instructions. None is repaired by simply determining that Louis meant well.

## Protection, retaliation and the credit for work

<a id="e021"></a>
**E021 — A barrier must answer the way a building fails.** Chapter nine, p-012:P244–354. The catastrophe realizes Kara's ignored warning through an explicit chain: settling mana escapes unfinished tools, attracts lower spirits, and meets an old anti-spirit barrier no longer renewed. The spirits' delight in rich mana can be lethal to people without requiring a consciously malicious species. Lin's greater power could worsen this system, so Louis does not bring her. Appropriate help depends on causal fit, not simply selecting the strongest ally.

Major already holds twenty people alive—eight artisans, eleven children and herself. Her current formula leaks little magic and therefore fits this particular danger. Her continuing study has survived retirement from Minerva. But an intact hemisphere cannot prevent fatal acceleration if the floor gives way: the egg in an unbroken vessel supplies a brutally plain explanation. Louis's new contribution responds to that failure mode. He seals the spirits, recalls the plan, reconstructs load-bearing surfaces with the Glass Hollow Castle, contains fire and supplies an escape slope. Subordinates assist the injured, receive the children and clear the perimeter. He checks for stragglers before releasing the supports; remaining sections contain flying debris. It is temporary managed collapse, not a magically repaired factory.

His identification, **「そこにいる、すごい先生の教え子です」** (P314), grants Major public credit before her pupils. She recognizes conduct worthy of the name; he names the teacher's part again. Transmission is a current practical relation, not only a sentimental farewell. Their different skills are necessary at different moments, and neither must become the other's invention. Louis's choice to answer the emergency also distinguishes family desire from total abandonment of office. He wants to find Rosalie but delegates that search; his understanding of what she would despise reinforces a real command responsibility. Love's influence here enlarges obligation, even as elsewhere it narrows whom he regards.

<a id="e022"></a>
**E022 — A true accusation can end in excessive punishment.** Chapter ten, p-013:P4–220/P298–352; chapter eleven, p-014:P157–162. Alone, Rosalie remembers pride in her medical choice and affection for the person who supported it. She then distinguishes Adolf's declaration from its object: the intensity seems directed at something other than her. He claims a reciprocal romance that never existed, tightens an unwanted embrace despite her attempt to push him away, causes pain and throws her aside to fight. These are observed acts, not merely Louis's jealousy. The asymmetry between assailant and rescuer must survive criticism of the rescue.

Depleted Louis uses a cloak, a thrown staff and physical speed rather than a miraculous reserve of power. The previously managed roof information catches Adolf's revealing knowledge; inquiry concerning Owen, guest tea and the hospital supplies a reconstruction later independently confirmed by Owen. Not every inference is confirmed on the spot. Clockford's possible disposal of a compromised subordinate is Louis's politically plausible threat, not an enacted order from the duke. Such distinctions matter because an efficient intimidation scene can otherwise promote all his speech into omniscient narration.

After subduing Adolf, Louis continues to grind his heel into him and fulfils the old threat against the genital insult. Lin reports near-death injury; Mary's prediction frames permanent sexual incapacity. The comedy of the precise bodily revenge does not make it a harmless euphemism. Lionel warns of excess and the appointment risk, then corrects the register while accepting substantially the same punitive purpose. Nate later covers up **excessive violence**, explicitly named at p-014:P158. The scene therefore combines protection, revenge, class/gender insult, practical evidence and concealment. A pure justice account loses the excess; an account of morally equivalent rivals loses Adolf's coercion and attack. Louis's romance can be real while this use of mastery remains a serious counterweight to its celebratory frame.

<a id="e023"></a>
**E023 — A body's limit changes a father; another body's limit changes a vacancy.** Chapter ten, p-013:P225–294. Birdland has measured reserves of 152, close to the required minimum of 150. This quantifies Louis's earlier inference that he was on the brink of falling below it. The decline, not accomplished disqualification, prompts retirement. Experiencing a limit gives him a way to understand the daughter he once judged effort-deficient. He explicitly recognizes the arrogance of confusing his own capacity with what anyone could achieve. That explanation materially deepens the remorse known in Upper. It still occurs in conversation with Mary, not in a received acknowledgment to Rosalie.

Graham's painful back and refusal to sit through more meetings create the second seat. Others try to retain the national hero, even offering a new chair; his own decision holds. A heroic career does not forfeit his right to retire. The contingency permits Louis's appointment without revising the contest result. Meanwhile Birdland would prioritize Monica for national magical development despite personal desire to recognize Louis. Mary weighs factional balance, ability, protection through pupilhood and public incapacity. Her phrase about a lack in Monica's humanity is an attributed appraisal situated beside demonstrated attachments, inquiry and fear, not an analyst's diagnosis. National protection can preserve an exceptional person's work while treating her location and affiliation as assets. No consultation with Monica about these alternative arrangements is shown. Selection has genuine evidence of ability and contingent political and bodily conditions; neither a pure examination meritocracy nor a wholly fake competition accounts for it.

## A shared account is work, too

<a id="e024"></a>
**E024 — The carriage supplies the conversation success had postponed.** Chapter ten, p-013:P298–433. Removing Glen's spell restores memories; it does not by itself repair what the lovers believe. Rosalie's first response is guilt about burden, not the grateful embrace Louis imagines. He recognizes the familiar priority and still resents its effect. Lionel's insistence that they talk, and the carriage's unusually slow progress, create time that their jobs and Louis's ambition have failed to make. A political friend has to supply a setting for private truth.

Louis explains the conditions he had withheld lest Rosalie feel responsible. His silence was meant to protect her from guilt and produced a worse explanation instead. He also tells her about his origins, without offering them as a bid for pity. His account of choosing office is not only possession of a wife. He wants to preserve her relation to her father and carry some of Lionel's unwanted political work. Although he lacks a comparable parental bond, he takes her attachment seriously. The rival that he simply defeats all obstacles to ownership misses this costly recognition. The opposite rival, total sacrificial self-erasure, misses his repeatedly stated wanted life and anticipated benefits.

Her response, that she has ruined his life, shows the risk has not vanished merely because he discloses the truth. He answers **「俺は、迷惑なんて、思ってねーからな」** (P415) in the familiar northern register. It is his avowal that this was his choice, not a demand that she repay his career. She then names her actual attraction to the bold, rude boy. Their exchange acknowledges that both waited; her **「私もよ」** (P429) checks the tendency to make his endurance the whole story. Laughing over foolish things realizes a desire he had described earlier more accurately than office alone could. No explicit apology to Rosalie is shown in this carriage; disclosure, avowal, named preference and received affection are the specific repair mechanisms the text does show. Q067 consequently gains a real lover-to-lover answer, while its father/daughter component stays more limited.

<a id="e025"></a>
**E025 — Owen's guilt is not the objective measure of his agency.** Chapter eleven, p-014:P4–153. The change of focalization confirms blackmail, remote attack and poisoning independently of Louis's reconstruction. It also complicates Owen's first statement that he could not refuse. He initially says **「お断りします」** (P34). Adolf then brings his father's treatment into the demand. Calling the resulting compliance simply weak will would delete an observed refusal and the material reason it was overridden. Owen tries to prepare an intervention and later urges surrender, but misjudges the attack and the staged remorse. His guilt and regret are real; his total self-condemnation is a character's judgment under shock.

Louis's first instruction to clean the room disrupts anticipated damnation through an ordinary shared grievance. He has also arranged another hospital for Owen's father, actually removing leverage. Rosalie supplies her own protective account; the couple knowingly alter the public call-to-the-roof story to preserve a coerced man's place. This is compassionate partiality and managed official truth, not an entirely transparent exoneration. Owen explicitly apologizes to the injured Rosalie. She asks him to rely on Louis next time, interpreting the latter's anger as hurt at not being trusted. The language of repaying through useful work may carry pressure, but it also gives an immediate alternative to disappearing in shame. Their jokes, her wish to join the farewell prank and Owen's relaxed laughter demonstrate renewed interaction. They do not prove all guilt, workplace suspicion or future vulnerability cured.

<a id="e026"></a>
**E026 — A frightening colleague gives recognition, then collects a debt.** Chapter eleven, p-014:P165–236. Louis refuses Monica's description of victory as an accident because it would deny what his own skill encountered. This grants her a serious opponent's recognition rather than generic encouragement. Yet his explanation of winners' rights is his own formative worldview: those who win may press claims, those who lose get nothing. The novel has just shown how politically mediated a seat can be; it does not universally certify that worldview.

Monica asks why he can believe in himself. His answer recalls people who believed in him, not only his own effort: women, teachers, the people at Gore's, Owen, Rosalie and Lionel. It is a genuinely relational explanation of confidence from a man who declines universal benevolence. She names a foster mother and a friend, decides to report success, steadies and briefly smiles. **「同期殿」** (P222) gives her a place beside him rather than beneath an idol. Her statement that he is a little less frightening is deliberately small. He immediately jokes about her supposed attraction, which she denies, then makes a request she supposedly cannot refuse because he took on the speech. The temporal proximity matters: useful help is not a stage that expels exploitation from the relation. The same conversation can enlarge confidence and prepare leverage.

<a id="e027"></a>
**E027 — Forgiveness and delight do not erase the differences between recipients.** Chapter eleven, p-014:P240–322. Rosalie chooses not to prosecute Glen and publicly attributes the memory loss to the fall. That is her decision about an injury to herself, not proof his act was authorized or a formal finding that it was harmless. Louis imposes supervision of magic, with the wall damage separately coloring his prohibition of flight. Glen's next generous plan—to lift her so she can see the parade—again outruns her expressed preference and bodily situation. His kindness needs recipient information, not simply more effort.

Louis's appearance repeats that difficulty. He interrupts Glen's touching, then lifts Rosalie himself despite her protest. The narration explicitly supplies her later joy, attraction and response to the familiar unruly boy, so it would be false to describe the entire flight as unrelieved coercive distress. It would be equally false to turn that later pleasure into universal permission for touching her or others. The formal contrast is sharper with Monica: Louis has obtained the parade illusion by blaming her rehearsal failures for his lost private time. Glen calls it blackmail. Louis translates it into the teacher's maxim of collecting debts. One woman's desired romantic adventure is enabled through pressure on another colleague. The lovely scene is not invalidated by that fact; its costs and its recipients must remain visible together.

<a id="e028"></a>
**E028 — The wedding is a network of aims, not a chorus of approval.** Epilogue, p-015:P21–94. Rosalie invites Rutherford, whom Louis had not intended to invite. Kara's work prevents attendance; her gift travels through her teacher. Major enjoys the children's renewed interest in barriers and her continuing teaching, rather than merely taking pleasure in the hero's marriage. Lionel expends effort to attend without advertising the cost; Louis asks Rosalie before giving him one of her flowers. This particular consultation is worth retaining beside his unconsulted lifting at the parade. Changed conduct is not uniform conduct.

Owen's coffee carries both habitual difference and the memory of poisoning. He teases and refuses the disparaging comparison with Monica's bitter brew; Louis wants the familiar drink despite not generally liking it. Glen controls meat preparation well enough that Louis elects not to interfere. His competence and authority here are practical and recipient-specific, not bestowed by a magic credential. Raul's overwhelming cart of flowers is sincere but ill-timed; Mary finds a way to distribute the surplus as departing gifts. These exchanges complicate any account in which giving automatically communicates correctly. They also keep supporting figures' preferences alive after their plot usefulness is exhausted.
