The quietness of sharing space matters because the boy's established method of securing space is to frighten others away.

His response exceeds an efficiency calculation: he changes his study sequence to see her, lingers over an already memorized history book, and asks her name while she expects another academic question. The reader can infer attraction from his attention; Rosalie's returned affection cannot yet be inferred at the same strength. Her first holiday gift is a whole bag of old exercises, sincerely matched to his studying. His delight at receiving his first souvenir becomes disappointment at the contents, then actual thanks. A gift can be useful and disappointing without being a fraudulent gift. Rutherford's staff similarly marks admission but is initially appraised as something worth four silver coins in an emergency. Recognition has not supplied a settled ambition: the boy wants to surprise the teacher rather than hear generic praise, then to obtain Rosalie's particular “amazing.” This is recipient-specific desire, not an already formed sage vocation. Rival: she simply civilizes a violent boy through romance. She offers a discriminating standard and resources; his continuing violence, desire to impress and independent appetite for study remain his own.

<a id="e007"></a>
**E007 — technical excellence does not define a situation's proper use.** Chapter four, **悪童式魔法戦必勝法**, p-007:P4–157/P312–393, makes Louis's spectacular thirty fireballs both technically excellent and unsuitable indoors. MacLagan praises the later report while extinguishing the demonstration and demanding its explanation. The instruction “no maximum specified” is real, so the boy's protest has an intelligible basis; the teacher's criterion, **「状況に応じた魔術を使うのも、魔術師の実力の内よ」** (P41), adds environmental judgment rather than denying the feat. Rosalie's frantic warning is misread as cute performance before he reads her lips. Seeking admiration interferes with the reception of care. Her demand that he change clothes likewise exceeds his northern survival threshold: absence of lethal cold is not absence of a health concern. She offers to explain his lateness to the next teacher; he instead crosses the roof half-dressed to avoid wasting class. The comic consequence follows several rationalities that do not fit together.

The staff-room scene prevents “the institution” from becoming one voice. Allison likes the attentive law student and takes his loophole remark as spirited humor; Rutherford understands the evasion it literally proposes. Redmond admires a detailed dragon drawing whose selected details reveal an intention to hunt. Major objects to the scholarship pupil's violence and exemplarity failure. MacLagan has both safety and intellectual interest. Rutherford welcomes Major's restraint on the boy even while administering blows himself. These different appraisals can all be locally accurate without resolving the conduct into either genius or delinquency. Louis's measured mana and wind affinity also separate magnitude, technique and bodily force: concurrent maintenance and shortened chants are exceptional learned operations, not proof of unlimited reserves.

At the practice ground he is more interested in the protective barrier's engineering than in the fight. His inference about buried devices and site dependence is marked as his reasoning, not an already established construction specification. He is relieved that Rosalie will not participate because pain remains real even where injury is prevented. Yet he uses an unconscious classmate as a shield, beats the opponents, then prepares a finishing fireball. Adolf's arranged three-against-one attack is an actual threat; its failure does not make the response proportionate. Louis exploits the gap between the exercise's magical form and the practical aim of stopping an enemy before he chants. Rutherford stops the blow and punishes him. Calling the ensuing reputation “ミネルヴァの悪童” yields a comic anticlimax because it still sounds as uncool to its recipient as “jam hunter.” Reputation is both a deliberately acquired defense and something others can name beyond his control. The prologue's mature containment remains a comparison available to the reader, not an outcome this first class has already taught.

<a id="e008"></a>
**E008 — Owen's coffee is a gift with an independent taste and ambition.** p-007:P161–227 places a deliberately small flame beside the public fireball display. Louis recognizes the skill of maintaining constant low heat and says so before learning what Owen wants to become. Owen's dream of the magic corps grows from being rescued by the Cannonball Magician; his parents' costly support and sent beans make family encouragement concrete. He is neither a timid scholar whose future can be guessed from his manner nor a mere witness to Louis's talent. Louis feels at a disadvantage because his own aims remain immediate, though he still considers his talent and work real. Recognition from a peer reveals an absent horizon rather than straightforward envy of superior skill.

Owen shares coffee as a belated entrance celebration. Louis hesitates because he has already received marmalade, then accepts through a boastful phrase rather than a grateful self-erasure. Their “make/brew” correction lets ordinary expertise run from Owen to him. The taste does not harmonize: Louis finds it intolerably bitter, adds precious jam unsuccessfully and drinks on because wasting the jam is unthinkable. Owen smiles over his own cup and laughs at the other boy's face. Shared life here can sustain contrary pleasures and teasing, not require one person to adopt the other's valuation. That is a consequential counterexample to treating all Louis's early relationships as utility exchanges.

The intervening laundry retaliation (P231–308) keeps this kindness from becoming a conversion story. Terence believes himself a friend serving Minerva by removing an alien commoner; Adolf privately expects nothing of him and anticipates his bungling. Their directional relationship is not symmetrical. Louis's correct identification of the theft and forced confession do not authorize dumping Terence in a cesspit. The narrator enters both boys' self-protective appraisals, including Terence's minimization of discarded clothing as mere rubbish. The same sequence can expose class exclusion and the excess of the boy resisting it. Neither truth cancels the other.


### Defeat teaches a technique; responsibility requires another person

<a id="e009"></a>
**E009 — a defeat can be accepted without producing moral reform.** Chapter five, **岩窟の魔術師**, p-008:P4–35/P107–323, turns repeated rule evasion into an institutional problem. The five practices are not one accidental misunderstanding. Louis successively redescribes a body blow as an accident, a thrown person as neither punching nor kicking, and a trip as nonviolence; the teachers know the difference. Remington declines expulsion because of exceptional scholarship but does not claim the conduct acceptable. The barrier already had recognized problems before Louis exposed them. Its new physical-attack nullification belongs to Sams Horayson and Major's professional network, not a device invented solely to teach one boy a lesson.

The duel makes his limits precise. He knows two barrier forms, but the hemispherical one costs more mana, prevents movement and obstructs his own outgoing spells; he has not learned remote casting. He notices the missing floor only when Rutherford's earth attacks enter through it. Smoke cannot be treated as ordinary loose matter blown away by his wind. Rutherford's spectacular spirit-king summoning supplies a visible disparity, then his explanation reverses the boy's ranking of spectacle: changing from earth to smoke saves mana and cleanup, while magical endowment of smoke is uniquely difficult. Louis's admission of complete defeat is an achieved revision in appraisal. It is not the staff's assertion alone and not an ethical conversion.

Major's later reading supplies an independent test of the revision. Louis has reconstructed plausible mechanisms from the bodily feel of a dispersed kick, not stolen an unpublished formula or understood everything at sight. She finds roughness as well as essential insight. He brings questions on ground coverage, smoke directionality, durability and strength, then asks for remedial instruction though his grades do not require it. Her duty to answer an earnest pupil overcomes personal dislike. Her provisional hope that he has reformed is interrupted by his gift of liquor. She lectures, confiscates it, corrects the reports and admits him anyway. The resulting research relation rests on sustained work and expertise under ongoing disagreement. Rival: his defeat simply breaks pride into obedience. He retains the aim of defeating Rutherford and expands the research beyond the assigned report. Opposite rival: the boy has outgrown the teacher as soon as he reverse-engineers a component. His errors, limited repertoire and need for Major's correction remain explicit.

<a id="e010"></a>
**E010 — Rosalie crosses the boundary she had observed, and both misread the meaning of burden.** Chapter six, **悪童、責任の重みを知る**, p-009:P4–234, begins with shared snow-clearing, a job Louis hates and does well. Competence is no evidence that he welcomes endless service. His private map study then converts barrier engineering into a hazardous shortcut: he infers a nearby mana-rich zone, knows concentration carries poisoning risk, and plans training there to enlarge his reserves. His account to Rosalie is literally true but omits the intended trespass. This is an early instance of information selected to secure another person's noninterference.

Rosalie knows the temptation because she has considered it herself. Her twenty mana, essentially unchanged since elementary school, recasts her first-place written results, nonparticipation in duels and uncertainty over advancement. Effort is already present; a speech demanding more effort does not remove her present physiological limit. Her father's sage status adds expectation and fear of disgracing him. Those facts do not establish that he has threatened her or prohibited further study. She says she could not cross the rope because of possible injury and others' burden, then crosses it to stop Louis. He cannot deliver the ready answer that, unlike her, he has no father to inconvenience. The narration locates his new felt responsibility in the cost of her action for him, followed by the physical emergency he has helped occasion.

The carried body is not a romantic cure. Poisoning and wet, cold feet require Woodman's competent combination of medical and magical knowledge, warmth and a costly absorbing stone. The general bodily-enchantment prohibition and the absence of an established medical-magic field are historical conditions at this childhood point; V12's later policy trajectory must not be transported into the doctor's present. His grumbling self-interest does not prevent treatment, just as Louis's threatening appraisal of him does not cause care already being offered. The eventual diagnosis is mild poisoning without frostbite, not an invented permanent injury.

Focalization then changes to Rosalie. She reads his angry face as resentment at her intervention and reproaches herself for a childish “I endured it, so you must” demand. Her account has truth in the envy she later acknowledges, but does not exhaust either her protective purpose or his anger at himself. **「迷惑なんて、思ってねーからな」** and **「森の奥には、もう行かねぇ」** (P211/P214) correct her inference through actual speech and a specific promise. He offers a hand on the icy path; she accepts. This is a chosen practical contact after an agreed escort, not a generic claim that physical intimacy always signifies consent. Her admiration of his freedom also simplifies a life whose constrained origin the reader has seen. Each sees a freedom or capacity the other lacks; the narration grants access to both without making either appraisal omniscient.

<a id="e011"></a>
**E011 — an encouraging demonstration remains a frightening misuse of another pupil.** p-009:P238–357 makes Louis's promised proof—mana is not everything—both ingenious and ethically unstable. The preparatory handshake tests whether the new barrier can distinguish ordinary grasp from crushing pressure. It cannot. Sand escapes its attack threshold, and a hold can choke while the victim's scratching is nullified. His miniature barrier concentrates strength at his fist; its magical status lets him beat Adolf inside a system designed to prevent physical attack. Gagging ends the opponent's ability to chant without using the agreed method of depleting mana. These are demonstrated vulnerabilities, not merely his speculative proposals. Adolf is an active rival with a history of organized bullying; he is also the current recipient of pain and coercive silencing.

Louis's delight turns toward Rosalie at the precise point that her repeated “above” warning announces punishment. The repetition of the classroom water gag links two failures to read a recipient while displaying skill for her. Rutherford's rebuke draws a finer intended rule—use attack magic—than Louis's deliberately literal “I hit him with a barrier.” Once the protection is removed, their exchange becomes bodily injury: the teacher dodges his attack, knees him and punches him into a nosebleed. Comic rhythm does not make the blood metaphorical.

Rosalie subsequently cleans his face while refusing his bid to be called impressive. Her **「一緒に高等科に上がれないわよ」** (P352) reveals that she now imagines continuing alongside him. The demonstration has helped shift an actual choice without increasing her mana, and he registers her response with delight. It has not proven that her precise constraints are solved: he is physically formidable and has far more mana than she does. Strong rival to a pure-care reading: he uses a classmate and his own prowess to secure admiration. Strong rival to a pure-domination reading: his worry that he has only angered her, his restraint from the forest and her own newly expressed wish to advance are not reducible to an applause-seeking fraud. Successful uptake and harmful means must remain separate evaluations.

### Expertise can be received without inheriting a master's immobility

<a id="e012"></a>
**E012 — Major's authority grows through correction and a pupil's unflattering respect.** Chapter seven, **結界の魔女**, p-010:P4–287, moves to the end of his third middle-school year, nearly three years into tavern work. The continued food-bolting, ill-fitting boots and distrustful glance at the tap show that improved circumstances do not erase acquired habits on schedule. Sally is a practiced coworker who opens the door for an expulsion, rejects being paired with Louis and treats her own daily performance of cuteness as work toward a desired person's esteem. His joke about “wearing a cat” meets an independent account of effort. Row worries about the pupil's festival time; Gore offers extra wages. Their interests neither revolve entirely around Louis's ascent nor become interchangeable forms of support.

The imposed research presentation is a condition added because of persistent misconduct after he has passed the normal academic and magical requirements. Kara independently offers help with presentation materials, and Louis bows because he perceives no concealed price. Owen independently asks permission before bringing his parents into their shared room. Louis respects the request, the parents' support and time together; Owen can call the punishment self-inflicted and still recognize the honor of presenting. Their future tournament banter is mutual anticipation, not proof at this stage of a result. Owen's report of Kara's seven concurrent spells and predicted sage appointment is hearsay here, with actual demonstration still to come.

Louis chooses barriers because they interest him and remain improvable, not simply because Rutherford orders the topic. Major answers the death of her revered teacher by imagining an irreplaceable technical limit. Louis's retort—research is even more necessary if its originator is dead—does not deny the predecessor's achievement. It opposes reverence that would arrest succession. His respect for Major rests on having watched alternatives and read their papers; he identifies the expert available here rather than flattering an idealized founder. She catches multiple errors and supplies newer, more efficient connections than the older formula contains. The narrator expressly attributes her flexibility to teaching's continuing need to learn.

Her initial “I fall far short” repeats a recognition problem in a teacher's voice. The boy's informal **「結界の魔女」** is not an official epithet awarded by the guild. It names knowledge and pride he actually recognizes; her laughter receives the judgment while explaining the standard the title would demand. At the same time he regards threats and bribes as preferable to flattery. Respect has become possible within a repertoire that still includes coercion; neither the softening teacher nor the earnest student is made uniformly gentle. His unequal vision and the cost of glasses, newly noticed during close written work, also connect a future public style to bodily and financial conditions rather than an already chosen aristocratic persona.


<a id="e013"></a>
**E013 — the festival places public credit beside hidden labor and continuing cruelty.** Chapter eight, **大忙しの学園祭**, p-011:P4–383, compresses paid work, humiliation, research and two generations' recognition failures into one day. Sally's choice of disguise is not neutral amusement to Louis: the narration explicitly recalls the brothel keeper's threat to put him in a dress and sell his usefulness differently. Extra wages, needed clothes and stationery, and his promise to leave Owen's family undisturbed narrow his immediate alternatives. He objects to the old wound and agrees reluctantly. Sally answers that he does much the same to others; the reply has pointed relevance without proving she knows the whole history. Her resilience to his threatening face is not consent for everyone else to endure it.

Adolf's focalized resentment includes real differential treatment—Louis has been selected for a rare presentation—while treating his own provocations as small and his conduct as good. He burns the uniform to force public disgrace. Louis's retaliation exceeds recovery of the loss: the ending reveals stripped exposure, suspension from a tree and smoke torment. The narration withholds the full act until after the public achievement, preventing the presentation from closing the day's moral account. A deserved academic success and an act the next chapter calls an infamous wrongdoing coexist in one person and one afternoon.

The successful presentation is visibly collaborative. Major's correction, Kara's assistance and friends' collection of other papers support Louis's labor. His conspicuous supervisor credit places **クラリス・メイジャー** above his own name (P260). Its reception matters: a teacher who expected to retire without leaving a name can now be proud of how her instruction is recorded. He modulates register before expert questioners without losing his accent, and skeptical listeners begin to attend. Public style is acquired situational work; it does not require a claim that all his outward courtesy is either his essence or a lie. Major recommends broad training with Rutherford because his capacities exceed her specialty, even though Louis had intended to join her laboratory. Her departure is her decision, connected to age, strength and her master's death, not simply an obstacle the hero must conquer.

Their parting promise gives an epithet an interpersonal origin. His proposed “Barrier Magician” will let her say she taught him as the “Barrier Witch.” This is a future claim made here, before guild recognition, and a way of carrying a living teacher's contribution forward. It differs from immobilizing a dead master's formula. Her praise and his boast are immediately interrupted by discovery of the assault; her recognition does not exempt him from the conditions she names.

Two adjacent viewpoints complicate the event further. Mary takes the little Raul away before Louis's presentation; she hears his loneliness and offers a secret stall visit against grandmother's rule. The boy follows Woodman's medical-device argument but wants school friends, not merely technical inheritance. Mary's momentary kindness neither makes her omniscient nor establishes what she saw in a presentation she misses. Rosalie approaches her father herself and receives permission to continue. His later admission to Sanders—that he imposed ideals and was too harsh—confirms a paternal cause she did not invent. His belief that she must now dislike him is also not something he has told her. Sanders's “conversation matters” identifies the failure without performing the conversation or repairing the family.

<a id="e014"></a>
**E014 — Lionel's readiness to meet an equal does not abolish rank's effects.** Chapter nine, **悪童と金のゴリラ**, p-012:P4–333, retrospectively identifies the soup customer and his attendant. At the stall (p-011:P81–125) Lionel apologized for Nate's tactless poison-check request while acknowledging its necessity, asked to receive the food again and credited its taste. Louis's response protected Gore's and Row's actual craft. The customer did not yet know that the worker was a future fellow pupil. The later royal identification changes Louis's risk, not the earlier soup's quality or his words.

Lionel physically intervenes in a fight he does not understand and receives a headbutt. His claim to be an interfering passerby is sincere, but teachers' panic and Adolf's abasement demonstrate how little a prince can make the event ordinary by saying so. Nate supplies the important limit: some people are more comfortable showing a degree of deference. Lionel accepts this correction. The attendant's bland “wall” persona includes observation, royal-maternal instructions, tactical concealment and dangerous capability; he is twenty-seven, not the peer his appearance suggests. His explanation of Louis's injury as a fall is an actual public cover story. It is not narrator fact, and Louis's later humiliation at the rumor is a cost of it.

Minerva's relative neutrality explains an enrollment Serendia could not supply under Clockford's governance. Louis then infers factions from sparse greetings, Adolf's family connection and known princely ages. These are an adolescent's plausible political readings, not privileged access to every faction or the later Felix/Isaac history. The younger princes' ages and reported health establish the childhood setting.

The duel revises his estimate of Lionel through observed technique. A weak magic sword and declining mana do not mean poor swordsmanship. Louis wants to continue outside the protective boundary because the magically weighted contest hides a worthwhile bodily opponent. Lionel interprets the invitation as accommodation and explicitly accepts. This agreed contest differs from the earlier ambushes and gagging, while still creating real danger and a head injury. Louis loses. Lionel carries him for care, apologizes and remains concerned; the recipient experiences the public carrying as intolerable embarrassment. Rosalie's care and censure have still another temperature. Louis is capable of both admiring a rival and resenting the rival's familiarity with Rosalie. Subsequent lunch brings school relations into a workplace that had never seen his friends, with everyone exercising an unwelcome freedom to tease or insist. Ordinary social exposure becomes a new discomfort he cannot solve simply by frightening one exploiter away.

<a id="e015"></a>
**E015 — hidden advocacy becomes knowable, while a particular apology receives a particular answer.** Chapter ten, **悪童のアルコール戦争**, p-013:P4–271, makes communal life visible through buying ink and cards before the spectacular break-in. Owen calls charms foolish while keeping his corps wish under his pillow; Louis looks at love ink's price despite scoffing. Neither boy's worldly tone exhausts his hopes. Cheap black ink, unaffordable blue ink and alcohol spending keep desire inside a finite budget.

Confiscation is not proof that the teachers plan to drink Louis's liquor. His laxative substitution rests partly on that false assumption; Rutherford is a nondrinker and privately considers returning it after a reflection paper. His continued distrust of water has an intelligible rural history but wrongly denies present infrastructure and, unknowingly, Rosalie's father's contribution. Her departure from the quarrel with Lionel and Nate refuses to be conscripted into another scheme. He takes her hairpin without asking and initially construes later knowledge she withheld as an impressive refusal to fear or favor him. This admiration does not repair the taking by itself.

Overheard praise first startles Louis, then the criticism penetrates because he can now name people who have cared for him. The decisive new information is that Rosalie and Lionel repeatedly explain circumstances to Rutherford privately; reprimands had concealed advocacy from its beneficiary. **「他人の厚意を蔑ろにする人間は、上には行けねぇよ。いずれ孤立する」** (P167) challenges his proud picture of independent survival. It does not prove the adult speaker's methods benign. Knowing the boy is on the wall, Rutherford's sneeze-inducing smoke blocks chanting and prolongs a dangerous hanging until he falls; restraint and a hundred overnight pages follow. The next missed class and bandaged hand make the injury consequential. “Soft on him” is MacLagan's relational appraisal, disputed by the boy's experience and qualified by the shown violence.

The later reconciliation is limited and explicit. Louis brings an ornamented replacement pin and says **「……悪かった」** (P235) after acknowledging the waterworks insult; he does not give a comprehensive account of every wrong. Rosalie accepts by wearing it despite her self-deprecating view of her appearance, says thank you and offers her imperfectly practiced scones. She had prepared food before receiving the apology. His remaining grimace over tea concerns bitterness rather than refusal of her father's water, a distinction her question makes possible. His compliment pleases her and makes her avert her eyes; he mistakes the movement for continued anger. Alternating access to their interiors creates dramatic irony, not a license to call either a flawless mind reader. A real received apology, maintained difference in taste and unresolved misreading coexist. This supplies a precise comparison with the retained V12 correction: apology must be established by words or an attributable act in its own scene, never imported from the general pattern of repair.


### A future can be shared without making it the same future

<a id="e016"></a>
**E016 — the tournament recognizes skill through coordination, constraints and public visibility.** Chapter eleven, **研究室対抗魔法戦大会**, p-014:P4–280, makes collective timing part of the boy's competence. His water does not extinguish the composite fire bird; its steam conceals Rutherford's smoke. Kara protects him before he notices the ambush. Owen's initial hit costs substantial mana, and his subsequent tracking flame, net and Adolf's remote wind constitute a genuinely effective combination. Adolf's long-range accuracy is explicitly exceptional; moral dislike does not invalidate his technique. The two senior teachers' capacities similarly exceed their classroom personae. MacLagan's water can avoid an earthen prison by changing shape; Rutherford cannot rely on smoke against him. Their matchup is not settled by one global hierarchy.

Rules shape the event rather than remove invention. Professors may maintain only one spell, Kara cannot use a star lance powerful enough to break the protection, and observers can now see concealed misconduct. The projection grew from Louis's own research, through other researchers' development: his contribution creates oversight inconvenient to him. Meanwhile the teachers have come to accept barrier striking as analogous to a magic sword. This is an institutional revision in category, not a later discovery that gagging and choking had always been harmless. He still has to abandon those methods for this contest.

Rutherford changes the terrain so hidden enemies must move, flights gain an advantage and Adolf loses the known geography his remote attacks require. Louis and Kara preserve enough distance to work independently while giving support; requests and answers make the coordination explicit. His reflection barrier draws on retired Major's papers. It works once against Owen and breaks; accurate return depends on angle, distance and the incoming attack. The later partial reflection of spirit-king water sweeps away Louis, both teachers and nearby competitors. Achievement includes uncontrolled consequences and a limit to durability. The narrator does not supply a clean final victory scene. The reference panel later attributes Redmond's continuing survival records to hiding; that separate paratextual fact must not be inflated into a narrated tournament championship.

Rosalie's spectator viewpoint changes the meaning of the applause. She recognizes the bodily skill and high precision his supposedly simple barrier fighting requires, and believes he will become great. Her own reserves have not grown. His delight, directed imaginatively toward her through a projection without sound, cannot reveal her decision back to him. She experiences a need to choose, not merely a deficit to be compensated by cheering harder. Formally, the silent image makes unequal reception visible: many see the same accomplishment, but the one recipient he most wants to impress is also measuring the future she cannot share in the same way.

<a id="e017"></a>
**E017 — Rosalie's decision corrects both a savior story and an entirely wasted education.** Chapter twelve, **背負うもの、覚悟の形**, p-015:P4–120/P170–189, first lets Owen have the research presentation Louis disdains. His distress over four stumbles coexists with successful answers; Louis attends, assesses it fairly, brings sweets and then eats more than his share. Owen explicitly keeps the last one. Care does not require surrendering appetite or accepting unlimited claims. Louis's present freelance plan is also real: he believes skill can secure food and the Barrier Magician epithet without guild service. The corps decision has not yet been made.

His overhearing of Rosalie's planned withdrawal initially produces a harsh revision of his own earlier encouragement: perhaps it had been imposition rather than encouragement. That is his frightened self-questioning. Rosalie supplies the more discriminating account: she does not regret advanced school, the knowledge remains useful, and he helped her see possibilities beyond mana. She chooses a doctor like Woodman, able to address mana-related conditions; the new path neither cures the physiological constraint nor renders prior study valueless. She thanks him while leaving. An interpretation of gratitude as an obligation to stay would contradict what she says and does.

Her later solitude prevents an uncomplicated “found her true calling” closure. She remembers a single contemptuous remark from her father, his withdrawal from teaching her magic and her compensatory rule that everything else must be done perfectly. Guilt about failing the expected magical future will persist. Louis's support becomes something she consciously elects to remember. Neither his encouragement nor medical study has magically dissolved filial hurt. She also decides against confessing because she believes it would burden him when she leaves. That private restraint is not his actual preference.

Lionel then distinguishes the country's welfare from possessing its throne. His brothers' safety matters whether he or either of them becomes king. Louis's admiration of this elder-brother stance is available now; it must not be made childhood knowledge of later replacement, crimes or adult political outcomes. Lionel's reading that Rosalie has chosen to carry inherited expectation corrects Louis's claim that birth should be irrelevant, without making every expectation rightful. Chosen responsibility can be meaningful even when its origins are unequal. Louis is ashamed that he cannot understand its weight, not suddenly free of all self-regard.

<a id="e018"></a>
**E018 — Row's lantern changes the imagined unit of marriage.** p-015:P124–166 returns large abstractions about bearing responsibility to a cart on a cold road. Louis can pull the cart more effectively than frail Row can push it, but asks him to light the way. Help fits a person's actual capacity. The narration also gives the flame its affective use: the northern boy is comforted by fire even before full darkness. This ordinary redistribution of labor resists an account in which only the most powerful person's contribution counts.

Sally's engagement is to the older man she had desired, independently of Louis. Row's explanation that the fiancé respects Gore **and Row** supplies the missing dimension of “proper.” Louis had equated marriage with purchasing a woman's release; caring for her alone had seemed sufficient. Sally calls Row family-like, and the fiancé honors that relation. Row's own happiness is given time, with damp eyes and a gentle smile. His long friendship with Gore and work after Gore's wife's death explain the relation without reducing it to blood, ownership or merely employment. The final reference page adds his former noble-house work and retirement after unreasonable demands; this is a paratextual addition, not knowledge everyone expresses on this walk.

Louis's renewed wish for family does not invalidate Shona's earlier refusal to label the brothel one. What has changed is the imaginable practice: attachment reaches people the beloved values. His thought of Rosalie's caring hands is selective and possessive in vocabulary—only what he can hold—but is interrupted and expanded by Row's example. The source supports a movement toward accepted obligations, not proof that his existing thought has ceased to carry control.

<a id="e019"></a>
**E019 — the window stages a refusal he promises to accept.** p-015:P190–228 makes entry an explicit condition rather than an accomplished fact. Louis arrives improperly at the window but stops on its frame. **「お前が拒むなら、俺はここから先には進まない。友人のまま、お前を見送るから」** (P202) establishes what he proposes to do if rejected. Rosalie answers with her own declaration and initiates fingertip contact before he interlaces their hands. She accepts a future in which she becomes a doctor and he comes for her as an accomplished magician; he does not demand she abandon medicine or remain in Minerva to secure the relationship.

The embrace is accompanied by a second invitation to state dislike, because he recognizes her tendency to endure quietly. Her first **「言わないわよ」** is clarified by **「嫌じゃない」** and her own arms around him (P220/P223–224). The scene supplies affirmative conduct and speech, not silence interpreted for convenience. His final refusal to restrain himself if she does not refuse remains local language spoken after this reciprocity; it cannot become a general permission rule. The two original illustrations separate her opening movement from the mutual embrace (p293/p295), allowing the threshold to occupy duration. Rival: a declaration of future marriage simply claims her. Her articulated medical future, offered refusal and returned declaration resist that. Opposite rival: this consent makes Louis's conduct generally safe. The earlier taking of the pin and violence toward other recipients remain on the same record, while her tendency to suppress discomfort remains a named vulnerability.

<a id="e020"></a>
**E020 — gentlemanly style is learned for love and rivalry, with an ending that exposes its mismatch.** Chapter thirteen, **悪童卒業**, p-016:P4–200, and **初めての竜討伐**, p-017:P4–68, supply a chain of revisions rather than a born ambition. Row's “proper” becomes guild membership, advanced qualification and a corps post. Owen accepts the apparently impure motive because readiness for the work matters more than a noble-sounding origin.
