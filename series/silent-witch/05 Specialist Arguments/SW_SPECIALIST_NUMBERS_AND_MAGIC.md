# Silent Witch — Expertise, Mathematics and the Constraints of Magic

## 1. Thesis

*Silent Witch* is often summarized as the story of a girl too shy to speak who became the only person in the world able to cast magic without chanting. That summary is accurate, but it hides the series' larger design. Numbers in this series are not a talent added to a character. They are one faculty that the books run through every register available to them: a way of seeing, a hiding place, a weapon, a limit, a form of evidence and, finally, a body of research that changes the law. And magic is not built as a fund of wonder. It is built as an economy: breath, seconds, mana, spell slots, land, tools and licences are all scarce, and every rule the narration states is a price.

The two halves of that design explain each other. Because magic is an economy, expertise in it means economy: doing more with less breath, less time and less power. That is exactly what Monica's numerical mind is good at, and it is why the series can call her a genius of numbers rather than a genius of power. Because her numbers began as a refuge from a man who beat her for speaking her father's figures aloud, the same faculty that makes her the most economical mage in the kingdom is also the faculty that keeps her out of reach of people. The series does not resolve that doubleness. It keeps both sides in view and lets their balance shift: numbers stop being a refuge and become evidence, and her father's burned formulas return as an instrument that helps move the law.

The economy also carries the ethics. The recurring wrong in this world is not the use of great power. It is spending what belongs to someone else: a spirit's life inside armour, a spirit housed in a man without his consent, a village's land, a rival's research, another person's mind. The forbidden arts and their regulation are the places where the series asks who may spend what, and on whose authority.

This argument has one thesis, stated in three parts:

1. **The faculty.** Monica's numbers are a single mechanism with several uses. The series shows the mechanism changing function, not changing nature (SW-C-0002, SW-C-0018, SW-C-0035).
2. **The system.** Magic is a rule-bound economy of scarce resources. Competence is measured by economy under constraint, and the series ranks quantity of power below understanding and control (SW-W-0026; SW-C-0060; SW-C-0103).
3. **The ethics.** Magic's costs and restrictions shape plot and moral judgment. The series' crimes are thefts from that economy, and its laws are rationing devices whose justice the books test rather than assume (SW-C-0080, SW-C-0083).

## 2. Evidence, order and knowledge

### 2.1 What counts as evidence

The argument works from the frozen readings and the master ledger at commit 60b21a7. Claims are cited by ledger ID (SW-C-, SW-W-, SW-F- and others) and observations by unit and number, for example "V04 obs. 085" or "ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 111". No Japanese is quoted. Where a reading's English paraphrase carries the point, this argument paraphrases it again.

Some of the most memorable statements about Monica's magic sit in paratext: the bilingual chapter-opener plates. A plate in V02 explains her chantless casting as instant derivation of a formula's optimal solution (V02 obs. 011). A plate in V06 calls her accuracy terrifying and not a human act (V06 obs. 011). A plate in -another- (upper) calls Louis a genius of hard work (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 004). These are cited here only as paratext. Where the narrative gives the same words to a character, as V06 does by assigning the plate's words to Hubert (V06 obs. 156; SW-F-0037), the narrative instance is the evidence.

### 2.2 Four orders kept apart

The series is read in one order and happens in another. The argument keeps four orders distinct.

- **Source order** is the order of the volumes: V01 to V04, IV -after-, V05 to V09, IX -extra-, V10 to V12, then the prequel volumes -another- (upper) and (lower).
- **Diegetic order** is the order of events. Louis's school years come first. Monica's childhood, her father's execution when she was ten, a year with her uncle, her adoption by Hilda Everett, Minerva, and the Sage selection at fifteen all precede V01, in which she is seventeen.
- **Revelation order** is the order in which the reader learns these things. Monica's past arrives backwards and in pieces: as a comic explanation (V01), a drugged memory (V02), a rival's confession (V03), a sober prologue (V04) and an adoptive mother's flashback (IX -extra-). The Sage selection, which happened before V01, is narrated in full only in the last book read, -another- (lower).
- **Participant knowledge** is what each character knows. Isaac, then known to everyone as Felix, admires the Silent Witch's papers while sitting beside her and does not know her (V03 obs. 103; SW-C-0008). Monica does not know that the young man whose study is forbidden is forbidden because his magic would expose his identity; the reader learns that at V08 (V08 obs. 153).

Prequel evidence is marked **retrospective** throughout. It was learned last, it happens first, and it does not revise frozen readings of the numbered volumes.

## 3. The faculty: numbers before magic

### 3.1 A way of seeing

The earliest scene of Monica's life that the series supplies is not magical. At about five she asks her father for a tape measure so that she can work out the areas and volumes of the things around her. He gives it to her, and she measures the furniture and both their hands and feet until the markings wear away (V01 obs. 043; repeated in IX -extra- obs. 041). Her father's sayings, quoted as remembered voice, frame what follows: that the world, and the human body too, is full of numbers; that when the superfluous is stripped away the numbers that remain are simple; that the harder the challenge, the more enjoyable (V01 obs. 043–045; SW-C-0018).

This is how Monica perceives. She does not remember faces. She remembers the angles and widths that compose them, and V01 presents this as a practical method a bodyguard adopts: remember people as numbers (V01 obs. 028). She recognizes a hooded figure in the dark by the ratio of the back of his head and the lengths of his torso and legs (V01 obs. 042). She reconstructs a broken pot's size and weight from a handful of shards and cannot understand why Nero cannot (V01 obs. 054). The same habit persists late into the series: at V10 she recognizes a prisoner late because she remembers faces by ratio (V10 obs. 057).

The faculty is also a pleasure. Shown the student council's ledgers, she comes alive, finds three discrepancies at a glance and works through every year on record. The narration says she loves beautiful equations as others love artworks, and that an error in a ledger is a stain on a masterpiece; it adds that she had been starved of numbers (V01 obs. 068). When she proves to Isaac, then known as Felix, that his proportions follow the golden ratio, her sentences become long and unbroken for the length of the proof (V01 obs. 049). Calculation is one of the conditions under which her voice works (V01 §6.7).

### 3.2 A hiding place

The same faculty is a refuge, and the series shows exactly where the refuge came from. In revelation order the reader first sees only effects. In V01 she sinks into computing a riddle-song's number series when frightened, and the narration calls it half an escape from reality (V01 obs. 014). In a dream after a blow to the head, an angry drinking man shouts, she follows a rule that one must say nothing unnecessary, and she counts the series (V01 obs. 037). When she is struck by Bridget, a passage in a child's broken grammar states the mechanism: the more pain or fear she meets, the deeper she sinks into numbers, because while she thinks of them she does not have to feel, and the world of numbers never hurts her (V01 obs. 071). The same number series, the one in which each term is the sum of the two before it, serves escape, memory, explanation and punishment within one volume (SW-F-0006).

V02 supplies the history, in a delirium after poisoning. Her father is burned as a heretic; she forces her eyes open in the smoke to memorize the numbers on his burning papers, as proof that he had lived; her uncle beats her for reciting them and for saying her father's work had value; she promises to stay silent in front of people if only he will stop (V02 obs. 044, 045). V04 then retells this soberly. After the execution she survived her uncle's beatings and starvation by repeating her father's formulas, a perfect world that did not hurt, until the defence consumed her: she stopped recognizing people as people, stopped understanding speech and answered only in computed numbers (V04 obs. 003, 004). Hilda Everett, her father's former assistant, ended it by continuing the number series Monica was reciting and calling her by name (V04 obs. 006, 007).

The ledger's formulation holds these together: pain, fear or exposure drive Monica into numerical absorption; the habit began as a child's escape from her uncle into her father's formulas and as a vow to keep his numbers (SW-C-0002). IX -extra- adds Hilda's memory of the newly adopted child, who spoke numbers more easily than words and explained that numbers were not frightening (EXTRA09 obs. 003; SW-CR-0399).

The doubleness matters for everything after it. The inheritance her father gave her, the thing her uncle beat her for, and the place she has hidden ever since are one history (V02 §6.9). The series never lets her numerical mind be only a gift, and never lets it be only a wound. This argument describes that mechanism and does not name it clinically.

### 3.3 What the faculty cannot reach

From V01 the series marks the faculty's limit. Drinking coffee from her father's pot, Monica tries to apply his second saying to a person and finds that she cannot: an equation yields its answer, but what counts as superfluous in a heart does not (V01 obs. 044). Lana Colette, teaching her to braid, supplies the counter-principle: some things are learned by hand, not by number (V01 obs. 047). Monica says she has attachments only to equations and formulas and cannot attach to people (V01 obs. 066). Her conduct in the same volume contradicts her, which is the point.

The limit persists to the boundary. At IX -extra- she cannot tell what other people mean by "normal" and wishes it could be quantified (EXTRA09 obs. 078). At V12 Isaac places her hand on his racing heart; she proposes to measure it with instruments and concludes that he has a cold (V12 obs. 070, 106; SW-CR-0669). The comedy is gentle, but it is the same structure as the ledgers: she reads what can be measured exactly and misreads what cannot.

## 4. Chantless casting: one mechanism, three explanations

### 4.1 Cause and capacity

The series' founding premise, chantless casting, is explained three ways, and the books never fully reconcile them.

The narrator's account in V01 is deliberately deflating. Humans must chant to shape magic (SW-W-0001); shortened chanting halves the time but still takes seconds (SW-W-0002); one girl was so shy that she failed practical examinations she could not speak in, and so she learned to cast without speaking. The narrator calls the reason not in the least moving and the result the end point of effort aimed off at an angle (V01 obs. 007).

The public account is computational. MacLaggan tells his class that his former pupil understood formulas so deeply that she came to cast without chanting, and that she overturned the theory of modern magic (V02 obs. 016). Isaac, then known as Felix, explains chanting privately as the written-out intermediate steps of a hard equation, and the Silent Witch as the one person who goes straight to the answer (V02 §6.3; SW-W-0027).

The third account is biographical and arrives in V03. At Minerva her one friend, Bernie, offered to teach her to halve her chant. At the next practical she did not halve it; she cast with none, and the friendship broke on his jealousy (V03 obs. 006, 007; SW-C-0027).

The ledger holds all three as layers of one claim: chantless casting began as avoidance of speech, and silence and mastery are one mechanism; the public account explains the capacity as instant computation (SW-C-0001). The distinction between cause and capacity is the key. The computational account is not false. Monica plainly does compute faster than anyone around her. But it is an account of what she can do. The narrative's interest is in why she does it. The kingdom admires as genius what began, on the evidence, as a child's defence against being heard (V02 §6.3).

### 4.2 The limits of the premise

The series states limits on chantless casting from the start and respects them.

- She can cast roughly eight in ten known formulas without chanting, not all of them (V01 obs. 008; SW-W-0003). Which formulas resist is never stated.
- Because she cannot chant in front of people, any magic she shows identifies her. At school this makes her the most powerful person present and the least free to act (V01 obs. 033; V02 obs. 025).
- Summoning a higher being requires a chant of respect that cannot be omitted (SW-W-0028).

The last limit is the most important. At V02's climax, to stop an assassination device, Monica must summon a spirit king, and so must speak. The ritual chant names her as one of the Seven Sages, in front of Casey, the person who has been deceiving her (V02 obs. 084). The reading observes that this is the first time she says her own name and title aloud before another person, and that the book shows a single occasion on which speech and power are the same act (V02 §6.14). The rule of the world forces speech at exactly the point where her silence and her power would otherwise coincide. The pattern recurs: at V10 she again speaks the ritual chant she cannot omit and names herself (V10 obs. 109).

### 4.3 What Monica thinks it is worth

Monica's own valuation of chantless casting is low. At V06 she says it only makes casting faster and holds it of little worth (V06 obs. 103). When Hubert Dee stages an imitation, firing planted tool-arrows from his earring and rings while humming, three observers in turn take it for chantless magic (V06 obs. 118, 145, 146). Monica explains the trick and dismisses the thing being imitated (V06 obs. 160, 162).

The ledger records the effect: Hubert's imitation makes chantless magic a threat in others' eyes and a thing of little worth in Monica's (SW-C-0055). The imitation reveals what each side values. Hubert and the onlookers value the spectacle and the terror of the unchanted attack. Monica values accuracy, economy and the beauty of a formula. She prefers an afternoon with friends to victory and honour (V06 obs. 164). The thing that made her famous is, to her, a convenience.

## 5. The system: magic as an economy

### 5.1 How the rules are built

The world records in the ledger's SW-W section are not a single codex. They accumulate volume by volume, from different speakers, and the ledger keeps their sources apart: a narrator's rule, a character's belief and a single demonstration are distinguished, and one success does not prove an unlimited rule (ledger §4, preamble). Read together, though, they form a consistent shape. Almost every rule is a constraint on a scarce resource.

| Ritual speech | Summoning a higher being needs a chant of respect that cannot be omitted. | SW-W-0028; V02 obs. 084; SW-W-0127 |
| Spell slots | Holding two spells is very hard and is said to be a mage's limit; a former Sage held seven. | SW-W-0029, 0087, 0151; ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 068, 072; V05 obs. 058 |

Two features of this table matter. First, the constraints are stated in numbers wherever the series can manage it: seconds, mana figures, spell counts, grades of reflection barrier from five down to one with humans reaching the second (SW-W-0093; V07 obs. 090). The series' world speaks the same language as its heroine. Second, the constraints interact. Flight is mana-hungry, and flying limits Monica to one further spell (SW-W-0049). The magic battle converts damage into mana drain, so that the duel is literally a contest of budgets (SW-W-0043, 0109; V09 obs. 050). Even the magic-battle barrier is costly: it needs land, tools and two mages to keep it (SW-W-0071; V09 obs. 036).

### 5.2 Three talents, ranked

The series also tells the reader how to grade competence within this economy. MacLaggan's lecture in V02 divides talent into three: quantity of power, understanding of formulas and control of power (SW-W-0026; V02 obs. 016, 017). The rest of the series can be read as a sustained argument that the first matters least.

That argument begins with a joke. Monica's measured mana is just over 200, enough to make her rare but not unique (V02 obs. 017; SW-W-0079). Glen Dudley, measured in the same class, drives the needle off the scale and cracks the crystal, and everyone assumes the instrument has failed (V02 obs. 018). The most powerful person in the room by the instrument's measure is not the most competent mage in it. The instrument reads only the first talent.

### 5.3 What Monica's genius actually is

If quantity is not her distinction, what is? The series' answer is economy. Her signature effects are small, exact and cheap.

- Against Cyril's spray of ice arrows in V01, each arrow meets a small flame of its own, none of which touches the trees; the narration says that to anyone trained, the showy serpent of fire is nothing beside those small precise fires (V01 obs. 098).
- She stops V02's assassination device not by overpowering it but by rewriting Louis's barrier: inward trigger, fire only, no oxygen, compressed to the device's size, until it goes out like a capped lamp (V02 obs. 086).
- Under the ancient tool's volleys in V03 she computes the arrows' speed and the shield she can hold, then cancels her own flight so that the arrows can come only from above and she can hold two shields instead of one, a manoeuvre the narration says only a chantless caster could make. She uses the three-and-a-half seconds between volleys to climb straight at the target (V03 obs. 096).
- At V04, from the last line of an enemy's chant, she identifies an imperial school of mana sequencing and spins a web of matching lightning that absorbs the attack (V04 obs. 086; SW-W-0060).
- At V07 she says that buying time is something she is rather good at, and computes barriers for economy against an upper spirit (V07 obs. 006, 101).
- At V11 she reads and detaches a composite spell in five seconds (V11 obs. 128).

Interference with another mage's formula requires far superior skill and is wildly improper (SW-W-0013). Monica does it twice in V01 alone: she rewrites the brooch on Cyril's chest, restraining herself from improving it too much (V01 obs. 101), and she unpicks Thornley's mind-destroying spell like knotted thread and reweaves it, more intricate and more beautiful, until its light becomes white butterflies (V01 obs. 106). Thornley, watching, recognizes the girl of fifteen who had published a formula more advanced than one he had worked on for twenty years (V01 obs. 107).

That detail points to the side of her expertise the series takes most seriously and dramatizes least: she is a researcher. Isaac, then known as Felix, summarizes her paper on positional coordinates in wide-area formulas accurately enough that she agrees in silence with every word (V03 obs. 103). At V10 she tests and first uses a new formula that stabilizes spirit-king summoning and allows two summonings at once (V10 obs. 039, 115, 121). Half a year after the school mission she runs plant-enchantment research with Raul and Cyril, funded against famine, and suggests ringing the plots with peas because they absorb soil mana (EXTRA09 obs. 162, 163). Her numbers become public work.

### 5.4 Beauty as a constraint

Monica's sense of economy is also an aesthetic, and the series gives it consequences. At V07 she can cast quadruple reinforcement only with a split formula and says it is not beautiful; she refuses it until the whistle must be destroyed, then splits the formula and laments it (V07 obs. 053, 110, 111). The reading notes that Bradford, the Cannon Mage, shares the aesthetic (V07 §6.1). Her one recorded rule for anger in V01 is aesthetic too: she will not allow a perfect formula to be defiled, and Thornley's botched spell, like his falsified ledgers, is a defilement (V01 obs. 105). The beauty she prizes is the beauty of a solution with nothing superfluous, which is her father's second saying turned into a working standard.

### 5.5 The selection, retrospectively

The clearest single statement of her kind of genius comes last in source order and first in diegetic order. -another- (lower), read after V12, narrates the Sage selection at which Monica, fifteen, and Louis Miller, already head of the Magic Corps, were both chosen (retrospective; ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 013, 156). Its judge of the matter is Rutherford, Louis's master. To Mary he calls Louis a genius of effort and Monica a genius of numbers (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 104).

The battle bears him out. At the opening bell, without a word of chant, she opens a wind spirit king's gate above the forest (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 107). With a finger she casts a remote wind spell that puts Adolf out of the contest, and when asked she says it is only a lot of calculation; she has never used it in combat before (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 109, 111). She casts the economy formula without chanting too (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 112). The ledger records the result: Monica, a genius of numbers, wins the selection by wordless spirit-king summoning, remote casting and economy formulas (SW-C-0103).

Revelation order makes this retrospective account an answer to a question the reader has carried since V01. There Monica believes she was a makeweight Sage, and Louis, in the secret episode, remembers the opposite: she wept and panicked and shut him out with spells whose power, range and complexity should have required long chants (V01 obs. 013, 117; SW-C-0004). The prequel confirms Louis's memory and shows what the spells were. Monica herself, at the time, did not know she had won by skill; Louis told her so and told her not to be servile (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 159).

## 6. A gallery of competences

The series sets Monica among other mages who embody different positions in the economy. Each is defined less by how much power they have than by how they spend it.

### 6.1 Louis: the genius of effort

Louis Miller is introduced in V01 as Monica's coercive colleague, and in the numbered volumes he is seen almost entirely from outside. His maxim, delivered over a dead fire dragon in IV -after-, is that money and violence solve most difficulties (AFTER04 obs. 099). V07 gives him a counter-maxim to Emmanuel's envy of the Sages' uniqueness: being able to do most things as a matter of course is greater (V07 obs. 078). At V09 the reader learns that he was the first "Minerva brat" and that his strength is effort and experience; he fights with barriers as fists, rams and cages, and uses the rules of the magic battle, under which trips and chokes pass the barrier (V09 obs. 057, 059–065; SW-W-0109, SW-W-0110).

The prequel, read last, shows how this style was built (retrospective). The boy who arrives at Minerva has mana of about one hundred and a wind affinity (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 073). Every effort answers a slight: his first spell answers Rutherford's contempt; a week of spells answers a challenge (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 025, 027; SW-C-0095). Every defeat becomes theory. He discovers that his hemisphere barrier blocks his own attacks and has an open floor; he reconstructs the nullification formula from a kick; he reasons that a battle barrier must draw on dense land (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 103, 104, 108, 111). From these come the barrier fist, the reflecting barrier that turns back a spirit king's water, and the Mirror Prison (ANOTHER-UPPER §6.6; obs. 196). The more he learns, the more he sees how far behind he is, but he does not break (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 157).

The ledger's revision captures what this does to the series' moral contrast. Cyril's ethic of earned, patient effort had been set against Louis's maxim of money, violence and strength; the prequel shows the maxim's holder as himself a genius of effort driven by refusal to lose, so that the opposition lies in motive and display rather than in effort (SW-C-0043; SW-CR-0766). At V11 Louis names his adult method as money, violence and connections, and says he learned the third from losing to Monica at the council (V11 obs. 183; SW-CR-0593).

The two geniuses therefore face each other across the economy. Louis has ordinary resources and spends effort to multiply them. Monica has a faculty that finds the cheapest path. At the selection, effort lost to numbers (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 104, 113). At V09, numbers plus asking for help beat effort again, and the narration names Louis's error: he assumed the Witch would never ask anyone for help (V09 obs. 072; SW-C-0033). At V11, restoring Louis from the Minerva illusion, Monica casts his own Mirror Prison without a chant (V11 obs. 148). The genius of numbers can reproduce the genius of effort's signature technique. The reverse is never shown.

### 6.2 Glen: quantity without precision

Glen Dudley is the series' test case for quantity. He has enormous power and poor accuracy; flight is his best spell and he cannot hold two spells at once (V02 obs. 015, 018, 041). Ray discloses at V05 that his mana exceeds 250 and that he once ran away with it, which is why Louis took him in (V05 obs. 115). The prequel adds the history (retrospective): a butcher's son of eleven whose mana exceeded 200, sent to Minerva by a prophecy, who lost control and was confined, and whom Louis took out of confinement with the lesson that his runaway is no enemy (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 039, 044). V06 adds that at Minerva he poured his mana into a broken formula in a night battle and lost consciousness; what the fireball caused is withheld (V06 obs. 088–090).

Raw mana in this economy is a liability before it is an asset. Glen's arc is the slow conversion of quantity into control. He trains daily on holding two spells, resists an easier path, takes the Silent Witch's written advice to hold the same spell twice first, and applies it under attack (V05 obs. 058–060). He hits Hubert in a duel with a formula learned for the purpose (V06 obs. 143, 144). By V12 he has passed the lower-mage examination (V12 obs. 155).

His one serious misuse confirms the point. In the prequel (retrospective), believing Rosalie's fall a suicide, Glen seals her memory with an incomplete, quasi-forbidden spell, forced through by sheer quantity of mana after reading on mind interference almost daily (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 118). The ledger records it as misplaced mercy, privately punished by restriction (SW-C-0101). Quantity let him cast what understanding should have stopped him from casting.

### 6.3 Cyril: surplus, effort and a condition turned to use

Cyril Ashley represents earned effort in the series' moral argument (SW-C-0043), and his magic is also a lesson in the body's economy. A poor distant relation adopted into a marquisate, outshone by his adoptive sister, he drove himself at magic until he developed over-absorption, a constitution that keeps taking in power past its capacity until it poisons itself (V01 obs. 095; SW-W-0010). The marquis's brooch, which drains the excess, became his token of belonging. In V01 his crisis is treated by a duel disguised as treatment: the quickest remedy is to make the patient spend his power to exhaustion (V01 §6.9).

Monica's response to him is the first act of care she initiates in the series. She repairs the brooch and tells Nero that his constitution, managed well, is an advantage some mages court deliberately; she did not want him to think of it as a curse, as she thinks of her own talent (V01 obs. 101). Her judgment of him is economic and humane at once. A surplus that injures can, if budgeted, become a resource.

The series later makes that literal. At V09, holding Louis off during the rescue, Cyril drops his brooch and lets his condition run wild to freeze Louis; he would rather break correctness than be an ignorant passive good person (V09 obs. 066, 067). Cyril's own magic is ordinary in kind. He uses shortened chants, filling a glass with ice between explanations (SW-W-0002; V01 §6.9), and his ice is his one element by Nero's judgment (SW-W-0007). His teaching is moral rather than technical: he tells Glen that skill earned through scrapes deserves pride (AFTER04 obs. 079), and Glen prefers that to his master's maxim (AFTER04 obs. 075).

### 6.4 Isaac: a suppressed study

Isaac Walker's relation to magic is defined by prohibition, and the reason for the prohibition is one of the series' neatest joins between its magic system and its plot.

In the numbered volumes before V08 the reader sees Isaac only as Felix, the second prince, who publicly claims no talent for magic while privately reading the formula in Louis's brooch and having his spirit destroy it (V01 obs. 121; SW-C-0007). In V03 he admits to Monica, whom he does not know to be the Witch, that he is interested in magic and forbidden to study it, and he summarizes her paper accurately (V03 obs. 103). At V08 the narration explains. The present Felix is the page Isaac Walker, remade to replace the dead prince (SW-C-0063). Clockford forbids him magic because the mismatch of elemental affinity might be noticed (V08 obs. 153). Affinity in this world is inherited and fixed (SW-W-0078, SW-W-0106). The prince's affinity was earth; the spirit Isaac carried was water (SW-C-0050). A rule of the magic system, that affinity is hereditary, is exactly what makes Isaac's study dangerous.

The same rule links him to Monica's father. Venedict Rain's research was on the inheritance of mana, and his design, the black chalice, reads hereditary information by analysing the mana in blood (V08 obs. 077, 266). By the characters' agreed conclusion, that tool could show that the present prince had no blood tie to the king, and this was the motive for Venedict's death (V08 obs. 078; SW-C-0064). The heredity of affinity is therefore at once the reason Isaac must hide his magic and the reason Monica lost her father. The magic system is not scenery behind the plot. Its rules are the plot's hinge.

Isaac's study is also a study in effort under scarcity. An upper-spirit contract needs about 150 mana, the Sages' minimum, and he exhausts himself nightly to raise his own (V08 obs. 164). He learns the Silent Witch's name and reads her papers after watching her destroy the wyverns at Kerbeck, the magic he calls beautiful and the thing he can lose himself in (V08 obs. 174, 175). After V09 he becomes her apprentice by his own request (V09 obs. 169, 173). Her standard for him is absolute command of formulas, toward which she gives him thirty books (V10 obs. 163, 164; SW-C-0084). At V12 his first paper fails at a point only practice reveals, and Monica blames her own rule of six months of theory before practice (V12 obs. 111, 112). The genius of numbers learns, through her apprentice, the limit of numbers alone.

### 6.5 Rosalie, Birdland and the threshold

The economy is crueller at its edges. Rosalie Miller, in the prequel (retrospective), has mana of twenty against a middle-division mean of about seventy and a beginner's minimum of fifty; she is the daughter of a Sage, first on paper and last in mana (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 113). She crosses into dense forest to stop Louis and collapses with mana poisoning; low-mana people are poisoned sooner (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 115, 117). She leaves magic for medicine, and thanks Louis for showing her that little mana is not everything (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 199). Her father, Birdland, a Sage, had mana of 152 against a minimum of 150 (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 144). The threshold that governs a Sage's seat is a number, and the series lets the reader see a man who cleared it by two.

### 6.6 Borrowed power: Hubert, Adolf and Bradford

Three further mages complete the gallery by contrast.

- **Hubert Dee** borrows the appearance of chantless magic from planted tools (V06 obs. 160; SW-C-0055). At V12 Monica rewrites his rings to seal his magic and makes him a tutor (V12 obs. 107).
- **Adolf** relies on borrowed power: the ice bell, Thornley's formula, the first Thorn Witch's book. Louis judges that the bell had to amplify Adolf's own mana and that Adolf only borrowed another's research (V11 obs. 179; SW-C-0043).
- **Bradford**, the Cannon Mage, is the series' exception to its own ranking. He alone in the world uses sixfold reinforcement, his mana exceeds 250, and Monica cannot reproduce it (V11 obs. 096). His power feeds on his fire spirit king (V07 obs. 094). Even here the narration attaches a cost: dragon hunting has bred a power-first doctrine (V11 obs. 200; SW-W-0137). Monica later chooses Bradford as a model, not for his power, but as a master his apprentice is proud of (V11 obs. 194).

The ledger's claim for V07 states the series' verdict: the volume sets uniqueness against competence and cooperation, and favours the latter (SW-C-0060). The forest battle against Emmanuel is won not by any Sage's unique art but by planning in which each part is ordinary: a decoy, a cue, a shield (V07 obs. 100).

## 7. Numbers turned outward: weapon, limit, evidence

### 7.1 Weapon

V04 makes the faculty a weapon by choice. Faced with Yuan wearing Cyril's face and asking whether she can attack him, Monica says she can, by thinking of him as a mass of numbers (V04 obs. 085). The narration follows her into a number world where she can be merciless, and the technical display that follows is the most detailed in the series to that point (V04 obs. 086, 087). Yuan names her as someone who removes lives like pieces from a board, and she agrees: she is a heartless witch who sees people as numbers, and she will use it to protect those dear to her (V04 obs. 088). The prologue of the same volume has already let the reader hear her uncle's word for the child who spoke only numbers; Louis's judgment of her echoes it almost exactly (V04 §6.1; obs. 050).

### 7.2 Limit

The plot then exposes the agreement as half false. When Yuan threatens the real Cyril, Monica computes that the threat is a bluff and still cannot act, because the number world will not hold Cyril (V04 obs. 090). She is poisoned and captured. Afterwards she tells Louis that with Cyril's life as a shield she could not calculate anything (V04 obs. 096), and concludes that her father's saying must no longer be an excuse or an escape (V04 obs. 097). The ledger records the turn: Monica's numerical dissociation, born of abuse, became a chosen weapon and a limit (SW-C-0035). The weapon fails exactly where attachment begins. That failure is the strongest evidence that the series does not treat her numbers as a superpower. A superpower would not stop working because she loves someone.

### 7.3 Evidence

From V05 the faculty turns outward in a new way. Monica identifies the curse-caster Sams by the bodily measurements she took from a cursed dragon's memory (V05 obs. 139). As she accuses him she speaks her father's saying aloud, and the narration states that numbers, once her refuge, are not that now (V05 obs. 144; SW-CR-0120). The saying she had hidden inside becomes a public declaration. The same faculty catches a card cheat in the prologue, because she remembers every discard (V05 obs. 006).

The black chalice carries the arc further. At V08 Hilda gives Monica her father's design, hidden since his arrest, and the reading records the phrase that it is proof that her father had lived (V08 obs. 003, 267). Monica decides to use it to save the man whose existence cost her father his life (V08 obs. 268; SW-C-0067). At V09 the tool is built with the help of everyone she asked (SW-C-0072). It is meant for hereditary disease, and she adapts it to prove kinship (V09 obs. 032).

The trial then shows what numbers as evidence can and cannot do. The chalice shows its result by colour rather than number, for show, and Monica explains to her helpers that she is going to bluff in front of many people (V09 obs. 033). The tool is demonstrated honestly on Count Barrel and his son (V09 obs. 088). When Isaac's blood is tested, it turns red only because Monica secretly adds Albert's blood (V09 obs. 091). The ledger calls the verdict a staged truth: a real tool with borrowed blood, a dead culprit, a performed cure and royal ratification (SW-C-0069). Monica uses Louis's lesson that power makes truth (V09 obs. 096).

This is a sharper treatment of evidence than a simple triumph of science. The instrument is genuine and her father's numbers are vindicated as engineering. But the public cannot read numbers, so the number must be translated into colour. And the verdict the court needs is not the one the instrument would give. Monica, who could clear her father's name by full exposure, chooses to save Isaac instead (SW-C-0045, SW-C-0067). Her numbers serve as evidence and as performance in the same scene, and the series keeps the difference visible.

### 7.4 Research that changes the law

The arc closes, so far as the boundary allows, in V10's prologue. Forbidden-art crimes are now punished by life imprisonment instead of death; the black chalice was accepted as a medical tool, and medical body-manipulation magic is to be eased step by step (V10 obs. 007, 008). Monica thinks her father's research changed the country, and wishes it had happened in his lifetime (V10 obs. 009). Later she reflects that the chalice changed a penalty that might once have been death (V10 obs. 132; SW-C-0083).

In diegetic order, then, her father's numbers were burned, memorized by a ten-year-old in the smoke, beaten out of her voice, kept as a hidden refuge, turned into evidence, built into an instrument and finally written, indirectly, into law. The faculty has not changed. Its addressee has: from herself, to an enemy, to a court, to a country.

## 8. Forbidden arts and the rationing of power

### 8.1 What the law forbids, and why

The series' forbidden arts are not arbitrary. Each one rations a power over something that cannot defend itself.

- **Body magic.** All magic acting on human bodies is forbidden in Ridill, including healing, because bodies lack resistance; it is stricter than mind interference (SW-W-0030). The prequel gives the poisoning risk as the reason (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 118; SW-W-0152). The Empire has recently allowed limited medical use and body manipulation (SW-W-0044, 0059, 0100). Isaac's remade face is the series' central product of that art (SW-C-0063).
- **Mind interference.** It is a restricted or quasi-forbidden art, permitted for interrogating grave criminals or in national emergency with authorization from the mages' guild or the Seven Sages; its side effects include memory loss and unconsciousness, and unauthorized use costs a licence (SW-W-0014, 0050, 0157; ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 119). Research is allowed; few practise it.
- **The greatest taboos.** Black flame is classed with necromancy and weather control (V05 obs. 126; SW-W-0076). V11 calls the first Thorn Witch's book of black flame one of the three great forbidden arts and a mage-killer that burns the defensive barrier together with the caster (V11 obs. 087, 169; SW-W-0131).
- **Curse-craft.** It is held as a monopoly by the Albright house, distinct from magic, leaving residue and ageing its user (SW-W-0055, 0077). The crown keeps the curse house and the Roseburg magic house to balance regional nobles and the guild (SW-W-0057).

### 8.2 Who authorizes

The law's weakness is its authority. Monica's first use of mind interference, on Thornley in V01, leaves her unsure whether a Sage's special status covers her; the narration states the law and leaves the legal question as uncertain as she does (V01 obs. 110; SW-W-0014). At V03 she uses the same art on an ancient tool, as mercy rather than attack, and Lynn remarks that Mary will overlook its use in an emergency (V03 obs. 098, 099). The answer to whether her use has consequences is an institutional tolerance, not a reckoning (V03 §6.12).

The Sages as a body protect themselves the same way. At V06 they hide a member's crime to keep their independence from the noble assembly, and the volume presents this as self-protection, not justice (SW-C-0054). Glen's illicit seal on Rosalie escapes official censure through her statement and is punished privately (SW-C-0101). The pattern is consistent: the law of forbidden arts is real, but its enforcement passes through people with power, and the series shows them using discretion.

### 8.3 The forbidden category as a weapon

The series' most serious case reverses the direction. Venedict Rain was charged with necromancy, a forbidden art, on planted papers, after a curse-caster he refused had him denounced under a great noble's pressure, and was executed without trial (V05 obs. 166; SW-C-0032). The crowd that burned him chanted that he was a heretic who touched the forbidden (V02 obs. 044). The category meant to protect people from dangerous power became the instrument by which a powerful man disposed of a scientist whose research threatened him. V09's narration, from Monica's viewpoint, states that Clockford arranged the execution (SW-C-0045, SW-C-0064). The patron Sams called 閣下 remains unidentified by name (SW-C-0039), and this argument does not identify him.

The law changes only after the father's own instrument has been used before the Supreme Council (SW-C-0083). That is why the change satisfies Monica without clearing her father: the system that killed him is softened, partly by his work, but his name is not restored (V10 §6.7).

### 8.4 Spending other beings

The economy's ethics is clearest in its crimes. Again and again the wrong is to use another being as a mana source.

- Emmanuel's private research powers tools with captured spirits; the armour in the forest is driven by a forest spirit used up inside its gem (V06 obs. 193, 196).
- Darrell, unable to buy a contract stone, inscribed formulas on his sleeping brother to house a dying water spirit, and later did the same to seven men with lesser spirits, calling it a gift of power. Monica judges the art unprecedented, probably a form of body manipulation, and likely to shorten lives (V10 obs. 110, 111, 119; SW-C-0080).
- Hubert's scheme at V12 aims at a mana furnace for his family's tool business, using a village and its child; the volume exposes him, binds his magic and lets him become the child's tutor, a mixed judgment (V12 obs. 094, 107; V12 §8.3).
- Adolf borrows a bell's power and another man's research (V11 obs. 179).

Against these the series sets an uncomfortable mirror. At V07 Monica seals the ice spirit Romaria into Cyril's brooch as a stand-in for a contract stone, without a contract, and the narration calls her act her own ego (V07 obs. 126, 127). At V10 she measures it against Darrell's art and judges her own act less pure, because she saved a spirit for a person's sake while Darrell saved one for the spirit's (V10 obs. 135). Cyril claims the responsibility, and she asks to share it (SW-C-0061). The series refuses to let its heroine stand outside the economy she polices.

### 8.5 Mana at the scale of states

The economy also scales up. At V08 the young emperor explains that large wars have stopped because mages have multiplied: mages are weapons, and a modern war would kill tens of thousands and pollute the land with mana (V08 obs. 058; SW-W-0103). Monica's bargain with him to avert war is struck in this register (SW-C-0066). At V11 and V12 mana furnaces become a political project: barred where spirits or dragons live, they require roads to the north-east where dragons are few, and Isaac's road and furnace project in the East is framed as the service on which his pardon rests (V11 obs. 232; V12 obs. 100, 101; SW-C-0092). At V12 Monica reads a village's egg pattern as the record of an old-era device that drains mana, and frees the land by breaking buried rocks with spirit-king summons (V12 obs. 090, 091). The same reading of pattern that once caught a ledger cheat now clears a polluted valley.

## 9. How the economy constrains plot

The economy is not only described. It does narrative work, and the series' plots routinely turn on its costs.

1. **Concealment.** Because chantless casting is unique, any magic Monica shows betrays her. Her school life is therefore a sequence of disguised uses: a gust that looks like luck, a barrier laid over a staircase rather than her body because a barrier around her would make the fall look wrong (V01 obs. 033, 056). The staircase scene puts the three parts of her character in one minute: the finest control of magical power in the country, spent on a staircase, and then the physics of a slope, down which she rolls howling (V01 §6.6). In IV -after- her power appears only where no one can see it, and only a spirit recognizes the strongest person present (AFTER04 obs. 092, 095; SW-C-0042).
2. **Budgets in combat.** The two-spell limit decides fights. At V05 Monica cannot attack while holding two barriers and wishes for Louis's ability to merge them (V05 obs. 086). Flight costs her all but one further spell (SW-W-0049). The magic battle is won by draining the opponent's budget (SW-W-0109).
3. **Forced speech.** The ritual chant that cannot be omitted makes her speak her name at the two moments her power is greatest (V02 obs. 084; V10 obs. 109).
4. **Thresholds.** The 150-mana minimum governs a Sage's seat and an upper-spirit contract alike, and so shapes both Birdland's standing and Isaac's nightly training (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 144; V08 obs. 164).
5. **Containment.** The prison tower repels spirits and binds prisoners with anti-magic bracelets; Nero passes because he is not a spirit (V08 obs. 256, 263; SW-W-0107). A rule of classification opens the only door to Isaac.
6. **Ancient tools.** Ancient tools cannot be analysed, repaired or remade (SW-W-0046). At V03 Monica therefore refuses the easy shot at the tool and reaches for mind interference instead (V03 obs. 092). Scarcity dictates mercy.

The most important constraint is negative. The series' decisive outcomes are almost never produced by more power. V07's forest is won by ordinary roles set in advance (V07 obs. 100). V09's rescue is collective, every element supplied by people Monica asked (SW-C-0072). The genius of numbers, at her most effective, computes a plan that others carry out.

## 10. Counterarguments and counterevidence

### 10.1 "The mathematics is decorative"

**The counterargument.** The strongest objection is that the series' mathematics is a characterizing flourish, not a structure. On this view:

- The page never shows a magical formula. The reader is told that Monica derives optimal solutions, not shown any.
- The narrator's own explanation of chantless casting is psychological, not computational, and is framed as an anticlimax (V01 obs. 007). Computation is the public account, partly carried by paratext plates (V02 obs. 011).
- Monica herself thinks chantless casting is worth little, a matter of speed (V06 obs. 103).
- The mathematics that does appear is schoolroom material used for comedy: a riddle-song's number series, the golden ratio of a prince's navel, ledgers, a tape measure, laboratory balances for biscuits (V01 obs. 014, 049, 068; EXTRA09 §6.3).
- The decisive victories are won by other means: asking for help, a staged truth, political bargains (SW-C-0069, SW-C-0072). When numbers are asked to carry the weight of a crisis at V04, they fail (V04 obs. 090, 096).
- At the trial, the instrument's number is turned into a colour for show (V09 obs. 033).

**The counterevidence it rests on is real.** The series does not present mathematics as content. A reader learns no mathematics from it, and the magic system has no notation.

**The answer.** The objection mistakes what kind of mathematics the series is about. It is not mathematics as worked content. It is mathematics as disposition and as constraint, and in both senses it is load-bearing.

First, as disposition. The numerical faculty organizes Monica's perception (faces by ratio), her detection (shards, ledgers, a dragon's memory), her pleasures, her refuge and her failures. Each of the series' turning points in her development is staged as a change in what the faculty is for: refuge at V01–V02, weapon and limit at V04, evidence at V05, instrument at V08–V09, law at V10 (SW-C-0002, SW-C-0035). A decorative trait would not change function on schedule, and would not fail at the exact point where attachment begins.

Second, as constraint. The world is built in numbers: seconds of chant, a crystal scale to 250, a 150 threshold, a two-spell limit, five grades of reflection barrier, three and a half seconds between volleys (§5.1). These numbers decide fights and plots (§9). The genius the series celebrates is precisely an economy within such limits, and the selection scene states it in her own words: only a lot of calculation (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 111).

Third, the objection's best evidence actually supports the thesis. That the narrator gives the cause of chantless casting as psychological is the point of SW-C-0001: the series separates capacity, which is computational, from cause, which is a child's silence. That Monica values chantless casting little is consistent with an aesthetic of economy and beauty, which she does value intensely (V07 obs. 053, 111). That the chalice's number becomes colour shows the series thinking about the gap between evidence and persuasion, which a decorative use of numbers would not notice. And the failure at V04 is the series insisting that numbers are a human faculty with human limits.

The objection keeps one valid point. The series' account of magic is partial by design: the reader is never told which formulas resist chantless casting, how spirit-king summoning was learned, or what ancient artefacts truly are (V01 obs. 008; ANOTHER-LOWER §8.5; V12 obs. 108). The system's rules are consistent but not complete.

### 10.2 "The rules of magic are ad hoc"

**The counterargument.** The rules bend whenever the plot needs it. The two-spell limit is broken by Carla, who held seven (V05 obs. 058; SW-W-0087). Bradford alone uses sixfold reinforcement (V11 obs. 096). Monica alone casts without chant. Rutherford alone enchants smoke, which passes through every barrier (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 106). A rule with so many unique exceptions is not a rule.

**The answer.** The exceptions are named as exceptions, attributed to particular people and priced. Each is presented as extraordinary by the characters themselves, and each costs something visible: Bradford's power feeds on a spirit king and on a doctrine the narration criticizes (V07 obs. 094; V11 obs. 200); Monica's uniqueness exposes her and isolates her (V01 obs. 033; V01 obs. 090); Carla's case is reported, not shown. The ledger's own method warns against reading one demonstration as an unlimited rule (ledger §4, preamble), and the series largely observes the same discipline. The rules hold for ordinary mages and are bent only by named, costly genius. That is consistent with the thesis that the series ranks economy above uniqueness (SW-C-0060).

### 10.3 "Reading the numbers as expertise aestheticizes harm"

**The counterargument.** Monica's numerical faculty grew from abuse. To praise it as expertise is to admire the defence and forget the injury.

**The answer.** The series itself refuses that separation, and this argument follows it. The same chapters that show the faculty's brilliance show its origin in her uncle's violence and its cost in her isolation (V02 obs. 044, 045; V04 obs. 003, 004). The faculty does not become safe; it becomes hers to direct. At V04 she refuses to let her father's saying serve as an excuse (V04 obs. 097), and at V05 she speaks it as a declaration (V05 obs. 144). The argument describes mechanism and conduct, and makes no clinical claim.

## 11. Limits of this argument

**Boundary.** The argument rests on the Japanese light novels V01–V12 and the four companions, as read in the frozen snapshot at 60b21a7. No web serial, adaptation, translation or outside commentary is used. The readings' Japanese fragments are not quoted.

**Form of the evidence.** All evidence is written prose. The magic system is known only through what narrators and characters state and what scenes demonstrate. No formula is ever printed, so claims about Monica's computation are claims about how the text presents it, not about any mathematics the reader can check.

**Uneven interiority.** Monica, Isaac, Louis and Cyril are seen from inside. The Sages other than Monica and Louis, Mary, Clockford and most antagonists are seen from outside (checkpoint §4.2). Statements about why Bradford fights or why the Sages tolerate rule-breaking are inferences from conduct.

**Prequel retrospection.** The selection, Louis's training, Glen's confinement and Rosalie's mana figure come from -another- (upper) and (lower), read last. They are supplemental retrospection and do not revise any frozen numbered reading.

**Open threads at V12.** These are open and receive no prediction here:

- the ice spirit's plan, the gate of audience and the ice Spirit-King it invokes (SW-C-0091; SW-W-0141);
- the true nature of ancient artefacts, which Hubert withholds (V12 obs. 108; SW-C-0094);
- the plan, set for "next year" in IX -extra-, to relax the regulation of medical magical tools (EXTRA09 obs. 008), beyond the changes V10 reports;
- the selection of a seventh Sage, with one seat unfilled (SW-W-0115; SW-P-0053);
- Isaac's foretold loss (SW-P-0066).

**Withheld and implied identities.** The patron called 閣下 is not identified (SW-C-0039). The seventh Sage is not named. Hilda's collaborator, the physicians'-association authority of IX -extra- (SW-C-0076), is not identified, and this argument does not identify Woodman, whose lobbying for deregulation the prequel and V10 report, with that collaborator (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 029; V10 obs. 007). The researcher whose execution Woodman mentions in the prequel is unnamed in the text and is not identified here. The prison's attacker is not named; V10 records only an attack of Bradford's magnitude (V10 obs. 011). The ice spirit's identity with the pale-gold girl who made the bell is implied, not stated (SW-C-0086).
