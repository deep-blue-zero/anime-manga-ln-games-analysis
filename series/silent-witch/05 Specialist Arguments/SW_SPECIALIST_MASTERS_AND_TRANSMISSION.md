# Silent Witch — masters, apprentices and transmission

## 1. Thesis

In *Silent Witch*, no one hands on a method by itself. Every teacher in the corpus passes on a skill together with the conditions in which it was learned. Those conditions include harm, debt, a name, a code of conduct and coercion. The series asks each pupil to sort the two: to keep the method and decide what to do with the rest.

The pupil can turn the inherited thing outward. Monica does this with her father's numbers, which move from refuge to evidence to gift. The pupil can rename it, as Louis turns his teacher Majer into the Barrier Witch and himself into the Barrier Mage. Or the pupil can refuse it, as Raul refuses to be the first Thorn Witch's image, and as Ray, with Frieda's help, begins to do so with his mother's forbidden sentence (V12 obs. 204, 205).

Some pupils fail to sort. Adolf takes the most dangerous inheritance in the books, a dead woman's forbidden grimoire, and borrows it as power. Glen, in the prequel, uses a half-learned forbidden spell as mercy and harms the person he means to protect.

The adult volumes, V10 to V12, turn the question around. Monica becomes a master, and the series makes her learn that mastership is not the giving of method. It is responsibility for what travels with the method (SW-C-0084; V11 obs. 192, 194).

The prequel was read last but happens first. It recasts the numbered track's masters, above all Louis, as pupils whose methods were themselves inheritances. His coercion of Monica in V01 can then be read, retrospectively, as the form in which care first reached him: hard handling that hides goodwill, and a debt that carries a rescue.

## 2. Orders and boundaries

Four orders matter here, and they must be kept apart.

**Source order** is the order of admission: V01 to V04, IV -after-, V05 to V09, IX -extra-, V10 to V12, then -another- upper and lower (checkpoint §2).

**Diegetic order** puts the -another- volumes first. Louis's childhood, his Minerva years, Glen's apprenticeship, the Sage selection and Louis's first meeting with Monica all come before V01.

**Revelation order** is what the reader learns, and when. Here the prequel comes last. Its disclosures are supplemental retrospection and do not revise any frozen numbered reading (checkpoint §4.2 item 3). Every prequel citation below is marked *retrospective*.

**Participant knowledge** is what each character knows. Louis knows his own past; Monica does not know most of it. She knew his name first from Rutherford's stories about his "idiot apprentice" (EXTRA09 obs. 164). She sees his student self only when the Minerva illusion of V11 makes him regress (V11 obs. 146; SW-C-0088). Nothing below gives a character knowledge that only the reader holds.

One alias matters. Isaac Walker is known to everyone as Felix until V08 discloses him (SW-C-0068). Where a pre-V08 scene matters, the text says "Isaac, then known as Felix". One Minerva friend appears in the readings both as Bernie and, in V11 and the lower prequel volume, as Barney. This argument calls him Bernie Jones, following the ledger (SW-C-0027). The Roseburg house is spelt as the ledger spells it. The upper prequel reading's "Roseberg" for Sabrina refers to the same family.

## 3. The first inheritance: Venedict Rain's numbers

### 3.1 What the father taught

The series begins its account of transmission with a father and his daughter. The memories of Venedict Rain are its earliest teaching scenes.

At about five, Monica asked her father for a tape measure so that she could compute the areas and volumes of things. He heard her reason and gave it to her, and she measured furniture and hands until the markings wore away (V01 obs. 043). Three of his sayings come back to her across V01: the world is full of numbers; strip away the superfluous and what remains is simple; the harder the challenge, the more fun. Already in V01 she finds the limit of one: drinking coffee from his pot, she cannot say what counts as superfluous in a person's heart (V01 obs. 044; V01 reading §6.5). SW-C-0018 records that her numerical perception and her comfort derive from this teaching.

What he passed on was a method, a way of seeing. It was also, from the start, more than a method. V02 shows his learning in mathematics, pharmacy, medicine and biology, and his aim to save the sick by analysing the formulas that make a person human (V02 obs. 034). V03 gives his book, a study of heredity and magical properties, which opens with the saying that the world is made of numbers (V03 obs. 105; SW-C-0014, SW-C-0032). The inheritance is a subject as well as a habit, and that subject is inheritance itself. Magical affinity is inherited, and the black chalice that grows from his design reads hereditary information in the blood (SW-W-0106; V08 obs. 266). The book about heredity becomes the thing Monica inherits.

### 3.2 What came with it: the uncle's lesson

The same numbers carried harm. V02 tells it through a drugged delirium, and V04 repeats it in a sober prologue. When Venedict was burned, Monica forced her eyes open in the smoke to memorise the numbers on his burning papers, the proof that he had lived (V02 obs. 044). Her paternal uncle, who is never named (sw.monica-uncle), beat her for reciting them and for defending her father in public. She promised to keep quiet if only he would stop hitting her (V02 obs. 045). Under his beatings and starvation she survived by repeating her father's formulas until she could no longer see people as people, only as measurements (V04 obs. 002–004; SW-C-0003).

The V02 reading draws the conclusion this argument builds on. The counting refuge, the father's teaching and the rule of silence are one history. The same habit was the inheritance her father gave her, the thing her uncle beat her for, and the place she has hidden ever since (V02 reading §6.9; SW-C-0002, SW-C-0018). SW-C-0001 adds the layer that makes the whole series possible. The silence that gives the Silent Witch her name, and the chantless casting the kingdom admires, began as a child's defence against being heard. V02 lets an expert account, a plate and MacLaggan all praise as instant computation what began as a learned avoidance of speech (V02 obs. 011, 016).

The uncle is the series' first anti-teacher. He taught by violence, he taught silence, and his lesson became part of a famous mastery. This is the first and plainest case of the thesis: a method and a harm passed down together, and indistinguishable for years.

### 3.3 The relay: Hilda Everett

The second teacher in the line repairs the first. Hilda Everett was Venedict's former assistant, recorded in V03 as her father's pupil and later as Monica's adoptive mother (V03 obs. 023). She does not reach Monica by pity. She reaches her through the numbers. In the V04 prologue Monica is reciting a number series; a woman continues it, names it Rain's formula, and calls the girl by her name (V04 obs. 006). Hilda is the first adult in the series to honour the father in Monica's presence (V04 obs. 007).

The same prologue ends on a cookie-baking spell. Numbers can also be a gift to someone at home (V04 obs. 008). IX -extra- deepens this: Hilda's first shared task with Monica rested on a recipe book's rule that baking is measurement (EXTRA09 obs. 005). Monica later brings that rule, and a laboratory balance, to her friends' baking (EXTRA09 obs. 051).

Hilda is also the line's archivist. On the night after Venedict's arrest she took his design and the coffee pot he had a friend make, foreseeing that both would be his last effects (V08 obs. 001–004). She gives Monica the design in V08 as the proof that her father lived (V08 obs. 264, 267; SW-R-0138). The narration also says she kept back something else: Duke Clockford's part in Rain's death (V08 obs. 265). Even the kind relay sorts what it passes on.

### 3.4 Turning the inheritance outward

Monica's development, read through this argument, is a sequence of decisions about the inheritance.

- **V04.** When numbers fail her because Cyril's life is at stake, she tells Louis that her father's saying must no longer be an excuse or an escape (V04 obs. 096, 097; SW-C-0018 as revised at V04).
- **V05.** She identifies the curse-caster by the measurements she took from a dying dragon's memory, and speaks her father's saying as an accusation. The narration states that numbers are no longer her refuge (V05 obs. 139, 144; SW-C-0035).
- **V08.** She binds Isaac, then known as Felix, to remember her father's name, and calls the binding a curse (V08 obs. 180, 181).
- **V08–V09.** She uses the father's design to save the man whose existence, she believes, cost her father his life (V08 obs. 268). At the council the black chalice is demonstrated honestly, then fed borrowed blood (V09 obs. 088, 091, 116; SW-C-0069).
- **V10.** Because the chalice is accepted as a medical tool, forbidden-art crimes are no longer punished by death. Monica thinks her father's research has changed the country, and wishes it had happened in his lifetime (V10 obs. 009, 132; SW-C-0083).

The book on heredity therefore ends up deciding the most public question of inheritance in the series: whose son the second prince is. It decides it falsely, by design. The inheritance is put to use, not just preserved.

One more relay belongs here. In V11's secret episode the chalice's rights are managed by the Earl of Amberd, Bernie Jones (V11 obs. 225). The friend who once needed Monica to depend on him now administers her father's legacy. The line from the father to the daughter has passed through an assistant, a stolen bundle, a forger's workshop and a council, and it now sits with a former rival.

### 3.5 The inheritance in the enemy's mouth

The numbers were not passed down only to people who loved Venedict. The traitor of V04, a curse-caster who had fled the Albrights, recognises Monica's refrain as Venedict's catchphrase. He says that Venedict's existence is like a curse (V04 obs. 123; SW-C-0039). In V05 the same man, Barry Oates, known at Leinbourg as the footman Peter Sams, faces Monica's eyes and calls her by her father's name before his own curse consumes him (V05 obs. 147; SW-R-0102).

The daughter inherits the father's face and saying, and so she inherits his enemies' fear. The name passes down too. When Monica calls her own act of naming her father a curse in V08 (obs. 181), she repeats the enemy's word, now about her own speech.

The patron to whom Sams says he sold Venedict, the one he called 閣下, stays unidentified (SW-C-0039). This argument does not identify him.

## 4. Minerva: teachers who are outgrown

Minerva is the series' school of masters. Its first appearance in Monica's history is a lesson that goes wrong.

### 4.1 Bernie: a teacher who needed a dependent pupil

V03's prologue is Bernie Jones's, and it is an account of teaching corrupted by need. He rescued the thirteen-year-old Monica from bullies. Half knowing that classmates were cutting her hair and hiding her things, he looked away, because he wanted her isolated so that she would rely on him (V03 obs. 002, 004). When she froze before the practical teacher MacLaggan, Bernie offered to teach her a shortened chant (V03 obs. 005). At the next practical she did not shorten the chant. She cast with none at all (V03 obs. 006).

His first thought was that he had not taught her this. His second was that without him she would be alone. He felt betrayed (V03 obs. 007). When the fifteen-year-old Sage came to thank him, he told her she was not his friend and should be shut away alone in a mountain hut, and the rumour of a hermit witch began (V03 obs. 009). SW-C-0027 records the friendship ended by envy and the hut life begun by his curse. IX -extra- shows the fifteen-year-old arriving at the hut and wondering whether Bernie will forgive her now (EXTRA09 obs. 049).

This is transmission by overshoot. A teacher offers a small technique, and the pupil carries it to a limit the teacher cannot follow. The method's success is what breaks the relation. In V04 Bernie names the envy as a wish to be her equal rival, and he leaves as a future count offering help (V04 obs. 037, 038). By V09 he supplies the workshop for her rescue plan (V09 obs. 022–023). The relation has not been repaired into friendship. It has been turned into a debt he collects, as SW-R-0044 records: he demands lifelong gratitude.

### 4.2 MacLaggan and Rutherford in the numbered track

MacLaggan taught Monica and Louis both (sw.maclaggan). In V02 he gives his class the expert account of the Silent Witch's chantlessness, without knowing that his former pupil is enrolled in the school where he now teaches (V02 obs. 016). He comes back in V11 with a heavier role, discussed in §7.

Gideon Rutherford, the Purple Smoke Mage, is a figure in the numbered track almost wholly through others' memory. He let Monica research freely and sent her to the Sage selection, where she fainted at the interview (V03 obs. 023). Bernie warns her of him (V04 obs. 034). He is Louis's master, and Louis threatens, comically, to bury him (V04 obs. 040). In one line he asks whether Louis's disciple is "that brat" who blew up his laboratory (V04 obs. 049). He still corrects Monica's papers, and his disciple Carla, Louis's senior, advises her (V06 obs. 112). His laboratory produced three Sages (V11 obs. 140).

IX -extra- makes a distinction that matters to this argument. Monica was a research student in Rutherford's laboratory, which was not an apprenticeship (EXTRA09 obs. 164). Monica, the series' central adept, was never anyone's formal apprentice. She was taught by a father, then by an adoptive mother, then by a laboratory that left her alone. The apprenticeship she later gives Isaac is one she never had.

## 5. Louis as master in the numbered track

### 5.1 Coercion and investment

The numbered track introduces Louis as a master by the way he treats people below him. In V01 he forces Monica into the mission. She objects, he has no veto to fear, he grips her shoulder, she nods by reflex, and execution is mentioned. The reading records the result as consent produced by coercion (V01 obs. 011). The narrator calls his reasoning abuse (V01 obs. 010). Yet his own memory of the selection duel says she shut him out, and he dragged her to the dragon hunt to give her confidence (V01 obs. 117, 118).

SW-C-0017 holds both halves as the claim has been strengthened at every unit since V02. Louis combines coercion with investment in Monica's self-respect and in his own pride. His V01 relation to her is recorded as respect hidden behind insult (SW-R-0002).

His teaching is mostly lessons learned by losing. In V05 he teaches Monica, at a card game, that the game begins before you reach the table, and she learns it by being beaten (V05 obs. 005–007, 129, 130; SW-C-0048). In V09 she returns the lesson to him as a message, and uses his other lesson, that power makes truth, to stage the council's verdict (V09 obs. 096, 104). The series' clearest return on a master's teaching is a pupil defeating the teacher with his own maxims.

### 5.2 The heartless-witch model and its error

Louis's verdict on Monica is a teacher's model of a pupil. In V03 he tells Casey that Monica is a heartless witch who sees people as numbers, and that this is why he chose her (V03 obs. 130, 131). In V04 he calls her a creepy girl, using the same word her uncle used for a child who spoke only in numbers (V04 obs. 050). The reading notes that "the volume lets the reader hear the uncle in the colleague" (V04 reading §6.1).

SW-C-0033 records the model as half right. At V09 the narration names its error: Louis assumed she would never ask others for help (V09 obs. 072). Monica's rescue is collective. Every element comes from someone she asked (SW-C-0072). Louis concedes his second clean defeat (V09 obs. 137).

This is a master whose model of his pupil fails because the pupil learned elsewhere. Monica learned to ask from Bartholomeus, who told her a child may lean on adults (V06 obs. 207), from Isabelle's held-out hand (V04 obs. 043), and from Cyril's order to rely on others (V04 obs. 109).

### 5.3 Louis and Glen in the numbered track

Glen is Louis's actual apprentice, and the numbered track reveals him slowly. In V02 Louis tells Lynn that he has enrolled his disciple as a decoy for Felix and told him nothing. The disciple is big, loud, a bad liar, and he wrecked a Minerva building (V02 obs. 010; SW-R-0066). V04 confirms that the disciple is at the school (V04 obs. 049). V05 has Ray explain why Louis took him: mana above 250, and a past runaway (V05 obs. 115). IV -after- gives Louis's maxim that money and violence solve most troubles, spoken over a dead fire dragon. Glen says he became a disciple "by being swept along" (AFTER04 obs. 099). The narration calls the maxim the speech of someone who has suffered and thrown off much unreason (AFTER04 reading §6.9).

Glen does not simply absorb it. He compares it with Cyril's patient effort and prefers Cyril's (AFTER04 obs. 075; SW-C-0043). He takes the Witch's advice, which he does not know is Monica's, and decides his master lied (V05 obs. 059, 060; SW-R-0094). In V09 he fights his master: he rams Louis with his own fireball (V09 obs. 068). Glen and Monica both fear Louis's fists more than his spells (V09 obs. 051).

V11 reports the master's method as others see it. Owen tells Louis to rethink his teaching. Glen studies with Owen in his breaks and looks up to him as a brother (V11 obs. 006). V12 has Louis order the newly qualified Glen to hunt a dragon (V12 obs. 155).

The method is fists over formulas (V11 obs. 006, 195), and Glen grows partly by taking his teaching from elsewhere: from Cyril, from Owen, and from the Witch he does not recognise. That mirrors Monica. The numbered track's masters are outgrown by pupils who learn sideways.

### 5.4 The adult method revised

In V11 Louis names his adult method as money, violence and connections. He says he learned the third from losing to Monica at the council (V11 obs. 183). The reading revises SW-C-0043 on this basis (SW-CR-0593): what he gained against Adolf's borrowed power was a network (V11 obs. 183, 186). Transmission has run backwards. The senior Sage learns a method from the junior he coerced, and the lesson is the very thing he was wrong about: asking others.

## 6. The prequel recasts the masters (retrospective)

Everything in this section comes from the two -another- volumes. They were read after V12, but they happen before V01. They do not change what the numbered readings recorded at their own boundaries. They change how the reader can now see the numbered track's masters.

### 6.1 Rutherford: blows that hid care

The upper volume is "as much as anything, a book of teachers" (ANOTHER-UPPER reading §6.4). Rutherford's method is to hit.

He judged from the start that the boy grows by being beaten (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 048). His paralytic smoke and heel drops end Louis's fights (obs. 088). His daily brawls with Louis in the laboratory continue into the high division (obs. 161).

He recruits Louis by contempt. Furious at being looked down on, Louis casts a spell learned from Rutherford's dropped textbook (obs. 025). He then learns every spell in the book within a week, and Rutherford offers to recommend him to Minerva (obs. 027–029).

The book's moral hinge is not a blow but a judgment. Louis, climbing to his teacher's window, overhears Rutherford tell MacLaggan that the boy is a giant who will leave a name in history, but blind to others' goodwill (obs. 183; SW-C-0097). He thinks of Rosalie, Lionel, Owen and the people at the diner, and the words stab (obs. 184). The apology to Rosalie follows (obs. 187).

The lower volume adds the fact that changes the whole relation. Thirteen years earlier, Vivian, a senior woman at the brothel, asked Rutherford to take the boy away before the doomed house could sell him. Rutherford refused her money and would only recommend him (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 170; SW-R-0214). The rescue was arranged in secret, and the boy was never told until his honeymoon. Louis's reflection is that the kindness was always hard to see until too late (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §6.6).

The lower volume also shows Rutherford refusing the Sages' seat more than once (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 103). He declines to recommend Louis because Duke Clockford would crush the recommendation. He recommends Monica instead, so that public candidacy will protect her from foreign poaching (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 067).

This recasts something the numbered track left open. In V01 Monica believes she was a makeweight Sage, and the ledger holds that belief as unsupported, with the examiners' view open (SW-C-0004). The prequel shows the reader a teacher's protective reason behind her candidacy. The numbered readings do not record Monica learning that reason, and this argument does not give it to her.

### 6.2 Majer: a teacher kept in a name

The numbered track gives Louis an epithet, the Barrier Mage, as a plain fact. It never names the teacher behind it. The upper volume does (SW-C-0098).

Clarice Majer, the barrier teacher, is herself a disciple of the Water-Mirror Mage, Sams Horaceson (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 091). He is not the same person as the footman Peter Sams. Majer teaches by correction: she lectures Louis for an hour before accepting his reconstructed formulas (obs. 109). She is honest about her limits. She says she has left no great achievement (obs. 149). She has no epithet. Louis calls her the Barrier Witch, and she laughs aloud for the first time (obs. 138). He credits her above his own name on his presentation's cover (obs. 151). He promises to call himself the Barrier Mage so that the neighbourhood will know the Barrier Witch taught him (obs. 158).

In the lower volume, retired and teaching children, she is still learning, her barrier modern and economical (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 129). Louis's Glass Phantom Castle rescues her, and she calls it worthy of the Barrier Mage and says she had a good teacher (obs. 130, 131).

The upper reading observes that the run ends its chapters on epithets given or taken (SW-F-0064). Majer's line is the series' purest case of a name as an inheritance. The pupil turns a teacher who left no achievement into a title, and wears the title himself. The method also changes in the handing on. Majer's barriers protect; Louis's are "the most aggressive" barriers anyone uses (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 007). The prologue's Mirror Prison is a defensive art turned into a weapon (ANOTHER-UPPER reading §6.6).

### 6.3 What the brothel taught

Not all of Louis's teachers were mages. The miserly owner of the brothel taught him letters and accounts (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 017). Shona, a dying woman, gave him his first jam and an instruction: leave, and make a family someday, because the women were strangers huddling together and not a family (obs. 018, 020; SW-C-0096). The women sent him off with a jar holding twelve silver coins, one from each, and a label telling him not to come back (obs. 031). The coins are still in the jar in the prologue, unspent through his rise (obs. 011).

The lower volume shows him solving Adolf's crime with the brothel's cunning, recalled at the moment of need: controlling information, testing for the slip, and attending to a friend's habits (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 083, 085). The two volumes close Shona's instruction at her grave. He leaves marmalade and tells her he has a family (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 172; SW-C-0104).

What the brothel taught was method (accounts, cunning) and conduct (a village code in which violence protected a poor boy with a girlish face; V11 obs. 146). It also taught a model of care: given without words, hidden behind a hard label, paid in coin.

### 6.4 Louis takes Glen as Rutherford took Louis

The lower volume's second chapter repeats the upper's first. Glen Dudley is eleven, a butcher's son with mana over 200. Mary Harvey's prophecy brought him to Minerva: the country will fall if he inherits the family butchery. A runaway of his mana destroyed Rutherford's laboratory, and he was confined in the Tower of Arindal (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 039). An earlier hint now resolves. V02's disciple who "wrecked a Minerva building" (V02 obs. 010) and V04's "brat" who blew up Rutherford's laboratory (V04 obs. 049) were Glen.

Louis offers him lifelong confinement or apprenticeship. He also names a third choice, to break the building and escape (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 041). He takes Glen to put Minerva in his debt (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §3; SW-R-0207, "master for a debt"). He teaches as Rutherford taught, by exposure and a contempt that hides care. Your runaway is no enemy, he says; your master's barriers will not break (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 044). Because Glen cannot grasp formulas, Louis limits him to fire attack and flight (obs. 045).

The reading puts the parallel plainly: Glen enters the run as the upper volume's Louis once did, a boy of great untrained power taken out of confinement by a master who wants a debt repaid (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §6.3).

Two order points keep this honest.

- Louis takes Glen before he learns of Vivian's request (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 170 falls in the epilogue). He is not consciously copying the hidden rescue that was done for him. The resemblance belongs to the form of care he knows, not to an imitation.
- The red-haired senior who drew Glen into secret night battles at Minerva is given in V06 through Glen's dream and grievance (V06 obs. 088–090, 094; SW-R-0109). The V06 reading connects that senior to Hubert Dee, who by his own account hunted Monica in battles too (V06 obs. 156). The lower volume says only that "a senior" egged Glen on to practise in secret (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 040). The harm that put Glen in Louis's hands thus came from a fellow student's hunt. This argument does not make the prequel's unnamed senior into a named person beyond what V06 records.

### 6.5 Glen's mercy: harm that travels upward

The lower volume's central mystery has a benign author, and the author is the apprentice. Glen overheard his master tell a guild executive that he would not be engaged to Rosalie if she were not a Sage's daughter, and he saw her weep (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 066, 122). When she fell from the barracks roof, he believed it was a suicide attempt. He sealed her memories with an incomplete, quasi-forbidden spell he had taught himself from library books, to prevent another attempt (obs. 118, 123). Then he shielded his master by silence (obs. 124; SW-C-0101).

This is transmission in reverse direction and in the wrong currency. The master's careless remark, itself a product of the refinement he adopted to meet Birdland's terms (SW-C-0100), becomes the apprentice's act of harm. The apprentice's loyalty becomes concealment. The method used, mind interference, came from no master at all. Glen read it alone (obs. 118). The art is quasi-forbidden; unauthorised use can cost a licence for ever (obs. 119; SW-W-0157). Louis names the risk: she might never have woken (obs. 120).

The master's response is the clearest break in the line of punishments. Rosalie's statement spares Glen official censure, and Louis restricts his magic privately (obs. 162). The reading calls this punishment "private and proportionate, unlike the punishments of Louis's own youth" (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §6.3). The book judges the act wrong but forgivable (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §8.4). Rutherford's smoke and heel drops do not pass down to Glen. The responsibility does.

### 6.6 Louis to Monica: what the investiture passed on

The lower volume gives Louis's first meeting with Monica. It happens before V01, and the reader learns it last.

Monica hides in a curtain and begs not to be killed (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 100). In the forest battle she opens with a wordless Spirit-King summons, fells Adolf with a remote spell she calls only a lot of calculation, and defeats Louis (obs. 107, 109, 111–113). Rutherford names the two candidates for Mary: Louis is a genius of effort, Monica a genius of numbers (obs. 104; SW-C-0103). Louis judges that she should be a Sage (obs. 114).

At the investiture, Louis tells her that she won by skill and must not be servile (obs. 159). She asks how he can believe in himself. He says that others believed in him, and names Shona, the women, Rutherford, the diner, his teachers, Owen, Rosalie and Lionel. She names an adoptive mother and one friend (obs. 160). He calls her his fellow-intake, and she fears him a little less (obs. 161). Then he skips the parade and has her keep a wordless illusion of him in the carriage, to repay a "debt" (obs. 163).

The scene condenses the prequel's argument about teaching. Louis passes on to Monica, in one sentence, what his teachers passed to him: belief that came from others. In the same breath he passes on the coercion. The help is extracted as a debt, and the help itself is a lie told by illusion. The lower reading records both as the origin of what V01 showed (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §6.5). It strengthens SW-C-0017, his coercion mixed with investment in her self-respect, and SW-C-0033, his model of her as heartless (SW-CR-0834, SW-CR-0850).

Read in revelation order, V01's coercion was the reader's first sight of Louis. Read in diegetic order, it is the second appearance of a pattern the reader saw last: care offered in a hard form, and a debt that is also a rescue. Monica knows the investiture conversation, because she was there. She does not know where Louis learned the pattern, and the numbered track never gives her that knowledge.

### 6.7 What the recasting changes, and what it does not

The prequel changes the numbered track's masters in four ways.

1. **Louis becomes a pupil.** The coercer of V01 is shown as the product of a line of teachers who hid care in blows, labels and refused payment.
2. **The epithet gains a lineage.** "Barrier Mage" stops being a bare title and becomes a teacher's name carried forward (SW-C-0098).
3. **The opposition between effort and strength moves.** SW-C-0043 set Cyril's patient effort against Louis's maxim of money, violence and strength. The upper volume shows the holder of that maxim as himself a genius of effort, driven by refusal to lose. The difference lies in motive and display, not in effort (SW-CR-0766; ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 004).
4. **Monica's Sagehood gains a teacher's motive.** Rutherford recommended her to protect her (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 067).

The prequel does not soften the violence. Both books treat it comically. The upper volume's one weighted judgment concerns goodwill, not violence (ANOTHER-UPPER reading §8.3; SW-C-0097). The lower volume frames the maiming of Adolf as a promise kept and does not condemn it (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §8.3). The recasting explains Louis. It does not excuse him, and the books do not ask the reader to.

## 7. MacLaggan and Adolf: a master's responsibility

V11 is the volume whose "organising subject is the master–apprentice relation" (V11 reading §6.2). Its villain is a failed pupil.

Adolf Farron was in MacLaggan's laboratory. MacLaggan regrets doing nothing earlier, and says a master clears up after his apprentice (V11 obs. 139; SW-R-0180). He reads Adolf's plot as a wish to redo his school days and to have Louis pay attention to him, an urge that escalates when it comes from malice (V11 obs. 141, 143; SW-C-0085). He is glad to have helped stop Adolf as his teacher (V11 obs. 191).

The upper prequel volume deepens this retrospectively. It shows years of Adolf being outshone in the field he was born to lead (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 139), and Clockford's promise of a Sage's seat (obs. 215; SW-C-0099).

Adolf is the anti-pupil of the series because he sorts nothing. His power in V11 is borrowed: the bell from the pale-gold girl, Thornley's formula, and the first Thorn Witch's grimoire of black flame (V11 obs. 090, 179). The grimoire is an inheritance that belongs to another house, sealed away from that house by a past king's caution (V11 obs. 087, 088). Adolf opens what its heirs are afraid to touch.

Louis's answer to him in V11 is that Adolf never became a rival because he aimed his fists at the people around Louis, and always ran at the end (V11 obs. 189). The contrast is between a method inherited and earned, Louis's connections, and power taken from others without its conditions.

MacLaggan's lesson is aimed at Monica as well. He tells her she will understand what students want when she teaches, and Monica, now a master, feels the weight (V11 obs. 142). His reading of Adolf's wish for attention makes her remember that as a child she could not ask her father for attention (V11 obs. 143). It also makes her remember that her failure to understand others is what broke things with Bernie (V11 obs. 144). A master's account of one failed pupil becomes another master's lesson about herself.

## 8. Monica as master

### 8.1 How the apprenticeship began

Isaac's apprenticeship to Monica is the fulfilment of a dead friend's wish.

The real prince, whom Isaac served as page, friend and brother (SW-R-0133; V08 obs. 118), asked him to promise to find something he loved for himself (V08 obs. 140). Wildianu heard that request and asks him not to scorn it (V08 obs. 168). Isaac found it only once, in the Silent Witch's magic at the wyvern attack: the gate of light, and his realisation that this was what he could lose himself in (V08 obs. 174, 175). Long before V08, the reader saw Isaac, then known as Felix, praise the Silent Witch's paper as beautiful in a bookshop and call his admiration a first love (V03 reading §6.13). IX -extra- shows him keeping her correction of his paper and her note asking to see more (EXTRA09 obs. 133).

When the obsession leaves him after the rescue, Monica asks him to live and find joy (V09 obs. 125). He debates magic with her through the night and becomes her self-appointed apprentice (V09 obs. 173; SW-C-0073). The relation begins with the apprentice's initiative. The master did not seek him. IX -extra- adds the sign of the new relation: the cookie Monica decorates shows an apprentice in an apron, not a glittering prince, and he is glad of it (EXTRA09 obs. 177; SW-R-0162).

The apprenticeship also carries a name from the dead friend. Isaac told the prince that his friends call him Ike (V08 obs. 115). The prince's last diary entry hoped to become a kind prince "Ike" could be proud of (V09 obs. 122). Monica welcomes her apprentice home as Ike (V10 obs. 160). The diary's hope and Monica's resolve in V11 to be a master Isaac can be proud of (V11 obs. 194) answer each other across two volumes. This argument records that echo as structural, for the reader. It does not claim Monica intends it.

### 8.2 What Isaac brought with him

The apprentice arrives with his own inheritances, and not all are innocent.

- **Method from a dead mother.** Isaac's knowledge of magic was self-taught from the grimoires of Irene, the real prince's mother (V10 obs. 144, 161). Wildianu, Irene's spirit, passed from her to the prince and then to Isaac. The spirit guards Irene's wish that her child not forget what he loves, a wish that "passed to Felix and to Isaac" (V10 obs. 172; SW-R-0143).
- **A name, and a coerced conduct.** Clockford chose a scarred orphan, cut a scar into him, and made him the prince (V08 obs. 106, 110). The relation the ledger records is "tool and discard" (SW-R-0134). The Kerbecks later judge Isaac "raised too much as a royal" (V12 obs. 104; SW-C-0092). A conduct imposed by coercion has become a working skill.
- **A hidden violence.** Provoked about his face, he stabs a mana-laden shell into Hubert's eye, and hides a cruel face Monica must not see (V12 obs. 089).
- **A place in the master's own harm.** Isaac knows he is to Monica an indirect perpetrator, her parent's enemy, and still hopes she will take his hand (V12 obs. 105). The motive for Venedict Rain's death, as Monica, Porter and Isaac conclude, was the hereditary tool that could expose the false prince (SW-C-0064). The master's inheritance and the apprentice's inheritance are the same knot.

The series does not resolve this. It shows a master teaching formulas to the bearer of the name for whose sake her father's numbers were burned. It shows her praising him for wanting to make the country better, which is the condition of his pardon (V12 obs. 104).

### 8.3 What Monica passes on

V10 asks what makes Monica an adult and a master (V10 obs. 049, 153). Its first answer is transmission of words she was given. Confronting the boy Rick in anger, she searches for the words an adult once gave her as a child (V10 obs. 101). She repeats Bartholomeus's line that a child may rely on adults (V10 obs. 102; V06 obs. 207). She names the people she wants to resemble: Lana, who reaches out; Cyril, who scolds what is wrong; and Bartholomeus (V10 obs. 103). SW-C-0084 calls her adulthood transmission: she passes on words she was given.

With Isaac she gives what she has, sometimes too much of it.

- She buys him bookshelves (V10 obs. 040).
- She gives him a first expectation, a single standard of absolute command of formulas (V10 obs. 163), with a primer and twenty-nine more volumes. The reading calls it a standard only she could think reasonable (V10 obs. 161, 164; V10 reading §6.3).
- She tells him he is home (V10 obs. 160; SW-R-0165).

He asks to learn under her, and she resolves to think about how to teach (V10 obs. 146).

V11 turns resolve into learning. Louis's prologue says he cannot imagine the Silent Witch teaching: a rationalist and perfectionist without life skills (V11 obs. 010). Ugo's trust in Bradford strikes Monica as a proper apprenticeship (V11 obs. 108). After the battle she sees that she has set Isaac a standard without thinking about a master's responsibility (V11 obs. 192). She takes three models: Cyril, a dependable adult; MacLaggan, a master who understands his pupil; and Bradford, a master his pupil is proud of (V11 obs. 194; SW-CR-0634). The comic test follows. She practises her scary face, Isaac disarms her, and she admits she is still immature (V11 obs. 206, 207). Cyril tells her that he is immature too, and that his junior can grow (V11 obs. 193).

V12 shows a master who learns from her apprentice's failure. His paper fails at a point only practice reveals. Monica blames her own rule of six months of theory before practice, and decides that his first spell will be his own new formula (V12 obs. 111, 112). The master revises her method on the pupil's evidence. That is the opposite of Bernie, who could not bear a pupil who went beyond his teaching, and of Rutherford, who taught by beating. V12 reading §6.7 links this to SW-C-0084.

### 8.4 Monica as a placer of masters

Monica's last act of mastership in V12 is to make another person a master. Hubert Dee and his apprentice Norman draw her into a battle at Sechen (V12 obs. 079, 082). She binds Hubert's rings so that his magic is sealed, and asks him to tutor Norman, whom she recommends to Minerva (V12 obs. 107). V12 reading §6.7 calls this the completion of the volume's master–apprentice subject.

Hubert comes from a teaching family of his own. V11's secret episode makes him the son of Vanessa Dee, Bradford's sister and the real head of the Dee house, who rebukes him and sets his work (V11 obs. 228; V11 reading §3). His magic is a toolmaker's. In V06 he fakes chantless casting with tools planted before the start (V06 obs. 160; SW-C-0055). He hunted Monica at Minerva (V06 obs. 156). A V07 character card adds that he respects his uncle and, lacking mana, chose tools and traps, but that is paratext only (V07 reading §7.5). Norman is an orphan whose father left Minerva to elope. Norman taught himself remote magic from the textbooks, and Hubert refined it (V12 obs. 084). Norman judges his teacher a bad model but useful (V12 obs. 085; SW-R-0191). Hubert deceived the boy about who Monica was, and the deception is never corrected by any apology (V12 obs. 083, 093). The reading's judgment on Hubert's scheme is mixed (V12 reading §8.3).

Monica does not purify the line. She puts conditions on it: sealed rings, a recommendation, a placement under institutional eyes. That is mastership as responsibility for what travels: she keeps the useful method and fences off the harm.

## 9. Houses: inheritance without a teacher

The Roseburg and Albright chapters show inheritance with no single master. The house itself, its seat and its reputation do the teaching.

### 9.1 The Roseburgs

Raul Roseburg is the fifth Thorn Witch, made head of his house at sixteen after his sister Melissa, the fourth, broke the house's tacit pact with the crown by selling its strong drugs (V11 obs. 047, 075, 076). He is shunned as the image of the first Thorn Witch, Rebecca Roseburg. She was a hero who destroyed invaders alone and a feared seductress who enthralled kings. As a child Raul was refused school and stoned by other children (V11 obs. 086). The house's own disciples revere him as Rebecca reborn (V11 obs. 043). Her book of black flame, one of the three great forbidden arts, is sealed away from the family (V11 obs. 087, 088). Only Raul can reproduce her man-eating rose fortress, and he would rather have magic to make friends (V11 obs. 089; SW-C-0087).

This inheritance is a face, a seat, a skill and a fear, and Raul's task is refusal of the fear. MacLaggan sends him to the library building, the place he dreads (V11 obs. 157). Raul catches the grimoire. Then Monica, dropped from the sky, clings to his hand without recoiling (V11 obs. 177, 178; SW-R-0184). SW-C-0087 records that her unafraid hand answers his fear.

The prequel shows the inheritance from outside, retrospectively: Mary Harvey takes the child Raul, "the Roseburg family's treasured child", to Minerva's festival in secret, against his grandmother's view that he needs no school (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 147, 148). The heir's first school was a visit on another Sage's arm.

### 9.2 The Albrights

Ray Albright is the third Abyss Shaman, under a grandmother, Adeline, who is the second and still the head of the house in fact (sw.ray, sw.adeline; SW-R-0190). His inheritance is the curse-craft itself, and a single prohibition.

His mother wept that she was unfit to teach him, and forbade him to say that he loved anyone. Six months later his parents died (V12 obs. 185). He believes that his mother's love was a curse that made his father her puppet (V12 obs. 178, 186). Mary knows otherwise: his mother cursed only herself, believing she could not be loved, and his father loved her to the end (V12 obs. 202; SW-C-0090).

What a parent-teacher passed on here is a prohibition and a false history of why. The repair comes from people outside the line. Frieda shows feeling without the sentence and kisses his curse-marked hand (V12 obs. 204, 205). Monica and Raul declare themselves the villains so that Ray will not be one (V12 obs. 198). Monica's motive is her own knowledge of how easily one curses oneself (V12 obs. 195). Her own inheritance, a childhood rule of silence imposed by fear, is what lets her recognise his.

The Albright inheritance also reaches Monica's line through harm. The traitor of V04 fled the Albrights and hoped his patron would protect him from them (V04 obs. 122). Their curse-craft, carried out of the house, became the instrument that denounced Venedict Rain (SW-C-0039; V05 obs. 166). A house's art, separated from its house's discipline, destroyed another house's father. In V09 the same craft, performed by Ray, gives the council a staged cure (V09 obs. 097; SW-C-0069). The art serves whichever use its holder chooses.

### 9.3 Cyril: teaching attached to the wrong father

Cyril Ashley belongs here as a comparison, though he is not a house heir by birth. In V10 Brendan praises the lesson to respect one's elders as the marquess's teaching. Cyril knows it was his birth father's, and his pleasure collapses into self-loathing (V10 obs. 037; SW-C-0037). His father was a drinker who boasted of noble blood, and Cyril was told he is like him (V10 obs. 136).

A sound precept can come from a harmful teacher, and the pupil cannot separate the two by wishing. Cyril's answer is not to discard the lesson. He takes a senior's duty toward juniors (AFTER04 obs. 093; V11 obs. 193), and he becomes one of the three models Monica chooses (V11 obs. 194).

Two patterns cut across the rows.

First, the series rarely lets the load be purely bad or purely good. Debt carries rescue: Vivian's request, Rutherford's recommendation, Louis's investiture lesson. Names carry both lineage and curse. Venedict's name binds Isaac to remember, and Monica calls that binding a curse in the same scene (V08 obs. 180, 181). Coercion can leave a usable skill, as the Kerbecks' verdict on Isaac's manners shows.

Second, the sorting is done by pupils more often than by masters. Monica turns the numbers outward. Louis turns a teacher into a title. Glen prefers Cyril's ethic to his master's. Norman judges his teacher a bad model but useful. The masters who sort deliberately come late and are few: MacLaggan, after the fact; Louis, privately, with Glen; and Monica, in V11 and V12. That is why the adult volumes matter. They give the protagonist the master's side of a task the series has so far shown only from the pupil's side.

## 11. Mary's disciples and the minor lines

The corpus records several lines thinly. They support the pattern without carrying it.

- **Mary Harvey.** She takes boys from orphanages, pays for their schooling and releases them at sixteen with references. One became her apprentice. She calls this her hobby, not charity (EXTRA09 obs. 149, 150). V11 lists her apprentice as advanced but thin in record (V11 obs. 009). Her disciple Clarence Hall runs the selection battle in the prequel (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 105). Her prophecy is also the first cause in Glen's line: it brought him to Minerva (V06 obs. 086; ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 039). Mary's motives are seen mostly from outside (checkpoint §4.2 item 4), and this argument does not go beyond the recorded acts.
- **Bradford and Ugo.** Ugo calls his master the strongest man in the kingdom (V11 obs. 024). Monica reads their trust as a proper apprenticeship and takes Bradford as a model (V11 obs. 108, 194; SW-R-0182).
- **Graham Sanders.** An apprentice of this former Sage, the Thunder Mage, is rising in solo dragon kills (V11 obs. 011). The volume does not develop him.
- **House disciples.** Raul and Ray have house disciples only, not apprentices (V11 obs. 009). The houses train their own.
- **Isaac and Glen.** Isaac teaches Glen for the lower-mage examination and calls him a younger brother who needs looking after (V11 obs. 029, 032). An apprentice teaches his own master's peer's apprentice. The lines cross.
- **Carla Maxwell.** Rutherford's only student in the upper prequel volume, and the Star-Spear Witch (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 129, 132). Lynn contracted with Louis to be near her (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 050). Her absence on a family matter is open at V12 (V12 obs. 182).

## 12. Counterarguments and counterevidence

### 12.1 The strongest counterargument: the material is genre comedy, and it does not judge

The strongest objection runs like this. Master and apprentice pairs are standard furniture in a magic-academy light novel. The series uses them as comic engines, not as an argument about transmission.

The evidence is substantial:

- Louis's violence toward his pupils and rivals is judged comically in both prequel volumes and is never condemned (ANOTHER-UPPER reading §8.3; ANOTHER-LOWER reading §8.3; SW-C-0097).
- The comedy of coercion runs through the numbered track (SW-C-0016).
- Monica's mastership in V11 is staged as a scary face that fails (V11 obs. 206).
- The prequel's own frame is a romance and a found family: the story of a boy who became a Sage and gained a family (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 173; SW-C-0104). It is not a story about lineage.
- The worst harms in the corpus come from villains outside any teaching line: Monica's uncle, the curse-caster, Clockford, Adolf. On this view the teachers who matter, Venedict, Hilda, Rutherford and Majer, pass on method cleanly, and harm comes from elsewhere.

If that is right, "transmission of harm" is a reader's moral overlay on comic material.

### 12.2 Answer

The thesis does not need the series to condemn anything. It claims that the series records what travels with a method and makes the pupil sort it. Comedy is one of the ways the series does the recording.

First, the comic register registers harm. SW-C-0016 says so in so many words: the comedy of coercion registers harm in most cases. V01's narrator calls Louis's reasoning abuse in a one-line deadpan correction (V01 obs. 010; SW-F-0003). V04 lets the uncle's word sound in Louis's mouth (V04 obs. 050). The tone is light. The record is exact.

Second, the "villains outside the line" are inside it. The uncle is not a teacher by role, but his lesson, silence enforced by beating, is the root of the chantless mastery the whole kingdom praises (SW-C-0001; V02 obs. 011, 016, 045). The curse-caster is a product of a teaching house, carrying its craft out of the house's discipline (V04 obs. 122). Adolf is MacLaggan's pupil, and MacLaggan says a master must clear up after his apprentice (V11 obs. 139). The harm comes through teaching lines as well as from outside them.

Third, the clean teachers are not clean. Bernie's teaching was a scheme to keep Monica dependent (V03 obs. 004, 007). Rutherford's care came by heel drop and was hidden for thirteen years (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 088; ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 170). Even Hilda, the kindest relay, withholds Clockford's part in the father's death (V08 obs. 265).

Fourth, the prequel's family frame is itself a transmission frame. Shona's instruction to make a family is a teaching, completed at her grave (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 020; ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 172). Louis's investiture sentence, that he believes in himself because others believed in him, is the books' distillation (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §6.5), and it is a sentence about inheritance.

Fifth, the reading closest to this subject states the point without the thesis's help. V11's reading calls the master–apprentice relation the volume's organising subject (V11 reading §6.2), and the checkpoint classified the domain as warranted on exactly this ground (checkpoint §6).

### 12.3 Second objection: sorting is not a pattern if many pupils do not sort

Adolf does not sort. Glen repeats a harm. Hubert is unrepentant at V12. So the claim that pupils sort may be selective.

The answer is that the series makes failure to sort legible as failure. Adolf is set against Louis precisely as borrowed power against earned connections (V11 obs. 183, 189). Glen's sealing is named as a risk, punished, and judged forgivable but wrong (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 120, 162; reading §8.4). Hubert is bound and placed under conditions (V12 obs. 107). The thesis is not that every pupil succeeds. It is that the series frames the pupil's task as sorting, and measures success and failure by it.

### 12.4 Third objection: the prequel is being used to retrofit the numbered track

Reading V01's Louis through a later-published prequel risks retrofitting.

This argument keeps the prequel's evidence marked as retrospective, and makes no claim that the numbered readings change. The numbered track already shows most of the pattern on its own:

- Rutherford as Louis's master (V04 obs. 040);
- Glen's wrecked building (V02 obs. 010);
- Louis's maxim given over a dragon, as the speech of someone who has thrown off unreason (AFTER04 obs. 099);
- his coercion mixed with investment (SW-C-0017, strengthened at every unit before the prequel).

The prequel supplies origins. It does not create the pattern.

## 13. Limits of this argument

**Boundary.** The evidence is the Japanese LN V01 to V12 and the four companions, as closed at commit 60b21a7. No web serial, adaptation, translation or commentary is used. Paratext is cited only as paratext. For example, the V11 character list's description of Hubert as a second "Bad Boy of Minerva" is not used as narrative fact (V11 reading §7.8), and the upper prequel list's note that Majer teaches neighbourhood children is likewise paratext only (ANOTHER-UPPER reading §7.8). The narrative's own scene of Majer teaching children (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 129) is what this argument relies on.

**Uneven interiority.** Louis, Monica, Isaac, Cyril and, in the prequel, Glen have interior access. Mary, Rutherford, MacLaggan, Majer, Hilda and the house elders are seen mostly from outside. Statements here about their motives rest on their recorded speech and acts (checkpoint §4.2 item 4).

**Open threads at V12.** The series is unfinished, and this argument makes no predictions. In particular:

- Monica's mastership of Isaac is in progress. His first spell, his own formula, is decided but not cast (V12 obs. 112).
- Isaac's foretold loss is a prophecy (SW-P-0066), and the unnamed feelings around Monica and Cyril stay unnamed (SW-C-0093).
- The seventh Sage is unselected (SW-P-0053). Louis's search for a candidate and Graham Sanders's rising apprentice are recorded without being connected to it (V11 obs. 008, 011).
- Carla's family matter is open (V12 obs. 182).
- Hubert keeps a withheld secret about ancient artefacts (V12 obs. 108).

**Withheld identities.** This argument preserves every identity the text withholds. The patron called 閣下 is not identified (SW-C-0039). That an honorific of the same form is used to Duke Clockford in a prequel scene is the prequel's usage, and it is not extended (SW-C-0099). The physicians'-association authority who helped Hilda is not identified with anyone, Woodman included (SW-C-0076). The researcher whom the lower prequel volume says was executed two years before the soirée is unnamed, and no identity is asserted (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §8.5). The person Mary does not want to die, the hidden small star, the seventh Sage and the prison's attacker are not discussed. The ice spirit's identity with the pale-gold girl is implied, not stated (SW-C-0086, SW-C-0091). This argument refers to the girl only as the giver of Adolf's bell.

**Distinct persons with similar names.** Sams Horaceson, Majer's teacher, and Peter Sams, the traitor, are different people. The red-haired senior of Glen's Minerva memory is given as the V06 reading gives him. The prequel's unnamed "senior" is not otherwise identified.
