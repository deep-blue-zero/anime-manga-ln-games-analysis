# Silent Witch — disclosure, knowledge and narrative withholding

## 1. The argument in brief

In *Silent Witch* a truth is rarely just a fact. It is a position that someone holds. Rank protects it. A pact of silence trades it. And it becomes true in public only when an authority ratifies it.

The series' main movement runs through that economy. Monica begins by concealing herself. Her concealment works less because she lies well than because other people are sure that someone like her cannot be what she is. She ends the first arc by disclosing herself, but only to particular people, at a stated price, and in order to ask for help. The climax of V09 does not replace power-made truth with plain truth. Monica takes over the machinery by which authority certifies what is true and turns it to mercy. Meanwhile the narration keeps the real account for the reader.

The narration works on the same principle. It hands out knowledge unevenly. Sometimes it puts the reader ahead of every character. Sometimes it puts the reader behind the person who is acting. The reader therefore learns the discipline the best characters practise: to hold what one knows, to keep knowledge apart from inference, and to leave disclosure to the person entitled to make it. What stays withheld at V12 is not a lapse in that design. It is the design still running.

The argument has five parts:

- a map of the secrets and their holders (section 3);
- how concealment works and fails, and what loyalty means inside it (sections 4 to 6);
- how rank and institutions act as authorities on truth (section 7);
- the staged truth of V09 and the accounting of disclosure (sections 8 and 9);
- how the narration withholds from the reader (section 10).

The strongest counterargument is that the secrets are only plot devices. Section 11 takes it seriously.

## 2. Four orders of knowing, and how this argument names people

The checkpoint requires four orders to be kept apart (checkpoint §8, criterion 4). They matter more here than anywhere else in the corpus.

- **Source order** is the order of the books: V01 to V04, IV -after- (AFTER04), V05 to V09, IX -extra- (EXTRA09), V10 to V12, then the two prequel volumes, -another- upper and lower (ANOTHER-UPPER, ANOTHER-LOWER).
- **Diegetic order** is the order of events in the story world. The prequel volumes happen years before V01 but were read last. Their evidence is marked here as retrospective.
- **Revelation order** is the order in which the reader learns a proposition. It differs from diegetic order constantly. The real prince died about ten years before V01, and the reader learns how in V08.
- **Participant knowledge** is what a given character knows at a given point. The ledger records it proposition by proposition in the SW-K series. No character inherits what another knows, and no character knows what only the reader has been shown.

Names follow the handoff contract's section 15. The young man everyone calls Felix is Isaac Walker. V08 discloses this in narration (SW-C-0063). Before that point almost every character knows him only as Felix. The exceptions are Clockford, Elliott, his spirit Wildianu and, as V09 reveals, the king. This argument calls him Isaac when it speaks analytically, and "Felix" where the name as known inside a scene does the work. The dead boy whose name Isaac carries is called the real prince, or Arc, his secret name (SW-C-0063). Monica Everett is "Monica". Her cover name, Monica Norton, is used where the cover is the point.

The traitor curse-caster is Barry Oates, who served at Leinbourg as the footman Peter Sams (SW-C-0039). The readings mostly call him Sams, and so does this argument. Five identities stay withheld throughout, as the checkpoint requires (§4.2): the patron Sams called 閣下; the person Mary does not want to die, and the hidden small star; the seventh Sage; Hilda's collaborator; and the prison's attacker by name. Two identities are only implied: the ice spirit of V12 and the pale-gold girl of V11. They are written here as implied.

The table shows two things before any argument is made. First, almost every secret is held by more than one person, and the holders do not share it with each other. Second, the protagonist is at the centre of only some of these secrets. On the second prince's identity, the father's case and the Sages' affairs, Monica begins as an outsider who must infer. The series is less about one hidden identity than about a society in which nearly everyone is hiding something from the person who matters most. The V04 reading says this in one sentence: almost every major character hides something from the person who matters most (V04 §6.10).

## 4. Concealment that depends on other people's certainty

### 4.1 How the cover holds

The cover identity looks like a mystery plot. The first volume shows that it is not one, at least not for the reader. The colour frontispiece prints Monica's name and title before the prologue withholds them (V01 obs. 124; SW-F-0009). The reader of this edition knows who the Silent Witch is before meeting her. What the prologue shows instead is how she looks to people who do not know her (V01 §6.1).

That is the key to how her concealment works. In the first scene the delivery girl Annie asks whether Monica can use magic. Monica cannot answer, and the secret is kept only because Annie answers her own question: a real mage would be making a career in the capital, not hiding on a mountain (V01 obs. 005). The V01 reading calls this a pattern. Monica's concealment rarely depends on her lying well. It depends on other people's confidence that someone like her could not be what she is.

The cleverest person in the school confirms it. Isaac, as Felix, lists three explanations for her: a minder from the duke, an agent of the king, or an assassin. He rejects the true one, the king's agent, because she is too incompetent for anyone to have sent (V01 obs. 069; SW-K-0004). He rejects it for exactly the reason Louis gave for choosing her. The cover is built from other people's judgments about rank, competence and appearance. It is a social fact before it is a lie.

Rank is part of it in a second way. Every Sage holds a count-equivalent title. Monica has mislaid the patent and the ring among her papers (V01 obs. 017). She spends the volume being despised as a commoner by people she outranks or equals. The cover therefore inverts the school's rule that rank is truth (section 7). It also prepares the climax. When she finally speaks at the Supreme Council, she speaks with the authority of the rank she has hidden.

### 4.2 Small lies, and power seen only by a spirit

Within the cover Monica tells small lies. In AFTER04 she says she knows a famous workshop only by name, invents a contagious illness to inspect the prince's skin for curse marks, and lies to protect a boy's forbidden training (AFTER04 obs. 044, 119, 137). Her power is seen only by an earth spirit and by the reader; everyone else reads the spirit's last strike as an attack on the weakest person present (AFTER04 obs. 092, 095; SW-F-0030).

The AFTER04 reading notes that the volume treats these lies as harmless (AFTER04 §8.4). That is a fair limit: not every concealment in the series is weighed. But the same companion shows the cover beginning to fail before Isaac, whose inferences outpace her lies (SW-C-0042). The pattern of later volumes is set. The lie works against people who are certain, and fails against people who are curious.

### 4.3 Breaches, and the price of a kept secret

V05 makes Monica act openly as the Silent Witch before people who know her as Monica Norton. Her concealment fails in three directions at once (SW-C-0046). Bartholomeus unveils her and holds a misread secret over her. Isaac recognises Nero, infers that the Witch saved Cyril, and finally extracts that she belongs to his school (V05 obs. 063, 106, 129). He does it by a deliberate slip that she cannot help answering.

V06 adds pressure from three sides, and Monica counts them herself: Isaac's search for a small girl with an injured left hand, a Kerbeck inquirer, and Hubert Dee (V06 obs. 095). Hubert knows who she is and bargains with it. He will keep her secret for a price, and she cannot accuse him without his revealing her (V06 obs. 133, 134). Here knowledge becomes currency for the first time between two mages. A secret held over someone is a lien on them.

This is the series' first lesson about disclosure: a secret is rarely lost all at once. It leaks to particular people, and each leak creates a new relation, often a debt. Bernie, who works out her secret guard duty in V03, hides her from Cyril and names his price as lifelong gratitude (V03 obs. 054–056). The V03 reading leaves open whether that is repentance or possession. Either way, the person who learns the secret gains a hold, and the series makes the hold part of the relationship.

### 4.4 Loyalty as the choice of whom to deceive

The checkpoint folds the candidate domain of loyalty, deception and self-authorship into this argument (§6). The evidence shows why. In this series loyalty is defined by whom one lies to and whom one tells.

Monica's first act of operational disloyalty is a kept confidence. In V04 she reports meeting Isaac to Louis but withholds what Ike told her. She does not want to betray her first friend of the night (V04 obs. 067; SW-K-0050; SW-C-0029). The V04 reading puts the choice precisely. Read as loyalty to a friend it is sympathetic; read as a breach of the mission it is the first time she withholds operational information from the man who commissioned it (V04 §8.5). By V07 she withholds four matters from Louis: the night outing, her own inquiry, Bartholomeus and Raul's knowledge (V07 obs. 143).

Ike himself is a mask that permits candour. At the festival Isaac offers Monica a fiction: think of him as a ghost, a friend who is not really anywhere (V03 obs. 087). Monica answers that she too is a ghost tonight, neither the Silent Witch nor Monica Norton, and asks him to play on (V03 obs. 100). The ledger names the device: disguise as the condition of candour (SW-F-0019). Two people who are both concealing identities can only be honest with each other inside a third, agreed fiction.

The same holds for Isabelle's villainess and the cast's other roles (SW-F-0016). Performance is not the opposite of sincerity here; it is the form sincerity takes under concealment. Monica authors Monica Norton, Isaac authors Felix, Isabelle authors a villainess, and each authored self carries real loyalties. The series does not ask whether the self is false. It asks who is allowed to know it was authored.

## 5. A name held for a dead boy

### 5.1 The longest secret, held without hypothesis

Isaac's identity is the series' largest secret and its most carefully managed. From V02 to V07 the readings kept a growing list of details around "Felix" and declined to join them (SW-C-0022). The list is long:

- a child in Elliott's memory who was sickly, shy and devoted to astronomy (V03 obs. 018–019);
- a vow, made ten years ago, to inscribe the name Felix Arc Ridill in history, spoken of the name in the third person (V03 obs. 065);
- Mary's loss of his fate at the same date (V03 obs. 070);
- a dream of a frail boy and a page whipped for smuggling him an astronomy book (V03 obs. 112–113);
- a scar he calls necessary (V03 obs. 121);
- a water spirit against an earth name, and eye colours that differ (SW-C-0050);
- in V07, four witnesses whose accounts do not fit the man the school sees, and a page called Isaac Walker said to have died in a fire (V07 obs. 159–176, 193, 202; SW-C-0058).

The text supplies these as facts. It does not state the relation among them. The V07 reading records Monica beginning to say that the real Felix is gone, and the text breaking off (V07 obs. 211, 213; SW-K-0098). The conclusion exists in a character's mind and is not spoken.

This is fair-play withholding, but it is more than that. The series makes its readers practise the same discipline its characters must. Bridget half knew. Monica is convinced. Neither can say it, because neither can prove it, and to say it would be to destroy someone. Knowledge without proof, held in silence, is the condition of most characters in the middle volumes.

### 5.2 The bystanders

Two characters knew and did nothing, and V08 makes their silence a theme.

Elliott recognised the page's eyes in the new prince as a boy. The new prince nearly strangled him and told him the prince had died to set him free. Elliott chose to watch as a bystander, and to laugh at his ruin (V08 obs. 216–223). Porter knew the present prince was false and, in his own words, stayed a bystander (V08 obs. 081). Porter is the foil for Monica. She tells him that she too could do nothing when her father was burned, but that she can do more now, because she is the Silent Witch (V08 obs. 084).

The contrast is the moral hinge of the father's case and the identity together. Knowing is not a virtue in this series. What one does with knowledge is. Elliott and Porter each hold a truth that could have changed things, and each keeps it for reasons the volume makes intelligible. Monica holds two truths, Isaac's identity and her father's innocence, which cannot both be honoured. Her choice between them is the subject of V08 and V09.

### 5.3 Disclosure in three voices

V08 tells the secret three times (SW-F-0044). Monica states it as a conclusion and Yuan's side endorses it (V08 obs. 040). Then the narration gives it as memory, from inside Isaac. He falls asleep in a carriage, dreams of his first carriage ride, and the chapters that follow are his memory. When he wakes, the narration corrects itself from "Isaac" back to "Felix" (V08 obs. 097, 176; SW-F-0043). Finally the arrest, the testimonies and Monica's decision follow from it.

The order matters. The identity is not finally established by the heroine's deduction. The V08 reading is explicit that it rests on narration from inside the person's own memory, on Elliott's independent account, and on Clockford's words within that memory (V08 §8.1). Monica's account is even wrong in one particular: she supposes the prince died of some cause and the page's death was faked. The memory shows how he died (V08 obs. 143). The series lets its best reasoner reach the truth and then shows that the truth belongs, first, to the person who lived it.

The narration also judges what it discloses. It calls Isaac's adoption of the name a childish and foolish wish for which he distorted the prince's death (V08 obs. 149). Disclosure here is not neutral exposition. It comes with a verdict on the self that was authored.

### 5.4 Recognition, and who is told

The identity is known by V08 to Clockford, Isaac, Elliott, Monica and Nero, and through Elliott to Bridget, Cyril, Neil and Claudia (SW-K-0102). The public believes the official story. The king knows Isaac's name and has known all along (V09 obs. 106; SW-K-0115).

Recognition runs the other way too. Isaac did not identify Monica as the Silent Witch from V01 to V08, though he admired the Witch and grew close to Monica. He recognises her only when she enters the Supreme Council wearing his pendant, and calls himself a fool (SW-C-0008; V09 obs. 080–081). Cyril had half guessed from her hand and the hooded figure. When she reveals herself he kneels and apologises, and is mocked back into scolding her as his junior (SW-C-0009; V09 obs. 008–011).

These two recognitions show how the series values knowledge. Isaac, the master of inference, missed the one identity he most wanted to find, because he had placed the Witch at a distance as a hero. Cyril, who attends to hands and handwriting, half knew and did not act. Neither recognition is a triumph of deduction. Each is a matter of attention and of what a person is prepared to see.

## 6. The father's case: knowledge in pieces

### 6.1 Each witness holds a fragment

Monica's father, Venedict Rain, was executed as a heretic when she was ten, after a curse-caster he refused had him charged on planted evidence, under a great noble's pressure and without trial (SW-C-0032). No one person holds that history. It is distributed among people who will not or cannot share it.

- **Sams** holds the denunciation. In the V04 secret episode he rides hidden in the Duke of Leinbourg's carriage, relies on a master he calls 閣下, and hates the memory of Venedict's catchphrase (V04 obs. 122, 123; SW-K-0053). In V05, dying, he calls Monica by her father's name, says he sold Venedict to 閣下, and is consumed by his own curse (V05 obs. 147, 148; SW-K-0070, SW-K-0071).
- **Hilda Everett**, Venedict's former assistant and Monica's adoptive mother, holds the shape of the plot and will not name the noble, to protect her (V05 obs. 166, 167; SW-K-0072). The narration of V08 states that she had hidden Clockford's part from Monica (V08 obs. 265). She also holds the designs for the black chalice, taken from the seized laboratory on the night of the arrest (V08 §3, prologue; obs. 267).
- **Porter** holds the motive in code. "The black chalice" is the name his novels give to Venedict's design, a tool that reads hereditary information from blood and could show that the present prince is not the king's son (V08 obs. 077–078; SW-K-0104).
- **Bartholomeus** holds Sams's employment at Clockford's house from about eight years ago (V06 obs. 204, 205; SW-K-0083), and a gardener holds Sams's presence there as a physician (V07 obs. 197–199; SW-K-0095).

Monica assembles the fragments. In V06 she infers that Clockford is likely connected to her father's death, and the text marks this as her inference (SW-C-0053; V06 §6.4). In V08 the narration states that Clockford had a part; in V09 the narration, from Monica's viewpoint, states that he arranged the execution (SW-C-0064, SW-C-0045).

The structure is a detective's, but its emotional content is different. The people who hold fragments are mostly people who love Monica or loved her father. Hilda's silence is protection. Monica decides against it: she will seek the truth even at the cost of her seat (V05 obs. 170). The V05 reading frames the conflict exactly. Each silence protects someone, and each leaves another person acting blind (V05 §8.4). The series does not settle which is right. It shows that to inherit a parent's story, a child must take it from people who withheld it out of love.

### 6.2 What stays separate

The readings hold one line firmly, and so does this argument. Three references in the father's case are kept apart because the text keeps them apart:

- the patron Sams called 閣下;
- the great noble whom Hilda will not name;
- the duke whose part the narration states.

The V05 and V06 readings set out the details that seem to point towards an identification and the reasons against it, and register none (V05 §8.2; V06 §8.2). The V08 reading keeps 閣下 open even after narration states the duke's part, because the mechanism of the denunciation still runs through an unidentified patron (V08 §8.2). Emmanuel's patron, whom he calls only "that person" in his thoughts, is identified as Clockford by the same speaker's usage across V06 and V07. That identification is not extended to 閣下 or to the great noble (SW-C-0057). In the prequel's time, Adolf addresses the duke with a high honorific that the reading pointedly declines to extend to the patron of SW-C-0039 (SW-C-0099; ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 215). In V09, when the father's case is resolved as far as it will be, the word 閣下 is not used at all (SW-C-0039; V09 §8.5).

This is not pedantry. It is the series' central habit at its most sustained: the text leaves unsupplied what a careful reader might supply by inference, and makes the temptation visible. The open question is part of what the father's case means. Monica's father was killed by a structure, not only by a man, and its top is still unnamed at V12.

### 6.3 The name as a curse

V08 gives the father's name a new function. On a rainy evening Monica asks Isaac whether he would sacrifice someone for a wish, and he says he would. She tells him her father's name, begs him never to forget it, and runs out into the rain. She calls it a curse (V08 obs. 180, 181).

Disclosure here is a weapon turned on oneself. Monica cannot expose Isaac. Exposure could clear her father but would have Isaac executed (V08 obs. 182). So she gives him the one piece of knowledge that will bind him to remember what his existence cost. Isaac, for his part, is glad that the Witch knows his own name. He can climb the scaffold at ease (V08 obs. 261). The volume turns on remembering names (SW-C-0068). In this series to tell someone a name is to place an obligation on them, and the obligation can be grief.

## 7. Rank and institutions as authorities on truth

### 7.1 The school: rank is truth, with a counterexample

The checkpoint also folds in the candidate domain of school, aristocratic institutions and available agency, because its evidence is mostly concealment under rank (§6). The founding scene is the staircase in V01. A count's daughter tells Cyril, as a council officer, that Lana pushed Monica, and invites him to prefer an old family's word to a merchant baron's. The narration gives Monica's knowledge as knowledge: in this school, when a person of higher rank says you are at fault, that becomes the truth, and rank is everything (V01 obs. 058). So Monica makes herself the cause. That is the one statement a person of no standing is always allowed to make (V01 obs. 059).

Two chapters later Cyril mentions, as an administrative aside, that he questioned the witnesses, established what happened, and required the count's daughter to write an apology (V01 obs. 091). Monica's reaction is that such people exist too. The ledger records it as the first counterexample to rank as epistemic authority (SW-C-0011; SW-K-0014). An authority may investigate rather than defer.

The rest of the school arc complicates the rule rather than refuting it. In V02 rank is used as a weapon of justice. Isabelle turns her family's power against the bully, and the Kerbeck troops are named as protectors of a whole region (V02 obs. 052, 053). Elliott's creed ties rank to duty (V02 obs. 024). It has a biographical root: an uncle ruined by a marriage across rank (V03 obs. 022; AFTER04 obs. 112; SW-C-0044). His warning that talent crossing class walls is either envied or used is fulfilled by the series' own plot. The talented commoner he once said was ruined by cunning people is Isaac, by his own acknowledgement (SW-C-0044).

So the school teaches a precise lesson. Rank does not make a statement true. It makes a statement count. That distinction is what Monica will exploit at the Supreme Council.

### 7.2 The Seven Sages: unity outward, concealment inward

The Sages are the institution with which Monica is officially identified, and they are a body of secrets. V06 shows the full company for the first time and sets out its politics (V06 §6.5). A movement in the noble assembly would put the Sages under it (V06 obs. 051). Even Clockford can only ask a Sage, not command one (V06 obs. 062). When one of their number, Emmanuel, is found holding a whistle that brings war and using captured spirits as power, the Sages hide his crime. The reason is plain: a scandal would let the assembly seize them (V06 obs. 187). Monica sees no justice on either side (V06 obs. 190). The ledger records the cover-up as self-protection, not justice (SW-C-0054).

V07 adds the inward face. The Sages fight as one body for the first time in the forest, and conceal from one another throughout (SW-C-0062). Louis infers that Monica has a helper she has not disclosed. He resolves to withhold information and test her (V07 obs. 135, 136). His proposal that she seal the witnesses' memories is narrated as such a test (V07 obs. 138). Mary forbids both sanction and memory sealing and swears the students to silence (V07 obs. 139, 141). The affair is closed by a pact of silence, enforced by the senior Sage's word.

The Sages are therefore the series' model of an epistemic institution. Its authority depends on independence. Its independence depends on hiding its own failures. And its members trust each other only as far as they must. Monica belongs to it and learns its methods. In V09 she uses them.

### 7.3 The court: the official story

The court produces the series' plainest false truths. When Isaac is arrested, Emmanuel's forged note blames the Empire. It says the prince was killed and replaced to make the kingdom a vassal (V08 obs. 209). The official story dates the substitution to just before the end of the winter break, years after it happened (V08 obs. 211). At the Sages' meeting every faction finds it convenient to blame the Empire. Louis gives the principle: what suits those in power becomes the truth (V08 obs. 252).

Above the court sits the king, the most silent knower in the series. The checkpoint notes that the king is unshown for most of the run (SW-C-0005). When he appears, he has known Isaac's name all along, and he overlooks his son's killers for the country (V09 obs. 106). He set Louis on the prince to catch a slip (V08 obs. 185). He accepts Monica's settlement because a game without checkmate often suits the winner (V09 obs. 105). The epistemic summit of the kingdom is a man who knew, said nothing, and ratified a fiction.

### 7.4 The Mage Guild and the law

The Mage Guild appears less often, but always as a licensing authority over knowledge. In V01 the narration states that mind interference is permitted only for interrogating grave criminals or in national emergency, with authorisation from the Guild or the Sages. Monica is unsure whether her status covers her, and Lynn answers with Louis's motto that what is not discovered does no harm (V01 obs. 110). The power to erase knowledge is itself regulated.

The Guild also confers trust on people the reader cannot see. Hilda's collaborator is an unnamed authority of the physicians' association, trusted by the Guild (SW-C-0076; EXTRA09 obs. 007, 008). In the prequel a neutral Guild executive reports which noble factions are pushing which candidates for a Sage's seat (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 063). Rutherford knows that a recommendation of Louis would be crushed by Clockford (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 067). Isaac, speaking as Felix, tells Elliott that his grandfather, Clockford, crushed Louis's earlier candidacy (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 036; SW-K-0159). The bodies that certify mages are arenas in which rank decides outcomes.

The law changes once in the run. By V10 crimes of forbidden art carry life imprisonment rather than death. The black chalice helped bring the change, and Monica feels that her father's research has changed the country (SW-C-0083; V10 obs. 007–009). This is the only point at which institutional truth moves towards her father. It moves without clearing his name.

### 7.5 What the institutions have in common

Across all four, the pattern is the same. Each institution:

- certifies what counts as true for its members and for the public;
- protects its own standing by concealing its failures;
- holds more knowledge at the top than it publishes;
- can be moved by someone who knows its rules well enough to use them.

That last point is the bridge to V09. Rank as epistemic authority (SW-C-0011), the Sages' cover-up (SW-C-0054), their inward concealment (SW-C-0062) and the staged truth (SW-C-0069) are one structure seen at four scales. The heroine does not escape it. She learns to operate it.

## 8. The staged truth

### 8.1 The settlement

The Supreme Council of V09 resolves the V08 crisis by an argued fiction, not by exposure (SW-C-0069; V09 §6.2). Its elements are worth setting out, because each one is a different use of authority over truth:

- **A real tool, honestly demonstrated.** The black chalice is genuine, and Monica first shows it on a count and his son (V09 obs. 088).
- **Borrowed blood.** When Isaac's blood is added, the chalice reddens only because Monica adds Albert's blood from her sleeve (V09 obs. 091, 116).
- **A dead culprit.** The confession is blamed on a curse laid by Sams, who is also blamed for the curse dragon and Emmanuel's death. Monica names Sams but deliberately leaves out his tie to Clockford (V09 obs. 094, 095).
- **A performed cure.** Ray "lifts" the curse with a glowing sign (V09 obs. 097).
- **Royal ratification.** The king accepts the verdict because it serves the country. Mary says Monica brought him the plan in advance (V09 obs. 098, 105, 113).
- **A silent threat.** Monica warns Clockford without words that she will reveal everything if he moves again (V09 obs. 099). The bilingual plate prints that warning before the narrative begins (V09 obs. 004).

Each element borrows an authority. The tool borrows the authority of her father's science. The blood borrows a prince's. The dead culprit borrows the court's existing story that someone foreign or forbidden was to blame. The cure borrows a Sage's expertise in curses. The verdict borrows the king's. And the threat borrows the one thing Monica alone holds: what she knows about Clockford. She uses Louis's lesson that power makes truth (V09 obs. 096). She does not refute it.

### 8.2 Rank finally spoken

The Sage's rank that Monica hid for nine volumes is what lets the settlement work. She enters as the Silent Witch, reveals her secret guard duty, and silences a minister (V09 obs. 082). She declares the accused man the real prince (V09 obs. 083). The school's rule from V01 (when a person of higher rank says something, it becomes the truth) is applied by the girl who suffered it. Her declaration counts because of who she is.

The volume's frame for this is silence imposed and silence broken (SW-C-0074). The Silent Witch breaks silence at the council and imposes silence on Clockford. Rank is both voice and gag. Monica's silence, once a refuge, is now an instrument of political control.

### 8.3 Knowledge after the verdict

The verdict creates a new distribution of knowledge. The ledger tracks it precisely:

- The trick with Albert's blood is known to Monica, Albert, Isaac and the conspirators (SW-K-0114).
- The king knows Isaac's identity, and so do Isaac and Mary (SW-K-0115).
- The accident is known to Isaac, Monica and the council members (SW-K-0116).
- Monica's knowledge of Clockford's part is known to Monica, Clockford and Mary (SW-K-0117).

The settlement holds because each party knows something the others need kept quiet. Isaac and Clockford alone know he has no royal blood (V09 obs. 092). Clockford knows that Monica knows. Monica keeps Clockford's weakness by omitting his tie to Sams (V09 obs. 095). The king knows everything and wants none of it said. The peace is a balance of mutual knowledge. Each holder's silence guarantees the others'.

Three volumes later the arrangement has become a recognised political asset. The Earl of Kerbeck tells Monica that his house knows the value of her silence and will not disturb the peace it brings (V12 obs. 015). Silence has become, in effect, a treaty.

### 8.4 The truth the narration keeps

One could take the council's verdict as what happened. The V09 reading shows how the volume prevents this (V09 §8.1). It shows the trick (obs. 116). It shows what Isaac and Clockford know (obs. 092). It shows the king's private knowledge (obs. 106) and Mary's account to Clockford (obs. 113). The reading therefore registers the verdict as the official story within the fiction, and the narrated events as the story's truth.

That is the essential asymmetry. Within the story world, the staged truth replaces the truth. For the reader, it does not. The narration lets the reader hold both. It also tells the trial from both sides, alternating the accused and the advocate (SW-F-0047). The reader sees Isaac's despair and Monica's pain in heels behind her composure (V09 obs. 087). The fiction is shown being made, at a cost to the person making it.

### 8.5 How the volume judges it

The V09 reading's normative finding is plain. The volume counts the costs: her father's name stays uncleared, Clockford goes unpunished, and a dead man is blamed for crimes in which Clockford had a hand (V09 obs. 120, 095, 094). It counts the gains: no war, no execution, the king's interest served (V09 obs. 105, 130). It lets Neil defend Clockford's usefulness to the country (V09 obs. 103). It presents the deception as wise and does not frame it as a wrong (V09 §8.3).

This argument accepts that finding and adds one thing. The series can present a public lie as wise because it has spent eight volumes showing that public truth in this world is always certified by power. The staged truth is not a fall from an honest order. It is the honest order's own method, used by someone who has learned it, for an end the method's usual users would not choose.

## 9. What disclosure costs, and what it buys

### 9.1 The cost of Monica's disclosure

Monica's own disclosure is priced in advance. In V02 Louis reminds her that Monica Norton is a temporary person (V02 obs. 096). In V04 she thinks she will not be at next year's ball and wants to remain Monica Norton a little longer (V04 obs. 098). Before the reveal in V08 she sets out her treasures. She decides to rely on others even if it costs her life as Monica Norton (V08 obs. 283). The cost of disclosure is the self the cover allowed her to have: an ordinary schoolgirl with friends.

Earlier concealment had costs that fell on others. When she hid Casey's assassination attempt in V02, Cyril believed the falling timber was his oversight and apologised to her (V02 obs. 098; SW-K-0026). She wept, the narration says, not from grief but from guilt for all her lies. The V02 reading records that the volume presents the mercy sympathetically and leaves its justice unjudged (SW-C-0021; V02 §8.4). By V03 Cyril still blames himself and she cannot clear him (SW-C-0021). Concealment is not costless kindness. Someone pays for it, and often it is a person who does not know they are paying.

Disclosure, when it comes, is costly in a different way. Lana is angry. Cyril kneels and then recovers his scolding. Isabelle wipes her face (V09 obs. 008–011). The friends who were deceived forgive, but the forgiveness is shown as work they do.

### 9.2 What disclosure buys

What Monica's disclosure buys is help. The rescue is collective: every element of the plan is supplied by someone she asked (SW-C-0072; V09 obs. 022–037, 048–072). Bernie lends a workshop, Lana obtains materials, Hubert and Bartholomeus build the chalice. Isabelle and the magic-history club bring petitions, and Bridget coaches her bearing. Glen, Hubert, Robert and Cyril hold off Louis, with Ray and Raul setting the trap. Albert gives his blood.

This links disclosure to the series' larger arc of asking for help (SW-C-0052, SW-C-0059). Monica could not ask while she was concealed, because asking requires saying who one is and what one needs. Disclosure is therefore not just the end of a secret. It is the condition of a new kind of relation. The ledger records this at the level of mission and belonging: Louis chose her for her distrust and read her attachment as a risk, and at V08 she ends the warning herself by revealing her identity and asking for help (SW-C-0024).

The narration names Louis's error as assuming she would never ask anyone for help (V09 obs. 072; SW-C-0033). Louis is the best knower in the series. He reads the brooch's purpose and the mission's real meaning. He proves the prince false by his own inquiry (V08 obs. 185) and keeps that from Monica because he thinks her too soft (V08 obs. 188; SW-K-0109). He is nearly certain of the swap and Clockford's part, but must accept the official story without proof (V09 obs. 046). Monica keeps him away from the council precisely because he would expose everything and see through her (V09 obs. 049). His model of people is a model of what they hide. What it cannot model is a person who chooses to tell.

### 9.3 Isaac's release

Isaac's release is a disclosure too, and it comes from a document. The real prince's diary was withheld in two ways. Its last page was hidden from Isaac, who never had it, and its last sentence was withheld from the reader even while Monica read it (V08 obs. 246, 247; SW-F-0045). In V09 Isaac reads it. The prince meant to live and to visit Ike one day as a grown man and hear what life he had chosen (V09 obs. 122). With Isaac's memory of the prince letting go with one hand to reach for a star, Monica concludes the death was an accident (V09 obs. 123, 124; SW-C-0070). The obsession leaves him (V09 obs. 125).

Here what disclosure buys is release from a false belief. Isaac had built his life on reading the death as a suicide committed for his sake. The knowledge that frees him was always there, held by a dead maid and a convent, and it reached him only because Monica went looking. The series makes the longest-withheld truth in the first arc also the most merciful one.

Isaac then offers Monica revenge. She refuses: she chose his life over her father's name (V09 obs. 118–120). The choice of rescue over justice (SW-C-0067) is itself a choice about disclosure. Full exposure, the course Monica calls the most correct, would have told the whole truth and killed him (V08 obs. 253). She chose a partial truth and a living friend.

### 9.4 After the first arc: secrets as political capital

After V09 the economy of knowledge does not end. It moves into the open politics of adult life.

- **Isaac** lives two lives: a restrained duke publicly still called Felix, and an apprentice overjoyed to learn (SW-C-0081). He recruits Brendan with the dead prince's pleading face. He dislikes himself because calculation outweighs his sincerity (V10 obs. 143, 145). Brendan infers that the spirit's master serves Felix without being Felix (V10 obs. 169; SW-K-0131). He does not know that the duke he serves is not Queen Irene's son.
- In V11 Isaac keeps a backer from his old assassination plan as an ally, holding that man's secret over him (V11 obs. 073–074; SW-C-0089). In V12 he works through a pact of silence in the East (SW-C-0092; V12 obs. 015, 021).
- **Raul's family** lives under tacit pacts with the crown over drugs and a sealed book (SW-C-0087; V11 obs. 075, 087–088).
- **The Sages** have not shared Darrell's testimony about a bell (V11 obs. 162).
- **The public** will be told that Monica met an upper spirit by chance (V12 obs. 114).

The secret that once threatened a kingdom has become ordinary statecraft. The series' adults hold knowledge over one another as a matter of course, and the ex-prince is good at it. The V10 reading notes that the volume presents his persuasion as effective and harmless, while giving his own judgment against it (V10 §8.4). The question of whether managed knowledge is manipulation stays open.

### 9.5 Withholding moves inward

In the late volumes withholding turns towards feeling. Ray's chapters in V12 turn on a sentence a curse-user may not say. He believes his mother's love cursed his father. Mary knows she cursed only herself (SW-C-0090; V12 obs. 145, 185, 202). The series stages a truth that the son does not have and others do, as it did with the prince's diary. Frieda's answer is that feeling can be shown without the sentence.

Monica's own feelings are not named either. Isaac's love is stated in his viewpoint and goes unrecognised. Monica shows unnamed distress and shame concerning Cyril, and Cyril cannot read his own reluctance. The volume names none of these feelings (SW-C-0093). In a series whose plot has been driven by withheld identities, the last thing withheld at V12 is what the characters do not know about themselves. That continuity is part of the argument. The habit of withholding is not only a plotting technique. It is how the series understands interior life.

## 10. How the narration withholds from the reader

### 10.1 Two directions of irony

The narration distributes knowledge between reader and characters in two directions, and the series alternates them deliberately.

**The reader ahead.** In V01 the reader sees Isaac reject the true explanation for Monica (V01 obs. 069). The reader sees him kiss her struck cheek while she takes the kiss for Nero's paw. Nero, the one witness on her side, tells her to forget it (V01 obs. 073, 076; SW-K-0010; SW-C-0016). The reader watches Cyril's memory being erased, printed as a sentence the text stops printing (V01 obs. 081), and knows Thornley is the culprit before any character does. Isaac credits Louis for Thornley's arrest, which was Monica's (V01 obs. 120; SW-K-0013). In V06 Isaac orders Hubert killed "as an accident", and no character ever learns of it (V06 obs. 147, 149; SW-K-0087). The V01 reading describes the chapter of ten viewpoints in one line: no one in it sees what the reader sees (V01 §6.8).

**The reader behind.** At other times the reader knows less than the person acting.

- Louis forms a hypothesis about Felix in V04, and the text does not state it (V04 obs. 120; SW-K-0049).
- Monica's conclusion about Felix in V07 is not spoken (V07 obs. 211, 213).
- She reads the diary's last line in V08, and the reader does not (V08 obs. 246).
- In V09 she presents a plan she calls her life's greatest cheat, and its content is explained off the page (V09 obs. 019).
- Hubert's price is a battle against someone to be held off. The name is given in the room, not to the reader (V09 obs. 024).
- Some unnamed others will help (V09 obs. 035).
- What she asks of Heidi and of Mary in V08 is withheld until the result (V08 §8.5).

The two directions train different habits. When the reader is ahead, the reader learns to watch characters act on false beliefs, and to register costs they cannot see. When the reader is behind, the reader learns to trust a character's competence and wait. Both teach the same thing: knowledge belongs to particular people at particular times, and the reader's knowledge is one position among others, not a view from above.

### 10.2 External views of power

The series repeatedly shows power from outside. Monica's full power is first narrated through people who do not know her: Isabelle in the prologue, Cyril in the forest, Thornley, Louis (SW-F-0001; SW-C-0010). The word "monster" recurs in four viewpoints. From V02 her own viewpoint narrates her power too, but as calculation under constraint, and the awe stays with others (SW-F-0010). The ledger calls the resulting structure a three-layer discrepancy: legend, observers' misreadings and her interior shown side by side, with the reader alone holding all three (SW-F-0007).

The checkpoint notes a related asymmetry: Mary, Clockford, Raul and the other Sages are seen mostly from outside (§4.2). This is a form of withholding too. Clockford in V08 is seen acting four times, and given reasons in the secret episode. He never speaks of Venedict Rain in his own viewpoint (V08 §6.4). In V09 he is seen only from outside, and Mary supplies his history (V09 §6.5). Mary reads the substitution as revenge on the crown. He neither denies nor confirms it, and the reading registers it as her inference (SW-C-0071). The series' most powerful man is opaque by design. His motives reach the reader through someone who loved him.

### 10.3 Small techniques of withholding

The ledger's form records name a set of recurring techniques. Each is a way of telling the reader that something is being withheld, without saying what.

- **Typographic silence.** Withheld words are printed as dots or dashes (SW-F-0002). Cyril's erased walk to the records room is the clearest case (V01 obs. 081).
- **The narrator's deadpan correction.** One-line paragraphs correct a character's inference (SW-F-0003). In V08 the narrator marks Monica's decision to keep the truth to herself as something she believed "at this time" (V08 obs. 017). The withholding is announced as temporary at the moment it begins.
- **Anonymity resolved by object or trait.** An unnamed agent is identified by an object, such as the red jewel of V02's conspirator (SW-F-0013). Figures enter by red hair, rings or humming, and are named later (SW-F-0038).
- **The authoritative wrong verdict.** A knowing adult closes a volume with a judgment the reader has seen contradicted. Louis calls Monica a heartless witch at the end of V03, after the reader has watched her mourn, weep and hold out her hand (SW-F-0021; V03 obs. 130, 131).
- **The document withheld.** The diary is read in the story with its last line kept from the reader (SW-F-0045).

Each sets the reader's knowledge against a character's, so that the reader must notice the gap. The gap is where most of the series' ethical attention falls.

### 10.4 Implied identities, and the discipline they require

The series' first implied identity was resolved. The V01 prologue gives an unnamed, blue-eyed watcher who has found something he can lose himself in. The V01 reading recorded his identity with Felix as a strong inference, not a stated fact (V01 §6.11). V02 strengthened it to an observable text event (SW-C-0006). V08 retold the scene from the watcher's own side (V08 §6.7).

A reader might take that precedent as a licence: implied identities in this series turn out to be true. The readings decline the licence, and so does this argument. At V12 an ice spirit in a white dress, carrying an ice bell, devours the fire spirit Kayan before the prison break. She means to open the gate of audience and bury humanity in eternal winter. Her features match the pale-gold girl of V11, who gave Adolf the bell. The interlude does not name her or state the identity, and the reading treats it as implied (SW-C-0086; SW-C-0091; V12 §6.2, §8.2).

The same discipline governs the prison break. Adolf infers that the girl broke the prison with the bell (V11 obs. 021). Owen and Louis infer that the bell's owner did (V11 obs. 216, 217; SW-K-0135). V10 says only that the prison was broken from outside by an unknown mage (V10 obs. 173). The attacker is not named, and this argument does not name one.

The prequel offers the same temptation in a quieter form. ANOTHER-LOWER mentions a researcher executed two years before a court soirée. The text does not name him, and the reading does not identify him (ANOTHER-LOWER §8.5). The series consistently places gaps where an informed reader can feel the shape of what fits. It consistently refuses to fill them. Respecting that refusal is the only way to read the series on its own terms.

### 10.5 Paratext before prose

The edition itself discloses out of order. In V01 the frontispieces print the Witch's name and the climax's key caption before the reader meets Monica (V01 obs. 124; SW-F-0009). The cover's finger to the lips asks for silence and promises a secret at once, and the volume later gives the same gesture to Isaac (V01 obs. 123). In V08 an epigraph plate prints Isaac's thought on seeing the Witch's magic four chapters before he thinks it (V08 obs. 007). In V09 the plate prints Monica's silent warning before the narrative (V09 obs. 004).

These are paratext, and the brief requires that they be cited only as such. Their function is still worth stating. They show that the series' withholding is not mainly about suspense. The reader is often told what will happen. What the prose withholds is how it will happen, at what cost, and who will know.

### 10.6 The prequel read last, and the companions reread

The two prequel volumes were read after V12 but happen before V01. Their disclosures are supplemental retrospection and revise no frozen reading (checkpoint §4.2). They change what the main story means in hindsight in three ways.

First, they show the main story's secrets from before they were secrets to the reader. In ANOTHER-LOWER, at a court soirée, Louis watches the second prince, twelve or thirteen. Louis reads his smile as manufactured and his manner as too perfectly reproduced (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 031, 032). The reader, having read V08, can explain that manner. Louis in the prequel's time cannot, and the reading records his suspicion as his own (ANOTHER-LOWER §6.8). The knowledge boundary is exact. The character sees the symptom. The reader, coming last, knows the cause. Neither may lend that knowledge to the other.

Second, the prequel's secret episodes disclose causes that their protagonist never learns. The ANOTHER-UPPER secret episode shows that the rumour that sent Louis to Birdland was started by Adolf, out of spite. Louis never knows it (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 215; SW-K-0149). The same scene shows Clockford planning to seat his own men among the politically neutral Sages within ten years, naming Adolf and Emmanuel (SW-C-0099; SW-K-0150). Read last, this reveals a long design behind events the numbered track showed piecemeal.

Third, the prequel's own plots turn on information. Louis solves the selection crime by controlling information, testing for the slip and attending to a friend's habits (SW-C-0102; ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 083, 085, 136). Adolf's mistake is to say "roof" where only "stairs" had been given out. Glen seals Rosalie's memory out of misplaced mercy and shields his master by silence (SW-C-0101; ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 118–124). He escapes official censure only because Rosalie claims the fall caused her amnesia (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 162). The prequel's frame is itself a withholding device. Its prologue shows results whose causes the run supplies (SW-F-0063).

EXTRA09 works the same way at smaller scale. Most of its stories were written as store bonuses before V08's disclosure. Read after V09, they change. Isaac tells Wildianu to burn his drawer of treasures if anything happens to him, as things Felix Arc Ridill must not possess (EXTRA09 obs. 134). He tells Monica one can change an unwanted future because he is royal (EXTRA09 obs. 062). The reader now knows the speaker was not royal. The EXTRA09 reading records what each story shows without treating it as new disclosure (EXTRA09 §6.7). The same text yields a second meaning once the reader's knowledge has moved. The series rewards rereading because it was built around knowledge that changes what earlier sentences mean.

## 11. Counterarguments and counterevidence

### 11.1 "The secrets are only plot devices"

**The case.** This is the strongest objection. On this view the series is a light novel built from genre parts. There is a hidden-identity heroine, a prince with a secret, a murdered parent and a shadowy patron. Chapters end on revelations and volumes on secret episodes. The withholding of 閣下 into V12 is a serial hook. The cover identity is a comic engine: the joke is that the shy girl is the greatest mage, and the joke needs the secret. Calling this an "economy of knowledge" dresses up the conventions of the mystery and the romantic fantasy. The objection can cite real counterevidence:

- **Genre framing in the text.** The cover plot is borrowed openly from a romance novel within the novel (V01 §6.3; SW-F-0004), and the V01 reading lists "genre convention" as the rival reading of the performance device (SW-F-0016).
- **Unweighed lies.** Many lies are not weighed. AFTER04 treats Monica's small lies as harmless (AFTER04 §8.4).
- **The kiss.** The kiss Monica never learns of has no consequence and is played for laughs (SW-C-0016; V02 §8.7).
- **Prior disclosure.** The paratext discloses the heroine's identity before the prose, which suggests that the publisher, at least, treats her secret as a premise, not a theme (SW-F-0009).
- **Hooks.** The new threats of V10 to V12 are framed as contained plots inside a larger threat (SW-F-0057), a serial pattern.

**The answer.** The objection is right about the parts and wrong about the whole. Five kinds of evidence show that the secrets do more than drive plot.

1. **The secret most like a plot device is not treated as one.** If the Witch's identity were the suspense, the edition would not print her name before the prologue. It does (V01 obs. 124). What the prose withholds instead is how other people see her, and why they cannot see the truth (V01 obs. 005, 069). The interest lies in the social mechanism of the cover, not in its reveal.
2. **Costs are registered where a device would not need them.** The volumes count who pays for concealment. Cyril bears the blame in V02 (V02 obs. 098). Glen is a decoy and the narration calls Louis heartless for it (V04 §8.4). The settlement of V09 leaves a father uncleared and a guilty man free (V09 §8.3). A plot device needs revealing, not accounting. The series does the accounting.
3. **The disclosures are relational, not informational.** V08 tells the Felix secret three times, from three positions. Most of the volume concerns who knows and what they do with it: Porter, Elliott, Bridget, Louis, Monica (V08 §6.1, §6.8). V09's knowledge records (SW-K-0114 to SW-K-0117) show a settlement that holds only because specific people know specific things. A device reveals and moves on. This series stays with the new distribution.
4. **The withholding is ethically differentiated.** The series distinguishes protective silence (Hilda), bystanding (Porter, Elliott), institutional cover-up (the Sages), political lies (the court), merciful erasure (Glen) and the staged truth (Monica), and evaluates each differently. Machinery would not need such distinctions.
5. **The habit continues where no plot needs it.** In V12 the withheld matter is unnamed feeling and a forbidden sentence (SW-C-0090, SW-C-0093). There is no mystery to solve. The same form of attention to what is known and unsaid has become the series' way of describing people.

**What survives of the objection.** Some withholding is serial machinery. The secret episodes that end volumes are partly hooks, and 閣下's continued anonymity is at least partly an open thread kept open. Some concealments go unevaluated. The objection is a useful check on over-reading individual lies. It does not account for the series' sustained attention to who holds knowledge and what holding it does.

### 11.2 "The staged truth endorses cynicism: power makes truth"

**The case.** V09 rewards a lie told to the highest court. The volume calls it wise (V09 §8.3). Louis's maxim is used, not refuted (V09 obs. 096). On this view the series' last word on truth is that it is whatever the powerful agree to.

**The answer.** Within the story world, yes. For the reader, no. The narration shows the trick, the knowledge of the guilty and the king's private knowledge, so that the official story is always visible as a story (V09 §8.1). The volume also counts what the lie costs. The series' position is subtler than cynicism. Public truth is made by power. Private truth is kept by narration and by the people who know. And the ethics lie in what one does with the gap. Monica uses the gap for mercy. Clockford used it for substitution. The method is the same; the ends are not.

**Counterevidence that remains.** The volume does present the deception as wise and does not frame it as a wrong. A reader who wants a series to condemn institutional lies will not find that condemnation here.

### 11.3 "The reader's advantage turns characters into dupes"

**The case.** When the reader sees what no character sees, characters look foolish. Isaac rejects the truth about Monica. Louis's verdict on her is wrong. Isaac credits Louis for her work.

**The answer.** The reader's advantage alternates with the reader's disadvantage (section 10.1). The characters most often wrong are also the ones whose reasoning the series most respects. Isaac's inferences outpace Monica's lies (SW-C-0042). Louis proves the prince false by his own inquiry (V08 obs. 185). Monica reaches the identity before the narration states it (V08 obs. 040). What makes a character wrong is rarely stupidity. It is a model of other people that excludes something: in Louis's case, that Monica would ask for help (SW-C-0033). The series uses dramatic irony to show the limits of good minds, not to mock weak ones.

### 11.4 "Rank as epistemic authority is just setting"

**The case.** Every aristocratic school story has snobs. The rank material is background, not argument.

**The answer.** The rank material is structurally tied to the climax. The rule of V01 obs. 058 is applied in reverse at V09 obs. 082–083, by the same character, with the hidden rank of V01 obs. 017. The Sages' cover-up of V06 and their pact of silence in V07 are the institutional pattern Monica follows at the council. Remove the rank material and the staged truth has no mechanism.

## 12. Limits of this argument

### 12.1 Boundary

The argument rests on the Japanese light novels V01 to V12 and the four companions as read in the sixteen sequential readings and the master ledger at commit 60b21a7. It uses no web serial, adaptation, translation or commentary. All evidence is written Japanese prose, read through the readings. No new Japanese text is quoted; the only Japanese word used is the label 閣下, as the ledger and the brief give it.

Paratext (frontispieces, plates, character cards, afterwords and jackets) is cited only as paratext (V01 obs. 123, 124; V08 obs. 007; V09 obs. 004). No narrative fact here rests on it.

Prequel evidence is marked as retrospective. It is used to show how hindsight changes meaning, never to give a character knowledge in diegetic time that the character could not have had.

### 12.2 Uneven interiority

Interior access is uneven (checkpoint §4.2). Monica, Isaac, Louis and Cyril have extensive interior narration. Clockford, Mary, the king, Raul and most Sages are seen mostly from outside. Claims here about Clockford's motives, the king's intent and Mary's reasons are therefore claims about what the text shows of them, and about what others infer. Mary's reading of Clockford's revenge is hers (SW-C-0071).

### 12.3 Open threads

The series is unfinished at V12. The following are open, and no answer is predicted:

- the ice spirit's plan and the gate of audience (SW-C-0091);
- Mary's foreseen event;
- the selection of a seventh Sage;
- Isaac's foretold loss (V12 obs. 171, 173);
- Monica's and Cyril's unnamed feelings (SW-C-0093);
- what the plan of Hilda and her collaborators to relax the regulation of medical magical tools will bring (SW-C-0076).

### 12.4 Withheld and implied identities

The five withheld identities remain withheld here:

- the patron called 閣下 (SW-C-0039);
- the person Mary does not want to die, and the hidden small star;
- the seventh Sage;
- Hilda's collaborator (SW-C-0076);
