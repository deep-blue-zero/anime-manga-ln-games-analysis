# Silent Witch — relationship arguments

## 1. Thesis, scope and method

### 1.1 The thesis

In each of the four dyads argued here, conduct runs ahead of naming. Monica, Isaac, Louis, Cyril and Rosalie guard, coerce, rescue, teach and tend one another long before either partner can say what the other is to them. The decisive turns come when an asymmetry of knowledge is ended by one person's choice: Monica's choice to reveal herself and ask for help, Isaac's choice to tell Nero his own name, Louis's choice to speak again in his northern voice. Feeling, where the books state it, is stated on one side, or by third parties, or both. Only the prequel's couple names love to each other, and even they are nearly undone by a misreading of manner.

At the V12 boundary, the two dyads in which Monica's feeling is at issue remain unnamed on her side. Isaac's love is stated and unrecognised. Monica's distress concerning Cyril, and Cyril's reluctance concerning a marriage proposal, are shown and not named (SW-C-0093; SW-P-0069). This document argues what the evidence supports about those relations, and stops there.

The checkpoint also merges the candidate domain *affiliative or romantic ambiguity* into this document, because its evidence is dyadic (section 6). Section 6 below argues that domain across the four pairs.

### 1.3 Method

The master ledger stores each direction of a relationship separately. Its contract for relationship records keeps attraction, affection, loyalty, duty, dependence and understanding as separate variables, and stores no romance score and no assumed mutuality (ledger section 2). This document follows the same rule. For each dyad it argues:

- **initiative**: who acts first, and who answers;
- **dependence**: what each needs from the other, and what each could do without;
- **refusal and repair**: what each declines, and how breaches are mended;
- **obligation and information asymmetry**: who is bound to whom, by what, and who knows what the other does not;
- **what is unresolved** at the boundary.

Each dyad closes with its strongest counter-reading.

### 1.4 Four orders

Four orders must be kept apart.

1. **Source order** is the order in which the volumes were admitted and read in this run: V01 to V04, IV -after- (AFTER04), V05 to V09, IX -extra- (EXTRA09), V10 to V12, and then -another- upper and lower (ANOTHER-UPPER, ANOTHER-LOWER).
2. **Diegetic order** is the order of events in the story's world. The prequel's events come first: Louis's school years, then his rise to the Sages and his marriage. They are followed by EXTRA09's first chapter, the year after Monica's appointment, and then by V01.
3. **Revelation order** is the order in which the reader learns things. The reader meets Rosalie as Louis's wife in V01 and learns how they met only at the end.
4. **Participant knowledge** is what a given character knows at a given moment. It is not the same as the reader's.

Evidence from the two prequel volumes is marked *retrospective* throughout. It was learned last, though it happened first, and it revises no frozen reading (checkpoint section 4.2, item 3).

A word on names. Isaac Walker is known to everyone, including Monica, as the second prince Felix Arc Ridill until V08 discloses him (SW-C-0063). This document calls him Isaac, and says "then known as Felix" where the earlier knowledge state matters. "Ike" is the name he gives Monica on their first night out in V03. Only at V08 does the reader learn that it was the real prince's childhood name for his page (SW-C-0029). Monica in V03 hears it as a playful alias.

## 2. Monica ↔ Isaac

### 2.1 The shape of the relation

The relation passes through six phases:

| Phase | Units | Monica → Isaac | Isaac → Monica |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guard and ward | V01–V02 | Fear of proximity and rank (SW-R-0009, SW-R-0032) | Suspect, pet, game (SW-R-0010, SW-R-0031) |
| The nights out | V03–AFTER04 | Fellow "ghost" and fellow delinquent (SW-R-0045, SW-R-0075) | Keeper of Ike's memory; listener (SW-R-0046, SW-R-0076) |
| The Witch in letters | V05–V07 | Fear and suspicion beside attachment to Ike; the wish to know him (SW-R-0091, SW-R-0104, SW-R-0119) | Adoration of the Witch; wish to be relied on (SW-R-0090, SW-R-0103) |
| Disclosure | V08 | Wish to save and to know (SW-R-0131) | Cannot harm her; glad to be remembered (SW-R-0132) |
| Rescue and release | V09 | Friend; master (SW-R-0145) | Love stated in his viewpoint (SW-R-0146) |
| Master and apprentice | V10–V12 | Teacher with a standard; master's care; blind to his feeling (SW-R-0165, SW-R-0175, SW-R-0186) | Devoted apprentice; love stated to her and unrecognised (SW-R-0166, SW-R-0176, SW-R-0185) |

Through V08 the relation rests on mutual concealment. Monica hides that she is the Silent Witch and that she is guarding him. Isaac hides that he is not the prince, and that he studies magic and keeps a spirit. His devotion to the Witch he does not hide from Monica; he confides it to her without knowing who she is (V03 obs. 103). The concealments are not equal. Hers is known to the reader from the first page, his is disclosed to the reader at V08, and each learns the other's at a different moment.

### 2.2 Monica → Isaac

**Initiative.** In V01 Monica's initiative toward the prince is nil. She thanks him and avoids him (SW-R-0009). Her first question, on learning who he is, is why he was out at night (V01 obs. 048). That is attention to an anomaly, not to rank. The first time she reaches out is the first night out in V03. When Isaac, then known as Felix, invites her to treat him as a ghost who is not really anywhere, she answers that she too is a ghost tonight, and asks "Ike" to play (V03 obs. 087, 100). The ledger records this as her first outreach (SW-C-0029; SW-R-0045: "She reaches out first"). In AFTER04 she promises, as a fellow delinquent, to help with his next scheme (AFTER04 obs. 063; SW-R-0075).

From V07 her initiative changes kind. She wants to know him: the reading marks this as the first time she has wanted to know anyone (V07 obs. 179; SW-C-0059). At V08 the wish becomes a decision. She asks him whether he would sacrifice someone for a wish. He says he would (V08 obs. 177). She then speaks her father's name to him and begs him never to forget it (V08 obs. 180). Running out into the rain, she calls the act a curse (V08 obs. 181). She states what she wants: to know him, and to save him (V08 obs. 235, 241). She cannot name what he is to her (V08 obs. 240).

After V09 her initiative is a teacher's. She buys him bookshelves (V10 obs. 040). She greets him with "welcome home, Ike" for the first time (V10 obs. 160). She sets him one standard, absolute command of formulas, and gives him thirty books toward it (V10 obs. 163, 164). In V11 she realises she has set that standard without thinking about a master's responsibility, and takes Cyril, MacLaggan and Bradford as models (V11 obs. 192, 194; SW-C-0084). In V12 she corrects his paper, blames her own rule of six months' theory for his failure, and starts his practice with his own new formula (V12 obs. 111, 112).

**Dependence.** Monica's dependence on Isaac is small and specific. On the first night out she wants nothing from him, and finds that easy and lonely at once (V03 obs. 119). What she comes to need is not the prince but Ike. When the perfect prince returns, she misses the friend she played with (V04 obs. 009; SW-R-0061). In V06 she cannot reconcile the two faces, which seem to belong to different people (V06 obs. 113). This is the root of her later wish: the person she wants to know is the one behind both.

Even as master she does not lean on him for her work. At Sechen she trusts Wildianu, not Isaac, to hold Hubert off, and Isaac notices (V12 obs. 098).

**Refusal and repair.** Her first refusal of him is gentle and absolute. In V06 he says he would like her to ask him for help. She declines, and his eyes cloud (V06 obs. 114, 115; SW-R-0103). The refusal matters because it comes at the point where the series begins to argue that asking for help is not wrong (SW-C-0052). Monica learns to ask, but not from him first.

Her greatest refusal is a refusal on his behalf. Exposing him would clear her father's name and have him executed. She thinks that she cannot do it (V08 obs. 182). She names full exposure "the most correct way", keeps the prince's diary from Louis, and declines it (V08 obs. 253; SW-C-0067). At V09, when Isaac puts her hand to his throat and offers her revenge, she cannot take it. She says that she wanted to help the man living now (V09 obs. 118–120). The V09 reading frames this as her own choice, made from fear of the feeling and a wish for him to live, not as forgiveness owed (V09 reading section 8.4).

The repair is a sentence. A friend, she tells him, needs no reason to be helped (V09 obs. 126). At V08 she could not name what he is to her. Now she names it, and the name is "friend".

**Obligation and information asymmetry.** Monica's obligation to Isaac is imposed. Louis, carrying the king's order, makes her the prince's secret guard (SW-C-0005). She cannot refuse the prince's assignments (SW-R-0009). Her concealment is double: she hides her mission from the prince, and she hides her title from the admirer of the Witch. When Isaac, then known as Felix, shows her a journal paper by the Silent Witch and calls his feeling a first love, not knowing whom he is telling, she vows to keep her hood up (V03 obs. 103, 104). In V05, as the Witch, she judges his feeling admiration, not love, and resolves to stay the Witch he admires (V05 obs. 056).

She also keeps his secrets from her own superior. She reports to Louis that she met the prince at night, but not what Ike told her, because that would betray her first friend of the night (V04 obs. 067). By V07 she has also withheld her inquiry and her helpers from Louis (V07 obs. 143).

From V08 the asymmetry tips her way. She reaches the substitution by her own reasoning before the narration discloses it (V08 obs. 040–043; V08 reading section 8.1). She reads the last page of the prince's diary before Isaac does (SW-K-0110). At V09 she alone, with her conspirators, knows that the chalice reddened on Albert's blood (SW-K-0114). What she does not know is his feeling. The ledger records Isaac's love as known to Isaac and in part to his friends, and not to Monica (SW-K-0120).

### 2.3 Isaac → Monica

**Initiative.** Through AFTER04 nearly every move is his. The ledger says so twice: "Initiative from him; she consents" and "His initiative" (SW-R-0075, SW-R-0076). In V01 he drops keys to test her, and rejects the idea that she is a royal agent because she seems too incompetent (V01 obs. 069). He kisses her cheek, and she takes it for Nero (V01 obs. 073). He feeds her sweets as a reward, stakes his office on her, calls her a little squirrel and braids her hair (V01 obs. 036, 075, 088, 113). The first volume calls this taming by charm and status (V01 obs. 075). The ledger notes that the comedy of coercion registers harm, and that the kiss is unevaluated and unknown to its recipient (SW-C-0016).

On the nights out he shows her the self outside his role (SW-C-0029). He pays for a book she wants, gives her a pendant, and asks that she, at least, remember him (V03 obs. 107, 119). In V04 he would give her a flower if he were free to (V04 obs. 081). In V06 he admits that he wanted her to rely on him more, and that he overlays a remembered friend on her (V06 obs. 166, 167).

After V09 his initiative is open. He gives her a yellow-rose corsage, the dance-promise flower, and calls it a charm to make her like him (V09 obs. 143). He visits the grave that bears his own name, then goes to Southerndoor and asks to be her apprentice (V09 obs. 167, 169). He cleans her house and argues magic with her through the night until she accepts him (V09 obs. 173). In V12 he tells her plainly that he wants her to realise how much he cares for her, and puts her hand on his racing heart (V12 obs. 068, 070).

**Dependence.** Isaac's dependence on Monica has two layers, and the series distinguishes them.

The first is dependence on the Silent Witch. In the V08 memory chapters the real prince asks Isaac to find something he loves for himself (V08 obs. 140). Nothing moves him until he sees the Witch's spirit-king summoning bring down the wyverns, and recognises the one thing he can lose himself in (V08 obs. 174, 175). This explains the watcher of V01's prologue (SW-C-0006) and his rapture over her papers in V02, V03 and AFTER04 (V02 obs. 065, 088; V03 obs. 103; AFTER04 obs. 034). It is dependence on an image whose face he has never seen.

The second is dependence on Monica as the person who will remember. On the first night out he asks to be remembered (V03 obs. 119; SW-R-0046). At V08, in the tower, he is glad that the Witch knows his name: he can climb the scaffold at peace (V08 obs. 261). In V12, after Mary's prophecy, he thinks he still has Monica, who let him stay (V12 obs. 172).

The two layers meet at V09. When the Witch enters the council wearing his pendant, he calls himself a fool for not seeing it (V09 obs. 080, 081; SW-C-0008). The narration says the obsessive fire is gone (V09 obs. 125). Then he thinks that of course he could not help loving her (V09 obs. 128; SW-C-0073). EXTRA09's secret episode shows the result in his viewpoint. Monica ices a cookie of "Ike" in an apron, and to him the apprentice matters more than the prince (EXTRA09 obs. 177).

**Refusal and repair.** Isaac's refusals toward Monica are refusals of his own rules. By his own logic she should be removed once he learns that she is Venedict Rain's daughter and knows his secret. He cannot wish it (V08 obs. 199, 202). The V08 reading keeps both halves of him on record: the man who suborns Emmanuel and nearly strangles Elliott, and the man who cannot harm Monica (SW-C-0065).

At V09 he tries to repair what cannot be repaired by offering himself. He guesses her real name is Monica Rain and wishes her to hate him (V09 obs. 078). After the trial he offers her the right to revenge (V09 obs. 118). Her refusal returns the life he had offered.

In the later volumes his restraint is tested by his own means. He keeps a love potion but will not use it on anyone, because that would be no fun, and because he wants to be able to face his dead friend and Monica (V11 obs. 077). Wildianu's doubt about his scruples is left standing (V11 obs. 078; V11 reading section 8.4). In V12 he grips her wrist and pins her hand, then realises he has misjudged and retreats to a step's distance (V12 obs. 055, 068, 072, 073). The V12 reading treats his feeling as sincere and his force as real (V12 reading section 8.4).

**Obligation and information asymmetry.** Until V08 Isaac's knowledge of others outruns theirs of him. He knew Glen was a Sage's disciple from his enrolment and used him (V04 obs. 079). He suspected Monica of being an assassin (V03 obs. 118). But he did not know that she was the Witch, and he credited Louis with an arrest that was her work (V01 obs. 120; SW-C-0008). In V05 he tricks the Witch into revealing a connection to Serendia, and resolves to search there for a woman with an injured left hand (V05 obs. 129, 133, 160). He does not search the girl beside him.

At V08 Porter tells him who Venedict Rain was. He concludes that Clockford killed Monica's father to protect the substitution, and realises that she knows (V08 obs. 196–199). When Nero visits the tower and cannot remember his name, Isaac tells it (V08 obs. 259). That is his own disclosure, made to the Witch's familiar.

After V09 the obligation reverses. He says he could have died; she says many people helped to save him (V09 obs. 117). He chooses to serve her as apprentice. But one asymmetry remains. In V12 he thinks of himself as, to her, an indirect perpetrator of her father's death, and still hopes she will take his hand (V12 obs. 105). The text does not show her seeing him so.

### 2.4 What is unresolved at V12

Three things are open.

1. **His love is stated and unrecognised.** Monica has no equation for his feeling (V12 obs. 069). She proposes to measure his pulse with instruments, and later concludes that he has a cold (V12 obs. 070, 106). The ledger records her direction as a master's care, blind to his feeling (SW-R-0186), and his as love stated, with jealousy, hope and fear of loss (SW-R-0185; SW-C-0093).
2. **His position as her parent's enemy** is his own view and remains unanswered (V12 obs. 105).
3. **Mary's prophecy.** Mary tells Isaac that he was born under the star of loss, and that he will lose half the world in his eyes (V12 obs. 171, 173). In the epilogue he sees Monica ashamed before Cyril, cannot move, leaves, and whispers Arc's name (V12 obs. 209–211). The reader is made to set the scene beside the prophecy. The text does not say what the prophecy means, and this document registers no prediction (SW-P-0066).

### 2.5 The strongest counter-reading

The strongest counter-reading holds that Isaac's love is a third substitution. He first took the prince's name. He then took the Witch's magic as the one thing he could love, in place of the life the prince wished him. Now he takes Monica. On this reading, what he calls love is the latest object of a man who has always lived for someone else's image.

The counterevidence is real. He took to Monica, by his own account, because she apologised like the young prince, and because he saw the old bruises on her back (V08 obs. 201). He knows he overlays the friend on her (V06 obs. 167). In V02 a boy apologising like a royal makes him angry, and he identifies with her (V02 obs. 049). The love is stated in the moment after she saves his life, which might be gratitude (V09 obs. 128). The ledger's alternative for SW-C-0029 is "pure performance on his side".

Two things weaken this reading. First, the series orders the change. The narration ends the obsession before it states the love (V09 obs. 125, 128). The love is not part of the devotion to the dead boy's name. It comes after the diary shows him that the prince meant to live and to hear what life Ike had chosen (V09 obs. 122). Second, the later evidence directs his feeling at Monica as a person, not at the Witch. He reflects that she is not conscious of him (V12 obs. 053). He is glad to be drawn as an apprentice in an apron rather than as a prince (EXTRA09 obs. 177). He keeps her bookshelves as a place where he need not hide his treasures (V10 obs. 042).

What this document concedes is that Monica's side of the relation shows no romantic feeling the text states. The early records say so outright (SW-R-0009, SW-R-0045: "No romantic interest shown"). The V09 reading lists her feeling toward Isaac as not stated (V09 reading section 8.5). What she has is a wish to know him, a choice of his life over her father's name, the word "friend", and a teacher's care. The argument claims no more.

## 3. Monica ↔ Louis

### 3.1 The shape of the relation, and its two orders

The source order and the diegetic order of this relation run in opposite directions.

In source order, the reader meets Louis in V01 as the senior Sage who forces Monica into the mission (V01 obs. 011). His view of her arrives in pieces: a memory of the selection duel at V01's close (V01 obs. 117), his verdict that she is a heartless witch at V03's close (V03 obs. 131), his retelling of it to his own teacher in V04 (V04 obs. 050). The selection itself is told last, in ANOTHER-LOWER.

In diegetic order the relation begins at the selection. Monica, fifteen, hides in a curtain and begs Louis, who has greeted her politely, not to kill her (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 100). She then beats him (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 107–113). They become Sages together (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 156). EXTRA09's first chapter shows the year after: Louis wrapping her in a blanket and flying her to a ceremony she forgot (EXTRA09 obs. 015). Then comes V01.

So the reader watches Louis's model of Monica fail before seeing how it was formed.

### 3.2 Louis → Monica

**Initiative.** Louis's initiative toward Monica is coercive from the start. In V01 her objection meets no veto. He grips her shoulder, mentions execution, and she nods by reflex (V01 obs. 011). The first volume's narrator calls his rationale abuse (V01 obs. 010). In the selection's aftermath he makes her keep a wordless illusion of him in the parade carriage, to repay a "debt", while he flies off with Rosalie (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 163). In the year after, he drags her to ceremonies and resolves to drag her to the next dragon subjugation (EXTRA09 obs. 015, 023). The prequel's secret episode ends with him resolving to make the reclusive Witch help on a black-dragon mission (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 178).

Coercion comes mixed with investment (SW-C-0017). He once dragged her to a dragon to give her confidence, and discounts his threat of execution (V01 obs. 118). He wants her confident (SW-R-0002). He brought a mirror to her hut, and the V03 reading reads his grooming of her as arming (V03 obs. 034). He gives her a plain dress and coat as a reward (V02 obs. 009). He gives her jam as a universal seasoning (EXTRA09 obs. 022). After the selection, when she botches the rehearsal and grovels, he tells her she won by skill and must not be servile (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 159).

**His model of her.** Louis's model of Monica is the most important single fact in this dyad. In V03's secret episode he tells Casey about the Worgan black dragon. Monica feared the dragon knights more than the dragon, vomited in hiding, and coped by memorising them as numbers (V03 obs. 130). From this he concludes that she fears and hates people from the bottom of her heart, can therefore be endlessly merciless, is a heartless witch from whom no feeling should be expected, and that this is why he chose her (V03 obs. 131; SW-C-0005). In V04 he calls her a disgusting girl and a true genius (V04 obs. 050). The V04 reading points out that the word repeats, almost exactly, the uncle's word for the child who spoke only numbers (V04 reading section 6.1). The volume lets the reader hear the uncle in the colleague.

The model is half right (SW-C-0033). Monica herself accepts it when Yuan uses it against her, and then fails to apply it when Yuan threatens Cyril (V04 obs. 088, 090). In V04's aftermath Louis tells her that her humanity does not matter to him either way, and that she had not, after all, treated Ray as a number (V04 obs. 096).

The model then shifts. By the end of V04 Louis keeps his hypothesis about the prince from her because she has grown too soft (V04 obs. 120; V04 reading section 6.10). He sees that she is too attached to the second prince (V04 obs. 119). In V07 he fears that the prince has won her over (V07 obs. 134). At V08 he again keeps his plan from her because he thinks her too soft (V08 obs. 188). So Louis's model moves from heartless to soft. Both versions lead him to the same conduct: he withholds.

At V09 the narration names the error. Louis assumed the Witch would never ask anyone for help (V09 obs. 072; SW-C-0033; SW-C-0072). That assumption is the one his "heartless" model and his "soft" model share. Both treat her as someone who acts alone.

**Dependence.** Louis depends on Monica as an instrument, and says so. He chose her as an incongruous instrument because she is an unknown face, drab, an amateur, and a silent caster (V01 obs. 010; SW-R-0002). EXTRA09 adds that, in the year before the mission, his concern for her squalor was that his conqueror's state reflected on him (EXTRA09 obs. 020; SW-R-0158).

That dependence becomes something else by V11. Louis names his adult method as money, violence and connections, and says he learned the third from losing to Monica at the council (V11 obs. 183; SW-C-0043). Her V09 defeat of him changed how he works. At Cresburn the two Sages open two gates and summon the wind Spirit-King together, on a plan she proposes (V11 obs. 173, 175).

**Refusal and repair.** Louis refuses Monica belonging. In V02 he reads her attachment to the school as a risk, and reflects that Monica Norton is temporary (V02 obs. 096; SW-C-0024). The ledger notes that Monica shared that view, and that at V08 it is she who ends Monica Norton, by revealing her identity to her council and asking for their help (V08 obs. 283, 291). Louis also withholds curiosity about her. In EXTRA09 he does not know why she hides away, and is not interested (EXTRA09 obs. 020). He refuses her information: his disciple Glen, planted as a decoy (V01 obs. 116; V02 obs. 010); his hypothesis about the prince (V04 obs. 120); his plan for the garden party (V08 obs. 188). In V07 he tests her. He proposes that she seal the witnesses' memories with mind interference, and the narration says he is testing her (V07 obs. 136, 138; SW-R-0118).

His repairs are few and exact. After V04's failure he takes the blame: the error, he says, was his plan's (V04 obs. 096). In V06 he licenses her anger. She tells him she was angry, and he answers that she rarely angered because she did not care, and that fighting without anger had made her uncanny (V06 obs. 184, 185; SW-C-0051). At V09 he receives her message in his own words, that the game begins before the table, and admits his second clean defeat by her (V09 obs. 104, 137; SW-C-0048).

**Obligation and information asymmetry.** Louis holds the king's mandate and the power of choice (SW-R-0002). He knows things about the mission that Monica does not. She reads some of them anyway: she sees at a glance that the brooch is for surveillance, and judges the plan too flawed for a guard mission (V01 obs. 009, 034). Louis's own memory confirms her reading from his side (V01 obs. 119).

One knowledge gap runs in her favour from the selection onward. Monica believes she was a makeweight Sage (V01 obs. 013). Louis remembers that she shut him out in the duel (V01 obs. 117). The ledger records her belief as unsupported, and the examiners' ranking as unstated (SW-C-0004). ANOTHER-LOWER gives the duel: a wordless Spirit-King summons at the opening bell, a remote spell that fells Adolf, an economy formula cast without chanting (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 107–113). She does not know what Louis knows about her victory.

**The fellow-intake bond.** Louis uses one word for what they are. In V04 he tells his teacher that he is neither her guardian nor her friend, and asks whether that is not what fellow intakes are (V04 obs. 050). ANOTHER-LOWER, read last, shows the word's first use. In the waiting room after the selection she asks how he can believe in himself. He says that others believed in him: Shona and the women, Rutherford, Goa's diner, his teachers, Owen, Rosalie and Lionel. She names an adoptive mother and one friend. He calls her his fellow-intake, and she fears him a little less (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 160, 161; SW-C-0103; SW-R-0209). The prequel's reading calls his answer the distillation of both volumes (ANOTHER-LOWER reading section 6.5).

So the bond Louis disowns in V04 is one he had defined, in the scene of their first meeting, as the condition of being believed in. Retrospectively, his "heartless witch" verdict sits beside his own diagnosis that she trusts neither herself nor others.

### 3.3 Monica → Louis

**Initiative.** Monica's initiative toward Louis grows from bargaining to command. In V02 she asks him directly about his faction, bargains on its interest and wins a concession on Casey (V02 obs. 095; SW-R-0035). In V06 she tells him, unasked, that she was angry (V06 obs. 184). At V09 she plans the rescue around him. She will keep him from the council because he would expose everything and see through her (V09 obs. 049). The students waylay him in a forest (V09 obs. 047–071). Her message to him, carried by Neil, is his own lesson (V09 obs. 104).

In V11 her initiative becomes rescue. Louis, caught in a fog that returns people to their Minerva selves, is fighting as a middle-division delinquent. Monica restores him by casting his own Mirror Prison without a chant, a spell she calls horrible (V11 obs. 148, 150). She tells him that Adolf wants his attention, and he laughs (V11 obs. 152). She proposes the decoy gate and the twin summoning (V11 obs. 173).

**Dependence.** Monica fears Louis and trusts his competence and his word (SW-R-0001, SW-R-0035). She reports to him in writing, where her fluency does not fail (V01 obs. 115). In the year before the mission she depends on him, in practice, to be dragged to the duties she forgets (EXTRA09 obs. 015–017).

One fact of address records her relation to him. Among the Sages, only Louis is "Louis-san" to her. She knew his name before she met him, from Rutherford's stories about his idiot apprentice, while she was a research student in Rutherford's laboratory (EXTRA09 obs. 164). The name came to her through their shared teacher, before the curtain.

**Refusal and repair.** Monica's refusals of Louis are concealments, and they increase. She withholds Ike's confidences (V04 obs. 067). By V07 she has also withheld the night outing, her inquiry, Bartholomeus and Raul's knowledge (V07 obs. 143). At V08 she keeps the prince's diary from him, judging that he would publish everything and see Isaac executed (V08 obs. 253). Louis's own principle, stated at the Sages' meeting, is that whatever suits the powerful becomes true (V08 obs. 252). At V09 she uses that principle against him (V09 obs. 096; SW-C-0069).

She defeats him twice. The first defeat is the selection (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 113). The second is the forest at V09, achieved through the people she asked (SW-C-0072). Louis concedes both (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 114; V09 obs. 137).

The repairs are on her side as well. She repays the parade "debt" without complaint (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 163). She restores him in V11, though the method frightens her (V11 obs. 150).

**Obligation and information asymmetry.** Her obligations to him are imposed. She obeys under coercion (SW-R-0001). She keeps goodwill toward his family from the start (SW-R-0001). Her information advantage over him grows across the series. From V04 onward she knows things about the prince that Louis does not. At V08 she knows the substitution and the chalice motive, while Louis knows the prince is false by his own inquiry and keeps his plan from her (SW-K-0104, SW-K-0109). The ledger calls the V07 state "mutual concealment; a test" (SW-R-0118).

### 3.4 What is unresolved at V12

Little in this dyad is open as a plot thread. Its unresolved part is the asymmetry of temper. After V09 they are colleagues: he means to warn her when Adolf escapes, and at Cresburn explains why enemies target her first (V10 obs. 177; V11 obs. 171). But his model of her remains partial. In V11 he cannot imagine the Silent Witch teaching: a rationalist and perfectionist without life skills (V11 obs. 010). In V12 the Earl of Kerbeck reveals that Louis told him the Witch longs to be bullied by villains (V12 obs. 014). The comedy of his coercion is never judged. The EXTRA09 reading notes that Monica's list of frightening stories puts three of his impositions beside a poisoning, and that the collection asks for no verdict (EXTRA09 obs. 053; EXTRA09 reading section 8.4; SW-C-0016).

### 3.5 The strongest counter-reading

The strongest counter-reading holds that Louis's "investment" is self-interest throughout. His concern is his pride, his standing and his faction. The reader's sense of care is projection, and the comedy launders coercion.

The counterevidence is in his own words. He tells her that her humanity does not matter to him (V04 obs. 096). In his own viewpoint, a year before the mission, his concern is that her state reflects on him (EXTRA09 obs. 020). His coercion is called abuse by the first volume's narrator (V01 obs. 010). He tests her rather than trusts her (V07 obs. 138).

Against it stand three kinds of evidence. First, the ledger's discriminating observation for SW-C-0017 is "Louis acting against her interests", and no unit records it. Second, he models her inner life more than he admits. He sees the attachment to the prince, fears her softness and licenses her anger (V04 obs. 119; V06 obs. 185; V08 obs. 188). Third, in the prequel's waiting room he tells her where his own self-belief came from, a rare self-disclosure made to her (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 160).

The reading this document adopts is narrower than either. Louis's investment in Monica's self-respect is real because her self-respect is a condition of his own pride. He was beaten by her, and he will not have his conqueror grovel (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 159; EXTRA09 obs. 020). The two motives coincide, and the series does not need to separate them.

A second counter-reading holds that Louis was simply right about her, at least toward strangers. The ledger keeps this as an alternative to SW-C-0033. EXTRA09's first chapter supports it: the early Monica wants to be alone, is indifferent to almost everything, and thinks the dragon-cat could be shot down in an instant if it returned (EXTRA09 obs. 019, 027, 029; SW-C-0075). The answer is that the model describes a state, not a nature. The V04 reading says the scene records the model's expiry, not its falsehood about the past (V04 reading section 6.7).

## 4. Monica ↔ Cyril

### 4.1 The shape of the relation

Cyril Ashley is vice-president of the student council when Monica arrives, and her senior in council work throughout. Their relation is the slowest of the four to change in form, and the richest in address. Its core is a mirror. The V04 reading records that Cyril, like Monica, grew up with a violent man and an absent approval, and responded with effort (V04 reading section 6.8; SW-C-0037).

Three things organise the dyad:

- his suspicion of her, kept from V01 to V08 (SW-C-0009);
- his care, given unasked or under pretexts (SW-C-0078);
- feelings that, at V12, neither of them names (SW-C-0093).

### 4.2 Cyril → Monica

**Initiative.** Cyril begins in hostility. In V01 he dislikes Monica for disrespect to the prince he serves, Isaac, then known as Felix (SW-R-0012). Yet his conduct runs ahead of his attitude from the first scene. When she covers her head expecting a blow, he checks her injury (V01 obs. 057). He finds her review perfect and still dislikes her (V01 obs. 080). He punishes the true offender in a dispute where rank might have decided (V01 obs. 091).

From V02 his care is constant and always disguised. He gives her chocolate to educate her palate, for the prince's sake (V02 obs. 059). He tells her a victim does not bow, and tells her to call him Cyril (V02 obs. 046, 069). In V04 he pockets the brooch he cannot live without, ties his own ribbon to a white rose, and calls it a charm so that she will not be embarrassed (V04 obs. 032). He asks her to dance (V04 obs. 110). In V06 he tells her that asking for help when one's strength fails is never wrong (V06 obs. 182; SW-C-0052).

EXTRA09 shows the care that the main volumes hid (SW-C-0078). He leaves when tea is served, because his chill would cool it (EXTRA09 obs. 037). His monthly checks as treasurer had blocked Thornley's embezzlement, without his knowing it (EXTRA09 obs. 100). He takes heavy boxes from a girl with a hurt hand, citing his chill and efficiency; the reading does not infer whether he noticed the hand (EXTRA09 obs. 137). After V09 the care keeps its form: the old roll call shouted into a giant pea, a birthday wreath that never reached her, a request that Raul look after his junior (V11 obs. 057, 061, 158).

**Dependence.** Cyril's dependence is on recognition, and at first it runs through the prince. Each early record notes that he seeks the prince's approval in parallel (SW-R-0012, SW-R-0028, SW-R-0060). V04 explains why. His mother, Myra, feared that her clever son would become his violent father and withheld the praise he worked for (V04 obs. 077). V10 names the father, Wayne: a drinker who boasted of noble blood. Someone told Cyril he was just like his father, and he fears night waters (V10 obs. 136; SW-C-0037).

At V08 that dependence breaks. Cyril learns that the prince he served was never the prince, and calls himself a clown (V08 obs. 227). Monica shouts him down (V08 obs. 228). He recovers by deciding that he was chosen for himself, not his title, and keeps the council (V08 obs. 229). After this, the recognition he wants is Monica's. In V12 he settles on three aims: his adoptive father, his birth mother, and to be respected by Monica as her senior (V12 obs. 208).

**Refusal and repair.** Cyril's refusals are refusals to let Monica abase herself. She prostrates herself to apologise, and he tells her a victim does not bow (V02 obs. 046). His repairs are apologies, often for faults that were not his (SW-R-0028). He apologises for the rope, though she let him bear the blame (V02 obs. 098; SW-R-0027). In EXTRA09 he tells her that her charge of sloppy accounts was right, and apologises for shouting (EXTRA09 obs. 102). At V09 he kneels and apologises to the magic count, and says he had half guessed from her hand (V09 obs. 008).

That apology is the turning point of his direction, and he undoes its formality at once. Monica weeps that Cyril is speaking to her politely and she hates it. He shouts at "Norton treasurer" as before, and she prefers it (V09 obs. 009, 011).

**Obligation and information asymmetry.** Cyril suspected Monica for most of the first arc and never verified it (SW-C-0009). In V01 he recalls her small calloused hand and her remark about the cold, and is about to check her glove when the prince interrupts (V01 obs. 111). In V04 his attention returns to her pen-callused hand and the unexplained magical ice (V04 obs. 108). In V06, before he faints, he hears Hubert call the small figure the Silent Witch (V06 obs. 152). In V07 he remembers the small Witch shielding her left hand (V07 obs. 062). He never acts on any of it. Monica's reveal at V08 answers his question before he asks it (SW-P-0004, SW-P-0031).

His knowledge of his own heart is the other gap. In V04 the narration gives him only a tightness in the chest, which he explains as concern for the council's dignity (V04 obs. 028; V04 reading section 6.3). In V12 he asks Aurelia, Mary's niece, to decline a marriage proposal, cannot promise to marry her later, and feels he has deceived his own heart without knowing how (V12 obs. 166, 167).

### 4.3 Monica → Cyril

**Initiative.** Monica's first act of care for anyone at Serendia is for Cyril. She repairs his brooch so that he will not come to see his talent as a curse, as she saw hers (V01 obs. 101). Her first refusal of a superior is also for him: she refuses to recant a statement, for his sake (V02 obs. 043). In V04, when his mother asks whether Cyril bullies quiet girls, Monica, who cannot ask for help for herself, testifies fluently for him. Myra decides to write and invite him home (V04 obs. 074, 078). Cyril learns only that a woman with a mole came (V04 obs. 075).

At V08 she shouts down his self-contempt (V08 obs. 228). At V09 she asks him for a white-rose charm for the day of the council, and he gives it, telling her to give everything she has (V09 obs. 039, 054). Half a year later she asks him to call her Monica (V09 obs. 147). In V10 she asks to share his responsibility for the ice spirit Romaria, whom she had sealed in his brooch unasked (V10 obs. 138). In V12, to spare him embarrassment, she volunteers to play the villainess in Raul's plot (V12 obs. 170).

**Dependence.** Monica's dependence on Cyril is on his ordinary self. Already in V06 she says she hates it when Cyril is deferential to her (V06 obs. 048). At V09 she weeps when he is (V09 obs. 009). In the V09 coda she opens a window to feel his cold when he is due (V09 obs. 177). In V10 and V11 she takes him as a model twice: the one who scolds what is wrong, and a dependable adult (V10 obs. 103; V11 obs. 194).

**Refusal and repair.** Her failures toward Cyril are concealments and small lies. She lets him bear the blame for the rope (SW-R-0027). Her identity is hidden from him until V08 (SW-R-0105). She claims to be cat-tongued, though she drinks hot coffee every morning, so that he need not leave when tea is served (EXTRA09 obs. 037). That lie is an act of care disguised exactly as his are.

Her one open fault-finding is his accounts. In EXTRA09 she charges them with sloppiness. He apologises, and she insists on sharing the work as the current treasurer (EXTRA09 obs. 102, 103).

**Obligation and information asymmetry.** Monica knows things about Cyril's life that he does not know she knows. She knows what she told his mother; he knows only that a woman with a mole came (V04 obs. 075). She sealed Romaria into his brooch unasked, and only in V10 asks whether that differs from Darrell's art (V10 reading section 6.4). Cyril answers that the responsibility is his (V10 obs. 138).

She also misreads his gift. She does not know that a flower in the giver's colours is a request for the first dance (V04 obs. 018). She takes Cyril's rose as a folk charm that lends her his composure, and thanks him for it (V04 obs. 071, 111). Every other reader reads it correctly: Lana, Isaac (then known as Felix) and Myra (V04 obs. 099, 105; V04 reading section 6.3). The V04 reading records the rose as an unspoken invitation that Monica receives as protection, and records no romantic interest on her side.

### 4.4 Naming and address

This dyad is argued most clearly through what each calls the other. The EXTRA09 reading notes that the name a person is called by registers the relationship (EXTRA09 reading section 6.5).

| Moment | Address | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| V02 | Cyril tells Monica to call him Cyril. | V02 obs. 069 |
| V04 | Monica unmasks a false Cyril because he uses the wrong form of her name, not her office title. | V04 reading section 6.12 |
| V09, the reveal | Cyril's polite speech to the magic count makes her weep; he returns to "Norton treasurer". | V09 obs. 008–011 |
| V09, the coda | She asks him to call her Monica. | V09 obs. 147 |
| EXTRA09, after the council | He calls her Monica in their work; it pleasantly unsettles her, and this is her secret. | EXTRA09 obs. 165 |

The pattern is that both partners prefer the forms that keep the original relation. She wants his scolding and his "Norton treasurer". He wants to be her senior. Her own name, when he uses it, unsettles her in a way she keeps secret. The V11 slip shows the other side: a trust in an old Minerva friend, Barney Jones (Bernie), that she has never given Cyril, and that he resents in himself (V11 reading section 6.4). The V11 reading records this as relationship evidence, not as a stated feeling.

### 4.5 What is unresolved at V12

V12 is the volume in which Monica's feeling concerning Cyril first becomes visible, and it names none of it (SW-C-0093; SW-R-0187, SW-R-0188).

- At Mary's party Monica hears that Cyril is in marriage talks with Aurelia, Mary's niece. She is shocked without knowing why, leaves and cries (V12 obs. 161). Her chest creaks, and she pretends otherwise (V12 obs. 162).
- Cyril asks Aurelia to decline. He cannot promise to marry her later, and feels he deceived his own heart without knowing how (V12 obs. 166, 167). His adoptive father had told him to choose a woman he wants to share his life with (V12 obs. 165).
- In the epilogue her borrowed dress gives way as she begins to congratulate him. He covers her with his coat, red to the ears, and she says she is embarrassed (V12 obs. 209, 210).

The V12 reading sets this beside Monica's unselfconsciousness before Isaac. She fainted naked in the bath and was rescued by him, and does not see why it matters (V12 obs. 053; V12 reading section 6.5). The contrast is the volume's, and the epilogue's title, the reason for shame, names it. What the feelings will become is open (SW-P-0069). This document registers no prediction.

### 4.6 The strongest counter-reading

The strongest counter-reading holds that this is a senior-junior bond and a mirror, and that reading romance into it repeats a third party's projection. Bridget, recalling her own first love, takes Monica to be in love with Cyril without knowing it (V09 obs. 040; SW-R-0148). The V09 reading records that as Bridget's belief, and no more (V09 reading section 6.6). On this reading, Monica's tears at V12 are the distress of someone who fears losing her senior and her place, as she wept at V09 when he was polite to her (V09 obs. 009). Her shame at V12 is the shame of a woman whose dress has failed in public.

The counter-reading has strong support. The text names no feeling on either side (SW-C-0093). Cyril's stated aim is respect as her senior (V12 obs. 208). Her early dependence on him is explicitly on his ordinary scolding (V06 obs. 048).

Its weakness is the contrast the volume draws. Shame before Cyril is set against its absence before Isaac, in the same volume (V12 obs. 053, 070, 209, 210). Her shock comes at news of a marriage, not of any change in their work, and she does not know why she cries (V12 obs. 161). On Cyril's side, he asks that a match be declined, though his father leaves him free to choose, and he cannot read his own reluctance (V12 obs. 165–167). EXTRA09 adds Elliott's observation that Cyril, reading the yellow rose in Monica's hair as the prince's kindness, smiles at his own bouquet (EXTRA09 obs. 161).

The argument therefore claims an asymmetry of response and an unnamed reluctance. It claims no feeling the text does not state.

## 5. Louis ↔ Rosalie, with Birdland

### 5.1 The shape of the relation, and its orders

This is the only dyad of the four whose ending the reader knows before its beginning.

**In revelation order**, Rosalie enters the series in V01 as the physician who examines Monica and finds signs of malnutrition (V01 obs. 016). The same volume brings word that Louis is to be a father (V01 obs. 094). In AFTER04 he flies to the capital on his own mana for "the woman he fell for", crashes into a fountain, and is scolded and nursed by his fiancée, a doctor who treats him as a patient. Her birthday gift is the handkerchief she dried him with, and her hairpin was his student gift (AFTER04 obs. 023–028; SW-R-0088). In V08 she gives birth (V08 obs. 189). In V10 Louis moves his wife Rosalie and daughter Leonora to safety (V10 obs. 177). In V11 the reader learns that at the Sage selection Adolf hurt Rosalie, then Louis's fiancée, and that she is the daughter of the Sage Birdland Verde (V11 obs. 013, 145).

**In diegetic order**, the relation begins in Minerva's library, soon after Louis arrives there at eleven (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 061), and ends, within the boundary, with a marriage half a year after his investiture and a visit to the grave of the woman who told him to make a family (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 165, 172).

The prequel's evidence is retrospective. It was read after V12 and it revises nothing frozen. Its function for this dyad is to supply the how of an outcome already known.

### 5.2 Louis → Rosalie

**Initiative.** Louis's initiative toward Rosalie is the engine of his career. In his first months at Minerva his goal becomes top marks, so that she will call him amazing (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 066). When she tells him she has only twenty mana, he tells her to watch: tomorrow he will prove one can win with little (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 122). He gives her a flower hairpin and apologises for scorning the waterworks her father built (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 187). On her last night at Minerva he comes to her window and says he loves her (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 205).

Every later move follows from that promise (SW-R-0195). He passes the senior-mage examination to be proper for the woman he means to marry (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 207). He flies to confront Birdland and swears to become a Sage (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 210). He spends a week learning upper-class speech and manners from Lionel (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 211). Even the epithet he earns on the way, the Barrier Mage, is taken in his teacher Majer's honour (SW-C-0098). The narration's last word on the upper volume's run is that he resolved to become a Sage only to marry Rosalie (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 212; SW-C-0095).

The upper reading notes the comic reversal. The Sage's ambition that the prologue showed as a vow is a marriage condition (ANOTHER-UPPER reading section 6.1). The upper reading also rejects the reading of his ambition as a public vocation (ANOTHER-UPPER reading section 8.2).

**Dependence.** What Louis needs from Rosalie is stated in one sentence. Walking beside Lionel after her news, Louis remembers Shona's instruction to make a family someday, and finds that what he wants is Rosalie's hands tending him (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 202). The near goals of his boyhood become a long one because of a person (SW-C-0095; SW-C-0096). The AFTER04 scene, read early in source order, already shows it: the doctor's care of a man who has flown himself to collapse (AFTER04 obs. 024, 025).

The lower volume adds the reverse dependence. Louis says that, having nothing to carry, he will carry part of what Rosalie and Lionel bear: for her, her father's hopes, by becoming a Sage (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 150; SW-C-0104). He needs a burden, and she has one.

**Refusal and repair.** His first breach is contempt for her father's work, and the repair is the hairpin (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 187). It follows from Rutherford's judgment, overheard, that Louis is a giant blind to others' goodwill (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 183, 184; SW-C-0097).

His second breach is the refinement itself. The manners he learned to meet Birdland's terms make him a stranger to her. At Birdland's house he greets them as a perfect gentleman, and she freezes (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 056). The narration says he did not notice how shaken she was by his manner and his talk of the Sages (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 059). To a guild executive who pities him for becoming engaged to become a Sage, he answers with sarcasm that he would not be engaged were she not the Waterworks Mage's daughter (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 066). The Corps, knowing his ambition and his violence, read the engagement as a means to the seat (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 061). Nate states the reading the book itself endorses: his conduct produced the rumours, and his transformation looked "fishy" (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 126; SW-C-0100).

The repair is a return of voice. In the carriage, after unsealing her memory and explaining her father's conditions, he drops the polish and tells her in his northern accent that she was never a trouble to him (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 151). The lower reading's summary is exact: the refinement remains as a means, but the book places love in what it was meant to hide (ANOTHER-LOWER reading section 6.2).

**Obligation and information asymmetry.** Louis is bound by a promise he made, and by conditions imposed by a third party. He does not explain the conditions to Rosalie until he unseals her memory (ANOTHER-LOWER reading section 3, chapter ten). He does not know that Adolf started the rumour that sent him to Birdland (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 215). He does not know why his letters went unanswered. The lower reading's knowledge table records that Birdland, drunk, had ordered all letters between them destroyed, and that this is known to Birdland and the steward, not to Louis or Rosalie (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 060; ANOTHER-LOWER reading section 7.11).

### 5.3 Rosalie → Louis

**Initiative.** Rosalie's initiative is less loud than Louis's and more decisive at each turn. She is not afraid of him on their first meeting (ANOTHER-UPPER reading section 3). She follows his tracks into the restricted forest to stop him. She had never crossed that rope for herself, fearing after-effects, trouble to others and her father's disgrace, and she crosses it for him (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 115). The cost is mana poisoning (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 117). She cleans his face after his fight with Adolf and warns that trouble may stop them going up together (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 124).

Her second crossing is her decision to leave magic for medicine (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 197, 199). At her window she answers his declaration with her own (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 205). At medical school she refuses suitors who come because her father is a Sage, and tells a classmate that her type is an unconventional, rather troublesome delinquent (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 214).

The lower volume shows that her choice of career was also a choice of him. She graduated early, worked in a hospital, studied mana illness, and volunteered for the Corps to tend Louis (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 052). She refuses Adolf, saying she will be engaged to Louis and that Louis has no interest in the Sages (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 054).

Her refusal of the engagement is also hers. Believing it was for the seat, she ends it (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 116). Her last act in the plot protects someone else: she claims that the fall caused her amnesia, and so spares Glen official censure (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 162).

**Dependence.** Rosalie frames her own turn as made possible by Louis. She thanks him for showing her that little mana is not everything (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 199). That is a dependence on an example, not on the man. Her deeper bond in the upper volume is to her father's hopes. She defers to him (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 153), and Lionel reads her choice of medicine as a choice to bear those hopes (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 201).

In the lower volume, amnesia isolates the core of her attachment. With her memory sealed, she recalls only a northern-accented boy she loved (ANOTHER-LOWER reading section 3, chapters five and six). She prefers the delinquent to the gentleman, and says so twice (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 153, 164).

**Refusal and repair.** Rosalie's refusal of Louis is a misreading, and it is built from real evidence. She and Glen overheard his remark to the guild executive, and she wept: she knew how she was thought of (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 122). Lynn, Louis's spirit, tells her that he would do anything to be a Sage, and she concludes the engagement was for the seat (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 095). The repair comes from his side, in the carriage. Hers is to accept it. She says she fell for a bold delinquent, and that both waited for years (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 153).

**Obligation and information asymmetry.** Rosalie knows less than Louis about the terms of her own marriage. She does not know her father's three conditions until Louis explains them (ANOTHER-LOWER reading section 3, chapter ten). She does not know, at the boundary, that her father destroyed their letters (ANOTHER-LOWER reading section 7.11). She does not know her father's regret, which he told the Thunder Mage and never told her (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 154; SW-R-0196).

### 5.4 Birdland, the third party

Birdland Verde sets the terms of the dyad without telling either partner what he feels.

The upper volume gives his regret before his daughter's wound. He tells the oldest Sage that he pressed his ideals on his low-mana daughter too harshly (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 154). Later, her memory gives his one dismissive sentence at her first lesson, after which he never watched her practise again (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 204). Then he loses his temper at Louis. He demands a man of refinement with a house in the capital who can become a Sage, and cries that Rosalie will marry a man like a prince (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 210; SW-R-0197). The upper reading records that neither father nor daughter learns the other's view on the page, and that the relation is unresolved (ANOTHER-UPPER reading section 8.4).

The rumour that provoked his outburst was Adolf's (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 215; SW-C-0095). So the dyad's central resolve, the vow to become a Sage, is the product of a rival's spite, and Louis never learns it.

In the lower volume Birdland reverses himself. He writes to recommend Louis as his successor and to propose the engagement (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 051; SW-R-0206). In private, he says that as a father he would acknowledge Louis (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 145). He calls himself the world's most foolish father (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 144). He attends the wedding (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 165). The character list adds, as paratext only, that he regrets neglecting his family and swore lifelong abstinence after the engagement (ANOTHER-LOWER reading section 7.8).

### 5.5 Glen's mercy

Rosalie's fall was Adolf's crime. Pushed by Clockford's faction, he threw her from the barracks roof by remote magic and coerced and poisoned Owen. Louis solves it by controlling information and attending to a friend's habits, Owen's coffee where the poison was in tea, and then maims him (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 084, 136, 137, 140; SW-C-0102). Adolf is the rival Clockford had meant to place among the Sages (SW-C-0099).

The lower volume also carries the dyad's misreading into a third person. Glen, Louis's apprentice, has an unspoken feeling for Rosalie that only Louis notices (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 115; SW-R-0208). He overheard Louis's remark and saw her weep. When she fell from the roof, he believed it a suicide attempt and sealed her memories with an incomplete, quasi-forbidden spell, to prevent another (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 118, 122, 123). He then shielded his master by silence (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 124; SW-C-0101).

Louis tells him that the order is the other way round: he wants to be a Sage in order to marry her (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 125). The lower reading judges Glen's act wrong but forgivable. Louis names the risk, that she might never have woken, and restricts Glen's magic in private (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 120, 162; ANOTHER-LOWER reading section 8.4).

Glen's mercy matters for the dyad because it is built from Louis's own words, misheard. The refinement that estranged Rosalie also misled Glen, who loved her without knowing it.

### 5.6 Marriage and the later family

Louis and Rosalie marry half a year after the investiture (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 165). On their honeymoon they visit his abandoned birthplace. He leaves marmalade on Shona's grave and tells her he has come to boast that he has a family (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 172). The narration closes the two volumes as the story of a boy who became a Sage and gained a family (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 173; SW-C-0104). Shona's distinction between strangers huddling together and a family organised the upper volume, and Louis's wish for Rosalie's care turned it toward a family to be made (SW-C-0096).

The numbered track, read first, holds the later family in small domestic facts:

- Louis once put jam on his wife's cooking and was not spoken to for a week (EXTRA09 obs. 022).
- Newly married, with a child due, he fends off an ancient tool's love talk by an hour of praise of his wife, his former classmate (EXTRA09 obs. 093).
- His daughter is born (V08 obs. 189). He cannot hold her without her crying (V09 obs. 045).
- He moves his wife and daughter to safety when Adolf escapes (V10 obs. 177), and leaves them under Owen's protection (V11 reading section 3).
- He pads an injury report, and Rosalie finds out (V11 obs. 213).

### 5.7 What is unresolved at V12

Within the boundary this dyad is resolved. They are married and have a daughter. Three things remain unknown to Louis or Rosalie, though known to the reader:

- that Birdland destroyed their letters (ANOTHER-LOWER reading section 7.11);
- that Adolf's rumour provoked Birdland's terms (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 215);
- Birdland's regret, which he never tells Rosalie on the page (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 154).

The relation between father and daughter is not reconciled on the page (ANOTHER-UPPER reading section 8.4).

### 5.8 The strongest counter-reading

The strongest counter-reading holds that Rosalie is the prize of a story about Louis's rise. On this reading, she is the reward and the motive, her own story is subordinated, and her agency consists of waiting and refusing. The afterword says the original short work was narrated almost wholly by Rosalie, which might suggest the prequel took her story from her. That statement is paratext and can only be cited as such (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 217).

The narrative evidence cuts against the counter-reading. The upper reading calls Rosalie's story the book's second line, told from both sides (ANOTHER-UPPER reading section 6.5). Her two crossings, into the forest and into medicine, are her choices, not his (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 115, 199). She chooses the Corps to tend him (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 052). She ends the engagement on her own reading of the evidence (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 116). She saves Glen by her statement (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 162). The lower volume shows her view of the refined Louis before Louis learns it (ANOTHER-LOWER reading section 7.12).

A second counter-reading concerns violence. Louis maims Adolf, and the book frames the act as a promise kept and vengeance for Rosalie and Owen, without condemning it (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 140; ANOTHER-LOWER reading section 8.3). One could argue that the book makes violence a proof of love. The evidence supports the narrower statement that the book treats this violence comically and without consequence, and lets Lionel's concern for the selection stand unanswered except by Louis's priorities (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 148).

## 6. Affiliation and romance across the four dyads

The checkpoint merged *affiliative or romantic ambiguity* into this document because its evidence is dyadic (checkpoint section 6). Across the four pairs, the series handles that ambiguity in four consistent ways.

### 6.1 Feeling is stated asymmetrically

The four dyads distribute stated feeling unevenly:

| Dyad | Stated by A | Stated by B | Stated to each other |
|---|---|---|---|
| Louis ↔ Rosalie | Yes (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 205) | Yes (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 205) | Yes |
| Isaac → Monica | Yes, in his viewpoint (V09 obs. 128) | — | His care declared to her, unrecognised (V12 obs. 068, 069) |
| Monica → Isaac | Not stated (V09 reading section 8.5) | — | "Friend" (V09 obs. 126) |
| Monica ↔ Cyril | Not stated (SW-C-0093) | Not stated (SW-C-0093) | No |

Only the prequel's couple names love mutually. Every other expression is either one-sided or unnamed. The series does not supply the missing side by inference, and neither does this argument.

### 6.2 Third parties name what the principals do not

Where principals are silent, onlookers speak.

- Lana reads Cyril's rose and says he will be pleased (V04 obs. 099).
- Isaac, then known as Felix, privately calls Cyril unfair for using the word "charm" (V04 obs. 081, 105).
- Bridget takes Monica to be in love with Cyril without knowing it (V09 obs. 040).
- Bartholomeus believes the prince and the Witch are secret lovers, and that the prince renounced the throne for her (V10 obs. 044).
- Elliott sees Cyril smile at his own bouquet (EXTRA09 obs. 161).
- Isabel and Agatha see Isaac pin Monica's wrist and delight in the novel trope (V12 obs. 056).
- Louis knows that Glen loves Rosalie without knowing it himself (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 115).
- Lionel swears to Louis's love since school (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 125).

Some of these readers are right, some wrong, and some unverifiable. The readings record each as that character's belief (for example SW-R-0148). The device lets the books acknowledge a possible feeling without committing the principal to it.

### 6.3 The word "charm" carries the flower code

The series runs the flower custom through one word. At Serendia a flower in the giver's colours is a request for the first dance (V04 obs. 018). Cyril gives Monica a white rose and calls it a charm, so that she will not be embarrassed, and she takes the word literally (V04 obs. 032, 071). At V09 she asks him for a white-rose charm for the council, and he gives it (V09 obs. 039, 054). Isaac, at graduation, gives her a yellow-rose corsage, the dance-promise flower, and calls it a charm to make her like him (V09 obs. 143).

Isaac's use of the word says openly what Cyril's conceals. Monica reads neither: the V09 reading records that she calls Isaac a friend and does not read his corsage (V09 reading section 6.6). The ledger accordingly records Cyril's direction as concealed invitation (SW-R-0060) and Isaac's as love unspoken to her at V09 (SW-R-0146). Her own direction, in both, shows no reading of the code.

### 6.4 Monica reads feeling through what she can measure

Monica's difficulty with feeling is not presented as coldness. It is presented as a lack of method. In V05 she judges Isaac's feeling for the Witch admiration, not love (V05 obs. 056). In V12 she fears a feeling she cannot calculate, proposes to measure his pulse, and diagnoses a cold (V12 obs. 069, 070, 106). Her own feeling concerning Cyril reaches her as a chest that creaks and tears she cannot explain (V12 obs. 161, 162).

Her numbers began as a child's refuge from her uncle (SW-C-0002), and Louis read them as heartlessness (SW-C-0033). The books show them failing first with people she knows, with Cyril in V04 (V04 obs. 090, 096), and now with feeling itself.

V12's subplot gives a contrasting model. Frieda tells Ray Albright that she does not love him yet, but thinks she can come to like him (V12 obs. 205). Feeling is acknowledged as not yet, and stated as such. No such statement is available to Monica at the boundary.

## 7. Counterarguments and counterevidence

Each dyad's strongest counter-reading is given in its own section (2.5, 3.5, 4.6, 5.8). Four counterarguments apply to the whole document.

### 7.1 "The dyads are instruments of Monica's development"

On this view, the relationships serve one protagonist's arc from chosen solitude to asking for help, and arguing each direction separately overstates their independence. The evidence for it is strong: the V09 rescue is collective, and every element is supplied by people Monica asked (SW-C-0072). Louis's error is defined by her conduct (V09 obs. 072).

The answer is that three of the four dyads change on the other partner's side, by causes that are not Monica's. Isaac's release comes from the diary, not from her (V09 obs. 122–125). Cyril's recovery at V08 is his own decision about being chosen for himself (V08 obs. 229). Louis's adult method changes after her defeat of him, but his account of it is his own (V11 obs. 183). The fourth dyad, Louis and Rosalie, does not involve Monica at all.

### 7.2 "Retrospective romance"

On this view, the argument reads V12's unnamed feelings back into earlier scenes, such as the V04 rose, that the earlier readings recorded as protection and charm. The concern is real, and the V04 reading's record of no romantic interest on Monica's side stands (V04 reading section 6.3). This document describes the V04 rose as that reading does, and does not convert earlier scenes into evidence of a feeling first shown at V12.

### 7.3 "Paratext supplies what the narrative withholds"

Cards and afterwords make relationship statements, such as the lower volume's note that Rosalie studied on without minding when she could not reach Louis (ANOTHER-LOWER reading section 7.8). No claim here rests on a card, an afterword or a jacket.

### 7.4 "The four dyads mirror one another"

One could read this document's thesis as a claim of symmetry: each dyad a concealment ended by disclosure. That overstates it. The Louis and Rosalie dyad turns on a misreading of manner, not on a secret identity (SW-C-0100). The Monica and Cyril dyad turns on suspicion never verified, and its central concealment is of feeling, not of fact (SW-C-0009, SW-C-0093). The V11 reading also warns against a mirror reading of Louis and Adolf, recording asymmetry instead (V11 reading section 8.2).

The thesis claims less than symmetry. It claims that, in each dyad, conduct precedes naming, and that the ending of an information asymmetry is what changes the relation. The kinds of asymmetry differ.
