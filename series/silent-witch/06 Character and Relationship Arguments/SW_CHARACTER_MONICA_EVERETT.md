# Silent Witch — Monica Everett

## 1. Thesis

Monica Everett's silence and her genius are one mechanism, and the series never takes that mechanism apart. What changes is where it points.

As a child Monica survived her uncle by retreating into her father's numbers, until she could no longer hear speech or see people as people (SW-C-0001, SW-C-0002, SW-C-0003). That retreat became her method. It let her cast magic without a chant, see the world as measurement, and hold people at a distance by turning them into figures. At the start of V01 the method is a closed room: a mountain hut, a familiar who is not human, and calculations begged from colleagues.

The series then moves her through a sequence of state changes. Each has a mechanism that the text shows. In almost every case the mechanism is the same: fear meets a sentence someone else has given her, and the sentence is carried out the way she once carried out her father's formulas. Elliott's code of duty, Lana's advice to learn by hand, Cyril's refusal of the word "cannot", Bartholomeus's line that a child may rely on adults, Louis's lesson that the game begins before one sits at the table: Monica takes each in and runs it. By V10 she is passing such sentences on to others (SW-C-0084). Her growth is not a cure of fear. It is a widening of the set of people whose words she will admit into the system that kept her alive.

Three points follow, and the rest of this monograph argues them.

1. **The numbers are re-tasked, not abandoned.** They move from refuge (V01), to chosen weapon and then limit (V04), to evidence (V05), to the measure of a master's standard (V10). At V12 they meet a feeling for which, in Monica's own reckoning, there is no equation (SW-C-0035; V12 obs. 069).
2. **Her self-report lags behind her conduct, almost always.** She says she cannot be attached to people, cannot be proud, owes her seat to pity, and is heartless. Her acts contradict each claim, usually long before she revises it (SW-C-0023, SW-S-0008, SW-C-0004, SW-C-0033).
3. **The decisive turn is not courage in combat but a request.** From V06 to V09 the series asks whether she may ask for help. Her answer, at the end of V08, dissolves the false self the mission required and makes her rescue of Isaac a collective act (SW-C-0052, SW-C-0059, SW-C-0067, SW-C-0072).

The series is unfinished at V12. Monica's shock, tears and shame concerning Cyril, and her blindness to Isaac's stated love, are left unnamed by the text, and this monograph leaves them unnamed (SW-C-0093; SW-P-0069).

## 2. Sources, citation and the three orders

This argument binds to the frozen snapshot at commit 60b21a7. It cites the master ledger's claim, record and question IDs and the readings' observation numbers in unit-prefixed form ("V04 obs. 096"). It quotes no Japanese.

Three orders must be kept apart, because Monica's history was disclosed backwards.

- **Diegetic order** is the order in which events happen in her life.
- **Source and reading order** is V01–V04, IV -after- (AFTER04), V05–V09, IX -extra- (EXTRA09), V10–V12, then the prequel -another- upper and lower. The prequel was read last but takes place before V01.
- **Revelation order** is the order in which the reader learns each fact. Monica's childhood arrives first as a dream (V01), then as delirium (V02), then through another person's memory (V03), and only then in a sober narrated prologue (V04).

The bookkeeping matters for knowledge. In V01 Monica does not know who the man in her dream is; the reader does not either (V01 obs. 037). ## 3. The mechanism: silence and number as one system

### 3.1 Three accounts of the silence

V01's narrator gives a comic origin. Monica was so afraid of speaking before strangers that she failed her Minerva practicals, and instead of working on the fear she learned to cast without speaking (V01 obs. 007). The narrator calls this effort that shot off at an angle. The account is true as far as it goes. The public explanation, instant computation, is not false either (SW-C-0001).

The same volume plants a darker root without explaining it. Monica dreams of a drunken man, a bottle and a rule that one must not say anything unnecessary, and retreats in the dream into a counting song (V01 obs. 037). She covers her head when a hand is raised (V01 obs. 057). Her familiar Nero, in the form of a grown man, terrifies her though she knows it is Nero (V01 obs. 040). She knows that in a school ruled by rank the higher-ranked speaker's word becomes the truth (V01 obs. 058). And she will not let someone else be falsely accused, though she will let herself be (V01 obs. 059).

V02 and V03 add the middle. The man is her paternal uncle, who beat her for reciting numbers and for defending her father, and forbade her to speak of it in public (V02 obs. 045; SW-S-0026). At Minerva her one friend, Bernie Jones, offered to teach her to shorten her chant; she removed it entirely (V03 obs. 005–007). V04 then tells the whole sequence soberly: after the execution she survived her uncle by repeating her father's formulas, until she lost the ability to understand speech and answered only in numbers. Hilda Everett, her father's former assistant, brought her back by continuing the number series she was reciting and calling her by name (V04 obs. 001–008; SW-S-0065).

So the silence has three layers, each disclosed later than the last: an examination, a friend's plan, and a year in which speaking was punished. Silence and mastery are one mechanism because the casting that made her famous is the same avoidance that kept her alive (SW-C-0001).

### 3.2 Numbers as shelter, sight and weapon

The narration states the mechanism once, in V01. The more pain or fear Monica meets, the deeper she sinks into numbers, because while she thinks about them she does not have to feel (V01 obs. 071). That passage is the only one in the series written in the grammar of a small child. It is also the plainest statement of SW-C-0002.

The same faculty is her way of seeing. She remembers faces as ratios (V01 obs. 028, 042), reconstructs a pot from its shards (V01 obs. 054), and still recognises an escaped prisoner late in V10 because she remembers faces by measurement (V10 obs. 057). She does not know that this is unusual (V01 obs. 054). Her father's saying that the world is made of numbers is the hinge. It was his, it was her shelter, and at V03 it turns out to be the first line of his burned book (V03 obs. 105; SW-C-0018).

Louis supplies the third use. In his memory of the black-dragon campaign, she vomited from fear of the dragon knights, not of the dragon, then memorised them as numbers, and destroyed the dragon alone (V03 obs. 129–131). Turning people into numbers makes them bearable. Louis draws the wrong conclusion from this, as section 5 shows. But the technique is real, and in V04 she chooses it in combat. Faced with an imperial agent wearing Cyril's face, she says she can attack him by thinking of him as a block of numbers (V04 obs. 085).

## 4. Development and state transitions

This section follows Monica's states in source order, which is also, for the main story, diegetic order. For each transition it names the mechanism the text shows.

### 4.1 Baseline: chosen solitude and the "heartless witch"

At V01's opening Monica is in her seventeenth year, a Sage and a count-rank peer for two years, underfed and alone in a hut with Nero (SW-S-0001). The ledger's baseline is "recluse by preference", with the rival reading that the preference is fear. She believes she was a makeweight Sage, chosen to fill a vacancy (V01 obs. 013).

The companion volumes add earlier scenes of this state, and they must be read as retrospective. EXTRA09's first chapter shows the newly appointed Sage refusing a servant because she would rather be alone, living on dried fish in water, and deciding that the strange cat that follows her can always be shot dead in an instant if it turns out to be a dragon (EXTRA09 obs. 019, 027). Nero's own judgement in that scene is that she is not defenceless but indifferent to almost everything (EXTRA09 obs. 029). She clears a river so that people will stop coming up the mountain (EXTRA09 obs. 033). The story's title calls her heartless (SW-S-0189; SW-C-0075).

The hut has a cause beyond preference. In V03's prologue Bernie, envious of her chantless casting, tells the fifteen-year-old Sage that she is not his friend and should shut herself away alone in a mountain hut (V03 obs. 009). EXTRA09 adds that on arriving at the hut she wondered whether he would now forgive her (EXTRA09 obs. 049; SW-C-0027). The solitude is therefore partly a sentence carried out. It is the first of many sentences from others that Monica obeys.

The prequel's view of her at the selection, also retrospective, is consistent. She hides in a curtain and begs Louis not to kill her, demands a handicap or she will win, then defeats him with a wordless spirit-king summoning, a remote spell and an economy formula, and calls the remote spell only a lot of calculation (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 100, 101, 107–113; SW-C-0103). After winning she is servile. Louis tells her she won by skill and must not cringe, and when she asks how he can believe in himself he answers that others believed in him. She can name only an adoptive mother and one friend (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 159, 160).

Louis's verdict, first stated in V03's secret episode, is that she fears and hates people from the bottom of her heart and is therefore a warped, heartless witch from whom no feeling can be expected; that is why he chose her (V03 obs. 131; SW-C-0024). The verdict is the series' opening model of Monica. The numbered volumes test it and find it half right (SW-C-0033).

### 4.2 Monica Norton at Serendia (V01–V02): speech acts and duty

The mission is coerced. Louis grips her shoulder and she nods before she can stop herself; the narration repeats the nod as something regretted (V01 obs. 011). Her consent is narrated as something her body does. Yet even here she reads Louis correctly: she sees that his brooch is a device for watching the prince, not guarding him, and suspects the plan has too many holes to be what it claims (V01 obs. 009, 034).

V01's development can be traced as a series of speech acts rather than a change of mood (V01 section 6.13; SW-S-0005). She fails to introduce herself. She drills a thanks on Nero. She completes a thanks to Lana and asks to be taught a braid. She proves on a blackboard that the prince she knows as Felix was out at night. She lies that she slipped, to keep Lana from a false accusation. She tells Cyril she cannot be proud. She accuses Thornley to his face. She speaks in plain form to Lana. And she answers Cyril's use of her office title as loudly as she can (V01 obs. 030, 041, 045, 049, 059, 090, 104, 112, 114).

The mechanism is stated in the ledger: fear, then recalled duty or care, then a speech act forced out, then recognition or rebuff (SW-S-0005). The reading's caution is important. The change is in how many situations she will attempt, not a cure (V01 section 7.1).

V02 adds duty as a concept. Elliott Howard, who despises her as a commoner, tells her a noble is bound to protect commoners. Monica derives from this that her imposed roles carry duties, first as treasurer and then as Sage (V02 obs. 024, 033, 083; SW-C-0020, SW-S-0027). Duty is the bridge from compulsion to choice. A role forced on her becomes a reason she can give herself. At the climax she acts alone as the Silent Witch, speaking aloud the one chant she cannot omit, in which she names herself (V02 obs. 084, 085; SW-S-0033). She then bargains with Louis to spare Casey, using his own faction's interest. It is the first negotiation she wins (V02 obs. 095; SW-S-0032).

Two warnings are planted. Louis tells her Monica Norton is a temporary person she must not get attached to, and she tells herself the same (V02 obs. 096; SW-C-0024). And she says aloud, to Nero, that she cannot expect anything of anyone; only numbers are safe (V02 obs. 057; SW-S-0031). Both statements are contradicted by her conduct in nearly every other chapter (SW-C-0023).

### 4.3 Renunciation and wanting (V03)

V03 is built as a pair of confessions under masks (V03 section 6). Its central transition for Monica is the renunciation of Bernie's approval. Bernie accuses her of feigning weakness to get close to the prince. She hears in it how he always saw her and blames herself. Then Lana is insulted, and Monica speaks. First she defends Lana: if he speaks ill of her friend she will not forgive him. Only then does she speak for herself: she relied on him, wanted to be someone he could rely on, and will no longer want his praise (V03 obs. 042–045; SW-S-0046). The order is the mechanism. Defending another person is the condition under which her voice works, and once begun it carries her into her own case.

Three other changes follow. She asks Felix for something for herself, an end to the nickname "squirrel", and gets her name instead (V03 obs. 025). She acts from a motive she calls selfish: that the bereaved be able to mourn, as she could not mourn her father (V03 obs. 077, 088; SW-C-0020, SW-C-0028). And when Felix, disguised as the ghost Ike, asks whether she disliked playing with him, she answers that she too is a ghost tonight and asks him to play on. It is the first time in three volumes that she holds out her hand first (V03 obs. 100; SW-S-0047; SW-C-0029).

The same volume shows what has not changed. She still fears touch and tall men (V03 obs. 013, 087, 117). She uses number to make people bearable (V03 obs. 096). She keeps her friends in the dark (SW-S-0049).

### 4.4 Numbers fail (V04)

V04 prints Louis's verdict on its opening plate, has an antagonist repeat it to her face, and has Monica agree with it (V04 obs. 050, 088, 124). Then the plot breaks it. When the agent threatens the real Cyril, Monica computes that the threat is a bluff and still cannot act, because the world of numbers will not hold Cyril (V04 obs. 090). She is poisoned. Afterwards she tells Louis that thinking only of numbers had been easy, but with Cyril's life at stake she could not calculate anything (V04 obs. 096). She concludes that her father's saying must no longer be an excuse or an escape (V04 obs. 097; SW-S-0066; SW-C-0035).

This is the series' clearest transition with a stated mechanism. The weapon fails because the people of the school have become people to her. Louis, who has said twice that he does not care about her humanity, points out that she had not treated Ray as a number, which is why Ray was there to save her (V04 section 6.7).

V04 also shows the limit on asking. She will not use her ally's signal for her own problems, and collapses rather than ask for help with a tub of ice; she accepts help only when it is offered (V04 obs. 031, 042, 043, 107; SW-S-0067). She initiates speech to a lost stranger for the first time, relying on Cyril's rose as a charm for courage (V04 obs. 073, 071). And she chooses, knowing it deceives her friends, to remain Monica Norton until the mission ends, expecting to return to the hut (V04 obs. 098, 110, 115; SW-S-0068).

### 4.5 Ordinary firsts (AFTER04)

The first companion is a casebook of ordinary time, and its developments are small and exact (SW-S-0085–0088). Monica keeps the first keepsake she has made, Cyril's rose, dried and jarred as "a charm that makes me stronger" (AFTER04 obs. 066). She has her first birthday celebrations since Minerva and asks for a gift plainly (AFTER04 obs. 146, 147). She writes her first season's card, to Hilda, and thanks Nero (AFTER04 obs. 160). Asked why she studies magic, she says she drifted into it so as not to be a burden to her adoptive mother, and finds herself wishing she could be proud of something (AFTER04 obs. 079; SW-S-0086).

The locked drawer is the volume's inventory. It holds her father's coffee pot and book, and gifts from Lana, Felix, Casey, Cyril and Isabelle. Every gift comes from a person she is deceiving or will leave (AFTER04 section 6.3).

### 4.6 Numbers turned outward (V05)

V05 puts her in public as the Silent Witch, unable to speak before people who know her as Monica Norton. She writes in the earth and on paper, and refuses every meal to keep her veil (V05 obs. 049, 059, 125; SW-S-0103). She vows at a winter market to keep doing better among people, then loses a negotiation to Felix because she came unprepared and shrugged at his question, the tell Louis had named (V05 obs. 004, 021, 130). She grieves, and is sent back downstairs by the sight of Glen practising (V05 obs. 136, 137; SW-S-0104).

The volume's transition is the outward turn of the numbers. In a cursed dragon's memory she measured the body of the man who had killed the dragon's young. She recognises him by those measurements and speaks aloud, as a Sage, to accuse him (V05 obs. 139, 142). The narration says that numbers, once her refuge, are not that now, and she speaks her father's saying as a declaration (V05 obs. 144; SW-S-0105). The man, Peter Sams, cries out her father's name before he dies. Hilda then tells her that her father was framed under a great noble's pressure, and will not name the noble to protect her (V05 obs. 147, 166, 167). Monica overrules the protective silence. She will prove her father innocent, even at the cost of her seat, because she is no longer a powerless child (V05 obs. 149, 170, 180; SW-S-0106). Her purpose now has two parts: the mission, and her father.

### 4.7 Anger named; help refused (V06)

V06 names a feeling she had not named before. At Duke Clockford's table she is seized by a heat she cannot identify and lets the white butterflies of a mind-interference formula surround him as a warning (V06 obs. 064). Later, standing over the injured Glen, she recognises the same heat as anger, tells Hubert Dee she is angry, and strikes (V06 obs. 154, 155). Louis then tells her she seldom grew angry because she did not care about others, and that fighting without anger made her uncanny (V06 obs. 184, 185). The ledger records the change from unnamed loss of control to a recognised feeling with a cause (SW-C-0051, SW-S-0121). The volume does not judge whether the anger is right, and Monica herself asks whether a Sage should cast in anger (V06 section 6.1).

The volume also poses, five times, whether one may ask for help (SW-C-0052; SW-F-0036). Felix says he would like her to ask him; she refuses (V06 obs. 114, 115). Carried off by Hubert, she cannot say the word "help" to Cyril (V06 obs. 130). Cyril tells an apologising ice spirit that asking for help is never wrong (V06 obs. 182). Bartholomeus refuses payment and tells her a child may rely on adults (V06 obs. 207). The volume shows three people asking her to accept help and does not yet show her doing it (SW-S-0125).

### 4.8 Asking and knowing (V07)

V07 opens with the request V06 withheld. Attacked by Lynn under the spirit-binding whistle, Monica hesitates to rely on a civilian, then asks Bartholomeus for help, and his plan works (V07 obs. 005; SW-S-0139). The reading notes that her first request is made to someone she had not counted as a fighter.

Her second first follows. Hearing Bridget's account of the prince's change ten years before, she finds that she wants to know why Ike sometimes looks as if he has given something up. The narration marks this as the first time she has wanted to know another person and the first time she has stepped forward of her own accord (V07 obs. 179; SW-S-0142). She decides on the infiltration of Clockford's house herself (V07 obs. 157). She inquires into love because Bridget's devotion should not be slighted, and reaches a provisional view that it differs for everyone and is cherished as she cherishes numbers (V07 obs. 186, 190). The ledger joins these as SW-C-0059: asking and knowing, each by her own decision.

### 4.9 From bystander to actor (V08)

V08 discloses that the present Felix is the page Isaac Walker, remade after the real prince's death (SW-C-0063). Monica reaches the substitution by her own reasoning before the narration confirms it, though her account errs in detail (V08 obs. 040–043; V08 section 8.1).

Her transitions in V08 are the largest in the series (SW-S-0157–0160). She threatens the young emperor Leonhardt with death and binds herself to a bargain to avert war (V08 obs. 061, 064; SW-C-0066). She shows the bookseller Porter, her father's friend and a self-described bystander, that she is the Silent Witch: she could do nothing when her father was burned, but can do more now (V08 obs. 084). She speaks her father's name to Felix and begs him never to forget it, then calls this a curse (V08 obs. 180, 181).

Then she makes the choice the ledger records as rescue over justice (SW-C-0067). Exposing the substitution would clear her father and have Isaac executed. She cannot do it (V08 obs. 182). She keeps the real prince's diary from Louis, who would publish everything (V08 obs. 253). Her motive is in her own words: she wants to save Isaac, and to know him (V08 obs. 235, 241). She cannot name what he is to her (V08 obs. 240).

The mechanism of the final transition is a refusal of sacrifice. Isaac's spirit Wildianu offers to destroy himself to free Isaac, and Monica says no one is to be sacrificed (V08 obs. 282). From that she reasons to the opposite of her old rule: she will rely on others even if it costs her life as Monica Norton (V08 obs. 283). She says goodbye to Lana as if for the last time (V08 obs. 274). Then she gathers the council, puts on the Sage's robe, names herself, and asks for their help (V08 obs. 291; SW-S-0158). The warning that Monica Norton is temporary, shared since V02, ends by her own act (SW-C-0024, revised at V08 by SW-CR-0292).

### 4.10 The Supreme Council (V09)

V09 makes the rescue collective. Every element of the plan comes from someone she asked: a workshop, materials, a tool built by Hubert and Bartholomeus, petitions, coaching, a team to hold Louis off, a trap, borrowed blood, a performed cure, and the king's authority (V09 obs. 022–037, 048–072, 097, 113, 116; SW-C-0072). The narration names Louis's error: he assumed the Witch would never ask anyone for help (V09 obs. 072; SW-C-0033). Louis's own lesson, that the game begins before one sits at the table, comes back to him as her message (V09 obs. 104; SW-C-0048).

The outcome is a staged truth: a real tool, demonstrated honestly, given borrowed blood; a dead culprit; a performed cure; and royal ratification (SW-C-0069). Monica leaves her father's name uncleared, and the narration from her viewpoint now states that Clockford arranged his execution (SW-C-0045, SW-C-0064). She ends the council by warning Clockford without words that he should stay silent if he does not want ruin (V09 obs. 099). The Silent Witch, who could not speak, now imposes silence (SW-C-0074).

Afterwards Isaac offers her his throat and her right to revenge. She cannot take it. She tells him she gave up clearing her father because she wanted to save the man living now, and that one needs no reason to help a friend (V09 obs. 118–120, 126; SW-S-0177).

The coda records the dispositional change (SW-S-0178). She asks Cyril to call her Monica (V09 obs. 147). She decides she may go anywhere, thanks the villagers who kept her hut, and moves to Southerndoor near Lana's new company (V09 obs. 148–151). The village girl Annie says she has changed and become brighter (V09 obs. 150). She accepts Isaac as her apprentice after a night of arguing magic (V09 obs. 173). The volume ends with her saying she is glad to have people who will drink coffee with her (V09 obs. 182). The afterword, cited only as paratext, says that ending was planned from the start (SW-F-0050).

EXTRA09 frames its school-year stories with scenes from this later life so that the distance can be measured (SW-C-0075). The Monica who once could not see why anyone would cook for her watches her apprentice bake and ices a cookie of him in an apron (EXTRA09 obs. 177). The narration says plainly that kindness once confused and shamed her, and that now she can take it in with joy (EXTRA09 obs. 171).

### 4.11 The master (V10–V12)

V10 opens a new arc with Monica as master. Its question is what makes her an adult. She was a Sage at fifteen with adult work, and thinks her heart did not keep pace (V10 obs. 049, 153). She is repeatedly taken for a child (V10 section 6.3).

Her answer is transmission (SW-C-0084). Confronting an eleven-year-old, Rick, she first speaks in cold anger, then searches for the words an adult once gave her and repeats Bartholomeus's line that a child may rely on adults (V10 obs. 099, 101, 102). She names her models as Lana, who reaches out, Cyril, who scolds what is wrong, and Bartholomeus (V10 obs. 103). The volume's theme word is "whatever", the indifference of Rick's brother Darrell. Monica's cry that something is not "whatever" gives Rick the courage to defy him (V10 obs. 113, 114; SW-C-0079). The reading measures the distance: the girl who once thought nothing outside numbers mattered now fights for what does (V10 section 6.2).

Toward Isaac she buys bookshelves, greets him with "welcome home" for the first time, and sets him a single standard: to be a mage absolutely sure of his formulas, with thirty books toward it (V10 obs. 040, 160, 163). Isaac reads it as her first expectation of him.

V11 revises the transition. After the Cresburn crisis Monica sees that she has set Isaac a standard without thinking about a master's responsibility, and takes three models: Cyril as a dependable adult, MacLaggan as a master who understands his pupil, and Bradford as a master his pupil is proud of (V11 obs. 142, 192–194; SW-CR-0634; SW-S-0212). She tries to scold Isaac, is disarmed, and judges herself immature (V11 obs. 206, 207). V12 shows the study applied. She corrects Isaac's paper, sees that her own rule of six months' theory had delayed his practice, and starts him on his own formula (V12 obs. 111, 112). She binds Hubert's rings and recommends the orphan Norman to Minerva under Hubert's tutoring (V12 obs. 107; SW-S-0225).

### 4.12 Unnamed feelings (V12)

V12 widens her emotional range in a direction no earlier volume had. Before Isaac she remains unselfconscious. When he puts her hand on his racing heart, she proposes to measure it with instruments; later she diagnoses his symptoms as a cold (V12 obs. 070, 106). The reading records that she fears a feeling she cannot calculate (V12 obs. 069).

Before Cyril she responds differently. Told he is in marriage talks, she is shocked without knowing why, leaves, cries, and feels her chest creak while pretending otherwise (V12 obs. 161, 162). She volunteers to play a villainess to spare him embarrassment (V12 obs. 170). When her borrowed dress gives way while she is offering him congratulations, and he covers her, she says she is embarrassed (V12 obs. 209, 210). Isaac watches and leaves (V12 obs. 211).

The text gives these feelings no name, and neither does Monica (SW-C-0093; SW-S-0224; SW-R-0187). Bridget believed at V09 that Monica loves Cyril without knowing it; that is Bridget's belief (V09 obs. 040; SW-R-0148). The state at the boundary is a shame that the earlier Monica, who did not see why fainting naked in a bath mattered, did not feel (V12 obs. 053). Something has entered the system that it cannot yet process. What it becomes is open (SW-P-0069).

## 5. Motive and self-report against conduct

Monica has more interior access than any other figure in the series. That makes her self-reports unusually testable, because the narration often shows the act and the thought side by side.

**"I cannot be attached to people."** At V01 she reflects that she has attachments only to equations (V01 obs. 066). In the same volume she keeps a dead father's coffee pot as her only treasure, tastes nuts because a stranger gathered them, keeps braids because a classmate made them, and risks exposure to treat a boy who insulted her (V01 obs. 026; V01 section 6.7). At V02 she says aloud that she cannot expect anything of anyone (V02 obs. 057), then treasures Lana's notes in her locked drawer (V02 obs. 058, 071). The ledger's rival reading is that the statement is accurate for romance only (SW-S-0008; SW-C-0023). That reading holds through V11. V12 strains it, without overturning it, because the text names nothing.

**"I cannot be proud."** She tells Cyril this at V01, and the narration gives the history: a friend who broke with her when she was praised (V01 obs. 090). The sentence does not move in V01 (V01 section 7.1). At AFTER04 it becomes a wish to be proud of something (AFTER04 obs. 079). At EXTRA09 she hopes to finish the mission with her head held high, and names her fear of a future self who would disappoint her (EXTRA09 obs. 061, 063; SW-S-0191). Conduct had already outrun the sentence: she claims her place as a council officer three times in AFTER04 (AFTER04 section 6.3).

**"I was a makeweight."** She believes the king chose her to fill a gap (V01 obs. 013). Louis remembers that she beat him completely at the selection (V01 obs. 117; SW-C-0004), and the prequel shows it, retrospectively (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 107–113). Her report to Louis in V01 shows the same habit in writing: her most important acts reduced to "various things happened" (V01 obs. 115).

**"I am the heartless witch."** She agrees with the imperial agent at V04 and resolves to use it to protect people dear to her (V04 obs. 088). The plot shows the agreement half false (V04 section 8.1). The motive behind the "heartless" acts is already care.

**"It is my ego."** Twice Monica gives a self-accusing name to an act of mercy. At V03 she calls her wish that the bereaved be able to mourn selfish, as selfish as the tool's (V03 obs. 088, 097). At V07 she calls her rescue of the dying ice spirit Romaria her ego (V07 obs. 126). At V10 she sets that unasked sealing beside Darrell's use of people as contract stones and asks whether her own act differs (V10 obs. 135; SW-C-0061, SW-C-0080). The self-report is harsher than the conduct. It is also not empty: she did bind a spirit without consent, and the volume lets her question it.

**Revenge.** At V09 the reading records two motives for refusing Isaac's offer of revenge: fear of the feeling and a wish for him to live (V09 obs. 119, 120; V09 section 8.4). The volume frames the refusal as her choice, not as forgiveness owed. Her stated reason, that she wanted to save the man living now, is consistent with every act from V08 on.

The pattern across these cases is stable. Monica's conduct changes first; her account of herself follows, sometimes by several volumes; and in several cases the narration, or another character, supplies the revision she cannot.

## 6. Relationships by recipient

Monica's behaviour is strongly conditioned by who is before her. The ledger stores each direction separately (SW-R). This section argues Monica's side; the relationship arguments treat four dyads in both directions.

**Nero.** Nero is the one being to whom Monica speaks without breaking from the first page, and her explanation is the simplest sentence she gives in V01: he is not human (V01 obs. 039). The relation began, retrospectively, with indifference. She ignored the creature until he insisted on his name, and she pulled a bone from a dragon's mouth because dragons frighten her less than people (EXTRA09 obs. 030; V05 obs. 123, 124; SW-R-0159). By V02 his human form no longer frightens her, though she only notices when he points it out (V02 obs. 006; SW-S-0029). He keeps one thing from her, Felix's kiss in V01 (SW-K-0010; SW-R-0004). She misses him when he sleeps through the cold (V05 obs. 168). Nero is the baseline: the comfort of a listener outside human judgement.

**Lana.** Lana's friendship is the series' longest, and it rests on a shared wound. Both are called boring and both are out of place (V01 obs. 031, 046; SW-C-0015, SW-C-0026). Lana's kindness arrives in the grammar of irritation, and Monica learns from her a knowledge that is not her father's: some things are learned by hand, not by number (V01 obs. 047). Monica's first independent request is to Lana (V02; SW-R-0023), and her first full declaration in her own defence begins as a defence of Lana (V03 obs. 045). The mission strains the bond: Lana offers her a future after graduation that she cannot accept (V06 obs. 124), and she says goodbye as if for the last time (V08 obs. 274). At the reveal Lana is angry and remains her friend (V09 obs. 010). In Southerndoor Monica thanks her cleanly (EXTRA09 obs. 171). Lana is also a model Monica names when she becomes a teacher (V10 obs. 103).

**Cyril.** Cyril is the mirror the series sets beside her: a violent drinking father, withheld praise from a mother, and striving for recognition (SW-C-0037). Monica's first act of care in V01 is for him. She repairs his brooch anonymously because she does not want him to think of his constitution as a curse, an explicit projection of her own view of her talent (V01 obs. 101). His fairness gives her the first counterexample to the rule that rank makes truth (V01 obs. 091; SW-S-0007). She testifies fluently for him to his mother though she cannot ask anything for herself (V04 obs. 074). As the Witch she cannot bear his formal courtesy (V06 obs. 048), and at the reveal she weeps when he kneels to her, until he scolds her as before (V09 obs. 009, 011). She asks him to call her Monica, and his use of her name unsettles her pleasantly, a secret she keeps (V09 obs. 147; EXTRA09 obs. 165). His care is mostly hidden or given under pretexts (SW-C-0078). Her fear of Yuan's threat to him is what breaks her numbers in V04. Her response to him at V12 is the one the text leaves unnamed (SW-R-0187).

**Felix, then Isaac.** Before V08 Monica guards a prince she knows as Felix and fears his rank and his touch (SW-R-0009). As Ike he becomes the first person to whom she holds out her hand (V03 obs. 100). She keeps Ike's confidences from Louis, her first withholding of operational information (V04 obs. 067; SW-S-0069; V04 section 8.5). She wants to know him (V07 obs. 179). After V08 she saves Isaac at the cost of her father's name, calls him a friend, and becomes his master (SW-S-0177; SW-R-0145, 0165). Her side contains care, interest, a standard, and no recognition of his love (SW-R-0186). Isaac's side is stated: love, devotion, jealousy (SW-R-0146, 0185). V12 shows physical pressure from him, a gripped wrist and a hand pinned to a wall, and records her fear of a feeling she cannot calculate (V12 obs. 055, 068, 069; V12 section 8.4). She answers with instruments. The asymmetry is the relationship's state at the boundary.

**Louis.** Monica fears Louis and trusts his competence (SW-R-0001). She reads his motives correctly from the first scene (V01 obs. 009). She bargains with him on his own terms (V02 obs. 095). From V07 she withholds four matters from him (V07 obs. 143; SW-R-0118), and at V09 she plans to keep him away from the council because he would expose everything (V09 section 6.3). The prequel shows the root of their bond, retrospectively: he answers her distrust with his reliance on those who believed in him, and calls her his fellow-intake; she fears him a little less (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 160, 161; SW-R-0209). At V11 she restores him under the Minerva fog by casting his own Mirror Prison without a chant, and flies with him to open a twin summoning (V11 obs. 148, 175). Louis is the only Sage she calls by his given name, because she knew him first from Rutherford's stories of his apprentice (EXTRA09 obs. 164).

**Hilda.** Hilda is the person who brought Monica's speech back by continuing her number series and calling her by name (V04 obs. 006). The child said numbers were not frightening (EXTRA09 obs. 003). Monica's first season's card goes to Hilda (AFTER04 obs. 160). Hilda protects her by silence, withholding the noble's name, and Monica decides against that protection (V05 obs. 167, 170; V05 section 8.4). At V08 Hilda gives her the designs of the black chalice, which Monica uses to save the man whose existence cost her father (V08 obs. 264–268). Their homecomings mark the change: a stammered "I'm home" in V05, and an evening of school talk in EXTRA09 (V05 obs. 164; EXTRA09 obs. 009, 010).

**Bernie Jones.** Bernie was her helper and, the prologue shows from his side, needed her to stay helpless (V03 obs. 004). (Later readings write the name as Barney.) His curse sent her to the hut (V03 obs. 009). Monica's renunciation of his approval is a key transition (V03 obs. 045). He then hides her mission, demands lifelong gratitude, and later says he wanted to be her equal rival; she answers with formal thanks (V03 obs. 056; V04 obs. 037, 038). He lends the workshop for the council plan and manages the chalice's rights as Earl of Amberd (V09 obs. 022–023; V11 obs. 225; SW-C-0027). At V11 the Minerva fog makes her call Cyril by Bernie's name, and she reflects that her failure to understand others broke things with him; she resolves at least to know that such feelings exist (V11 obs. 111, 144; SW-S-0213).

**Others.** Isabelle Norton protects her by staged cruelty, and Monica receives it with grateful bewilderment and worries for Isabelle's reputation (SW-R-0005, 0006; SW-C-0025). Glen Dudley's friendship is the one bond in the school that rests on no secret on his side, and Monica helps him unseen as the Witch because a friend was trying (V04 obs. 068; V05 obs. 060). Raul Roseburg fears that people recoil from him; Monica's unafraid hand answers that fear (V11 obs. 178; SW-C-0087; SW-R-0184). With Ray Albright she shares the recluse's condition, and at V12 she takes the villain's part so that he will not be one, from her own knowledge of how easily one curses oneself (SW-R-0068; V12 obs. 195, 198). Bartholomeus, a second-rate craftsman with no pride, is the adult whose sentence she later passes on (V06 obs. 207; V10 obs. 102). Casey Grove, the friend who used her, is accused, saved and later reconciled with her (SW-R-0025; SW-R-0144).

The conditioning is consistent. Monica is most fluent and bold with non-humans, in defence of others, and when correcting a fact. She is weakest when a body is close to hers or when she must speak for herself. The recipients who change her most are those who give her a sentence she can use.

## 7. Ordinary behaviour and humour

Much of Monica's characterisation is comic, and the comedy is exact about her habits.

She neglects her body. Rosalie's examination in V01 finds signs of malnutrition, against which Monica's claim to be healthy carries no weight (V01 obs. 016). She lives on dried fish dropped in water (EXTRA09 obs. 019). She cannot name the ranks of the peerage, and has mislaid the patent that makes her a peer (V01 obs. 017).

She measures everything. She proves a prince's night outing from the golden ratio of his body (V01 obs. 049). She dances well by calculating the reflections of fifty-eight facets on Felix's brooch (V02 synopsis). She brings a laboratory balance to Casey's baking and measures room temperatures to soften butter (EXTRA09 obs. 051, 105). Measuring is how she joins in, and her friends give her the part of the task she can do (EXTRA09 section 6.3).

Her genius and her blind spot belong to one mind. Pushed down a staircase, she fills the steps with an invisible barrier to hide her magic, turning the stairs into a slope, and rolls the length of it howling (V01 obs. 056). After Felix kisses her cheek she reports in solemn confidence that she has found the prince's great secret: he has paw pads (V01 obs. 076). Drunk on mulled wine by mistake, she curls up like a cat (V03 synopsis). In V10 she and Wildianu hide chantless casting behind muttered prime numbers and tea brands, and a thirteen-year-old sees through them (V10 obs. 075, 094, 095). She tries to make a scary face to scold her apprentice (V11 obs. 206). Her villainess fails until she drops before Ray with a stammered aristocratic apology and a shower of calming petals (V12 obs. 175, 199). She proposes to measure Isaac's racing heart with instruments (V12 obs. 070).

Her keepsakes are a quiet counterweight: her father's coffee pot, the locked drawer, Lana's first ribbon (V01 obs. 026; AFTER04 section 6.3; EXTRA09 obs. 169). The coffee pot links the first chapter to the last. She served coffee from it as her gift at the tea party, calling herself the most out of place of all (V02; SW-S-0030), and the first arc ends on coffee with friends (V09 obs. 182).

The comedy has a moral edge that the ledger tracks. The comedy of coercion, chiefly Louis's, mostly registers harm on Monica even when the tone is light (SW-C-0016). EXTRA09 lets her list Louis's impositions beside a poisoning as her real frightening stories (EXTRA09 obs. 053). One case is not registered: Felix's kiss in V01, given while she was absorbed and just struck, is unknown to her and unevaluated by the text (V01 obs. 073; SW-K-0010; SW-C-0016). This monograph does not supply the evaluation the text withholds, but records that Monica never learns of it within the boundary.

## 8. Written speech: stammer, address and silence

All evidence is written Japanese prose. Nothing here concerns voice or delivery.

**Stammer.** Monica's speech breaks in printed ways: repeated syllables, long internal ellipses, and bitten final syllables when she forces words out (V01 section 6.13; V02 section 6.17). The narrator's whole comment on one such moment in V01 is a single word noting that she bit it (V01 obs. 045). The marks persist at moments of courage: offering to guide a stranger in V04 (V04 section 6.12), reading the treasurer's report to the whole school in V08 (V08 obs. 009), asking Cyril to call her Monica in V09 (V09 obs. 147). The stammer is not removed by growth. It accompanies the acts that growth makes possible.

**Conditions of fluency.** The conditions are stable across the series. She speaks without breaking to a non-human listener; when explaining a calculation, a proof or a ledger; when angry at a botched formula; when defending another person or correcting a fact; and in writing, where sentences are whole but the order of events is lost (V01 section 6.13; V02 section 6.17). Her confrontation with Thornley in V01 has no stammer at all, because she is facing a broken formula (V01 obs. 104, 105). Her declaration to Bernie begins with Lana (V03 obs. 045). At V11 she answers a hostile question by filling a board, then invites the audience to take notes if they like, unaware it sounds like a rebuke (V11 obs. 201, 203).

**Register.** She uses polite forms to almost every human, plain forms to Nero throughout, and to Lana by V01's epilogue (V01 obs. 112). Her self-deprecating phrases recur. She apologises as a habit, even for being poisoned (V02 section 7.1; SW-S-0125). A small but marked change comes in V08, when she thanks Bartholomeus instead of apologising (V08 obs. 271).

**Address.** Forms of address track her relationships and her names.

- Cyril moves from hostile second-person forms to her office title, "Treasurer Norton", which she likes because it makes her a member of the council (V01 obs. 114; V04 section 6.12). She uses that very title to unmask the impostor who calls her something else (V04 section 6.12). She addresses Cyril with the respectful honorific "sama" and at the reveal weeps at his formal speech to her (V09 obs. 009).
- Felix calls her "squirrel"; her first personal wish is that he stop, and he calls her Monica (V03 obs. 025). She names him Ike only with effort (V03 section 6.18).
- She calls only Louis by name among the Sages (EXTRA09 obs. 164).
- At V09 she asks to be called Monica, neither Norton nor the Witch (V09 obs. 147). Isaac had guessed her birth name, Monica Rain (V09 obs. 078). The card's statement that Rain was her surname is paratext (V03 obs. 133).

**Silence.** Silence is her epithet, her limitation and, later, her instrument. At school any magic she shows will identify her, because she can only cast silently (V01 obs. 033). The one chant she cannot omit, the spirit-king summoning, names her as the Silent Witch, so every use of it is a self-disclosure (V02 obs. 085; V10 obs. 109). As the Witch in public she writes rather than speaks (V05 obs. 059). There are things she cannot say: the word for help, in V06 (V06 obs. 130). And at V09 silence becomes a weapon, imposed on Clockford without a word (V09 obs. 099; SW-C-0074). The series ends its first arc on silence broken and silence imposed, and both belong to her.

## 9. Past harm and coping, without diagnosis

This section describes what the text shows and the behaviour it records. It uses no clinical labels.

**What is established, and how.** Monica's father was executed when she was ten. Her paternal uncle then beat and starved her, shut her in a winter barn, and forbade her to defend her father, for about a year until Hilda took her (SW-C-0003; SW-C-0032). The evidence comes in stages of rising reliability: a dream (V01 obs. 037), drug-induced delirium (V02 obs. 044, 045), memory set off by a crowd (V03 obs. 076), a sober narrated prologue (V04 obs. 001–008), and third-party sight of the scars on her back by Felix and by Lana (V03 obs. 121; EXTRA09 obs. 106). At V12 she tells Isaac herself that the uncle who beat her was a tall man, and that humans frightened her more than the black dragon (V12 obs. 054; SW-CR-0636, SW-CR-0637). The V02 reading kept open the rival reading that the delirium distorts (V02 section 8.1). The V04 prologue and the later corroboration close most of that gap.

There are other harms. At Minerva she was bullied, her hair cut and her things hidden, and she still writes her name on nothing and knows to look in the bin (V03 obs. 004; V02 section 7.1). She watched her father burned, and crowds bring the memory back (V03 obs. 076; V06 obs. 138).

**Coping behaviours the text records.**

- *Numerical absorption.* Pain or fear sends her into numbers; it reduces awareness and can be productive or comic (SW-S-0003; SW-C-0002). It fails when people she knows are threatened (V04 obs. 090).
- *Silence and minimal speech.* Her rule from the dream, to say nothing unnecessary and endure, recurs almost word for word on the V01 staircase (V01 section 6.6).
- *Self-blame and apology.* She apologises for being harmed and makes herself the cause rather than accuse another (V01 obs. 059; V02 section 7.1).
- *Self-exile.* She enters the hut wondering whether Bernie will forgive her, which gives the solitude a note of self-punishment (EXTRA09 obs. 049; EXTRA09 section 6.4).
- *Bracing against touch.* She covers her head when a hand is raised and stiffens at touch (V01 obs. 057; SW-S-0049).

**What changes and what does not.** Her fear of Nero's human form ends at V02 (SW-S-0029). At V11, dropped from the sky, she clings to Raul's hand without recoiling, though he holds his ancestor's feared book (V11 obs. 178). Her fear of tall men persists to V12 and is tied there to her uncle (V12 obs. 054). Crowds remain hard at V10 (V10 section 7.1).

The most important change is in the function of the defence, not its existence. At V04 she refuses her father's saying as an excuse (V04 obs. 097). At V05 she turns it into a declaration (V05 obs. 144). Later volumes keep showing calculation as her tool, with the ledger strengthening SW-C-0035 through V12. At V12 she meets a feeling with measurement, and the reading records this as the same habit operating on new material (SW-CR-0669; V12 obs. 070, 106).

**The father's case as a coping structure.** Her father's name is a wound she turns into a task. She vows to prove his innocence (V05 obs. 180), binds Felix to remember the name (V08 obs. 180), and then gives up clearing it to save Isaac (V09 obs. 120). The black chalice built from his designs is accepted as a medical tool, and at V10 the law on forbidden arts is changed in part because of it. Monica thinks her father's research has changed the country (SW-C-0083; V10 synopsis). The name stays uncleared; the work survives. The text lets that stand as her settlement.

## 10. Contradictions and tensions

Several tensions run through Monica and are not resolved by the boundary.

**Timidity and coercive power.** The girl who cannot introduce herself also unmakes a mind-destroying spell and seals its caster in a dream (V01 obs. 106), surrounds a duke with the butterflies of mind interference as a warning (V06 obs. 064), threatens an emperor's life (V08 obs. 061), and silences Clockford with a look (V09 obs. 099). Louis's "heartless witch" has evidence behind it. What the series contests is his inference about her feeling, not her capacity.

**Indifference and care.** EXTRA09 shows her ready to shoot a creature she does not understand (EXTRA09 obs. 027). V01 shows her first act of care, for a boy who had insulted her (V01 obs. 101). Both are true of the same period, as the companion's frame argues (SW-C-0075).

**Honesty and the staged truth.** Monica cannot let another person be falsely accused (V01 obs. 059), yet she lives by small lies (AFTER04 section 6.4; SW-C-0042), and at V09 she builds the council's verdict on borrowed blood and blames a dead man for crimes in which Clockford had a hand (V09 obs. 094, 116). The volume presents the deception as wise and does not frame it as a wrong (V09 section 8.3). The tension is visible in her conduct: she lies freely for others and to protect secrets, and she refuses to let truth be used against the innocent.

**Mercy without consent.** Her mind spell on the stolen tool is mercy (V03 obs. 098). Her sealing of Romaria is mercy given unasked, and she questions it herself at V10 (V10 obs. 135; SW-C-0061). Louis tests whether she would seal her friends' memories; Mary forbids it (V07 synopsis). Monica's power to help is also a power to act on others without asking, and the text gives her the question rather than an answer.

**Duty and the self.** Her duty began as a role imposed (SW-C-0020), and her strongest motives are personal: mourning, her father, Isaac. The volumes often name the personal motive as selfish while it serves others (V03 obs. 088; V07 obs. 126).

**Justice and rescue.** She calls full exposure the most correct course and declines it (V08 obs. 253; V08 section 8.4). The series leaves her father's justice to the council, and the council's verdict is a fiction.

## 11. Thematic function

Monica carries the title's premise. Silence is her epithet and the mechanism of her power (SW-C-0001). The series' main thematic movements pass through her.

**Being known.** The first eight volumes are organised by the gap between what the kingdom says of the Silent Witch, what each person decides Monica must be, and what the narration shows (V01 section 6). At V01 exit she has at least four selves, each held by different observers (V01 section 7.1). The reveal at V08's end collapses them by her own act, and V09's request to be called Monica gives her one name among friends.

**Asking for help.** The refrain of V06, answered in V07 and completed in V09, is the series' account of how a self-sufficient genius becomes a member of a group (SW-C-0052, SW-C-0059, SW-C-0072). Louis, the genius of effort, believes in himself because others believed in him (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 160). Monica, the genius of numbers, reaches the same place by asking (SW-C-0103).

**Transmission.** Monica's adulthood is shown as passing on words she was given (SW-C-0084). This ties her to the series' third warranted specialist domain, masters and apprentices. Her case is distinctive because what she transmits is not mainly technique. It is permission: to rely on adults, to be angry at what matters, to come home.

**Names and remembering.** Isaac took a dead boy's name so that it would be remembered (SW-C-0038). Monica binds him to remember her father's (V08 obs. 180), and then chooses the living man over the name (V09 obs. 120). In the dyad she is the one who prefers the person to the name.

**Mirror and foil.** Cyril shares her history of a violent man and withheld approval and answers it with effort and concealed care (SW-C-0037, SW-C-0078). Louis is her foil in method: effort against numbers, money, violence and connections against chantless genius, with Louis learning the third from losing to her at the council (V11 obs. 183; SW-C-0043).

## 12. Counterarguments and counterevidence

**"This is a light comedy about shyness, and the reading of harm over-reads it."** The V01 reading states this rival directly (V01 section 8.1). The comic narrator does frame the origin of the silence as an anticlimax (V01 obs. 007), and much of Monica's characterisation is farce (section 7). Against it: the V04 prologue narrates the harm soberly, not as dream or delirium (V04 obs. 001–008); third parties see the scars (V03 obs. 121; EXTRA09 obs. 106); and at V12 Monica herself links her fear of tall men to the uncle (V12 obs. 054). The comedy and the harm coexist, and the ledger records that the comedy mostly registers harm (SW-C-0016).

**"Her development is situational, not dispositional."** The ledger keeps this rival on several records (SW-S-0005, SW-S-0028, SW-S-0067). It has strong support. She still stammers at V08 and V09 (V08 obs. 009; V09 obs. 147); she still fears tall men at V12 (V12 obs. 054); she still cannot cast well on the move or bear crowds (V10 section 7.1; V11 section 7.1). Against it: the changes recur across recipients and settings, and some become standing arrangements (a move, an apprentice, a name). The ledger retired the question of whether her speech gains generalise to strangers at V09, when she spoke before the Supreme Council (SW-P-0012). This monograph's position is that the disposition changes in what she will attempt and whom she will admit, while the reflexes of fear persist.

**"Louis's verdict is right."** The rival has more support at V04 than ever (V04 section 8.1), and EXTRA09 shows an early Monica who was indifferent and ready to kill (EXTRA09 obs. 027, 029). Against it: the decisive failure at V04 is her inability to treat Cyril as numbers, and her merciful acts precede any change in her self-report (V04 obs. 090; V01 obs. 101; V03 obs. 098). The ledger records the verdict as half right and its error as the assumption that she would never ask (SW-C-0033).

**"Her change is others' work, not hers."** V09's rescue is supplied entirely by others (SW-C-0072), and her key sentences are borrowed. Against it: the asking is hers, decided in the face of fear (V08 obs. 283, 291), and the ledger records asking and wanting to know as each made "by her own decision" (SW-C-0059). This monograph's thesis absorbs the objection: she changes by admitting others' words, and admission is the act.

**"V12 shows romantic love for Cyril."** Bridget believes it (V09 obs. 040). V12 shows shock, tears and shame (V12 obs. 161, 162, 210). Against stating it: the text names none of these feelings and records Monica's words only as embarrassment (SW-C-0093; V12 section 8.5). The monograph records unnamed feeling and keeps SW-P-0069 open.

## 13. Limits of this argument

**Boundary.** The argument uses only the sixteen Japanese witnesses as read in the snapshot. No web serial, adaptation, translation, wiki or outside knowledge is used. Paratext (cards, jackets, afterwords) is cited only as paratext, notably the card naming Rain as Monica's own surname (V03 obs. 133) and the V09 afterword on the coffee ending.

**Written speech only.** All observations about stammer and register are about printed text. There is no voice or performance evidence.

**Interiority.** Monica has the most interior access in the series, which makes self-report testable. But Louis, Cyril and Isaac also have interior access, and their views of her are their beliefs. Others, such as Mary and Raul, are seen mostly from outside, and this monograph does not attribute motives to them beyond what the readings record.

**Prequel retrospection.** The -another- volumes were read after V12 and do not revise the numbered readings. Their scenes of Monica at Minerva and at the selection are retrospective, and -another- upper contains no narrative scene of her that this monograph uses.

**Open threads at V12.** These remain open and are not predicted:

**Coverage.** The monograph draws on each reading's synopsis, its Monica sections, its close readings where they concern her, and targeted observation rows. It did not re-read every observation register line by line.
