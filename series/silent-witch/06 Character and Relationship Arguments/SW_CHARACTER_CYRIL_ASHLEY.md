# Cyril Ashley

## Introduction

Cyril Ashley enters *Silent Witch* as a joke. He is the student-council vice-president who shouts that any slight to the prince deserves ten thousand deaths, freezes offenders' feet, and insists that he is always calm (V01 reading §6.9). The first volume then gives him more interior access than anyone except Monica and Felix. It uses that access to think about one subject: what it means to be wanted, expected of, and recognized (SW-S-0013; V01 obs. 095, 096).

This monograph argues that Cyril is the series' study of *care given under the cover of duty, by a person who cannot yet see himself as worth caring for*. His development has three parts:

1. He moves from a loyalty fixed on an assigned object (the prince, the house that adopted him) to a loyalty he has chosen.
2. He moves from a need to be expected of to the habit of expecting things of others.
3. He moves from a family history that cast him as his father's copy to a position in two families that accept him.

Under all three runs a constant. Cyril almost never names his own motive. He gives his care for other reasons, such as the council's dignity, His Highness's sake, efficiency, or his own chill (SW-C-0078). When at last he looks inward, at V12, he finds that he cannot read his own heart (SW-C-0093; V12 obs. 167).

The series builds this figure as a mirror of its heroine. Monica's history and Cyril's rhyme: a violent man in the house, praise withheld, effort as the answer, and a sentence that could not be spoken to a parent (SW-C-0037; SW-F-0024). But the mirror is mostly the reader's to see. Neither character is shown learning the other's history in full, and each gives the other care that the recipient does not know about. That double blindness is the character's design, not a gap in it.

## 1. Evidence, order and method

All evidence is Japanese prose from the authorized units, read through the sixteen sequential readings and the master ledger at snapshot 60b21a7. There is no voice or performance evidence. Where this document describes Cyril's "shouting" or "gentle" register, it means what the prose narrates (checkpoint §4.2 item 5).

Cyril is one of the figures with sustained narrated interiority (checkpoint §4.2 item 4). He has viewpoint scenes in V01, V02, V04, AFTER04, V05, V06, V07, V08, V09, EXTRA09, V10, V11 and V12, though not in V03. That access is uneven, however. His interior is generous about duty, fear and self-reproach, and spare about what he wants from Monica. The readiness ledger records this repeatedly: his feeling is "unnamed by himself" at V04 (SW-RD-0020), and his view of Monica is "unstated" at EXTRA09 (SW-RD-0040).

Four orders are kept apart here.

- **Source order** is V01 to V12, with AFTER04 after V04 and EXTRA09 after V09.
- **Revelation order** is the order in which the reader learns things. Much of Cyril's past arrives late. His father is named only at V10. His year as treasurer, which blocked Thornley's theft, arrives only in EXTRA09.
- **Diegetic order** places those facts years before V01.
- **Participant knowledge** is different again. Cyril never learns that Thornley erased his memory (SW-K-0011) or that his diligence once stopped the embezzlement (SW-K-0126).

The two -another- volumes are prequels, read last, and barely touch Cyril. Their one bearing on him is retrospective. It concerns Louis's ethic, against which Cyril's is measured (SW-C-0043).

Isaac Walker is known to everyone, Cyril included, as Felix until V08 discloses the substitution. Cyril learns it through Elliott's account (SW-K-0102). This document says "Felix" for Cyril's dealings with him before that point, and "Isaac" after it, or for the person in general.

## 2. Development and state transitions

### 2.1 The vice-president at entry (V01)

At entry Cyril has four defining traits (SW-S-0013):

- He is loyal to Felix to the point of violence.
- He is scrupulous in procedure.
- He needs recognition.
- He is an adopted son carrying a sentence he never said to his mother.

The volume establishes each trait through conduct before it explains it.

When Monica rolls down a staircase into him, she covers her head, expecting a blow. What Cyril actually does is move her fringe aside and ask, three times, where it hurts. He checks for injury before asking whose fault it was (V01 obs. 057). Two chapters later he mentions, as an administrative aside, that he questioned the witnesses, established what happened, and disciplined the real offender, Caroline (V01 obs. 091). Monica has taken it as law that rank decides truth in this school. Her reaction is the discovery that such people exist too (V01 §6.6). Cyril is the first counterexample to the school's rule that rank makes truth (SW-C-0011; SW-W-0023; SW-K-0014). The ledger's rival reading is that his fairness is exceptional, not representative. The series never fully answers that, but it keeps adding to the list of authorities who investigate (SW-C-0011 STRENGTHEN at V02 to V09).

His judgment of Monica separates fairness from feeling. He finds her review of the accounts perfect, and goes on disliking her for disrespecting the prince (V01 obs. 080). As her instructor he rejects her apology for creating extra work: necessary work is not extra. Then he asks why she is so servile when the prince has trusted her (V01 obs. 089). He tells her he hates the word "cannot."

The next night supplies the reason, in his viewpoint (V01 obs. 095, 096):

- **His constitution.** He absorbs magical power past his body's capacity (SW-W-0010).
- **His adoption.** He was a poor distant relation adopted into the Highorn marquisate because the marquess had only a daughter and Cyril was the ablest candidate.
- **His sister.** His adoptive sister proved far more learned than he.
- **His overwork.** He drove himself at magic until the condition developed.
- **The brooch.** The marquess gave him a brooch that drains the excess, and he received it as permission to belong.

At the edge of collapse he wishes to be expected of by someone. The text prints the object of that wish as dots (V01 obs. 096; SW-F-0002). In delirium he speaks to his mother and remembers what she said when the adoption came: with a sigh, that he was a noble's child after all. He wanted to answer that he was her child, and could not (V01 §6.9).

Two mechanisms then act on him without his knowledge.

1. He noticed a left-handed slant in the falsified accounts. Thornley erased that memory, and the text blanks out where he went (V01 obs. 081, 108; SW-S-0014).
2. His brooch was damaged, so it fed the excess power back into him instead of draining it. In the forest Monica meets his frenzy without a word, cancelling each spell and shielding his body from his own frost. Exhausting his power is the treatment (V01 obs. 098, 099). He faints believing he has seen a monster.

He wakes with a key, a headache and no memory. He later asks Felix whether his rescuer was small and hooded (V01 obs. 103). The knowledge table at V01 exit is exact: Cyril does not know his memory was altered, does not know he was treated, and remembers a monster (V01 §7.8; SW-K-0015).

The exit state is a recognition. Finding Monica asleep over the ledger, he studies her small, pen-callused hand. He hooks a finger under the edge of her glove, and is interrupted (V01 obs. 111). A week later he offers to accompany her round the club presidents, because her work has earned it, and for the first time he addresses her by her office (V01 obs. 114; SW-S-0015). The mechanism of change is plain: *work observed, judgment revised, recognition conferred by title*. It is the mechanism of his whole ethic, applied to someone else.

### 2.2 The concealed protector (V02–V03)

The V02 reading names Cyril's arc as "care that he cannot admit" (V02 §7.4; SW-S-0037). He:

- worries when Elliott takes Monica out, and denies it when Bridget calls him overprotective (V02 obs. 026);
- arranges his work so that he can watch her practise;
- scolds Glen rather than her (V02 obs. 037);
- carries her, poisoned, until his strength fails;
- does her week's work;
- makes her drink chocolate to educate her palate, for His Highness's sake (V02 obs. 059);
- covers her with his jacket.

The pattern is fixed here and holds to V12. Each act of care comes with a stated reason that is not care.

Two speech acts mark a change of state. In the infirmary Monica throws herself on the floor to apologize, and Cyril puts her back to bed with the reproach that a victim has no reason to bow (V02 obs. 046). The reading notes that this is the first time anyone in the series tells her she is not the guilty party (V02 §6.9). Then he tells her to call him by his name (V02 obs. 069). The change of address is his initiative, and it is offered to her.

V02 also charges a cost to him that the volume's plot needs. Casey cut the rope that dropped the timber, but the accident is recorded as Cyril's oversight. He apologizes to Monica for it, and she weeps from guilt because she knows the truth (V02 obs. 098; SW-K-0026; SW-C-0021). At V03 he still blames himself, and she still cannot clear him (V03 obs. 016). His readiness to take blame that is not his first appears here, as the price of someone else's secret.

### 2.3 The rose and the mother (V04, AFTER04)

V04 discloses Cyril's history and gives his care its most elaborate disguise (SW-S-0072, SW-S-0073).

**The history.** Claudia's memory shows that she decided at twelve to marry Neil Maywood. That engagement led her father to adopt Cyril, at thirteen, as heir (V04 obs. 015). Elliott and Benjamin tell Monica that Cyril is an adopted son whose father had no title (V04 obs. 070). The fullest account comes from Cyril's mother, Myra Wayne (V04 obs. 077):

- His father, a man of Ashley blood without a title, berated and struck his wife, and died of drink.
- Myra feared that her clever son would become his father, and withheld the praise he worked for.

The V04 reading also records that Cyril learned magic to have a weapon of his own (V04 §7.3, from others' accounts).

**The rose.** The flower custom of the festival makes a flower in the giver's colours a request for the first dance (V04 obs. 018). Cyril chooses a pure white rose for Monica, whom his thoughts refer to only obliquely, after weighing colours (V04 obs. 029). He pockets the brooch he cannot live without, ties his own ribbon to the stem, and gives it to Monica as a charm against embarrassment (V04 obs. 032). His own viewpoint explains the tightness in his chest as concern for the council's dignity (V04 obs. 028). Every other reader of the rose reads it correctly: Lana, Felix, Elliott, and Myra when she hears of it (V04 §6.3; SW-K-0055). Monica alone takes it as a folk charm, and uses it as one.

**The mother.** Monica becomes the hinge of Cyril's family story without knowing it. A shy woman asks her whether Cyril bullies quiet girls. Monica, who cannot ask for help for herself, testifies fluently that he is kind, and shows the rose (V04 obs. 074). The woman is Myra. She decides to invite him home (V04 obs. 078; SW-R-0069). Cyril learns only that a woman with a mole came, and thanks Monica with a trembling voice (V04 obs. 075).

The chapter is titled for the invitations he sent every year (V04 obs. 076; SW-R-0070). It closes on a memory: the child Cyril saying he would make a sweet drink for someone dear rather than strike them. The reading ties that disposition to the chocolate of V02 and the ice of the ball (V04 §6.8).

The ball completes the state. Carrying Monica after she collapses, he notices the unexplained magical ice and her small hand (V04 obs. 108). He commends her, tells her to rely on others, and plans her future as Neil's right hand next year (V04 obs. 109). Then he asks her to dance, addressing her for once as a lady (V04 obs. 110). She leaves her head free of numbers so as to remember it. She also tells him that next year she will not be there. He does not know why: the end of her mission at Felix's graduation is hidden from him and her friends (SW-K-0056).

AFTER04, set between V04 and V05, turns Cyril outward, to his juniors (SW-S-0091; V04 and AFTER04 readings):

- He defends Glen against a theft charge once Glen swears his innocence before the prince (AFTER04 obs. 074).
- He rebukes Elliott's class sneer at his juniors (AFTER04 obs. 093).
- Asked why he studies magic, he answers that it is to be of use to his father. He tells Glen to take pride in a flight earned through many scrapes (AFTER04 obs. 079).

Glen and Monica, both of whom drifted into magic, take the lesson to heart. Monica finds herself wishing she could be proud of something (AFTER04 obs. 079; SW-C-0043). A retrospective interlude, diegetically years earlier, shows Cyril at fourteen: isolated as the adopted heir, taunted by Elliott, glad to be spoken to by Byron and hoping for a friend (AFTER04 obs. 069).

### 2.4 Going home (V05)

V05 resolves the V01 silence. Cyril goes home. His mother, who once met his childhood reports with sighs, now asks to hear about him (V05 obs. 153). She tells him he has worked hard, and he answers that he is her son (V05 obs. 155). The V01 sentence he could not say has been said, in a new form, to the person it was meant for (SW-S-0112; SW-R-0098). The reading finds the source of his ethic of patient effort in a weaving maxim of hers (V05 §6.8). The ethic he set against Louis's maxim in AFTER04 turns out to have been his mother's teaching.

The marquess then takes him to the New Year audience as heir (V05 obs. 154). At the Ortelia chime Cyril makes a vow of his own, to bear himself boldly, and the marquess tells him he can do it (V05 obs. 179).

Earlier in the volume, at the winter market, Glen and Monica make vows before him. The reading notes that they make them because a senior is there to witness. Cyril tells them he expects it of them (V05 obs. 022). In V01 Cyril wished to be expected of. In V05 he is the one who expects. This is the hinge of the second movement named in the introduction.

### 2.5 Heir at court and witness in the fog (V06–V07)

V06 shows Cyril at the castle as a nervous heir. His adoptive father sends him out to walk off his nerves (V06 obs. 018), and he doubts his own fitness beside Claudia (V06 reading §6.7). He praises a gardener's mana-fertilizer research as able to save tens of thousands, and the gardener turns out to be the Thorn Witch, Raul (V06 obs. 019). The first prince, Lionel, praises Cyril and asks his help for the country (V06 reading §6.8; obs. 034). Cyril privately favours Felix; his father is neutral (V06 obs. 035).

At school he is a protective senior. He tells Monica to call him if Robert troubles her (V06 obs. 127) and says he will drill her until he graduates (V06 obs. 139). In the cave where spirits have carried him and Glen, he says the series' central sentence about help: asking for help when one's strength fails is never wrong (V06 obs. 182; SW-C-0052; SW-F-0036). The man who hated "cannot" in V01 now licenses it in others. In V07 he keeps his promise to the spirits against a Sage's advice (V07 obs. 025). He gives the dying ice spirit, whom he has named Romaria, a frozen rose, which she calls a gift (V07 obs. 068, 124). He takes her into his brooch (V07 obs. 127; SW-R-0122; SW-K-0092). Back at school he apologizes to Monica for losing the duel and failing to protect his junior (V07 obs. 166; SW-S-0146).

His knowledge also narrows in these volumes (section 3).

### 2.6 The clown and the choice (V08)

V08 tests the loyalty that defined Cyril at entry. Early on he calls being chosen by Felix his pride (V08 obs. 016). He praises Monica's accuracy, fairness and effort, and she savours being praised by him (V08 obs. 193). Then Isaac, still known to the school as Felix, is arrested as an Imperial impostor and hands the council to Cyril. Cyril overhears Elliott admit that the prince he served was never the prince, since before they met. He calls himself a clown (V08 obs. 227).

Monica shouts down his self-contempt (V08 obs. 228). He recovers by moving the ground of his loyalty: he was chosen for himself, not for a title, and he will keep his choice and the council (V08 obs. 229; SW-S-0168; SW-R-0140). This is the volume's central state change for him. Before it, his loyalty was attached to a title and a person given to him. After it, the loyalty rests on his own choice and on the work. The person stays in view, but is no longer the only foundation. He sees the contradiction in the accusation (V08 obs. 231), studies the spirits' language for what he could not do in the forest (V08 obs. 275), and helps Nero recover a stolen watch (V08 obs. 277).

### 2.7 The kneel, the rescue and graduation (V09)

When Monica reveals herself to the council as the Silent Witch, Cyril kneels and apologizes to her as magic count, her title as a Sage. He says he had half guessed it from her hand and the hooded figure (V09 obs. 008; SW-C-0009). Monica weeps at being spoken to formally by him (V09 obs. 009). He returns to scolding her as "Treasurer Norton", which she prefers (V09 obs. 011; SW-S-0182).

He then declares the rescue of Isaac (V09 obs. 025) and leads the preparations (V09 obs. 034). On the morning of the Supreme Council he gives Monica the white-rose charm she asked for, telling her to give everything she has (V09 obs. 039, 054). In the forest battle to hold Louis away, he sees that the hooded monster he feared has always been beside him (V09 obs. 052). When Louis chokes him, he drops his brooch and lets his excess power run wild, freezing Louis in place (V09 obs. 066). He reflects that he would rather break with correctness than be an ignorant, passive good person (V09 obs. 067).

Two things have turned over. His constitution, once the illness his striving produced, is now a weapon he chooses to use (compare V07 obs. 043, where it is "useful for once"). And the scrupulous procedure of V01 now yields to a judgment about when procedure must be broken.

After the rescue Cyril weeps when the council receives Isaac back (V09 obs. 133). Knowing who Isaac is, Cyril tells Isaac that he is still the man Cyril respects (V09 obs. 180; SW-R-0153). He is outraged to find the former prince serving Monica as her apprentice (V09 obs. 183). Cyril graduates with Isaac's year, in the second week of Fivriu (V09 obs. 138; V10 obs. 033).

### 2.8 After graduation (V09 coda, EXTRA09, V10, V11)

**Research and address.** Half a year after graduation Cyril manages Highorn's funding of Monica's and Raul's joint plant research against famine (V09 obs. 146; EXTRA09 obs. 162). Monica asks him to call her Monica (V09 obs. 147), and he does in their work. The name pleasantly unsettles her, and she keeps that a secret (EXTRA09 obs. 165).

**The Library Society and Brendan.** At V10 he helps run his father's lands and serves the Library Society. Highorn is a former priestly house that guards the great library (V10 obs. 033; SW-W-0129). The volume gives his birth father a name and a character for the first time (V10 obs. 136; SW-C-0037 REVISE; SW-S-0205):

- He was called Wayne, a drinker who boasted of noble blood, isolated and unstable.
- Someone told Cyril that he was Wayne's son and just like his father.
- Cyril fears night waters because of him.

The old knight Brendan praises "respect your elders" as the teaching of Cyril's father, meaning the marquess. Cyril's pleasure collapses into self-loathing, because the teaching was his birth father's (V10 obs. 037).

**Growth at V10.** He confides his fear of night water to Isaac, though not to his juniors (V10 obs. 157), and accepts a lantern from him (V10 obs. 158). He takes responsibility for Romaria if she resents her rescue. Monica, as his junior, asks to share it, and he thanks Monica herself, not the Silent Witch (V10 obs. 137, 138; SW-C-0061; SW-R-0171). Watching her, he is proud of her growth and resolves to grow himself (V10 obs. 139).

**V11.** Cyril sits between two fathers (V11 §6.7; SW-S-0216):

- The marquess, Vincent Ashley, feels like a parent whose son has brought friends home for the first time (V11 obs. 080, 085).
- At a board game Cyril falters, saying that his father forbade such vulgar things. He is relieved when the marquess, a former board-game club chairman, brings out his collection (V11 obs. 082).

The reading notes that he then addresses the marquess with a different paternal term (V11 §6.7). The ledger reads the prohibition as part of the mirror of Monica's history (SW-CR-0587). A V11 character card, as paratext only, says that he was strictly raised by his birth father (V11 §7.8).

In the Minerva illusion, which traps others in their memories of Minerva, Cyril is immune because he never went there. He restores Monica and Raul by voice, shouting the old council roll call (V11 obs. 057, 112, 113; SW-C-0088). He calls Raul by name for the first time (V11 obs. 049). He asks Raul to look after "his junior" (V11 obs. 158). When Monica calls herself immature, he tells her that he is too, and that his junior can grow (V11 obs. 193).

### 2.9 The marriage talk (V12)

V12 gives Cyril a marriage talk with Aurelia, Mary's niece, which would raise the adopted heir's standing (V12 obs. 164). The marquess tells him to choose a woman he wants to share his life with (V12 obs. 165). Cyril asks Aurelia to decline, saying he cannot promise to marry her later (V12 obs. 166). He feels he has deceived his own heart without knowing how (V12 obs. 167).

In the epilogue he feels useless as Highorn's heir (V12 obs. 207). He sets himself three aims: to answer his adoptive father, his birth mother, and to be respected by Monica as her senior (V12 obs. 208; SW-S-0228; SW-R-0188).

When Monica, who has heard of the marriage talk, tries to congratulate him on a betrothal, her borrowed dress gives way. Cyril covers her with his coat, red to the ears (V12 obs. 209). The reading records the scene as unnamed feeling on both sides (V12 §6.5; SW-C-0093; SW-P-0069 OPEN). Monica believes there is a betrothal. Cyril knows he has asked for the talk to be declined. Nothing shows her learning otherwise before the boundary.

## 3. The suspicion: a knowledge history (SW-C-0009)

Cyril's suspicion that Monica is the Silent Witch is the series' longest-running test of a participant's knowledge. It must be told in his order, not the reader's.

| Unit | What Cyril perceives | What he concludes |
|---|---|---|
| V01 | A small, pen-callused hand like the one that reached for him in the forest; her half-awake remark that he is no longer cold (obs. 111) | Nothing shown; he is interrupted twice |
| V02 | Isabelle's account of the wyverns recalls the silent monster (obs. 054) | Links the forest mage to the Silent Witch; not restated for Monica (SW-S-0038) |
| V03 | — | No viewpoint on either link (V03 §8.7) |
| V04 | Unexplained magical ice and her small hand (obs. 108) | No conclusion shown |
| V06 | The Witch's small chapped hands; a sense of having met her; her injured left hand; Monica's flexed one; Hubert calling the small figure in the fog the Silent Witch (obs. 031, 032, 036, 083, 152) | No conclusion shown (SW-K-0080) |
| V07 | From Ray: the Witch's left hand is cursed; he remembers her shielding it (obs. 061, 062) | No conclusion shown (SW-K-0091) |
| V08 | Present at Monica's reveal (obs. 291) | Response not shown |
| V09 | — | Had "half guessed" from her hand and the hooded figure; kneels (obs. 008) |

Three things follow from the table.

First, Cyril is the only character whose route to Monica runs through her body rather than her magic. It runs through her hand, her callus and her small size. That is consistent with his conduct throughout. He notices people's physical states: injury before fault, cold before comfort.

Second, the readings refuse him a conclusion before V09. The V01 synopsis originally said he was "almost sure", and an erratum corrected it to say he "appears to connect" the hand and says nothing (V01 editorial revision record). This monograph follows the correction. Through V07, Cyril has evidence and no stated verdict. At V09 he speaks of having half guessed. The rival reading, that this is idle curiosity (SW-C-0009), is weakened by how often his attention returns to the hand. It is not refuted, because the text never shows him acting on either link (SW-P-0004, retired at V08 by the reveal, not by Cyril).

Third, the knowledge runs both ways with unequal stakes. Monica knows she treated him in the forest (SW-K-0015). She keeps his rose as a charm and he does not know (SW-K-0059). She cannot bear his formal courtesy toward the Witch (V06 obs. 047, 048). She does not want him to thank the Witch; she wants him to praise his treasurer (V07 obs. 129). For Monica the concealment is a cost paid inside a relationship. For Cyril it is an absence he cannot see. When it ends, his first act is the formal one, kneeling to her as magic count. His second, at her tearful request, is to return to the scolding that marks her as his junior (V09 obs. 009, 011). The order matters. Recognition of rank comes first, and he gives it up for the relationship.

| Transferred in his second middle-school year | Age about thirteen | EXTRA09 obs. 101 |
| Raised partly by a commoner mother, and knows ordinary household practice | Childhood | EXTRA09 obs. 105 |

Two corrections to a simple picture emerge from the late evidence.

**Praise was not wholly withheld.** EXTRA09 shows a mother who smiled and praised him when they sang together. The withholding V04 describes was specific. Myra withheld praise for his successes, the cleverness she feared would make him his father (V04 obs. 077; V05 obs. 153). Cyril's late refusal to sing, even at the prince's request, and his willingness to sing when someone needs help (EXTRA09 obs. 086–088; SW-C-0078) belong to this history. The song carries the memory of a praise he once had.

**The father was not only violent.** He also taught. The precept that Brendan admires and the prohibition on vulgar games came from him (V10 obs. 037; V11 obs. 082). Cyril carries the father's rules as well as the father's face. This is why praise can wound him. When an outsider praises a teaching, Cyril cannot simply accept it, because the teaching marks him as his father's son, the identity he has spent his life refusing.

A retrospective prequel detail, learned last, should be noted and left alone. A guest in ANOTHER-LOWER overhears that the Highorn house has a succession dispute (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 027). That scene is set years before V01. The reading does not connect it to Cyril's adoption, and neither does this monograph.

## 5. Motive and self-report against conduct (SW-C-0078)

The ledger's formulation of Cyril's hidden care is compact. His care is given unasked or under stated pretexts (SW-C-0078). The evidence runs from V02 to V12, and the pretexts form a small, fixed vocabulary:

| Act | Stated reason | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Chocolate for Monica | To educate her palate; for His Highness | V02 obs. 059 |
| The white rose | A charm against embarrassment; the council's dignity | V04 obs. 028, 032 |
| Leaving when tea is served | His chill would cool it | EXTRA09 obs. 037 |
| Monthly treasurer's checks | Correct procedure | EXTRA09 obs. 100 |
| Covering Neil's duty so he can dance with Claudia | None given; asks for secrecy | V04 obs. 106; EXTRA09 obs. 113 |
| Taking the heavy boxes from a girl with a hurt hand | His chill and efficiency | EXTRA09 obs. 137 |
| Light duties for Monica at the festival | Unstated; she realizes later | V04 obs. 103 |
| The wreath of dried white roses | Unstated; a present for her birthday month, never given | V11 obs. 048, 061 |
| Lending his brooch to a sick guard | Unstated | V11 obs. 190 |
| Covering her with his coat | Unstated | V12 obs. 209 |

Two points about this vocabulary.

First, the pretexts are not lies. His chill does cool tea. Monthly checks are correct procedure, and they did stop Thornley's theft for a year without his knowing it (EXTRA09 obs. 100; SW-K-0126). The pretexts are true and incomplete. The EXTRA09 reading is careful with the boxes: whether Cyril noticed the hurt hand is not stated, and the reading does not infer it (EXTRA09 §6.6). This monograph keeps that restraint. The claim is not that Cyril hides a known motive behind a false one. It is that he acts on care and accounts for it as duty, and the text gives only the account.

Second, the care is not reserved for Monica. Neil, Glen, a sick guard, a starving old man (V10 obs. 035), spirits in a cave, and a lost three-year-old in the council room (AFTER04 synopsis) all receive it. Benjamin's explanation, that Cyril cannot ignore anyone who asks for help (EXTRA09 obs. 087), describes a general disposition. That matters for the romance question (section 12). Hidden care is Cyril's ordinary mode, not a sign reserved for one person.

Where Cyril does report a motive, the report is narrow and directed upward:

- He studies magic to be of use to his father (AFTER04 obs. 079).
- Being chosen by Felix is his pride (V08 obs. 016).
- At V12 his aims are three people: his adoptive father, his birth mother, and Monica's respect (V12 obs. 208).

His self-reports name the people whose recognition he seeks. They do not name what he feels toward them. The V12 sentence about deceiving his own heart is the first time the narration lets him notice the gap (V12 obs. 167). The heart itself remains unread.

His self-blame runs the other way and is broad. He apologizes for the timber (V02 obs. 098), for losing the duel (V07 obs. 166), and for shouting at Monica's charge of sloppy accounts, which he accepts as true (EXTRA09 obs. 102). He is angry with himself because Monica showed "Barney", a figure from her Minerva memories, a trust she has never shown him (V11 obs. 131). He feels useless as heir (V12 obs. 207). The pattern is consistent. He holds himself responsible for more than he caused, and claims credit for less than he did.

## 6. The mirror (SW-C-0037)

The ledger's formulation is that Cyril's history mirrors Monica's: a violent father, withheld praise and striving for recognition. His care for her is a disguised invitation and practical help, and his attention returns to her hand (SW-C-0037). The V10 revision adds Wayne and the fear of night water. The claim was strengthened in every numbered unit from AFTER04 to V12 (SW-CR-0115 to SW-CR-0671) and preserved through the prequels. The formal record is SW-F-0024, "paired abused children".

The terms of the mirror need care.

**What is mirrored.**

- A violent man in the house: Monica's uncle; Cyril's father.
- Speech forbidden or impossible toward a parent figure. Monica's uncle forbade her to defend her father (SW-C-0003). Cyril could not tell his mother that he was her child (V01 obs. 096).
- A response through competence.
- A habit of self-accusation. Monica's prostration in V02 and Cyril's apologies for what he did not cause are the same reflex in different registers.

The V01 reading draws the conclusion that matters: two characters carry a sentence they could not say to a parent. The heroine's silence becomes a shared human condition rather than a quirk (V01 §6.9).

**What is not mirrored.**

- Monica's violent man was her father's brother, after her father's execution. Cyril's was his father.
- Monica's refuge is numbers, begun as a vow to keep her father's work (SW-C-0002). Cyril's is effort, which the reading traces to his mother's maxim (V05 §6.8).
- Monica's history ends in a rescuer, Hilda. Cyril's adoption is a dynastic arrangement made for a sister's engagement (V04 obs. 015).
- Cyril's mother is alive and is reconciled with him at V05. Monica's father's case remains to be proved.

The mirror is a rhyme, not an identity. The rival reading registered with SW-F-0024, coincidence, cannot be excluded by evidence. It is weakened by the deliberate structure: the V04 chapter on Myra is built as the volume's parallel to its prologue on Monica's tenth year (V04 §6.8).

**Who sees the mirror.** This is where order discipline matters most.

Monica's first act of care for Cyril, repairing his brooch in V01, is explicitly a projection. She does not want him to think of his constitution as a curse, because she cannot stop thinking of her own talent as one (V01 obs. 101; SW-C-0014). She acts on a likeness she perceives before the text has told her, or the reader, anything about his father. At V04 she meets Myra without knowing who she is (SW-K-0052). She learns from Elliott and Benjamin only that Cyril's father had no title (V04 obs. 070). His V10 disclosure of Wayne is in his own viewpoint, and he confides the fear to Isaac, not to his juniors (V10 obs. 136, 157). Nothing in the readings consulted shows Monica learning the detail of Cyril's father, or Cyril learning the detail of her uncle.

So the mirror is fully visible only to the reader. Each character responds to a partial likeness: Monica to a gifted person who might see his gift as a curse; Cyril to a junior who cringes and cannot be proud (V01 obs. 089). Each gives the other what they themselves lacked: Monica, an unasked treatment and a corrected brooch; Cyril, recognition by office and the absolution that a victim need not bow. The mirror works as a structure of mutual, partly blind care. That is why SW-C-0037 and SW-C-0078 belong together.

## 7. Effort and earned recognition (SW-C-0043)

AFTER04 sets Cyril's ethic of patient, earned effort against Louis's maxim that money and violence solve most problems. Glen and Monica, who both drifted into magic, take Cyril's side (AFTER04 obs. 075, 079, 090, 099; SW-C-0043). The claim was revised twice:

- At V11 Louis names his adult method as money, violence and connections, learned from losing to Monica at the council (V11 obs. 183; SW-CR-0593).
- The prequel shows Louis himself as a genius of effort, driven by refusal to lose. The opposition therefore lies in motive and display, not in effort (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 025, 028, 108, 212; SW-CR-0766). The volume's opening plate says the same in its motto, but that is paratext (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 004).

For the reading of Cyril, the revision is clarifying. It is retrospective evidence, learned last, about a man whose school years precede V01. What distinguishes Cyril is not that he works and Louis does not. It is three things.

**His effort is public and procedural.** He makes lists, fills a glass with ice by short chants between explanations, and checks the records monthly (V01 §6.9; EXTRA09 obs. 100). Louis's effort is hidden behind a maxim of force.

**His effort aims at being of use and being recognized.** He wishes to be expected of (V01 obs. 096). He studies magic to be of use to his father (AFTER04 obs. 079). He wants Monica's respect (V12 obs. 208). Louis's aims at not losing.

**He teaches pride in what is earned.** His sentence to Glen, that skill earned through scrapes deserves pride (AFTER04 obs. 079), is the one his own childhood lacked. He gives it to juniors before he hears it from his mother at V05.

Cyril's recognition economy is lopsided. He gives recognition accurately and promptly:

- the office title once her work has earned it (V01 obs. 114);
- a passing word of approval (V08 obs. 011);
- praise of Monica's accuracy, fairness and effort (V08 obs. 193);
- praise of Raul's research (V06 obs. 019);
- pride in her growth (V10 obs. 139; V11 obs. 193).

He receives it late, partly, or not at all:

- Byron, his rival, cannot help praising Cyril's rise from commoner to vice-president (AFTER04 obs. 049).
- His mother's acknowledgement that he has worked hard comes at V05 (obs. 155).
- His adoptive father's assurance that he can keep his vow comes at V05 (obs. 179).
- The teachers call him their saviour for rescuing Glen from a remedial class (EXTRA09 obs. 129).

The recognition he would most deserve never reaches him. He does not know that he stopped Thornley's embezzlement for a year (SW-K-0126). He does not know that his memory was erased for noticing the next step (SW-K-0011). A character card adds, as paratext only, that later councils will thank him for a work handbook he wrote for Glen (V08 §7.5).

Recognition can also wound him when it is misattributed. Conrad's praise of Claudia's legitimate bloodline leaves him dejected (V04 obs. 014). Brendan's praise of his "father's" teaching turns his pleasure into self-loathing (V10 obs. 037). For Cyril, praise is welcome when it is addressed to his work. It hurts when it is addressed to his blood.

The constitution belongs here too. In V01 his over-absorption is the bodily cost of striving. Monica hopes he will not think of it as a curse, and notes that, managed well, it is an advantage some mages cultivate (V01 obs. 101; SW-W-0010). Later volumes bear her out in conduct, though not in his own words:

- He is the school's strongest duellist and its only user of shortened chants (V03 obs. 015).
- Monica rates him fit for higher mastery (AFTER04 obs. 041).
- The excess is "useful for once" in the forest (V07 obs. 043).
- He uses it deliberately against Louis (V09 obs. 066) and against Raul's giant pea (V11 obs. 049, 050).

But at V07 he still calls it a burden. The change is in use, not in self-description.

## 8. Relationship conditioning

Cyril behaves differently with each recipient. The differences are consistent enough to be read as conditioning by the relationship, not as mood.

### 8.1 Monica

Toward Monica, Cyril's register is the shout. His content is supervision, protection and recognition (SW-R-0012, SW-R-0028, SW-R-0060, SW-R-0147, SW-R-0161, SW-R-0171, SW-R-0178, SW-R-0188). Monica's direction runs from fear of his temper (SW-R-0011), through trust and guilt (SW-R-0027), to respect (SW-R-0105) and, at V12, unnamed distress and shame (SW-R-0187). The relationship argument treats the dyad in both directions. Here only what it shows about Cyril is drawn out.

**He is the one person whose ordinary manner Monica needs.** She is relieved when he shouts again after a careful dance (V02 obs. 037). She cannot bear his formality toward the Witch (V06 obs. 048) or toward the magic count at her reveal (V09 obs. 009). After graduation she opens a window when he is due, to feel his cold (V09 obs. 177). She lies that she cannot drink hot tea, so that he need not leave when tea is served (EXTRA09 obs. 037). That is a small hidden care in his own idiom, given back to him.

**He is one of her models.** She names him among the people she wants to be like:

- Lana, who reaches out; Cyril, who scolds what is wrong; Bartholomeus, who told her a child may rely on adults (V10 obs. 103);
- Cyril, MacLaggan and Bradford, as masters to learn from (V11 obs. 194; SW-C-0084).

She steadies herself with his remembered voice (V11 obs. 163), and answers his roll call (V11 obs. 057).

**His address to her tracks the relationship exactly** (section 10): a contemptuous pronoun, then the office title, "Lady" once at a dance, formal address to the magic count, the office title restored, and finally her name.

What the readings do not record is Cyril's interior view of Monica as a woman. Bridget believes Monica loves Cyril without knowing it (V09 obs. 040; SW-R-0148). That is Bridget's belief. Felix guesses that Cyril sees her as a younger sister (V02 §7.4). That is Felix's guess. Cyril's own words are "my junior" (V11 obs. 158, 193). His own aim is to be respected by her as her senior (V12 obs. 208). The reader knows something he does not: Monica had already called him a senior she respects (V06 obs. 157). Either way, the aim he settles on is one the reader can see she has already granted.

### 8.2 Isaac, known first as Felix

Cyril's loyalty to Felix is the most visible trait at entry (SW-R-0014). It is also asymmetric in knowledge. Felix trusts his fairness, assigns him to Monica deliberately, and hides Wildianu and his own magic from him (SW-R-0013; SW-K-0007). Felix privately thinks Cyril too soft (V02 obs. 061). Felix is jealous that Monica names Cyril while still calling the prince "Highness" (V03 obs. 026), and thinks Cyril's "charm" unfair (V04 obs. 081, 105). Felix orders Cyril to win the duel to keep his treasurer (V06 obs. 136), and calls Cyril and Glen his important future aides (V06 §6.9). Cyril sees none of this.

What Cyril gives Felix is devotion as service. He makes Felix's tea himself as an honour, though he rarely uses his own servant (AFTER04 obs. 071). He vows to guard the prince's hair (AFTER04 obs. 126).

The V08 collapse and recovery (section 2.6) separates the loyalty from its first object. After it, Cyril's conduct toward Isaac has three features:

- **Respect kept with knowledge.** He tells Isaac that he respects him, knowing who he is (V09 obs. 180).
- **Formality held against invitation.** He still salutes Isaac as "Your Highness" while Isaac seeks a friend's distance (V10 obs. 159; SW-R-0172).
- **Confidence offered upward.** He admits his fear of night water to Isaac but not to his juniors (V10 obs. 157), and accepts Isaac's lantern (V10 obs. 158).

The jealousy now runs the other way. Isaac questions how often Monica meets Cyril (V09 obs. 179), grows serious on hearing she stayed at the Highorn mansion (V12 obs. 033), and keeps a thought from Cyril, who would misread it (V10 obs. 155). At V12 he watches Monica's shame before Cyril and leaves (V12 obs. 211). Cyril stands to shield Isaac at the battle (V12 obs. 197) and shows no sign of seeing himself as Isaac's rival. EXTRA09 adds a glimpse, set in the school year, of Felix imagining a conversation with Cyril about the Witch and burying it as a future that cannot be (EXTRA09 obs. 125). It adds pathos only for the reader.

### 8.3 Claudia and Neil

With Claudia, Cyril is the endurer of mockery (V02 §7.8). She calls him "elder brother" one syllable at a time as a weapon (V02 §6.17). She sees through his request to lend Monica a dress and teases him for wanting to see Monica in one (V04 obs. 017). Underneath the exasperation runs a structural wound: her engagement is the reason he was adopted (V04 obs. 015), and praise of her legitimate blood dejects him (V04 obs. 014). He doubts his fitness beside her (V06 §6.7). Yet the two Ashleys independently decide that Monica needs looking after (V02 §6.10).

Toward Neil, Cyril's care is pure hidden care. He covers Neil's duties so that Neil can at last dance with Claudia, and asks for secrecy (V04 obs. 106; EXTRA09 obs. 113; SW-R-0073, SW-R-0164). Neil is glad Cyril will be his brother-in-law. Cyril serves the engagement that displaced him into a family, and serves it gladly.

### 8.4 Elliott

Elliott is the one peer who has wounded Cyril. He once told the commoner-born Cyril to know his place (V02 obs. 026) and taunted him at fourteen (AFTER04 obs. 069). By V04 they keep a truce (V04 obs. 070), and Elliott counts Cyril among his circle of odd people (V04 obs. 072).

Their present relation is comic and unequal:

- Cyril grants Elliott the status of closest friend on command (AFTER04 obs. 086).
- Elliott braids the sleeping Cyril's hair like Monica's (EXTRA09 obs. 114).
- Elliott privately calls Cyril unfit as a noble because he will not command servants (EXTRA09 obs. 126).
- Elliott thinks his own father far more frightening than Cyril (AFTER04 §6.7).

Elliott is also a witness. He reads Cyril's smile at his own bouquet when Cyril has explained Monica's rose as the prince's kindness (EXTRA09 obs. 161). It is through Elliott's confession that Cyril learns about the substitution (V08 §3; SW-K-0102). The person who first told Cyril where his place was becomes the one who tells him the truth about the place he chose.

### 8.5 Glen

With Glen, Cyril is the senior in full:

- He shows him how to lead in a dance (V02 synopsis) and scolds him in Monica's place (V02 obs. 037).
- He defends him against the theft charge (AFTER04 obs. 074).
- He gives him the lesson of earned pride (AFTER04 obs. 079), which Glen applies when he stops an indoor fireball with Cyril's words (AFTER04 obs. 090).
- In the cave he takes a senior's duty to bring Glen back (V06 §6.7).

Glen gives Cyril smoked meat (SW-R-0080). Glen misreads Cyril's pocketful of dried fish as a love of fish (AFTER04 obs. 097), and Glen's mnemonic assigns Cyril the fillet (EXTRA09 obs. 116). Glen also, unknowingly, sets Cyril's V10 plot moving: fleeing Louis, he presses a lantern-ship ticket on Cyril, who mistakes it for a duty (V10 obs. 034). It is the one relationship in which Cyril is simply admired, and his conduct in it is the most relaxed. When Cyril shouts at Glen, he shouts as a teacher.

### 8.6 Raul

Raul is the one friend Cyril makes as an adult, and the friendship is staged as a slow change of address (SW-R-0177). They meet when Cyril praises the research of a gardener who turns out to be the Thorn Witch (V06 obs. 019). Raul wants friends (SW-R-0123). In EXTRA09, Cyril's "Lord Roseburg" already counts, for Cyril, as informal (EXTRA09 §6.5).

At V11, under the giant pea, Cyril removes his brooch and calls Raul by name for the first time; Raul calls it friend-like (V11 obs. 049). The friendship then turns comic in a way that confirms it:

- Cyril breaks off the friendship when Raul has not planned how to find Monica (V11 obs. 055).
- Raul animates Cyril's wreath of dried white roses, meant for Monica, into a rose dragon (V11 §3).
- Raul enjoys Cyril's anger as a quarrel between friends (V11 obs. 155).

Cyril's last request of him is the senior's: look after my junior (V11 obs. 158). The reserve Cyril keeps with Isaac, he gives up with Raul (V11 obs. 059).

### 8.7 His mother and the Highorn family

The family relations have been traced in sections 2.4 and 4. Their conditioning effect is visible in Cyril's speech and posture.

- **With Myra** he is a son who sends invitations revised many times (V04 obs. 076), stands frightened at a door, and is welcomed (V05 obs. 152).
- **With the marquess** he is a nervous heir sent out to walk (V06 obs. 018), led to court (V05 obs. 154), and gently corrected into enjoyment by a former board-game chairman (V11 obs. 082). He receives the freedom to choose a wife he wants (V12 obs. 165).

The marquess is consistently fond (V11 obs. 080, 085). The pressure on Cyril as heir does not come from the marquess. It comes from Cyril's own sense of what an adopted heir owes (V12 obs. 207) and from the marriage market, which values Aurelia's family for what it adds to his standing (V12 obs. 164).

### 8.8 Thornley

Thornley is the relationship Cyril does not know he has. Thornley erased Cyril's memory for noticing the left-handed slant (V01 obs. 081, 108; SW-C-0012). EXTRA09 adds, in Thornley's viewpoint, that he could not embezzle at all in the year Cyril was treasurer, because Cyril checked the records monthly (EXTRA09 obs. 100). Cyril, unaware, was twice the obstacle to the crime, first by diligence and then by attention, and was twice silenced: by Thornley's spell, and by the narration's blanks (SW-F-0002). The patron Thornley served, called 閣下 in the readings, remains withheld (SW-C-0039; V04 obs. 122). Nothing here identifies that patron.

### 8.9 Others

- **Byron** counts Cyril as his rival and the thief of his first love. He is mistaken: his fiancée Sheila named Cyril as her type only from embarrassment (AFTER04 obs. 049, 156). Cyril accepts Byron's formal duel because it is asked in earnest, and wins it (AFTER04 obs. 109, 154).
- **Brendan** is fed by Cyril (V10 obs. 035) and wounds him with praise (V10 obs. 037).
- **Romaria** receives his courtesy, a frozen rose and custody (SW-R-0114, SW-R-0122).
- **Lionel** asks his help for the country (V06 §6.8).
- **Melissa** falls for him without learning his name. Cyril takes her for a lost civilian and shields her from duty as Highorn's heir (V11 obs. 051, 052; SW-K-0139).

In every case Cyril's courtesy is the same, offered on the same grounds, whatever the other person feels.

## 9. Ordinary behaviour and humour

Cyril carries much of the series' gentle comedy, and the comedy is usually tenderness that his manner undercuts.

**Animals.** He is tender with animals that avoid him. A character card, paratext only, says animals avoid him because of his mana (AFTER04 §7.8). In the narrative:

- He strokes a white rabbit with delight and releases it when Glen proposes butchering it (AFTER04 §3).
- He carries dried fish "by chance" for the backyard black cat (AFTER04 obs. 097).
- He tempts the black cat with a foxtail, wanting to hold it (EXTRA09 obs. 043).

**Small domestic tastes.** He likes fine fabrics (EXTRA09 obs. 042). He makes the prince's tea himself (AFTER04 obs. 071). He knows, from a commoner mother, how ordinary households judge room temperature for butter (EXTRA09 obs. 105). He worries about chilling others as winter comes (AFTER04 obs. 070). A V03 character card, as paratext, says he worries about being too thin (V03 §7.7).

**Being misread.** Ray takes him for a woman in men's dress and curses him on learning otherwise (V06 obs. 030). Byron at fourteen took him for a girl (AFTER04 obs. 068). A booklet's "cold recluse" type for ice-users fails for him (EXTRA09 obs. 046). Elliott braids his hair (EXTRA09 obs. 114).

**Literal-mindedness.** His drawing of a crest leaves out the bull's tail, which matters later (V02 §6.6). He calls the meat theft impossible, and Glen says it would be easy (AFTER04 obs. 073). He misreads a ticket as a duty (V10 obs. 034). Board-game mishaps send him back to the start (V11 obs. 084). He is appalled to learn of the rose defences Raul and Louis planted at Serendia (V11 obs. 107). He shouts that nobody could work that out when Monica's far-sight calculation outruns him (V04 obs. 102).

**Order under strain.** The entry joke, a vice-president who freezes feet and insists he is calm (V01 §6.9), recurs as a method. In the Minerva illusion his shouted roll call restores others to the present (V11 obs. 057, 112). The comedy of his rigidity is also its use.

None of this is filler. The AFTER04 reading's counterargument section notes that the companion's comic episodes carry Cyril's isolation at fourteen (AFTER04 §8.1). EXTRA09 is "the figure the collection enlarges most" (EXTRA09 §6.6). The ordinary scenes are where the hidden care is most visible, because nothing else is at stake.

## 10. Written speech

All of this is written speech, as the readings record it (V01 §6.13; SW-S-0013).

**Base register.** Cyril uses a formal first-person pronoun and a stiff, archaic masculine register. He swears hyperbolic oaths on the prince's behalf.

**Two narrated modes.** A shouting register is his default, especially toward juniors. A gentle register appears in narrowly bounded situations: the careful dance (V02 obs. 037), the sickroom (SW-S-0037), the song (EXTRA09 obs. 088; SW-S-0195), and the dance at the ball (V04 obs. 110).

**Address as a ledger of relationships.**

| Recipient | Address | Shift and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Monica | A contemptuous second-person pronoun and the full false name | → the office title, "Treasurer Norton" (V01 obs. 114) → "Lady" once (V04 obs. 110) → formal address to the magic count (V09 obs. 008) → "Treasurer Norton" restored (V09 obs. 011) → "Monica" in their work (EXTRA09 obs. 165) |
| Himself, to Monica | Asks to be called by his given name (V02 obs. 069) | She bites the name (V03 §6.18) |
| Felix, then Isaac | "Highness" | Kept after the disclosure (V10 obs. 159) |
| Raul | "Lord Roseburg", informal for Cyril (EXTRA09 §6.5) | → Raul's name (V11 obs. 049) |
| His fathers | A remembered father's prohibition; a different paternal term for the marquess (V11 obs. 082; V11 §6.7) | — |
| His mother | Could not say he was her child (V01) | → says he is her son (V05 obs. 155) |

Two further features are recorded:

- **The one-word assent.** He answers Monica's promise to make the festival succeed with a curt one-word assent (V03 obs. 063), and gives the same one-word answer when she asks for help at V10 (V10 obs. 107).
- **His aversion to "cannot."** In V01 he will not accept the word from her. He faints repeating that it is not allowed (V01 §6.9). At V06 he tells a spirit that asking for help when one's strength fails is never wrong (V06 obs. 182). The vocabulary of self-reliance has opened into the vocabulary of help, without his ever naming the change.

Monica unmasks the impostor wearing Cyril's face partly by his address. The false Cyril calls her "Miss Norton" instead of her full name or her office title (V04 §6.12). The series treats Cyril's speech as distinctive enough to work as identity evidence.

## 11. Contradictions

Several tensions in Cyril are real and should not be smoothed away.

**Calm and rage.** He insists he is always calm while shouting (V01 §6.9). The contradiction is comic, but it marks a person whose self-image is procedural while his conduct is passionate.

**Loyalty and judgment.** At entry his loyalty to Felix overrides his fairness: he dislikes Monica for disrespect even after finding her work perfect (V01 obs. 080). By V09 he would rather break with correctness than be passively good (V09 obs. 067). The contradiction resolves over time. It is never quite renounced: he keeps "Highness" for Isaac (V10 obs. 159).

**"Cannot will not do" and "asking for help is never wrong".** He forbids himself what he licenses in others. He confides his fear of night water to Isaac, a person he ranks above himself, but not to his juniors (V10 obs. 157). He gives others more permission to be weak than he allows himself.

**Two accounts of why he studies magic.** He studies to be of use to his father (AFTER04 obs. 079). Others' accounts say he learned it as a weapon of his own (V04 §7.3). He also drove himself to justify an adoption that bewildered him (V01 obs. 095). The accounts are compatible, but they do not coincide. The plainest self-report is the most dutiful one.

**Hidden care and visible care.** The rose is hidden as a charm, yet four other characters read it at once (V04 §6.3). His care is concealed from its recipient more effectively than from onlookers. Elliott sees his smile at his own bouquet (EXTRA09 obs. 161). Bridget reads Monica (V09 obs. 040). The concealment is less a secret than a refusal to name.

**Praise as cure and as wound.** He seeks recognition, yet recognition of his blood or of his father's teaching hurts him (V04 obs. 014; V10 obs. 037).

## 12. Thematic function

Cyril carries six of the series' arguments.

**1. Recognition.** V01 makes him the character through whom the book thinks most directly about recognition (V01 §6.9). His arc runs from being unable to tell his mother he was her child (V01 obs. 096) to hearing her say that he has worked hard (V05 obs. 155), and from wishing to be expected of to expecting of others (V05 obs. 022). That arc gives the series' language of recognition a second, complete trajectory beside Monica's slower one. Monica receives recognition in V01 and cannot accept it as true (V01 §7.1). Cyril's acceptance at V05 shows what accepting it looks like.

**2. Rank and truth.** He is the school's first counterexample to rank as truth (SW-C-0011). As the commoner-born heir he embodies what Elliott's creed fears, talent crossing class walls (V03 obs. 037, 038 via SW-C-0011). Byron praises him for exactly that (AFTER04 obs. 049). Elliott's late verdict that he is unfit as a noble because he will not command servants (EXTRA09 obs. 126) keeps the class question open.

**3. External focalization of power.** Monica's full power is first shown through Cyril's eyes, as a "monster" (SW-F-0001; V01 obs. 098). At V09 the same viewer sees that the monster has always been beside him (V09 obs. 052). His point of view frames the series' handling of how power looks from outside, and his recognition closes it.

**4. Help.** He is one of the voices of the help refrain (SW-F-0036; SW-C-0052). He tells Monica to rely on others (V04 obs. 109), and she later recalls that he told her so (V08 obs. 033). He tells a spirit that asking is never wrong (V06 obs. 182).

**5. The present against the past.** In the Minerva illusion he is the one figure without such a past. He restores the others (SW-C-0088; V11 obs. 113). This is formally exact. Cyril's history happens at home, not at Minerva. In a book about the pull of backstory, he is the anchor in the present.

**6. Transmission.** Monica adopts him as a model of mastership (SW-C-0084; V11 obs. 194). His lesson on earned pride passes to Glen and Monica (AFTER04 obs. 079, 090). He also transmits what he did not choose: his father's precept, which he lives by and cannot bear to have praised (V10 obs. 037).

## Counterarguments and counterevidence

**1. "Cyril is simply a dutiful officer; his pretexts are his motives."** This is the strongest rival reading. The ledger registers "duty" as the rival for SW-S-0037, SW-S-0073 and SW-R-0060. It rests on real evidence:

- Cyril never once names care as his reason.
- His own viewpoint explains the rose as concern for the council's dignity (V04 obs. 028).
- His pretexts are true: the chill does cool tea; monthly checks are correct procedure.
- His care extends to everyone, which suggests a general sense of duty, not personal attachment (EXTRA09 obs. 087).

This monograph accepts most of that. Duty and care are not exclusive in Cyril. The care is general, not reserved for Monica. It rejects the reading's strong form, that the pretexts exhaust the motive, on three grounds:

- The custom is explained twice as an invitation, and he chose the rose's colour with her in mind (V04 obs. 018, 029).
- He asks for secrecy about acts that duty would have no reason to hide (EXTRA09 obs. 113).
- At V12 he himself notices that he has deceived his heart (V12 obs. 167). A pure sense of duty would not deceive anyone.

**2. "The mirror is the reader's construction."** The rival recorded with SW-F-0024 is coincidence. Counterevidence:

- The differences are substantial (section 6).
- Monica's V01 act of projection precedes any knowledge of Cyril's father (V01 obs. 101).
- The characters never compare histories on the page.

The response is that the mirror is offered *as* the reader's, and that this is its function. The V04 chapter on Myra is composed as a parallel to the prologue (V04 §6.8). The V01 reading draws the same parallel at the level of the unsaid sentence. The claim is about structure. It does not say that Monica or Cyril knows.

**3. "Cyril does not develop; only his circumstances change."** There is good evidence for this:

- He shouts at V09 as at V01 (V09 obs. 011).
- He keeps "Highness" for Isaac at V10 (obs. 159).
- He still calls his constitution a burden at V07 (obs. 043).
- He still blames himself at V12 (obs. 207).

His temperament is constant. The monograph's answer is that his development lies in the *foundations* of his conduct, not in its surface:

- His loyalty is re-founded on choice (V08 obs. 229).
- His relation to procedure gives way to judgment (V09 obs. 067).
- His relation to his own name and lineage changes (V05 obs. 155; V10 obs. 157).
- His ability to say what he lacks grows (V10 obs. 157; V12 obs. 167).

These are state changes recorded by the ledger as DEVELOPMENT from V05 onward (SW-S-0112, SW-S-0130, SW-S-0146, SW-S-0168, SW-S-0216, SW-S-0228). They are compatible with a constant temperament. *Amendment, version 1.1:* the ledger audit of 2026-10-14 retired DEVELOPMENT as a change kind. It re-kinded SW-S-0216 as a relationship change toward Raul, in keeping with SW-C-0078, and SW-S-0228 as an unresolved, unnamed feeling. Neither is a change of temperament, so the conclusion stands.

**4. "Cyril's feeling for Monica is romantic, and the text is building toward it."** There is evidence for this:

- Bridget believes Monica loves Cyril unawares (V09 obs. 040).
- Isaac watches Monica's shame before Cyril and leaves (V12 obs. 211).
- Cyril cannot promise to marry Aurelia (V12 obs. 166).
- Monica's chest "creaks" at the news of the marriage talk (V12 obs. 162).

Against it, the text names none of these feelings (SW-C-0093). Cyril's own aim is the respect of a senior (V12 obs. 208). The readings consistently refuse to record a feeling the text does not state (V04 §8.2; V09 §6.6; V12 §6.5). The question is registered as open (SW-P-0069). This monograph makes no prediction.

**5. "Cyril's suspicion was always near-certain knowledge."** The V01 synopsis once said so and was corrected by erratum (V01 editorial revision record). His V09 statement says "half guessed" (V09 obs. 008). The monograph keeps the weaker formulation.

## Limits of this argument

**Boundary.** The argument rests on the Japanese LN V01 to V12, IV -after-, IX -extra-, and both -another- volumes as read in the snapshot at 60b21a7. No web serial, adaptation, translation or outside knowledge is used. Paratext, such as character cards and afterwords, is cited only as paratext: Cyril's thinness, animals' avoidance, spirit-language study, the work handbook, his strict upbringing and his board games.

**Interiority.** Cyril's interior is generous on duty, fear and self-reproach, and spare on desire. The readiness ledger records INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE for any reconstruction tier at every Cyril checkpoint (SW-RD-0004, SW-RD-0012, SW-RD-0020, SW-RD-0023, SW-RD-0040). Two gaps in particular limit what can be said: his view of Monica, and his reasoning about her hand before V09.

**Open threads at V12.** These are treated as open, with no predictions:

- Monica's and Cyril's unnamed feelings (SW-C-0093; SW-P-0069).
- The marriage talk. Aurelia has been asked to decline, and Monica believes in a betrothal.
- Why Cyril's brooch glowed in the winter wind, and why Romaria did not answer (V11 obs. 159, 160; SW-P-0065). The ice spirit of the V12 interlude is a separate figure from Romaria. Its identity with the pale-gold girl is implied, not stated (SW-C-0086, SW-C-0091). This monograph does not link it to Cyril's brooch.
- Whether Romaria resents her rescue (V10 obs. 138; SW-C-0061).
- The Highorn succession dispute, mentioned in retrospective prequel evidence and not connected to Cyril by the text (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 027).
- What Cyril would make of his own erased memory or of his treasurer's year, since he knows neither (SW-K-0011, SW-K-0126).

**Withheld identities.** These remain withheld here:

- the patron called 閣下, whom Thornley served (SW-C-0039);
- the person Mary does not want to die, and the hidden small star;
- the seventh Sage;
- Hilda's collaborator;
- the prison's attacker by name.

None is resolved or inferred.

**What this monograph does not argue.** It argues the Monica–Cyril dyad only as it bears on Cyril. The dyad in both directions belongs to the relationship arguments. It makes no clinical claim. Over-absorption is a world rule of magic (SW-W-0010), and Cyril's fears and self-blame are described as behaviour and mechanism.
