# Isaac Walker, known as Felix

## 1. Thesis

The first scene of the series belongs to Isaac Walker, and the series does not tell the reader so for seven volumes. At the end of V01's prologue an unnamed, blue-eyed man says, in the plain first-person pronoun he uses in private, that he has at last found something he can lose himself in (V01 obs. 003). V01 and V02 let the reader identify him as the second prince Felix (SW-C-0006). V08 retells the same night from inside his memory. By then the reader knows that the man watching the Silent Witch strike down the wyverns was not the prince at all. He was the prince's former page, wearing the prince's face (V08 obs. 172–175; SW-C-0063).

This monograph argues three things.

1. **One mechanism.** Isaac's life is organised by devotion to a remembered person. He expresses it as total self-subordination: he gives up his own name, face, tastes and future to keep someone else's name alive. The mechanism is formed before the story begins, in a series of failures to protect people he loved (V08 obs. 100–104, 126, 144–146).
2. **A changed object, a kept method.** V09 ends the obsession with the dead prince's fame, and Isaac learns that he may love something for himself (V09 obs. 122–125, 144). What changes is the object and the aim of his devotion. It moves from the dead boy's name to magic, to Monica and to the country his pardon obliges him to serve. What largely survives is his method: calculation, concealment, persuasion by borrowed faces, and a readiness, in Wildianu's words, to choose no means (V10 obs. 143, 145; V11 obs. 078; SW-C-0081, SW-C-0089).
3. **A private release.** The release is private, not public. To the world he remains Felix, the retired prince and Duke of Erin, living under the dead boy's name (V09 obs. 164). Only a handful of people call him Isaac or Ike. At V12 Mary's prophecy, that his loss will not end, keeps loss as the condition of his life at the boundary (V12 obs. 171–173; SW-C-0038).

Isaac is also the series' fullest mirror of Monica. Each of them carries a hidden self under an assigned name. Each took up a dead person's legacy: her father's numbers, his friend's name. Monica's decisive act in the first arc is to choose the living person over the dead name. She gives up clearing her father to save Isaac (V09 obs. 120; SW-C-0067). That choice is the one Isaac failed to make for himself when he set aside the prince's last wish for the prince's fame (SW-C-0038).

## 2. Names, concealment and the order of knowledge

### 2.1 How this monograph names him

The reader meets one figure under one name for seven volumes. The text calls him Felix Arc Ridill, the second prince, president of the Serendia student council. V08 discloses, in narration from his own memory, that he is Isaac Walker, a doctor's son from Lugaroa. Clockford took him as a double for his frail grandson and remade his face by body manipulation after that grandson died (V08 obs. 097–158; SW-C-0063).

This monograph follows four conventions.

- **"Isaac"** is the person, at every point in diegetic time.
- **"Felix", written plainly,** is the persona: what the text presented in V01–V07, what other characters see, and what the world still calls him after V09. Where the distinction matters the monograph writes, for example, "Isaac, then known to everyone as Felix".
- **"Arc"** is the real prince, Felix Arc Ridill, who died about ten years before V01. Arc was his middle name, used as a secret name between the two boys (V08 obs. 115). The ledger key is sw.arc. He is never merged with Isaac here.
- **"Ike"** is the childhood name Arc gave Isaac (V08 obs. 115; SW-C-0029). It is also the name Isaac gave himself, without explanation, when he wore a mask before Monica at V03 (V03 obs. 078).

### 2.2 What the reader could know, volume by volume

The concealment is not a defect to be corrected in retrospect. It is how the books are built. The readings held the "Felix cluster" open without hypothesis from V02 to V07, and so does this monograph when it reports those volumes (SW-C-0022, SW-C-0050, SW-C-0058).

| Boundary | What the text presented | Ledger |
|---|---|---|

| V02 | He is the watcher. He secretly studies magic and adores the Silent Witch. He must become king without wanting to, and will play the role "while he bears the name". The court seer cannot see his star. | SW-S-0034, SW-S-0035; V02 obs. 062, 064, 093 |

| V04 to V07 | The vow becomes a wish for a hero's posthumous light. Contract stone, water spirit and earth name. Witnesses describe a different child who changed after an illness ten years ago. | SW-C-0038, SW-C-0050, SW-C-0058 |
| V08 | Narration from his own memory: he is Isaac Walker. | SW-C-0063; SW-S-0161 to SW-S-0166 |

The concealment works inside his own viewpoint. That is the volumes' boldest technique. From V01 the reader has frequent access to "Felix's" thoughts, yet the narration withholds what those thoughts contain. Wildianu knows the wish, and the narration declines to state it (V01 obs. 078). The pronoun shift is shown but never explained (V01 reading §6.13). When V03 gives him a dream of a boy called Felix and an attendant page, his own viewpoint describes the boy from outside, "a little boy who cried", not as himself (V03 obs. 112–114; V03 reading §6.14). Read after V08, the sentence is exact. Isaac was the page.

### 2.3 Who knows, and when

Participant knowledge must not be collapsed into the reader's.

- **Clockford** knows from the start. So does **Wildianu**, from the contract about five years after the substitution (V08 obs. 147, 165).
- **Elliott** recognised the page's eyes in the "new" prince as a boy. The page nearly strangled him and told him the prince had died to set him free (V08 obs. 219–220).
- **Bridget** believed for years that her prince had been taken from her. V08 confirms to her that he is dead (V04 obs. 027, 118; V07 obs. 171–176; V08 obs. 224).
- **Monica** reaches the conclusion herself by V07 and states it to Yuan at V08 (V07 obs. 211, 213; V08 obs. 040–047).
- **Louis** proves it by his own inquiry at V08, having formed an unstated hypothesis at V04 (V04 obs. 120; V08 obs. 185).
- **Cyril** learns it at V08 by overhearing Elliott (V08 obs. 227).
- **The king** knew Isaac's name all along (V09 obs. 106).
- **The public** receives a false official story (V08 obs. 211; SW-K-0102, SW-K-0112).

Isaac's own ignorance runs the other way. From V01 to V08 he does not identify Monica as the Silent Witch, though he admires the one and grows close to the other (SW-C-0008). At V08 he learns two separate facts and is not shown to join them: that Monica Norton is Venedict Rain's daughter, and that the Silent Witch knows his name (V08 obs. 199, 259, 261; SW-CR-0276). At his trial he guesses that her real name is Monica Rain (V09 obs. 078). He recognises her only when the Witch enters the council wearing his pendant, and calls himself a fool (V09 obs. 080–081). The two concealments meet in one room, and each is broken in the presence of the other.

## 3. Development and state transitions

The arc runs in six states. The first is told last: it is recovered from V08's memory chapters and, for one glimpse, from the prequel. It is set first here because diegetic order requires it, and it is marked as retrospective.

### 3.1 Before the story: the failed protector (retrospective, V08 memory)

Isaac was a doctor's son in Lugaroa in the east, with a mother and a baby brother, Colin (V08 obs. 098). Earth dragons destroyed the town and his father died treating the wounded (V08 obs. 099). He promised his father he would protect his mother and brother, as the elder brother (V08 obs. 100). A dragon killed his mother and clawed the right side of his face. He was rescued with Colin dead in his arms. He survived alone, having failed his promise (V08 obs. 103–104).

The memory chapters build his character on that failure. In an orphanage he studied without rest (V08 obs. 105). A cold noble examined his scarred face, judged that the scar could be dealt with when the face was remade, and took him. The noble was Duke Clockford (V08 obs. 106). Isaac had just turned seven. The prince was five and sickly (V08 obs. 108–109).

The friendship that follows is the emotional source of everything later. Clockford calls his grandson "that thing" and asks whether the child can really be Irene's (V08 obs. 110–111). Isaac sees his dead brother in the boy and wipes his tears with a handkerchief rabbit, the same trick he had used for Colin (V08 obs. 102, 112). The prince, feverish, fears being forgotten. He wishes to become a constellation like the first king Ralph (V08 obs. 114). He asks for a friend. Isaac tells him that friends call him Ike, and answers him as Arc (V08 obs. 115). The reading records the relation in the memory's own terms: master, friend and brother, a time too tender to call play-acting (V08 obs. 118; SW-R-0133).

Two further injuries shape him. When Elliott dared the prince into a fall that pierced his side, Clockford had the physician Arthur cut the same wound into Isaac (V08 obs. 119–125). Isaac called it a necessary scar (V08 obs. 125). It is the scar Monica sees, unexplained, at V03 (V03 obs. 121). The prince found it, confronted his grandfather, and learned that Isaac was his double and had known from the start (V08 obs. 134–136).

Then comes the night that fixes Isaac's course. About two years in, the prince takes him to watch the stars from a tree. He asks Isaac to promise to find something he loves for himself (V08 obs. 138–140). The prince gives Isaac a letter and his mother's contract stone, asks him to flee while he causes a commotion, climbs to the roof, reaches for a star, and falls (V08 obs. 141–143). Isaac concludes that his own existence drove the prince to his death (V08 obs. 146). Clockford hides the death and tells Isaac that from today he is Felix Arc Ridill (V08 obs. 145, 147).

The decisive choice is Isaac's own. To keep the prince from being forgotten, he chooses to become him, and asks Arthur to proceed (V08 obs. 148). The narration itself calls this a childish, foolish wish, for which he distorted the meaning of the prince's death (V08 obs. 149). Clockford stabs Arthur. Isaac takes it as a warning, sets fire to the shed with both bodies, and asks only that, if he and the prince meet below, the prince not forgive him (V08 obs. 155–158).

The mechanism is complete before V01. A person he loves is lost. He believes the loss his fault. He pays for it by subordinating himself entirely to that person's wish, or to what he takes the wish to be. The wish he serves is the prince's fear of being forgotten, not the prince's last request (SW-C-0038).

### 3.2 The prince as presented (V01–V07)

Read only at its own boundaries, V01–V07 present a strategic actor whose kindness and calculation coincide (V01 reading §8.2; SW-S-0010). The volumes gather four strands. Each is explained, in retrospect, by V08.

**Public perfection and private remainder.** In public he silences rooms with a word and a smile (V01 obs. 020). In private he calls his life a remainder, to be lived out until a king is chosen (V01 obs. 078). He says he must become king without wanting anything prepared for "his name", and will play the role while he bears it (V02 obs. 062, 077; SW-S-0035). Bridget remembers him as a poor dancer who apologised. He responds with the embarrassment of a man reminded of an old blunder, then compares Monica with "his own former self" (V02 obs. 039). V02 also gives him a memory of a small boy apologising for failing as a royal (V02 obs. 049). After V08 the reader can see what the persona was doing in those scenes: narrating Arc's childhood as its own.

**The vow.** On a day he calls special, once a year, he repeats a vow unbroken for ten years: to do anything to inscribe the name Felix Arc Ridill in history (V03 obs. 065; SW-S-0050). At V04 the wish turns darker. After Glen's triumph he says people like Glen are called heroes, fit to be remembered. Under Ralph's constellation he asks Monica whether it would not be happiness to leave such a light even after death, and she names what she sees as a quiet obsession (V04 obs. 064, 116–117; SW-S-0071). He says he will pursue this wish even at the cost of breaking a dead friend's request that he find something to love for himself (V04 obs. 116). V05 sharpens the grammar: his private vow to his best friend promises to leave "your name" in history, and ends with the word Arc (V05 obs. 135; SW-S-0110).

**Resistance under obedience.** He states twice, in his own viewpoint, that he absolutely cannot defy the duke (V01 obs. 062). Yet the two men he expels are the duke's placements (V01 obs. 120). At night in Coalrapton he collects letters from nobles who resent his grandfather (V03 obs. 082). Before the duke's contempt his face empties into a doll's (V03 obs. 061). At V05 he reads an anonymous letter as his grandfather's command to secure the throne and goes out "as Clockford's puppet". He sees himself in a dragon driven by a curse, and concludes that Clockford means to make him a true puppet through Thornley's and Sams's researches (V05 obs. 096–099, 159; SW-C-0047). Each of these is his inference (SW-S-0109).

**Ruthless means.** He let an embezzler run for a year to make the expulsion certain, and told his council the opposite (V01 obs. 077). At V06 he had planned to have Hubert disposed of through Wildianu if the challengers lost, and then orders it outright, to look like an accident (V06 obs. 147, 149). No character learns of the order (SW-K-0087). He asks Ray whether a living thing can be controlled by curse, and the text never says why (V06 obs. 023; SW-P-0044, open).

Against all this runs a fifth strand, his devotion to the Silent Witch. It begins at the prologue and recurs as rapture: papers kept in a locked drawer, a search of the school, and an admiration he calls, at Porter's shop, probably a first love (V02 obs. 065, 087–088; V03 obs. 103–104; SW-S-0108). He studies magic secretly, and lies that he has no talent for it (V01 obs. 121; V02 obs. 014, 065). The reason is hidden until V08. Clockford forbade him magic because the mismatch in elemental affinity might betray the substitution (V08 obs. 153; SW-CR-0275).

### 3.3 Disclosure and the plan against Clockford (V08)

V08 gives the identity three ways: as Monica's conclusion, as Isaac's memory, and as consequence (V08 reading §6.1). The hinge is a frame. "Felix" falls asleep in a carriage and dreams of his first carriage ride. The narration of the memory is Isaac's. When he wakes, the narration corrects its own naming of him from Isaac back to Felix (V08 obs. 097, 176; SW-F-0043). The device is the series' clearest formal statement of his condition. The name changes with the mask, inside the narration itself.

In the present, V08 shows him at his most dangerous. He suborns Emmanuel to make a tool in Clockford's collection run wild during an inspection. The death is to be blamed on a bishop not yet known to be dead. Four nobles who covet Clockford's place have promised to back Felix if his grandfather dies (V08 obs. 090–094; SW-S-0164). Emmanuel sees Clockford in him (V08 obs. 092). His plan, as he states it, is to treat the throne as a starting point and to die a great king remembered in history (V08 obs. 095). Asked by Monica whether he would sacrifice someone for a wish, he says that if necessary he would (V08 obs. 177).

The volume then shows the one limit on that method. From Porter he learns who Venedict Rain was and what his death served. He becomes certain that Clockford killed Monica's father to protect the substitution, and that his own kindness to her was hollow (V08 obs. 196–199). By his own rule she should be removed, and he cannot wish it (V08 obs. 202; SW-C-0065). Clockford discards him at once. Central soldiers arrest him as an Imperial impostor, citing Emmanuel's forged note. He hands the council to Cyril and calls it his reward (V08 obs. 203–208).

In the tower he says he is long since dead (V08 obs. 258). He will not confess the truth, because the prince's credit should stay. He will play the worst villain instead (V08 obs. 260). Told by Nero that the Silent Witch knows his name, he is glad: he can climb the scaffold at ease (V08 obs. 261). He asks that Wildianu be given to her (V08 obs. 262). This is the purest form of the mechanism. He sacrifices his life to the dead boy's honour, comforted only that one person he reveres will remember his own name.

### 3.4 Rescue and release (V09)

V09 ends the obsession, and the volume is careful about how. Two conditions are needed: the plan that saves him, and the truth about the prince's death.

The rescue is collective and staged. Every part of it is supplied by someone Monica asked (SW-C-0072). At the council Isaac refuses to give a name and confesses to every crime, including her father's death, wishing she would hate him (V09 obs. 076–079). The Witch enters wearing his pendant and declares him the real prince (V09 obs. 080). The black chalice reddens only because Monica adds Albert's blood from her sleeve. Isaac and Clockford alone know he has no royal blood (V09 obs. 091–092, 116). His confession is blamed on a curse laid by Peter Sams, and Ray performs the cure (V09 obs. 093–097). The reading registers this as a staged truth that leaves everyone a victim (SW-C-0069). The king tells Isaac that he knew, overlooks the matter for the country, and thanks him as a father, smiling like Arc. Isaac answers that he could not protect him (V09 obs. 106–108).

Release comes privately, from the diary. Monica refuses the revenge he offers her: he puts her hand to his throat, as he did at V03. She says she gave up clearing her father because she wanted to save the man alive now (V09 obs. 118–120). He reads the last page. The prince meant to become a kind prince Ike could be proud of, and hoped to visit Ike one day as a grown man and hear what life he had chosen (V09 obs. 122). Monica concludes that the death was an accident, not a suicide. Isaac remembers that the prince let go with one hand to reach for a star (V09 obs. 123–124; SW-C-0070). The narration then says the obsession leaves him. She asks him to live and find joy, and tells him a friend needs no reason (V09 obs. 125–126). He thinks, in his own viewpoint, that he could not help loving her (V09 obs. 128).

The mechanism's premise has been falsified. The loss was not his fault, and the dead boy's wish was for the living Isaac, not for the name. The conduct that follows is new in kind. At graduation his address ends in his own words, the prince's wish (V09 obs. 139). He gives Monica a yellow-rose corsage as a charm "to make you like me" and says he has stopped giving up what he loves (V09 obs. 143–144).

### 3.5 Renunciation, Duke of Erin, apprentice (V09 coda, EXTRA09, V10)

He renounces the succession and is sent to convalesce on a domain (V09 obs. 132). He becomes Duke of Erin under the prince's name, and solves local problems quietly under other names (V09 obs. 164–165). He signs one letter, to the Witch, with his own name (V09 obs. 166). At the grave that bears his name, where the prince lies, he finds Bridget's rose (V09 obs. 167). Then he goes to Southerndoor, cleans Monica's house, cooks, offers a year's papers and any test, debates magic with her through the night, and makes himself her apprentice (V09 obs. 163, 169–173). The ledger records the transition: obsession ended, love stated in his viewpoint, apprenticeship begun, while she regards him as a friend (SW-C-0073; SW-S-0181).

EXTRA09's coda fixes the routine: about a week a month in Southerndoor, baking, glad to be "Ike" in an apron rather than a prince (EXTRA09 obs. 173, 177; SW-S-0194). V10 shows both lives at once (SW-C-0081). As duke he holds back, because zeal could look like a bid for the throne (V10 obs. 031). As apprentice he is overjoyed. Monica has bought him bookshelves, and one becomes a Silent Witch collection: here, he says, he need not hide his treasures (V10 obs. 040, 042). She welcomes him home as Ike for the first time (V10 obs. 160).

### 3.6 The political duke, stated love, prophesied loss (V11–V12)

V11 and V12 make him a political actor in his own right. At V11 he trades intelligence with Marquess Baldia on the Empire, Falfolia and Randall. Baldia was one of the backers Isaac recruited when he planned Clockford's assassination. Isaac keeps him as an ally under a gentleman's agreement, holding Baldia's secret over him (V11 obs. 063–074; SW-C-0089). At V12, in the East, he confirms a pact of silence with the Earl of Kerbeck, offers intelligence on the Anderson Company, and sets up a north-eastern road and furnace project. He brings it to the village through Isabel without his name and admits it when asked (V12 obs. 020–029, 095, 100–104; SW-C-0092). Monica frames it as the condition of his pardon: he wants the country better (V12 obs. 104). The Kerbecks judge him raised by Clockford to do what Clockford does, "too much as a royal" (V12 obs. 029).

His love, stated in V09, becomes the main matter of V12 (SW-C-0093). He is jealous of her stay at the Highorn mansion and of the cipher's maker (V12 obs. 033, 077). He grips her wrist and tells her he wants her to understand how much he cares. He puts her hand to his racing heart. She proposes to measure his pulse with instruments and later diagnoses a cold (V12 obs. 068–070, 106). He knows that to her he is an indirect perpetrator, her parent's enemy, and still hopes she will take his hand (V12 obs. 105).

The volume ends his strand with prophecy. Mary tells him that he once wished to be a false star, that he was born under the star of loss, that his loss will not end, and that he will lose half the world in his eyes (V12 obs. 171, 173; SW-CR-0672). He thinks he still has Monica, who let him stay, and wonders whether to keep away from her or guard her closer (V12 obs. 172, 174). In the epilogue he watches Monica's shame before Cyril, cannot move, leaves, and whispers Arc's name (V12 obs. 209–211). The prophecy is recorded as prophecy, not prediction. What he will lose is open at the boundary (SW-P-0066).

## 4. Motive and self-report against conduct

Isaac has extensive interior access, which makes him unusual among the figures who deceive (checkpoint §4.2 item 4). It lets the comparison be made at every stage. It does not let the motive be read off his self-reports, because his self-reports are often wrong about himself.

**What he says he wants, and what he does.** Before V08 he says he wants nothing for himself and must become king (V02 obs. 077; V03 obs. 101). His conduct contradicts the first claim. He keeps the Witch's papers, schemes around his grandfather's check on his reading to see her paper, and cannot bring himself to burn the drawer of things "Felix Arc Ridill must not possess" (AFTER04 obs. 035; EXTRA09 obs. 134–135). The thing he loses himself in is the one desire the role forbids. The prince's last wish was that he find exactly such a thing (V08 obs. 140, 174–175). So he fulfils the request he says he is overriding, without admitting it.

**What he says he is doing for the prince.** He believes he serves the prince by making the name remembered. The narration judges the wish childish and foolish, a distortion of the prince's death (V08 obs. 149). Wildianu tells him not to slight the last request (V08 obs. 168). Irene, through Wildianu, had wished her child personal happiness despite royalty (V08 obs. 169; V10 obs. 172). Three voices, the dead boy's, his mother's and the spirit's, all ask for Isaac's happiness. His self-report reads the prince's fear of being forgotten as an order and the prince's request as a sentiment. The diary at V09 shows the reverse (V09 obs. 122).

**What he says about Monica.** In V01 he calls her a game, a frightened squirrel to be tamed. What amuses him is that she has no interest in him (V01 obs. 077–078). Yet his acts toward her are mostly protective in form. He is angry at her poisoning, an anger he says he rarely wastes, and sits at her bedside (V02 reading §8.2). He lets her value her father's book (V03 obs. 107). He wants to be relied on (V06 obs. 114, 166). At V06 he recognises that he overlays a remembered friend on her, a pale-eyed boy who apologised and called him Ike (V06 obs. 165–167; SW-C-0056). At V08 he tells himself he took to her because she apologised like the young prince, and because he saw the old bruises on her back (V08 obs. 201). Self-report and conduct converge here, but on a displaced object. The kindness is real, and part of it is addressed to Arc.

**What he says about his methods.** After V09 his self-judgement is harsher than the narration's. At V10 he dislikes himself because calculation outweighs his sincerity (V10 obs. 145). If unmasked, he thinks death at Brendan's hands would be fair (V10 obs. 141). At V11 he refuses the love potion, not on principle but because using it would be no fun. He must grant his wishes in a way he can truly smile at, for his dead friend and for Monica (V11 obs. 077). Wildianu answers that he chooses no means, and Isaac laughs it off (V11 obs. 078). The reading registers no change of disposition (V11 reading §8.4). Joy and self-respect, not a rule, limit his means.

**What he hides.** Before V08 he hides from almost everyone: the spirit from Cyril, the night outings from Elliott, his magic from Louis (V01 obs. 079, 121; SW-R-0013, SW-R-0015, SW-R-0019). After V09 he hides less, but he still hides his violence from Monica. When Hubert taunts him about his face, he stabs a mana-laden shell at Hubert's eye, keeping a cruel face she must not see (V12 obs. 089). The taunt touches the substitution itself, since the face is Arc's. The violence that once nearly strangled Elliott for seeing through it survives in a smaller act (V08 obs. 220).

The pattern is consistent. Isaac's account of his motive lags behind his conduct. Before V09 his conduct is more loving and more self-interested than his account admits. After V09 his account is more self-condemning than the volumes are.

## 5. Relationship conditioning

### 5.1 Monica, as Monica Norton

The relation begins in asymmetry (SW-R-0009, SW-R-0010). To him she is first a suspect. He drops his keys as a test and rejects the hypothesis that she is the king's agent because she seems too incompetent (V01 obs. 069). Then she is a pet, a game and a useful accountant. His pet lexicon names her a squirrel (V01 obs. 036; SW-F-0005). He kisses her struck cheek out of curiosity while she is unaware of her surroundings. She takes it for her cat's paw, and Nero keeps it from her (V01 obs. 073, 076). The reading records the kiss as non-consensual in form, unknown to its recipient, and unevaluated by the narration (SW-C-0016). No later volume revisits it.

The festival night at V03 changes the terms. Masked, he becomes Ike: laughter, the plain pronoun, an invitation to be fellow delinquents (V03 obs. 078). When she flinches from his hand, he offers her a fiction: think of him as a ghost who is really nowhere (V03 obs. 087). She answers, for the first time in three volumes holding out her hand first, that she is a ghost too, neither the Silent Witch nor Monica Norton (V03 obs. 100; SW-C-0029). In the morning he puts her cold hands to his throat, gives her a peridot, tugs the chain and asks her to remember Ike (V03 obs. 117–120). The reading holds the scene's readings as coexisting: test, confession, possession and farewell (V03 reading §6.15).

The persona then becomes the channel of candour. In daylight he asks for help as her fellow delinquent, and she silently mouths Ike (AFTER04 obs. 043). A friend who listens to what he loves is, he says, an excessive luxury for a ghost (AFTER04 obs. 062). Monica keeps Ike's confidences from Louis, feeling that to report them would betray her first friend of the night (V04 obs. 067; SW-S-0069). His attachment is stated in his viewpoint from V04. He would give her a flower if he could. He calls Cyril unfair for calling his rose a charm. He is jealous that she did not wear his pendant (V04 obs. 081, 105, 114; SW-S-0070).

Two details stay open. When he complains that something is unfair and says he did not mean her, the object is unstated. It is unclear how he knew her birthday (AFTER04 obs. 064, 145, 149; SW-P-0036, untested).

What conditions the relation is mutual concealment with unequal stakes. She hides her identity and her mission. He hides his name, his wish and the fact that the boy he sees in her is dead (SW-R-0045, SW-R-0046, SW-R-0103). At V07 she wants, for the first time, to know another person: why Ike sometimes looks as if he has given something up (V07 obs. 179; SW-C-0059). At V08 she speaks her father's name to him and begs him never to forget it. Running into the rain, she calls the request a curse. The binding is laid before he knows who her father was (V08 obs. 180–181; SW-S-0160).

### 5.2 Monica, as the Silent Witch

The prologue's object of absorption is the Witch's magic, not a person (V01 obs. 003). His admiration is for an author of a silent, merciless magic whom he has never seen unveiled (V03 obs. 104). When Monica must act openly as the Witch at V05, he kisses her hand "like a youth in love". She annotates his paper on underwater wide-area formulas and asks to see more of his work. After his rescue from the cursed dragon he feels respect, longing and adoration (V05 obs. 042, 053–055, 102; SW-S-0108). Monica, for her part, judges it admiration rather than love. She resolves to remain the Witch he admires, so that he will not give up what he loves (V05 obs. 056). The reading leaves both judgements to their focalizers (V05 reading §8.1).

Before the Witch, his inferences outpace her small lies. He infers that she is connected with Serendia and knows of her familiar (SW-C-0042, SW-C-0046; V05 obs. 129, 133). Here the mechanism of concealment is ironic. The Witch hides from the one person who most wants to see her, because he is also the prince she guards. He in turn hides from her the fact that the prince she guards does not exist. The ledger records the state as mutual concealment (SW-R-0091).

### 5.3 Monica, after the council

After V09 the relation is that of master and apprentice, friend and stated lover. Each direction is recorded separately (SW-R-0145, SW-R-0146).

- **His side.** He is devoted, jealous and comic. He bristles at Cyril and at Robert's chess invitations, and questions how often she meets Cyril (V09 obs. 175, 179). He calls her "my great master" when she buys him shelves. He asks to learn under her with an English loan phrase meaning "my master" (V10 obs. 040, 146).
- **Her side.** She calls him a friend and does not read the corsage (V09 obs. 126, 143). She learns that she has set him a standard without considering a master's responsibility, and takes Cyril, MacLaggan and Bradford as models (V11 obs. 192, 194; SW-C-0084). She remains blind to his feeling at V12 (SW-S-0224; SW-R-0186).

V12's normative question is whether his pursuit harms her. The volume shows force: a gripped wrist, a pinned hand, and her fear of a feeling she cannot calculate. It also shows his retreat to a step's distance. It treats the feeling as sincere and his position as her parent's enemy as an unresolved obstacle (V12 obs. 055, 068–069, 073, 105; V12 reading §8.4). This monograph adds one structural point. The pinned wrist follows directly on her confession of a fear of tall men (V12 synopsis, chapters two and three). The V01 kiss was given to someone who could not know of it. In V12 the force is visible to her, and she answers it with instruments and a barrier (V12 obs. 070). The relation has moved from her ignorance to her incomprehension. It has not moved to reciprocity.

### 5.4 Clockford

Clockford is Isaac's maker and the model he resists and repeats. The ledger's formulation covers the arc: domination by contempt, the taking of a substitute, a discard on rebellion, and a hold broken at V09 by Monica's silent threat (SW-C-0030; SW-R-0048, SW-R-0049, SW-R-0134). In the memory chapters Clockford chooses him, has Arthur cut the scar into him, forbids him magic, stabs Arthur before him and tells him that only the two of them know the truth (V08 obs. 106, 123, 153, 156). In the present, Isaac's resistance takes Clockford's own forms. He works by placements and factions, gathers resentful nobles in Coalrapton, and suborns a Sage to stage an accident (V03 obs. 082; V08 obs. 090–093). Emmanuel sees Clockford in him (V08 obs. 092). At V12 Isaac recalls using a bishop purged over a drug against Clockford, and falling into Clockford's trap (V12 obs. 024). The Kerbecks say he was raised by Clockford to do what Clockford does (V12 obs. 029).

What Clockford wants is seen mostly from outside. V09 gives Mary's inference: making a non-royal king would be the ultimate revenge on the crown, rooted in his father's killing by Ridill's own war party. Clockford neither denies nor confirms it (V08 obs. 294; V09 obs. 110–111; SW-C-0071). If Mary is right, Isaac was an instrument of revenge from the start, before he chose the role for his own reasons. The volumes do not decide the point. They keep Isaac's choice and Clockford's purpose apart. Isaac chose the name for the prince (V08 obs. 148). Clockford chose the boy for reasons only Mary guesses at. Mary's comparison of the discarded boy with Clockford's own end is her prophecy, not a fact (V08 obs. 296).

### 5.5 The real prince (Arc)

Arc must be kept distinct, and he is not a mirror of Isaac. He was frail, of average mana and earth affinity. He was kind, blamed himself for his mother's death at his birth, loved the stars and kept an ideal-prince list in his diary (V08 obs. 113, 127–132; SW-S-0166). On learning of the double, he acted to free Isaac (V08 obs. 142). The diary's last page shows that he meant to live (V09 obs. 122).

Isaac's relation to him has three layers. Before the fall he was Arc's protector, friend and secret elder brother (V08 obs. 118). After the fall he becomes the custodian of the name and of the list. He will become the prince of the list (V08 obs. 159). After V09 he becomes, at last, the addressee of Arc's real wish. The prince's presence lingers in small acts. A lantern given to Cyril recalls a weak, kind prince who feared the dark (V10 obs. 158). Isaac persuades Brendan, Irene's former knight, with the dead prince's pleading face (V10 obs. 143). Facing loss at V12, he whispers Arc's name (V12 obs. 211).

Bridget's love is for Arc, not for Isaac. She calls the clumsy real prince lovable (V09 obs. 140) and leaves a rose at his grave (V09 obs. 167). She is revolted by the present prince (V07 SW-R-0120; V08 obs. 224). The king's thanks are addressed to Isaac as a father to his son's friend, with Arc's smile (V09 obs. 107).

### 5.6 Cyril

Cyril's relation to Isaac is the clearest case of devotion given to the persona and kept after the persona falls. Before V08 Cyril is loyal to Felix to the point of violence. Felix trusts his fairness, assigns him to Monica deliberately, and hides Wildianu from him (SW-S-0013; SW-R-0012 to SW-R-0014). At V08 Cyril learns that the prince he served was never the prince, and calls himself a clown. Monica shouts down his self-contempt. Cyril recovers by holding to his own choice: he was chosen for himself, not his title (V08 obs. 227–229). Isaac, arrested, has already handed him the council (V08 obs. 208). At V09 Cyril weeps when Isaac returns. In the epilogue he tells him he is the man Cyril respects, knowing who he is, and is outraged that the prince serves Monica (V09 obs. 133, 180, 183; SW-R-0153).

From Isaac's side the relation is warm, guarded and jealous. In EXTRA09's school-year stories he knows that Cyril sings in the records room. He imagines discussing the Witch with Cyril, a future he calls one that cannot be (EXTRA09 obs. 084, 125). After V09 he bristles at Cyril (V09 obs. 179). At V10 Cyril confides his fear of night water to Isaac but still calls him "Highness", while Isaac seeks a friend's distance (V10 obs. 157–159; SW-R-0172). Cyril's fidelity to his own choice is what lets the friendship survive the name. But Cyril's formality keeps Isaac in the role. At V12 Isaac's last scene is spent watching Monica and Cyril (V12 obs. 211).

### 5.7 Elliott

Elliott is the witness and the antagonist of the substitution. As a boy he was chosen as the real prince's playmate. He taught him violin and chess and watched a page boy serve the tea (AFTER04 obs. 122–123, retrospective). He was also the bully who hid the book that led to the prince's first fall (V03 obs. 019; V08 obs. 119–120, 217). He alone recognised the page's loathing and slightly greener eyes in the prince who returned (V08 obs. 219). The page choked him and said the prince had died to free him, that he had as good as killed him. He warned Elliott that Clockford would destroy his family (V08 obs. 220–222). Elliott chose to watch and laugh at his ruin. When Isaac answered that Elliott should watch a fool's end, Elliott saw that he hated himself (V08 obs. 223; SW-R-0139).

The relation conditions Elliott more than Isaac. The talented commoner Elliott once said had been ruined by cunning people is Isaac Walker, by Elliott's own acknowledgement. Elliott admits he cannot call Isaac a fool, and that it would be easier if he could deny it (V08 obs. 226; SW-C-0044). His creed of rank is contradicted by the person who caused his view of it. At V09 he joins the rescue in order to see Isaac's face when he is saved by the man he hates (V09 obs. 027). In EXTRA09 he calls him the "damned attendant" in his thoughts, and says he chose to stop being a bystander (EXTRA09 obs. 158, 160; SW-R-0154). In the prequel, Felix at twelve or thirteen talks with his childhood friend Elliott in a manner the reading calls indifferent (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 036).

### 5.8 Wildianu

Wildianu is Isaac's one chosen ally and the keeper of the wish he betrays. The spirit was born of a dying wishing-spring. He served Queen Irene as the maid Diana, guards her wish that her child not forget what he loves, and passed it from Arc to Isaac (V10 obs. 074, 167, 172; SW-C-0082). Isaac contracted him alone, exhausting himself nightly to raise his mana. When Wildianu appeared, about five years after the substitution, Isaac called him his ally (V08 obs. 160–165). After Irene, Wildianu would have let himself fade. He stayed because the boy needed looking after (V10 obs. 170–171).

The relation is service with a conscience attached. Wildianu complies with the order to kill Hubert, hesitantly (V06 reading §8.4). He reminds Isaac of the last wish (V08 obs. 168). At V08 he offers to destroy himself to free Isaac, a love for Irene he cannot name (V08 obs. 280–282; SW-S-0173). At V09 he keeps the contract (V09 obs. 129). At V10 Isaac tells him, in thought, that he trusts him (V10 obs. 126). At V11 Wildianu names Isaac's readiness to choose no means (V11 obs. 078). Wildianu is also part of the comedy. At V12 Isaac learns that Monica trusted Wildianu, not him, to hold Hubert off (V12 synopsis, chapter six). The card says Wildianu is poor at imitating human behaviour (V08 reading §7.5, paratext).

### 5.9 Lionel, the king and the court

The first prince Lionel is seen in relation to Isaac mostly through others.

- **V06.** Lionel says that Felix is the more fitting king, yet he will protect the country (V06 obs. 034).
- **V08 memory.** Asked by Wildianu about Lionel, Isaac says he would watch him rather than kill him, to avoid provoking Randall, but cannot promise never to execute him (V08 obs. 167).
- **V08 present.** Louis says Lionel loves his brothers (V08 obs. 187).
- **V09.** Lionel is to be named heir (V09 obs. 112).
- **V11.** After the renunciation the retired prince keeps political value, since Lionel and Albert are unmarried and childless (V11 obs. 072).

The pairing is lopsided. Lionel's goodwill is directed at the "Felix" he believes to be his half-brother. Isaac's calculation about Lionel is made in a viewpoint Lionel never sees. No scene shows Lionel learning the truth, and this monograph claims none.

### 5.10 The prequel's view (retrospective)

The two -another- volumes happen before V01 and were read last (checkpoint §4.2 item 3). They show the second prince only from outside.

ANOTHER-UPPER mentions a second prince called Felix, seven in Louis's first high-division year and "especially frail" in Lionel's account (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 168, 201). At Louis's graduation it mentions a long-sick second prince, "recently recovered", whom Clockford is presenting (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 209). The companion does not say which boy is meant at either point. The reading draws no identity inference from the dates, and this monograph draws none either.

ANOTHER-LOWER gives the one sustained look, through Louis's suspicion. At a soirée Louis sees a boy of twelve or thirteen. He appears healthy and tall, though reported sickly. His eyes are pale blue with a drop of green. Louis reads his smile as manufactured and his manner as "too perfectly reproduced" (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 031–032). Lionel says Felix had a grave illness some years ago and is now well (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 034). Louis, from dislike of the second prince, resolves to stand with Lionel (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 035). The ledger counts the scene as support for the present Felix's divergence between public and private selves (SW-CR-0824). The eye description matches the present Felix's at V03 (V03 obs. 065), and not the child's plain light-blue eyes in Elliott's memory (V03 obs. 018–019).

Louis knows nothing of the substitution at this point. His distrust is his own reading of a manner, and the numbered track's V01 confirms only that he finds the prince uncanny (V01 obs. 119; SW-R-0018). The scene's value for this monograph is that the performance was legible as performance to a hostile outsider years before the story. Louis read the perfection as wrong without knowing why.

## 6. Ordinary behaviour and humour

Isaac's ordinary life is the evidence that the role did not exhaust him. The volumes record a consistent private repertoire.

- **Care work.** In V01 he braids Monica's hair, saying a prince must do everything perfectly (V01 obs. 113). After V09 the same skill becomes service: he wakes his master, braids her hair, serves her friends and cooks (V09 obs. 163, 174). The narration calls him a born servant, weak for those who need looking after (V09 obs. 171). The page who comforted a frightened child with a handkerchief rabbit had not disappeared (V08 obs. 102, 112). Wildianu stayed with him for the same quality turned inward: the boy needed looking after (V10 obs. 170).
- **Cooking and craft.** EXTRA09's coda has him baking (EXTRA09 obs. 173). At V12 the painted eggshells call up a half-memory of quarrelling with a neighbour boy over wax in his home village, a boy whose face he has nearly forgotten (V12 obs. 052; SW-C-0094). At V11 he recalls his mother's pies and a promise to a sibling about to be born. He says he stopped remembering when Arc died and now can again (V11 obs. 039). The recovery of ordinary memory is part of the release.
- **The fan's comedy.** His devotion to the Witch is played for laughs. He is rapt over a donation list, gloomy at thirteen readers waiting for her paper, and delighted by a Kerbeck book about her (AFTER04 obs. 034, 059, 144). He cannot burn her papers and plans to bequeath them instead (EXTRA09 obs. 135). Twenty-nine more volumes follow the first, and he shortens his goal for the first to two months (V10 obs. 164).
- **The apprentice's comedy.** He attends her lecture disguised by Wildianu's illusion, having recruited Isabel so that Monica cannot scold him. When she tries her scary face, he disarms her (V11 obs. 205–207). At V12 she answers his racing heart with a proposal to measure it, tickles his old wound and seals him in a barrier (V12 synopsis, chapter four; obs. 070).
- **Jealousy as running joke.** He bristles at Robert's chess invitations and questions how often Monica meets Cyril, while Monica, for her part, opens a window to feel Cyril's cold when he is due (V09 obs. 175, 177, 179). Cyril is outraged that the prince serves her (V09 obs. 183). The card has Nero call him "Kirakira", sparkly (V08 reading §7.5, paratext only).

The humour has a function. Before V09 his comedy is the comedy of control. A room is silenced, a squirrel tamed, a kiss mistaken for a paw (V01 obs. 020, 073, 078). After V09 it is the comedy of a man who has lost control of his feelings and has given up control of his time. The apron, not the crown, is the costume he is glad of (EXTRA09 obs. 177). The comedy of harm, though, is not retired. The V01 kiss remains unevaluated (SW-C-0016), and V12's force is presented within a comic register that the reading evaluates separately (V12 reading §8.4).

## 7. Written speech

All evidence is Japanese prose. Nothing here concerns voice or performance (checkpoint §4.2 item 5). The readings record the following features.

**Two pronouns, two selves.** In public he uses the formal first-person pronoun watashi. In private he uses the plainer boku: with Wildianu, and in the unnamed watcher's line at the prologue (V01 reading §6.13; SW-S-0009). The volumes never comment on the shift. It is the most consequential linguistic fact in V01. The boundary moves with the mask.

- **V03.** Boku is the voice of Ike. He returns to watashi for the one sentence in which he says he must become king (V03 obs. 078, 101).
- **V04.** Boku becomes the voice in which he speaks to Monica privately as the prince, not only as Ike (V04 reading §6.12).
- **V06.** He uses boku in private speech while carrying Monica to the infirmary (V06 obs. 167).
- **After V09.** Boku is his voice with her as apprentice (V10 obs. 040, 042).

A related device is the third person. At V01 he names himself in full, Felix Arc Ridill, in the third person when he speaks of his own competence (V01 obs. 062). His vow speaks of the name in the third person, and at V05 it addresses the name as "your name" (V03 obs. 065; V05 obs. 135). Read after V08, the grammar is literal. The name is someone else's.

**Command and courtesy.** His public register is gentle and interrogative, with requests softened by friendly endings and a room-silencing "well, then" (V01 reading §6.13). The courtesy is a form of dominance, as Aaron's viewpoint registers (V01 obs. 020). His address to Monica replaces her name with an animal (V01 reading §6.13 table; SW-F-0005). After V09 he addresses her by her title as master, in reverent and playful forms (V10 obs. 040, 146).

**Lines repeated across masks.** At Porter's shop the present prince tells Monica he does not know the book's value, but that it is clearly worth that much to her. The page's line to the boy in the dream is almost the same: but it is precious to you, isn't it? (V03 obs. 107, 112; SW-F-0020). V03 offered the echo without explanation. V08 explains it: the same person said both lines.

**Silence and names.** His speech acts at the crisis are refusals of naming. He refuses to give a name at his trial, and he confesses to everything except the truth (V09 obs. 076, 079). His one act of self-naming is written. He signs a letter to the Witch with his own name (V09 obs. 166). He is glad that the Witch knows his name before he dies (V08 obs. 261). Nero, who garbles human names throughout, cannot remember it and is told (V08 obs. 259).

## 8. Contradictions and counterevidence within the character

The volumes let several contradictions stand. This monograph records them rather than smoothing them.

1. **Kindness and killing.** In one chapter at V06 he wants Monica to rely on him and orders Hubert killed as an accident (V06 obs. 114, 149). At V08 he cannot wish Monica removed, yet means to stage his grandfather's death and frame a dead man (V08 obs. 090–091, 202). The ledger keeps both halves (SW-C-0065). Neither is promoted into his essence.
2. **Serving the prince by betraying his wish.** The substitution is undertaken for the prince and against the prince's request (V08 obs. 140, 148–149). Isaac knows the second part: he says he will break a dead friend's wish if he must (V04 obs. 116). He does not admit what the narration states, that the wish was a distortion.
3. **Freed from the name, living under it.** After V09 he is privately Isaac and publicly Felix, Duke of Erin (V09 obs. 164). He tends the grave bearing his own name (V09 obs. 167). His pardon rests on service performed in the dead boy's name (SW-C-0092). The release did not restore his name to the world.
4. **Self-condemnation and continued method.** He judges his calculation a fault (V10 obs. 145), yet keeps Baldia under his secret and the potion in his pocket (V11 obs. 073–077). He hides a violence Monica must not see (V12 obs. 089).
5. **Love and the overlay.** At V06 and V08 he admits that his care for Monica is partly addressed to Arc (V06 obs. 167; V08 obs. 201). At V09 and V12 the love is stated for Monica herself (V09 obs. 128; V12 obs. 068, 105). In the V12 epilogue, faced with losing her, he speaks Arc's name (V12 obs. 211). The volumes do not say whether the overlay ended.
6. **The prophecy and the remainder.** In V01 he called his life a remainder (V01 obs. 078). V08 explains the remainder as Isaac Walker's leftover life (SW-P-0002). At V12 Mary says his loss will not end (V12 obs. 173). He has gained a life of his own, but the volumes frame it as provisional.

## 9. Thematic function

Isaac carries four of the series' structures.

**Names and remembering.** V08 turns on remembering names: the prince's fear of being forgotten, Isaac's assumed name, the curse of Monica's father's name, and the Witch's knowledge of Isaac's (SW-C-0068). Isaac embodies the problem the series sets: whether the name or the person should be remembered. The prince's wish favours the person. Isaac's vow favours the name. V09 resolves it for him privately, through Monica's choice of the living man over the dead father's name (V09 obs. 120). V12 reopens it as loss (SW-C-0038).

**Concealment and disclosure.** He is the second great secret of the first arc, mirrored formally by Monica's. V08 ends on two reveals: Isaac's identity through his own memory, and Monica's self-naming before her council (V08 reading §6.1; SW-F-0043). The volumes make the two concealments meet in one room at V09, with the pendant he gave her as the token of both (V09 obs. 080).

**Masks as the condition of candour.** In the series' formula, disguise is the condition of candour (SW-F-0019). Isaac is honest only when masked: as Ike, as a ghost, as a fellow delinquent. The prince's face is his deepest mask. After V09 the apprentice's apron is the costume under which he can be candid about what he loves (EXTRA09 obs. 177).

**Masters and transmission.** He completes the series' chain of teaching. He learns from Monica, teaches Glen and calls him a younger brother who needs looking after (V11 obs. 029, 032). He makes Monica learn what a master's responsibility is (V11 obs. 192, 194; SW-C-0084). The apprenticeship also transmits Arc's last request. He is now studying the one thing he loves for himself.

Above all he is Monica's mirror in a precise sense. Each of them made a dead person's legacy into a refuge or a rule. For Monica it was her father's numbers (SW-C-0018). For Isaac it was the prince's name. Each lived under an assigned name: Monica Norton, Felix Arc Ridill. At the same crisis each meets a refusal of sacrifice. Monica refuses to let Isaac die a villain, and refuses Wildianu's offer to destroy himself to free Isaac, deciding instead to rely on others (V08 obs. 282–283; V09 obs. 120). The narration names Louis's error as assuming the Witch would never ask anyone for help (V09 obs. 072; SW-C-0072). The difference is the direction of the bond. Monica learns to ask. Isaac learns that the dead did not ask what he gave.

## 10. Counterarguments and counterevidence

**1. "Felix and Isaac are, for readers, two characters; reading V01–V07 through V08 is hindsight."** The readings deliberately held the Felix cluster without hypothesis, and V01–V07 contain no statement of the identity (SW-C-0022). A reader at V07 meets a prince, not an impostor. This monograph accepts the point for revelation order. It reports each earlier volume at its own boundary (§2.2, §3.2). But the text itself joins the two retrospectively. V08 retells V01's prologue from the watcher's side (V08 obs. 172–175). It explains the repeated line, the scar, the eyes and the vow (SW-C-0063). V08 also frames the identity inside the person's own viewpoint, not only through others' beliefs (V08 reading §8.1). A single-character reading is the text's own instruction, provided the earlier presentation is not erased.

**2. "The release at V09 is not a change of character but a change of object."** This is the strongest counterargument, and the evidence for it is substantial.

- **The original absorption.** The prologue's "something to lose himself in" was the Witch's magic, before he knew Monica (V01 obs. 003; V08 obs. 174–175).
- **The overlay.** His care for Monica began partly as an overlay of Arc (V06 obs. 167; V08 obs. 201).
- **The method.** After V09 his method persists: borrowed faces, mixed truths, secrets held over allies, a readiness to choose no means (V10 obs. 088, 143; V11 obs. 073–078; V12 obs. 089).
- **The old name.** At V12, facing loss, he whispers Arc's name (V12 obs. 211).

On this view the devotion is unchanged, and only its object has moved from a dead boy to a living girl.

The monograph's answer is partial, and it concedes much of this. The method does persist; that is part of the thesis. What changes is the aim. Before V09 his devotion aims at his own erasure: a name remembered, his death as a villain (V08 obs. 095, 260). After V09 it aims at his own joy, with joy as the stated limit on his means (V09 obs. 144; V11 obs. 077). The volumes mark this by the narration's statement that the obsessive fire is gone (V09 reading §6.7). But the counterargument cannot be refuted at V12. Whether the overlay has ended is not stated (§8 item 5).

**3. "The comedy of the apprentice softens crimes the books never judge."** Isaac planned his grandfather's murder and suborned Emmanuel, who died in Clockford's trap. He ordered Hubert killed. He nearly strangled Elliott as a boy (V08 obs. 090–094, 190–191, 220; V06 obs. 149). The settlement that saves him is a staged untruth that blames a dead man (SW-C-0069). The volumes present it as wise, not as a wrong (V09 reading §8.3). After V09 the comedy of aprons and jealousy dominates his scenes. The point is well founded. This monograph records the absence of judgement as the volumes' choice, not as an acquittal. Isaac's own self-judgement is harsher than the narration's (V10 obs. 141, 145), but the books never weigh Emmanuel's death against him.

**4. "His love is the romance of the series, and Monica's indifference is a delay."** Nothing in the boundary warrants this. Monica's feelings toward Isaac are not stated. Her unnamed distress concerns Cyril. The volume names none of these feelings (SW-C-0093). Bridget's belief that Monica loves Cyril is Bridget's (V09 reading §6.6). This monograph registers no reciprocity and no prediction.

## 11. Limits of this argument

**Boundary.** The argument rests on Japanese LN V01–V12 and the companions IV -after-, IX -extra- and both -another- volumes, as read in the snapshot at commit 60b21a7. No web serial, adaptation, translation, wiki or commentary is used. Paratext is cited only as paratext: the character cards at V04, AFTER04 and V08, and the plates at V08 and V09 (V04 obs. 125; AFTER04 obs. 164; V08 obs. 007, reading §7.5).
