# Louis Miller, the Barrier Mage

## Thesis

Louis Miller is the series' most consistent speaker of the language of use. He calls his junior a tool, his disciple a decoy, his spirit a business partner and his engagement a means to a seat. His conduct keeps contradicting that language. He takes the blame for Monica's failures, buys his disciple a suit, flies across the country on his own mana for the woman he loves, and in the end tells her dead benefactor that he has a family.

The numbered track shows this gap from outside. It gives a polite, beautiful, violent man whose investments leak out through comedy and through short secret episodes. The two prequel volumes, read after V12 but set before V01, show it from inside. They give a brothel boy whose effort always answers a slight, whose goals stay near at hand until one person makes them long, and whose rise is carried by people who believed in him before he noticed them.

Read together, the two views expose one mechanism. Rutherford names it in the upper prequel: Louis is quick to see malice and blind to goodwill (SW-C-0097; ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 183). The series tests that blindness against him again and again. He misreads Monica as a witch who will never ask for help (SW-C-0033). He hides his love for Rosalie behind a sarcasm that she overhears and believes (SW-C-0100). He cannot see that Adolf Farron wants nothing from him but attention (SW-C-0085). His development is not a reform of his violence, which the books never condemn. It is a widening of what he can see and what he is willing to carry.

## 1. Two views and three orders

Louis is the one major figure whose inside view arrives last. That fact governs every argument in this monograph, so the orders are set out first.

**Source and reading order.** The project admitted the units in this order: V01–V04, IV -after- (AFTER04), V05–V09, IX -extra- (EXTRA09), V10–V12, then -another- upper (ANOTHER-UPPER) and -another- lower (ANOTHER-LOWER) (checkpoint §2). A reader following that order meets Louis first as Monica's coercive senior Sage, and learns his childhood, his schooling and the terms of his marriage only after the main story has stopped.

**Diegetic order.** Louis's life runs the other way. The brothel, Minerva, the Magic Corps, the Sage selection and the wedding all precede V01. The AFTER04 prologue and interludes and the first chapter of EXTRA09 are also retrospective: they fall in his first year or so as a Sage (AFTER04 obs. 002, 006; EXTRA09 obs. 015).

**Revelation order.** Some of the earliest facts of his life are disclosed latest of all. That a brothel woman, Vivian, asked Rutherford to take the boy away before he could be sold is the last thing the lower volume tells (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 170). That Adolf started the rumour which sent Louis to confront Birdland Verde is shown to the reader in a secret episode that Louis never sees (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 215; SW-K-0149).

Prequel evidence below is marked as retrospective. It explains the numbered track; it does not revise a frozen numbered reading (checkpoint §4.2, item 3).

The interior access is uneven in a second way. In the numbered track Louis does get viewpoint scenes, but they are short and mostly strategic: the secret episodes of V01, V03 and V04, the epilogue of V02, and brief passages in V07, V08 and V11 (V01 obs. 116–119; V02 obs. 096; V03 obs. 128–131; V04 obs. 119–120; V07 obs. 133–136; V08 obs. 185–188). They show what he calculates. The prequels give continuous interior narration that is mostly about what he wants and fears. A reader who meets the calculating mind first may take the calculation for the whole man. The prequels correct that impression, but they were written and read later, and the monograph keeps that in view (section 13).

## 2. The outside view: Louis as the numbered track first shows him

### 2.1 The polite coercer

V01 introduces Louis through register. He speaks an elaborate, slightly old-fashioned polite style, addresses Monica as his colleague of the same intake, and smiles throughout. The politeness never drops; the grammar underneath it hardens from invitation to bare command within one line (V01 reading §6.3; SW-S-0016). The narrator calls his remark that no one would suspect so drab a girl abuse (V01 obs. 010). Monica's consent is narrated as something her body does: an objection, a gripped shoulder, a reflex nod, and then a mention that failure might mean execution (V01 obs. 011).

Two details in that first scene matter for everything that follows. First, Monica reads his brooch at a glance and names it what it is, a device for watching Felix rather than protecting him; Louis answers with a mock-scriptural motto to the effect that what is not found out does no harm (V01 obs. 009). Second, the secret episode at the end of the volume shows that the threat of execution was a lever. He tells Lynn he thinks it unlikely (V01 obs. 118).

### 2.2 The patron behind the insult

The same secret episode turns the bully into something harder to summarize. Louis remembers being beaten by Monica in their selection duel, completely, by a girl who wept and panicked. He regards her as a monster in power. He dragged her to the dragon to give her confidence. He finds her humility an insult to his own defeat (V01 obs. 117–118; SW-S-0017). He also feeds her sweets when she flags (V01 reading §7.3). The V01 reading's verdict holds for the whole series: the volume does not ask the reader to choose between the bully and the patron. It shows a man who uses people and invests in them, often in one act (SW-C-0017).

V02 extends the pattern. Louis buys Monica a plain day dress and coat as a reward, half fondly thinking she would prefer a mathematics book, and dictates a message about carrots and sticks that Lynn delivers word for word (V02 obs. 009). In the same scene he explains that he enrolled his own disciple at Serendia as an unknowing decoy for Felix (the name under which Isaac Walker lived until V08 disclosed him), and answers Lynn's charge of heartlessness by saying that testing a pupil is a teacher's duty (V02 obs. 010; V02 reading §6.2).

### 2.3 The verdict and the warning

Louis is also the source of the most influential wrong judgment in the early volumes. In the V02 epilogue he notices that Monica believes his promise at once, and this troubles him, because he chose her partly for her distrust. He warns her that she is getting too attached and that Monica Norton is a temporary person (V02 obs. 096; SW-C-0024). In the V03 secret episode he tells Casey, from his memory of the black-dragon campaign, that Monica fears and hates people from the bottom of her heart, that she is a heartless witch from whom no feeling should be expected, and that this is why he chose her (V03 obs. 130–131). V04 prints the verdict on its opening plate, as paratext (V04 obs. 124), and has Louis repeat it to Rutherford (V04 obs. 050).

The verdict is half right, and the series knows it. Monica's numerical vision is real; it began as a child's defence and is now a technique she can choose (SW-C-0033; V04 reading §8.1). What Louis calls heartlessness she can no longer apply to people she knows (V04 obs. 088, 096). The ledger records the pattern as a form: a knowing adult closes a volume with a judgment the reader has just seen contradicted (SW-F-0021).

### 2.4 The family man

From the start the numbered track also gives Louis a domestic side that his public manner does not predict. He adores his wife Rosalie, a physician, and has a modest, homely house (V01 reading §7.3). He is a giddy father-to-be (V01 obs. 094; V02 reading §6.2). He waits through the birth hugging his knees in a corridor, by the V08 character reference, which is paratext (V08 reading §7.5). In V09 his infant daughter cries whenever he holds her (V09 obs. 045, 158). When Adolf escapes in V10, he moves his wife and daughter, Rosalie and Leonora, to safety before warning Monica (V10 obs. 177). In V11 he trusts their guard only to Owen Wright, because, northern-raised, he trusts only those whose betrayal he would blame on himself (V11 obs. 014). In V11 and V12 he pads an injury report to take his family to a hot spring, and Rosalie finds out (V11 obs. 213; V12 obs. 117).

These scenes are short. They matter because they show, before the prequels do, that the man who speaks only of interest acts from attachment.

## 3. The inside view: the prequel's run (retrospective)

### 3.1 A frame of results

The upper prequel opens on its end state. Louis, in his mid-twenties, is the youngest head of the Magic Corps in its history, with a braid and a monocle. He traps a herd of dragons in a reflecting dome, the Mirror Prison, and summons the wind Spirit-King inside it (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 006–008). That night he counts his savings, including twelve large silver coins kept in an empty jam jar, cheers that he can buy a house in the capital, and vows over cheap liquor to become a Sage (obs. 011–012). The narration adds that this refined man came from a snowbound village in the north and grew up in a run-down brothel (obs. 013).

The run then supplies a cause for each element of the frame: the coins, the house, the seat and the manners (ANOTHER-UPPER reading §6.1; SW-F-0063). The lower volume closes the frame with the story of a boy who became a Sage and gained a family (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 173; SW-F-0068).

### 3.2 States in order

The prequel readings record Louis's states in sequence. In plain terms:

1. **A boy with no ambition.** He sees himself as a stray dog living day to day and expects the brothel's owner never to let him go (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 030). Asked what he will do as a mage, he says earn money and eat marmalade (obs. 033). The narration states that at eleven he does not yet imagine the Sages (obs. 034).
2. **A delinquent.** He answers every slight out of proportion: three boys beaten over spilt marmalade, a roommate thrown into a cesspit, an enemy hung naked from a tree and smoked (obs. 046, 083, 159).
3. **A student defeated into theory.** Caged by Rutherford's earth Spirit-King, he grasps the gap and next day brings the barrier teacher Majer reports that reconstruct an unpublished formula (obs. 104–108).
4. **A friend who learns he is blind to goodwill.** Overhearing Rutherford's judgment, he recalls Rosalie, Lionel, Owen and the people at Goa's diner, and the words stab (obs. 183–184). He then apologises to Rosalie (obs. 187).
5. **A lover who wants one person.** Remembering Shona's words, he finds that what he wants is Rosalie's hands tending him (obs. 202), and declares himself (obs. 205).
6. **A graduate resolved to become a Sage only to marry her** (obs. 212).

The lower volume continues the sequence: refined in manner and unchanged in temper; frustrated that the Sages are far away; a master who takes an apprentice to put Minerva in his debt; blind to how his refinement looks; cunning in an investigation; beaten by Monica and conceding; speaking again in his own voice; carrying for others; and a husband who reports to the dead that he has a family (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §7.1; SW-S-0245, SW-S-0246).

## 4. Development and state transitions

Each transition below has a mechanism the text supplies. They are listed in diegetic order. The reader learned them in a different order, as section 1 shows.

### 4.1 From brothel boy to scholarship student

Louis was born in Dangrots to a prostitute; his father is unknown. His mother died when he was small, the women raised him, and the miserly owner taught him letters and accounts (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 013, 017). He worked as odd-job boy and bouncer, and as a boy he spoke with a northern accent (obs. 015–016).

The mechanism of his departure is a slight. Rutherford, stranded by an avalanche, paralyses him with smoke and slaps him. Furious at being looked down on, Louis casts a wind spell he has learned from Rutherford's dropped textbook (obs. 024–025). Told it is half a year's work at Minerva, he scoffs; challenged to learn four spells in a week, he learns every spell in the book, planning and analysing his failures (obs. 027–028). Rutherford offers a recommendation (obs. 029).

What the boy does not know is disclosed only in the last unit the project read. Vivian had asked Rutherford to take him before the doomed brothel could sell him, and Rutherford refused her money and would only recommend him (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 170; SW-K-0158). The women sent him off with a jar holding twelve silver coins, one from each, and a label telling him not to come back (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 031). He keeps those coins unspent through his rise (obs. 011). The transition is thus carried by goodwill he does not see at the time, and the books make that the point: their kindness, he reflects at the end, was always hard to see until too late (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §6.6).

### 4.2 From delinquent to barrier specialist

At Minerva Louis is quick, rational and cautious, by Rutherford's judgment, and grows by being beaten (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 048). He also wins fights by violence until the rules change. A new battle barrier nullifies physical attacks (obs. 101–102). Rutherford, inside it, cages him with columns of earth rising beneath the open floor of his hemisphere (obs. 104). Louis learns that his teacher was the Rock-Cavern Mage and that his smoke passes through every barrier (obs. 105–106).

Defeat becomes theory. He reconstructs the nullification formula from a kick, reasons that large barriers must draw on dense land, and turns rules into loopholes: sand too small to count as an attack, a hold that cannot be told from a handshake, a small shield used as a fist, a gag to stop chanting (obs. 108, 111, 123). Majer lectures him for an hour, confiscates the liquor he offered, and admits him to her remedial class (obs. 109). The mature forms follow: the barrier fist, the shield used to ram in flight, the reflecting barrier, and the Mirror Prison that wins him the Corps (obs. 196, 208; ANOTHER-UPPER reading §6.6).

The numbered track had already shown the result. At V09 Monica tells her helpers his real style is barrier punching, kicking and ramming, and Louis uses the rules of the duel barrier by tripping and choking (V09 obs. 063, 065). The prequel supplies the origin of exactly those tricks. The V01 world record that the duelling barrier had been made to nullify physical attacks "after a past abuse" names no student (SW-W-0043). The upper prequel shows Louis's battles as the occasion of the barrier's replacement (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 089, 091, 102). Reading Louis as that unnamed past abuser is an inference across units; it fits, but no text states it.

### 4.3 From student to Corps head and Barrier Mage

Louis's epithet is presented as a teacher's lineage. He names Majer, who has no epithet, the Barrier Witch, and she laughs aloud for the first time (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 138). He credits her above his own name on his festival presentation (obs. 151) and promises to call himself the Barrier Mage so that the neighbourhood will know who taught him (obs. 158). The book's practice, though, turns the defensive art into a weapon (SW-C-0098).

He passes the senior-mage examination, presents the Mirror Prison and joins the Corps' Third Skirmish Unit (obs. 207–208). His first dragon kills his fist method: a barrier opened inside a wound is too slow (obs. 213). The lower volume's prologue then gives the step that makes him head. In a once-a-decade flood at Nadin he saves Sally, the diner owner's daughter, now a mother, and holds a failing embankment with the largest barrier of his life until Birdland can tame the river. For this he is appointed head of the Corps and receives the title of the Barrier Mage (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 006–013). The promise made to a retired teacher thus receives institutional form, and the books close the circle when Majer, rescued from a collapsing workshop by his Glass Phantom Castle, calls the barrier worthy of the Barrier Mage and says she had a good teacher (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 130–131).

How he learned Spirit-King summoning and met Lynn, beyond the contract's motive, is not shown (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §8.5; SW-P-0071, open).

### 4.4 From Corps head to Sage

The obstacle is political. Louis was not called to the previous selection although he could summon a Spirit-King and held an upper-spirit contract. Minerva's professors did not recommend him, mainly for his school conduct (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 020). Felix tells his friend Elliott that their grandfather, Duke Clockford, deliberately crushed Louis's candidacy as a commoner first-prince man (obs. 036). Rutherford tells Carla that any recommendation of Louis would be crushed (obs. 067). Louis does not know this until told (SW-K-0159).

The selection itself turns on two mechanisms. The first is Adolf's crime: pushed by Clockford's faction, he pushes Rosalie from a roof by remote magic, coerces Owen through his father's hospital care, and poisons him (SW-C-0102; ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 063, 136–137). Louis solves it by the cunning of the brothel, controlling information and attending to a friend's habits: the poison was in tea, and Owen drinks coffee (obs. 083–085, 136–137). The second is Monica. She opens the forest battle with a wordless wind Spirit-King summons, fells Adolf with a wordless remote spell, and defeats Louis (obs. 107–113). He judges that she should be a Sage (obs. 114). He becomes one only because two seats open (obs. 143, 156). The lower-volume reading notes that the book does not present this as luck alone, but leaves the Sages' deliberation unshown (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §8.2).

### 4.5 Husband, father, Sage at work

Half a year after the investiture Louis and Rosalie marry (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 165). On the honeymoon they visit the abandoned village. The brothel is gone. He leaves marmalade on Shona's grave and tells her he has come to boast that he has a family (obs. 171–172).

The numbered track then shows the married Sage at work. In the AFTER04 prologue, half a year into his seat, he drags Monica and Ray on a cart, assigns Monica the chantless seals to spare his own throat, ranks a date with his fiancée above all work, praises Monica's ability without irony, and flies to the capital on his own mana for the woman he loves (AFTER04 obs. 002–023). In EXTRA09's first chapter, about a year after Monica's appointment, he wraps her up and flies her to a ceremony, finds her living on dried fish in water, gives her jam as a universal seasoning, and resolves to drag her into the next dragon subjugation (EXTRA09 obs. 015–023). Then V01 begins.

Within the numbered track his state changes little and his roles accumulate. He is the king's agent who reads his order as surveillance (V01 obs. 119; SW-K-0005), the Sage who proves by his own inquiry that the second prince is false and plans to denounce Clockford (V08 obs. 185–187; SW-S-0170), a new father (V08 obs. 189), the opponent waylaid and outfought at the Supreme Council (V09 obs. 048–072; SW-S-0183), the hunter of the escaped Adolf (V10 obs. 176–177; V11), and the showman who turns a crisis into a display of Sages' battle (V12 obs. 196).

### 4.6 Master of Glen

Glen Dudley enters the lower volume as Louis once entered the upper: a boy of great untrained power taken out of confinement by a master who wants a debt repaid (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §6.3). After Glen's mana runaway destroys Rutherford's laboratory, Louis offers him lifelong confinement or apprenticeship, and names a third choice: break the building and escape (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 038–041). He teaches by exposure and contempt that hide care. He tells the frightened boy that his runaway is no enemy and that his master's barriers will not break, and he limits him to fire and flight (obs. 044–045).

The numbered track learned these facts first from Glen's side. Glen was swept into the apprenticeship (AFTER04 obs. 099), enrolled as an unknowing decoy (V02 obs. 010; V04 obs. 065), and given his master's maxim that money and violence solve most difficulties (AFTER04 obs. 075, 099). He respects his master's skill and doubts his character (EXTRA09 obs. 111). In V11 Owen tells Louis to rethink his teaching (V11 obs. 006). In V12 Glen passes the lower-mage examination and Louis orders him to hunt a dragon (V12 obs. 155).

## 5. Motive and self-report against conduct

Louis explains himself often, and his explanations are usually true in part and misleading in sum.

### 5.1 Effort out of refusal to be looked down on

The prequel's narration and its plate agree on the root motive. The plate, which is paratext, calls him a hard worker because he hates to lose (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 004). The narration repeats the idea at his greatest effort, calling him a genius of effort at his best when proving someone wrong (ANOTHER-UPPER reading §6.2). Every effort in the run answers a slight: the first spell answers Rutherford's contempt, the week of spells a challenge, the assignment boxes an exemption made to look impossible, the barrier reports a beating, the examination and the week of manners Birdland's outburst (obs. 025, 027–028, 036, 064, 107–108, 207, 211).

The numbered track already had the motive in compressed form. Under the V11 fog Louis regresses to the middle-division delinquent, and the narration explains that his violence came from refusing to be looked down on, with a girlish face, under a village code (V11 obs. 146). The prequel shows that the same refusal powered his study. SW-C-0095 records this: the rise is driven by refusal to be looked down on and spent on near goals until one person makes it a long one.

His goals stay near. Marmalade; making Rutherford eat his words; top marks so that Rosalie will call him amazing; beating his teacher (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 033, 049, 066, 109). He knows it. Beside Owen's dream of the Corps he feels he has no future in view, and is privately ashamed (obs. 080, 097). To MacLaggan he says he wants only to feed himself, that magic is just a useful skill, and that flattering nobles is the one thing he will never do (obs. 098).

### 5.2 The Sage's seat as a marriage condition

The book's turn gives him a person, not a vocation. When Birdland hears that Louis means to marry Rosalie, he loses his temper and demands a man of refinement with a house in the capital, fit to become a Sage, and shouts that Rosalie wants to marry a prince (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 210). Louis swears to become a Sage. The narration's last word on the run is that he resolved to become one only to marry her (obs. 212). The upper reading rejects the alternative that his ambition is a public vocation: the prologue's people praise him as the country's guardian, but the vow follows his count of his savings (ANOTHER-UPPER reading §8.2; obs. 010–012).

The comic reversal has a sting the reader alone can see. The rumour that Birdland meant to marry Rosalie to a successor, which sent Louis flying to him, was Adolf's (obs. 215; SW-K-0149). Louis's central resolve is the product of a rival's spite, and he never learns it.

The motive does not stay purely instrumental. By the lower volume Louis feels that his gains are not enough, because the Sages are far (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 018). He offers himself to Lionel as a guard so as to enter society and find backers (obs. 024), which sits awkwardly with the boy who would never flatter nobles. In V11 he asks Owen to become a Sage (V11 obs. 008), and in V10 and V11 he searches for a new candidate (V10 obs. 174). The seat taken as a condition has become an office he works at.

### 5.3 "The order is reversed"

The decisive gap between self-report and conduct in the lower volume runs in an unusual direction. Louis hides love behind interest, not interest behind love. A guild executive pities him for engaging himself in order to become a Sage. Louis answers with a sarcasm: he would not be engaged were she not the Waterworks Mage's daughter (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 066). Rosalie and Glen overhear it, and Rosalie weeps, because she knows how she is thought of (obs. 122). Lynn's literal-minded report, that Louis said a contract helps the selection and that he would do anything to be a Sage, completes the misreading (obs. 095). Rosalie ends the engagement (obs. 116).

When Glen confesses, Louis's reply is that the order is reversed: he wants to be a Sage in order to marry her (obs. 125; SW-CR-0912). Nate supplies the judgment the book endorses: Louis's own conduct produced the rumours, and his transformation looked suspicious (obs. 126; SW-CR-0914).

### 5.4 The same pattern elsewhere

The pattern recurs in the numbered track with other recipients.

- **Faction.** In V02 Louis says he is Lionel's schoolfriend and calls himself a first-prince man chiefly because he dislikes Duke Clockford and the second prince (V02 reading §6.15). In V06 he repeats that he does not back the first prince (V06 obs. 056), and in V07 that he does not need the first prince to reign (V07 obs. 137). The lower prequel shows the friendship underneath. At the soirée he resolves to stand with Lionel and to bear the sly work Lionel cannot do (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 035), and in the carriage he says that, having nothing to carry, he will carry part of what Rosalie and Lionel bear (obs. 150; SW-C-0104). In V08 he is angry on Lionel's behalf when he learns the second prince is false (V08 obs. 187).
- **Monica.** In V04 he tells her that her humanity does not matter to him either way, in the same scene in which he takes the blame for the festival's failure as his own tactical error (V04 obs. 096). The V04 reading records this as character complexity, not contradiction (V04 reading §8.8).
- **Method.** In V11 he says he learned connections, the third term of his adult method, from losing to Monica at the council (V11 obs. 183). The prequel shows that his whole rise was carried by connections he did not use as method: Rutherford, the women, Goa's diner, Owen, Rosalie, Lionel (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 160). What V09 taught him, on his own account, was to treat as a resource what had always been a gift.

The general rule, then, is that Louis's self-reports describe his acts in the vocabulary of interest, and his conduct shows attachment. This is not hypocrisy in the ordinary sense; he is not claiming virtue he lacks. He claims a hardness that his acts keep failing to display.

## 6. Coercion mixed with investment: Louis and Monica

This is the relationship by which most readers first know Louis, and the one whose origin they learn last. The dyad is argued in both directions in the relationship arguments; here the subject is what it shows about Louis.

### 6.1 The first meeting (retrospective)

In diegetic order the relationship begins at the selection, when Monica is fifteen, more than a year before the Worgan campaign that precedes V01 (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 064; EXTRA09 obs. 015, 024). Louis greets the candidate politely; she is hiding in a curtain and begs him not to kill her (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 100). She asks for a handicap or she will win (obs. 101). Then she beats him (obs. 107–113). Rutherford, to Mary, calls Louis a genius of effort and Monica a genius of numbers (obs. 104; SW-C-0103).

The two most characteristic acts of the numbered track's Louis appear here at their source. In the waiting room after the botched rehearsal he tells her she won by skill and must not be servile (obs. 159). He diagnoses that she trusts neither herself nor others and answers her question of how he can believe in himself: because others believed in him. He names his benefactors; she names an adoptive mother and one friend (obs. 160). He calls her his fellow-intake, and she fears him a little less (obs. 161). Then, skipping the parade to fly Rosalie over the roofs, he has Monica keep a wordless illusion of him in the carriage to repay a "debt" (obs. 163). Insistence on her self-respect and coercion through obligation arrive together on the first day (SW-CR-0834, SW-CR-0850).

### 6.2 The mission years

In the numbered track the fusion of use and investment is constant (SW-C-0017, strengthened at almost every unit). The ledger's discriminating test is whether Louis ever acts against her interests (SW-C-0017, discriminating observation). The evidence divides.

**Against her interests, or at her cost:**

- He coerces her into the mission under the threat of execution, a threat he privately discounts (V01 obs. 011, 118).
- He builds her cover from a romance novel and later tells the Nortons, and a Kerbeck earl, that she longs to be bullied by villains (V01 obs. 012; AFTER04 obs. 151; V12 obs. 014).
- He chose her for her distrust and warns her against attachment (V02 obs. 096; SW-C-0024).
- In V07 he keeps her on the mission while withholding information and testing her, and proposes that she seal witnesses' memories by mind interference; the narration says he is testing her (V07 obs. 136, 138).
- In V08 he keeps his plan to denounce Clockford from her because he thinks her too soft (V08 obs. 188, 210).

**For her, or at his own cost:**

- He takes the blame for the festival's failure as his own tactical mistake (V04 obs. 096).
- In V02 he hands her his wife's handkerchief, with orders to wash and iron it (V02 obs. 099); in V04 he gives her the handkerchief Casey sent (V04 obs. 097).
- He concedes Casey's life to keep Monica's cooperation (V02 obs. 096).
- In V06 he licenses her anger: she seldom grew angry only because she did not care, and fighting without anger made her uncanny (V06 obs. 184–185; SW-C-0051).
- In V10 he spares her a duty because she is busy with the chalice's rights (V10 obs. 012–013); in V11 he reads all her papers and warns her to time them for the textbook makers (V11 obs. 196).

EXTRA09's first chapter tests the investment reading hardest. There, about a year after her appointment, Louis does not know why Monica hides away and is not interested; his concern is that his conqueror's state reflects on him (EXTRA09 obs. 020; SW-R-0158). The investment in her self-respect is real, but it began as an investment in the standing of the person who beat him. That is consistent with V01's secret episode, in which her humility insults his defeat (V01 obs. 117), and with the lower volume, in which her servility after victory is what he forbids (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 159).

### 6.3 The table lesson and its return

Louis also teaches Monica, though he is not her master. In V05's prologue he names Bradford's tell and Monica's, and says the game begins before one sits at the table, and whoever trusts another's account of the rules deserves to lose (V05 obs. 003–005; SW-C-0048). Monica loses her negotiation with Felix by forgetting it (V05 reading §6.1; SW-F-0031). She had quoted the same lesson to Elliott in V03 (V03 obs. 017). In V08 Louis gives her a second political lesson, that whatever suits the powerful becomes the truth (V08 obs. 252), and in V09 she uses it (V09 obs. 096).

Then the lessons turn on him. Monica keeps the father's diary from him because he would publish everything and see Isaac executed (V08 obs. 253), and plans to keep him away from the council because he would expose everything and see through her (V09 obs. 049). At the council her helpers waylay him with a plan, and her message to him is his own table lesson (V09 obs. 104; SW-CR-0378). The narration names his error: he assumed the Witch would never ask anyone for help (V09 obs. 072; SW-C-0072). He admits his second clean defeat by her (V09 obs. 137; SW-C-0033).

### 6.4 Why the model failed

The prequel, read after this, supplies the reason the error was Louis's in particular. His own answer to Monica at the selection was that he believes in himself because others believed in him (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 160). His own transformation at school came when he overheard that he could not see goodwill (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 183–184). The ledger draws the connection when it strengthens SW-C-0033 with the upper volume's judgment (SW-CR-0756). The man whose whole life had been carried by people who believed in him assumed that Monica, whom he had diagnosed as trusting no one, would never learn the same thing. His model of Monica was a model of his own younger self, sharp on fear and malice, and blind at exactly the point where he himself had changed.

## 7. Money, violence and connections

### 7.1 The maxim in three forms

SW-C-0043 is the claim that changed most under later evidence, and its revisions track Louis's method.

1. **AFTER04 (registered).** Glen's case rests on his master's maxim that money and violence solve most difficulties, which an interlude shows Louis delivering over a dead fire dragon; Glen prefers Cyril's ethic of patient effort (AFTER04 obs. 075, 099). The narration calls the teaching the speech of someone who has suffered and thrown off much unreason (AFTER04 reading §6.9). Monica, who also drifted into magic, takes Cyril's side (AFTER04 obs. 079).
2. **V11 (revised).** Louis names his adult method as money, violence and connections, says he learned the third from losing to Monica at the council, and sets what he has gained since he was poor against Adolf's reliance on borrowed power (V11 obs. 179–183; SW-CR-0593). The money buys Owen's barrier tool; the connections are MacLaggan, Bradford, Ugo, Owen and Lynn (V11 obs. 186; V11 reading §6.3).
3. **ANOTHER-UPPER (revised).** The prequel shows the maxim's holder as himself a genius of effort driven by refusal to lose, so that the opposition with Cyril lies in motive and display rather than in effort (SW-CR-0766). V09 had already hinted this: Louis was the first Minerva brat, and his strength is effort and experience (V09 obs. 057; SW-CR-0373).

### 7.2 Money

Money is the thread that runs from the first page of Louis's life to the last. The boy plans to keep Shona's empty jam jar for savings (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 019). At Minerva the scholarship covers fees but not consumables, so he works lunch and evening at Goa's diner and saves his silver (obs. 054–055). The festival dress is decided by the pay (obs. 140). The twelve coins stay in the jar (obs. 011). He creams his chapped hands, wears gloves over his calluses and grows his hair as a sign of wealth so that he will never again be called unsightly (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 016). He buys a house in the capital's best quarter with a room ready for Rosalie (obs. 048).

In the numbered track money becomes a tool he spends freely and a joke he shares with the reader. He bills Glen's formal wear to the king as mission expenses (EXTRA09 obs. 110). He pays for Owen's barrier tool himself (V11 obs. 186). V10 records that Lynn's contract stone cost him his dream of a house (V10 obs. 081); section 11 records the seam this leaves against the prequel.

### 7.3 Violence

The books judge Louis's violence comically and do not condemn it (SW-C-0097). The upper prequel shows punishment, but its one weighty judgment concerns goodwill, not violence (ANOTHER-UPPER reading §8.3). The lower prequel frames his maiming of Adolf as a promise kept from school and as vengeance for Rosalie and Owen, and does not condemn it (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 140; reading §8.3). V11 gives the violence a history in poverty and a village code, plays it for comedy, and through Monica marks it as a model to avoid (V11 reading §8.3).

Two details complicate the comic surface. First, the boy's answer to theft at the brothel was to beat the thief until he never tried again; only then came peace (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 085). The violence is a learned method for making a place safe. Second, the lower volume shows him renouncing it for a purpose: in the selection he resolves not to fight like a delinquent because Birdland is watching (obs. 106), and in V12 he behaves because his father-in-law is present (V12 obs. 181). He can stop. He chooses when.

### 7.4 Connections

The third term arrives late in his own account and early in his life. Goa rejoices that Louis has friends (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 174). Lionel and Rosalie secretly explain his incidents to Rutherford (obs. 183; ANOTHER-UPPER reading §7.11). Lionel gives him a monocle hoping it will curb his violence (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 017). Owen's own account, once he recovers, supplies the last pieces of Adolf's crime (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 154). In V11 the method is completed by people Louis has gathered: MacLaggan guards the capital at his foresight, Bradford provides for a missed shot, Owen deflects the last fireball, Lynn scatters the sparks (V11 obs. 182, 185–188).

The contrast with Adolf is what V11 makes explicit. Adolf relies on borrowed power, the bell made and given by an unnamed pale-gold girl, another's formula, an old grimoire (V11 obs. 179). Louis relies on people. He tells the bound Adolf that he never became a rival because he aimed at the people around Louis and always ran at the end (V11 obs. 189; SW-C-0085).

## 8. Refinement, voice and written speech

### 8.1 What the readings can and cannot say

All evidence is written Japanese prose. Nothing is known of voice, timbre or delivery (checkpoint §4.2, item 5). What the readings record is register: self-reference, address, sentence endings, dialect marks and the narration's comments on accent. No Japanese is quoted here; the readings hold the verified fragments.

### 8.2 The polite register of the numbered track

The V01 speech table gives Louis the formal first-person pronoun; he addresses Monica as his colleague of the same intake or with a polite "you", and uses a contemptuous word for "little girl" when she is absent. His sentences use elaborate, slightly archaic polite endings; under pressure the politeness thins to bare imperatives; he has a mock-scriptural motto (V01 reading §6.13; SW-S-0016). Lynn speaks a matching expressionless formal politeness (V01 reading §6.13). The joke of the register is that its courtesy never interferes with its force.

The numbered track also lets the register break. He calls Rutherford an old geezer (SW-S-0075; V04 obs. 040). In V09, attacked at the council, he swears at the "monster girl" and announces punishment time for the brats in rough speech (V09 obs. 048, 055). In V11, under the fog, he appears with a northern accent and asks where Adolf Farron is (V11 obs. 119), calls Monica "shorty" (V11 obs. 121) and shouts a schoolboy insult at Adolf (V11 obs. 124). When Monica casts his own Mirror Prison and returns his monocle, he thanks his colleague and is the Barrier Mage again (V11 obs. 148–149; SW-S-0218).

### 8.3 The prequel's account: a sealed accent

The prequel turns these breaks into a history. As a boy Louis speaks with a northern accent (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 016). He knows curses only in the imperial tongue and uses it to threaten his first roommate (obs. 037–038). His handwriting is neat because he wrote letters for the women (obs. 064). After Birdland's outburst he asks Lionel to teach him upper-class speech and manners, and studies posture, cutlery and dancing for a week (obs. 211). At graduation he begins to insult his teacher and corrects the word to "master"; the narration says the first Bad Boy of Minerva sealed his accent (obs. 212). In the Corps the new speech still makes him taciturn (ANOTHER-UPPER reading §6.1).

The lower volume opens by showing both registers at once. At the Nadin flood he speaks with an upper-class accent to the townsfolk, then, drenched by Lynn's wind, roars an insult at the maid, and only then does Sally recognise him (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 008–009). Nate is still correcting his speech at the celebration (obs. 014).

### 8.4 Refinement misread

SW-C-0100 states the lower volume's argument. The refinement Louis adopted to meet Birdland's terms estranges Rosalie and convinces the Corps that the engagement serves the seat. At Birdland's house he greets father and daughter as a perfect gentleman and Rosalie freezes; the narration says he did not notice how shaken she was (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 056, 059). The Corps assume a political marriage (obs. 061). With amnesia, Rosalie remembers only a northern-accented boy she loved, and feels guilty toward her gentle fiancé because of him (obs. 089–090, 094). Louis keeps the truth back, thinking a gentle fiancé better for her now (obs. 091).

The cure is a return of voice. In the carriage, after unsealing her memory and explaining her father's conditions, he drops the polish and tells her in his old accent that he was never troubled by her (obs. 147–151). She says she fell for a bold delinquent, and both had waited for years (obs. 153). The ledger names the form: refinement as mask, the northern accent as truth (SW-F-0067).

The monograph adds one qualification. The refinement does not end. Louis in V01 still speaks the elaborate polite register, still wears his hair long, and still uses the monocle Lionel gave him. Read after the prequel, the numbered track's register is a costume that the books have taught the reader to see through, and the northern accent that surfaces under the V11 fog is the voice the lower volume calls true. Both remain his. The refinement survives as a means; the books place love in what it was meant to hide (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §6.2).

### 8.5 Hair, face and the word "womanly"

Louis's appearance is a running source of violence and comedy. His girlish face is like his mother's, by the women's account, and the brothel owner threatened to put him in a dress (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 057). Called girl-faced by Adolf, he goes cold (obs. 145), and years later stamps on Adolf's groin, as he once promised (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 140). V02 records that he hates being called womanly and keeps his hair long (V02 obs. 028; V02 reading §7.3). In V09 Robert calls him a queen (V09 obs. 069). Yet he dresses as a girl to work Sally's stall for pay (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 140), and he grows his hair as a sign of wealth (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 016). The long hair the numbered track presents as vanity is, by the prequel's account, part of the costume of a man who would never again be called unsightly.

Louis behaves differently toward each person, and the differences are consistent. The table gives the conditioning; the prose that follows argues the main cases.

### 9.1 Rosalie

Rosalie is the person around whom Louis's ambition is built, and the one before whom his concealments hurt most. They meet in the library, where she is the first person not afraid of him (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 059). She has twenty mana against a middle-division mean of seventy, and is a Sage's daughter (obs. 113–114). When she follows him into the restricted forest to stop him and collapses from mana poisoning, he feels for the first time the weight of having involved someone (obs. 115–117). He promises to show her that one can win with little mana (obs. 122), and her later choice of medicine is framed as made possible by that demonstration (obs. 199).

The relationship also teaches him to see the goodwill Rutherford said he missed. His rural distrust of tap water insults the waterworks her father built; after the overheard judgment he gives her a flower hairpin and apologises (obs. 179–180, 187). When she leaves Minerva, he feels lonely at not sharing her burden (obs. 200) and declares himself (obs. 205).

In the lower volume Rosalie is the victim of his refinement and of Adolf's crime. Her father's drunken order destroyed all their letters for years (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 060; SW-K-0157), so she reads the polished stranger without the context Louis assumes she has. His care for her during the amnesia uses the skills of the brothel: he cooks and tends her hair, as tending the women was his work (obs. 081). He tells her he has no family and has never told her of the village or the brothel (obs. 077). In the carriage he tells her his birth (obs. 150). The relationship ends the prequel as marriage (SW-R-0205).

In the numbered track Rosalie appears mostly as the doctor who treats Louis as a patient and sees through him (AFTER04 obs. 025; V11 obs. 213). That she is a physician who examines Monica on sight in V01 (V01 obs. 016) has a different weight once the prequel shows that her profession was chosen partly on Louis's example.

### 9.2 Rutherford

Rutherford is Louis's first teacher and the series' clearest instance of care hidden behind blows. He judged the boy a type who grows by being beaten and kept to that method (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 048, 088, 161). He is also the one who sees Louis's blindness and names it to MacLaggan, while Louis clings to the wall outside (obs. 183; SW-K-0151). He did not recommend Louis for the Sages because Clockford would have crushed it, and recommended Monica instead (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 067). He had taken the boy at Vivian's request, refusing her money (obs. 170). In V04 Louis threatens comically to bury him and goes to keep him away from Monica (V04 obs. 040, 049). In EXTRA09 Rutherford tells Monica stories of his idiot apprentice Louis (EXTRA09 obs. 164).

The ledger records the relationship from Rutherford's side as hidden care from Vivian's request onward (SW-R-0214). Louis learns that care only at the end of the lower volume.

### 9.3 Majer

Majer is the one teacher Louis respects without reserve, and the respect is the source of his name. He respects her because she keeps learning even though her teacher's formulas are slightly old (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 137). He insists that her teacher's barrier must be improved by those who follow, and names her Minerva's best barrier mage (obs. 135). She, in turn, recommends him to Rutherford's laboratory (obs. 150). Her words in the lower volume, that his barrier is worthy of the Barrier Mage and that she had a good teacher, close the loop of transmission (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 131).

### 9.4 Owen

Owen is the first person helped by Louis's violence. When classmates press homework on him and one knocks over Louis's marmalade, Louis beats them; he denies he fought to protect Owen (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 046, 052). Owen repays him in marmalade and coffee (obs. 049, 081). Louis refuses Owen's further help because Owen has exams (obs. 051). Owen later tells him that a motive need not be pure, only the ability real (ANOTHER-UPPER reading §6.8).

In the lower volume Owen is coerced and poisoned by Adolf. Louis solves the crime through Owen's habit of drinking coffee, moves his father, gives a cover story, and tells him to repay it with work (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 084, 137, 157; SW-R-0211). In V11 Owen is the one man Louis trusts to guard his family (V11 obs. 014), and the narration notes that the old roommate, unlike Owen, is tidy and mends (V11 obs. 094). Louis's gruffness to Owen, ordering him to clean his room in the same breath as reporting the new Sages (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 156), is affection in the only register Louis permits himself.

### 9.5 Lionel

Lionel enters the upper prequel as a burly blond youth buying soup at the festival stall, and turns out to be the first prince (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 142, 166). Louis fights him with barrier fists and then bare hands, and loses to a headbutt (obs. 171). They become companions at Goa's diner (obs. 174). Lionel's aim, to make the country good for his young half-brothers whoever reigns, is set beside Rosalie's choice to bear her father's hopes (obs. 201). Lionel teaches Louis upper-class manners (obs. 211), gives him the monocle (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 017), suspects him wrongly of sealing Rosalie's memory and apologises as a friend (obs. 092–093), and calls him a man who lives for love (obs. 142).

Against this, the numbered track's Louis describes his faction as dislike of the duke and the second prince (V02 reading §6.15; V06 obs. 056). The friendship explains the faction, and Louis's self-report understates it (section 5.4).

### 9.6 Glen

Louis's relationship with Glen repeats Rutherford's method with one change. Like Rutherford, Louis takes a boy of great power out of confinement for a debt and teaches by contempt that hides care (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 041, 044). Unlike Rutherford's punishments, Louis's punishment of Glen is private and proportionate (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §6.3).

The occasion matters. Glen's illicit sealing of Rosalie's memory is misplaced mercy. He had overheard Louis's sarcasm, seen Rosalie weep, and believed her fall a suicide attempt (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 118–123; SW-C-0101). He then shields his master by silence (obs. 124). Louis's careless remark is thus the root of the harm his disciple does. Louis names the risk, that she might never have woken (obs. 120), and later restricts Glen's magic, although Rosalie's statement spares him official censure (obs. 162). Louis also sees, before Glen does, that Glen loves Rosalie without knowing it (obs. 115). The book does not develop it.

The numbered track shows the same mixture. Louis uses Glen as a decoy without his knowledge (V02 obs. 010), sends him a suit (V04 obs. 101), confines his teaching to fists over formulas (V11 reading §6.2), and sets him impossible tasks (V12 obs. 155). The masters-and-transmission specialist argument covers how this method transmits both care and harm.

### 9.7 Lynn

Lynn, the wind upper spirit Rinzbelfeed, is Louis's contracted spirit throughout the numbered track. Both describe the bond as mutual benefit. Lynn says it is a give-and-take cooperation without trust, friendship, respect or love (EXTRA09 obs. 097). V10 records that the two claim mere mutual benefit, and that needing is not trusting (V10 obs. 072). The lower prequel gives the contract's motive: Lynn adores Carla and contracted with Carla's junior to stay near her (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 050). She also says she loves an unnamed human (obs. 095); the text does not identify that person, and neither does this monograph.

Louis's conduct toward Lynn is consistent with the stated terms. When her contract is forcibly cut and she attacks Monica under control, Louis allows her destruction; it is Monica, owing Lynn, who prefers to seal her (V06 obs. 170; V07 obs. 004). He keeps her contract ring in his coat because he breaks jewellery in fights (EXTRA09 obs. 059). Lynn's literal-mindedness is, in the lower volume, one cause of Rosalie's misreading (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 095). The relationship is the one place where Louis's language of use and his conduct largely agree.

### 9.8 Adolf

Adolf Farron is the figure who most wants Louis's regard, and the one to whom Louis gives least. Adolf's viewpoint in V11 records Louis's kick to his forehead in class, Rosalie's preference, and Louis's look, which said Adolf was not on his radar (V11 obs. 134–135). MacLaggan reads Adolf's plot as a wish to have Louis pay attention, an urge that escalates when born of malice (V11 obs. 143). Monica repeats the reading to Louis, who laughs (V11 obs. 152). Louis tells Adolf he was never on his radar (V11 obs. 170).

The prequel shows the long history, retrospectively: the theft of uniforms, a burned uniform, the rumour, and the selection crime (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 082, 145, 215; ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 136–137). Each of Adolf's moves is answered out of proportion (ANOTHER-UPPER reading §6.8). The relation is asymmetric: Louis's violence was aimed at those who provoked him, Adolf's at Louis's people (V11 reading §8.2).

This is the clearest case of the Rutherford mechanism. Louis reads Adolf's malice perfectly and never reads his longing. That blindness costs Rosalie a fall and Owen a poisoning.

### 9.9 Carla

Carla Maxwell, the Star-Spear Witch, is Rutherford's only other student and Louis's senior (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 129, 132). She tends him after his laboratory brawls (obs. 161). Watching her Star Spear split the clouds, he sees that the more he learns, the more he sees how far he is behind, and does not break (obs. 155, 157). She becomes a Sage after he graduates and resigns within three years over a relative's scandal (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 019). Lynn's contract with Louis exists to keep her near Carla (obs. 050). At V12 Carla has come home and left without a word, and Louis knows her absence is a family matter (V12 obs. 116; SW-K-0146). The thread is open, and this monograph does not speculate on its content.

### 9.10 Birdland

Birdland Verde is the source of Louis's long goal and his most comic antagonist. His outburst sets the conditions (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 210). His drunkenness destroyed the letters (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 060). His later recommendation and proposal of the engagement open the lower plot (obs. 051). As a father he would acknowledge Louis, and he calls himself the world's most foolish father (obs. 144–145). Louis restrains himself in front of him in the selection and again at V12 (obs. 106; V12 obs. 181).

## 10. Ordinary behaviour and humour

### 10.1 Jam

Jam runs through Louis's life as gift, currency and comedy. Shona's jar is the first luxury he knew; before it he had only crushed lingonberry on spoiled meat (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 018). The canteen fight that names him is over marmalade (obs. 046, 058). Owen's thanks is a jar of it (obs. 049). He puts marmalade in his coffee and raspberry jam in his tea (obs. 081, 189), until Nate tells him the upper class do not put jam in tea (ANOTHER-UPPER reading §6.3). In EXTRA09 he gives Monica jam as a universal seasoning, and admits he once put it on his wife's cooking and was not spoken to for a week (EXTRA09 obs. 022). In V11 he drinks liquor and jam mixed one to one (V11 obs. 007). The last act of the prequel is a jar of marmalade on Shona's grave (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 172; SW-C-0096).

In reading order, the jam is a comic tic in EXTRA09 and V11 whose meaning is supplied only by the prequel. In diegetic order, it is the first sign that someone cared for him.

### 10.2 Liquor and water

At home cheap liquor warmed people better than firewood; his storeroom had no fireplace (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 092). He says liquor is safer than water there, since rural Lidil lacks waterworks, and he does not trust taps (obs. 056, 179–180). The alcohol war with Rutherford, a non-drinker, ends with Louis on a window ledge overhearing his teacher's judgment (obs. 182–184). The liquor he offers Majer is confiscated (obs. 109). He vows to become a Sage over cheap liquor (obs. 012). Carla sends liquor to his wedding (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 167). The comedy has a moral hinge: his distrust of water insults the life's work of Rosalie's father, and his apology for it is the first act of the man who has heard he cannot see goodwill (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 187–188).

### 10.3 The comedy of coercion

SW-C-0016 holds that the comedy of coercion registers harm in most cases. Louis is its main instrument. The narrator's dry lines mark his conduct as abuse in V01 and as tyranny in AFTER04 (V01 obs. 010; AFTER04 obs. 007). In EXTRA09 Monica's real frightening stories list three of Louis's impositions beside a poisoning (EXTRA09 obs. 053). The criterion the readings use is whether the effect on the affected person is registered independently of tone (SW-F-0008). For Louis's coercion it usually is: Monica's fear is shown at length (V01 reading §8.5). For his colleagues treated as cargo it is registered only as comedy (AFTER04 reading §8.4). For his violence to Adolf it is not registered at all (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §8.3).

Smaller jokes complete the ordinary picture. He lies to Glen that the Silent Witch is merciless and kills at a glance (V05 obs. 045). He introduces himself to an empty suit of armour as a passing woodcutter (V07 obs. 019) and fights with an axe in a way the narration says is not how mages fight (V07 obs. 048). He hates shovelling snow, being northern-born (V12 obs. 018). He mends, and Owen cannot sew (V11 obs. 094). He fends off Lynn's love talk with an hour of praise for his wife (EXTRA09 obs. 093). Monica once served him appallingly bitter coffee (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 168).

## 11. Contradictions

Most of Louis's apparent contradictions resolve once the two views are joined. A few remain.

**Resolved by the prequel.**

- *He will never flatter nobles; he learns noble manners and seeks backers.* The manners are a marriage condition (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 098, 210–211; ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 024).
- *He hates being called womanly; he wears his hair long and once wore a dress.* The hair is a sign of wealth; the dress was for pay (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 140; ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 016).
- *He distrusts water; he marries the Waterworks Mage's daughter.* The distrust is rural experience, and the apology for it is a turning point (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 179–187).
- *His humanity "does not matter" to him; he takes her blame.* The self-report is the vocabulary of interest; the conduct is investment (V04 obs. 096).

**Not resolved, only bounded.**

- *Connections learned at the council.* Louis dates his third method to V09 (V11 obs. 183); the prequel shows a life carried by others' belief (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 160). The monograph reads this as his learning to use, as method, what he had received as gift. The text does not state that reading.
- *Chosen for her distrust; himself sustained by trust.* Louis's reason for choosing Monica (V02 obs. 096; V03 obs. 131) contradicts his own credo (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 160). The books let the contradiction stand until V09 defeats it.
- *The house and the stone.* V10 says Lynn's contract stone cost him his dream of a house (V10 obs. 081). The lower prequel shows him saving for and buying a house in the capital after the contract (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 015, 020, 048). The prequel does not show the stone's purchase (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §8.5). The seam is recorded, not resolved.
- *The previous selection's timing.* Felix calls it last year's while the narration places Carla's resignation half a year before the soirée; the lower reading finds these consistent if the selection fell late in the previous year (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §8.7).

## 12. Thematic function

Louis does four kinds of work in the series.

**He is Monica's measure.** Both are commoners who became Sages through genius; both came to magic without ambition; both stand among nobles they do not belong to. Rutherford's pairing of a genius of effort with a genius of numbers sets the comparison (SW-C-0103). The difference the books dwell on is not talent but belief. He believes in himself because others believed in him; she trusts neither herself nor others (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 160). Monica's arc toward asking for help (SW-C-0052, SW-C-0059, SW-C-0072) is staged against Louis's assumption that she never would. His defeat at V09 is the narrative proof that the arc is complete.

**He is a voice of the adult world's methods.** His lessons are that the game begins before the table, that power decides what is true, and that money, violence and connections are an adult's way (SW-C-0048; V08 obs. 252; V11 obs. 183). Monica learns each one and uses some against him. The books endorse his adult additions, money and connections used to protect others, and mark his violence as a model to avoid (V11 reading §8.3). Through Louis the series asks what a protector's methods cost, without answering in moral terms.

**He carries the theme of the made family.** Shona's distinction between strangers huddling together and a family organises both prequel volumes (SW-C-0096). The brothel's coins, Owen's marmalade, Goa's diner and Rou's acceptance answer it, and Louis's wish for Rosalie's care turns it toward a family to be made. The numbered track's doting husband and fumbling father is, read backwards, the fulfilment of a dying woman's instruction (SW-C-0104).

**He is a link in a chain of masters.** Rutherford to Louis, Majer to Louis, Louis to Glen: method and harm pass together (checkpoint §6, masters and transmission). Louis's epithet is itself a record of transmission (SW-C-0098). His punishment of Glen is the one point where he changes the method he received (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §6.3).

## 13. Counterarguments and counterevidence

### 13.1 "Louis is self-interested, and the investment is only pride"

This is the strongest counterargument, and the ledger keeps it as SW-C-0017's alternative. Its evidence is substantial. Louis's first stated interest in Monica's confidence is that her humility insults his defeat (V01 obs. 117). In EXTRA09 he is not interested in why she hides, only in how her state reflects on him (EXTRA09 obs. 020). He uses Glen as a decoy without consent (V02 obs. 010). He coerces under threats he does not believe (V01 obs. 118). He tests Monica with a proposal of mind interference (V07 obs. 138). He would have exposed Isaac and seen him executed, by Monica's judgment (V08 obs. 253). He allows Lynn's destruction (V07 obs. 004).

The monograph accepts that pride is a constant part of the motive. It rejects the word "only". The discriminating test is whether he acts at his own cost or for another's good without gain. He does: the blame he takes in V04 (V04 obs. 096), the concession on Casey (V02 obs. 096), the carriage in the lower volume (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 150–151), the protection of Owen (obs. 157), the flight to Rosalie on his own mana (AFTER04 obs. 023). The prequel's narration states attachment from inside (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 202). Pride and care are fused, as V01 already showed.

### 13.2 "The prequel retrofits a warmer Louis"

The prequels were published and read after the numbered track. A sceptic could argue that they sentimentalise a figure the main story drew as hard, and that this monograph lets the late books govern the early ones.

The counterevidence is that the numbered track already contained the warmth the prequel explains: the doting husband (V01 reading §7.3), the handkerchiefs (V02 obs. 099; V04 obs. 097), the anger licensed (V06 obs. 184–185), the flight for Rosalie (AFTER04 obs. 023). The prequel adds causes, not a new character. The monograph has also kept prequel evidence marked as retrospective throughout, and has not used it to revise any frozen numbered reading.

### 13.3 "Louis and Adolf are mirror images"

Both are proud, violent and fixed on each other. V11 rejects the mirror explicitly: Louis did not regard Adolf as a rival, and his violence was aimed at those who provoked him, Adolf's at Louis's people (V11 reading §8.2; SW-C-0085). The monograph agrees and adds that the asymmetry itself is Louis's fault line: his indifference is the injury Adolf cannot bear.

### 13.4 "The books endorse his violence"

The violence goes uncondemned in both prequels (ANOTHER-UPPER reading §8.3; ANOTHER-LOWER reading §8.3), and it is played for comedy in the numbered track. But "uncondemned" is not "endorsed". V11 marks it as a model Monica will not follow (V11 reading §8.3). The moral weight of the upper prequel falls on goodwill, not on violence (SW-C-0097). The monograph records the evaluation as the readings do: mixed, comic, and without consequence for Louis.

### 13.5 "Louis became a Sage by losing"

He lost to Monica and was seated because a second seat opened (ANOTHER-LOWER obs. 113, 143, 156). Birdland's private view and Mary's reading suggest the outcome, but the deliberation is unshown (ANOTHER-LOWER reading §8.2). The monograph does not claim he earned the seat by the battle. It claims only that the seat was the condition he set out to meet, and that he met it.
