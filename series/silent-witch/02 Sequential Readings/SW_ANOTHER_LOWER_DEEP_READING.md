## 3. Current-unit story synopsis

The second volume runs from Louis's appointment as head of the Magic Corps to his marriage, with a secret episode set in his first years as a Sage.

**The prologue.** A once-a-decade storm floods the Nadin region in the south. Louis, captain of the Corps' Third Unit, saves Sally, now a mother, and her daughter with a barrier. He calls his contracted wind spirit, Rinzbelfeed, a maid-uniformed upper spirit who flies the townsfolk to safety. Then he holds the failing embankment with a wide-area barrier until the Sage Birdland Verde can tame the river. For this he becomes head of the Corps and receives the title of the Barrier Mage, a little over three years after graduation.

**Chapter one.** At a celebration with Lionel and Nate, Louis receives a monocle, and laments that the Sages are far away. Carla became a Sage and resigned over a relative's scandal, and the Gem Mage replaced her without Louis being called. Escorting Lionel to a soirée, he meets Woodman, who lobbies to deregulate body-manipulation magic, and the second prince Felix, twelve or thirteen, whose perfect manner Louis distrusts. Louis resolves to stand with Lionel. Felix tells his friend Elliott that their grandfather crushed Louis's candidacy.

**Chapter two.** A mana runaway destroys Rutherford's laboratory at Minerva. The culprit is Glen Dudley, eleven, a butcher's son with huge mana, sent to Minerva because of the Star-Reading Witch's prophecy and then confined in a tower. Louis takes him as an apprentice to put Minerva in his debt, and trains him in fire and flight. At Minerva a timid girl called Monica, guided by Barney (Bernie) Jones, solves Rutherford's puzzle in seconds.

**Chapter three.** Louis has bought a house in the capital, but none of his letters to Rosalie has been answered. Birdland writes that he will retire and recommend Louis as his successor. He adds that Rosalie will join the Corps as a doctor and proposes her engagement to Louis. Rosalie refuses Adolf Farron, now the Wind-Hand Mage, who vows to crush Louis. At Birdland's house Louis appears as a polished gentleman, and Rosalie is shaken. The steward reveals that Birdland, drunk years ago, had ordered all letters between the two destroyed.

**Chapter four.** Engaged and working beside Rosalie, who keeps a professional distance, Louis learns that there are four candidates: Adolf, pushed by Clockford's faction; the Soaring Mage Winston Barrett; the fifteen-year-old Silent Witch Monica Everett, recommended by Rutherford; and himself. Rutherford explains to Carla that Clockford would crush any recommendation of Louis, and that Monica must be shown publicly before the Empire poaches her. Four days before the selection, Rosalie falls from the Corps barracks' roof.

**Chapters five and six.** Rosalie survives with a broken arm and no memory. Louis takes her into his house. Owen, his old roommate, is found poisoned beside a confession note. Louis notices that the poison was in tea although Owen drinks coffee. Rosalie remembers only a northern-accented boy she loved. Lionel suspects mind-interference magic.

**Chapter seven.** Lynn tells Rosalie that Louis would do anything to be a Sage, and Rosalie concludes the engagement was for the seat. The Sages debate. Winston withdraws. Louis meets Monica, who hides in a curtain and begs him not to kill her.

**Chapter eight.** In the forest battle Monica opens with a wordless wind Spirit-King summons. She knocks Adolf out with a wordless remote spell and defeats Louis. Louis judges that she should be a Sage. That night Rosalie ends the engagement, and Louis accuses Glen of sealing her memory.

**Chapter nine.** Glen confesses. He had overheard Louis tell a guild executive that he would not be engaged were Rosalie not a Sage's daughter, and had seen her weep. Believing her fall a suicide attempt, he sealed her memories with an incomplete spell. Louis explains that it is the other way round: he wants to be a Sage in order to marry her. Then he is called to a runaway at the Arsoon workshop. He rescues Majer and a party of children with a barrier that rebuilds the collapsing building, the Glass Phantom Castle.

**Chapter ten.** Adolf tries to woo the amnesiac Rosalie. Louis exposes him as the man who pushed her by remote magic, coerced Owen through his father's hospital care and poisoned him, and maims him. Mary and Birdland discuss the candidates; Graham Sanders retires as well, so two seats open. Louis unseals Rosalie's memory, explains her father's conditions, and in his old accent tells her he was never troubled by her.

**Chapter eleven.** Owen recovers and is protected by a cover story. Louis and Monica become Sages together. In the waiting room Louis tells her he believes in himself because others believed in him. During the parade he has Monica keep an illusion of him in the carriage while he flies Rosalie over the roofs.

**The epilogue.** Married half a year after the investiture, Louis and Rosalie visit the abandoned village of Dangrots. Rutherford has revealed that Vivian once asked him to take the boy away before he could be sold. Louis leaves marmalade on Shona's grave and tells her he has a family.

**The secret episode.** As a Sage, Louis is given a black-dragon mission in the Kerbeck earldom. He resolves to make the reclusive Silent Witch help.

## 6. Close reading

### 6.1 Placement, form and the frame

The lower volume starts where the upper volume's prologue stood. The upper prologue showed Louis as head of the Corps, counting the jar and vowing to be a Sage (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 006, 011, 012). The lower prologue shows how he became head: the Nadin flood, a little over three years after graduation (obs. 013). About three years then pass before the Sage selection in mid-冬中月 (obs. 058). Carla's resignation lies half a year before the soirée (S13:E000674) and about three and a half years before the selection (S16:E000426), and Glen, eleven when taken, is fourteen in the summer before it (S15:E000097). The investiture follows (obs. 156), and a wedding half a year later (obs. 165). The epilogue travels back, in place, to the village (obs. 171). The two volumes together are a single story of a boy who becomes a Sage and gains a family, and the lower volume's last line says so (obs. 173), answering the upper's frame line (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 014).

The book is structured as a mystery inside a romance. A fall, amnesia, a missing friend and a forged confession are set four days before an examination. Clues are planted early and paid off by name:

- the tea and the coffee (obs. 084, 137);
- the word "roof" where only "stairs" had been given out (obs. 075, 086, 136);
- Glen's library records (obs. 118).

The culprit, Adolf, is the antagonist the secret episode of the upper volume set up (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 215). A second mystery, the amnesia, has a benign author, Glen (obs. 123), and its cause is Louis's own careless remark (obs. 066, 122).

### 6.2 The price of refinement

The upper volume ended with Louis adopting refinement to meet Birdland's terms (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 211, 212). The lower volume counts the cost.

- To Rosalie, the polished man who greets her father is a stranger (obs. 056, 059).
- The Corps, knowing his ambition and his violence, read the engagement as a means to the seat (obs. 061, 095).
- To a guild executive whose own marriage was political, Louis hides his feeling behind a sarcasm that becomes the decisive overheard remark (obs. 066, 122).
- Nate states the reading the book itself endorses: his conduct produced the rumours, and his transformation looked "fishy" (obs. 126).

The cure is a return of voice. In the carriage he drops the polish and speaks in his northern accent (obs. 151). Rosalie's preference for the delinquent over the gentleman is stated twice (obs. 153, 164). The refinement remains, as a means, but the book places love in what it was meant to hide.

### 6.3 Glen's mercy and a master's punishment

Glen enters the run as the upper volume's Louis once did: a boy of great untrained power taken out of confinement by a master who wants a debt repaid (obs. 039, 041; cf. ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 029). Louis teaches as Rutherford taught, by exposure and contempt that hide care: "your runaway is no enemy" (obs. 044).

Glen's crime is an act of misplaced mercy. Having seen Rosalie weep, he believes her fall a suicide and seals her memory with an incomplete, quasi-forbidden spell (obs. 118, 119, 123). He then shields his master by silence (obs. 124). The book does not let mercy excuse the act. Louis names the risk (obs. 120) and later restricts Glen's magic, although Rosalie's statement spares him official censure (obs. 162). The master's punishment is private and proportionate, unlike the punishments of Louis's own youth.

The book also gives Glen an unspoken feeling for Rosalie that only Louis notices (obs. 115). It is not developed.

### 6.4 Adolf's crime

Adolf's attack is the plot's engine. Backed indirectly by Clockford through second-prince nobles (obs. 063), he:

- coerces Owen through his father's treatment (obs. 137, 154);
- pushes Rosalie from the roof with remote wind magic (obs. 136);
- poisons Owen and frames him with a note (obs. 082, 137);
- woos the amnesiac Rosalie (obs. 133).

His motive is to unseat a rival candidate (obs. 136) and his old hatred (obs. 055).

Louis's method of solving it is the cunning of the brothel, recalled at the moment of need (obs. 083, 085): controlling information, testing for the slip, and attending to a friend's habits. His punishment is the violence he renounced for the selection, now justified by a promise kept from school (obs. 140; cf. ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 145). Lionel's question, whether violence will void the selection, is answered by Louis's priorities (obs. 148).

V11 recorded that Adolf hurt Rosalie and coerced Owen at a selection, and presented his later crimes as a wish for Louis's attention (SW-C-0085). The lower volume supplies the event in full. It is recorded as supplemental retrospection that agrees with V11's account.

### 6.5 The genius of numbers

The book introduces Monica Everett through Louis's eyes, and the meeting is engineered for comedy and reversal:

- a girl in a curtain who begs not to be killed (obs. 100);
- a demand for a handicap, "or I'll win" (obs. 101);
- a wordless Spirit-King summons at the opening bell (obs. 107);
- a remote spell that fells Adolf, which she calls only a lot of calculation (obs. 109, 111);
- an economy formula cast without chanting (obs. 112).

Louis, the genius of effort, is beaten by the genius of numbers, in Rutherford's terms (obs. 104). He concedes that she should be a Sage (obs. 114).

The book adds three readings of her that the numbered track already knew from other sides:

- Rutherford's reason for recommending her: protection from foreign poaching through the prestige of candidacy (obs. 067);
- Mary's star of hardship, whose choices will affect many (obs. 146);
- Louis's diagnosis that she trusts neither herself nor others (obs. 160).

His answer to her, that he believes in himself because others believed in him, is the book's distillation of both volumes (obs. 160). It names Shona, the women, Rutherford, Goa's diner, his teachers, Owen, Rosalie and Lionel. She names an adoptive mother and one friend (obs. 160). The scene at Minerva earlier shows her with Barney Jones (obs. 046).

The numbered track's first volume introduced Monica through Louis's view of her as a heartless witch (SW-C-0033) and his coercion mixed with investment in her self-respect (SW-C-0017). The lower volume's meeting supplies the origin of both: coercion through a "debt" (obs. 163) and an insistence that she stop being servile (obs. 159, 161). They are recorded as strengthenings.

### 6.6 Carrying for others

The upper volume ended on "bearing": Rosalie and Lionel carry expectations, and Louis carried nothing (ANOTHER-UPPER obs. 201, 202). The lower volume answers. Louis states that, having nothing to carry, he will carry part of what Rosalie and Lionel bear (obs. 150):

- for Rosalie, her father's hopes, by becoming a Sage;
- for Lionel, the sly political work he cannot do (obs. 035).

The epilogue completes Shona's instruction: he comes back to tell her he has a family (obs. 172). It also shows that the women knew the village was doomed, and that Vivian arranged his escape before he could be sold (obs. 170). Their kindness, he reflects, was always hard to see until too late (S24:E000233).

### 6.7 The teachers again

Rutherford's role is clarified. He refused the Sages himself, more than once (obs. 103). He would not recommend Louis because Clockford would crush it (obs. 067), and he recommended Monica instead. The epilogue adds that he took Louis at Vivian's request, refusing her money (obs. 170).

Majer, retired, is shown still learning, her barrier modern and economical (obs. 129). Louis's barrier and his words make her the Barrier Witch once more (obs. 130, 131). Woodman reappears as a lobbyist for deregulating body-manipulation magic after an unnamed researcher's execution (obs. 029). Section 8.5 records what this does not establish.

### 6.8 The court and the Sages

The soirée shows the court through Louis's suspicion. The second prince Felix, twelve or thirteen, is too perfect; his manner is "reproduced" (obs. 031, 032). In private with Elliott he is indifferent (obs. 036). The numbered track's account of the present Felix (V08, V09) explains such a manner. This reading records Louis's suspicion as Louis's, and draws no inference beyond V08 and V09.

The Sages are drawn as a body of factions and temperaments:

- Emmanuel for Clockford;
- Ray weary;
- Raul hoping for a friend;

### 7.12 Form and evaluation

## 8. Alternative readings, absence and contradiction

### 8.1 "A love story with a detective plot attached"

The volume could be read as romance only, its mystery a device. Against this, the mystery carries the book's argument about refinement. The overheard remark and the rumours that make Rosalie doubt are products of Louis's own manner (obs. 066, 122, 126). The investigation returns him to the cunning of his past (obs. 083, 085). The reading treats both strands as one.

### 8.2 "Louis became a Sage by losing"

Louis loses the battle to Monica (obs. 113) and becomes a Sage only because a second seat opens (obs. 143, 156). The book does not present this as luck alone. Birdland's private view as a father (obs. 145) and Mary's reading of the candidates (obs. 146) suggest the outcome. The book leaves the Sages' deliberation unshown; the reading records that.

### 8.3 Normative evaluation: violence to Adolf

**Criterion:** whether the book condemns Louis's maiming of Adolf.

It shows Lionel's concern for the selection (obs. 148), Nate's help in covering it (obs. 158) and Mary's report of the result as a joke (obs. 146). It frames the act as a promise kept and as vengeance for Rosalie and Owen (obs. 140). It does not condemn it. The reading records that the book treats the violence comically and without consequence.

### 8.4 Normative evaluation: Glen's spell

**Criterion:** whether the book excuses Glen's illicit mind interference.

It gives his motive sympathy (obs. 123), names the risk (obs. 120), records that he escaped official censure through Rosalie's statement (obs. 162), and has Louis restrict him. The book judges the act wrong but forgivable.

### 8.5 Meaningful absences

The book does not show or tell:

- the Sages' deliberation or the interviews in detail;
- how Louis learned Spirit-King summoning or met Lynn, beyond the contract's motive;
- who the researcher executed two years before the soirée was. The text does not name him, and the reading does not identify him;
- any link between Woodman and the physicians'-association authority of IX -extra- (SW-C-0076). Woodman's deregulation lobbying (obs. 029) is recorded as the text gives it, and no identity is asserted;
- what Carla's relative did (obs. 019);
- whom Lynn loves (obs. 095);
- the outcome of the black-dragon mission, or any link between that dragon and a dragon of the numbered track (obs. 175, 178);
- Monica's adoptive mother and friend by name (obs. 160).

It does not use 閣下, and it names no patron of the numbered track's traitor.

### 8.6 The afterword, the jacket and the character list

**The afterword** calls the volume the meeting of Louis and Monica. It jokes that, without Rosalie, a freelance Louis would have fought the guild and the Sages. It mentions a comic adaptation in progress and announces main volume VIII (obs. 180).

**The jacket blurb** outlines the selection and the fall (obs. 181).

**The character list** is described in section 7.8.

All of this is paratext.

### 8.7 Apparent contradictions

- **The timing of the previous selection.** Felix calls it "last year's" (obs. 036), while the narration places Carla's resignation half a year before the soirée (S13:E000674). These are consistent if the selection fell late in the previous year.
- **Lynn's love.** Lynn says she contracted to come to the capital to be with a human she loves (obs. 095), and elsewhere is said to adore Carla (obs. 050). The text does not equate the two; recorded, not resolved.
- **Woodman's prominence.** In the upper volume he is a lazy school physician; here he is an influential lobbyist (obs. 028). This is a development, not a contradiction.
