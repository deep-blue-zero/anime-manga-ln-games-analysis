## 2. Prior-volume developments

*Prepared 2026-10-14 before any V12 narrative member was rendered for reading. Sources: the closed V01 to V11 readings, the closed IV -after- and IX -extra- readings, their exit freezes and the master ledger only.*

Monica Everett, the Silent Witch, lives in Southerndoor and teaches Isaac Walker, publicly Duke of Erin under the dead prince's name. At V11 she explained formulas at Bradford's lecture in Highorn. She came to see that a master owes her apprentice understanding and responsibility, and took Cyril, MacLaggan and Bradford as models.

At Cresburn Magic University the escapee Adolf Farron trapped Louis in a fog that made him believe he was a Minerva student. Using an ice hand-bell that amplifies mana, he opened the first Thorn Witch's black-flame grimoire. Monica, Louis and Raul defeated him. MacLaggan, Bradford, Ugo and Owen stopped his last attack on the capital, and Adolf was captured.

The bell was a replica made by an unnamed pale-gold girl, who is inferred to have broken the prison. She plans to win over an ancient ice spirit before spring and speaks of an endless winter.

Monica is travelling to Kerbeck for the solstice holiday with Isabel. Isaac goes too, on business with the earl that he has not explained. Hubert Dee has been sent to survey a Kerbeck hot spring whose mana has suddenly risen.

## 3. Current-volume story synopsis

**The prologue.** The Norton family of Kerbeck, who play at villainy, plan a quiet welcome for the Witch. Monica arrives with Isabel and a disguised Isaac to find the town full of snow statues of herself. For orphanage children who pretend not to recognise her, she conjures a ring of chiming ice that a child names 冬精霊の氷鐘.

**Chapter one.** The earl, a dragon-hardened lord, apologises for not providing villain's backlighting: Louis had told him Monica longs to be bullied. He promises not to disturb the peace her silence keeps.

Over tea Monica learns of the painted eggshells, 儚き灯りの卵殻宿, and Sylvia's warning that investment can be invasion. In private the earl and Isaac confirm a pact of silence and trade intelligence:

- the last escapee, the drug-maker リュド・クラーセン;
- a Randall adventurer, サミュエル・スロース;
- the Anderson Company.

Afterwards the earl and his wife judge Isaac too thoroughly raised as a royal.

**Chapters two and three.** An old woman says the Witch loves the springs of Sechen, a village in neighbouring Ragott. Isaac, Isabel and Agatha scent an impostor, and Monica goes along to restrain them.

The springs near the volcano have been polluted with mana for a year. A clever orphan, Norman, runs a stamp rally of villagers playing the Witch, secretly supplying their magic himself.

At the inn Isaac pins Monica to a wall after she confides her fear of tall men. Isabel and Agatha are delighted. Wildianu goes to survey the springs.

**Chapter four.** Wildianu meets a passing wind spirit, who mentions a memorial for a famous upper spirit. In the tavern the villagers fear for the village; "the teacher" surveys the springs and tutors Norman.

At night Monica slips out to talk to the village head. Isaac stops her, tells her he wants her to know how much he cares, and puts her hand to his heart. She proposes to measure his pulse, tickles his old wound and seals him in a barrier. The head is out. They take a walk together.

**Chapters five and six.** The stamps and a cipher on Monica's card alone lead into the north forest. There Hubert Dee and his apprentice Norman draw her into a magic battle.

Hubert taunts Isaac about his face, and Isaac retaliates with a glowing eggshell. Monica reads the village's egg pattern as a map of old-era rocks. Breaking them with a Spirit-King summons, she uncovers an old-era plate that drains the land's mana, which ends the battle.

Isabel exposes Hubert's aim, a mana furnace for his family's tool business. Wildianu reports the cause of the pollution: the fire spirit Kayan, named in the calendar, has vanished, and the spirits gathering to mourn her raised the volcano's mana. Isaac learns that Monica had trusted Wildianu, not him, to hold Hubert off.

**Chapter seven.** At the village head's house Isabel sets out a project from "someone": roads from the East into the North-east, where furnaces can stand away from dragons and outside the Anderson Company's reach. Monica saves the village by offering its rock to the Earl of Amberd.

Isaac admits the project is his, and Monica praises him for thinking of the country. He thinks himself her parent's enemy and still hopes she will take his hand. She diagnoses his racing heart as a cold. She binds Hubert's rings and recommends Norman to Minerva under Hubert's tutoring. Hubert keeps to himself what he learned of the ancient artefacts.

**Chapter eight.** Vanessa Dee buys into the project as Kerbeck's paymaster. Monica corrects Isaac's paper, and decides he will start practice with his own new formula. Louis finds that Carla has come home and left without a word.

**The interlude.** Before the prison break, the fire spirit Kayan faces an ice spirit in a white dress. The ice spirit means to open 謁見の門 and bury humanity in eternal winter. Kayan was once the small fire spirit of the eggshell legend. After three days the ice spirit devours her and grows younger, needing only one more winter. Kayan dies warning her that clinging to humans destroyed the old-era monsters.

**Chapters nine and ten.** Ray Albright learns that his grandmother Adeline has betrothed him to Frieda Blanke. Frieda is the frank, impoverished sister of the margrave of Valmberg in the Empire.

The Sages fight a New Year team battle. Mary tells the king and Monica that layered ill omens preceded the prison break, and the king asks for unity and a seventh Sage. Ray sends Frieda daily notes and charms by bat.

**Chapters eleven and twelve.** Henrik Blanke, a dragon-killing swordsman, comes looking for Ray.

At Mary's party Monica hears that Cyril is in marriage talks with Mary's niece Aurelia. She is shaken without knowing why. Cyril asks Aurelia to decline, unsure of his own heart.

Mary tells Isaac he was born under the star of loss and will lose half the world in his eyes. Monica's attempt to play a villainess fails. Ray explains, in anger, that a curse-user's "I love you" becomes a curse, as he believes his mother's did. He runs into the snow, where Frieda finds him.

**Chapter thirteen.** Frieda says she has no reason to dislike him. Henrik challenges Ray to a duel; Frieda fights her brother instead, and loses. Henrik's killing intent sets off Ray's curses, which turn plants into monsters.

Monica shields everyone with a rebuilt anti-curse barrier, and Louis turns the scene into a show. Monica and Raul declare themselves the villains so that Ray will not be, and calm him with enchanted rose petals. Frieda holds him.

Mary reveals to Adeline that Ray's mother had cursed only herself, and that Frieda is the granddaughter of Adeline's first love.

**The epilogue.** Frieda kisses Ray's hand: she does not love him yet, but thinks she can come to like him. Cyril resolves to be respected by Monica as her senior. Monica's borrowed dress gives way as she congratulates him on his betrothal, and she is ashamed before him. Isaac sees this, leaves, and whispers Arc's name.

**The secret episode.** Earlier, while Monica was away, Lana finds a gold-eyed man in an antique robe at her house. He knows her name. He calls himself Bartholomew Alexander and inspires her new robe design.

## 6. Close reading

### 6.1 Two stories and a frame

V12 joins two stories, each with its own focalizers:

- **Kerbeck and Sechen** (prologue to chapter eight). Monica, Isaac, Isabel, Hubert and Norman.
- **Ray's betrothal** (chapters nine to thirteen). Ray, Frieda, the Sages and the Blankes.

The afterword says the two were first side stories of the web serial, revised for the volume (obs. 219). The reading treats the volume as published and does not consult the web text.

The frame of V10 and V11 continues, but less as a frame than as a hidden spine. The volume never shows the pale-gold girl of V11. Instead it gives three things:

- the snow festival's chime, which a child calls 冬精霊の氷鐘 (obs. 009);
- the fire spirit Kayan's death, which caused the Sechen pollution and is told in the interlude (obs. 097, 120–128);
- Mary's ill omens that preceded the prison break (obs. 148).

### 6.2 The ice spirit and Kayan

The interlude is the volume's largest disclosure. Before the Neigal explosion, an ice spirit takes the form of a young woman in a white dress, speaks of herself as ぼく and carries an ice bell. She means to open the gate of audience and bury humanity in eternal winter, and she claims the will of the ice Spirit-King フラウリシェル (obs. 121, 122, 125). She devours Kayan, an old acquaintance, breaking the taboo against eating her own kind. She re-forms younger, needing only one more winter (obs. 126).

These features match V11's pale-gold girl:

- her white dress and her ぼく (V11 obs. 018, 218);
- the bell, and her plan to reach "the gate of audience" through an old ice spirit (V11 obs. 219, 222);
- her apparent age falling from early teens to about ten (V11 obs. 018, 218).

The interlude does not name her or state the identity. The reading therefore treats the identity as strongly implied, not established. Section 9 revises SW-C-0086 to include the interlude's facts without asserting the identity.

Kayan's dying words give the ice spirit's condition a meaning. The old-era monsters died out because they clung to humans; love turned to hatred is greater for having been love (obs. 127). The volume ties this to Ray's chapters. There, monsters are born from the "abyss" of human feeling joined with mana, and a curse-user is the one who handles that abyss (obs. 193).

### 6.3 Memory kept in craft

The eggshell 儚き灯りの卵殻宿 carries the volume's theme of forgotten memory:

- The legend of a small fire spirit sheltering in an egg was a memory of Kayan herself. Her name became the craft's name, and no human remembers it (obs. 017, 123).
- Sechen's white-line pattern recorded an old-era device that cleared mana pollution. Monica reads the pattern and frees the land (obs. 051, 090, 091).
- Isaac half-remembers making eggs in his home village and quarrelling over the wax with a neighbour boy (obs. 052).

Hubert adds a withheld secret: the old era made ancient artefacts here, and they cannot now be reproduced because their material does not exist (obs. 108). Section 9 registers a question about it.

### 6.4 Isaac: love unreturned and loss foretold

V12 is the volume most concerned with Isaac's feelings for Monica. It states them in his viewpoint and through his actions:

- jealousy of the Highorn stay and of the cipher's maker (obs. 033, 077);
- a confession that she does not recognise (obs. 068, 070);
- hope, from the position of her parent's enemy, that she will take his hand (obs. 105).

Monica cannot read them. She has no equation for them, and she answers with instruments and a diagnosis of a cold (obs. 069, 070, 106).

His public and private selves both advance:

- **As a political actor** he sets up the north-eastern road and furnace project against the Anderson Company. The Kerbecks judge him "raised too much as a royal" (obs. 029, 095, 104).
- **As an apprentice** he begins practice, with his own formula as his first spell (obs. 112).
- **His violence.** He hides, as before, a violence that Hubert can provoke by mentioning his face (obs. 089).

Mary's prophecy turns his history into fate. She tells him he was born under the star of loss and will lose half the world in his eyes (obs. 171, 173). The epilogue makes the reader connect it with what he sees: Monica's shame before Cyril (obs. 209–211). The volume does not say what the prophecy means. The reading records it as a prophecy, not a prediction.

### 6.5 Monica's unnamed feelings

Monica's emotional range widens in two directions. Before Isaac she remains unselfconscious: she fainted naked in the bath and was rescued, and does not see why that matters (obs. 053, 070). Before Cyril she responds three ways:

- shock at his marriage talk, then tears, and a chest that "creaks" (obs. 161, 162);
- a resolve to spare him embarrassment by playing the villainess herself (obs. 170);
- shame when her dress gives way in front of him (obs. 209, 210).

The epilogue's title, the reason for shame, names the contrast.

The volume gives the feelings no name. Cyril's side is equally unnamed: he cannot promise to marry Aurelia, feels he deceived his own heart without knowing how, and settles on wanting Monica's respect as her senior (obs. 166, 167, 208). The reading records these as unnamed feeling, as the text does. Section 9 registers a question.

### 6.6 Love that cannot be spoken: Ray

Ray's chapters turn on a single forbidden sentence. His mother forbade him to say "I love you"; the volume first masks the word, then gives it (obs. 145, 185). He believes his mother's love was a curse that made his father her puppet (obs. 178, 180, 186). Mary then gives the truth, unknown to Ray:

- His mother cursed only herself, believing she could not be loved.
- His father loved her, and died shielding her (obs. 202).

The volume thus revises its own rumour, within the volume.

Frieda's answer is practical:

- She has no reason to dislike him (obs. 188).
- Feeling can be shown without the sentence; she kisses his curse-marked hand (obs. 204).
- She does not love him yet, but thinks she can come to like him (obs. 205).

Monica and Raul make themselves the villains so that Ray will not be the villain (obs. 198). Monica's motive is her own knowledge of self-cursing (obs. 195). Section 9 registers the theme.

### 6.7 Masters, apprentices and recommendations

The master–apprentice subject of V11 continues in new pairs:

- **Hubert and Norman.** Hubert is a self-interested but effective teacher. Norman is a clever orphan who judges his teacher fairly (obs. 084, 085).
- **Monica and Isaac.** Monica corrects his paper and recognises her own mistake in delaying his practice (obs. 111, 112).
- **Louis and Glen.** Louis sets Glen an impossible dragon hunt (obs. 155).
- **Rutherford and Louis.** Rutherford remains Louis's master, and the keeper of Carla's secret (obs. 182).

Monica's handling of Hubert completes the subject. She binds his rings and turns him into Norman's tutor and protector, with a Minerva recommendation (obs. 107). This continues SW-C-0084.

### 6.8 Politics and peace

The volume shows politics as the price of peace:

- the earl's pact of silence, made to keep the peace and to avoid a war in which the East would bleed first (obs. 015, 021);
- Sylvia's warning about investment (obs. 019);
- the road project as a check on a single dominant company (obs. 100, 101);
- Isaac's thought of a dragon-defence alliance with Valmberg (obs. 191).

The Empire now has a name, シュヴァルガルト (obs. 129).

### 6.9 Knowledge and misbelief

The reading tables these in section 7.11:

- **Lana** does not know the gold-eyed man (obs. 214–216). The name he gives, Bartholomew Alexander, is the human-form alias already recorded for Monica's familiar Nero. His gold eyes, a robe copied from a human who visited him long ago, and his memory of Monica's talk at Serendia agree. The episode itself does not use the name Nero.
- **Ray** believes his mother cursed his father; Mary and Adeline know otherwise (obs. 186, 202).
- **Norman** believed Monica was a tool inspector (obs. 083).
- **The public** will be told that Monica met an upper spirit by chance (obs. 114).

## 8. Alternative readings, absence and contradiction

### 8.1 "Two side stories, so no main development"

The afterword's account of the volume's origin could suggest a pause in the arc (obs. 219). Against this, the volume:

- discloses the ice spirit's plan and Kayan's death (obs. 122, 126);
- gives Mary's prophecy to Isaac (obs. 173);
- begins Monica's unnamed feelings toward Cyril (obs. 161, 210).

The reading treats it as a full unit of the arc.

### 8.2 "The ice spirit is the girl"

The shared features are strong (section 6.2). Against treating the identity as established:

- The interlude never names her.
- Its spirit is "a young woman", later late-teens, while V11's girl is early teens, then about ten. That decrease is consistent with continued loss, but is not stated as such.

The reading registers the identity as implied.

### 8.3 Normative evaluation: Hubert's scheme

**Criterion:** whether the volume condemns Hubert's use of a village and its child.

It exposes him and binds his magic (obs. 094, 107). It also lets him become Norman's tutor, a role the volume shows to benefit Norman (obs. 107). His deception of Norman stands uncorrected by any apology (obs. 093). The judgment is mixed.

### 8.4 Normative evaluation: Isaac's love

**Criterion:** whether the volume presents Isaac's pursuit as harmful to Monica.

It shows force: a gripped wrist, a pinned hand. It shows Monica's fear of the unknown, and Isaac's own retreat to a step's distance (obs. 055, 068, 069, 073). It treats his feeling as sincere, and his position as her parent's enemy as an unresolved obstacle (obs. 105).

### 8.5 Meaningful absences

The volume does not show or tell:

- the pale-gold girl herself;
- スロース's identity (obs. 025 records only a name);
- Romaria's glow;
- what half of the world Isaac will lose;
- the nature of ancient artefacts;
- Carla's family matter;
- whom Monica's feelings concern, in her own words.

### 8.6 The afterword, jacket and card

**The afterword** gives the volume's web origin; two rejected plots, a mystery with Cyril as suspect and a Sages' holiday; praise of Norman's design; and thanks. It says the next volume is being written (obs. 219–222).

**The jacket blurb** summarises both stories (obs. 223).

All of this is paratext. The web serial was not consulted.

### 8.7 Apparent contradictions

- **The ice spirit's apparent age.** It is given as a young woman, then late teens; V11's girl is early teens, then about ten. Recorded, not resolved.
- **The name スロース.** It appears for the girl's companion (V11 obs. 020) and for a Randall adventurer (obs. 025). The text does not connect them; the reading records the recurrence only.
- **The narrator's spelling of Adeline.** One narrated line gives a variant form of her name (S22:E000501). Recorded as a variant.
