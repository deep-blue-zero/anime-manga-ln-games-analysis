## 2. Prior-volume developments

*Prepared 2026-10-14 before any V09 narrative member was rendered for reading, from the closed V01 to V08 readings, the closed IV -after- reading, their exit freezes and the master ledger only.*

Monica Everett, the Silent Witch, has lived at Serendia Academy as Monica Norton, the student council's treasurer, guarding the second prince. V08 disclosed that the prince she guarded is not the prince. The boy Felix Arc Ridill died by a fall about ten years ago, after learning that his grandfather, Duke Clockford, was raising his page as a double. The page, Isaac Walker, took the prince's face and name so that the dead boy would not be forgotten. Clockford killed the physician Arthur and burned the two bodies to fake Isaac's death. Isaac contracted Queen Irene's water spirit, Wildianu, and fell in love with the Silent Witch's magic.

Monica learned that her father, Venedict Rain, was executed because his design, a tool reading hereditary information from blood, could have exposed the false prince; the narration states that Clockford had a part in his death. The patron 閣下 to whom Sams sold him is still unidentified. Monica met the young emperor Leonhardt in the forest and bargained with him. If she averts war, the false prince's secret stays hidden. If Ridill declares war on the Empire, she must serve him and end the war with the royal family's heads.

Felix tried to have Emmanuel Darwin kill Clockford; Clockford trapped and killed Emmanuel instead, forged a confession blaming an Imperial mage, and had Isaac arrested as an impostor. He now backs the third prince, Albert. Isaac means to die a villain at the Supreme Council, about two weeks away. Louis, who has proved the prince false, means to denounce Clockford at the garden party. Monica holds the real prince's diary, whose last page shows that Isaac misunderstands something; Wildianu's contract stone; and her father's designs, which Bartholomeus has begun to build. She has revealed herself as the Silent Witch to her council, with Albert, Hubert and Isabelle present, and asked for their help.

## 3. Current-volume story synopsis

**The prologue.** In the tower, Isaac expects to be convicted and executed. He wishes Clockford would at least pretend to grieve, so that the dead prince would be mourned and remembered.

**Chapter one.** Monica reveals herself to her council, Albert and Hubert. Hubert tests her and she answers chantlessly. Cyril kneels and apologizes; Monica weeps until he scolds her as before. Lana remains her friend. Monica has everyone read the real prince's diary. She then asks for help with a plan she calls her life's greatest cheat, built on the black chalice: Bernie lends a workshop, Lana gathers materials and Hubert builds the tool. Hubert's price is a battle against someone to be held off from the council. All agree, and Cyril declares the rescue.

**Chapter two.** At Amberd, Monica, Hubert and Bartholomeus build the chalice, made to show its result by colour as a piece of stagecraft. The others gather student petitions; Isabelle and the magic-history club bring the most. Bridget coaches Monica's bearing, and Monica asks Cyril for a white-rose charm. Bridget privately takes Monica to be in love with Cyril without knowing it.

**Chapter three.** On the morning of the Supreme Council, Lana dresses Monica in black and Monica puts on Isaac's pendant. Hubert, disguised as a coachman, drives Louis into a forest, where a magic-battle barrier rises. Glen, Hubert and the remote Witch attack him. Monica had planned to keep Louis away because he would expose everything. Cyril gives her the charm and she flies to the castle. Louis turns on the students.

**Chapter four.** Louis, the first "Minerva brat", defeats their traps, traps Glen and Hubert in his Mirror Prison and punches Robert with a barrier on his fist. Cyril frees the others and lures Louis away. When Louis chokes him, Cyril removes his brooch and lets his excess mana run wild, freezing Louis in place.

**Chapter five.** Glen rams Louis with his own fireball. Robert leads him onto a hidden insect-attracting curse among mana-drinking flowers, set by Ray and Raul. Louis's error, the narration says, was to assume the Witch would never ask anyone for help. At the council the king appears, brought in by Mary. Isaac refuses to name himself and confesses to every crime. The Silent Witch enters, wearing his pendant, and declares him the real prince.

**Chapter six.** Monica reads the students' petitions, then shows the black chalice, first on a count and his son and then with the king's blood and Isaac's. The chalice reddens. She explains the confession as the work of a curse laid by Peter Sams, whom she also blames for the curse dragon and Emmanuel's death. She names Sams but not his tie to Clockford. Ray performs a "lifting" of the curse, and the king orders his son treated. Monica silently warns Clockford to keep quiet.

**Chapter seven.** Neil tells Louis the settlement and gives him Monica's message in his own words, that the game begins before the table. The king tells Isaac he knew who he was, overlooks the matter for the country, and thanks him as a father. Mary tells Clockford that the ending was Monica's, and recalls his history. Monica tells Isaac that the chalice trick used Albert's blood. Isaac offers her revenge.

**Chapter eight.** Monica refuses revenge: she chose his life over her father's name. Isaac reads the diary's last page and learns the prince meant to live and meet him again; his death was an accident. Monica tells him a friend needs no reason. Isaac realizes he loves her. The emperor accepts the outcome and plans a treaty. Isaac renounces the succession and returns to his council for the last days.

**Chapter nine.** The Sages begin choosing Emmanuel's successor. At graduation Isaac speaks the prince's wish in his own words. Bridget will enter court service. Monica, hiding outside in uniform, gives him a bouquet. He gives her a yellow-rose corsage as a charm, and they dance.

**Chapter ten.** Half a year later Monica begins joint plant research with Raul, managed by Cyril, and asks Cyril to call her Monica. The following summer, after thanking the villagers who had cared for her hut, she moves to the port of Southerndoor and visits Lana's new Flax Company.

**Chapter eleven.** A year after graduation, the emperor visits Ridill and proposes to Bridget, now Albert's secretary. She answers that she will marry the man who brings her a blue rose. Eliane visits Glen's family. Lana's company makes mana-infused robes with Amberd. When Lana and her friends call at Monica's house, they find it clean and Isaac in an apron.

**Chapter twelve.** Isaac, now Duke of Erin, visits the grave that bears his own name and goes to Southerndoor. He asks to be Monica's apprentice, cleans her house, cooks, and argues magic with her through the night. He wins the place.

**The epilogue.** Five days later, Isaac wakes his master, braids her hair and serves her friends; he bristles at Cyril and at Robert's chess invitations. Raul plans to study incognito in the Empire. Monica, who once drank coffee alone, says she is glad to have people to drink it with. Nero finds a card in Porter's new novel dedicating it to a quiet witch.

## 6. Close reading

### 6.1 Structure: the trial and the coda

V09 is in two parts.

**The trial (prologue to chapter eight).** This part covers about two weeks, from the reveal to the council and its aftermath. It interleaves three threads (obs. 001–134):

- the preparations;
- the stalling of Louis;
- the council itself.

**The coda (chapters nine to twelve and the epilogue).** This part steps through graduation, half a year later, the following summer, and a summer a year after graduation (obs. 135–184).

The afterword states the design. The story was always to end with Monica, who drank coffee alone at the start, drinking it among friends (S37:E000013). The epilogue's line about coffee with others carries that design (obs. 182). The afterword also announces a story collection and a new V10 set in Southerndoor, so V09 closes an arc, not the series (obs. 186).

### 6.2 The settlement: a staged truth

The trial resolves the V08 crisis by an argued fiction, not by exposure. The elements are:

- **A real tool, demonstrated honestly** (obs. 088). The black chalice is a genuine instrument, and it is first shown on Count Barrel and his son.
- **Borrowed blood** (obs. 091, 116). When Isaac's blood is added, the chalice turns red only because Monica secretly adds Albert's blood from her sleeve.
- **A dead culprit** (obs. 094, 095). The confession is blamed on a curse laid by Peter Sams. Sams is also blamed for the curse dragon and for Emmanuel's death. Monica deliberately leaves out his link to Clockford.
- **A performed cure** (obs. 097, 100). Ray "lifts" the curse by making a sign glow on Isaac's forehead.
- **Royal ratification** (obs. 098, 105–106, 113). The king, who knew Isaac's name all along, accepts the result because it serves the country. Mary says Monica brought the plan to him in advance.
- **A silent threat** (obs. 099). Monica warns Clockford without words that she will reveal everything if he moves again. The S11 epigraph prints that warning (obs. 004).

The reading records that the volume presents this outcome as a calculated untruth that leaves everyone a victim: Clockford, Isaac and the dead prince (SW-C-0069). Louis's lesson that power decides what is true is used, not refuted (obs. 096).

### 6.3 Asking for help

V09 completes the arc of asking for help that began at V06 (SW-C-0052, SW-C-0059). Every part of the plan comes from someone Monica asked:

- Bernie supplies the workshop, Lana the materials, and Hubert and Bartholomeus the building (obs. 022–023, 029–031).
- Isabelle and the club bring the petitions, and Bridget the coaching (obs. 037–038).
- Glen, Hubert, Robert and Cyril hold off Louis, with Ray and Raul setting the trap (obs. 048–071).
- Albert gives his blood, Ray performs the cure, and the king lends his authority (obs. 097, 113, 116).

The narration names Louis's mistake: he assumed the Witch would never ask anyone for help (obs. 072). Louis's own lesson, that the game begins before the table, comes back to him as her message (obs. 104; SW-C-0048). The reading registers the rescue as collective (SW-C-0072).

### 6.4 The diary's last page

V08 withheld the diary's last sentence. V09 gives it, in paraphrase here (obs. 122). The prince meant to become a kind prince Ike could be proud of. He hoped that one day, as a grown man, he would visit Ike and hear what life Ike had chosen.

From this entry and Isaac's own recollection that the prince let go with one hand to reach for a star, Monica concludes that the death was an accident, not a suicide (obs. 123, 124). The reading registers the accident as the volume's account, given through Monica's reading and Isaac's memory. Isaac's misunderstanding (SW-P-0050) is that he took the death for a suicide committed for his sake (SW-C-0070).

### 6.5 Clockford and the king

Clockford is seen only from outside in V09:

- He objects at the council (obs. 086).
- He glares at Monica (obs. 099).
- He talks with Mary on a balcony (obs. 110–114).

Mary supplies his history:

- His father loved the country and was killed for urging peace (obs. 111).
- His wife was murdered by Ridill nobles (obs. 111).
- He loves and hates the country (obs. 111).

Mary also offers a reading of the substitution: making a non-royal king would be revenge on the crown. Clockford neither denies nor confirms it (obs. 110). The reading registers the revenge reading as Mary's inference (SW-C-0071).

The narration, from Monica's point of view, states two things as facts. Clockford arranged her father's execution (S18:E000277). He was the mastermind behind Sams's curse dragon (S18:E000522, E000595). The patron 閣下 named by Sams is not mentioned in V09.

The king is shown as the player who saw the whole board. He let the false prince stand for the country, thanked Isaac as a father, and now means to name Lionel heir (obs. 105–107, 112).


### 6.6 Monica

Monica's developments in V09:

- She reveals herself and asks for help (obs. 005–028).
- She performs a public role scripted by her friends (obs. 082–099).
- She chooses Isaac's life over her father's name (obs. 120).
- She refuses revenge (obs. 119).
- She asks Cyril to call her Monica (obs. 147).
- She leaves the hut by choice for a town near Lana (obs. 148–151).
- She accepts an apprentice (obs. 173).

The coda shows her able to make promises about the future (obs. 042). Her line about coffee with others closes the volume's arc (obs. 182). She calls Isaac a friend and night-out companion and does not read his corsage (obs. 126, 143). Bridget believes she loves Cyril without knowing it (obs. 040). The reading records Bridget's view as Bridget's belief, and records no feeling on Monica's part that the text does not state.

### 6.7 Isaac Walker

V09 resolves Isaac's obsession. He learns that the Witch is Monica (obs. 080–081). He learns that the prince's death was an accident and that the prince wished him a life of his own (obs. 122–125). The narration then says the obsessive fire is gone (S20:E000243).

His feeling is stated in his own viewpoint: he could not help loving her (obs. 128). His conduct follows from it:

- the corsage given as a charm (obs. 143);
- the visit to the grave (obs. 167);
- the request to become her apprentice (obs. 169).

His jealousy of Cyril and Robert is played for comedy (obs. 175, 179). Publicly he remains Felix, renounces the succession, and serves as Duke of Erin (obs. 132, 164–165). The reading registers the change (SW-C-0073).

### 6.8 Others

- **Cyril** kneels to the magic count and is mocked back into himself (obs. 008–011). He lets his own condition run wild to stop Louis (obs. 066–067). He stays loyal to Isaac knowing who he is (obs. 180).
- **Louis** turns out to be the first "Minerva brat" and a fighter with barriers (obs. 057–063). He is beaten by pawns and admits his second defeat by Monica (obs. 069–072, 137).
- **Bridget** coaches Monica and calls the clumsy real prince lovable (obs. 038, 140). She turns aside the emperor's proposal with the blue rose (obs. 155). She enters court service (obs. 141, 153).
- **Elliott** helps the rescue in order to laugh at Isaac's face (obs. 027).
- **Albert** gives his blood and his name (obs. 084, 116). He grows into the third prince's role (obs. 152–155).
- **The emperor** prefers a weakened Ridill, but accepts the result and seeks the chalice's technology (obs. 130–131).
- **Wildianu** keeps the contract (obs. 129).

### 6.9 Names and silence

The volume's title words return in two senses:

- Isaac refuses to give a name at his trial (obs. 076).
- He guesses Monica's real name is Monica Rain (obs. 078).
- He signs his letter to the Witch with his own name (obs. 166).
- He visits the grave that bears it (obs. 167).
- The Silent Witch breaks silence at the council and imposes silence on Clockford (obs. 099).
- Porter dedicates a novel to "a quiet witch" (obs. 184).

The reading records the pairing of silence imposed and silence broken as the volume's closing figure (SW-C-0074).

## 8. Alternative readings, absence and contradiction

### 8.1 "The settlement is the truth of the story"

One could take the council's verdict as what happened. The volume prevents this in several ways:

- It shows the trick (obs. 116).
- It shows the knowledge of Isaac and Clockford (obs. 092).
- It shows the king's private knowledge (obs. 106).
- It shows Mary's account to Clockford (obs. 113).

The reading registers the verdict as the official story within the fiction, and the narrated events as the story's truth (SW-C-0069).

### 8.2 "The prince took his own life"

Clockford and Isaac took the death for suicide (V08). V09 gives the diary's last page and Isaac's recollection of one hand let go (obs. 122, 124). Monica calls the death an accident "surely" (obs. 123). The narration adopts her reading; Isaac accepts it (obs. 125). The reading registers the accident as the volume's account, through these characters (SW-C-0070).

### 8.3 Normative evaluation: the staged truth

Criterion: whether the volume presents Monica's deception of the Supreme Council as justified. The volume counts the costs:

- Her father's name stays uncleared (obs. 120).
- Clockford goes unpunished (obs. 095).
- A dead man is blamed for crimes in which Clockford had a hand (obs. 094).

It counts the gains: no war, no execution, and the king's interest served (obs. 105, 130). It lets Neil defend Clockford's usefulness (obs. 103). It lets Louis call the plan cunning (S19:E000223). The volume presents the deception as wise, and does not frame it as a wrong.

### 8.4 Normative evaluation: revenge refused

Criterion: whether the volume weighs Monica's right to revenge, which Isaac offers her (obs. 118). She refuses for her own reasons: fear of the feeling and a wish for him to live (obs. 119, 120). The volume frames the refusal as her choice and the prince's wish, not as forgiveness owed.

### 8.5 Meaningful absences

- The content of the plan is withheld until it is enacted (obs. 019, 024).
- Who the "others" helping are is withheld (obs. 035).
- 閣下 is not named.
- The person Mary does not want to die and the hidden star are not advanced.
- The new Sage is not chosen.
- Monica's own feeling toward Isaac or Cyril is not stated (obs. 040, 126).

### 8.6 The afterword, the jacket and the cards

The afterword gives the coffee ending as the plan from the start, in web and book alike (S37:E000013). It announces:

- a story collection in spring 2025, which is the companion IX -extra-;
- a fully new V10 set in Southerndoor (S37:E000058–E000063);
- a comic of the Louis side story;
- a television anime in 2025.

It mentions the web version and its sequel only by name; the reading draws nothing from either. The jacket blurb is illegible (obs. 187). The cards are listed in section 7.5. All are paratext.

### 8.7 Apparent contradictions

- **The verdict.** The king's verdict of a curse contradicts no narrated fact; it is a fiction within the fiction.
- **Monica's knowledge.** Mary says she knows who killed Emmanuel (obs. 114). The council's version blames Sams (obs. 094). The two are consistent as truth and cover.
- **The seasons.** The coda's summers are distinguished by the move "a year" after the mission and the visit "a year" after graduation. Both fall in the summer after graduation, and are consistent.
