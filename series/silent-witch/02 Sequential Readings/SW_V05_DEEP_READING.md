## 2. Prior-volume developments

*Prepared 2026-10-14 before any V05 narrative member was rendered for reading, from the closed V01 to V04 readings, the closed IV -after- reading, their exit freezes and the master ledger only.*

Monica Everett, the Silent Witch, has turned seventeen. She is nearly three months into a secret mission at Serendia Academy, where, as Monica Norton, a second-year student and the student council's treasurer, she guards the second prince, Felix Arc Ridill, without his knowledge, on the order of Louis Miller, the Barrier Mage. Isabelle Norton covers her by playing her tormentor; her family took up villainy after misreading Louis's request. Nero, her familiar, and Louis's wind spirit Lynn help her. The winter break is near.

Her childhood is known in outline. Her father, the scholar Venedict Rain, was executed as a heretic when she was ten. Her uncle beat and starved her for about a year until she lost language. Hilda Everett, her father's former assistant at the Royal Magic Research Institute, rescued and adopted her and celebrated her last birthday before Minerva. Monica has just sent Hilda her first season's card, and Hilda hopes she will come home for the solstice. At Minerva Bernie Jones befriended her; he has since left to become heir to the Amberd county and declared himself her rival.

At the school festival Monica recovered a stolen Albright curse tool with the help of Ray Albright, the Abyss Curse Mage, and defeated the imperial agents Yuan and Heidi. They had sought a trace on Felix linked to a traitor named Arthur, and the capture of the Silent Witch, for a master; they escaped, and Yuan is under Ray's curse for about a month. A traitor curse-caster serving a patron called 閣下, who knew and hated Venedict Rain, has entered Duke Leinbourg's household, and the craftsman Bartholomeus has entered Leinbourg service. Louis holds an unstated hypothesis about Felix. Glen Dudley, Louis's disciple, unknowingly served as a decoy. Bridget means to take back "her prince".

After the festival, in the companion IV -after-:

- Monica dried Cyril's white rose and keeps it in a locked drawer of treasures. The drawer also holds her father's coffee pot and book, Lana's comb and letter, Felix's peridot pendant, Casey's handkerchief, a violet pouch Lana made her, Isabelle's quill and Felix's birthday card.
- She helped Felix, as his "fellow delinquent", try to read her own paper in secret.
- She and Glen caught an earth spirit.
- She uncovered a curse disguised as a charm, which Isabelle quietly settled.
- She wrote her first season's card.

Cyril defended Glen and taught him that earned skill deserves pride, and beat Byron Garrett in a duel. Elliott holds a creed that crossing rank brings unhappiness, rooted in an uncle's suicide and a withheld earlier event; as a child he was Felix's playmate at Duke Clockford's house, where a small, timid Felix was served by a page boy. Felix does not connect Monica with the Silent Witch; his grandfather checks what he reads. Monica plans to leave the school when Felix graduates, and expects to celebrate Lana's birthday after the winter break.

## 3. Current-volume story synopsis

**The prologue** is set more than half a year before the mission, the day after a New Year ceremony at the castle. Monica, fleeing servants sent by the court factions to court the neutral Sages, takes refuge in the Sages' chamber. There Louis and the Cannon Mage, Bradford Firestone, are gambling at a dragon card game. Bradford pairs with her against Louis. Louis feigns a tell, lets them win, then wins everything with an unexplained rule. Monica, who remembers every discard, finds a card too many, and Louis replies that the game begins before one sits at the table. The two men fight with magic; the Thorn Witch and the Star-reading Witch end it, and find Monica under the table murmuring numbers.

**Chapter one.** A week before the winter break, Monica goes to the Kreme winter market with Isabelle and her maid Agatha. Mary Harvey has prophesied dragon disaster this winter, so Monica cannot visit Kerbeck. A detective hired by someone watches "Monica Norton" and believes Isabelle's cruelty is real. Monica meets Cyril and Glen, advises them on gifts, and buys lavender soap for Hilda and her housemaid. At an Ortelia chime, Glen vows to work at magic and Monica vows to keep doing better among people; Cyril says he expects it of them.

Felix, alone, considers burning a paper on magic he has written in secret. Then a letter from Duke Clockford sends him to Leinbourg for talks with Falfolia and to court Eliane, and ends with news that delights him: he will meet the Silent Witch. Louis appears in Monica's attic. She is to guard Felix officially, as the Silent Witch, in the open, with Glen, who does not know her secret, as the second guard. Elsewhere Bridget, who hired the detective, orders the watch kept up over the break, and thinks that only one person can give her the flower she wants.

**Chapter two.** Felix, Glen and Eliane travel together; Glen keeps Eliane busy with coin tricks while Felix, who sat up finishing his paper, dozes happily. At Leinbourg, a handyman named Bartholomeus believes the real Silent Witch is a tall blonde maid. Felix recognizes the footman Peter Sams from his grandfather's household, and Sams is briefly alarmed. The Witch arrives by a spirit-king summoning, in fact a panic after Nero took human form in mid-flight on her staff. Felix recognizes her attendant, who calls himself Bartholomew Alexander, as the man who once carried Cyril, and concludes, to her alarm, that she was the one who saved Cyril in the woods.

**Chapter three.** Louis had told Glen the Witch kills at a glance; Glen is wary of her. Felix lectures on her selection victory. That night Felix brings the Witch supper and a paper on underwater formulas, "by a friend"; she annotates it seriously and writes that she wants to see more of his work. Nero eats the supper, which Bartholomeus had laced with a Hyatt family love drink, and Eliane's own plan fails. Bartholomeus concludes that the prince and the "false" Witch are lovers.

**Chapter four.** At dawn the Witch writes advice in the earth for Glen's practice of holding two spells, and he decides his master lied. Bartholomeus unveils her, recognizes the girl from the Coalrapton festival, and learns that the blonde maid was Lynn, a spirit. He offers his silence about her and "the prince" in exchange for an introduction to Lynn. Monica thinks he knows of her secret guarding; he thinks they are lovers. Felix, coldly, removes his hand from hers.

**Chapter five.** The Falfolia talks stall on a dragon-knight garrison that Felix believes Duke Clockford intends as a supply base for war with the Empire. During an afternoon hunt, Eliane wanders off with Sams, and Nero senses something almost like a dragon approaching.

**Chapter six.** A cursed green dragon kills Eliane's horse. Glen saves Eliane and Sams, is cursed himself, and still drives the dragon off with a fireball. The dragon then attacks the hunting party. Monica improvises a barrier against curses, but holding two barriers leaves her unable to attack, and Felix shoots the dragon through a fist-sized gap she opens. In its last cry, in the spirits' tongue, the dragon swears never to forgive a particular human.

**Chapter seven.** Glen lies unconscious, his mana drained toward the dragon's body. Felix receives an anonymous letter about the king's illness and reads it as his grandfather's order to secure the throne. He goes out alone with an enchanted gun "to clean up as a puppet should", finds the dead dragon moving under the curse, and nearly dies wondering how his name would be remembered. Monica and Nero save him. Monica burns the curse tool inside the body, but a thread of the curse catches her left hand. In the pain she sees the dragon's memory: a man whose curse tool killed her young, and her decision to eat the child, tool and all, to track him. Nero then turns into the Worgan black dragon, burns the corpse with black flame, and warns Felix that he will eat him if he tells anyone.

**Chapter eight.** Five days later Ray Albright arrives, calls her barrier a textbook achievement, and confirms the curse was human-made. Glen wakes; his mana is above 250, and a past runaway is why Louis took him in. Ray identifies the likely caster as Barry Oates, who betrayed the Albrights ten years ago. Monica proposes a secret inquiry, partly because she suspects Felix knows more.

**Chapter nine.** Felix questions Nero, who explains that he never ordered the wyverns and became Monica's familiar after she pulled a bone from his throat. Felix keeps the secret. When Monica presses him about that night, he answers with prepared stories, then asks whether she belongs to Serendia Academy and catches her flinch and her reaction to a deliberate slip. Nero tells her Felix has a water spirit, though his middle name points to earth. Felix, alone, vows to make the name of the best friend who loved the hero's star live in history, and the vow ends with the word Arc, marked for emphasis.

**Chapter ten.** Monica recognizes Sams, by his body's measurements, as the man in the dragon's memory. With Ray she confronts him in speech; he fights with a curse tool, Ray devours the curse, and Monica drives him out with an illusion of the dying dragon into the arms of Bartholomeus and the hounds. Facing her, Sams calls her Venedict Rain, says he sold Venedict to 閣下, cries that he will not follow Arthur, and is consumed by his own curse. Monica resolves to prove her father innocent and hires Bartholomeus.

**Chapter eleven.** Cyril goes home, and his mother says he has worked hard. Felix leaves with diplomatic success and resolves to find the Witch at Serendia by her injured left hand. The third prince Albert, forced from Minerva to Serendia, sets his servant to find Felix's weaknesses. Hilda tells Monica that a curse-caster whom her father had refused accused him of necromancy and that a great noble pushed the execution through without trial; she will not name the noble. Monica finds a recent note in her father's book, apparently from Porter, about "the truth of the black chalice", and dreams of her father with a novelist friend.

**The epilogue.** On New Year's Day the Thorn Witch is late, and Monica proposes a substitute offering: a giant ice Ortelia chime. Felix vows to find her; Cyril, listening from an inn, vows to bear himself boldly; Monica vows to prove her father's innocence.

**The secret episode.** Bernie, about to inherit, writes to warn Monica that Hubert Dee, who once pursued her for battles, is transferring to Serendia. Bridget, at home, rebukes her sister for speaking of Felix, then whispers "Your Highness" alone, near tears.

## 6. Close reading

### 6.1 Structure: the table, the chime and three vows

V05 leaves the school for the first time since V01. It is built on two framing devices, both set out early.

**The card table.** The prologue's lesson is Louis's: the game begins before one sits at the table, and whoever trusts another's account of the rules deserves to lose (obs. 005, 007). The same scene gives Monica a tell, her shrug when shaken (obs. 004), and shows her one strength in play: she remembers every discard and catches the cheat (obs. 006). Chapter nine replays the lesson against her. She comes to her negotiation with Felix unprepared, shrugs at his question, and loses, remembering Louis's words too late (obs. 129, 130). The prologue is therefore not a comic aside; it supplies the rule by which she is beaten.

**The chime.** The Ortelia chime is a weak spirit's bell that reaches the spirit god (obs. 019). At the market, Glen and Monica make vows before it because a senior is present to witness them (obs. 020–022). The vows run through the volume:

- Glen keeps his in dawn practice and in the curse fight, where he stops in the air to cast (obs. 058, 079).
- Monica fails hers at the negotiating table and grieves (obs. 136), then is sent downstairs by the sight of Glen practising (obs. 137).
- On New Year's Day she builds the chime herself as the Sages' offering (obs. 176, 178).

Three vows close the book under the same sound: Felix's to find her, Cyril's to bear himself boldly, and Monica's to prove her father innocent (obs. 178–180). The book's last line in S30 sets them together under the winter sky.

### 6.2 The Silent Witch in the open

For the first time Monica must act as the Silent Witch before people who know her as Monica Norton, without speaking (obs. 027, 028). The volume treats this as farce and as strain:

- Her descent by spirit-king summoning is a panic that becomes a legend (obs. 039).
- She refuses every meal to keep her veil (obs. 049).
- She writes in the earth and on paper (obs. 059, 125).
- Her attendant introduces himself under an adventure-hero's name and insults the prince (obs. 040, 041).

Her concealment is undone in three directions:

- **Bartholomeus** unveils her and holds a misread secret over her (obs. 063–066).
- **Felix** recognizes Nero, infers that the Witch saved Cyril, sees the black dragon, and finally extracts that she belongs to Serendia (obs. 040, 042, 106, 129).
- **Glen** works beside the Witch without connecting her with his friend; Louis's lie that she kills at a glance keeps him at a distance until her kindness makes him decide his master lied (obs. 044, 045, 060).

Her own speech reverses the volume's opening. She stays silent before Felix and Glen, but speaks aloud to accuse Sams (obs. 142), and to state her father's saying as a declaration rather than a refuge (obs. 144).

### 6.3 Felix: admiration, the paper and the puppet

Felix's attachment to the Silent Witch is the fullest yet. It is recorded in his own viewpoint:

- joy at the prospect of meeting her (obs. 026, 034);
- the kiss on her hand, "like a youth in love" (obs. 042);
- the urge to look well before her (obs. 071);
- the paper she annotates (obs. 053–055);
- after his rescue, respect, longing and adoration (obs. 102);
- the wish to see her face and hear her voice (obs. 133).

Monica, for her part, judges it admiration rather than love, and resolves to remain the Witch he admires so that he will not give up what he loves (obs. 056). The reading records both judgements; neither is the narrator's.

The paper shows Felix as a mage. He wrote, without access to books, a study of underwater wide-area formulas that Monica rates at the level of an upper-year Minerva student (obs. 053). He means to burn it to pursue the throne (obs. 024), and keeps it because of her (obs. 026). A girl's question, "you're giving it up?", is recalled in his memory without a name (obs. 024).

The other side of Felix is set against this. He reads his grandfather's anonymous letter as a command to secure the throne, and the dragon as a staged heroism (obs. 095, 096, 099). He goes out alone "as Clockford's puppet" to clean up (obs. 097), sees himself in the dead dragon driven by a curse (obs. 098), and, facing death, measures how his name would be remembered (obs. 100). The volume closes his arc with his conviction that Clockford, through the researches of Thornley and Sams, means to make him a true puppet, and with his resolve to begin acting for himself (obs. 159). Each of these is his inference; the reading registers them as Felix's beliefs (SW-C-0047).

Three further details extend the cluster around him without explanation (section 6.9):

- the aquamarine contract stone, once a lady's necklace, and eyes he will not see again (obs. 023);
- the water spirit against an earth middle name, with affinity inherited and fixed (obs. 131);
- the vow to his best friend that ends with the word Arc (obs. 135).

### 6.4 The cursed dragon and the curse-caster

The dragon plot joins the volume's two lines, diplomacy and Monica's past.

At the level of diplomacy it is effective. Malet's resistance to the garrison collapses after he lives through the attack, and Felix comes away with more wheat and a proposal for joint dragon defence (obs. 095, 156).

At the level of cause, the volume gives three accounts that converge:

- **The dragon's memory** shows a man whose curse tool killed her young, and her decision to eat the child with the tool to trace him (obs. 104).
- **The caster's viewpoint** says he was ordered to have the prince slay a controlled cursed dragon, and that his patron, 閣下, keeps him as a card against the Albrights (obs. 140).
- **Felix's inference** attributes the scheme to Clockford (obs. 099).

The caster's order matches Felix's inference in substance. The text does not identify 閣下 with Clockford, and the reading does not either (SW-C-0039).

Sams's end binds the plot to Monica. Facing her greenish eyes, he calls her Venedict Rain, says he sold Venedict to 閣下, cries that he will not repeat Arthur's fate, and is consumed by his own curse (obs. 147, 148). Hilda's account then supplies the earlier story: a curse-caster sought Venedict's collaboration on controlling creatures; refused, he caused Venedict to be charged with necromancy, with planted papers and a great noble's pressure, and executed without trial (obs. 166). Hilda will not name the noble (obs. 167).

The father's sentence, in Monica's memory, was secret first-class forbidden research and plotting to overthrow the state (S26:E000645). Sams and Hilda between them make that charge a fabrication by a curse-caster under a patron. Monica's conclusion is plain: her father was killed for someone's convenience, and she will prove him innocent (obs. 149, 180).

### 6.5 Monica's numbers turned outward

V04 recorded numerical absorption as escape, then weapon, then limit (SW-C-0035). V05 adds a use. Monica identifies Sams by the measurements she took from a dragon's memory, and the narration states that numbers, once her refuge, are not that now (obs. 139, 144). She speaks her father's saying, 「世界は数字でできている」, as she accuses him (S26:E000317). The same faculty catches the cheat in the prologue (obs. 006).

The volume also shows its limits. She cannot attack while holding two barriers, and she wishes for Louis's skill (obs. 086). She is defeated at the table by a better negotiator (obs. 130). In the prologue, confusion leaves her murmuring numbers under the table (obs. 008).

### 6.6 Nero: the black dragon disclosed

The volume answers SW-P-0008 in the text. Nero is the Worgan black dragon (obs. 106). The disclosure comes in stages:

- dogs shun him (obs. 061);
- he knows the speech of upper dragons and how they change their throats (obs. 074);
- the curse avoids him, and he eats it (obs. 081, 105);
- then the transformation, set in prose printed inside images (obs. 106).

His own account follows. He left the Empire's mountains when development disturbed them; the Worgan wyverns worshipped him, and he ordered nothing. Monica, sent to kill him, climbed into his mouth to pull out a bird bone, then showed him she could kill him before his black flame charged, and he chose her as his master because dragons obey the stronger (obs. 122–124). The public story that the Silent Witch slew him was untrue, and Felix keeps the secret (obs. 125, 127). Black flame is classed with necromancy and weather control as the greatest taboos (obs. 126), and Nero can use it only in his true form.

Three people now know Nero's nature: Monica, Felix and the reader. Louis does not (obs. 111). Felix's reasoning for silence is political: a Sage with a black-dragon familiar would be made a weapon, and Clockford would do it (obs. 108).

### 6.7 Glen, Ray and the professionals

**Glen.** Glen's arc runs from vow to wound. He trains daily on holding two spells, resists the easier path, takes the Witch's advice (obs. 058–060), and applies it under attack by stopping in the air to cast. He then throws Eliane and Sams clear and drives the dragon off while half consumed (obs. 079, 080). Ray discloses why Louis took him: mana above 250, and a past runaway (obs. 115). Felix now values him as a future Sage to be kept close (obs. 160). His good nature toward Eliane continues: he hides his pain from "a small child" (obs. 117).

**Ray.** Ray is shown as the master of his art. He:

- diagnoses human curse-craft at a glance (obs. 114);
- devours Sams's curse (obs. 145);
- names the traitor (obs. 120).

He holds an ethic: curses exist to make people suffer, and using them to control is scum's work (obs. 134). His insecurity is comic (obs. 112, 113), and Monica answers it seriously, calling curse-craft honourable work (obs. 118).

**Louis.** Louis appears in the prologue and the frame. He cheats at cards, protects only himself in the brawl (obs. 005, 008), and tells Glen the Witch is deadly so as to keep him away (obs. 045). On New Year's Day he is furious at a late colleague (obs. 174). Rosalie is now his wife (obs. 046).

### 6.8 Cyril, Isabelle, Bridget and the others

**Cyril.** Cyril goes home. His mother, who once sighed at his successes, now says he has worked hard (obs. 153, 155). The source of his ethic of patient effort is her weaving maxim (S27:E000206), and he answers that he is her son (obs. 155). Highorn takes him to the New Year audience as heir (obs. 154). At the chime he makes his own vow, and Highorn tells him he can do it (obs. 179). His purchase of lavender, "rare in his hometown", and the soap's welcome at home tie this to the market scene (obs. 018).

**Isabelle.** Isabelle plays the villain even at the market and covers Monica's journey with a body double (obs. 014).

**Bridget.** Bridget is revealed as the employer of the detective who watches Monica (obs. 030). She refuses flowers except from "one person in the world" (obs. 031), and, alone, whispers "Your Highness" in tears (obs. 183). Her sister's remark that Felix was unreliable as a child provokes a rebuke (obs. 183). What Bridget wants is unstated (SW-P-0030).

**Eliane.** Eliane schemes, is humbled, and turns protective toward Glen (obs. 032, 117).

**Albert and the factions.** The third prince Albert, forced from Minerva to Serendia, suspects Felix; he trusts the first prince, Lionel (obs. 161, 162). The third-prince faction joins the second (obs. 158).

**Bernie.** Bernie will be the best count he can, and writes to warn Monica of Hubert Dee (obs. 181).

## 7. Character, directional relationship, world and formal development

### 7.1 Monica Everett

Monica acts in public as the Silent Witch for the first time, without speech (obs. 027, 049). Her developments:

- She keeps her market vow imperfectly, losing to Felix and recovering (obs. 021, 130, 136, 137).
- She improvises an anti-curse barrier praised as textbook-changing (obs. 085, 112).
- She speaks aloud as a Sage to accuse a murderer (obs. 142).
- She uses numbers outward, as evidence (obs. 139, 144).
- She learns that her father was framed under a great noble's protection, and vows to prove his innocence even at the cost of her seat (obs. 149, 166, 170, 180).

Her bond with Nero is disclosed in full (obs. 123, 124), and she misses his company when he sleeps through the cold (obs. 168). She visits Hilda for the first time since becoming a Sage (obs. 164). Ledger: SW-S-0103 to SW-S-0107.

### 7.2 Felix Arc Ridill

Felix's admiration for the Silent Witch is stated at length, with respect, longing and adoration (obs. 042, 102, 133). His own magic scholarship is shown in the paper (obs. 053). His political self:

- reads Clockford's orders (obs. 096);
- calls himself a puppet (obs. 097);
- resolves to act against Clockford's design (obs. 159).

He knows that the Witch's familiar is the black dragon, and that she is at Serendia (obs. 107, 129). He plans to find her by her injured hand (obs. 160). His private vow to his best friend ends in the name Arc (obs. 135). Ledger: SW-S-0108 to SW-S-0110.

## 8. Alternative readings, absence and contradiction

### 8.1 "Felix is in love with the Silent Witch"

The volume's own words support it:

- "like a youth in love" (obs. 042);
- "like a boy going to meet his first love" (S14:E000246);
- longing and adoration (obs. 102).

Monica judges it admiration for a Sage, not love (obs. 056). The two judgements belong to different focalizers, and the narration does not settle them. The reading records Felix's stated feelings toward the Witch and Monica's interpretation, and makes no claim about romance on either side (SW-C-0023).

### 8.2 "Clockford is 閣下"

Three things point that way:

- Felix believes Clockford staged the dragon (obs. 099);
- the caster's patron ordered that the prince slay a dragon (obs. 140);
- Thornley, whom the caster names, worked for 閣下 in V04 (SW-C-0012).

Against the identification:

- No line names 閣下.
- Monica suspects Duke Leinbourg and then doubts it (obs. 169).
- Hilda refuses to name the great noble (obs. 167).
- The text keeps the caster's 閣下 and Hilda's "great noble" as separate references.

The reading registers no identification (SW-C-0039, SW-P-0029).

### 8.3 Normative evaluation: the staged dragon

Criterion: whether the volume registers the cost of political advantage gained from harm. Felix registers it in irony:

- 「よくできたシナリオだ」 (S19:E000174);
- guilt toward Glen (S24:E000343–E000351).

He also uses it: he takes the heroic credit, which secures the treaty (obs. 095, 156). The volume shows the dragon's suffering, and Monica's pity for it, through the mother's memory (obs. 104). The reading records that the volume locates the harm in the caster and his patron, while letting the prince's gain stand.

### 8.4 Normative evaluation: silence as protection

Criterion: whether withholding knowledge to protect someone is weighed against that person's autonomy.

- Hilda withholds the noble's name (obs. 167).
- Monica withholds her father's name from Ray (S27:E000714).
- Felix withholds what he knows from Monica (obs. 128).
- Monica withholds her identity from Felix and Glen.

Each silence protects someone. Each also leaves another person acting blind. The volume makes Monica decide against Hilda's protective silence: she will seek the truth at any cost (obs. 170). The reading records the conflict and does not judge it.

### 8.5 Meaningful absences

- Lana, Claudia, Neil, Elliott and the school appear only through cards and memory.
- Isabelle appears only at the market.
- Louis appears only in the frame.
- The Thorn Witch and the Jewel Mage are named; the Thorn Witch never appears.
- 閣下, Arthur, the black chalice and the great noble are named and withheld.
- Felix's reason for having a water spirit is withheld.
- The novelist guest in Monica's dream is unnamed (S27:E000769).

### 8.6 The afterword, the jacket and the cards

The afterword says V05 is the winter-break volume, set away from the school, and that V06 returns to school life in its latter half (S40:E000011; S40:E000077). The jacket summarizes the official guard and the dragon (obs. 187). The cards are listed in section 7.5. All of these are paratext.

### 8.7 Apparent contradictions

None of material weight inside V05. The date of the Heims-Naria job remains as recorded at IV -after- (SW-P-0035).
