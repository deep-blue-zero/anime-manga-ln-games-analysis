## 2. Prior-volume developments

*Prepared 2026-10-14 before any V02 narrative member was opened, from the closed V01 reading, its exit freeze `SW-FR-V01-EXIT` and the master ledger only.*

Monica Everett is sixteen, in her seventeenth year, and is one of the Ridill Kingdom's Seven Sages, the Silent Witch. She is the only human who can cast without chanting. She learned to do it because she could not speak in front of people, and she believes she holds her seat out of pity and a vacancy. Louis Miller, the Barrier Mage and a fellow Sage, has been secretly ordered by the king to guard the second prince, Felix Arc Ridill, without Felix knowing. Louis cannot enter Serendia Academy, which Felix's grandfather Duke Clockford controls, so he coerced Monica into going instead. She is enrolled as Monica Norton, a second-year student and the supposedly despised attendant of Isabelle Norton, daughter of Count Kerbeck. Isabelle is in fact her most devoted admirer and stages villainous cruelty to protect her. Monica lives in an attic room with Nero, a black cat who talks, reads adventure novels and can take the form of a black-haired man; what he is has not been disclosed.

In her first days Monica made one friend, Lana Colette, who re-plaited her hair and to whom she now speaks in plain form. She caught Selma Karsh, a classmate who had twice tried to drop heavy objects on the prince to save her embezzling fiancé Aaron O'Brien. She found that Aaron, Selma and the vice-president Cyril Ashley all showed the marks of restricted mind-interference magic. She treated Cyril's runaway over-absorption in a silent night fight he remembers as a battle with a monster, and repaired his defective brooch. Then she confronted her homeroom teacher and council adviser Victor Thornley, the embezzlement's organizer and the user of the memory magic, and sealed him in a dream. Public credit for the arrest went to Louis.

Felix, who had tested her with his keys and found her checking the council's entire ledger history, appointed her treasurer. In private he treats her as a frightened squirrel to tame, a game to pass what he calls the remainder of his life. He kissed her swollen cheek while she was absorbed in numbers; only Nero, the officers present and the reader know. Felix hides a water spirit, Wildianu, and a command of magic, lies to his officers about his night outings, and adores the Silent Witch without recognizing her in Monica. Cyril, who now respects Monica's work and calls her by her office, holds an unverified suspicion connecting her small ink-stained hand with the hand that reached for him in the forest. Louis privately respects her as a monster in power, reads the king's order as surveillance of Felix rather than protection, and has placed a disciple of his own in the school without telling her. The council secretary Elliott Howard and the secretary Bridget Graham have not accepted her; Neil Clay Maywood has welcomed her.

Unresolved at the boundary are the identity of Louis's disciple; Felix's wish, his nights and the source of the duke's hold on him; whether Felix or Cyril will connect Monica with the Silent Witch; who the man in Monica's dream was and what happened to her father; who the Minerva friend was; what Nero is; whether her use of mind magic has consequences; the role of the Jewel Mage, whose brooch failed; whether Elliott and Bridget will accept her; and whether her speech gains will generalize. Her mission runs until Felix graduates, about a year.


## 3. Current-volume story synopsis

The volume opens about two weeks after Monica Everett enrolled at Serendia Academy as Monica Norton. It is a holiday. Monica owns nothing to wear but her uniform and an old robe, and in the girls’ dormitory that marks her out. Caroline Simmons, the count’s daughter whose quarrel sent Monica down the stairs in the first volume, blocks her way to mock her, and Isabelle Norton rescues her in the only way her cover allows, by abusing her louder. Lana Colette invites Monica to go shopping for a comb at a jeweller’s; Monica, sure she would shame Lana in such a shop, refuses, and Lana leaves offended. Monica walks to the town of Kreme alone, panics in the crowd, loses Nero, and is helped by a cheerful, accented young man of about her age who flies off to fetch the cat. Then the alarm bell rings: a stray earth dragon has come near the town. The young man flies off to hold it. Monica has Nero, in human form, carry her up the town’s clock tower. From there she watches the young man fail to hurt the dragon with his fireballs and waits for the moment one of them flies. Under its cover, by remote casting and without a chant, she sends a twig-thin flame arrow through the dragon’s brow. The soldiers cheer the young man, who calls it a lucky hit. Nero points out that Monica no longer flinches from his human shape. Lana, persuaded by her maid that what her friend wanted was not a silver comb, comes back with a bag of warm chestnuts and a map to a shop that sells wooden ones, and Monica says aloud that she wants to go with her.

Two other scenes close the prologue. In the capital, Louis Miller buys clothes for the daughter he is sure his wife will bear, and a plain dress and coat for Monica as a reward for catching Thornley. He tells his spirit Lynn that he has also placed a disciple of his own in the school as a decoy for Felix’s suspicion, and has told him nothing. And in the academy an unnamed person, embittered that the central nobility treats dragon harm as a distant inconvenience, takes from a locked drawer a red jewel set in gold, with three rivets on its back, and resolves to use it while contractors are bringing materials in for the school festival.

Back at school, Monica finds that the new teacher of basic magic is William MacLaggan, her former teacher at the mage academy Minerva. Through a misapplied piece of Louis’s advice she accepts Felix’s offer to guide her round the elective classes, and he takes her to MacLaggan’s room. The young man from Kreme turns out to be Glen Dudley, a transfer student like her and the classmate of the council’s Neil Clay Maywood. Glen begs her not to mention his flying: he is an apprentice, and his frightening master forbids magic without supervision. MacLaggan, short-sighted, does not appear to recognize her. He praises the Silent Witch to the class as his best pupil and then makes everyone touch a crystal that measures magical power. Monica’s last measurement was 202, far beyond anyone ordinary. She is saved when Glen’s touch cracks the instrument and pushes its needle past its limit, and in the confusion she whispers that she needs the lavatory and slips away.

She drifts into the chess class. Elliott Howard, who despises her as a commoner, drags her to a board to humiliate her, teaches her the moves, and plays her with his queen removed. Monica, who had never played, takes the white pieces and dismantles him with textbook precision. Faced with losing, Elliott uses castling, a rule he has not taught her, and wins. Monica concedes without complaint and apologizes for having called the game simple. Elliott is left with the victory and with shame. The chess teacher, Boyd, a huge man who frightens her into stammering, presses an enrolment form on her.

Elliott then takes Monica to check a contractor’s carriage that has arrived a week early. She sees at once that its painted crest is wrong: the wheel has ten spokes instead of twelve and the bull has no tail. Elliott realizes the men are impostors and orders her to run for the guards, saying a noble is bound to protect commoners. When one man draws a knife Monica sends faint shocks into the horses and knocks out both men in moments hidden from Elliott, then nearly gets trampled by the carriage she has set bolting. A tall, strong girl from another class, Casey Grove, leaps onto the runaway and calms the horses. The men were thieves. Casey invites Monica to sit on the driver’s seat, recognizes the counting song Monica loves as a livestock song from her own country home, and talks her into taking riding as her second elective.

The dance class reveals that Monica and Glen, the two transfers, cannot dance at all; they must pass a retest or lose their afternoons until the festival. Neil, Casey and Lana coach them, Lana announcing in front of everyone that she is Monica’s friend. In the council room Bridget Graham says a council member who fails a dance test is an embarrassment and a question about Felix’s judgment. Felix lifts Monica’s chin and asks her to meet his expectations. That night the phrase reopens an old wound. Monica remembers her father, a learned man who studied the body as numbers, who met every expectation and was reviled and stoned at the end, and the fire. She remembers too the friend at Minerva whose praise she most wanted, who turned away from her when she mastered chantless magic. She decides that, as Elliott had said of nobles, she has duties attached to her roles, and practises dancing with Nero until midnight.

Felix and Cyril Ashley come to watch the practice. Cyril shows Glen how to lead, and Felix, dancing with Monica, chats to her so that she will stop thinking. She dances well because she spends the whole dance calculating the reflective properties of the fifty-eight facets of his brooch, and says so. Both transfers pass. Afterwards Felix takes tea with Bridget, who remembers a childhood in which he was a poor dancer who kept treading on her feet, and watches how he reacts. Felix admits privately that he talked too much. He is wary of Glen, whom he takes for the dragon-slayer of Kreme, and whose master he knows. Glen throws a barbecue behind the school to celebrate; Felix joins it, eats a skewer, and calls himself an accomplice. Lynn brings Louis’s gift of clothes and asks for a plan to secure the festival.

The tea-party class is the next ordeal. Isabelle supplies tea and teaches Monica the gestures of a stage villainess. Monica’s table holds Lana, Casey and a gloomy, beautiful, sharp-tongued girl called Claudia, who belittles Lana’s costly tea as out of place and then baits Monica by talking about the Silent Witch, whose given name, she observes, is Monica too. When Monica goes to brew her own tea she finds it thrown in the bin, with the folded paper she had used to mark it. Casey offers her own tea. Monica instead runs to her room and comes back with coffee made in a steam pot no one present has seen. Claudia rules that it is not tea and is out of the question. Monica says she wanted her friends to taste what she likes best, and that she is the most out of place of all. Lana, who had been privately hurting because her own gesture of generosity had been called vulgar, says she likes it.

A week later Caroline invites Monica to a public tea, demands that she tell Cyril the staircase fall was an accident, and when Monica refuses serves her tea dosed with a bitter eye medicine, meaning her to choke and be laughed at. Monica, used to coffee, drinks it all and collapses, delirious. In the delirium she sees her father tied to a stake and burning while a crowd calls him a heretic who touched what is forbidden, and his papers thrown on the fire while she tries to memorize the numbers on them. She sees her uncle bringing a bottle down on her for reciting numbers, blaming his brother’s research for ruining his trade, and threatening her with a poker if she speaks of it outside, while she promises to keep quiet. Claudia diagnoses the poison from Caroline’s pupils and makes Monica vomit. Monica wakes in the infirmary holding Felix’s hand, throws herself on the floor to apologize, and is put back to bed by Cyril, who tells her a victim does not bow. Claudia turns out to be Cyril’s sister. She has been following Monica because she believed Monica was stealing her fiancé, who proves to be Neil. Felix, walking away, recognizes in Monica’s apology a small boy saying he was sorry for failing as a royal, and is angry.

Caroline is interrogated by Felix and Cyril. Isabelle joins them, serves Caroline’s group tea dosed from a similar bottle, watches Caroline spit hers out, drinks her own dosed cup without a flicker, and announces she will tell her father: Caroline has insulted the house that protects her family’s lands from dragons. Caroline is to be expelled. Isabelle’s talk of the Silent Witch shooting down the wyverns makes Cyril remember the silent monster who fought him in the forest. Felix, alone with his spirit Wildianu, reveals that he saw the wyverns fall with his own eyes during a secret journey to the east, and that he keeps the Silent Witch’s student papers in a locked drawer and has read them until the paper creased. He tells Elliott that he will fulfil the role of Felix Arc Ridill, and adds silently that he will do so while he bears the name.

Monica convalesces for a week in Isabelle’s room, reads a romance and cannot understand why anyone expects so much from another person. Back in the attic she finds a week of notes from Lana under her door and locks them in the drawer that otherwise holds only her father’s coffee pot. Cyril, who has done all her work, makes her drink chocolate to educate her palate; Felix catches him and explains that the chocolate’s new technology comes from Randall, the homeland of the first prince’s mother, and that Cyril feared being thought a partisan.

Over lunch Casey says a kind patron paid her way into the school; Claudia remarks that it is careless to tell outsiders the council’s schedule. That afternoon Monica and Cyril oversee timber at the east gate while Felix and Elliott receive fireworks at the west warehouse. A rope snaps, timber falls toward Casey, and Monica deflects it by a few degrees with a silent gust. On the way to the infirmary Monica tells Casey that the rope was cut on site, and that outsiders can no longer carry blades. Casey admits it, then admits she threw away Monica’s tea, and claims to have befriended her only to reach the prince. Nero bursts in: something in the west warehouse is gathering magical power. Monica identifies it as a 螺炎, an assassination device that bursts into spiralling fire, and Casey’s reaction confirms that its target is Felix. Casey explains why. Her home, Bright, on the border with Randall, lost a village and her three brothers to dragons while the kingdom never came, and Randall’s knights secretly did. She believes that Duke Clockford means to make his puppet grandson king and use Randall as a stepping-stone to war with the Empire. Her father ordered her to win Felix or kill him, and gave her the jewel with three rivets.

Only Casey can stop the device, and she will not. Monica, deciding that this is her duty as a Sage and that no one can help her with it, attacks the school from outside so that Louis’s defensive barrier reveals its node in the old garden. She burns through the gate’s lock in front of Casey, triggers a lethal trap of roses she recognizes as the Thorn Witch’s work, and, to break it in time, opens the gate of the wind spirit king, speaking aloud the one chant she cannot omit, in which she names herself as the Silent Witch. She then rewrites Louis’s barrier in seconds into a tiny airless shell around the device, which bursts and goes out. She collapses, drained.

Louis arrives and takes Casey into custody. Monica asks what will happen to her. When Louis speaks of mind-breaking interrogation and certain execution, she bargains, pointing out that an assassination attempt by a Randall-connected partisan of the first prince would damage Louis’s own faction. Louis agrees that if Casey confesses fully she will go to a convent instead, privately calculating that he cannot afford to lose Monica’s cooperation, and warns her that Monica Norton is a temporary person she must not get attached to. Casey refuses to thank or apologize, tells Monica to hate her or forget her, apologizes only for the riding and embroidery she promised and cannot give, and says goodbye using her name. In the council room, empty of magic, Monica leans without noticing on Cyril’s back, which is rich in the power his body absorbs. Cyril apologizes to her for not checking the rope, since the accident has been put down to his oversight, and Monica, knowing the truth, weeps until she falls asleep.

The secret episode follows Felix to the ruined fountain, where he overhears Louis say that the Silent Witch neutralized an assassination device meant for him. He is overjoyed, and begins to wonder where on campus she might be hiding. The chess teacher plans to enter Monica in a tournament, and MacLaggan, learning that Monica and Glen are both transfers, says that things will become interesting. In the royal castle the Star-reading Witch, Mary Harvey, cannot see Felix’s star, and senses that a great event is coming.

## 6. Sustained diagnostic close reading

Volume 01 built its effects on the distance between the Silent Witch of legend, the shabby girl the school sees, and the frightened person the narration shows from inside. Volume 02 keeps that structure and changes what it is for. Its argument, stated in advance, is that Monica’s silence has a history and her magic has a duty. The book supplies the history in two installments, a memory in chapter 4 and a drugged delirium in chapter 7, and it supplies the duty through an unlikely teacher, Elliott Howard, whose class prejudice contains a code of obligation that Monica borrows and turns into the climax’s decision. Between them runs a story about friendship tested by a friend who is lying. Formally the volume does three new things. It moves Monica’s power inside her own viewpoint, so that the reader now watches her calculate, not only others gape. It multiplies the outsider’s view: Lana, Casey, Elliott, Claudia, Caroline, Isabelle, Louis and Felix each receive scenes of their own. And it lets the concealment strain from both sides, as Monica hides from Casey and Casey hides from Monica, and as both secrets break on the same afternoon.

Observation numbers in the form “obs. 007” point to the register in section 6.18.

### 6.1 Two kinds of courage on one holiday

The prologue is a diptych. Its first half is a social ordeal that the narration ranks above war: going shopping on a day off is, for Monica, harder than charging a flock of dragons (S11:E000119; obs. 002). The ordeal is drawn with exactness. She owns only her uniform and a shapeless robe, so in a dormitory full of girls in dresses she is visible before she says anything (S11:E000047–E000048). When Lana invites her to a jeweller’s, Monica refuses because she has already pictured the result: she would be out of place, and Lana would be ashamed to be seen with her (S11:E000153–E000157; obs. 003). Lana’s reply, 「あっそ、じゃあいいわよ」 (S11:E000165), is all offended pride. Nothing has happened except an imagined humiliation, and Monica has acted on it as if it were fact. This is the social form of the belief V01 recorded about her Sage’s seat, that she is present only by mistake (SW-C-0004), and the word for it, 場違い, will become the volume’s refrain.

The second half is a dragon. Monica crouches in the street with her ears covered, breathing in shallow gasps, retreating into numbers (S11:E000213–E000220): the mechanism SW-C-0002 names, now in a public street. A stranger helps her, and she manages to speak to him about her cat (S11:E000233). When the alarm sounds, the narration gives her reasons in order. Lana is in the town; the dragon might turn toward the school and the prince (S11:E000280–E000284). Then the sentence that the climax of the volume will repeat in a larger key: 「わたしが、やらなきゃ」 (S11:E000313). The comedy that follows is deliberate deflation, a side stitch and a run the narrator calls hopeless (S11:E000315–E000322), but the decision has been made first.

The kill itself is the first time the series narrates Monica’s power from inside her. She casts sight magic without a chant, measures the distance, and waits for the moment when dragon, young man and fireball line up (S11:E000410–E000413). Her face loses all expression and her eyes catch the light as a fresh green (S11:E000417; obs. 004). The narrator explains the art in a paragraph: remote casting moves a spell’s origin far from the caster at a heavy cost in accuracy, and doing it without a chant would leave any mage speechless (S11:E000502–E000506). Then the viewpoint crosses to the young man, who sees nothing of it. A thin, many-times-reinforced arrow pierces the brow 「恐ろしく静かに、正確に」 (S11:E000463), the same pairing of adverbs Isabelle used for the wyverns in V01, and the soldiers slap the young man on the back (S11:E000458–E000482; obs. 005). The credit goes to someone else, as the credit for Thornley went to Louis in V01, but this time Monica arranges the misattribution herself. The volume’s first act of heroism is designed to be invisible.

Two small results follow, and they matter more to the book than the dragon. Nero points out that Monica no longer shakes when he is in human form, though large men had been her particular fear (S11:E000545–E000549; obs. 006); his phrase, that she has become a little tougher, becomes the thing she tells herself an hour later. And Lana comes back. Her maid, a minor figure with a decisive line, tells her that what her friend wanted was probably not a silver comb, and that she herself uses a wooden one (S11:E000583–E000588; obs. 007). Lana returns with a warm bag of chestnuts she claims to be tired of and a map to a shop that sells wooden combs (S11:E000634–E000642). Monica, remembering Nero, forces out the request, 「い、一緒にっ…………行きたい……です」 (S11:E000657; obs. 008). The ellipsis printed inside the sentence is the effort. The prologue thus pairs two acts of courage and makes the reader feel that the second, asking a friend to go shopping, is the harder one for her. The volume’s frontispiece has already printed the clock-tower shot with its explanatory text (S03:E000004; obs. 101), so the reader meets the dragon as a promised spectacle and the chestnuts as the surprise.

### 6.2 What Louis buys, and whom he spends

Louis’s scene in the capital is one of the volume’s most economical. He is introduced with a narrator’s parenthesis, soon-to-be father, twenty-seven, giddy (S11:E000681), and the scene keeps that tone while delivering two facts with consequences. The first is the gift. He chooses for Monica a high-collared navy day dress and a coat, plain because her cover is a despised adoptee, as a reward for two weeks in which she has joined the council and caught a criminal, and he thinks, half fondly, that she would prefer a mathematics book (S11:E000718–E000724). The message he dictates for Lynn to deliver includes his own commentary on carrots and sticks (S11:E000730), and chapter 5 lets Lynn repeat it verbatim to Monica, who notes that the second half might have been better left out (S17:E000693–E000698). Louis is not only a coercer; he pays.

The second fact is the disciple. V01’s secret episode said Louis had placed a disciple of his in the school (SW-P-0001). Here he says why. Felix is quick to detect anyone hidden around him, and a transfer student will draw his suspicion; so Louis enrolled his disciple at the same time as Monica, as a decoy, and told him nothing, neither that he is a decoy nor what the mission is. He describes the disciple as a bad liar, big in body and voice, who once wrecked a building at Minerva (S11:E000739–E000752; obs. 010). Lynn twice names this heartless (S11:E000712; S11:E000762). Louis’s answer, that testing a pupil is a teacher’s duty (S11:E000766), is the same structure as his treatment of Monica: use and investment fused into one act (SW-C-0017).

The volume never names the disciple. It lets the reader assemble him. Glen Dudley transfers at the same time as Monica (S14:E000066–E000067), is tall and loud, speaks with a downtown accent (S14:E000052; S14:E000064), and is an apprentice whose frightening master forbids him to use magic unsupervised on pain of being rolled in a mat and hung from a roof (S14:E000137–E000142; obs. 015). His master suddenly ordered him to attend Serendia and paid the fees, and is stronger than almost anyone he knows, though not, he says, the Cannon Mage or the Silent Witch (S17:E000467–E000481). Felix, observing, suspects Glen precisely because he is a transfer with the power to kill a dragon, whose master is 「あの人物」, a phrase the book prints with emphasis dots (S17:E000414; obs. 040). Every clue points one way, and the decoy plan visibly works: Felix offers to coach the dancing in order to watch Glen and Monica together, finds no link, and remains wary of Glen (S17:E000419–E000428). This reading records the identification as strong inference, not as stated fact, because the text deliberately leaves the master unnamed and Monica, who has heard both halves, does not connect them.

### 6.3 Three accounts of the silence

V01’s narrator explained chantless magic as a comic overcorrection: a girl too shy to chant in front of examiners learned to do without the chant (V01 obs. 007). Volume 02 sets two other explanations beside it and never reconciles them. The chapter-opener plate, in English and Japanese, calls the Silent Witch the genius who derives a difficult formula’s optimal solution in an instant and therefore needs no chant (S12:E000004; obs. 011). MacLaggan tells his class that his former pupil’s understanding of formulas was so great that she came to cast without chanting, that her formulas are on the written examinations, and that she overturned the theory of modern magic (S14:E000240–E000248; obs. 016). Felix, alone with his spirit, explains chanting as the written-out intermediate steps of a hard equation, and the Silent Witch as the one person who can go straight to the answer (S20:E000621–E000626). The three agree, and the volume lets the plate’s wording reappear almost word for word in Felix’s mind, so that the public explanation is the one the paratext endorses.

Against them stands what the narrative shows. In chapter 1, alone in her own head, Monica reminds herself that she cannot chant in front of people and therefore can use only chantless magic, which would expose her at once (S13:E000218–E000220). And chapter 7 supplies a childhood in which speaking in front of people was punished with a bottle (section 6.9). The expert account is not false; Monica plainly does compute faster than anyone around her. But it is an account of capacity, and the narrative is interested in cause. The volume’s irony is that the kingdom admires as genius what began, on the evidence now available, as a child’s defence against being heard. SW-C-0001 is therefore revised in section 9 to hold both layers.

Chapter 1’s comedy turns on a third kind of speech, the borrowed script. Asked whether she will take a magic elective, Monica remembers Louis’s advice for dealing with tiresome people: say you will consider it positively and let it fade (S13:E000224). She applies the formula to the wrong question, so that her answer accepts Felix’s offer to escort her: 「はいっ、前向きに検討しますっ！」 (S13:E000239; obs. 012). Felix, who asked the second question immediately after the first, takes the answer and fixes the appointment (S13:E000244–E000251). The joke is in the chapter title, which renames her the witch of slips of the tongue, in Louis’s voice. It is also a precise picture of how Monica speaks when she must: she reaches for prepared words, and prepared words fit the wrong occasion.

### 6.4 Measured and hidden

Chapter 2 tests concealment against an instrument. MacLaggan’s lecture divides talent into quantity of power, understanding of formulas and control of power (S14:E000233), and his crystal reads quantity on a scale to 250 (S14:E000271–E000275). The narration gives the bands and then Monica’s private number: Sages must exceed 150 and are measured annually, and her last reading was 202, with growth possible in late adolescence (S14:E000279–E000282; obs. 017). The crisis that follows is not about magic at all. Monica must say one ordinary sentence, that she needs to go to the lavatory, and the narration spends a long paragraph on her inability to find the right gap in the conversation to say it (S14:E000294–E000310). She is rescued by accident: Glen’s hand turns the crystal red, cracks it and drives the needle off the scale, and in the uproar she can tug his sleeve and whisper (S14:E000319–E000348). The narration notes that fewer than a handful of people in the country exceed 250, two of them among the Sages, and that everyone assumes the instrument has failed (S14:E000331–E000335; obs. 018). It does not say otherwise.

The sequence repeats in another key the volume’s pattern of concealment by other people’s assumptions (V01 obs. 005). Monica is safe because a louder anomaly arrives first. Glen, too, has something to hide and is protected by the same disbelief. The two transfer students, unaware of their connection, shield each other.

### 6.5 Castling

The chess scene is the volume’s best piece of sustained point of view, and it belongs to Elliott. It begins as cruelty. He finds her peering into the classroom, calls her the prince’s favourite squirrel, adopting Felix’s private lexicon, and pulls her in by the wrist (S14:E000381–E000391). He explains that chess is a mock war, a noble’s training in strategy (S14:E000418). Monica asks which piece the battle mages are, then what their range is, the strength of their barriers, the infantry’s weapons, the fort’s provisions, the season, the slope of the ground and the wind (S14:E000425–E000432; obs. 019). The narration explains that she once trained with Louis in exercises of the magic corps and that he drilled her in reading strategic maps, because shooting wyverns needs terrain and wind (S14:E000441–E000443). The scene uses her literalism for comedy, and also lets the reader see a professional in a field Elliott takes for a parlour game.

Then the viewpoint becomes Elliott’s, and the reader watches him recognize her expressionless face from the ledger review (S14:E000463–E000466), give her white and the first move, and realize from her question that she already knows the first move matters (S14:E000484). Her handling of the pieces is a novice’s, her choices a textbook’s, her replies instant (S14:E000499–E000514). He is, the narration says, among the best three players in the room, and he is losing (S14:E000528–E000543). The choice that follows is the volume’s first moral crisis, and it is his. Castling would save him; he has not taught it; the spectators do not know he has not taught it (S14:E000549–E000562; obs. 020). His hand moves, the narration says, of itself. When she looks up, he thinks 「やめろ、見るな」 (S14:E000568).

Monica’s response is the key to her. She does not object. She concedes, explains that if castling is a legal move she cannot win, and apologizes for having called the game easy: whatever the best move, the opponent is a person, and persons are uncertain (S14:E000577–E000589; obs. 021). She treats the rule she was not told as a fact of the world rather than as an injustice to her. In V01 she accepted that rank decides truth; here she accepts that the board belongs to whoever knows its rules. Elliott is left with 「苦い敗北感と……自己嫌悪」 (S14:E000591) and an apology he cannot make. The illustration splits the page between them, Monica calm above the white king, Elliott below with sweat on his face (S14:E000522; obs. 102). Elliott then supplies her name to the frightening teacher she cannot answer (S14:E000620), a small act that is the first help he has given her.

The scene sets up chapter 3, where Elliott, still unable to apologize, takes her to inspect the carriage, and where his creed is finally spoken.

### 6.6 A crest, a tail, and noblesse oblige

Chapter 3 opens with a comic drawing contest that is also a plant. Cyril has drawn the Abbott company’s crest for Elliott, who sees a rabbit and a rotting orange where Cyril insists on a bull and a wheel (S15:E000022–E000026). Monica, after three tries at interrupting, draws the crest from memory in about a minute, with freehand circles and straight lines, a twelve-spoked wheel and a bull facing left (S15:E000089–E000107; obs. 022). She explains that she likes remembering figures and reproducing them, and that at Minerva she used to copy the patterns of enchantment formulas until her hands were black with ink (S15:E000102–E000104). Cyril notices only that his bull lacked a tail (S15:E000114). The joke is set up so that the tail will matter.

When the carriage arrives, Monica stops, her face empties, and she says that the crest does not match the record: ten spokes, not twelve (S15:E000239–E000247). Elliott doubts her, and she answers with a sentence unlike anything she has said to him before, 「わたしは、一度見た図形は、忘れません」 (S15:E000250; obs. 023). The narration, in Elliott’s viewpoint, describes the state she is in: when absorbed, she becomes frighteningly expressionless and seems to cut away everything but the object (S15:E000251–E000252). That is the same mechanism of absorption that protects her from pain (SW-C-0002), seen from outside as competence. The second discrepancy, the missing tail, decides him, and the narration gives his reasoning in his own idiom: a first-class supplier would not make a mistake worthy of Cyril (S15:E000272).

Then comes the line on which the volume turns. Elliott tells her to run for the guards while he stalls. She objects that he will be in danger. He answers that he is a noble, that 「貴族には平民を守る義務がある」, and that she, unlike him, has no duties at all (S15:E000312; obs. 024). The narration explains his creed at once. He is a man obsessed with rank; nobles should behave as nobles and commoners as commoners; and the duty of a noble includes setting an example and protecting the weak (S15:E000313–E000316). He is, the narration adds, no good at swordplay, fighting or magic (S15:E000306). The speech is condescending, since it tells Monica she has no responsibilities, and it is brave. The volume refuses to separate the two.

Monica’s fight is narrated in her viewpoint as calculation under constraint. A knife makes the men enemies, and an assassination cannot be ruled out; but she cannot be seen to act, and a sudden faint would be suspicious (S15:E000335–E000338). She apologizes silently to the horses, gives them a faint shock, and knocks out each man at the moment his fall can be blamed on the horses or the carriage, always when the horse’s body blocks Elliott’s view (S15:E000342–E000365). The narrator calls it 「酷く地味で、それでいて恐ろしく高度な戦い」 (S15:E000362). Then the comedy reverses the competence: the horses bolt too hard, the carriage circles back, she cannot climb a tree, and she falls (S15:E000378–E000403). Her thought, a cascade of rejected options each of which would reveal her (S15:E000404), is the purest statement of her situation at school. She is the most powerful person present and the least free to act.

Chapter 4 shows what Elliott’s sentence did. That night, after Felix’s demand that she meet his expectations, Monica thinks of Elliott choosing to stay. She has never considered that the roles she was pushed into carry duties, she reflects; she is both a Sage and the council’s treasurer, and if she turns away from the duty, then every time Cyril calls her by her office she will feel guilty (S16:E000388–E000392; obs. 033). She gets up and practises dancing. The lesson comes from the person in the council who most despises her, and the volume does not soften the irony.

### 6.7 Dancing without thinking

The dance chapters are comedy built on the mechanism this reading has been tracing. Monica fails because she thinks: she reads manuals, explains the waltz in terms of base three, and proposes to analyse the tempo, the stride and the angles of hips and shoulders (S16:E000103; S16:E000277). Cyril’s verdict, that she should move her body before using her head (S16:E000281), is correct and useless. What works is an accident of her nature. Felix, dancing with her, tells her to forget the steps and talk, and compliments her eyes, her hair, her comb (S17:E000199–E000234). Her attention fixes on his brooch, and the text runs his compliments and her calculations in alternating paragraphs: he suggests a green dress and autumn roses while she computes that a fifty-eight-faceted stone cut for reflection could strengthen a reflective barrier (S17:E000241–E000251; obs. 038). She dances well, glowing, and explains why (S17:E000269). Neil points out that she had thought of nothing but irrelevancies; Felix’s eyes darken; she tries to repair it by crediting the brooch (S17:E000276–E000284). The narrator closes with the deadpan observation that she has learned to survive dancing by absorption (S17:E000288).

The scene is funny, and it is also a portrait of a relation. Felix uses the full repertoire of romantic address, and none of it reaches her, not because she resists it but because her mind is elsewhere. V01 recorded no romantic interest on Monica’s side (V01 section 8.2); V02 adds that Felix’s charm is a performance she does not register as one. Cyril’s lesson, by contrast, works through a change of manner she can feel: the shouting vice-president becomes, for one dance, a careful partner who guides with his hands and eyes, and her relief afterwards is that he is shouting again (S17:E000103–E000122; obs. 037).

### 6.8 Bridget’s question and Felix’s answer

The tea between Bridget and Felix is short and is the volume’s densest scene about Felix. The narration calls it a battle under the surface (S17:E000341). Bridget, a marquis’s daughter and a clever woman whose father prefers women a little stupid (S17:E000318; S17:E000351), begins by remembering their childhood dance lessons, when Felix was a poor dancer who kept treading on her feet and apologizing, and the narration says she watches his face as she says it (S17:E000335–E000336; obs. 039). Felix responds with the embarrassed face of a man reminded of an old blunder, and asks why she is reminiscing. Then she asks about Monica, giving a sharp and fair assessment: a born scholar who would excel with proper facilities but cannot negotiate in public, for whom a council seat is the wrong reward (S17:E000374). Felix privately agrees (S17:E000377–E000381) and answers with a question: does Monica not make one think, why can she not do even that, and is it not like seeing his own former self (S17:E000387–E000390)? He leaves early, thinking that she is as hard as ever to relax with and that he said too much (S17:E000405–E000406).

What Bridget is testing the volume does not say. What the reader can establish is narrower. Bridget, who has known Felix since childhood, chooses a memory of his incompetence and watches his reaction; Felix accepts the memory and then, unprompted, identifies Monica with his own childhood self; and he regrets having said so. Chapter 7 adds a matching memory in Felix’s viewpoint, of a small boy apologizing for failing as a royal (section 6.10). Chapter 8 adds his unspoken qualification that he will fulfil the role of Felix Arc Ridill 「この名前を名乗る間は」 (S20:E000521; obs. 062). The secret episode adds that the kingdom’s seer cannot see his star at all (S28:E000231–E000240; obs. 093). This reading records these as a cluster of unexplained discontinuities around Felix’s identity and history (SW-C-0022) and offers no explanation for them; the volume offers none.

### 6.9 The father, the fire, and the uncle

Volume 01 left Monica’s past as a set of fragments: a learned father who taught her and died, talent that took him in some way she never explained, a dream of a drunken man with a bottle and a rule of silence (SW-C-0003; SW-C-0014). Volume 02 fills the gaps in two scenes and in two different modes.

The first is a memory, cued by Felix. His request that she meet his expectations, delivered with a finger under her chin (S16:E000264–E000270), is the kind of gesture the narration says would make most girls blush; Monica nods like a victim of extortion (S16:E000271). That night the phrase 「古い傷」 (S16:E000363) is used of what it touched. She remembers her father’s back at his desk; his learning in mathematics, physics, pharmacy and medicine, and above all biology; his saying that the human body is made of vast numbers (S16:E000369–E000371; obs. 034). He meant, the narration says, to save the sick by analysing the formulas that make a person human. He met everyone’s expectations, and at the end he was reviled by the people and stoned, and then the sentence breaks off (S16:E000375–E000376). What replaces it is the colour of fire, and his numbers vanishing in it (S16:E000381–E000382). She adds herself to the pattern: she too learned chantless magic to meet expectations, and the friend whose praise she wanted most turned his back (S16:E000386). Her conclusion is a wish not to be expected of at all (S16:E000387). The memory arrives immediately after a scene in which Monica explains to Nero that all magic on human bodies is forbidden in Ridill, and that the one exception is the Empire to the east, whose young emperor recently allowed limited medical magic; before she names it she clenches her fist (S16:E000324–E000344; obs. 035). The volume places the two side by side without connecting them in words.

The second is a delirium. Poisoned by Caroline’s eye medicine, Monica sees the tea turn red and the red become fire (S19:E000164–E000170). The scene that follows is the most violent in the series so far, and it is narrated in the present of a child. Her father is tied to a tree and disappears into the flames; she smells burning flesh; the crowd chants that he is a heretic, a criminal who touched the forbidden (S19:E000174–E000180; obs. 044). His papers are thrown on the fire, and she forces her eyes open in the smoke to memorize the numbers she can glimpse, because they are 「父が生きた証」 (S19:E000197). She recites strings of digits. A voice tells her to shut her mouth; a bottle comes down; she apologizes to her uncle; he shouts that his brother’s stupid research has ruined his trade and that if she says such things outside he will beat her with a poker; and she promises not to say anything unnecessary in front of people, to be quiet, if only he will not hit her (S19:E000201–E000212; obs. 045).

Read against V01, the passage answers three questions at once. The man in the V01 dream was called おじさん in hiragana, which can mean any middle-aged man; here he is 叔父さん, her father’s brother (S19:E000207). The rule of silence was imposed by violence, and the forbidden speech was specific: reciting her father’s numbers and defending him in public. And the numbers that comfort her in every crisis were first a vow to keep her father’s work alive. The counting refuge of SW-C-0002 and the father’s teaching of SW-C-0018 are thus one history: the same habit was the inheritance her father gave her, the thing her uncle beat her for, and the place she has hidden ever since. This also explains the extremity of chapter 7’s waking scene, where Monica, who has done nothing wrong, throws herself face down on the infirmary floor to apologize (S19:E000353–E000368; obs. 046). Cyril’s order, 「被害者が頭を下げる道理があるかっ！」 (S19:E000395), is the first time anyone in the series tells her that she is not the guilty party.

Two cautions keep this reading within its evidence. The father’s death is never stated outright; it is broken off in the memory and shown as fire in the delirium, and the delirium is a drugged state. Section 8.1 argues that the passage is nevertheless a disclosure, not a hallucination of something that did not happen, and gives the reasons. And the nature of the forbidden research, the place, the date, and how Monica passed from her uncle to the pupil who adopted her (V01 obs. 032) are not given.

### 6.10 Taste as a defence, and justice as a mirror

Chapter 7’s plot is a crime of humiliation that becomes a poisoning. Caroline’s purpose is to make Monica recant her account of the staircase so that Cyril’s reprimand is withdrawn (S19:E000105–E000111). Monica’s refusal is narrated as an achievement in its own right. Cyril investigated without being asked, and recanting would waste his effort; to go back on her word and cause 「アシュリー様に迷惑をかけるのは……嫌、です」 (S19:E000120), followed by the narrator’s two short sentences, said it, managed it (S19:E000121; obs. 043). It is the first time in the series that Monica refuses a person of higher rank to her face, and her reason is loyalty to someone else’s work, not her own interest.

Claudia’s lesson afterwards supplies the volume’s small philosophy of taste. Taste exists, she says, not to enjoy fine food but to detect poison (S19:E000523; obs. 047). Monica drank the bitter tea because she is used to coffee and because she did not want to give offence; both habits, her palate and her deference, made her defenceless. Cyril returns to the point in chapter 9 with chocolate: she must educate her palate, he says, for His Highness’s sake (S21:E000364–E000366). The comedy of Cyril’s excuse does not hide that two Ashleys have independently decided she needs looking after.

Isabelle’s answer is justice as a mirror. She serves Caroline and her friends tea dosed from a bottle very like Caroline’s, mocks them behind her fan in exactly the style she taught Monica, watches Caroline spit, and drinks her own dosed cup calmly (S20:E000176–E000217; obs. 052). She then turns the humiliation into politics: Caroline has insulted the house whose troops defend her family’s lands from dragons, and Isabelle will tell her father (S20:E000232–E000261). The chapter’s title calls Isabelle the true villainess, and the high laugh she rehearsed in chapter 6 arrives at the end of a speech about how one careless act can destroy a homeland (S20:E000266–E000267). Her private scene explains that the cruelty was deliberate policy: by making enemies she creates an unspoken rule that the house of Kerbeck is not to be touched, and so protects Monica; she owes the Silent Witch her land (S20:E000368–E000385; obs. 055). Section 8.5 evaluates the method.

### 6.11 The most out-of-place cup

The tea-party chapter is the volume’s argument about class, told through two outsiders and a third whose outsiderness is hidden. Claudia, whose family is called the house of the learned, sets the terms: Lana’s costly tea would be perfect for honouring a superior and is out of place at a table where everyone brings something, since it implies the others brought less (S18:E000420–E000423). Lana’s viewpoint then gives the history behind her pride. Her father was a merchant ennobled shortly before she was born; she was out of place among commoners’ children, who called her a show-off, and is called a vulgar parvenu’s daughter at Serendia (S18:E000627–E000645; obs. 029). She first spoke to Monica on a whim, because helping an outcast flattered her, and kept going because Monica smiled like a small flower opening when helped (S18:E000649–E000654). Her private sentence is the scene’s centre: all she wanted was to give her friends 「一番美味しいお茶を飲ませてあげたかった」 (S18:E000662).

Monica’s answer is the coffee. She comes back with a metal pot that uses steam to extract quickly, and tells Claudia, looking straight at her, that since Claudia said strong flavours numb the tongue, coffee is acceptable as the last cup (S18:E000684–E000686; obs. 030). She pours milk into three cups and none into her own (S18:E000695–E000696). Claudia, who has never seen such a pot even in books, rules that it is not tea and therefore out of the question (S18:E000714–E000727). Monica agrees, and says she wanted her dear friends to taste the thing she likes best, and that she is the most out of place of all (S18:E000733–E000737; obs. 031). The frontispiece has already shown this moment (S04:E000004). Lana, who a minute before thought herself the most out of place, says 「わたし、これ好きよ」 (S18:E000742).

Chapter 9 adds what the reader could not know in chapter 6: the locked drawer in Monica’s attic holds only her father’s keepsake coffee pot, and she now puts Lana’s notes beside it (S21:E000262–E000263; obs. 058). The cup she served was her father’s. The scene thus uses the father’s legacy for the first time not as a refuge from people but as a gift to them, and the word 場違い, which in the prologue was the reason Monica refused Lana, becomes the word she uses to give herself away.

### 6.12 The fan

V01 ended with Felix telling his spirit that, if a Sage must watch him, he would rather it were the one he adores, the Silent Witch, whom he does not recognize in Monica (SW-C-0008), and it began with an unnamed blue-eyed man who had found something to be absorbed in (SW-C-0006). Volume 02 closes the second question from Felix’s own viewpoint. Hearing Isabelle describe the wyverns falling, he thinks that he saw it too (S20:E000553). He had been travelling secretly in the east for an unstated reason, was caught among refugees, avoided being recognized, ran into the flock, and watched the gate open and the white wind lances fall (S20:E000556–E000577); the sentence he remembers thinking is 「なんて静かで、美しい魔術なのだろう」 (S20:E000581; obs. 064). The secret episode repeats the V01 watcher’s phrase in his mind as he learns that the Silent Witch has saved him: 「僕が、夢中になれるもの」 (S28:E000131; obs. 088). The identification that V01 could only infer is now a represented fact.

The same scenes show the depth of the admiration and its oddity. Felix keeps the Silent Witch’s student papers in a locked drawer, bought through an old bookseller, and has read one until it creased (S20:E000594–E000667). He talks to Wildianu, who cannot follow, about the coordinates of twenty-four wyverns and the magic circles he saw above each brow, concluding that she must have extraordinary spatial sense (S20:E000648; obs. 065). He calls himself a fan and wishes he could share it with Isabelle (S20:E000668). And he holds the paper to his chest like a love letter, with a sad smile, because in public he must pretend to know nothing of magic (S20:E000671–E000676). The reader has just heard him tell Monica, in chapter 2, that he has no talent for magic (S14:E000036; obs. 014). The public lie, the private devotion and the dramatic irony of his ignorance are now all on the page together.

The secret episode turns the devotion into a search. Felix goes to the old garden, whose key he copied long ago because the barrier formula in its fountain fascinated him (S28:E000029), finds the lock burned cleanly through, and overhears Louis say that the Silent Witch summoned a spirit king without a staff to protect the second prince (S28:E000092–E000098; obs. 087). He reasons through the possibilities: a passer-by, an infiltrator, a teacher, a student in the lower school, and even a man, since the Thorn Witch calls himself a witch and is male (S28:E000117). He catches himself flushing like a boy looking for his first love (S28:E000128). The title of the episode is his. Every term of his search excludes Monica, who is standing in his council room, and the volume knows it.

The rest of Felix’s private life in V02 is about duty without desire. Offered the role of the founding king in the festival play, opposite Eliane, the second cousin his grandfather intends him to marry, he refuses; he has no interest in her, in the bright future prepared for 「フェリクス・アーク・リディル」, or in anything prepared for that name (S23:E000073–E000085; obs. 077). Then: 「それでも私は王にならなくては、いけないんだ」 (S23:E000086), even if he is called the duke’s puppet. In chapter 9 he drinks from Monica’s cup to show that he thinks the factional meaning of chocolate absurd, and his face, agreeing that being royal is hard, shows contempt (S21:E000456–E000467; obs. 060). His treatment of Monica is unchanged in method: he likes having an obvious bully near her because it makes the squirrel easier to tame (S20:E000435; obs. 061). The kindness and the calculation still coincide.

### 6.13 A friend who was lying

Casey’s story is constructed to be read twice. On first reading she is the volume’s gift to Monica: the strong country girl who catches her from under a runaway carriage, makes her sit on the driver’s seat, knows the counting song from her own home, invites her to take riding, coaches the dancing, mediates between Lana and Claudia, and offers her own tea when Monica’s is thrown away (S15:E000409–E000591; S18:E000561–E000584). Each scene is warm and specific. On second reading, after chapter 10, each is also a move.

The text gives the second reading in Casey’s own viewpoint. She had been preparing for the fireworks delivery when the false Abbott carriage arrived early, went to look, and met Monica; the horses’ bolting, which Monica caused, seemed to her a lucky accident (S23:E000387–E000391; obs. 082). Monica’s friendship gave her access to the council’s schedule. She threw away Monica’s tea in order to rescue her and win her complete trust (S23:E000393–E000394). Her father’s order, delivered as head of house to a daughter who had asked what she could do, was to win the prince or, failing that, to kill him with the jewel he handed her, three long rivets on its back where a brooch would have a pin (S23:E000351–E000371; obs. 080). The object is the one the unnamed person took from a locked drawer in the prologue, described in the same terms (S11:E000796). The prologue’s anonymity is thus resolved by an object, not by a name, and the book had left two signs in plain view: her lunch-table mention of a kind patron who paid her way (S22:E000134; obs. 070), and Claudia’s idle remark that it is careless to tell outsiders the council’s schedule is made at a table where Casey is sitting (S22:E000270).

Monica works it out in the corridor, and the method is her own. The cut rope would part in five to fifteen seconds, so it was cut on site, and outsiders can no longer carry blades (S22:E000575–E000584; obs. 072). When Casey confesses with a rehearsed speech about disliking her, Monica says 「……噓、です」 (S22:E000608), and then reveals that she has known since the tea party who threw her tea away. She never writes her name on her things, because she was once bullied and her belongings hidden; she marked her jar with a folded paper, and only Casey saw the fold (S22:E000625–E000645; obs. 073). Her silence about it was not ignorance. It was a choice not to look at what she knew, because Casey had always helped her (S22:E000655–E000658). She has learned at this school, the narration says, a little of what it is to know another person, and that is how she can tell Casey is still hiding something (S22:E000667–E000669).

Casey’s motive, when it comes, is the volume’s most serious political speech, and the narration does not undercut it. Her land borders Randall; dragons destroyed a village and killed her three brothers, one dropped from a height, one torn, one burned inside his armour; the kingdom’s dragon knights rarely came in time, and some said dragons made a cheaper border than poor nobles (S23:E000149–E000164; S23:E000303–E000332; obs. 079). Randall’s knights crossed the border in secret, because her grandmother came from a Randall marquisate (S23:E000170; S23:E000340). And she believes, on what she has heard from Randall’s nobles through her father, that Duke Clockford means to invade Randall as a stepping-stone to war with the Empire, and that a puppet king could not stop him (S23:E000138; S23:E000179). These are her beliefs and reports. The volume does not confirm the duke’s plan, and Felix, in the same chapter, says privately only that he must become king (section 6.12).

Monica’s last perception of Casey is the volume’s most important evidence about Casey’s feeling, and it is ambivalent by design. Casey refuses to thank her or apologize, tells her to hate her or forget her, calls her a soft-hearted Sage who will be murdered in her sleep, and then apologizes for one thing only: 「乗馬と刺繡の約束……守れなくて、ごめんね」 (S26:E000390; obs. 097). Her last words use Monica’s name, not her title (S26:E000397–E000398). Whether her friendship was ever more than instrumental is the question section 8.3 takes up. The facing illustrations of the two girls, one smiling through tears and one crying (S26:E000341; S26:E000383; obs. 110), give a picture of shared grief that the prose leaves to gesture.

### 6.14 The duty and the chant

The climax is narrated as the completion of a decision that began with Elliott. Casey will not stop the device; Monica wants to cry like a child and cannot, because people will die (S23:E000212–E000231). Then the two roles are set side by side in one sentence: if being worthy of the council is the treasurer Monica Norton’s duty, this is the Sage’s, the Silent Witch’s (S23:E000239–E000242; obs. 083). The narration adds that at the dance and the tea others helped her, and that this she must do alone (S23:E000243). Her reasoning follows the pattern of the stair in V01 and the horses in chapter 3, a series of options rejected because each would fail or expose her, ending with the idea that is pure Monica: attack the school herself, so that Louis’s barrier reveals where it lives (S23:E000247–E000270). To Casey’s certainty that nothing can be done she answers 「いいえ」 (S23:E000289), and then, aloud, that she can stop it, that she must; and silently, because she is a Sage, the Silent Witch (S23:E000294–E000296).

Then she does what she has hidden for the whole volume, in front of the person who has been deceiving her. She burns through the lock with a fingernail-sized flame, deliberately keeping her eyes forward while Casey gasps (S23:E000415–E000420). She recognizes in the trap of roses the work of a second Sage, the Thorn Witch (S23:E000474–E000487). Her mind sinks, the narration says, into a soundless sea of formulas, three seconds of rapture (S23:E000517–E000520). And the summoning requires the one kind of chant she can never omit, a ritual address of respect to the higher being, which the narration explains for the first time (S23:E000538–E000539; obs. 084). Monica, who was afraid to open her mouth in front of people, says it in front of Casey, and the chant names her, one of the Seven Sages, 「〈沈黙の魔女〉モニカ・エヴァレットの名の下に」 (S23:E000545). The illustration shows her speaking, hand held out to the reader (S23:E000557; obs. 106).

The volume’s structure makes this the answer to the uncle. In the delirium, she promised to say nothing in front of people (S19:E000212). In the old garden, the first words she says aloud in front of another person as herself are her name and title, said because the ritual requires it and because lives depend on it. The book does not call it a cure. It shows a single occasion on which speech and power are the same act.

The rest of the rescue is described as quiet work, the reverse of the spectacle. She reads Louis’s barrier and admires it as architecture (S25:E000068–E000074), removes the decoy formulas, changes the trigger from outside attack to inside attack, restricts it to fire, excludes oxygen, and compresses it to the size of the device (S25:E000079–E000081). The device bursts and goes out like a capped lamp (S25:E000086–E000099). Her report afterwards is two words, done (S25:E000105). To Casey’s question about the Seven Sages she says only that Casey is not the only one who was hiding something (S25:E000065; obs. 085), the one place where the volume’s Japanese title is spoken in the story.

### 6.15 A bargain with Louis

The epilogue’s central scene is a negotiation, and Monica wins it. Louis arrives by a landing his spirit calls the tornado kick, takes in the wreckage, and takes Casey into custody when Casey herself calls herself the enemy (S26:E000095–E000131). Asked what will happen to Casey, Louis answers with the truth of his world: interrogation, the names of everyone involved, mind interference if she will not talk, and an execution that might be kinder if she were unconscious for it (S26:E000167–E000171). Monica, looking straight at him, asks whether he is of the first prince’s party (S26:E000185–E000197; obs. 095). He says he is Lionel’s schoolfriend and calls himself a first-prince man chiefly because he dislikes the duke and the second prince (S26:E000207–E000213). She points out that a Randall-connected first-prince partisan’s attempt on the second prince would hurt Louis’s own side, and that only she and Casey know of it (S26:E000218–E000229). He calls her impertinent and says he never expected her to offer him a political bargain (S26:E000225).

The narration then enters Louis. A purge would be cleaner, he thinks, but he would lose Monica’s cooperation, and her power is greater than she knows; so he offers a convent instead of execution if Casey confesses everything (S26:E000254–E000264; obs. 096). And he notices that Monica believes him at once, which troubles him, because one reason he chose her for the mission was her distrust of people: the Silent Witch fears everyone and opens to no one, and a guard who trusts easily cannot guard (S26:E000271–E000276). His diagnosis, 「……少々、絆されすぎですな」 (S26:E000277), and his reminder, a finger on her brow, that Monica Norton is a temporary person (S26:E000287–E000289), are the same warning she gave herself at the end of chapter 6, that she will leave and never meet these people as Monica Norton again (S18:E000792–E000798). Monica’s eyes move away as she agrees.

The volume ends by counting the cost of her mercy. Cyril, believing the falling timber was his oversight, bows to her and apologizes (S26:E000534–E000539; obs. 098). Because she shielded Casey, an honest man has taken the blame, and she weeps, the narration says, not from grief but from guilt for all her lies (S26:E000575–E000576). The chapter title, a soft wall, is Cyril’s back, against which she leaned without noticing because his body is rich in the power hers has spent (S26:E000483–E000505). Louis’s barrier, the hard wall, was the instrument of the rescue; Cyril’s back is where she rests from it. Cyril then covers her with his jacket (S26:E000610).

### 6.16 The visual channel

The images in V02 follow V01’s pattern of disclosing ahead of the prose, and add a new pattern of pairing. The frontispieces print the prologue’s climax and the tea chapter’s last line before the reader reaches either (S03:E000004; S04:E000004; obs. 101). Three caption plates introduce new students as institutional types, a name and a year, and Claudia’s plate withholds her surname as the prose does until chapter 7 (S18:E000371; obs. 103). The illustrated splits stage reversals of power in a single frame: Monica serene above the board and Elliott sweating below it (S14:E000522); Felix serene behind Cyril’s gaping face (S21:E000427). The climax has two images: a tall panel of Monica speaking and reaching toward the viewer, the only image in the volume in which she addresses the reader (S23:E000557), and a wordless spread of her back before the gate of light (S24:E000004; obs. 107). The farewell pairs tall panels of Casey and Monica on facing pages (S26:E000341; S26:E000383). The bilingual plate, finally, gives the volume’s official explanation of chantless magic as computation (S12:E000004), which section 6.3 sets against the narrative’s.

### 6.17 Japanese speech and address

Five features of the Japanese text carry arguments in this reading.

First, Monica’s speech still breaks under pressure in printed ways: repeated syllables when Boyd asks her name (S14:E000609), a bitten word at Casey’s first introduction (S15:E000474), long internal ellipses (S11:E000657). What is new is the number of full sentences of refusal and assertion she produces: to Elliott about the crest (S15:E000250), to Caroline (S19:E000120), to Casey (S22:E000608), and to Louis (S26:E000185). The distribution matters. She is most fluent when correcting a fact or defending someone else.

Second, address terms record the volume’s relationships. Cyril tells her to call him by his given name (S22:E000308), and she does, as シリル様, under pressure from Claudia’s teasing (S22:E000309; obs. 069). Claudia calls her brother お兄様 one syllable at a time as a weapon (S19:E000407). Casey asks to be called by her name and calls Monica Monica from the first afternoon (S15:E000517), and uses the name again in her last word (S26:E000397). Elliott takes over Felix’s word squirrel (S14:E000381).

Third, Felix has two first-person pronouns and two registers. His public 私 governs every scene with others; his private 僕 appears when he thinks of the Silent Witch (S20:E000553; S28:E000131). V01 recorded the same split.

Fourth, the narrator still corrects characters in deadpan single lines, as in the remark that Monica had forgotten she hunted more than twenty dragons a few months earlier (S15:E000541) or that she fell silent as her epithet requires (S17:E000480). These lines keep the reader outside every character, including Monica.

Fifth, the word 場違い appears at the beginning, middle and end of Monica’s social arc: as her reason for refusing Lana (S11:E000169), as Claudia’s charge against Lana (S18:E000420), and as Monica’s own self-description at the end of the tea party (S18:E000737). The word’s meaning moves from exclusion to self-gift.

## 7. Character, directional relationship, world and formal development

Romanized names are analyst conveniences, as in V01.

### 7.1 Monica Everett

**Entering V02 state.** As frozen at `SW-FR-V01-EXIT`: a Sage at school under cover, treasurer, one friend, a hostile but fair superior, a prince who suspects and teases her; speech gains confined to trusted people; a belief that her talent is a curse; no account of her father’s fate or of the man in her dream.

**What V02 reveals rather than changes.** The father was a learned researcher of the body as number, reviled, stoned and, on the evidence of the memory and the delirium, burned as a heretic with his papers (obs. 034, 044). The man in the dream was her paternal uncle, who beat her for reciting numbers and for defending her father, and forbade her to speak of it in public (obs. 045). The father’s coffee pot is her one keepsake (obs. 058). She was bullied at Minerva and keeps habits from it: she writes her name on nothing and knows to look in the bin (obs. 051, 073). These are disclosures of old facts, recorded in the ledger as revealed rather than developmental. They explain, without the narration saying so, the rule of silence, the absolute refusal of false accusation, the readiness to apologize for being harmed, and the counting refuge.

**What changes.**

- *Duty.* The volume’s main development. From Elliott’s claim that nobles must protect commoners, Monica derives the idea that her imposed roles carry duties, first as treasurer (obs. 033) and then as Sage (obs. 083). She acts on it alone, in front of the person deceiving her.
- *Speech.* She refuses a superior (obs. 043), contradicts Elliott on a fact (obs. 023), accuses Casey (obs. 072), and bargains with Louis (obs. 095). She still cannot ask to be excused from a class (S14:E000294–E000310). The conditions are now clearer: she speaks best to correct a fact or protect another person.
- *Fear.* Nero’s human form no longer frightens her (obs. 006). Large strange men still do (S14:E000609–E000611).
- *Knowing others.* The narration says she has learned a little of what it is to know another person (S22:E000667–E000668), and uses it to see through Casey.
- *Attachment.* She has friends she names as friends: Lana, Casey, and in a pressed form Claudia; she treasures their notes and looks forward to the festival (obs. 071). She is warned by Louis and by herself that Monica Norton is temporary (obs. 096; S18:E000792–E000798).
- *Agency in politics.* For the first time she uses the logic of factions, to save Casey (obs. 095).

**What does not change.** Her self-assessment: she still describes herself to herself as worthless and apologizes for being poisoned. Her stated inability to expect anything of anyone, or to be absorbed in anyone, is said aloud in chapter 9 (obs. 057) and contradicted by her conduct in every other chapter. No romantic interest on her side is shown.

**Public, private and attributed selves.** V01 counted four. V02 adds a fifth and closes a gap. Casey now holds the Silent Witch and Monica in one person, and is removed from the school. Lana, Glen, Neil, Claudia and Cyril each hold a different, partial Monica: the friend, the transfer, the colleague, the protégée, the treasurer.

### 7.2 Felix Arc Ridill

V02 confirms what V01 inferred and deepens what it withheld. Felix was the watcher at Kerbeck (obs. 064). He studies magic secretly and lies that he has no talent for it (obs. 014, 065). He adores the Silent Witch as the author of a beautiful, silent, merciless magic, and in the secret episode begins searching the school for her (obs. 087–088). In public his manner is unchanged: courteous control, teasing that silences rooms, justice delivered cold to Caroline, a wink that makes him an accomplice at a barbecue. In private he says he must become king without wanting anything prepared for him, calls himself a puppet’s candidate without protest, and adds that he will play his role while he bears the name (obs. 062, 077). Around him gather unexplained details: Bridget’s probe about his childhood clumsiness, his own identification with a small boy who apologized for failing as a royal, the seer’s inability to see his star (obs. 039, 049, 093). V02 explains none of them, and this reading does not.

Toward Monica his method is stated in his own viewpoint: a visible bully makes her easier to tame (obs. 061). He is also, twice, protective in a way that is not obviously instrumental: his anger at her poisoning, which he says he rarely wastes (S19:E000722–E000740), and his care after it (S19:E000464). He suspects Glen of being an agent or assassin because of his master (obs. 040). He has no idea that Monica is the person he is looking for.

### 7.3 Louis Miller

V02 shows more of Louis than V01, mostly at second hand and in his own secret scene. He is a giddy father-to-be who buys clothes for a daughter he has decided he will have, and practical clothes for Monica (obs. 009). He uses a disciple as an unknowing decoy (obs. 010), hates being called womanly and keeps his hair long (S15:E000663–E000670), built the academy’s barrier and had a second Sage add a lethal trap to it (S17:E000735–E000751; obs. 094), and calls himself a first-prince man because he dislikes the duke and the second prince (S26:E000213). In the epilogue he weighs Casey’s life against Monica’s cooperation and chooses mercy for a reason he keeps private (obs. 096). He also reveals one of his reasons for choosing Monica, her distrust of people, and is uneasy that she is losing it. SW-C-0017 is strengthened: instrumental use and investment are again fused, and now the investment includes a handkerchief his wife gave him (obs. 099).

### 7.4 Cyril and Claudia Ashley

Cyril’s V02 arc is care that he cannot admit. He worries when Elliott takes Monica out, is called overprotective by Bridget and denies it (obs. 026); arranges his work so he can watch her practice (S17:E000040–E000047); scolds Glen rather than her; carries her when poisoned until his strength fails (S19:E000443); tucks her in and calls her a victim (obs. 046); does her week’s work (S21:E000301–E000310); educates her palate (obs. 059); asks to be called by his name (obs. 069); apologizes for a fault that was not his (obs. 098); and covers her with his jacket. Felix guesses he sees her as a younger sister (S20:E000441). Separately, Cyril now connects the forest monster with the Silent Witch (obs. 054), but V02 never shows him returning to his V01 suspicion of Monica’s hand. He was a commoner adopted by Marquis Highown and was once told by Elliott to know his place (S15:E000170–E000172).

Claudia, his sister, is introduced as the volume’s most opaque new character. She is gloomy by design, sharp to the point of cruelty, learned in the manner of her family, called the house of the learned, and devoted to Neil, the small baron’s son she is engaged to (obs. 048). She saves Monica’s life with competent first aid and then refuses to be thanked (S19:E000555). She watched Monica for a week because she thought Monica was after her fiancé. She also baited Monica with the Silent Witch’s name and looked only at her during the tea party (obs. 050), and noted at lunch that telling outsiders the council’s schedule is careless (obs. 118). Whether she suspects anything beyond Neil, V02 does not say.

### 7.5 Elliott Howard

Elliott changes more than anyone except Monica. He begins by mocking her lack of accomplishments and dragging her into the chess room; he wins by a rule he did not teach and is ashamed; he supplies her name to Boyd; he cannot bring himself to apologize; he trusts her eye about the crest; and he orders her to safety in the name of noble duty (obs. 020–024). His creed, explained by the narration and by Felix, is not contempt for commoners but intolerance of anyone who fails their role, royal or common (S20:E000495–E000518). At the end he still thinks her presumptuous, and asks Felix outright why she was chosen (obs. 063). His last appearance is a glance at her before he leaves (S26:E000478).

### 7.6 Casey Grove

Casey is a fully motivated antagonist who is also, in every scene before chapter 10, a convincing friend. Count Bright’s daughter, a country noble from a dragon-ravaged border county, rider, hunter and embroiderer, who lost three brothers and believes the kingdom abandoned her people (obs. 079). She was ordered by her father to seduce or kill the prince and given the 螺炎 (obs. 080). She befriended Monica for access, threw away her tea to rescue her, and staged an accident to clear herself (obs. 082). At the end she calls herself an enemy, refuses forgiveness, apologizes only for the promises she cannot keep, and says goodbye by name (obs. 097). She is sent to a convent on condition of full confession (obs. 096). The volume never shows her inside a scene of calculation about Monica while she is with her; it shows the calculation only in retrospect.

### 7.11 Form and evaluation

Four formal patterns are promoted to the ledger. First, power now appears inside Monica’s viewpoint, as computation with constraints, while awe remains in other people’s (SW-C-0010 revised). Second, the volume is built on mirrored scenes: Caroline’s dosed tea and Isabelle’s; the prologue’s invisible rescue and the climax’s spoken name; the hard wall of Louis’s barrier and the soft wall of Cyril’s back. Third, it resolves a planted anonymity by an object rather than a name, the jewel with three rivets. Fourth, the word 場違い organizes the social plot of three outsiders, Monica, Lana and Casey, each out of place by class or region (SW-F records). The narrator’s deadpan single lines and the romance-novel lens continue from V01.

## 8. Alternative readings, absence and contradiction

### 8.1 “The father and uncle scenes are hallucination and should not be read as history”

The rival reading is serious. The fullest account comes during poisoning by a drug that causes hallucinations (S19:E000508–E000509), and it is narrated in a child’s present tense, with images, smells and voices. Against it, four things. The chapter 4 memory, given when Monica is sober and alone, already contains the decisive elements: the father’s learning, the public abuse, the stoning, and the fire into which his numbers vanish (S16:E000369–E000382). The V01 dream, before any drug, contained a man with a bottle, shouting, and a rule of silence (V01 obs. 037). The uncle’s reported words assign a motive that fits the father’s fate: his brother’s research has ruined the uncle’s trade, and a criminal in the family is bad for business (S19:E000208). And the delirium itself is framed as memory, not invention: the scene shifts back into the remembered father in the infirmary, with his glasses and his white coat (S19:E000296–E000309). The reading therefore treats the content as disclosure: STRONG_INFERENCE that the father was publicly executed for forbidden research and that the uncle abused Monica afterwards. What would weaken it is a later account in a sober voice that contradicts the details; what would strengthen it is any third-party report of the father’s case.

### 8.2 Felix: kind, playing, or both

V01 left the question open. V02 adds evidence on both sides without settling it. For the game: his own statement that a visible bully makes the squirrel easier to tame (S20:E000435), his extraction of her acceptance by a misread question (S13:E000238–E000251), his chin-lift demand, which she experiences as extortion (S16:E000264–E000271). For something more: his anger at her poisoning, which he says he does not waste (S19:E000722–E000740), his identification of her with a child version of himself (S17:E000390; S19:E000729–E000738), his presence at her bedside holding her hand (S19:E000332). The reading continues to record his kindness and his calculation as coinciding, and adds that V02 gives him an affect toward her, anger on her behalf, that the game alone does not explain. Whether that anger is about her or about the boy she reminds him of is not decidable from this volume.

### 8.3 Was Casey ever Monica’s friend?

Casey says no, repeatedly: she calls herself an enemy and a liar, says she disliked Monica from the start, and tells Monica to hate her (S22:E000609; S26:E000038; S26:E000347). Her own retrospective viewpoint shows the friendship as a plan (S23:E000393–E000394). Against this reading, Monica perceives the denial as rehearsed (S22:E000600–E000605); Casey’s single apology is for the riding lessons and embroidery she promised, which no longer served any purpose of hers when she made it (S26:E000390); and her last word uses Monica’s name rather than her title (S26:E000397–E000398). The reading concludes that the friendship was instrumentally begun and that the volume deliberately leaves its later affective reality ambivalent; Casey’s refusal to accept forgiveness can be read either as honesty about her acts or as a way of making Monica’s grief easier. The ledger records this as UNRESOLVED, not as a finding about Casey’s heart.

### 8.4 Normative evaluation: Monica’s concealment of the assassination

Criterion: whether the narration registers the costs to people other than Monica of her choice to hide Casey’s guilt. It does. Cyril publicly takes the blame and apologizes (S26:E000534–E000542); Monica herself names the cost as guilt for lying (S26:E000575–E000576); Louis treats the arrangement as a political trade made for his own reasons, not as a judgment that Casey deserves mercy (S26:E000254–E000264); and the assassination attempt, which could have killed Felix, Elliott, Mabel and the workers, goes unpunished in public. The volume presents Monica’s mercy sympathetically and the bargain as a growth in her agency. It does not say whether the mercy is just. This reading records that the outcome substitutes a convent for execution for a would-be murderer with a grievance the volume takes seriously, that an innocent man bears the blame, and that the prince remains ignorant of who tried to kill him. Whether later volumes return to these costs is an open question, not a prediction.

### 8.5 Normative evaluation: Isabelle’s revenge

Criterion: whether the means are proportionate to the wrong and whether their effects on third parties are registered. Isabelle humiliates Caroline by reproducing her method, and then threatens the welfare of Caroline’s home county through her father’s troops (S20:E000232–E000266). She herself says privately that she will not, and that her father would never let a territory fall for a grudge (S20:E000290); the threat is a performance meant to deter. The narration presents the scene as comic justice and gives it the chapter title. Caroline’s friends are punished alongside her. The volume’s judgment is that a lesson in power was deserved; this reading notes that the lesson is delivered by the same means it condemns, and that the volume enjoys it.

### 8.6 The afterword and the web serial

The author says that this book corresponds to chapters four to six of the web serial with additions, that the additions are meant as meaningful detours that deepen characters, and that the character pages follow the text deliberately, as on the web (S34:E000012–E000030; obs. 100). Together with V01’s afterword this places I–II at web chapters one to six. No web text was read or compared; the statement is recorded in the source map as a comparison lead.

### 8.7 Meaningful absences inside V02

- Monica never chants except the ritual chant in chapter 11, which the narration explains is of a different kind.
- The king, the princes other than Felix, and the duke never appear; every statement about the succession and the duke’s aims is a character’s report.
- The Minerva friend is mentioned once, without a name (S16:E000386). Monica’s adoptive guardian is not mentioned.
- Felix’s night outings, central in V01, are not mentioned.
- Cyril never returns, in his own viewpoint, to Monica’s hand.
- Nero’s nature is not touched.
- The kiss of V01 has no consequence or recurrence in this volume.
