## 3. Current-volume story synopsis

**The prologue.** Late summer, about two months after Monica's move. Only three Sages attend a meeting that confirms a change in the law. Forbidden-art crimes will no longer be punished by death, but by life imprisonment. The physicians' association had long sought the change, and the black chalice's acceptance as a medical tool turned the tide. Monica thinks her father's research has changed the country.

Mary and Louis arrive with news. Before dawn a magical attack of a Sage's strength blew open ネイガル監獄, the prison for mages, and twenty-three prisoners escaped.

In Southerndoor, Lana invites Monica to the water-spirit festival and its lantern ship. Her father has sent a servant, Rick Horace, who blushes at Lana and hides a blue lock under his cap.

One escapee, Darrell, a former prodigy who learned from Thornley in prison, walks toward the sea to meet "Ruferno". Thornley refused to escape for fear of the Silent Witch.

**The interlude** goes back more than twenty years. The knight Brendan Autred takes tea with Clockford's daughter Irene, a self-styled "curiosity monster", who has left Serendia to marry the king. A new silver-haired maid, Diana, serves. The coda: Irene bore Felix and died.

**Chapters one and two.** Isaac, governing Erin as the retired prince, plans to recruit Brendan as steward so that he can spend time in Southerndoor.

Cyril, now in the Library Society, misreads a ship ticket Glen pressed on him as a duty. On his way he feeds a starving old man, Brendan, whose servant Hans has been dragooned by Darrell. Brendan's praise of Cyril's "father" wounds Cyril, because the teaching was his real father's.

Isaac arrives at Monica's house and finds she has bought him bookshelves, one of which he fills with a Silent Witch collection. Bartholomeus, who believes the prince and the Witch are secret lovers, brings word of a rehearsal voyage. Nero senses something "mixed into" Rick. Rick hears the voice of the spirit Ruferno, and fears hurting Lana for his brother's sake.

**Chapters three to five.** On the rehearsal voyage, Darrell takes Lana hostage. Monica, Isaac and Nero free her. Rick is revealed as Darrell's brother and as possessed by Ruferno, an ocean-born higher water spirit; seven other men carry lesser spirits.

A serpent of water throws Monica into the sea. Wildianu saves her, and the two come ashore far away. The lantern ship turns to follow at Isaac's persuasion. Monica and Wildianu form a comic pair that hides chantless magic behind primes and tea names. They take in Hans, who reveals two facts:

- Darrell made the possessions;
- Rick is eleven.

Rick's backstory is told: his family's rejection, his brother's "whatever", and Lana's insistence that he was not "whatever". Darrell carves a stolen jewel into a contract stone.

**Chapters six to eight.** Cyril and Brendan raid Darrell's hut. Monica confronts Rick with a cold anger that turns into the advice an adult once gave her: a child may rely on adults.

Ruferno, now outside Rick, floods the hut out to sea. Ruferno's past is told: the child Darrell found her dying and made her a home in his sleeping brother. Darrell confirms that he used people as substitute contract stones. Rick rebels against his brother's "whatever", and Monica declares she fights for what is not "whatever".

Monica breaks out with a spirit-king summoning, holds two summonings at once with her new formula, and takes the sea from Ruferno. Cyril freezes the spirit's tail. Isaac surfs from the lantern ship on the altar's lid and fells Darrell. Darrell, told by Rick to atone, no longer says "whatever".

**Chapter nine.** At the lord's house, Monica asks whether her own unasked sealing of a spirit differs from Darrell's. Cyril, who has realised he fears night waters because of his drunken father, thanks her and takes the responsibility on himself. Isaac wins Brendan with the dead prince's pleading face, and tells Monica that he wants to learn under her.

**Chapter ten.** Lana takes Rick back on trust that must be rebuilt. At the festival Isaac buys Cyril a lantern against the dark. Monica greets Isaac with "welcome home" for the first time. She gives him the first of thirty books, and asks him to be a mage absolutely sure of his formulas.

**The epilogue.** Brendan recognises Wildianu as the maid Diana, and infers that Wildianu's master serves Felix without being him. Wildianu understands why he contracted with Isaac: Isaac needs looking after, and Irene's wish that her child never forget what he loves has passed to Isaac.

**The secret episode.** A month after the break-out, Louis learns that the culprit came from outside. The last name on the confirmed escapee list is Adolf Farron, the Wind-Hand Mage, a former Sage candidate who hates Louis and whom Monica once humiliated. Louis moves his wife Rosalie and daughter Leonora to safety.

## 6. Close reading

### 6.1 A new arc, built on swapped partners

V10 opens the arc that IX -extra- and the V09 afterword announced: Southerndoor, Monica as master, Isaac as apprentice. Its main plot is contained within one volume. It runs from the prison break in late summer to the festival on the last day of 秋巡月. Two threads stay open:

- the culprit who breached the prison from outside (obs. 173);
- Adolf Farron's escape (obs. 176).

The afterword calls the volume a swap of partners (obs. 182). The narrative bears this out:

- Monica spends the middle chapters with Isaac's spirit Wildianu;
- Isaac spends them with Monica's familiar Nero (obs. 061, 063, 089).

Each pair lacks what the usual partner supplies:

- Monica and Wildianu share the same literal-mindedness. They hide chantless casting behind primes and tea brands, and a thirteen-year-old sees through them (obs. 075, 094, 095).
- Isaac and Nero share a readiness for violence that Isaac must restrain (obs. 061, 089).

The comedy has a structural point. The volume asks what each partner gives the other, and answers through Wildianu: trust, which he says he does not understand (obs. 070).

The volume's title and its threads concern hidden things:

- Monica's hidden residence and title (obs. 066);
- Isaac's double life (obs. 031);
- Wildianu's former name (obs. 167);
- Rick's hidden lock and spirit (obs. 018, 059).

### 6.2 "Whatever" and its refusal

The volume's thematic phrase is the brothers' どうでもいい, "whatever", and its negation, which is chapter seven's title.

- **For Darrell** it is a principle. Others' circumstances do not matter; only spirits do (obs. 019, 108, 124). The character list calls him a genius who loves spirits and is indifferent to people (obs. 181).
- **For the child Rick** it was liberation. His brother dismissed the blue lock that made his parents call him a monster (obs. 090).
- **Lana** reversed the word. She washed the lock clean and told him it was not "whatever" (obs. 091).
- **The turn.** When Darrell calls Lana "whatever", the word cuts the other way (obs. 112). Monica's cry that it is not "whatever", and her declaration that she fights for what is not, give Rick the courage to defy his brother (obs. 113, 114).
- **The close.** Darrell, told by his brother to atone, answers "idiot" and no longer says "whatever" (obs. 133). Monica reads his turning away as care: what truly does not matter would not even be noticed (S23:E000470–E000472).

Monica's own past is the measure. She once thought everything outside numbers did not matter (S23:E000472). IX -extra- showed the girl who could shoot down a dragon-cat in an instant (EXTRA09 obs. 027). Here her anger is the opposite of indifference (obs. 099, 113). Section 9 registers the theme as SW-C-0079.

### 6.3 Adulthood, mastery and the words one was given

Monica turns nineteen in the winter (obs. 104). The volume repeatedly asks what makes her an adult (obs. 049, 153):

- She was a Sage at fifteen with adult work, and she thinks her heart did not keep pace (obs. 049).
- She is mistaken for a child by Isaac, by Rick and by Hans (S16:E000105; obs. 095, 104).

Her answer is to pass on what she was given. When she confronts Rick, she first speaks in anger. She then searches for the words an adult once gave her, and repeats Bartholomeus's line that a child may rely on adults (obs. 101, 102). She names her models:

- Lana, who reaches out;
- Cyril, who scolds what is wrong;
- Bartholomeus, who said children may rely on adults (obs. 103).

Cyril, watching, is proud of her and resolves to grow himself (obs. 139).

The teaching extends to her apprentice. She buys him shelves, greets him with "welcome home", and sets him a single standard: absolute command of formulas. She gives him thirty books toward it, a standard that only she could think reasonable (obs. 040, 160, 163, 164). Isaac reads the book as her first expectation of him (obs. 163).

### 6.4 Spirits, consent and the human contract stone

Darrell's art is the volume's moral problem:

- Unable to buy a contract stone, he inscribed formulas on his sleeping brother to house the dying Ruferno (obs. 106, 119).
- He later did the same to seven men with lesser spirits, telling them it was a gift of power (obs. 078, 110).

Monica judges the art unprecedented. It probably counts as body manipulation and may shorten lives (obs. 111).

The volume complicates the judgment:

- Ruferno calls Darrell her salvation (obs. 135).
- Monica asks herself how her own unasked sealing of the ice spirit Romaria in Cyril's brooch differs. She concludes that she saved a spirit for a person's sake, while Darrell saved one for the spirit's (obs. 135).

Cyril answers that the responsibility for Romaria is his, and Monica asks to share it (obs. 138). The volume leaves the question of spirits' consent open, and continues SW-C-0061's unresolved argument about helping spirits.

**Wildianu** gives the spirit's side:

- He was born of a dying spring where people left wishes he could not grant (obs. 074).
- He served Irene as the maid Diana (obs. 167).
- After her death he would have let himself fade, but could not leave the child Isaac, who called him his ally (obs. 171).

His contract with Isaac rests on neither benefit nor trust, but on what Brendan calls the need to look after him (obs. 170). The volume pairs the two kinds of bond:

- Louis and Lynn's mutual benefit (obs. 072, 183);
- Irene and Brendan's trust (obs. 071).

Section 9 registers the human contract stone as SW-C-0080, and Wildianu's account as SW-C-0082.

### 6.5 Isaac between duty and desire

The volume shows Isaac's two lives at once:

- **As duke** he is restrained, lest zeal look like a bid for the throne (obs. 031).
- **As apprentice** he is overjoyed by bookshelves and a "welcome home" (obs. 042, 160).

His recruitment of Brendan shows his method and his self-judgment:

- He chose a man shunned by his family, because a prince's help would look natural (S24:E000361, E000362).
- He offered good terms as sincerity (obs. 145).
- He won Brendan with the dead prince's pleading face (obs. 143).
- He dislikes himself because calculation outweighs his sincerity (obs. 145).
- If unmasked, he thinks death at Brendan's hands would be fair (obs. 141).

Two further features continue V09's Isaac (SW-C-0073):

- **His wish to learn.** He stops giving up what he wants (S24:E000467) and asks to learn under Monica (obs. 146).
- **His capacity for action.** He kicks open the altar and surfs a wave to fell Darrell, relying on Wildianu, whom he tells in thought that he trusts (obs. 126–128).

Wildianu's view frames him as a child who needed an ally, and as heir to Irene's wish that her son not forget what he loves (obs. 171, 172). Section 9 registers this as SW-C-0081.

### 6.6 Cyril, his father and the night sea

Cyril's chapters add his first plain statement about his birth father:

- a drinker who boasted of noble blood, isolated and unstable;
- the boy feared he would drown in the river;
- someone told Cyril he was "Wayne's son, just like his father" (obs. 136).

Brendan's praise of his "father's" teaching wounds him, because the teaching was his real father's (obs. 037). This continues SW-C-0037's mirror of Monica's history and adds the father's name, ウェイン.

His growth is shown in two steps:

- He admits his fear of night water to Isaac, though not to his juniors (obs. 157).
- He accepts a lantern from Isaac, who thinks of the dead prince's fear of the dark (obs. 158).

His relation with Isaac remains formal. He salutes "Your Highness" while Isaac seeks a friend's distance (obs. 159).

### 6.7 The law and the father

The prologue's legal change and the epilogue of the plot are connected:

- Forbidden-art crimes now carry life imprisonment, a change the black chalice helped bring (obs. 007, 008).
- Monica thinks her father's research changed the country (obs. 009).
- Darrell's crime might once have meant death, and now does not (obs. 132).

The volume gives Monica the satisfaction of her father's influence without clearing his name, which V09 set aside (SW-C-0067). The proposers named are "people of the physicians' association such as ウッドマン" (obs. 007). IX -extra- recorded an unnamed physicians'-association authority behind the plan (SW-C-0076). The volume does not state that ウッドマン is that man, and the reading does not identify them.

### 6.8 Threads opened

The volume closes its own plot and opens others:

- the prison was breached from outside by a strong, unidentified mage (obs. 173);
- a bell rang before the explosion (obs. 020);
- Louis searches for a seventh Sage (obs. 174);
- Adolf Farron, who hates Louis and was humiliated by Monica, is at large (obs. 176–178);
- Louis's wife and daughter are named and moved to safety (obs. 177).

Paratext adds that Mary is criticised for failing to predict the break (obs. 181).

Section 9 registers new questions: who breached the prison, what Adolf Farron will do, and what the bell was.

### 6.9 Knowledge and misbelief

Several characters hold partial or false knowledge, and the reading tables them in section 7.11:

- **Bartholomeus** believes Felix and Monica are lovers and that Felix is the true prince (obs. 044).
- **Hans** knew Monica only as a cautious young lady until she named herself (obs. 109).
- **Brendan** infers that Wildianu's master serves Felix without being Felix (obs. 169). He does not know that the duke he serves is not Irene's son.
- **Cyril** knows Isaac's identity and calls him Highness (obs. 159).

## 8. Alternative readings, absence and contradiction

### 8.1 "A light interlude after the arc"

The volume's comedy and contained plot could suggest a pause. Against this:

- it changes the law that killed Monica's father (obs. 007);
- it opens an external threat and a returning enemy (obs. 173, 176);
- it discloses Wildianu's history and Cyril's father (obs. 136, 167).

The reading treats it as the first unit of a new arc.

### 8.2 "Darrell is redeemed"

Darrell's last word is "idiot", and he no longer says "whatever" (obs. 133). The volume shows a crack in his indifference, read by Monica (S23:E000470). It does not show repentance, and the reading registers no change of disposition.

### 8.3 Normative evaluation: Darrell's art

**Criterion:** whether the volume presents the human contract stone as wrong.

It does. It is used without consent, sold to the men as a gift, and probably harmful (obs. 078, 111). The volume also gives the spirit's view, that it was salvation (obs. 135), and Monica's self-comparison (obs. 135). It condemns the means and leaves the question of spirits' welfare open.

### 8.4 Normative evaluation: Isaac's method

**Criterion:** whether the volume presents Isaac's persuasion as manipulation.

He persuades the captain with mixed truth and lies (obs. 088), and Brendan with the dead prince's face (obs. 143). The volume presents these as effective and harmless. It gives Isaac's own judgment that calculation outweighs sincerity (obs. 145), and Brendan's acceptance (S24:E000396).

### 8.5 Meaningful absences

The volume leaves the following unanswered:

- who breached the prison, and what the bell was (obs. 020, 173);
- Mary's foreseen event and the star of loss;
- whether ウッドマン is Hilda's collaborator (obs. 007);
- how Rick will change now that Ruferno is gone (obs. 152).

It also withholds Brendan's reaction should he learn who Isaac is (obs. 141).

### 8.6 The afterword, jacket and card

**The afterword** frames the volume as a swap of partners, jokes about the comedy, and says Louis and Lynn have no notion of joining forces (obs. 182, 183). It mentions:

- the comic adaptations, including one of the prequel companion, with Minerva teachers who taught Louis, Carla, Monica and Hubert;
- the airing anime;
- XI in progress (S37:E000057–E000076).

**The jacket blurb** calls the volume a new chapter (obs. 184).

All of this is paratext.

### 8.7 Apparent contradictions

- **Rick's age.** The narrative gives eleven, twelve at his next birthday (obs. 083); the card gives twelve. The narrative governs; the card may anticipate the birthday.
- **Owen Wright's post.** The IX -extra- design card called him vice-captain; V10's narrative makes him captain (obs. 175). The difference is recorded, not resolved.
