## 2. Prior-volume developments

*Prepared 2026-10-14 before any IX -extra- narrative member was rendered for reading, from the closed V01 to V09 readings, the closed IV -after- reading, their exit freezes and the master ledger only.*

The main arc closed at V09. Monica Everett, the Silent Witch, spent about a year at Serendia Academy as Monica Norton, guarding a second prince who was in truth the page Isaac Walker. At the Supreme Council she saved him and averted war by a staged truth. With her friends' help she proved him the prince using her father's design, the black chalice, and blamed his confession on a curse laid by the dead Peter Sams. ## 3. Current-unit story synopsis

The companion collects bonus stories written for volumes one to seven, with new writing, in a frame. A prologue and an epilogue, both new, bracket five chapters arranged by the stretch of the main story they belong to. A secret episode follows. Each chapter's label carries its placement in brackets.

**The prologue** is set on the eve of the winter solstice, more than half a year after the Supreme Council. Hilda Everett has forced a holiday from the institute's analysis of the black chalice.

While buying sweets, she remembers her first shared task with the newly adopted Monica: a disastrous batch of cookies. At the time the child spoke numbers more easily than words, because numbers did not frighten her.

In the shop a tired man, an authority of the physicians' association, speaks to her in passing. He was one of those who helped Hilda escape with the chalice design after Dr. Rain's arrest. He says their plan to relax the rules on medical magical tools will move next year.

Monica, who once clung to Hilda's clothes in an empty street, now runs to her through a crowd. By the fire she talks about school for the first time at length. She says she has no plans to take an apprentice. Then she announces a move to Southerndoor to help Lana's new company, and Hilda lets her go.

## 6. Close reading

### 6.1 Placement, form and the frame

The companion is a collection with a frame. Fifty-two stories were written as store bonuses, bookmarks and similar extras for volumes one to seven and the first companion. Three new pieces bracket them, and two web-first pieces are added (obs. 184, 187). The bonuses were written for separate occasions. The book gives them an order: five chapters by stretch of the main story, each labelled in brackets with its interval (obs. 014, 036, 109, 130, 156).

Within the chapters, story order follows the story's interval, not the bonus's occasion. A volume-seven bookmark can sit in chapter four (obs. 187).

The labels mostly hold:

- Chapter one runs from about a year after the appointment to just before the mission (obs. 014, 015).
- Chapter two runs from the transfer to before the festival. Two of its pieces look back: Isabelle before the autumn term, and Monica's arrival at her hut at fifteen (obs. 049, 064).
- Chapter three runs from the festival to the last exams before the break.
- Chapter four runs from after New Year to the duel and the Kellylinden night (obs. 131, 141, 144).
- Chapter five runs from graduation to the planting season half a year after the mission (obs. 157, 162).

One story falls outside its label. Mary's dinner, in chapter four, takes place after the Worgan subjugation in early summer before the mission (obs. 148, 155). Section 10 records this.

The frame is the new writing:

- The prologue is set more than half a year after the council (obs. 002).
- Chapter five reaches half a year after the mission (obs. 162).
- The epilogue and the secret episode reach the move to Southerndoor and the summer after it (obs. 166, 172).

All four fall inside the interval that V09's coda covers in summary, between the council and the summer a year after graduation (V09 obs. 145–151). They add scenes to that interval and do not move past V09's last scene.

The frame's motto, printed on the collection's title page and repeated in the epilogue, is "in a new land, a new life begins" (obs. 170, 183). The collection is thus framed as a departure: the stories of the school years are told from the far side of the move.

### 6.2 What the frame argues: the heartless witch and the girl who thanks

The book is arranged so that its first chapter and its last pieces answer one another.

In chapter one Monica is the figure V01 introduced through Louis's eyes. She lives on fish in water, is buried in invitations, and wants to be alone (obs. 017, 019). She is indifferent to almost everything (obs. 029). She treats Nero as a problem she could solve by killing: 「どうせ、撃ち殺すのは一瞬なのだから」 (S42:E000908) (obs. 027). She clears the river so that people will stop coming (obs. 033).

The chapter's title for the Nero story calls her a heartless witch. The narration does not soften this. It gives her reasons only in the world of numbers, which do not reject her (obs. 028).

The frame shows the change from three sides:

- **Hilda.** In the prologue Hilda remembers a child who said numbers were not frightening (obs. 003). She sees the young woman who runs to her through a crowd and talks about friends for an evening (obs. 009, 010).
- **The epilogue.** Monica keeps a worn ribbon because a friend gave it, and thanks that friend without stumbling. The narration states the change directly: kindness once confused and shamed her, and now she can take it in with joy (obs. 169, 171).
- **The secret episode.** Monica, who in chapter one could not see why anyone would cook for her, watches her apprentice bake and decorates a cookie of him (obs. 177).

V09 closed its arc on coffee with friends (SW-C-0074). The collection closes on the same movement, made visible through scenes that V09 summarized.

This is the collection's main contribution. It does not add new events to the main plot. It sets the early, cold Monica beside the later one so that the distance can be measured. Section 9 registers this as SW-C-0075.

### 6.3 Asking, giving and the measured gift

The bonuses repeatedly turn on gifts:

- Lana embroiders a pouch rather than buying one, because gifts chosen by parents are flattery (obs. 120).
- Monica asks Raul for roses so that she can make Lana a sachet (obs. 131).
- Lana values the sachet above her family's luxuries (obs. 139).
- The festival biscuit is halved for friendship, and Monica halves her half again for Agatha (obs. 098, 099).
- Cyril gives Monica a chocolate drink as a moving-in present, and Lana gives her a hair clip to mark their reunion (obs. 169, 172).

The asking is new. Monica's request to Raul is described as hard because it is personal rather than work (S47:E000061–E000065). V09 made asking for help the turn of the arc (SW-C-0072). Here a smaller request, made before the council, shows the habit already forming (obs. 131).

Measuring runs through the gifts as a comic motif with a serious root:

- Hilda's first shared task with Monica rested on a recipe book's rule that baking is measurement (obs. 005).
- Monica brings the same rule, and a laboratory balance, to Casey's baking (obs. 051).
- She measures room temperatures across the school to soften butter (obs. 105).
- Her father gave her a tape measure (obs. 041).

Measuring is how she joins in. Her friends give her the part of the task she can do (obs. 051).

### 6.4 Bernie, Rutherford and the Minerva years

The collection adds three glimpses of Monica's Minerva years, none of them a full scene:

- In the memory of her arrival at the hut at fifteen she wonders whether Bernie will forgive her now (obs. 049). The thought links the hut to a debt to Bernie, which V03 described from his side (SW-C-0027). It gives her flight from Minerva a motive of self-punishment.
- She cried to Bernie over history at Minerva (obs. 115).
- She was a research student in Gideon Rutherford's laboratory without being his apprentice. She knew Louis's name first from Rutherford's stories of his idiot apprentice (obs. 164).

The third explains a small fact of address: only Louis is "Louis-san" among the Sages. This matters to chapter five's subject, how names place people.

### 6.5 Names and address

Chapter five's second story is about forms of address:

- Raul wants to be called by name. Cyril's "Lord Roseburg" is already, for Cyril, informal (S48:E000566–E000570).
- Monica calls only Louis by name, for the reason above (obs. 164).
- Cyril now calls her Monica, which pleasantly unsettles her, and this is her secret (obs. 165).

V09 recorded that Monica asked to be called Monica (V09 obs. 147–151) and that Cyril returned to "Norton treasurer" at the reveal (V09 obs. 011). The story shows the next step: Cyril uses her own name in work.

Elliott, by contrast, cannot settle on any form of address. He cannot treat a magic count as the "little squirrel", and he cannot defer to her (obs. 159).

The secret episode turns the theme to Isaac. Nero must learn to call him by name, Isaac jokes about another name for Nero, and Monica's "Ike" is an apprentice in an apron (obs. 174, 177).

The theme continues V09's SW-C-0068, the volume turning on remembering names. Here the name a person is called by registers the relationship.

### 6.6 Cyril's song and Cyril's year

Cyril is the figure the collection enlarges most. Seven observations add facts about his past or private feeling:

- He hides his chill so that others' tea stays warm (obs. 037).
- He likes fine fabrics and cats (obs. 042, 043).
- He was adopted into Highorn and transferred in the second middle-school year (obs. 101).
- As treasurer two years before Monica he checked the records monthly, and so blocked Thornley's theft for a year without knowing it (obs. 100).
- He sang a weaving song with his commoner mother, who praised him, and stopped singing when his voice broke (obs. 085).
- He frees Neil to dance with Claudia and asks for secrecy (obs. 113).

In the song story he refuses Felix outright, which Monica finds shocking (obs. 086). He yields at Glen's plea, and Benjamin explains that he cannot ignore anyone who asks for help (obs. 087). The song he sings is a weaving song about colours woven while thinking of someone, ending in a journey to meet them. It is paraphrased here, not quoted (obs. 088).

The story's resolution is Monica's music essay. She learns to "read a soul" by hearing his (obs. 090). Her first understanding of music comes from a person, not a formula.

Thornley's viewpoint inverts what V01 showed: Cyril's diligence, which V01 framed through his harshness, was the one obstacle to the embezzlement (obs. 100). Cyril apologizes for shouting at Monica's charge of sloppiness and accepts it as true (obs. 102). Felix then shares the blame and the work (obs. 104).

The care he shows in chapter four is unexplained. He takes the heavy boxes from a girl whose left hand is hurt, giving the cold and efficiency as reasons (obs. 137). The text gives only his stated reasons. Whether he noticed the hand is not stated, and the reading does not infer it.

In chapter five he reads the rose in Monica's hair as the prince's kindness. Elliott, watching, sees him smile at his own bouquet (obs. 161). The story leaves this as Elliott's observation.

### 6.7 Felix, Isaac and the treasures

The collection was largely written before V08's disclosure. The Felix stories therefore read differently now. They carry the marks V02 to V07 recorded, and V08 explained (SW-C-0022, SW-C-0063). The reading records what each shows without treating it as new disclosure:

- He tests food for numbness before swallowing because royals need tasters (obs. 073).
- He restores two heavy boxes one-handed (obs. 045).
- He reads Cyril's drawings correctly (obs. 074).
- He knows Cyril sings in the records room (obs. 084).
- He identifies a spirit inside a dog (obs. 082).

His fan's life is private:

- He cannot borrow the Witch's book as who he is (obs. 123).
- He keeps her correction of his paper and her note asking to see more (obs. 133).
- He imagines discussing her with Cyril, a future he calls one that cannot be (obs. 125).

**The treasure story** is the most charged. He tells Wildianu to burn the drawer's contents if anything happens to him: these are things Felix Arc Ridill must not possess (obs. 134). In his viewpoint narration he is 僕 (S47:E000288). After V08 the line reads as the substitute's awareness that his own tastes would betray him. It adds nothing that V08 and V09 did not disclose, and its comedy is that he cannot bring himself to burn her papers (obs. 135).

**The mirror story** is the irony at the other end. Felix tells Monica to change a future one does not want, 「王族だからね」 (S43:E003128) (obs. 062). After V09 the reader knows the speaker was not royal, and that the future he wanted was the prince's name remembered and his own death.

**After the council** Elliott calls him the "damned attendant" in his thoughts and notes the rose in Monica's hair (obs. 160). The secret episode shows him as Isaac Walker, Duke of Erin, a week a month in Southerndoor, baking (obs. 173).

The title of the secret episode, there was no sugar-coated prince, is answered in his viewpoint. The "Ike" Monica draws is an apprentice in an apron, not a glittering prince, and he is glad of it (obs. 177). This continues SW-C-0073 and the V09 coda.

### 6.8 Mary's fiancé

Mary's dinner is the collection's most consequential scene for the open questions. Bradford asks Mary when she first read the stars. She answers that her first reading foretold the death of her fiancé's father (obs. 151). The fiancé did not blame her; he said he did not believe in prophecy, and still says so. They parted; she stayed single, and he married another woman (obs. 152).

In her thoughts:

- he sees everyone but himself as pieces;
- he wants no one's heart;
- he hates and loves the country more than anyone;
- he will die alone and content (obs. 153, 154).

She resolves to mourn him, as her own egoism, and so to live long (obs. 154).

The story does not name him; Bradford knows who he is, and the text calls it an old, fairly famous story (S47:E001696). The earlier volumes do name him. V08's secret episode states that Mary was Clockford's fiancée and that his father was killed by Ridill's own war party (V08 obs. 294). V09 has Mary recall that killing and his love and hatred of the country (V09 obs. 110, 111; SW-C-0071).

Joined, the record identifies the fiancé of Mary's first reading as Clockford, and the death she foretold as his father's. This rests on the earlier volumes' statements, not on this story's wording. The story was a volume-seven bookmark, written before V08's disclosure (obs. 187).

What the story adds is Mary's side:

- her first prophecy was of his father's death;
- he refused to blame her and refuses prophecy still;
- they parted; he married another woman, whom V09 recorded as killed (V09 obs. 111);
- she means to be the one who mourns him (obs. 151–154).

The scene bears on two open questions:

- **SW-P-0024**, why Mary let the tool be stolen. The scene gives her a lifelong relation to the man behind the false prince; it does not state her reason.
- **SW-P-0018**, the event she foresees. It is not answered. Her resolve to live long so as to mourn him does not say what she has seen.

The reading does not convert the resolve into a prediction of his death. Section 9 registers the scene as SW-C-0077.

The scene also explains Mary's household. She takes boys from orphanages, pays and schools them, and releases them at sixteen with references; one became her apprentice (obs. 149). She calls it her hobby, not charity (obs. 150). Her liking for boys, mentioned in earlier volumes, gains a practice that the text presents as generous and that she declines to call so.

### 6.9 The Sages at home and the adults' plans

The adult stories add three things.

**Louis.** He is newly married to a former classmate, and a child is due. He plans to name the child from the sound of a respected person's name (obs. 093). Glen's remark that his master's wife is a doctor (obs. 118) agrees with IV -after- (AFTER04 obs. 025). Lynn's ring is kept in his coat because he breaks jewellery in fights (obs. 059).

Lynn says that she once felt a heart she does not have beat (obs. 096). Asked whether it was Louis, she denies any feeling for him in a list that only invites the question (obs. 097). The text leaves the occasion unstated.

**Hilda's collaborators.** An unnamed authority of the physicians' association, trusted by the Mage Guild, helped Hilda escape with the chalice design. Their plan is to relax the regulation of medical magical tools, which would reopen the question of forbidden arts and of the crime for which Monica's father was executed (obs. 007, 008). His full name, ヴェネディクト・レイン, is given here (S40:E000430).

The plan is set for "next year", after the prologue's winter. It is a future thread the collection opens and does not follow. The man is not identified, and the reading does not identify him.

**Clockford's reach.** Two details recall Clockford's reach in the school years:

- The haunted mirror was donated to win his favour (obs. 055).
- Elliott foresees the decline of every family in his faction, his own included (obs. 157).

The collection lets the reader measure Monica's distance travelled and does not narrate it as a lesson.

## 8. Alternative readings, absence and contradiction

### 8.1 "A collection of bonus stories adds nothing"

The bonus stories were written as extras, and most are comic. One could treat the collection as a scrapbook with no evidential weight. The reading does not, for three reasons:

- **New facts.** The pieces add facts the numbered volumes do not state: Monica's age at appointment, Cyril's treasurer year, Cyril's song, Lana's sight of the scars, Hilda's collaborators and Mary's first prophecy (obs. 047, 085, 100, 106, 007, 151).
- **The frame.** The new writing frames the whole (section 6.1).
- **The order.** The order places the cold Monica of chapter one against the thanking Monica of the end (section 6.2).

Comic weight is recorded as such. The claims registered below rest on the pieces that carry statements.

### 8.2 "The collection revises V09's coda"

The prologue, chapter five, the epilogue and the secret episode fill the interval that V09 summarized. Do they change it? No:

- V09 already had the move, the Flax Company, the apprenticeship and Neil and Claudia's marriage (V09 obs. 151, 160, 173).
- The collection adds scenes to them and contradicts none.
- The one apparent tension is Monica telling Hilda that she has no plans to take an apprentice (obs. 011). That is consistent with V09: the apprenticeship was Isaac's insistence (V09 obs. 173).

### 8.3 Normative evaluation: the heartless witch

**Criterion:** whether the collection presents early Monica's indifference, including her readiness to kill the dragon-cat, as a fault.

It presents it without judgment and without softening. Nero's viewpoint names it indifference, not malice (obs. 029). The story's title calls her heartless, a word V01 gave to Louis (SW-C-0024).

The collection's order supplies the evaluation that the story withholds. The same girl later thanks a friend and draws her apprentice in an apron (obs. 171, 177).

The reading records that the collection judges by contrast, not by statement.

### 8.4 Normative evaluation: Louis's coercion

The first chapter shows Louis wrapping Monica up, dragging her to ceremonies and planning to drag her to a dragon (obs. 015, 023). His motive is stated as his own standing (obs. 020).

The collection treats this as comedy. Monica's "scary stories" list his acts beside a poisoning (obs. 053). This continues SW-C-0016's pattern: the comedy registers harm and asks for no verdict.

### 8.5 Meaningful absences

The collection is silent on:

- the name of Hilda's collaborator (obs. 007);
- the occasion of Lynn's heartbeat (obs. 096);
- why Cyril took the boxes (obs. 137);
- who gave the rose (obs. 160).

It is also silent on Mary's foreseen event and the star of loss.

It does not show V10's matters. The afterword announces them (obs. 186).
