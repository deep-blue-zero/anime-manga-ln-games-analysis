## 2. Prior-volume developments

*Prepared 2026-10-14 before any V03 narrative member was rendered for reading, from the closed V01 and V02 readings, their exit freezes and the master ledger only.*

Monica Everett, the Silent Witch, one of the Ridill Kingdom’s Seven Sages, is about six weeks into a secret mission at Serendia Academy. She is there as Monica Norton, a second-year student and the supposedly despised adoptee of the Kerbeck household, to guard the second prince, Felix Arc Ridill, without his knowledge, on the order Louis Miller, the Barrier Mage, pressed on her. She is the council’s treasurer. Isabelle Norton, Count Kerbeck’s daughter, protects her by playing her tormentor and has made it known that the house of Kerbeck is not to be crossed. Nero, her familiar, a talking cat who can take the shape of a black-haired man, helps her and patrols round the prince.

Her school life has filled out. Lana Colette, a new baron’s daughter as out of place among nobles as Monica is, is now her declared friend. Glen Dudley, a loud, kind butcher’s son and apprentice mage who transferred at the same time as she did, is her dance partner and fellow keeper of a secret; everything Louis has said about the disciple he planted as an unknowing decoy for Felix’s suspicion fits Glen, though no one has named him. Neil Clay Maywood, the council’s kindly clerk, is engaged to Claudia Ashley, Cyril Ashley’s gloomy, learned and sharp-tongued sister, who saved Monica’s life and now insists they are friends. Cyril, the vice-president, shouts at her and quietly looks after her; he has asked to be called by his name. Elliott Howard, who despised her, has been shaken by losing to her at chess and moved by his own code that a noble must protect commoners, though he still thinks her presumptuous. Bridget Graham remains unconvinced. Monica has passed a dance retest and chosen chess and riding as electives; the chess teacher Boyd wants to enter her in a tournament. Her former Minerva teacher, William MacLaggan, now teaches basic magic at the school and may or may not have recognized her.

Two disclosures changed what is known about her past. Her father, a learned man who studied the human body as numbers, was publicly reviled and burned as a heretic for forbidden research, with his papers; Monica, a child, tried to memorize the numbers on them as they burned. Afterwards her uncle beat her for reciting numbers and defending her father, and forced her to promise to say nothing in front of people. She keeps her father’s coffee pot locked in a drawer.

The volume’s plot was an assassination attempt. Casey Grove, a cheerful country noble from a dragon-ravaged border county who had befriended Monica, was acting on her father’s order to win or kill Felix, whom she believed to be the puppet of his grandfather Duke Clockford, said to be planning war on the neighbouring kingdom of Randall. She planted an assassination device in the warehouse where fireworks were being delivered while Felix was there. Monica stopped it by attacking the school to locate Louis’s barrier, breaking a trap set by the Thorn Witch, summoning a spirit king with a chant that named her aloud before Casey, and rewriting the barrier around the device. She then bargained with Louis to spare Casey execution; Casey was sent to a convent on condition of full confession, and Cyril was blamed for an accident Casey had staged. Earlier, the count’s daughter Caroline Simmons poisoned Monica with eye medicine and is to be expelled after Isabelle humiliated her in turn.

Felix knows that someone tried to kill him with that device and that the Silent Witch stopped it; he has learned this by overhearing Louis. He watched the Silent Witch destroy the wyverns at Kerbeck, keeps her papers, secretly studies magic while denying any talent, and is now searching the school for her, excluding Monica by the terms of his search. He says privately that he must become king though he wants nothing prepared for his name, and that he will play his role while he bears the name. Bridget tested his memory of a clumsy childhood; he remembered a small boy apologizing for failing as a royal; the Star-reading Witch, Mary Harvey, cannot see his star and senses a great event coming. Cyril now connects the silent monster who fought him in the forest with the Silent Witch. Louis warned Monica that Monica Norton is a temporary person and that she is growing too attached. The school festival is next month.

## 3. Current-volume story synopsis

The prologue goes back several years and stays inside the mind of Bernie Jones, the second son of Count Amberd, who entered the mage academy Minerva at ten meaning to become a Sage. At thirteen he rescues a small, silent transfer student, Monica Everett, whom the other boys call the silent Everett and are trying to make speak with a spider. She thanks him, stiffly, for saving the spider. He becomes her helper and she relies on him. He half knows that her hair is being cut and her things hidden, and looks away, because he wants her to need him. When she cannot chant in front of the practical-magic teacher MacLaggan, Bernie teaches her to shorten the chant. At the next practical she casts without any chant at all, and the room understands that it has seen something no one has seen before. Bernie, who taught her none of it, feels betrayed. He never speaks to her again. She joins Professor Rutherford’s laboratory, and two years after they met she is chosen as a Sage at fifteen. When she comes to him in her Sage’s robe to thank him, he tells her that she is not his friend and that she should shut herself up alone in a hut in the mountains. She does.

In the present, about a week after Casey’s disappearance, Lana lends Monica an old dress for the ball after the school festival, telling her that friends need no reason to be kind. Monica falls off a walking horse four times in her riding class. Felix mounts behind her, teaches her posture and the binary rhythm of the trot, and draws out of her that she wants to be able to tell a friend she has learned to ride; he guesses the friend is Casey and wishes her luck. He leads her off the course to watch a magic battle between Cyril and the club captain, and says that knowing what magic can do speeds judgment in an emergency. Monica sees his eyes shine.

In the chess class Elliott asks why she did not object when he used a rule he had not taught her. She says she cannot think of a reason to be angry. Elliott remembers the first time he met Felix, at six, at Duke Clockford’s house: a sickly, clumsy, painfully shy boy who loved astronomy, whose attendant was more poised than he was, and who fell from a tree when Elliott hid his book there, piercing his side; the boy lied to protect Elliott. Elliott decides that Monica reminds Felix of that gentle boy. Over the board he tells her that he hates upstarts, because his uncle married a commoner who robbed him and drove him to hang himself, and warns her that talent that jumps class walls is either envied by the incompetent or used by the cunning. Boyd names Monica, Benjamin Molding and Elliott as Serendia’s team for an inter-school tournament. In the council Felix bets her one wish if she can beat him; he throws the game and she forces a stalemate instead; her wish is that he stop calling her a squirrel, and he begins to call her Monica.

The tournament brings Minerva to Serendia. Monica, sleepless at the thought of meeting her old classmates, learns from Lynn, whom Louis has lent her, that Casey has been sent to a convent in the north and that Louis suspects Randall behind the device. On the morning of the tournament Lana puts her in a corset, makeup and plain glasses, and the mirror shows her a healthy girl who looks her age. Minerva’s captain is Bernie. Nero and Lynn, disguised as two young men in evening dress, chase him off when he corners her in a corridor. Monica plays first against Robert Winkel, an exchange student from Randall, and beats him; he proposes an engagement so that they can keep playing, and is pinned to the floor with a penitent’s sign. Bernie tests her with fire arrows, sees her block them without a chant, and calls her a cheat who feigns weakness to get close to the prince. Lana finds them and tells him he is jealous. Monica tells Bernie that if he insults her friend she will not forgive him, and that she will no longer want his praise. He makes himself vanguard to face her, and she crushes him.

Bernie then finds that Minerva’s teacher Pittman is an impostor, who answers his magic with scales and claws and the forbidden body-altering magic that only the Empire permits. Monica arrives, the impostor shuts her in a sphere of water, and she breaks it and drops him with a single silent shot to the brow. Bernie realizes that she is guarding the prince, takes the credit for the capture himself and tells her to be grateful to him for the rest of her life. Serendia beats Minerva. In the infirmary Claudia predicts that the festival will go ahead because Duke Clockford needs his grandson’s public debut, and that Felix will not refuse because he is the duke’s puppet.

The next day the duke, Darius Knightley, overrules the headmaster’s cancellation. Alone with Felix he calls him a disgrace; Felix’s face empties and he answers as a subordinate. That evening, telling his spirit that today is a special day, Felix repeats to himself a vow he has kept for ten years: to do anything to inscribe the name Felix Arc Ridill in history. Isabelle, dressed for the bell-ringing festival in the town of Coalrapton, explains that the bells send back the dead who slipped out when the underworld’s gatekeeper went to enjoy the festival.

Monica spends the evening at the mansion of the Star-reading Witch, Mary Harvey, with Louis. Mary thanks Monica for her calculations: the star they traced ten years back is Felix’s, and his fate became unreadable ten years ago. She tells Monica that her love fortune is at its peak. Louis explains that Mary will release the land’s gathered mana at the festival with an ancient tool, 〈星紡ぎのミラ〉, which has a will of its own and kills male owners. In the town, an odd-job craftsman called Bartholomeus, the man who forged the crest on the false carriage in V02, plans to look at the tool and sell replicas.

At the festival Mary takes Monica into the church under an illusion and tells her that the tool is also a weapon. Monica sees Felix in a gatekeeper’s costume and follows him into the crowd, where the press of people brings back the crowd at her father’s burning. Bartholomeus helps her up and lifts her to see a play about Bartholomew Alexander. Felix finds her, sees her off from the stranger, and when she claims to be a delinquent out for the night, laughs openly, says they are delinquents together, and asks her to call him Ike. Bartholomeus, meanwhile, finds the church’s vestry unguarded and the tool unlocked; it binds itself to his right hand and carries him away through the stained glass.

Felix takes Monica to a brothel, where the prostitute Doris dresses her in a slip and lectures her until she escapes into numbers, and where the proprietor, Madam Cassandra, hands Felix letters from nobles under the duke who resent him. At the stalls Monica notices that a brooch said to be the Jewel Mage’s work resembles Cyril’s brooch. She declines jewellery, recalling that the nuts Felix once picked up for her mattered more, and flinches when he touches her face. He tells her that if people frighten her she can think of him as a ghost. Mary’s owl brings word that the tool has been stolen. Monica leaves Lynn to guard Felix and has Lynn hurl her across the town. Bartholomeus, pleading that he is a victim, is lifted into the sky by the tool, which wants them to die together. Monica, who cannot fly, flies, steadying herself with what Felix taught her about riding, survives the tool’s volleys by dropping out of flight to raise two shields, and puts the tool to sleep with a dream-showing mind spell. Lynn catches them both. Felix, finding Monica again, asks whether she disliked playing with him; she answers that she too is a ghost tonight, and asks Ike to keep playing.

He takes her to Porter’s secondhand bookshop. On the way he says that a friend once asked him to find something he could be absorbed in for his own sake, that as king he would lose his freedom, and, with an emptied face, that he must become king. In the shop he finds back numbers of the Minerva journal with the Silent Witch’s papers, admits that he is studying magic in secret because he is forbidden to, calls himself her greatest admirer, and says that her chantless magic is beautiful and that this may be first love. Monica finds a book on heredity and magic by Venedict Rain, burned with its author five years earlier, which opens with the sentence she has always used; it is her father’s. Porter says it was written by his friend and prices it at two gold coins. Felix pays, telling her that he cannot know its worth but that it is clearly worth that much to her, and she weeps. During the magic offering Monica rings her bell for her father, and Felix rings it for a friend to whom he wanted to say that he would make the friend’s wish come true.

Back at the brothel they read; Monica drinks mulled wine by mistake, strips, curls up like a cat and falls asleep. Felix dreams of a sickly boy addressed as Felix who tried to contract his dead mother’s spirit to please his grandfather, of the attendant boy who smuggled him an astronomy book, and of the duke whipping the attendant and making the boy burn the book, calling him a failure unworthy of being Irene’s son. Mary, resealing the tool, sees beside Monica’s star someone’s star with a fate of loss. Lynn asks her why she let the tool be stolen on purpose. She answers that it was the stars’ guidance.

In the morning Felix gives Monica a peridot pendant the colour of her eyes. He tells her that he had at first suspected her of being an assassin, presses her hands to his throat, then calls her an amusing pet and a fellow delinquent who shares his secret. He asks her to remember Ike. He notices the old scars on her back; she has never wanted to remove them; he says the scar on his side is necessary. In the carriage yard his smile becomes the prince’s again. Lynn reports that the man who impersonated Pittman has died. Louis, examining the body, proves that it is the real Pittman, preserved, and that the impostor escaped with an accomplice. Bartholomeus, who has escaped and believes the beautiful maid is the Silent Witch, takes a commission to make a Serendia uniform from a young woman, Heidi, and a plain man whose face slips: the impostor, Yuan, who means to expose Felix’s secret and Monica’s at the festival. Monica learns from Nero’s favourite novel that Porter is the novelist Dustin Günther.

The secret episode follows Louis to a convent in the northern mountains where Casey now shovels snow. The 螺炎 was made with a ruby from an imperial mine, and the intruder used imperial magic; Louis fears that Randall and the Empire are allied. Casey asks after Monica. Louis tells her about the black-dragon campaign, in which Monica feared the dragon knights and not the dragon, turned the knights into numbers, and destroyed the dragon alone, and calls her a warped, heartless witch from whom no feeling should be expected. Casey gives him a small package for Monica, without her name.

## 6. Sustained diagnostic close reading

Volume 02 gave Monica’s silence a history and her magic a duty. Volume 03 asks what her silence has cost other people and what it would take for her to stop hiding inside it, and it asks the same of Felix. Its argument, stated in advance, is that the book is built as a pair of confessions made under masks. Monica finally confronts the friend who taught her to hide, and renounces his approval; Felix, disguised as a ghost called Ike, shows her a self he says will not survive the night. Each recognizes the other in a scene about a book that someone in authority destroyed. Formally the volume does three new things. It opens in another character’s retrospective mind, so that Monica’s origin as a Sage is told by the person who resented it. It gives the antagonists, the tool and even the narrator’s seer extended viewpoints of their own. And it closes with Louis telling Casey exactly the opposite of what the reader has just watched, so that the last word on Monica in the volume is wrong.

Observation numbers in the form “obs. 007” point to the register in section 6.19.

### 6.1 The helper who needed her helpless

The prologue is Bernie’s, and it is an indictment he writes against himself without meaning to. He rescues the thirteen-year-old Monica from boys who are trying to make her speak with a spider, and he does it with a code Elliott will repeat in chapter 2: violence against the weak is unworthy of a noble (S11:E000094; obs. 002). Monica’s first words to him are thanks for saving the spider (S11:E000129–E000135), and her first full sentence explains that she practised speaking but had gone so long without talking to anyone that the words would not come (S11:E000184–E000187). The narration then gives the fact the rest of the book depends on. Bernie half knew that classmates were cutting her hair and hiding her things, looked away, and wanted, without admitting it, that she stay isolated so that she would rely on him (S11:E000255–E000263; obs. 004). His help was real; so was his need for her to need it.

The turn comes through his own generosity. Seeing her freeze before MacLaggan, Bernie offers to teach her to halve the chant (S11:E000333–E000347). At the next practical she does not halve it. A candle’s tip is cut and the water that puts it out becomes a sphere and then a small snake, and the room understands 「これはモニカが行使している魔術なのだ」 (S11:E000392; obs. 006). Bernie’s first thought is that he did not teach her this (S11:E000402–E000404), his second that she can do nothing alone without him (S11:E000429–E000442). The jealousy is narrated as betrayal. V01 said that Monica learned chantless magic because she failed exams she could not speak in; V03 shows the immediate trigger was a friend’s scheme to make her speak less, which she carried to its limit (obs. 007).

The prologue ends with the origin of the hut. When the fifteen-year-old Sage comes in her robe to thank him, Bernie answers that she is not his friend and that she should be shut away alone in a mountain hut (S11:E000541–E000555; obs. 009). The narration’s last line notes that the rumour later said she lived like a hermit, and that Bernie regained his peace (S11:E000573–E000576). The hut life that V01 presented as Monica’s eccentricity is here the literal execution of a rejected friend’s curse.

### 6.2 Riding as posture and number

Chapter 1 begins the volume’s practical education. Monica, who introduces herself perfectly only to a horse, falls from a walking horse four times and asks why she cannot do what everyone else does; the narration answers that she ran away from what everyone does (S13:E000251–E000253; obs. 012). Felix mounts behind her and teaches posture, eyes up, and the trot as a binary rhythm, like the dance (S13:E000369–E000371). Monica volunteers that horses do not frighten her and people do (S13:E000340–E000342; obs. 013). The exchange matters twice. It gives Felix the first sentence in which Monica explains a personal motive, that she wants to tell a friend she has learned to ride (S13:E000409), and it plants the technique that saves her in chapter 10, where she straddles her staff like a horse and posts to the binary rhythm Felix taught her in order to fly at all (S22:E000431–E000446; obs. 095). The book’s most physical feat is assembled from a lesson in which she was afraid of being touched.

The same chapter lets Monica notice Felix’s interest in magic for the first time. He leads her off the course to watch Cyril fight in the magic-battle field, explains the field’s rules with unusual knowledge, and justifies it as preparation for emergencies; she sees his eyes shining and remembers that he says he cannot use magic (S13:E000636–E000641; obs. 016). Section 6.13 shows what that shine was.

### 6.3 Elliott’s gentle boy

Elliott’s chapter 2 memory is the volume’s first sustained account of the child Felix, and it is a portrait of the opposite of the prince Monica knows. At six, at Duke Clockford’s house, Elliott met a sickly boy convalescing away from the castle: clumsy, unable to lift a practice sword or ride unless his attendant held him, a poor dancer with a poor memory, so shy that he bit his tongue and could not greet a guest (S14:E000206–E000213; obs. 018). His attendant was more poised and articulate than he was (S14:E000233). He apologized for not being able to do things properly (S14:E000241), loved astronomy and read it secretly (S14:E000248–E000249). When Elliott hid his book in a tree, the boy climbed after it, fell, and was pierced in the side by a branch; he then lied, telling the adults that Elliott had tried to stop him (S14:E000269–E000319; obs. 019). The boy’s eyes are given as 水色 (S14:E000353–E000354).

Elliott draws his own conclusion: Monica reminds Felix of that boy, which is why he cannot leave her alone (S14:E000367–E000370; obs. 020). The reading records Elliott’s inference as his, not the book’s. What the chapter adds to SW-C-0022 is detail of a kind that later scenes can test: a scar in the side, light-blue eyes, a passion for stars, an attendant who was better at being a prince. Section 6.14 records how the volume returns to every one of these and explains none.

### 6.4 The stalemate and the name

Two games in chapter 2 frame Monica’s new willingness to want something. Against Elliott without handicap she plays to a stalemate (S14:E000386), and Elliott, reading her style, says she would use the king as a decoy, treats every piece as equal, and would become a monster with experience (S14:E000393–E000415; obs. 021). His account of why he hates upstarts, his uncle ruined by a commoner wife and found hanged (S14:E000454–E000467), ends in a warning he means kindly: talent that jumps class walls is 「無能に妬まれるか、ずる賢い奴に利用される」 (S14:E000499; obs. 022). The prologue has already shown the first half of that sentence, and the volume will supply the second in Louis’s secret-episode verdict.

The game with Felix is a bargain. He offers one wish if she wins and throws the game; she sees it and forces a stalemate instead, full effort spent on not winning his gift (S14:E000815–E000830). Her wish, when it comes, is 「子リスって呼ぶの、やめてほしい、ですっ！」 (S14:E000885; obs. 025). Felix calls her Monica from then on, and the narrator lets the imagined Louis add that she should have specified conditions (S14:E000911–E000916). The new name is the first thing Monica has asked of Felix for herself, and the rest of the volume tests its terms: he calls her a squirrel again under the festival’s masks (S21:E000356–E000362), and in the morning calls her a pet.

### 6.5 Armour

Chapter 3 converts grooming into arming. Lana laces Monica into a corset, which she presents as posture correction, applies makeup to change her impression, and adds plain glasses (S15:E000229–E000317). The mirror shows a girl Monica does not recognize: 「すごく、健康そうに見える！」 (S15:E000330; obs. 032). In the washroom before the tournament Monica remembers Louis bringing a mirror to the hut and telling her to attend to her appearance, and understands it now as armament: the corset is armour (S15:E000744–E000757; obs. 034). The theme returns in the corridor, where a slouch is corrected by the corset digging into her (S16:E000605), and in the aftermath, where she tells Lana that the corset and the makeup kept her upright and stopped her tears (S16:E000773–E000775).

The armour is also disguise, and the book is careful about what it does not hide. Bernie recognizes her by name and resemblance, then confirms it by attacking her with magic (S16:E000496). The disguise protects the Monica who must face him, not the identity he already suspects. Its value is the one Lana’s father taught: first impressions are posture and expression (S15:E000296).

### 6.6 “My friend”

Bernie’s accusation in chapter 4 is the volume’s hinge. He says that she is playing at a do-over school life with men around her, that she approached the prince by feigning weakness, a parasite, and that she is a cunning person, selfish and indifferent to others (S16:E000510–E000567; obs. 042). Monica hears in it how he always saw her and blames herself: she had wanted to be a friend he could be proud of, which was presumptuous (S16:E000580–E000620). Lana arrives, is warned that Monica uses people, and diagnoses him in one line: 「……貴方、モニカに嫉妬してるんでしょ」 (S16:E000715; obs. 044).

Monica’s reply has two parts, and their order is the argument. First she defends Lana: if he speaks ill of her friend, she will not be able to forgive him (S16:E000740). Only then does she speak for herself, telling him she always relied on him, wanted to be someone he could rely on, and will now give up on his praise and want nothing more from him (S16:E000746–E000753; obs. 045). The narration says her eyes no longer reflect him (S16:E000761). V02 showed that she is most fluent when defending someone else; V03 shows that the same fluency, once begun, carries her into her own case. The renunciation is the first time she refuses an expectation rather than failing it.

Bernie’s own viewpoint makes the cost visible. He had read all her papers since cutting her off and drew a dark satisfaction from having first cared for her and then trampled her; now she expects nothing of him, and he cannot accept it (S16:E000792–E000809; obs. 046). He makes himself vanguard to face her (S16:E000815). In chapter 5 she beats him without looking at him, with what the narration likens to the mercilessness of shooting wyverns (S17:E000102–E000112), and he names the wall between a true genius and an able ordinary person (S17:E000212–E000213; obs. 050).

### 6.7 The impostor and the cover returned

The intruder plot runs through Bernie’s eyes, and its moral turn is his. He notices that the Minerva teacher Pittman does not know MacLaggan or Boyd and taught a different subject (S17:E000278–E000305), asks 「……貴方は誰です？」 (S17:E000267), and is answered with scales, claws and a voice neither male nor female naming the dragonization spell; body-altering magic is forbidden everywhere except the Empire (S17:E000366–E000383; obs. 051–052). Monica arrives and is trapped in a sphere of water meant to drown her silently. The narration explains that a mage inside cannot chant and can only wait for death, then adds one line: 「詠唱が必要な魔術師なら」 (S17:E000433–E000435). The sphere cracks and breaks (S17:E000436–E000439). The trap that would have silenced any other mage fails against the one who never needed her voice. She drops the dragon with a wind bullet to its single weak point, the brow (S17:E000457–E000468; obs. 053).

The scene’s second half reverses the prologue. Monica, soaked, sees herself as the bullied silent girl again and concludes that her false school life is over (S17:E000506–E000529; obs. 055). Bernie, who has just understood from her commands to Lynn that she is secretly guarding the prince (S17:E000502–E000504; obs. 054), sends the spirit away, tells Cyril that he himself unmasked and beat the impostor and that Norton was a bystander, and then says to her: 「貴女なんて、一生僕に感謝してればいいんです」 (S17:E000533–E000587; obs. 056). The sentence is ungracious in form and generous in act. The boy who once wanted her to need him now hides her so that she can go on without him; his price is that she remain grateful. The volume does not resolve whether that is repentance or possession, and the reading records both (section 8.2).

### 6.8 The duke and the vow

Chapter 6 shows the duke for the first time and is scrupulous about what it shows. Darius Knightley, Duke Clockford, Felix’s maternal grandfather, overrules the headmaster with two words, 「決行せよ」 (S18:E000080; obs. 060). Before the headmaster he is a grandfather; the headmaster believes he loves the boy (S18:E000126–E000140). Alone, he calls Felix 「恥さらしが」 (S18:E000160), and Felix’s face loses all softness; his eyes are glass without light, and the narration calls him a doll (S18:E000162–E000169; obs. 061). Felix offers one reasoned objection, is told not to talk back, and answers 「仰せのままに、閣下」 (S18:E000183). The scene gives the first direct evidence of the hold that SW-P-0007 asks about: domination without affection, enforced by contempt. It gives no ground for it.

The same evening the narration gives Felix’s viewpoint its plainest statement. He tells his spirit that today is a special day once a year and that he will not mistake his most important purpose, and he repeats a vow he says has not wavered for ten years: to do anything to inscribe the name Felix Arc Ridill in history (S18:E000395–E000415; obs. 065). The vow names its subject in the third person. The narration notes his eyes as light blue with a drop of green (S18:E000414). The reading records the vow as the first stated answer to SW-P-0002, Felix’s wish, and records separately that the grammar of the vow and the colour of his eyes join the details in section 6.14 without explaining them.

### 6.9 The seer’s blind spot

Mary Harvey’s chapter is a comic dinner that delivers three facts. The roster of the Seven Sages is completed in her gossip: the Barrier Mage and the Jewel Mage, Emmanuel, bicker; the Thorn Witch, Raul, eats vegetables; the Cannon Mage, Bradford, snores; the Abyss Curse Mage, Ray, attends even less than Monica (S19:E000131; obs. 068). Monica, whose calculations Mary’s astronomers used, learns what the star was: 「第二王子、フェリクス・アーク・リディル殿下よ」 (S19:E000260), and that his fate alone became unreadable about ten years ago (S19:E000272; obs. 070). And Louis explains that the ancient tool for the festival has a will and kills male owners (S19:E000418–E000432; obs. 073).

The ten years align with Felix’s ten-year vow and with nothing else the book explains. The reading notes that Mary’s method, astronomy recomputed by Monica, makes the unreadability a mathematical fact within the fiction rather than a seer’s mood: the star’s computed course and the reading diverge (S19:E000239–E000250). Mary’s comic prophecy for Monica, a passionate night with a fine gentleman (S19:E000291), is fulfilled literally and innocently in the epilogue; her serious one, that this night will be the country’s turning point (S22:E000381), is not tested inside the volume.

### 6.10 Crowds and bells

The bell-ringing festival is constructed around the thing Monica has never been allowed: mourning. Isabelle explains its lore first, that the bells send back the dead who escaped when the underworld’s gatekeeper went to enjoy the festival (S18:E000455–E000475; obs. 066). In chapter 8 the crowd sweeps Monica away, and the pressure of bodies brings back the crowd at her father’s burning, 「罪人を燃やせ」 (S20:E000142), and her own voice, 「おとう、さん」 (S20:E000147; obs. 076). The memories, the narration says, are becoming clearer (S20:E000148).

The stranger who picks her up gives her the festival’s meaning. Bartholomeus tells her that in his homeland’s festival the vengeful dead are frightened off with masks, and that a bell to mourn and send off the dead is a good custom (S20:E000291–E000297). Monica thinks of how many people get to say goodbye, bury and mourn their dead, and of the pain of having none of these, and if the bells can mourn, they save those left behind (S20:E000306–E000327; obs. 077). The thought becomes the motive of the climax. When the tool is stolen she decides to stop it because 「弔うことで救われる人がいる……わたしみたいに」 (S21:E000682; obs. 088), and says to the tool itself 「お祭り、成功させたいんですっ！」 (S22:E000535). In chapter 11 she rings her bell for her father with his book in her arms, and the narration grants that on the night the dead visit, she did meet her father (S23:E000477–E000483; obs. 108). The volume’s action plot is a mourning plot by other means.

### 6.11 Ike

The disguise Felix chooses is the gatekeeper of the underworld, the festival’s truant, in a white half mask (S20:E000088–E000097). He finds Monica, sends the stranger off with thanks for protecting her, warns her not to trust strangers, and asks what she is doing there. She will not lie about Isabelle and claims instead to be a delinquent (S20:E000402–E000439). He laughs: 「じゃあ僕と同じだね。不良同士だ」 (S20:E000441; obs. 078). It is the first time he uses 僕 to her. He asks her to call him Ike, not Ike-sama, since they are fellow delinquents (S20:E000456–E000460).

Chapter 9 gives Ike a world. In Madam Cassandra’s house Felix is a known patron; the madam passes him letters from nobles under the duke who resent him, and he tells her he will probably not be able to come again (S21:E000193–E000213; obs. 082). The scene is in Felix’s viewpoint, and it shows him collecting the duke’s malcontents while the duke believes he commands him. He calls Monica 「僕の友人ですよ」 (S21:E000242). When Monica flinches from his hand and he understands that people frighten her, he offers her a fiction: 「僕のことは幽霊だと思えばいい」 (S21:E000600), a friend Ike who is 「本当はどこにもいない幽霊なんだ」 (S21:E000607; obs. 087). The narration sees kindness and loneliness in his eyes (S21:E000608). The chapter title, the kind ghost, takes him at his word.

The ghost conceit returns when Monica comes back from the flight. Felix, unmasked and hurt, asks 「僕と遊ぶのは嫌だった？」 (S22:E000661) and offers to vanish, since a ghost refused by the living departs (S22:E000694). Monica remembers Bernie’s charge that she is selfish and indifferent, finds it true, and refuses to flee again: 「今のわたしは幽霊ですっ」 (S22:E000699), neither the Silent Witch nor Monica Norton, and asks Ike to play on (S22:E000700–E000701; obs. 100). It is the first time in three volumes that she holds out her hand first. The narrator registers that his answering smile is not the prince’s but an unadorned young man’s (S22:E000723).

### 6.12 Flight, number and mercy

The climax is technically the most detailed action sequence in the series so far, and it is built from the volume’s lessons. Detection is a speciality Monica can construct perfectly and reads poorly (S21:E000687–E000689). Flying is something she cannot do. She asks Lynn to throw her at a bell tower (S21:E000738), and when the tool carries Bartholomeus away and shatters her barrier with arrows of light (S22:E000306–E000308; obs. 091), she flies for the first time in years. She cannot balance; she remembers that balance is needed in riding, straddles the staff, applies what Felix taught her about posture, and posts to the trot’s binary count (S22:E000431–E000446; obs. 095).

Under the tool’s volleys she retreats into number, 「世界は数字でできている」 (S22:E000491), computes the arrows’ speed, the shield size she can hold and their non-homing trajectories, and then cancels her flight so that the arrows can come only from above and she can hold two shields instead of one, a manoeuvre the narration says only a chantless caster could make (S22:E000492–E000510; obs. 096). She has counted the interval between volleys, about three and a half seconds, and spends it climbing straight at the target with no defence (S22:E000513–E000515).

The solution is not destruction. Ancient tools cannot be remade, so Monica refuses the easy shot (S22:E000327–E000328), remembers Selma, whom a mind spell had made impossible to persuade (S22:E000341–E000345), and reasons that a tool with a human will might respond to a spell meant for humans (S22:E000348–E000350; obs. 092). The spell she casts shows the tool a dream of a flower field, an ordinary girl, and a beloved whose face she cannot see (S22:E000558–E000567; obs. 098). Mind interference, the quasi-forbidden art she used on Thornley in V01, is here used as mercy, and the narration gives the tool the volume’s only viewpoint of simple happiness. Lynn notes that Mary will overlook its use in an emergency (S22:E000625–E000627; obs. 099). The question SW-P-0009 asked, whether Monica’s mind interference has consequences, receives a first, mild answer: an institutional tolerance, not a reckoning.

### 6.13 The value of a book

Chapter 11 is the volume’s centre, and it pairs two confessions in one shop. On the way, Felix quotes a friend who asked him to find something he could be absorbed in for his own sake, and says he has been looking ever since (S23:E000061). Monica reads in his face emptiness and resigned loneliness and concludes that the all-round genius has never been absorbed in anything (S23:E000066–E000076). He says that as king he will lose his freedom, so that the time he spends as a ghost is like the remainder of a life in which he can be himself (S23:E000077). Asked whether he wants to be king, he answers that it is not so, and his face empties: 「私は王にならなくてはいけないんだ」 (S23:E000079–E000082; obs. 101). The pronoun returns to 私 for that sentence alone.

In Porter’s bookshop the thing he is absorbed in turns out to be Monica. He finds back numbers of the Minerva journal, cries out that the Silent Witch’s paper is in one, and admits that he is interested in magic and forbidden to study it (S23:E000149–E000184; obs. 103). He summarizes her paper on positional coordinates in wide-area formulas accurately enough that she agrees with every word in silence (S23:E000198–E000200), calls himself her greatest admirer, says her chantless magic is 「すごく美しいんだ」 (S23:E000204), and accepts Porter’s comparison with a man in love with an actress: 「きっとこれを初恋って言うんじゃないかな」 (S23:E000215). He has seen her at two ceremonies and never her face, and as king he could summon her at any time (S23:E000245–E000262; obs. 104). Dramatic irony is at full extension: the person he admires is beside him, and she vows to keep her hood on for ever (S23:E000272).

Then Monica finds 『遺伝形質から読み解く魔力性質　著：ヴェネディクト・レイン』 (S23:E000301), recalled and burned with its author five years earlier, and opening with 『──世界は数字でできている』 (S23:E000305; obs. 105). Variants of the sentence have run through Monica’s thinking since V01, and V02 gave a related saying to her father; here it is printed as the first line of his book, and she uses it herself in the flight (S22:E000491). Porter prices the book at two gold coins because it was written by his friend (S23:E000323–E000332; obs. 106). Felix pays, and says 「僕にはその本の価値が分からないけれど」, adding that it is clearly worth that much to her (S23:E000350; obs. 107). Monica weeps because the crowd had mocked the book as worthless and burned it, and her uncle had beaten her, again and again, for trying to say what it was worth (S23:E000352–E000367). Felix did not value the book; he allowed her to.

The chapter places the two absorptions side by side and gives them the same structure. Felix studies in secret what he is forbidden; Monica keeps in a locked drawer what was burned. Each finds in the shop a printed record of the other’s private devotion, and neither knows the other’s. At the end the narration gives Felix a gaze that looks at her as one looks at an irreplaceable memory, nostalgic and gentle (S23:E000424–E000434). Chapter 11’s continuation tells the reader what memory.

### 6.14 The cluster around Felix, extended and unexplained

SW-C-0022 recorded at V02 a cluster of unexplained details around Felix’s identity and history. V03 adds to it more than any earlier volume, and the reading lists the additions without proposing what joins them.

- **The child in Elliott’s memory** is sickly, clumsy, shy, devoted to astronomy, light-blue-eyed, scarred in the side, and attended by a more poised boy (S14:E000206–E000354; obs. 018–019).
- **The vow** is to inscribe the name Felix Arc Ridill in history, made ten years ago, spoken of the name in the third person (S18:E000415; obs. 065).
- **The seer** lost his fate about ten years ago (S19:E000272; obs. 070).
- **The friend** asked him to find something to be absorbed in, is mourned with the festival bell, and was promised that his wish would be granted (S23:E000061; S23:E000501–E000508; obs. 101, 109). Monica infers that the friend is dead (S23:E000512).
- **The dream.** Felix, sleeping beside Monica, dreams of a room in which a boy addressed as 「フェリクス様」 (S25:E000232) tries to contract the high spirit in his late mother’s necklace to please his grandfather, and is told by a page boy that the spirit’s element differs from his own (S25:E000234–E000247; obs. 112). The boy is bad at study and sport, frail and shy (S25:E000250); the page smuggles him an astronomy book strapped to his own body and says 「でも、貴方には大切なものなのでしょう？」 (S25:E000287). The duke has the page whipped, makes the boy throw the book into the fire, and calls him 「こんな出来損ないがアイリーンの息子だとは」 (S25:E000308–E000357; obs. 113). The page looked at the boy like a younger brother (S25:E000303).
- **The waking.** Felix wakes and thinks of the dream coldly, as a nostalgic one, brought on by Monica’s face in the bookshop, which was like a little boy who once cried holding an astronomy book (S25:E000364–E000376; obs. 114).
- **The scar.** In the morning Monica sees a puckered scar in his side, conspicuous on an otherwise perfect body, and he says 「これは必要な傷だからね」 (S26:E000283–E000299; obs. 121).
- **The eyes.** The child’s eyes are 水色 (S14:E000353); the present Felix’s are 碧 throughout, once light blue with a drop of green (S18:E000414).

The volume narrates all of this and states none of the relations among the page, the friend, the boy in the dream, the child in Elliott’s memory and the present Felix. Three formal facts are nevertheless observable. Felix’s line to Monica in the bookshop repeats the page’s line to the boy almost word for word (S23:E000350; S25:E000287). Felix’s own viewpoint describes the boy of the dream from outside, as a little boy who cried, not as himself (S25:E000376). And the present Felix is narrated as an all-round genius at study, sport and magic, the inverse of the boy. The reading registers no hypothesis and keeps SW-C-0022 and SW-P-0016 OPEN (section 9.1).

### 6.15 The pendant and the throat

The epilogue’s morning scene is the volume’s most ambiguous. Felix, half-dressed, laughs at Monica’s fear of execution and asks whether anyone kills a kitten for creeping into bed (S26:E000062; obs. 117). He has put a peridot on her that matches her eyes (S26:E000073–E000096; obs. 117). Then he offers 「一つ、本音を話そうか」 (S26:E000119): when he found her in the town he thought she might be an assassin. He takes her cold hands and presses them to his own throat, as if inviting her to strangle him, and Monica finds this terrifying (S26:E000125–E000132; obs. 118). He says he knows she is not an assassin, since she would have done it already, and that because she seems no enemy but too unreliable to be an ally he has decided to think of her as an amusing pet, and now as a fellow delinquent who shares his secret (S26:E000138–E000156).

The scene can be read two ways, and the volume supports both. It is a test: he exposed his throat to see what she would do, as he tested her with keys in V01. It is also a confession: he has spent the night unguarded with someone he suspected, and tells her so. Monica says she wants nothing from him at all, and he answers that he knew, and finds it both easy and a little lonely (S26:E000177–E000190; obs. 119). He then says that the pendant is not for her but for his own satisfaction, tugs it so that the chain bites, and asks whether a thing worn on the body is not good for holding a person’s heart (S26:E000193–E000205). He kisses the stone and asks her to remember Ike (S26:E000211–E000212).

The epilogue’s title, tying a ribbon on a kitten, gives the scene the language of possession, and Felix’s words support it. Monica’s viewpoint gives it the language of farewell: she understands that she will never play with Ike again and that the excessive gifts are meant to keep a memory of him (S26:E000223–E000224; obs. 120). She decides not to take the pendant off while he can see, and knows she will never wear it of her own accord (S26:E000239–E000240). The reading records the kitten-and-ribbon framing, the throat and the farewell as coexisting; it does not resolve whether Felix’s attachment is to Monica, to Ike, or to a self he says the duke will not let survive.

### 6.16 Louis tells Casey the wrong story

The secret episode ends the book with a verdict the reader knows to be false. Louis, visiting Casey at the convent to pursue the Empire’s part in the 螺炎 (S28:E000112–E000136; obs. 128), answers her question about Monica with the story of the black dragon of Worgan. Monica, he says, feared not the dragon but the dragon knights, 「知らない人がたくさんで怖い」 (S28:E000231; obs. 129). His memory shows her ashen, vomiting in hiding, and then refusing his list of the knights because she had already memorized them: people are easy to remember and stop being frightening if you think of them as numbers (S28:E000284–E000309; obs. 130). She called the commander by a number and destroyed the dragon alone.

Louis concludes that she fears and hates people from the bottom of her heart and can therefore be endlessly merciless, a warped and heartless witch from whom no feeling should be expected, and that this is why he chose her (S28:E000330–E000332; obs. 131). The memory is precise and the inference is half right. The reader has just watched her mourn her father with a bell, weep over a book, refuse to destroy a tool because it cannot be remade, put that tool to sleep with a dream, and hold out her hand to a ghost. Casey, who knew her as a friend, storms out and returns with a package for Monica (S28:E000333–E000336). The episode’s irony runs against the most knowledgeable adult in the series: Louis knows the Silent Witch of the dragon campaign; Casey, and the reader, know the girl of this volume. The black-dragon memory also strengthens SW-C-0002 at its root: numerical absorption is not only Monica’s defence against pain but her technique for making people bearable.

### 6.17 The visual channel

The images continue V01’s and V02’s pattern of disclosure ahead of the prose: the frontispiece spread prints the prologue’s climactic sentence before the prologue (S03:E000004; obs. 139), and the eared-cape frontispiece prints a chapter 9 passage (S04:E000004). The bilingual plate frames the prologue as the story of a dropout who could not speak (S12:E000004; obs. 010). Two plates introduce new characters as types with names and years, Benjamin and Bernie (S14:E000991; S17:E000609). The full-page Mary plate is placed inside Monica’s flight rather than at Mary’s own scene, so that the seer appears while the person she is testing struggles (S22:E000423; obs. 094). The landscape spread of Monica raising her bell with Felix behind her, both looking up at rising lights, is the volume’s emotional image, and it shows the two as fellow mourners rather than as a couple (S24:E000004; obs. 110). Felix’s plate in the gatekeeper’s costume is the only image of him with an open grin (S20:E000410; obs. 079). The cover, viewed before reading, shows Lynn holding the ornate box that the reader later learns contains the tool; that is the visual channel’s only advance disclosure of the climax.

### 6.18 Japanese speech and address

Five features of the Japanese text carry arguments in this reading.

First, Felix’s pronouns now mark masks rather than audiences. V01 and V02 recorded a public 私 and a private 僕 used of the Silent Witch. In V03 he uses 僕 to Monica as Ike from chapter 8 (S20:E000441), uses 私 when he says he must become king (S23:E000082) and when he answers Bridget about Monica’s background (S16:E000252), and the boy in his dream uses 私 to his grandfather and his attendant (S25:E000234; S25:E000259). The pronoun 僕 belongs to the self Felix says will not survive his accession.

Second, the address terms chart the volume’s relationships. The squirrel name is retired by a wager (S14:E000885), revived by the festival cape (S21:E000356–E000362), and contested in the morning (S26:E000158–E000164). Monica names Felix as Ike only with effort and says it at the moment of her decision (S22:E000701). She calls Cyril by his name and bites it (S18:E000315). Bernie addresses her in distant 貴女 throughout, even when protecting her (S17:E000587).

Third, Monica’s speech is now capable of sustained declaration under emotion: to Bernie (S16:E000736–E000753), to the tool (S22:E000517; S22:E000535) and to Felix (S22:E000699–E000701). Her failures persist where a body is close to hers: the bitten 殿下 (S13:E000278; S21:E000289), and her body stiffens at Felix’s touch even after the night as Ike (S26:E000105).

Fourth, the sesame emphasis marks mark the decisive technical choices: 「あの魔術」 for the dream spell she is about to try (S22:E000353) and 「飛行魔術を解除した」 for the counter-intuitive drop (S22:E000500). Emphasis also marks MacLaggan’s 「チェスの才能も」 (S16:E000851–E000852), the reading’s strongest evidence that he recognized her (section 9.3).

Fifth, the narrator still corrects in single deadpan lines: that the world calls a professional of night play a playboy (S23:E000052), and that Lynn’s regret at not carrying Monica like a princess was a very trivial regret (S22:E000607). The lines keep the reader outside every viewpoint, including the tool’s.

## 7. Character, directional relationship, world and formal development

Each subsection states what V03 adds and where the ledger records it. Claims in section 9 carry the load-bearing arguments; this section carries the state.

### 7.1 Monica Everett

V03 supplies the missing middle of her history. Before Minerva she had gone so long without speaking that words would not come (obs. 003). At Minerva her only friend helped her and needed her helpless (obs. 004); his shortened-chant plan was the immediate occasion of her first chantless casting (obs. 005–007); his envy ended the friendship, and his curse sent her to the hut (obs. 009). Professor Rutherford sent her to the selection; she wanted to tell her adoptive mother and her friend (obs. 023). Her father is named, Venedict Rain, executed for forbidden research five years before the present, and the character card adds that Rain was her own surname and Everett is her adoptive mother’s (obs. 105, 133). The uncle beat her again and again for stating the worth of her father’s book, and the scars remain on her back (obs. 106, 121).

Her present conduct changes in four observable ways. She defends a friend and, from that footing, renounces the approval she had wanted for years (obs. 045). She asks Felix for something for herself, the end of a nickname (obs. 025). She acts from a motive she names as her own, that the bereaved be able to mourn (obs. 088, 097), and acknowledges that her claim is as selfish as the tool’s. And she holds out her hand first (obs. 100). Unchanged are her fear of people, especially tall men and close touch (obs. 013, 087, 117), her use of number to make people bearable (obs. 096, 130), and her secrecy toward friends (obs. 058). She flies for the first time, using riding technique (obs. 095), and casts mind interference on an object (obs. 098). Ledger: SW-S-0045 to SW-S-0049.

### 7.2 Felix Arc Ridill

Felix’s private viewpoint is extended further than in any earlier volume, and every extension deepens the cluster recorded in section 6.14. Stated: his vow, ten years old, to inscribe the name Felix Arc Ridill in history (obs. 065); his duty to become king without wanting it (obs. 101); his secret study of magic, which is forbidden to him, and his first-love-like admiration for the Silent Witch’s magic (obs. 103); his regular night outings and a life in the town in which he gathers letters from nobles who resent his grandfather (obs. 082). Shown: the duke’s contempt and his own emptied obedience (obs. 061); his laughter and his 僕 as Ike (obs. 078); his kindness to Monica’s fear of people (obs. 087); his permission to value her father’s book (obs. 107); his mourning of a friend (obs. 109); his dream of a boy, a page and a burned book (obs. 112–114); his suspicion of Monica as an assassin and his self-exposure to her hands (obs. 118); his gift framed as possession and farewell (obs. 119); his necessary scar (obs. 121). He still does not connect Monica with the Silent Witch (obs. 104). Ledger: SW-S-0050 to SW-S-0052.

### 7.3 Bernie Jones

New at V03. Second son of Count Amberd, Minerva student, captain of its chess team. The prologue presents him as rescuer, helper and betrayer in one person (obs. 001–009). In the present he recognizes Monica, accuses her, is defeated by her at chess, and then hides her identity and her mission while demanding lifelong gratitude (obs. 035, 042, 046, 050, 054, 056). He knows she is the Silent Witch on a secret guard mission for the second prince. Ledger: SW-S-0053.

### 7.4 Louis Miller and Mary Harvey

Louis lends Lynn to Monica, attends the festival for security and the tool’s unsealing, proves the body in the cell is the real Pittman, and pursues the Empire behind the 螺炎 (obs. 029, 073, 123, 128). His verdict on Monica, that she is a heartless witch chosen because she fears and hates people, is the volume’s explicit statement of his reason for choosing her and is contradicted by the narrative (obs. 131). He once brought her a mirror (obs. 034). Mary, the eldest Sage, trusted by the king, traced Felix’s unreadable star with Monica’s computations (obs. 070), permitted the tool’s theft as what she calls the stars’ guidance (obs. 116), tested Monica (obs. 093), and sees near Monica a star with a fate of loss (obs. 115). Ledger: SW-S-0054 to SW-S-0055.

### 7.5 The council and Monica’s friends

Elliott apologizes in effect for the castling game, discloses his uncle’s ruin as the root of his contempt for upstarts, and warns Monica that talent is envied or used (obs. 017, 022). His memory of the child Felix is the volume’s first evidence about Felix’s childhood from someone other than Felix (obs. 018–020). Lana dresses Monica for battle and names Bernie’s envy (obs. 032, 044). Claudia reads the duke’s politics and calls Felix a puppet (obs. 059). Cyril fights in the magic-battle field, still blames himself for the timber, gives Monica his jacket, and accepts her promise to make the festival succeed (obs. 015, 016, 057, 063). Isabelle explains the festival and gives up her wish to go with Monica (obs. 066, 089). Bridget probes Monica’s education, and Felix joins her (obs. 038). Benjamin Molding and Robert Winkel are comic, the second a Randall exchange student (obs. 027, 037, 040). Ledger: SW-S-0056 to SW-S-0059.

### 7.6 New figures outside the school

Bartholomeus Baal, an imperial craftsman expelled for striking his master, forged the V02 crest, was used by the tool, believes Lynn is the Silent Witch, and has taken a commission to make a Serendia uniform (obs. 074, 080, 090, 124). ## 8. Alternative readings, absence and contradiction

### 8.1 “The festival night is a romance”

The rival reading has strong surface support. Mary forecasts a passionate night with a fine gentleman (S19:E000291); Felix calls his admiration for the Silent Witch first love (S23:E000215); the two spend the night in a brothel bed; he gives her a pendant, kisses it, and asks her to remember him (S26:E000211–E000212); the narrator says the scene might look, from outside, like lovers vowing at dawn (S26:E000213). Against it, the volume is explicit at each step about what is not happening. The night in the brothel is a reading party ended by mulled wine and a girl asleep like a cat (S25:E000042–E000199). Monica tells Felix she wants nothing from him (S26:E000177–E000182). Her own viewpoint reads the pendant as a farewell gift to preserve a memory (S26:E000223–E000224). And the first love Felix names is for a figure he has never seen, whose magic he admires, not for the girl beside him (S23:E000245). The reading therefore keeps SW-C-0023 at its V02 formulation, strengthened: no romantic interest on Monica’s side is shown; Felix’s absorption is directed at the Silent Witch as an idea; and the night is shown as companionship, mourning and play. Whether either character later reinterprets it is an open question.

### 8.2 Bernie: repentance or possession

Bernie’s rescue of Monica’s cover can be read as repentance: he chooses to protect the person he wronged, at the cost of lying to Cyril and the authorities (S17:E000533–E000559). It can equally be read as a new form of the old possession: the price he names is lifelong gratitude (S17:E000587), the same dependence he wanted at thirteen, and his viewpoint in chapter 4 shows that her indifference, not her talent, is what he cannot bear (S16:E000800–E000809). The text supplies no viewpoint for Bernie after the rescue. The reading records the act as protective and the motive as UNRESOLVED.

### 8.3 Normative evaluation: Mary’s permitted theft

Criterion: whether the narration registers the risks to third parties of Mary’s choice. Lynn reports that the thief escaped confinement with tools no one confiscated, and asks why Mary let the tool be stolen on purpose; Mary answers only that it was the stars’ guidance (S25:E000455–E000474). The tool, by Mary’s own account and Monica’s calculation, could have destroyed the town (S20:E000029–E000037; S21:E000677), and it nearly killed Bartholomeus (S22:E000282–E000324). Mary watched and called it a test (S22:E000399). The volume presents Mary comically and sympathetically, and lets Lynn’s question stand unanswered. This reading records that a Sage endangered a festival crowd and a bystander to test a colleague or follow a prophecy, and that the volume declines to judge her.

### 8.4 Normative evaluation: the throat and the ribbon

Criterion: whether the effect on the affected person is registered independently of the scene’s tone. Felix presses Monica’s hands to his throat, and the narration says plainly that she found it terrifying (S26:E000130–E000132). He calls her a pet; she is crushed (S26:E000151–E000157). He tightens the pendant’s chain against her skin while speaking of holding a heart (S26:E000204–E000205). Her fear of touch, from the uncle’s violence, is stated in the same scene (S26:E000112). The epilogue’s title frames the morning as tying a ribbon on a kitten. The scene registers Monica’s fear and her choice not to hurt him, and also enjoys the comic register. The reading records that the volume registers the harm and treats Felix’s possessiveness as pathos, not as a wrong.

### 8.5 Normative evaluation: mind interference as mercy

Criterion: whether the use of a quasi-forbidden art is weighed. Monica hesitates before telling Lynn what she has done, because mind interference is permitted only in specified situations (S22:E000624–E000626); Lynn expects tolerance (S22:E000627). Against Thornley in V01 the spell was a trap; here it is a dream given to an object with a human will, which the narration renders as happiness (S22:E000558–E000567). The volume presents this use as mercy and as a solution that preserves an irreplaceable tool. It does not ask whether the tool consented, and the tool is resealed before dawn, weeping (S25:E000399–E000407). The reading records the use as narrated sympathetically and its ethics as unexamined.

### 8.6 The afterword, the jacket and the web serial

The afterword says that from V01 the author wanted the book edition to give Monica new challenges not in the web version, that V03 is that volume, and that V04 begins the school festival (S35:E000011–E000053; obs. 136). No web text was read or compared; the statement is recorded in the source map as a comparison lead, and the announcement of V04’s setting is publisher-stated content, not a reading prediction. The jacket copy, viewed only after the narrative had been read, summarizes the tournament chapters and quotes Monica’s fear that her false school life is over (S41:E000004; obs. 137). It is marketing paratext and is not used as evidence.

### 8.7 Meaningful absences inside V03

- The king and the other princes never appear; the succession is reported only by Felix, Claudia and the duke.
- Glen appears briefly flying in chapter 1 and as a spectator; nothing new bears on Louis’s disciple.
- Cyril never returns to his suspicion of Monica’s hand or of the forest mage.
- Nero’s nature is not touched; he is a reader of Dustin Günther and a jealous companion.
- The contents of Casey’s package are not given.
- Monica’s adoptive mother, the father’s pupil of V01, is mentioned once in the narrative, without a name (S14:E000622).
- Felix’s spirit Wildianu appears only as the concealer of his absence (S18:E000400–E000403).
- The kiss of V01 has no recurrence; Felix kisses a lock of her hair (S15:E000450–E000454) and the pendant.
