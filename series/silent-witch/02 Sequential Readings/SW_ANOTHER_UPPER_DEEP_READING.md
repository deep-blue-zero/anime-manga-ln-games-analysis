## 3. Current-unit story synopsis

The companion tells Louis Miller's boyhood and Minerva years, inside a frame. A prologue shows him in his mid-twenties, the youngest head of the Magic Corps. The thirteen chapters run from age eleven to his graduation. An epilogue follows his first dragon hunt, and a secret episode turns to a plot laid against him.

**The prologue.** On a south-eastern plain, Louis traps a herd of earth and fire dragons in his reflecting dome, the Mirror Prison. He summons the wind Spirit-King inside it and kills them all with a handful of men. That night, in his quarters, he counts his savings, which include twelve large silver coins kept in a jam jar. He cheers that he can buy a house in the capital, and vows over cheap liquor to become a Sage. The narration says he came from a nothing village in the snowy north, raised in a run-down brothel.

**Chapter one.** The boy Louis, odd-job boy and bouncer at that brothel near the imperial border, is given jam for the first time by a dying prostitute, Shona. She tells him to leave and make a family someday. He finds a teacher's annotated textbook dropped by Gideon Rutherford, the Purple Smoke Mage, a Minerva professor stranded by an avalanche. Paralysed by Rutherford's smoke and slapped, Louis answers with a wind spell learned from the book. He then learns every spell in it within a week. Rutherford recommends him to Minerva as a scholarship student. The women send him off with a jar holding twelve large silver coins, one from each, labelled with a command not to come back. He leaves at eleven, with no ambition beyond eating marmalade.

**Chapters two and three.** Admitted early to the dormitory, Louis frightens his assigned roommate, Terence Abernathy. When a classmate spills his marmalade, he beats three boys in the canteen, which earns him the name "Jam-Hunting Miller". His new roommate, Owen Wright, a year younger and exploited by richer classmates, thanks him with a jar of marmalade, and they become friends. In the library he meets Rosalie Verde, a serious girl with braided hair who is not afraid of him. He clears the assignment boxes Rutherford set and is admitted formally. His goal becomes top marks, so that Rosalie will call him amazing.

**Chapter four.** In class he casts thirty fireballs by shortened chanting and holds two spells at once, things he was never taught. The noble Adolf Farron has Terence throw away Louis's uniform. Louis throws Terence into a cesspit. In his first magic battle he punches two opponents before they can chant, shields himself with one of their bodies, and kicks Adolf. Rutherford stops him with paralytic smoke and a heel drop, and Louis becomes "the Bad Boy of Minerva".

**Chapter five.** A new battle barrier nullifies physical attacks. Rutherford fights Louis inside it and buries him with the earth Spirit-King. Louis learns that his teacher was once the Rock-Cavern Mage, captain of the knights' mage unit, and that his smoke passes through every barrier. Louis then reconstructs the unpublished barrier's formulas in reports for the barrier teacher, Majer. She lets him join her remedial classes, and he settles on barriers.

**Chapter six.** Louis looks for dense-mana land in the restricted forest, hoping to raise his mana. Rosalie follows him and stops him. She tells him she is the daughter of the Sage Birdland Verde and has only twenty mana. Then she collapses from mana poisoning. Louis carries her to the physician Woodman and feels the weight of having involved her. Next day, inside the barrier, he beats Adolf with tricks the barrier does not count as attacks: sand, a chokehold, punches with a small shield, a gag. Rutherford knocks him flat.

**Chapter seven.** Nearly three years later, Louis is almost fifteen. He works at Goa's diner beside Goa's daughter Sally. He meets Carla Maxwell, Rutherford's only student and the Star-Spear Witch. Because of his conduct, he must give a festival presentation to win promotion to the high division, which he otherwise qualifies for. He chooses the battle barrier, whose maker, Majer's teacher, has died, and works under Majer, whom he calls the Barrier Witch.

**Chapter eight.** At the festival, Sally dresses him as a girl to work her stall. He serves soup to a burly blond youth and his whispering attendant. Adolf steals his uniform and courts Rosalie. Louis takes revenge, and presents in Adolf's uniform, crediting Majer above his own name. Rosalie tells her father she wishes to go on; he lets her. Carla's light-attribute Star Spear splits the clouds. Majer recommends Louis to Rutherford's laboratory and retires. Louis promises to call himself the Barrier Mage.

**Chapter nine.** In the high division, the blond youth turns out to be the first prince, Lionel, and his attendant is Nate Wall. Louis fights Lionel with barrier fists and then with bare hands outside the barrier, and loses to a headbutt. Lionel, Nate and Rosalie become his companions at Goa's diner.

**Chapter ten.** Rutherford confiscates his liquor. Louis climbs to his teacher's window at night to swap it for laxative water, and overhears Rutherford call him a giant who is blind to others' goodwill. Rutherford's sneezing smoke defeats him. Louis then gives Rosalie a flower hairpin and apologises for scorning the waterworks her father built.

**Chapter eleven.** In the inter-laboratory tournament, Louis, Carla and Rutherford fight the laboratories of Allison, Redmond and MacLaggan. MacLaggan is the Water-Biting Mage, a former water-dragon hunter. Louis reflects Owen's thunder spear with a reflecting barrier, knocks Adolf out with a barrier fist, and partly reflects MacLaggan's water Spirit-King. Watching, Rosalie decides she must choose another path.

**Chapter twelve.** Rosalie leaves Minerva to become a doctor like Woodman, and thanks Louis for showing her that little mana is not everything. Lionel tells Louis his aim is to make the country good for his young half-brothers, whoever reigns. On her last night, Louis comes to her window and says he loves her. She answers that she loves him too, and they promise: when she is a doctor, he will come for her as a great mage.

**Chapter thirteen.** To marry her "properly", Louis passes the senior-mage examination. He presents the Mirror Prison and is accepted into the Magic Corps. Then he hears that Birdland means to wed Rosalie to a successor, and flies to confront him. The Sage loses his temper. He demands a man of refinement who owns a house in the capital and can become a Sage, and says Rosalie wants a prince. Louis swears to become a Sage. He spends a week learning upper-class speech and manners from Lionel, and graduates sitting upright.

**The epilogue.** In the Magic Corps, Louis kills his first fire dragon by opening a barrier inside the wound on its brow. At medical school, Rosalie refuses a suitor and tells a classmate that her type is an unconventional delinquent.

**The secret episode.** Adolf, now a researcher, resents the talk that Louis may follow Carla, newly a Sage. He reveals that he started the rumour about Birdland's successor. At an inn he and the Gem Mage Emmanuel Darwin are told by Duke Clockford, Darius Knightley, that both will become Sages within ten years.

## 6. Close reading

### 6.1 Placement, form and the frame

The book is built as a frame and a run.

The prologue shows the end state:

- Louis in his mid-twenties, head of the Magic Corps, not yet a Sage (obs. 006);
- his savings, which now reach a house in the capital (obs. 011);
- a vow to become a Sage (obs. 012).

The narration then names the book's subject as the boy from a nothing village who became a Sage (obs. 014). The thirteen chapters run forward from the brothel. The epilogue stops a month into his service in the Corps (obs. 213).

The prologue's three facts, the jar, the house and the Sage's seat, are each given an origin within the run:

- **The jar.** Twelve large silver coins sit in a jam jar in the prologue (obs. 011). They are the twelve coins the brothel women put in the jar when he left (obs. 031).
- **The house and the seat.** These are two of Birdland's three conditions for his daughter's husband. Louis swears to meet them at the end of chapter thirteen (obs. 210).
- **The manner.** The refinement the prologue notes over a northern past (obs. 013) is the speech and posture Lionel taught him in a week (obs. 211). The epilogue shows that this speech still makes him taciturn (obs. 213).

The prologue is therefore not a flash-forward for suspense. It is a statement of results whose causes the run supplies, ending with a comic reversal: the Sage's ambition is a marriage condition (section 6.2).

The run is dated by the school calendar:

- He arrives at the end of 春起月 and enters formally in 秋巡月 (obs. 036).
- Chapters four to six fall in his first middle-division year.
- Chapter seven jumps nearly three years, to the third middle-division year, when he is almost fifteen (obs. 125).
- Chapters nine to eleven cover the first two high-division years (obs. 160, 175).
- Chapter thirteen ends at graduation (obs. 212).

One marker ties the run to the numbered track. In Louis's first high-division year, the second prince Felix is seven (obs. 168). At his graduation a long-sick second prince has "recently recovered" and is being presented (obs. 209). Section 10 records what this does and does not establish.

The secret episode stands outside the run's viewpoint. It follows Adolf to Duke Clockford's inn after Carla becomes a Sage (obs. 215). Its function is causal disclosure: the rumour that sent Louis to Birdland was Adolf's (obs. 215). The book thus turns its central romantic resolve into the product of a rival's spite, without Louis knowing it.

### 6.2 The genius of effort and the near goal

The plate's motto calls Louis a hard worker because he hates to lose, a genius of hard work (obs. 004). The text repeats it at the point of his greatest effort: during the week of lessons in Lionel's rooms, he is called a genius of effort, at his best when proving someone wrong (S25:E000310).

The run shows this repeatedly. In each case the effort answers a slight:

- The first spell answers Rutherford's contempt (obs. 025).
- The week of spells answers a challenge (obs. 027, 028).
- The four boxes answer an exemption made to look impossible (obs. 036, 064).
- The barrier reports answer a beating (obs. 107, 108).
- The senior-mage examination and the week of manners answer Birdland (obs. 207, 211).

At every stage his goals are near ones:

- eating marmalade (obs. 033);
- making Rutherford eat his words (obs. 049);
- top marks for Rosalie's praise (obs. 066);
- beating the "bushy-browed geezer" (obs. 109).

He knows this. Beside Owen's dream of the Corps he feels he has no future in view (obs. 080, 097). To MacLaggan he says he wants only to feed himself, and that flattering nobles is the one thing he will never do (obs. 098).

The book's turn does not give him a large purpose. It gives him a person. In chapter twelve he finds that what he wants is Rosalie's hands tending him (obs. 202). From that point the near goal becomes a long one, though it stays personal:

- First he plans to be "proper" through the guild, the senior rank and the Corps (obs. 207).
- Then Birdland's outburst sets the terms: refinement, a house in the capital, and a Sage's seat (obs. 210).
- Finally he submits to the training he had refused, flattering manners (obs. 098, 211).

The narration's last word on the run is that he resolved to become a Sage only to marry Rosalie (obs. 212). This adds to SW-C-0043. There, Louis's maxim of money, violence and strength was set against Cyril's ethic of patient effort. The prequel shows the maxim's holder as a genius of effort himself. The difference lies in motive and display, not in effort (section 9.1).

### 6.3 Strangers huddling and a family to make

Chapter one gives the book's second thread in Shona's words. Leave this place and make a family someday, she says; the women are strangers huddling together, not a family (obs. 020). The boy dismisses it, and the women's coins and label prove her half wrong. When he leaves, he thinks such strangers are not so bad (obs. 031, 032).

The run then supplies, one by one, groups that are like families without being families:

- Owen, who repays a fight with marmalade (obs. 049);
- Goa's diner, where Goa rejoices at his first friends (obs. 174);
- Rou, who is greeted as family by Sally's fiancé though not her kin. Louis hears this on the evening after Rosalie's news, and his step lightens (obs. 203).

Chapter twelve joins the thread to the romance. Louis recalls Shona's words on the road and names what he wants (obs. 202). Lionel, beside him, speaks of brothers he means to protect whoever reigns (obs. 201).

Jam runs through all of it, as gift, currency and comedy:

- Shona's jar (obs. 018);
- the canteen fight (obs. 046);
- Owen's thanks (obs. 049);
- marmalade in coffee and raspberry jam in tea (obs. 081, 189);
- Nate's correction that the upper class do not put jam in tea (S25:E000255).

The jam jar of the prologue holds the women's coins, kept unspent through his rise (obs. 011, 031).

### 6.4 Teachers

The companion is, as much as anything, a book of teachers. Each has a different method.

**Rutherford** hits. He judged at the start that the boy grows by being beaten (obs. 048), and the run keeps the method: the smoke, the heel drops, and the daily brawls in his laboratory (obs. 088, 124, 161). He also discloses three things:

- His past. He was the Rock-Cavern Mage, captain of the knights' mage unit before the Magic Corps existed, with an earth Spirit-King he no longer troubles to summon (obs. 104, 105).
- His uniqueness. His smoke passes through every barrier, and no one else can enchant smoke (obs. 106).
- His judgment. Louis is a giant, but blind to others' goodwill: keen to malice and deaf to kindness, the kind who cannot rise and ends isolated (obs. 183).

That judgment, overheard while Louis clings to a wall, is the book's moral hinge. It turns a punishment comedy into recognition (obs. 184), and the apology with the hairpin follows from it (obs. 187).

**Majer** teaches by correction and by honesty about her limits:

- she lectures him for an hour before accepting his reports (obs. 109);
- she counts his formula errors by section (S19:E000456);
- she says she has left no great achievement (obs. 149).

Louis names her the Barrier Witch, and she laughs aloud for the first time (obs. 138). He credits her above his own name on the presentation's cover (obs. 151). He promises to take the matching name, the Barrier Mage, so that the neighbourhood will know who taught him (obs. 158). The epithet the prologue gives him as fact (obs. 006) thus carries, by the run's account, a teacher's lineage.

**MacLaggan** corrects with water and wit (obs. 069, 099). He turns out to be the Water-Biting Mage, a former water-dragon hunter whose water Spirit-King matches Rutherford's (obs. 193, 195).

**Woodman** is the reluctant physician. He treats Rosalie, does not report the trespass, and gives her the model of her later profession (obs. 118, 119, 199). At the festival he presents tool-based treatment of mana-caused illness (obs. 141). His place in a later controversy belongs to the numbered track; section 8.5 records what this book does not say.

The book defines the teachers by the student's misjudgment of them. Louis scoffs at the smoke and is told he cannot yet grasp it (obs. 099). He learns its uniqueness by being caged (obs. 106, 107). Later, watching Carla, he sees that the more he learns, the more he sees how far he is behind (obs. 157).

### 6.5 Rosalie: twenty mana and a chosen burden

Rosalie's story is told from both sides. It is the book's second line, and the afterword says the original short work was hers (obs. 217).

Her situation is plain arithmetic. Her mana is twenty against a middle-division mean of seventy and a beginner's minimum of fifty (obs. 113). She is the daughter of a Sage (obs. 114), first on paper and last in mana (S18:E000158). Her father is introduced through others' eyes in this order:

1. Owen's praise of the Waterworks Mage (obs. 133).
2. Her own deference to him: "do as you like" (obs. 153).
3. His confession to the Thunder Mage (obs. 154).
4. Her memory of his one dismissive sentence at her first lesson (obs. 204).
5. The outburst at Louis (obs. 210).

The book thus gives the reader the father's regret before it gives the daughter's wound. Neither learns the other's view on the page.

The chapters give her two crossings. She never crossed the rope into the dense forest for herself, out of fear of disgrace. She crosses it to stop Louis (obs. 115), and the cost is mana poisoning (obs. 117). Her second crossing is her decision to leave magic for medicine (obs. 197, 199). Lionel calls it a choice to bear her father's hopes (obs. 201). She frames it as made possible by Louis's demonstration that little mana is not everything (obs. 199).

Louis's response is the book's first view of him from inside a loss. He suspects that his encouragement was an imposition (S24:E000092). He feels lonely at not being able to share what she carries (obs. 200). He answers by declaring himself, and making the promise that becomes his career (obs. 205).

The numbered track already records Rosalie as a doctor and Louis's wife, and Birdland as her father and a former Sage (V11 obs. 145). The prequel supplies the origin of her medical path and the terms of the marriage. It is recorded as background, not as revision.

### 6.6 Barriers as weapons

The Barrier Mage of the prologue uses barriers more aggressively than anyone (obs. 007). The run shows how that style was built.

**The first barrier battles.** These turn on the rules: what counts as an attack, and what the barrier does to the body (obs. 074, 101). Louis wins by violence until the new barrier nullifies physical attacks (obs. 087, 102). Then he wins by loopholes: sand, holds, gags and a small shield used as a fist (obs. 123).

**Theory.** Each defeat becomes theory:

- the hemisphere's open floor (obs. 104);
- the nullification formula reconstructed from a kick (obs. 108);
- the battle barrier's need for dense land (obs. 111).

His festival study of the barrier's junctions (obs. 152) becomes the basis of the hall projection that later ends his loophole tactics (obs. 191).

**The mature forms.** These are:

- the barrier fist, accepted as a kind of magic sword (S23:E000113);
- the shield used to ram in flight (obs. 196);
- the reflecting barrier that turns back a Spirit-King's water (obs. 196, 197);
- the Mirror Prison, built from sealing and reflecting barriers, which wins him the Corps (obs. 208).

**The dragon.** The first dragon kills his fist method: a barrier opened inside a wound is too slow, and dragons need a wide, strong attack (obs. 213). The prologue's answer is the Mirror Prison with a Spirit-King inside it (obs. 007, 008). The epilogue thus leaves the second volume to explain the step from the reflecting barrier to the Spirit-King. That is left open in section 9.3.

### 6.7 The court at the edge of the school

The court enters the school through Lionel. The festival's "golden gorilla" is the first prince, incognito (obs. 142, 166). He keeps the manners of a royal: a sword laid down neatly, sugar added to match a guest (obs. 171; S25:E000267). He keeps the bluntness of a boy who would rather make the country good than be its king (obs. 201).

Nate Wall is Lionel's guard and is twenty-seven. He was sent by Queen Vilma, and he is dry, ironic and armed (obs. 173). He is the book's running commentary on Louis.

The succession appears only as background, in three places:

- Rosalie's account of Serendia and Minerva (obs. 169);
- Bradford's remark at the demolition site (obs. 209);
- the secret episode (obs. 215).

The book's own view of the second prince is limited to his age and frailty (obs. 168, 201) and his recent recovery (obs. 209). The numbered track's account of the second prince is recorded at V08 and V09. The prequel's characters know nothing of it, and this reading draws no new inference from the dates (section 10).

The secret episode gives Duke Clockford a scene in the prequel's time. He plans to place men of his own among Sages who are, as Adolf notes, politically neutral (obs. 215). Adolf addresses him with the honorific 閣下 (S28:E000050). The honorific belongs to the scene. This reading does not take it as identifying the patron whom the traitor of V04 and V05 called by that word (SW-C-0039).

The scene also gives the duke's personal name in the narrative. EXTRA09 had shown it only on a design card (EXTRA09 obs. 181). It places Emmanuel Darwin, a Sage in the numbered track, on the duke's list (obs. 215). That agrees with SW-C-0057 and is recorded as a strengthening.

### 6.8 Owen and Adolf

Owen and Adolf are drawn as the two ways of being Louis's classmate.

**Owen** is younger and a commoner. His father, a clerk, pays for his dream (obs. 050). He aims at the Corps because a Sage once saved him (obs. 078). He is the first to be helped by Louis's violence, and he repays it in marmalade and coffee (obs. 049, 081). In the tournament he hits Louis first and is reflected (obs. 193, 196). In chapter thirteen he tells Louis that a motive need not be pure, only the ability real (S25:E000042–E000046). The numbered track has Owen as a captain in the Corps under Louis; the prequel shows the friendship from its first day.

**Adolf** is noble, top in practice and close to Clockford's faction (obs. 070, 169). He moves from tripping Louis to having Terence steal his clothes. He burns a uniform (obs. 082, 145) and, in the secret episode, spreads a rumour (obs. 215). Each move is answered disproportionately: a cesspit for Terence (obs. 083), a tree and smoke for Adolf (obs. 159), a barrier fist in the tournament (obs. 196).

The numbered track's V11 reading presents Adolf's crimes as a malicious wish for Louis's attention (SW-C-0085). The prequel supplies the long history behind that wish: years of being outshone in the field he was born to lead (obs. 139). It ends with Clockford's promise of a Sage's seat (obs. 215). This is recorded as a strengthening.

### 6.9 What the prequel discloses

Measured against the numbered track through V12 and the two earlier companions, the prequel discloses the following. Each item is supplemental retrospection and does not revise a frozen reading.

- **Rutherford was the Rock-Cavern Mage** (obs. 104, 105). In V12 the epithet occurs only once, unattached to any person, within the passage of V12 obs. 131. There the old sword-saint, confused about time, recalls facing the Thunder Mage and the Rock-Cavern Mage's Spirit-King summons. The prequel attaches the epithet to Rutherford, captain of the knights' mage unit, with the earth Spirit-King.
- **MacLaggan's epithet and Spirit-King.** He is the Water-Biting Mage, with the water Spirit-King Lulucela (obs. 193, 195).
- **Carla's appointment.** She becomes a Sage after Louis graduates (obs. 215). The numbered track records her as a former Sage.
- **The Sages of that time.** Graham Sanders the Thunder Mage, the third Thorn Witch Sabrina Roseberg, the second Abyss Shaman Adeline Albright, Mary Harvey, Birdland Verde, Bradford Firestone and Carla Maxwell (obs. 215). None of these names conflicts with the numbered track's genealogy.
- **Origins.** Louis's brothel upbringing, his mother, Shona's jam and the jar (obs. 013, 017, 018, 031). Rosalie's mana and her medical turn (obs. 113, 199). Rutherford's recruitment of Louis (obs. 029).
- **The duke's plan for the Sages** and the source of the rumour (obs. 215).

What the prequel does not disclose is listed in section 8.5.

### 7.12 Form and evaluation

The book alternates between Louis's viewpoint and outside views: Rutherford's, the staffroom's, Adolf's, Rosalie's, Birdland's through Bradford, and Owen's. This lets comedy and judgment sit side by side.

The book repeats its patterns:

- a "foot slipped" and the same reply (S16:E000113, S16:E000122);
- "above" mouthed twice before a teacher's blow from above (obs. 069; S18:E000562);
- stories about a first and a later appearance of the same thing.

Its chapters end on epithets: Jam-Hunting Miller, the Bad Boy of Minerva, the Barrier Witch, the Barrier Mage.

## 8. Alternative readings, absence and contradiction

### 8.1 "A prequel adds nothing to the main story"

The book could be read as background only, since its events precede V01 and nothing in it changes a numbered outcome. Against this, it discloses:

- an identity the numbered track left unattached, the Rock-Cavern Mage (obs. 104, 105);
- the duke's plan to place men among the Sages, in a scene of his own (obs. 215);
- the source of Louis's ambition and of his marriage (obs. 210, 212).

It also revises the reading of an existing claim (section 9.1). The reading treats it as a full supplemental unit.

### 8.2 "Louis's ambition is a public vocation"

The prologue's people praise the country's guardian (obs. 010), and Louis's later office could suggest a calling. The run does not support this. Its narration states that he resolved to become a Sage only to marry Rosalie (obs. 212), and the prologue's vow follows his count of his savings (obs. 011, 012). The book's view is comic and personal, and the reading records it so (SW-C-0095).

### 8.3 Normative evaluation: the delinquent's violence

**Criterion:** whether the book condemns Louis's violence.

The book shows punishment: smoke, blows, toilet duty, a hundred pages (obs. 088, 185). It also shows teachers who admire his work despite his conduct (obs. 075, 090). Its comedy spares no victim:

- Terence in the cesspit (obs. 083);
- Adolf hung in smoke (obs. 159);
- Lionel's bloody nose (obs. 166).

The one judgment it gives weight to concerns goodwill, not violence (obs. 183). Graduation is presented as a comic transformation, not a reform (obs. 212). The judgment is mixed and is recorded as such.

### 8.4 Normative evaluation: Birdland

**Criterion:** whether the book presents the father as wronging his daughter.

It gives his regret first (obs. 154) and her memory of one cold sentence later (obs. 204). It gives his outburst as a "joke-like" loss of temper (S25:E000204–E000205). It does not reconcile them on the page. The reading records an unresolved relation.

### 8.5 Meaningful absences

The book does not show or tell:

- how Louis becomes a Sage, or whether Birdland consents;
- Lynn, whom the numbered track records as his contracted spirit;
- how he learns Spirit-King summoning, which the prologue shows and the epilogue wants;
- Carla's family, beyond her being neither noble nor of a magic house (obs. 155);
- who the "first" Bad Boy's successor is, though the word implies one (obs. 212);
- any link between the prequel's Woodman and the physicians'-association authority of IX -extra- (SW-C-0076). The book gives a resident physician with medical and magical knowledge (obs. 118). It does not connect him to Hilda or to any later plan.

It also does not show anything that identifies the patron called 閣下 in the numbered track. The secret episode uses the honorific for a duke in its own scene (obs. 215). The reading does not extend it.

### 8.6 The afterword, the jacket and the character list

**The afterword** gives the book's origin: a short work, narrated almost wholly by Rosalie, posted before the web Silent Witch and heavily revised (obs. 217). It also says that the two-volume plan preserved the school years. It thanks the illustrator, and jokes about how carelessly characters treat their staffs, Louis abandoning his and Monica using hers as a laundry pole. It announces numbered volume VII for February 2024 and the lower volume for spring 2024 (obs. 217).

**The jacket blurb** calls Louis the Sage of Monica's generation (obs. 218). The numbered track agrees, and the blurb adds no narrative fact.

**The character list** is described in section 7.8.

All of this is paratext. The related web work and the short work it names stay outside the run.

### 8.7 Apparent contradictions

- **Rutherford's epithet.** He is the Purple Smoke Mage throughout the numbered track and in the prequel's present, and the Rock-Cavern Mage in his knightly past (obs. 023, 105). This is a change of name, not a contradiction.
- **The Queen's key.** The ledger holds two keys for Lionel's mother, sw.wilma and sw.vilma, both given as ヴィルマ. The prequel's ヴィルマ妃 (obs. 173) fits both. The duplication is recorded, not merged.
- **Mana figures.** Louis's mana is given as about 100 at entry and 110 a few months later (obs. 073, 101). This fits growth with use (obs. 073) and is not a contradiction.
