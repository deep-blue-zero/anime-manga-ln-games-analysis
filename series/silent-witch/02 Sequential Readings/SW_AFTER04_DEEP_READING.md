## 2. Prior-volume developments

*Prepared 2026-10-14 before any narrative member of IV -after- was rendered for reading. Sources: the closed V01 to V04 readings, their exit freezes and the master ledger only.*

Monica Everett, the Silent Witch, is a little over two months into a secret mission at Serendia Academy. She attends as Monica Norton, a second-year student and the student council's treasurer. Her task, pressed on her by Louis Miller, the Barrier Mage, is to guard the second prince, Felix Arc Ridill, without his knowledge. Isabelle Norton covers her by playing her tormentor. Her familiar Nero and Louis's wind spirit Lynn help her. The school festival and its ball are now over.

Her past is now nearly complete in outline. Her father, the scholar Venedict Rain, was executed as a heretic when she was ten. Her uncle then took her in for about a year, beat and starved her, and she escaped into her father's formulas until she lost language and could no longer see people as people. Her father's former assistant, Hilda Everett of the Royal Magic Research Institute, rescued and adopted her, and she recovered speech before entering Minerva. There Bernie Jones befriended her while wanting her dependent and, as he now says, wanting a rival. He has since left Minerva as heir to the Amberd county and ended the friendship, with an offer of future help.

At the festival, Monica recovered a stolen Albright curse tool with the help of Isabelle's family of performers and of Ray Albright, the Abyss Curse Mage. She protected the founding play, and defeated the imperial agents Yuan and Heidi when they impersonated Cyril Ashley. She then lost to them by poison, and they escaped. They had sought a trace on Felix linked to a traitor named Arthur, and the capture of the Silent Witch, for a master. Yuan is under Ray's curse for at least a month. Glen Dudley, unaware that he served as a decoy, is now publicly Louis's disciple. Eliane Hyatt, Duke Leinbourg's daughter and the bride the duke prefers for Felix, sabotaged the play while partly under a curse, and was not exposed.

Cyril gave Monica a white rose that she believes is a charm. He danced with her, carried her when she collapsed, and noticed the magical ice and her small, pen-callused hand. His mother, Myra, came to the festival and has resolved to invite him home. Felix's viewpoint states an attachment to Monica herself, with jealousy, while he still does not connect her with the Silent Witch. He entrusted his dead friend's wish to her and spoke of being remembered like a star-hero. Bridget means to take back "her prince". Louis holds an unstated hypothesis about Felix. A traitor curse-caster serving a patron called 閣下 has entered Duke Leinbourg's household, with the craftsman Bartholomeus. Monica has chosen to remain Monica Norton until Felix graduates, knowing that it deceives her friends, and expects to return to her hut.

## 3. Current-unit story synopsis

The companion is built as a casebook: a prologue, four cases each followed by an interlude, an epilogue and a secret episode.

**The prologue** is set about a year before Monica's mission, in summer. Louis Miller hauls Monica, who has been a Sage for about half a year, and the Abyss Curse Mage Ray Albright on a cart to the Heims-Naria library. The library holds more than four hundred grimoires and curse books, which need resealing and repair. He wants the work finished in a day because he has a date with his fiancée the next day.

Ray rebinds the curse primer he wrote himself in a pink cover. He retitles it 初めてのおまじない so that girls will pick it up, and boasts of curses that hole the socks of people he dislikes and harass enemies in their dreams.

Louis remarks that charms were popular in his Minerva days: a flower trinket wetted with dew brings luck, and a love letter in blue ink brings love. Monica dismisses both as unreasoned and thinks charms will never be anything to do with her. She then finds a hidden room behind a shelf, where an illegally acquired grimoire by the first Thorn Witch has grown a mass of mana-swollen vines. Louis, Monica and Ray subdue it together, Ray by cursing the vines that had seized him in what he took for love.

They work through the night. In the morning Louis's wind spirit, Lynn, has left a note saying she is on holiday. Louis flies to the capital on his own mana, crashes into the fountain at the meeting place, and is nursed by his fiancée.

**The first interlude** follows that day. The fiancée, Rosalie, is a doctor and treats him as a patient on his birthday. In her hair is a flower hairpin that Louis, scorning charms, gave her as a student after searching for dew.

**Case I** opens two days after the school festival. A closed library's books, the Heims-Naria donation, are coming to Serendia. Felix finds the Silent Witch's paper on chant-shortening among them. Because his grandfather checks his borrowing records and magic is forbidden to him, he asks Monica, as his fellow "delinquent", to keep watch while he reads it standing at the shelf.

Claudia, who sees through the pretext and ignores Felix as if he were absent, drags Monica off to find a book. Felix finds the room crowded: the magic-battle club head Byron Garrett has borrowed the paper to beat Cyril, and thirteen students are waiting for it. Felix is visibly crestfallen. He thanks Monica for being a friend who listens to what he loves, and she promises to join his next scheme.

Nero and Lynn, playing detective after a popular mystery novel, have deduced from Monica's drying flowers and a plant book that she is making poison to kill Louis. In fact she is drying the white rose from the festival into a keepsake in a jar, which she calls a charm that makes her a little stronger. She puts it in a locked drawer of treasures.

**The second interlude** returns to Byron at fourteen. He took the newly enrolled Cyril for a girl and tried to help. Cyril, isolated as the Highorn heir by adoption, was glad to be spoken to.

**Case II.** The kitchen accuses Glen of stealing twenty pieces of meat through a high window, since he is the school's only flier. Cyril takes up his defence after Glen swears innocence before the prince. Glen compares Cyril's insistence on patient effort with his master's maxim that money and violence solve most problems.

Monica realizes that Nero ate the meat and tries to hide his traces. On the search Glen asks Cyril why he studies magic. Cyril answers that it is to serve his father, and tells Glen to take pride in a flight he earned through many scrapes; both Glen and Monica take this to heart.

Cyril sprains an ankle after something strikes his foot. They find a lost three-year-old girl, whom Glen calms. In the council room Felix entertains her with a handkerchief rabbit. A white rabbit appears and Cyril, delighted, strokes it, then lets it go when Glen proposes to butcher it.

MacLaggan arrives for the girl, his granddaughter Lucile. The rabbit is her family's mid-level earth spirit, Istreia, which had eaten Glen's ham. Monica and Glen catch it by baiting it with the council's heating tool. While Monica and Glen are out catching the spirit, MacLaggan starts to ask Cyril whether Glen can safely be left alone, mentioning Minerva, and Glen's return cuts him off. The spirit, recognizing the strongest person present, attacks Monica.

**The third interlude** returns to Glen at fifteen, on a fire-dragon hunt as Louis's apprentice. Louis tells him to acquire strength, because sudden unreason leaves no room for negotiation.

**Case III** covers two rainy days of the school bazaar. Benjamin Moulding, struck down after Claudia dismisses his serenade, needs a new love or a new fascination before the winter concert. Elliott and Monica are conscripted to help him at the bazaar.

Monica finds a lily-of-the-valley handkerchief with stray blue thread on its reverse and returns it to Byron, hiding the embroidery from Cyril. Byron insists on a formal duel with Cyril. Benjamin buys blue ink to write to Claudia.

Elliott tells Benjamin why he hates rain: his father slapped him for blaming the weather for his violin's muffled tone. He also recalls an uncle who killed himself after his commoner wife betrayed him, and forbids Benjamin to ask about a second turning point.

Benjamin hears a rumour of a maid's ghost at the abandoned old dormitory and drags his companions there at dusk. Monica finds that the ghost is Lynn, using the high-mana building as a base. The fire-lights are Byron practising remote fire spells in the forbidden garden. Monica silently stops one of his spells from exploding, warns him, and learns that he duels to win back a fiancée who named Cyril as her type. She guesses that the blue thread under the embroidered leaves hides a message, and keeps silent.

Benjamin finishes a new piece around Elliott's scream at a glimpse of the maid. Neil tells him he will accept a duel over Claudia at any time. Benjamin turns to Bridget, whom he has asked about ten times. Elliott concludes that whoever crosses the walls of rank always makes someone unhappy.

**The fourth interlude** returns to Elliott at seven, the second prince's playmate at Duke Clockford's estate. There a small, timid Felix, poor at lessons and games and kind above all, asks to play chess instead of violin because Elliott hates the rain. An attentive page boy serves the tea.

**Case IV** begins two weeks after the festival. Girls swarm the council room for Felix's hair, for a charm said to bring dreams of the one you love. A grimoire page in the library has been torn.

Isabelle reports that the charm uses a secret symbol. Monica recognizes a likeness to Ray's curse seals and writes to Ray. At the embroidery club, Isabelle's staged cruelty sends Monica to the vice-head Sheila, who teaches her to use a thimble and mentions that the charm came from a donated book. Glen's holed sock reminds Monica of Ray's pink-covered primer.

Monica confirms that the book has been shelved as a general book and checks Felix for curse marks with a story about an illness. Felix turns the questions back on her, says she is the one short of breath, and feeds her a cake. Lynn brings Ray, abducted, and he explains that the original curse uses the caster's own hair to intrude on the target's dreams. Monica sees that the inverted charm involves royalty in a curse ritual, which is a capital matter, and that the torn page supplied mana paper.

Isabelle, in a private tea, makes the embroidery-club head Cecily confess that she adapted the curse for her cousin Wanda, who later tore the grimoire. Isabelle then offers a quiet cover story. Monica listens behind a curtain.

A week later the charm has faded. The book has gone back to the Albrights, and Ray has been scolded by his predecessor. Isabelle's attempt to popularize a Kerbeck-published book about the Silent Witch has failed, and Felix reads that book raptly.

On Monica's birthday Lana gives her a berry cake and a violet-embroidered pouch she made at the club sessions. Isabelle gives her a quill. A basket of the first sweets Felix ever gave her waits at her ladder with a starred card from her "fellow delinquent".

**The fifth interlude** returns to the Norton family two months after the black dragon. They misread Louis's request to bully Monica as an instruction to act villains, and debate how to greet her.

**The epilogue** takes place on a winter holiday. Cyril beats Byron in the duel. Monica overhears Byron apologize to his fiancée, who is Sheila. Sheila tells Monica that her verbal tic comes from never finding the right words: she named Cyril only from embarrassment, and the blue thread says "I love you".

Monica decodes Byron's season's card for her: a hand-drawn yellow rose and an orange ribbon reproduce the festival corsage in his colours. Sheila, though Monica does not know what the corsage means, looks forward to the graduation dance. At home Monica thanks Nero and writes her first season's card.

**The secret episode** shows its arrival. Hilda Everett, encasing a burning ironing board in ice, receives the card with Nero's paw print, hopes Monica will come home for the solstice, and goes out to buy a frame.

## 6. Close reading

### 6.1 Placement, form and the casebook

The companion's own markers place its four cases in a run of about three and a half weeks:

- **Case I** opens on the second night of the two-day cleanup after the festival (obs. 029).
- **Case II** follows the next day.
- **Case III** comes about a week later, over the two bazaar days.
- **Case IV** opens two weeks after the festival and ends on Monica's birthday, a week after Isabelle's tea (obs. 124, 148).
- **The epilogue** takes place on a winter holiday after the bazaar week, and the secret episode shows the epilogue's card arriving.

Late autumn gives way to winter: the rain of Case III "brought winter" (S21:E001330). Every case is therefore inside the interval that V04's afterword and the publisher assign, and the afterword calls the book a "4.5" centred on episodes between V04 and V05 (obs. 165).

Five scenes lie outside that interval, all earlier:

- the prologue and the first interlude, about a year before the mission (obs. 001);
- Byron's and Glen's interludes, in their fourteenth and fifteenth years (obs. 068, 098);
- Elliott's interlude, at seven (obs. 122);
- the Nortons' interlude, two months after the black dragon (obs. 150).

None presupposes a mainline event absent from V01 to V04. Section 10 records them as supplemental retrospection.

The book's form is a casebook in the literal sense. The frontispiece spread is laid out as an album of four labelled cases (obs. 163). Each case names a pair, and each case has a detective:

- Nero and Lynn, reading a popular mystery, seek the "irrational" in Monica's room (S15:E000218);
- Cyril defends Glen with an investigator's method;
- Monica, unasked, reads a handkerchief's reverse and a stranger's flashes of fire;
- Isabelle solves the charm by social inference (obs. 038, 074, 105, 115, 140).

The heading images carry the conceit, ending with Monica in a deerstalker (section 5.2). The form fits the volume's subject: the cases are small, domestic and resolved; no one dies; nothing of the main plot advances.

The interludes do something the cases do not. Each one returns to the past of the case's secondary figure:

- Byron's first meeting with Cyril;
- Glen's first dragon;
- Elliott's childhood with Felix;
- the Nortons' decision to play villains.

They supply the reasons behind present conduct that the cases show only from outside (obs. 068, 099, 122, 151).

### 6.2 Charms: what the book argues about おまじない

The prologue sets out a theory and Monica rejects it. Louis lists the dew-on-a-flower luck charm and the blue-ink love letter, and explains that both work by raising the user's self-esteem through something special: 「つまりは気分です、気分」 (S12:E000437) (obs. 014). Monica answers as a mage:

- pure water would take an enchantment better than dew;
- ordinary blue ink could do nothing without a mind-interference formula.

She concludes that charms will never concern her (obs. 015). The first interlude shows that Louis, who calls it all mood, searched for dew as a student to make Rosalie's hairpin (obs. 028).

The rest of the book tests the theory. The Case I mystery turns out to be a charm:

- Monica preserves Cyril's rose with "the knowledge and skill of a Sage";
- she calls the jar 「強くなれる、おまじないなの」 (S15:E001162) (obs. 066), a charm that makes her a little stronger.

The rose was given as a charm against embarrassment (V04 obs. 032). Monica now treats it as one, on her own account, which is the self-esteem theory she dismissed (obs. 015, 066).

The blue-ink charm returns twice:

- Benjamin buys ink for a letter Claudia will never open (obs. 107);
- Sheila hides a confession in blue thread under embroidered leaves (obs. 156).

Monica, who half remembers the charm, reads the thread and decides not to reveal it, because that would be 「人の気持ちを無理やり暴く」 (S21:E001244; obs. 118). The epilogue shows her method changing. Told the truth by Sheila herself, she decodes Byron's card and gives Sheila "a little courage" (obs. 158). She understands the corsage as a charm that lends courage from the giver; she still does not know that it reserves a dance (obs. 159).

Case IV supplies the inverse: a charm that is really a curse (obs. 129, 139). Ray's primer, re-covered in pink so that girls would like curses (obs. 012), ends on a general shelf. Its dream-intrusion curse is turned into a charm for meeting the beloved in dreams. Ray's account of the original makes the inversion exact:

- the curse uses the caster's own hair to harass the target;
- the charm uses the beloved's hair under one's own pillow, so the user, in effect, curses herself (obs. 139).

Whether it works does not matter. Involving royalty in any curse ritual is a capital matter, and the danger is legal, not magical (obs. 139).

The two strands meet in the prologue's wish that curses might be loved as charms are (S12:E000405). In this book they very nearly were.

### 6.3 Monica's firsts and the drawer of treasures

The companion records a set of firsts, not a development of plot:

- the first keepsake made for its own sake;
- the first birthday celebration since Minerva;
- the first gift she asks for aloud;
- the first season's card;
- the first thanks spoken to Nero (obs. 066, 146, 147, 160).

Two details show the change in practice:

- She draws real water for the rose, though she makes her own coffee with magic water (obs. 039).
- She hung Nero's earlier gift of flowers by the door as insect repellent (obs. 039).

The locked drawer is the volume's inventory of attachments: her father's coffee pot and book, Lana's comb and letter, Felix's peridot, Casey's handkerchief (obs. 067). The book adds the rose, then the violet pouch, Isabelle's quill and Felix's starred card (obs. 149). Every item comes from a person except her father's things, and every person is one she is deceiving or will leave.

The last birthday anyone celebrated for her was Hilda's. Hilda baked a cake half raw and half burnt and dug out the small edible middle for her plate (obs. 146). Lana's family custom of a berry cake, her clumsy first slice and her choice of the neat slice for Monica repeat the gesture (obs. 146).

Lana's birthday falls after the winter break. The narration marks with emphasis that Monica can ［まだ］ ("still") be at school to celebrate it (obs. 148). The planned departure at Felix's graduation, chosen in V04 (V04 obs. 098), is in the background of each gift.

Monica's speech advances in small, marked steps:

- she introduces herself to Byron without stumbling (S21:E000698–E000699);
- she volunteers to guide him (obs. 109);
- she three times claims her standing as a council officer (S15:E000969; obs. 077; S21:E001207);
- she says 「……欲しい」 (S24:E001345) for the pouch "unusually clearly" (obs. 147).

She also understands something new about speech. Watching Sheila, she reflects that even a thank-you needs courage, and that she knows the joy of having goodwill received (obs. 157).

Monica turns seventeen; she tells Elliott the age a month ahead (obs. 083). Her birthday is the first day of the first week of Shelgria (obs. 102). Mary Harvey's popular astrology describes her as kind, easily swept along and burdened with hard work, which she finds alarmingly accurate (obs. 102).

The question of why she studies magic receives its first plain answer. She did not choose it:

- she wanted only not to burden her adoptive mother;
- she became a Sage by drifting (obs. 079).

Cyril's remark that earned skill is something to be proud of makes her wish she could be proud of something. She is surprised to find herself wishing it, as she would not have in the hut (obs. 079).

### 6.4 Concealment and capability

The cover holds through a set of small lies and silences:

- Monica tells Felix she knows Heims-Naria only by name (obs. 044).
- She attributes her knowledge of an expensive magic tool to having "seen one once"; Cyril supplies the Kerbeck explanation himself (obs. 072).
- She invents a contagious illness to inspect Felix's skin for curse marks (obs. 137).
- She lies to protect Byron's forbidden training (obs. 119).
- She cannot report the mis-shelved curse primer without explaining how she knows what it is (obs. 136).

The narrator notes twice, in Case I, that the mission itself has been forgotten while she plays lookout and decoy (obs. 053). In Case III she thinks of Felix's safety at the bazaar in professional terms (obs. 103).

Her power appears only where no one can see it:

- she raises a decoy light to send Elliott and Benjamin away (obs. 114);
- she seals Byron's swelling fireball without his knowing it (obs. 116);
- she stops an earth spirit's sandstorm with a wind no one notices;
- she warns the spirit with a finger to her lips (obs. 092).

The spirit's verdict that this girl is the strongest person present is shown to the reader and misread by everyone else. Its last attack looks like the strongest prey falling on the weakest (obs. 095).

The same face recurs: the expressionless concentration that came, in V04, with numbers. Here it appears:

- over the heating tool (obs. 072);
- in diagnosing Byron's faulty clauses (obs. 041);
- in counting the intervals between the fire-lights (obs. 115).

The calculations in the prologue show the technique working without the dissociation that V04 attached to it:

- Monica computes a root's strength from the mana in its vines and calibrates a blade to cut it without destroying what lies beneath (obs. 020);
- the same chapter notes that confusion leaves her unable to build a formula at all (obs. 017).

### 6.5 Felix in daylight

The Ike persona of V03 now appears in daylight:

- Felix asks for help as the 「不良仲間の僕に」 (S15:E000399) friend, with an emphasis mark on the pronoun, and Monica silently mouths Ike (obs. 043);
- he calls her his fellow delinquent to Wildianu (obs. 037);
- his birthday card is addressed to her as one (obs. 149).

The scheme he proposes is "absurd", in his word: he wants to read a paper, standing at a shelf, with his guard keeping watch. It rests on three plain facts:

- his grandfather checks his borrowing records (obs. 035);
- magic study is forbidden to him;
- a spirit barrier keeps Wildianu from the grimoire room (obs. 036).

The narration's sentence is that under Duke Clockford's power he has no freedom even in what he reads (obs. 035). Monica now infers, from the envy in his voice at Cyril's spells, that he wants to study magic, and wonders why it is forbidden (obs. 042). The text gives no reason.

His admiration for the Silent Witch is undiminished and comic:

- the rapture over the donation list (obs. 034);
- the plan to learn the paper's return date (S15:E000837);
- the gloom at thirteen readers waiting (obs. 059);
- the delight in the Kerbeck book (obs. 144).

Under this lies a sentence of loneliness: a friend who will listen to what he loves is happiness, and for a ghost who exists nowhere that is 「過ぎた贅沢だ」 (S15:E001001) (obs. 062). Monica, for whom his favourite subject is a stomach-ache, chooses to promise help with his next scheme (obs. 063). The character card later states that he is "very happy" to have someone to talk to secretly about what he loves (obs. 164).

His attention to Monica continues, without explanation:

- he notices her riding (obs. 040);
- he takes her questions about his symptoms and turns them back on her: she is the one out of breath (obs. 137);
- he feeds her a cake and offers to nurse her (obs. 138).

On hearing what she will make from the plant book, he says 「……ずるいなぁ」 (S15:E001031), and adds that he did not mean her (obs. 064). The narration does not say whom he meant or what she had told him. Earlier, V04 recorded his jealousy that she did not wear his pendant (V04 obs. 114).

Three moments leave his sources unstated:

- he knew her birthday (obs. 145, 149);
- he wishes her a good day on it (obs. 145);
- he leaves the sweets he first gave her at the school (obs. 149).

The public prince is shown working:

- he appraises his officers;
- he plans Neil's succession as president (obs. 030);
- he wants Isabelle's friendship as a neutral power and Kerbeck's weight against the treatment of Monica (obs. 032);
- he tolerates Claudia's open contempt because the 〈識者の家系〉 is not a house to make an enemy of (obs. 057).

Claudia never looks at him and enjoys his rare visible disappointment (obs. 055, 061). With the lost child he is gentle and inventive. He answers Bridget's remark that she never knew this of him with a formula about royal duty, and she replies coolly (obs. 087).

### 6.6 Cyril, Glen and the value of effort

Case II and the epilogue make a single argument about effort. It is conducted through three young men who misread one another.

**Byron.** He counts Cyril as a rival and "first-love thief". He has lost twenty-seven times this year and cannot help praising Cyril's rise from commoner to vice-president (obs. 049). The interlude shows the origin: at fourteen he took the new pupil for a girl (obs. 068). The same scene, from Cyril's side, shows an isolated adopted heir, taunted by Elliott, hoping for a friend (obs. 069). Cyril's sincerity carries into the present: he accepts Byron's formal duel because it is asked of him in earnest (obs. 109), and wins it (obs. 154).

**Glen.** Glen's case rests on his master's maxim that money and violence solve most difficulties, which the third interlude shows Louis delivering over a dead fire dragon (obs. 099). Glen compares it with Cyril's insistence on patient effort and prefers the latter (obs. 075). He applies it when he stops an indoor fireball with Cyril's words (obs. 090). His account of himself matches Monica's: he became an apprentice "without understanding why" (obs. 079), and in the interlude he was "swept along" into Louis's tutelage (obs. 099). He has few good memories of magic battles (obs. 078). MacLaggan begins to say something about him and Minerva and is cut off (obs. 094); the text does not resume it.

**Cyril.** Cyril answers Glen's question with a single purpose: to be of use to his father (obs. 079). His argument that skill earned through scrapes deserves pride is addressed to Glen, and is heard by Monica too (obs. 079). His conduct is consistent:

- he defends Glen from the kitchen's suspicion once Glen swears before the prince (obs. 074);
- he defends his juniors from Elliott's class sneer (obs. 093);
- he hands the capture of the spirit to them with a hard-won 「……頼む」 (S18:E000996).

His body and tenderness are given in comic detail:

- the cold of his over-absorption, and his care not to chill others as winter comes (obs. 070);
- his delight in a rabbit, his release of it when Glen proposes to butcher it, and a "chance" pocketful of dried fish for the backyard black cat (obs. 097);
- the character card adds that animals avoid him because of his mana (obs. 164).

The dried fish is meant for a black cat that usually lounges in the backyard (S18:E001373); the text does not say which cat.

MacLaggan's closing words frame the case: he is glad Glen has found good friends and a senior, and young people growing up healthily is what matters (obs. 096).

### 6.7 Elliott and the walls of rank

Case III gives Elliott his first sustained interior. He has a rule:

- commoners should stay commoners and nobles nobles;
- whoever crosses the walls of rank makes himself or someone else unhappy (obs. 112).

He traces the rule to an uncle. That uncle had welcomed people of every rank, then killed himself after his commoner wife betrayed him, on a rainy day whose smell Elliott still remembers (obs. 112). His dislike of rain has a second source. His father slapped him for blaming the weather for his violin's muffled tone (obs. 110), and Elliott thinks this father far more frightening than Cyril.

Benjamin names two turning points: the uncle's death, and a time just before Elliott was ten, before he entered Serendia. Elliott forbids him to ask about the second, privately "for your sake", as a burden he will not share (obs. 112). The text withholds what it was.

The rule meets contrary evidence in the same scene, from Benjamin, an upstart's son who is Elliott's friend:

- Elliott came to the bazaar for him;
- he calls him a friend (obs. 111).

Elliott cannot say any of his reasons aloud (S21:E000861–E000863). He keeps the rule anyway. At the case's end he sees that Bridget is in love and decides that her love must cross a wall of rank, and calls it "a tragedy" (obs. 121). The object and the wall are unstated. The reading records only Elliott's inference.

The fourth interlude adds two things (obs. 122):

- Elliott was the second prince's playmate;
- his father is Count Dazvie.

In the dormitory his old nurse's son now wakes him, using his mother's words (S21:E000051–E000056); the card adds that he once called Cyril "ばあや" half asleep (obs. 164). In the present he sneers at commoners and at Glen (obs. 082). Yet he explained the flower customs to Monica in V04, applauds Bridget here (obs. 125), and stands by a slumping friend.

### 6.8 Isabelle's villainy as a method

The fifth interlude shows where the villainy came from. Louis asked Count Kerbeck to have the Silent Witch "bullied a little" to help the cover. The family council took this as an instruction to perform villains, and Isabelle has studied the role since (obs. 151). Louis's offhand instruction, misread with enthusiasm, joins V04's misread gifts (SW-C-0036).

The family's affection is unguarded:

- the Count would greet the Witch as a sister;
- the children want to call her elder sister;
- the evening ends with a debate about the proper villain's laugh (obs. 152).

Isabelle's dream returns to the scene that made her a devotee: the black dragon's wyverns over Kerbeck, struck down by a Silent Witch who also lays their corpses away from people (obs. 150).

Case IV shows the role turned into a method. Isabelle:

- knows the family, alliances and temper of every girl in the embroidery club (obs. 131);
- stages Monica's humiliation so that the kind vice-head will take her in;
- solves the charm by joining small facts: who reads, who loves the prince, who sought paper at the bazaar (obs. 140).

Her interrogation follows a technique she names:

1. withhold information and seem to know everything;
2. frighten;
3. then offer rescue (obs. 142).

Cecily ends by seeing her as a saviour (S24:E001152). The kindness inside the method is real: the cousins are spared execution or confinement, and the curse is quietly undone (obs. 141).

The narration calls both of Isabelle's faces true, the girl who fusses over Monica's pallor and the countess's daughter (S24:E000952). Her one failure is the attempt to popularize the Kerbeck book about the Silent Witch (obs. 143).

### 6.9 Louis, Ray and the Sages at work

The prologue is the first sustained view of the Sages working together. It is set at a time when Monica had barely spoken to any of them but Louis (obs. 006). Louis:

- drags her and Ray on a cart (obs. 003);
- exploits her chantless magic to save his throat (obs. 007);
- ranks a date above everything (obs. 008).

He also:

- praises her ability without irony (obs. 010);
- flies after her into the pit and directs the fight with precision (obs. 018, 020);
- endangers his life flying to the capital for the woman he loves (obs. 023, 024).

The first interlude shows him tender and outmanoeuvred by Rosalie, a doctor who treats him as a patient (obs. 025–027). The card calls her his wife and says she knows her taste in men is bad (obs. 164). The book's Louis therefore holds three things at once:

- the decoy-user and blame-taker of V04;
- the dragon-slayer who teaches that strength is the only answer to sudden unreason (obs. 099);
- a man who searched for dew.

The narration calls that teaching the speech of someone who has suffered and thrown off much unreason (S19:E000121).

Ray is introduced in daylight. He:

- loves only shade;
- rebinds his primer in pink so girls will like curses (obs. 012);
- proposes curses that make holes in socks or harass people in dreams, because curses exist for what one cannot say to someone's face (obs. 013);
- falls in love with a vine that is eating him and then curses it (obs. 019).

He is the one who speaks to Monica first (obs. 011). In Case IV he answers her letter after Lynn abducts him. He is delighted that a girl read his book and horrified at what it has caused (S24:E000866–E000870). He is scolded by his predecessor (S24:E001174).

The world records that the prologue adds (obs. 009, 021):

- grimoires against magic books;
- the first Thorn Witch's grimoire;
- the Rosebarg seat among the Sages.

### 6.10 The cluster around Felix

The fourth interlude extends SW-C-0022 without explaining it. It records the following:

- At seven, Elliott was chosen as the playmate of the second prince, Felix Arc Ridill.
- He visited Duke Clockford's house, where Felix was recuperating with his grandfather, and taught him violin and chess (obs. 122).
- The child is small for his age, quiet and timid.
- He is poor at lessons and games, and Elliott beats him at chess without a queen.
- An attentive page boy serves the tea and pretends not to hear a secret about learning to climb trees.
- The narration, focalized through Elliott, calls this child the kindest prince of all (obs. 123).

The present Felix differs from this child in several ways:

- he reads fast and memorizes;
- he is the strongest candidate for the throne;
- he wins Byron's admiration and Bridget's attention.

V03 recorded Elliott's own memory of a child Felix who loved astronomy and a page boy in a dream; V04 recorded Felix's statement that a friend taught him the stars (SW-C-0022). The character card adds that Wildianu can take the form of a young male attendant (obs. 164). The reading registers all of these as details. It registers no identification, connection or hypothesis among them, and it does not use the paratext card to read the interlude.

### 6.11 Chronology and apparent contradictions

Three internal dating formulations do not agree:

| Item | Formulations | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| When the Heims-Naria job took place | Prologue: about a year before the mission (obs. 001). Case IV: Monica thinks of it as two years ago (obs. 133). | Unresolved chronology; recorded, not reconciled. |
| How long Monica had been a Sage | Prologue: nearly half a year (obs. 002, 006). Case I: "when she had just become a Sage" (S15:E000411). | Compatible as loose phrasing; recorded. |
| When the black dragon was repelled | Case I public account: about half a year ago (obs. 058). Fifth interlude: two months before an autumn enrolment (obs. 150). | Compatible: half a year before late autumn is roughly spring. |

Monica's age is fixed for the first time: she turns seventeen on the first day of the first week of Shelgria (obs. 083, 102).

### 6.12 The visual channel and paratext

The colour frontispieces read as follows (obs. 163):

- The first frames the prologue as a Sage's duty and shows Louis, Monica and Ray at the grimoires.
- The second is an album of the four cases with labelled pairs.
- The third prints Monica's line about the west garden over her night run with a lantern. It discloses, before the case, that Monica will go to stop something there.

The narrative illustrations stay with comedy and with small, kept objects:

- the rose in its jar;
- the rabbit's bite;
- the pouch;
- the card held up before Byron and Sheila.

The character reference adds statements not in the narrative (section 7.8). The afterword confirms the book's placement and its "4.5" status, and announces V05 as the winter break arc (obs. 165). The jacket summarizes the cases (obs. 166). All of these are paratext and are kept apart from narrative evidence.

## 7. Character, directional relationship, world and formal development

Every ledger record below cites the companion's witness `SW-LNJP-AFTER04`. A record from a retrospective scene says so.

### 7.1 Monica Everett

The companion shows ordinary time rather than plot. Its developments for Monica:

- She keeps the first keepsake she has ever made and calls it a charm (obs. 066).
- She receives her first birthday celebrations since Minerva and asks plainly for a gift (obs. 146, 147).
- She writes her first season's card, to Hilda (obs. 160, 162).
- She thanks Nero (obs. 160).
- She claims her standing as a council officer and volunteers (obs. 077, 109).
- She says she became a Sage by drifting, and that she would like to be proud of something (obs. 079).
- She reads other people's hidden feelings and refrains from exposing them, until invited (obs. 118, 157, 158).
- She protects her cover with small lies (obs. 044, 072, 119, 137).
- She uses power only unseen (obs. 092, 114, 116).
- She turns seventeen (obs. 083).

The retrospective prologue shows her half a year into the post of Sage, still frightened of strangers and of the other Sages (obs. 004, 006, 011). Ledger: SW-S-0085 to SW-S-0088.

### 7.2 Felix Arc Ridill

Felix appears as follows:

- as Ike in daylight (obs. 043);
- as a fan denied his reading by his grandfather's surveillance (obs. 035, 059);
- as a lonely "ghost" glad of a listener (obs. 062);
- as an attentive, teasing presence (obs. 137, 138);
- as a working prince (obs. 030, 032, 057, 087).

He knew Monica's birthday and left her the first sweets he gave her (obs. 149). The text withholds his source for the date and the addressee of his 「……ずるいなぁ」 (S15:E001031) (obs. 064).

The fourth interlude adds the childhood scene to the cluster (section 6.10). Ledger: SW-S-0089 to SW-S-0090.

### 7.3 Cyril Ashley

Cyril appears as follows:

- the defender of a suspected junior (obs. 074);
- the teacher of earned pride (obs. 079);
- the adopted heir who hoped for a friend at fourteen (obs. 069);

He stays the comic victim of his own tenderness toward animals (obs. 097). He serves Felix's tea as an honour and worries about chilling others (obs. 070, 071). Ledger: SW-S-0091.

## 8. Alternative readings, absence and contradiction

### 8.1 "A companion of comic episodes adds nothing"

The book has no antagonist, advances no mystery of the main line, and ends every case happily. The reading still records material change, of two kinds.

**Changes of practice for Monica**:

- keeping, asking, thanking, writing (section 6.3);
- the first stated reason for her magic (obs. 079).

**Disclosures of origin**:

- Elliott's creed and its causes (obs. 112);
- the Nortons' villainy (obs. 151);
- Glen's apprenticeship (obs. 099);
- Cyril's early isolation (obs. 069).

It also extends the cluster around Felix (section 6.10). None of these alters a V04 claim's direction. Several strengthen one.

### 8.2 What Felix's complaint means

Felix says it on hearing what Monica will make from the plant book, and says it was not meant for her (obs. 064). Two readings are possible:

- **Jealousy of the rose's giver.** It would continue V04's recorded jealousy over the pendant (V04 obs. 114).
- **Envy that she may borrow a book he cannot.** It would continue the grievance of the case.

The narration supports neither over the other. Monica's words are elided on the page (S15:E001023). The reading records the line and leaves its object open, under SW-P-0036.

### 8.3 Normative evaluation: Isabelle's quiet settlement

Criterion: whether a just outcome is reached when the formal remedy is disproportionate. The charm's spread involved royalty in a curse ritual, a capital matter (obs. 139), and Wanda knowingly continued after learning it was a curse (S24:E001131). Isabelle's settlement is as follows:

- it conceals the curse from the school;
- it lets Cecily return the book with a plausible story;
- it binds both cousins by obligation (obs. 141, 142).

The volume presents this as mercy and as skill. It does not register the cost to the school's knowledge, or the leverage Isabelle now holds. The reading records both the proportion and the manipulation, and makes no ruling between them.

### 8.4 Normative evaluation: Louis's tyranny and Monica's lies

Criterion: whether costs to others are registered.

- **Louis.** He conscripts Monica for her chantless seals and treats colleagues as cargo (obs. 003, 007). The narration registers this only as comedy: 「横暴である」 (S12:E000182).
- **Monica.** Her lies protect herself and others (obs. 044, 119, 137). The volume treats them as harmless. Only the lie to Felix about the library (obs. 044) and the deception her friends' gifts imply (section 6.3) bear on persons she cares for.

The reading records the comic register and the unregistered costs.

### 8.5 Meaningful absences

- Bridget appears only in command and in others' eyes; her love is inferred by Benjamin and Elliott (obs. 121, 125).
- Yuan, Heidi, the traitor, 閣下 and Louis's hypothesis do not appear; Monica recalls Yuan's curse once (obs. 103).
- Eliane appears without consequence from the festival (obs. 130, 135).
- Mary Harvey appears only as an author of popular astrology (obs. 102).
- The peridot stays in the drawer (obs. 067).
- MacLaggan's sentence about Glen and Elliott's second turning point are cut off (obs. 094, 112).

### 8.6 The afterword, the jacket and the cards

Three paratexts bear on placement and are not narrative evidence:

- The afterword: the book is centred on episodes between V04 and V05, a "4.5"; it was newly written; V05 begins the winter-break arc (S37:E000017; S37:E000104).
- The jacket: it summarizes the cases as winter-term incidents (obs. 166).
- The cards: section 7.8.

### 8.7 Apparent contradictions

As tabled in section 6.11. The one unresolved item is the Heims-Naria date (about a year before the mission, or two years before Case IV). It is recorded, not reconciled.
