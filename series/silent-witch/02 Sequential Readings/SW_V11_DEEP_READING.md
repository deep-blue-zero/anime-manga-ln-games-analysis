## 2. Prior-volume developments

Monica Everett, the Silent Witch, lives in Southerndoor near Lana's Flax Company, with Isaac Walker as her apprentice for part of each month. Isaac is publicly Duke of Erin under the dead prince's name.

At V10, forbidden-art crimes were changed from death to life imprisonment, which Monica felt was her father's research changing the country. An outside attack of a Sage's strength breached Neigal prison for mages. In Southerndoor, the escapee Darrell Horace, a spirit researcher who had used his eleven-year-old brother Rick and other men as substitute contract stones, hijacked the festival's lantern ship.

Monica, Wildianu, Cyril, the old knight Brendan Autred and Isaac captured him. Rick joined the Flax Company. Brendan became Isaac's steward and recognised Wildianu as Irene's former maid Diana. Monica gave Isaac the first of thirty books.

The prison's attacker is unknown. Adolf Farron, the Wind-Hand Mage, who hates Louis and was humiliated by Monica at the Sage selection, is among the escapees. Louis has moved his wife Rosalie and daughter Leonora to safety.

## 3. Current-volume story synopsis

**The prologue.** Louis is leaving the capital to review the barriers of important buildings. He visits Owen Wright, captain of the Magic Corps first unit and his former roommate, and leaves his own family under Owen's protection. They talk about apprentices, and about the escapee still at large, Adolf Farron. Adolf is Louis's old classmate. At the Sage selection he hurt Rosalie, then Louis's fiancée, and forced Owen to help him.

On the eastern road a mage guards a book-carrier's wagon. He is an escapee in stolen clothes. His memory returns to the night of the prison break. In the smoke a pale-gold-haired girl in a white dress offered to ruin the world for him. He refused: he would twist one man's face himself. Pleased, she gave him an ice hand-bell that briefly amplifies mana, good for five uses. Her tall companion, called スロース, came for her. The guard is Adolf Farron.

**Chapters one and two.** Bradford's apprentice Ugo Galletti brings Monica an invitation. She is to explain the formulas at Bradford's lecture at Cresburn Magic University in Highorn, in mid-冬招月. Glen reports that Louis will re-barrier the same university, which keeps the black-flame grimoire 『終焉招く黒』.

Isaac teaches Glen for the lower-mage examination. He calls Glen a younger brother who needs looking after, and remembers his own mother's pies and a sibling about to be born. Raul's sister Melissa, the fourth Thorn Witch, who was deposed, tampers with a vial of his fertiliser.

At the joint research field the pea absorbs mana, grows into a giant and swallows Monica. Raul had misread the decimal point by two places. Cyril removes his brooch to hold the plant off, and calls Raul by name for the first time.

**Chapter three.** Melissa, fleeing, meets Cyril without learning who he is, and falls for him. Raul animates the dried white roses of a wreath in Cyril's bag into a dragon, ホワイトハリケーン一号. Cyril calls the old student-council roll call into the plant, and Monica answers him. The wreath was meant for Monica, whose birthday falls in 冬招月.

**Chapter four.** Isaac visits Marquess Baldia. They trade intelligence: the Black Lion Emperor means to marry a sister into Lidil; Falfolia is split. Isaac detects a Roseburg love potion in his tea. Baldia meant to pair his daughter with the retired prince for the sake of his Falfolian ties.

Baldia was one of the backers Isaac recruited when he planned Clockford's death. Isaac keeps him as an ally under a gentleman's agreement and keeps the potion. He tells Wildianu that he will not use it, because that would be no fun. He learns that Brendan has seen through his wish to study under the Witch.

**Chapter five.** Marquess Highorn, Vincent Ashley, hosts the three researchers, glad that his adopted son has brought friends home. Cyril falters at a board game, remembering that "Father" forbade such vulgar things, and is rescued by the marquess's collection.

Raul's childhood is told. He is shunned as the image of the first Thorn Witch, Rebecca Roseburg. 『終焉招く黒』 is her book of black flame, which modern mana cannot fire. That night a man plants jewelled stakes around Cresburn, stolen and rewritten with Thornley's mind-interference magic, and touches a bell.

**Chapters six and seven.** Owen learns that Adolf is the suspect in a theft from the mage guild. Lynn and Byron mind Louis's daughter Leonora, who breaks a doll that Lynn says resembles Louis.

Ugo brings word that a bell rang at Cresburn, and a barrier full of white fog closed over the grounds. Inside are Louis, MacLaggan, staff and Bradford, who went in after them. In a corridor a man thinks he is a Minerva student and finds a Sage's staff in his hand.

Monica, Raul and Cyril enter. Monica judges the barrier not to be Louis's work. The fog is a dry illusion joined to mind interference, which makes people believe they are at Minerva, in proportion to their memories of it. Monica calls Cyril "Barney"; Raul calls him "Mary-san"; Cyril, who never saw Minerva, is unaffected. They find Louis's thrown staff, a rewritten barrier formula with a stake, and then Louis himself. He has a northern accent and the manners of his student days, and asks where Adolf Farron is.

**Chapter eight.** The first "Bad Boy of Minerva" meets "Silent Everett". A wind blast from hiding strikes Cyril, and Louis gives chase. He drops a note saying that Rosalie is held at the library building.

Monica detaches the composite spell in seconds; there are thirteen linked stakes. Raul pulls one out by sheer mana. They split up. Cyril is angry at himself for the trust Monica showed "Barney".

Adolf's viewpoint tells his life: a prodigy who meant to become a Sage like the Cannon Mage, until Louis kicked his forehead in class and never once regarded him as a rival. MacLaggan, unaffected inside his own barrier, tells Monica that Adolf was his student. Adolf wants to redo his school days, and to have Louis pay attention to him.

**Chapter nine.** Wounded, Louis fights as a middle-division boy. Monica restores him by casting his own Mirror Prison without a chant, imperfectly. She then tells him that Adolf wants his attention, and he laughs. Raul's rose dragon returns, and the regrown pea drinks the fog.

Bradford comes out, having rescued the staff while feeling like a student. MacLaggan sends Raul to the library building despite his fear of his ancestor's book. Cyril asks Raul to look after his junior. Cyril's brooch glows briefly as a north wind rises.

**Chapter ten.** The bell rings; Adolf breaks the library seal and opens 『終焉招く黒』. The black flame burns spells and barriers alike. Louis flies with Monica and tells her why enemies target her first. With Raul closing the ground below, Monica opens a decoy gate and then, with Louis, two summonings of the wind Spirit-King. Adolf drops the grimoire to save the bell. Raul catches the book, and Monica, dropped from the sky by Louis, clings to his hand without fear.

**Chapter eleven.** The bell's last ring sends seven fireballs at the capital. MacLaggan had foreseen it. With Ugo sensing, he and Bradford shoot down six with water and fire Spirit-Kings. Owen, with a barrier tool Louis paid for, deflects the last over the capital, and Lynn scatters the sparks.

Louis tells the bound Adolf that he never became a rival because he aimed his fists at the people around Louis. Louis names his adult method: money, violence and connections. Monica sees what a master owes an apprentice, and resolves to be one Isaac can be proud of. Cyril tells her that his junior can grow.

**Chapter twelve.** At the relocated lecture Monica answers an off-topic question by filling the board, then turns: if they'd like to record it, please do. Isabel and Conrad watch, with a silent guard who is Isaac under Wildianu's illusion. Monica scolds her apprentice and finds that Isabel is his accomplice. Isaac will travel with them to Kerbeck on business with the earl, and asks her to correct his paper.

**The epilogue.** Louis, on padded sick leave, hears from Owen that Adolf said a child gave him the bell. Before dawn the pale-gold girl, about ten, sits on a warehouse roof. She made the bell as a replica of 冬精霊の氷鐘, and Adolf failed her. She plans to win over the oldest ice spirit on a snowy mountain, which can summon a gate she cannot use, and to finish before spring. She sings of a winter without end.

**The secret episode.** One summer, Vanessa Dee, Bradford's sister and the real head of the Dee house, rebukes her son Hubert. He had kept from her his part in the black chalice, whose rights Barney Jones manages. She orders a six-month survey of a Kerbeck hot spring where mana density has suddenly risen, as a site for a mana furnace. Hubert expects Isabel to bring the Silent Witch home for the solstice holiday.

## 6. Close reading

### 6.1 A contained plot inside a frame of winter

V11 repeats V10's design: a contained adventure inside a frame of threat. The adventure is the fog at Cresburn. It runs from the invitation in the fourth week of 秋中月 to the lecture in mid-冬招月 (obs. 026, 035, 042). It closes with Adolf captured and the grimoire recovered (obs. 176, 189).

The frame is the bell:

- **Prologue.** A pale-gold girl gives Adolf the bell (obs. 018–021).
- **Epilogue.** The girl reappears, about ten, and plans for an endless winter (obs. 218–223).

V10's question of who breached the prison is answered only in part. Adolf infers that the girl broke it with the bell (obs. 021). Owen and Louis infer that the bell's owner broke it (obs. 217). The girl's own words say she made the bell (obs. 219). Her name and nature are withheld. Adolf judges her a high-rank spirit (obs. 019). Her breath does not fog (obs. 218).

The volume also uses winter as an image:

- the early winter (obs. 026);
- the legend that the winter spirit announces winter with leaves as letters (obs. 026);
- the north wind of the winter spirit at which Cyril's brooch glows (obs. 159, 160);
- the girl's leaf and her verse of undelivered letters (obs. 221).

The reading records that these images recur. It does not identify the girl with the winter spirit of the legend.

### 6.2 Masters and apprentices

The volume's organising subject is the master–apprentice relation. It sets out several pairs:

- **Louis and Glen.** A master who teaches fists over formulas (obs. 006, 195).
- **Bradford and Ugo.** A master his apprentice calls the strongest man in the kingdom (obs. 024, 108, 185).
- **MacLaggan and Adolf.** A master who blames himself for his student's crime, and stops him at last (obs. 139, 191).
- **Rutherford and Louis.** Rutherford's discipline of the young Louis (obs. 212).
- **Isaac and Glen.** Isaac teaches Glen, and calls him a younger brother (obs. 029, 032).
- **Monica and Isaac.** The pair the volume measures against all the others.

Louis's prologue sets the measure: he cannot imagine Monica teaching (obs. 010). The volume answers by making her learn. MacLaggan tells her she will understand what students want when she teaches (obs. 142). After the battle she sees that she has set Isaac a standard without thinking about a master's responsibility (obs. 192). She takes three models (obs. 194):

- Cyril, a dependable adult;
- MacLaggan, a master who understands his pupil;
- Bradford, a master his pupil is proud of.

Chapter twelve tests the resolve comically. She tries to scold Isaac, he disarms her, and she judges herself immature (obs. 206, 207). V10's transmission of what she was given (SW-C-0084) becomes, in V11, the deliberate study of other masters. Section 9 revises SW-C-0084.

### 6.3 Rivalry, attention and the adult's method

Adolf's story turns on attention. His own viewpoint gives:

- a childhood plan to become a Sage like the Cannon Mage (obs. 132, 133);
- Louis's arrival, a kick to his forehead, and Rosalie's preference (obs. 134);
- Louis's look, which said that Adolf did not register (obs. 135).

MacLaggan reads the plot as a wish to redo his school days and to have Louis pay attention, an urge that escalates when it comes from malice (obs. 141, 143). Monica repeats the reading to Louis, who laughs (obs. 152).

Louis answers Adolf twice:

- In battle he says that Adolf was never on his radar (obs. 170).
- Afterwards he tells the bound man why he never became a rival. He aimed at the people around Louis, and he always ran at the end (obs. 189).

The volume contrasts their methods:

- **Adolf** relies on borrowed power: the bell, Thornley's formula, the first Thorn Witch's book (obs. 090, 179).
- **Louis** is shown with what he has gained since he was poor (obs. 183, 186):
  - money, which buys Owen's barrier tool;
  - violence;
  - connections: MacLaggan, Bradford, Ugo, Owen and Lynn.

He says he learned the third from losing to Monica at the council (obs. 183). This revises V03's opposition of Cyril's patient effort to Louis's maxim of money, violence and strength (SW-C-0043). Section 9 records the revision.

Owen's part completes the theme. He once obeyed Adolf, because Adolf's relatives ran his father's hospital, and he calls that an excuse (obs. 092, 187). He now stops the last fireball over the capital (obs. 188). The narration says that the long-range mage lost to the Barrier Mage who guards the capital (S23:E000261).

### 6.4 The past as a trap

The fog makes each victim believe they are at Minerva, in proportion to their memories (obs. 113). What each person slips into shows what they carry:

| Person | Minerva time | Slip |
|---|---|---|
| Monica | Under three years | Calls Cyril "Barney"; fears others' eyes (obs. 111). |
| Raul | Visits | Calls Cyril "Mary-san"; he visited with Mary (obs. 110, 113). |
| Louis | About six years | Becomes the middle-division delinquent hunting Adolf (obs. 104, 119, 126, 145). |
| Bradford | Student | Unchanged in conduct; rescues the staff (obs. 156). |
| MacLaggan | Long tenure | Shielded by his own barrier (obs. 138). |
| Cyril | None | Immune; restores the others by shouting (obs. 112). |

Two consequences matter:

- **Cyril and Barney.** Monica's slip shows a trust she has never shown Cyril, and he is angry with himself (obs. 131). The volume gives this no further development. The reading registers it as relationship evidence, not as a stated feeling.
- **Monica and Barney.** MacLaggan's lesson recalls that Monica's failure to understand others ended her friendship with Barney (obs. 144). The secret episode adds that Barney, as Earl of Amberd, manages the chalice's rights (obs. 225).

Louis's regression shows his past self directly: a poor northern boy whose violence protected him (obs. 146). He is restored when Monica casts his own Mirror Prison before him (obs. 148, 150).

### 6.5 Lineage and fear: Raul and the Roseburgs

Raul's chapters set out the Roseburg inheritance:

- The first Thorn Witch, Rebecca, was a hero and a feared seductress (obs. 086).
- Her book of black flame is sealed away from the family by royal caution (obs. 087, 088).
- A tacit pact lets the family make strong drugs, provided they do not sell them (obs. 075).
- His sister broke the pact and was deposed, and Raul became head at sixteen (obs. 047, 076).

Raul alone can reproduce the rose fortress, and he would rather have magic to make friends (obs. 089). The volume tests this fear twice:

- MacLaggan sends him to the library building, the place he fears (obs. 157).
- He catches the book; Monica, dropped from the sky, clings to his hand without recoiling (obs. 178).

His friendship with Cyril advances throughout:

- the first use of his name (obs. 049);
- a wreath he promises Cyril in return for the one used up (obs. 062);
- a quarrel he enjoys (obs. 155);
- Cyril's request that he look after Monica (obs. 158).

Melissa is the counter-figure. She broke the pact, envies her brother, and tampers with his work. She is also comic: she falls for Cyril without learning his name (obs. 041, 047, 051).

### 6.6 Isaac's politics and means

Chapter four shows Isaac as a political actor:

- He trades intelligence about the Empire, Falfolia and Randall (obs. 064–068).

## 8. Alternative readings, absence and contradiction

### 8.1 "A comic interlude with a villain of the week"

The comic set pieces could suggest a light volume: the pea, the rose dragon, the sleepover, the scary face. Against this, the volume:

- partly answers V10's question of the prison break (obs. 021, 217);
- introduces a non-human antagonist with a plan (obs. 222);
- reopens Isaac's past and politics (obs. 039, 073).

The reading treats it as the second unit of the arc.

### 8.2 "Louis and Adolf are mirror images"

Both are proud, violent and fixed on the other, and Louis's past is shown as delinquent (obs. 146). Against a mirror reading, the volume makes the asymmetry explicit:

- Louis did not regard Adolf as a rival (obs. 135, 170).
- Louis's violence was aimed at those who provoked him; Adolf's fists were aimed at Louis's people (obs. 189).

The reading registers asymmetry, not symmetry.

### 8.3 Normative evaluation: Louis's violence

**Criterion:** whether the volume endorses Louis's methods.

It plays them for comedy: the smoking, the thrown staff (obs. 117, 195). It also gives them a history in poverty and a village code (obs. 146). Through Monica it marks them as a model to avoid (S23:E000466; S23:E000519). It endorses his adult additions, money and connections, used to protect others (obs. 183, 186).

### 8.4 Normative evaluation: Isaac's restraint

**Criterion:** whether Isaac's refusal of the potion is presented as ethical.

He refuses for joy and self-respect before the dead and before Monica (obs. 077). Wildianu's doubt is left standing (obs. 078). The volume keeps the question open, and the reading registers no change of disposition.

### 8.5 Meaningful absences

The volume leaves the following unanswered:

- the girl's name and nature, and スロース (obs. 019, 020, 218);
- why Romaria's brooch glowed (obs. 159);
- Isaac's business in Kerbeck (obs. 209);
- what Mary's foreseen event is, or the star of loss;
- whether the new Sage selection has advanced (obs. 098).

It also withholds Rosalie's and Leonora's direct speech.

### 8.6 The afterword, jacket and card

**The afterword** reports a ranking, thanks the illustrator and the editor, recounts a joke about a ruby reading, and says that the next volume is being written (obs. 237–239). It mentions adaptations, which are excluded from this reading.

**The jacket blurb** summarises the fog trap (obs. 240).

All of this is paratext.

### 8.7 Apparent contradictions

- **The girl's age.** Adolf gives early teens (obs. 018); the epilogue gives about ten (obs. 218). The difference is recorded, not resolved.
- **Rick's age.** The card again gives twelve, against V10's narrative eleven; the narrative governs.
