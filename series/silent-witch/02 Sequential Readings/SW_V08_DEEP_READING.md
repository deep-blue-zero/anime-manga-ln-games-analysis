## 2. Prior-volume developments

*Prepared 2026-10-14 before any V08 narrative member was rendered for reading, from the closed V01 to V07 readings, the closed IV -after- reading, their exit freezes and the master ledger only.*

Monica Everett, the Silent Witch, is seventeen and at Serendia Academy as Monica Norton, the student council's treasurer. She guards the second prince, Felix Arc Ridill, on Louis Miller's order. Her familiar Nero is awake again. Her left hand still bears the cursed dragon's injury. It is early spring.

In Kellylinden forest the Sages destroyed the ancient whistle Galanis. Monica asked the craftsman Bartholomeus for help for the first time, and the two sealed Louis's spirit Lynn. Raul's rose fortress, Ray's cursed armour, Bradford's fire, Carla Maxwell's Star Spear, Mary's illusion and Monica's barriers broke the Jewel Mage Emmanuel Darwin's tools; Monica cut the whistle in two. Emmanuel fled, and Felix met him on the road and offered help. In Emmanuel's thoughts his long-served patron, "that person", is Clockford. The dying ice spirit Romaria now rests in Cyril's brooch. Mary closed the affair and swore the students to silence; she has given Bartholomeus a workshop to support the Witch. Cyril has learned from Ray that the Witch's left hand is cursed. Louis suspects that Monica has an undisclosed helper, withholds information from her and tests her; she in turn withholds four matters from him.

At school, Bridget Graham found Monica out as a mage, ended Felix's search for a girl with an injured left hand by a rumour, and enlisted her. Bridget remembers a timid, kind Felix who returned changed after an illness about ten years ago; she believes the present Felix false. Monica found she wanted to know him. Disguised as gardeners with Raul, Monica and Bridget entered Duke Clockford's house on Sheffield's holiday. They found no captive. An old gardener told them that Peter Sams came there about ten years ago as physician, replacing a Dr Arthur, and that a fire killed Arthur and the prince's page, Isaac Walker. Bridget learned that Arthur's Imperial form is Artur; Monica said that everything connects and did not say what she concluded. Monica has addressed the Imperial agent Yuan through a newspaper notice. Yuan's master, a dark-haired man, means to come to Ridill.

## 3. Current-volume story synopsis

**The prologue.** On the night after Venedict Rain's arrest, Hilda Everett slips into his seized laboratory. She takes a set of designs she had chosen in advance and his coffee pot, knowing both will be his last effects. She wants to kill Peter Sams, who denounced him, and thinks of his shy daughter.

**Chapter one.** Ten days after the infiltration of Clockford's house, Monica reads the treasurer's report aloud to the whole school. Cyril praises her. Bridget says nothing of the failed search. Monica has guessed the "terrible truth" the Imperial assassin spoke of and has already made a move to check it. Heidi, Yuan's partner, whispers a meeting that night. Elsewhere Duke Clockford visits Bishop Labiana, who wants him to legalize a Randall drug, Vikeriyuon. Clockford names the places where the bishop used it, offers him a pistol, and has his guards kill him when he aims it. Mary arrives at dusk and asks after the Jewel Mage. Clockford learns that intruders have entered the country.

**Chapter two.** Yuan, wearing Felix's face, meets Monica in the forest. She sets out her conclusion. The physician Arthur was Yuan's former friend Artur, who stole his body-manipulation research. The boy who died in the fire ten years ago was the real prince, and the present Felix is the page Isaac Walker, remade. Yuan's side means to expose this when Felix takes the throne, to throw Ridill into strife. Monica refuses an invitation to the Empire. When she is attacked, she brings down the hidden master from a tree. He is Leonhardt, the young emperor called the Black Lion. He refuses to spare Felix: he wants to avoid a war he says Clockford desires. Monica threatens his life and bargains. If she averts war, the prince's secret stays hidden. If Ridill declares war, she must serve him and end the war by presenting him the royal family's heads. Heidi stays as liaison and is given a request. In Nero's adventure novels Monica finds the black chalice and concludes why her father was killed.

**Chapter three.** At Porter's shop Monica learns that the black chalice is the novel's name for her father's design: a tool reading hereditary information from blood, which could show that the present prince is not the king's son. Porter has known the prince was false and done nothing. He hid the note when the prince brought her. Monica shows him she is the Silent Witch. Meanwhile Felix meets Emmanuel at an inn and asks him to make a tool in Clockford's collection run wild during an inspection. The death would be blamed on the dead Bishop Labiana. He offers Emmanuel a place as the next king's adviser and the backing of four nobles.

**Chapter four.** On the way back Felix dreams. Isaac Walker, a doctor's son from Lugaroa, lost his father, mother and baby brother to dragons and was scarred. In an orphanage he was chosen by Clockford to become the prince's double, and became the prince's page. He comforted the frail five-year-old with a handkerchief rabbit, became his secret friend Ike to the prince's Arc, and watched the stars with him.

**Chapter five.** After Elliott dared the prince into a fall, the prince was left scarred. Clockford had Arthur cut the same scar into Isaac. The prince, ashamed of himself and of his mother's death at his birth, found the scar on Isaac and confronted his grandfather. He learned that Isaac was being raised as his replacement and had known it from the start.

**Chapter six.** Two years in, the prince took Isaac to watch the stars from a tree. He asked him to find something he loved for himself. Then he gave him a letter and his mother's contract stone, telling him to flee while he caused a commotion. He climbed to the roof and fell to his death. Clockford hid the death. Isaac chose to become the prince so that the name would not be forgotten, and Arthur remade his face. Clockford killed Arthur, and Isaac set the shed burning with the two bodies.

**Chapter seven.** Isaac learned politics, secretly studied magic, and after about five years contracted Irene's water spirit, Wildianu, as an ally of his own. Wildianu reminded him of the prince's last wish. Nothing moved him until, caught by the Worgan black dragon's wyverns, he saw the Silent Witch's spirit-king summoning strike them down. He found the thing he could lose himself in.

**Chapter eight.** In the evening Monica asks Felix whether he would sacrifice someone for a wish; he says he would. She tells him her father's name and begs him never to forget it, then runs out into the rain. She cannot bring herself to expose him. With Nero she plans to make Clockford yield the throne to the first prince, at a garden party a month away. Louis, having proved the prince false, means to denounce Clockford at the same party and keeps it from Monica. His wife gives birth. Emmanuel tries to rewrite the bishop's dagger in front of Clockford; it was a trap, and he dies.

**Chapter nine.** A week later Cyril praises Monica's work and asks her to stay on the council; she declines. Felix learns from Porter who Venedict Rain was and what his death served, and realizes that Monica knows. He cannot bring himself to harm her. Central soldiers arrest him at the dormitory as an Imperial impostor, citing Emmanuel's suicide note. He judges that Clockford has cut him loose and hands the council to Cyril.

**Chapter ten.** Emmanuel's note blames the Empire. Monica sees Clockford's hand. Elliott admits he knew: as a boy he recognized the page's eyes in the new prince, who nearly strangled him and told him the prince had died to set him free. Cyril, overhearing, despairs; Monica shouts down his self-contempt, and he resolves to keep the council.

**Chapter eleven.** The school closes for three days. Heidi brings the result of Monica's request, the location of Marcy, the prince's head maid. Nero flies Monica north to the convent where Casey now lives. Marcy is dead, but she left the real prince's diary for Isaac. Its last page shows Monica that Isaac has misunderstood something. Monica and Casey are reconciled.

**Chapter twelve.** At the Sages' meeting every faction finds it convenient to blame the Empire; Louis says power decides what is true. Monica keeps the diary from him. The Supreme Council will decide in sixteen days. Mary gives Monica Bartholomeus's address, and Monica asks her for a meeting. Nero visits Isaac in the tower. He will die as a villain to keep the prince's honour, and asks that Wildianu go to the Silent Witch. Hilda gives Monica her father's designs for the chalice.

**Chapter thirteen.** Bartholomeus says the chalice cannot be built without facilities and materials that would alert Louis, but starts work. Monica says goodbye to Lana as if for the last time. Nero and Cyril recover the watch from crows. Wildianu tells of his love for Irene and offers himself to free Isaac; Monica refuses any more sacrifice and decides to rely on others.

**The epilogue.** Eliane is to be engaged to Albert, who thinks himself unfit to rule and never relied on. Monica gathers the council, Albert and Hubert, with Isabelle at her side. She apologizes for lying, puts on the Sage's robe and staff, names herself, and asks for their help.

**The secret episode.** Clockford reviews the council list. Mary, his former fiancée, visits. His father proposed peace in the war fifty years ago, was killed by Ridill's own war party, and was branded a coward; Ridill then lost. Clockford says Ridill would win a war now. Mary says the discarded boy is like his own end.

## 6. Close reading

### 6.1 Structure: one secret told three ways

V08 tells the Felix secret three times, each in a different voice:

- **As a conclusion.** In chapters one to three Monica states her inference, Yuan's side endorses it, and Porter confirms its motive (obs. 039–047, 077–078, 081).
- **As memory.** In chapters four to seven the story is told from inside the person it concerns (obs. 097–175).
- **As consequence.** In chapters eight to thirteen and the epilogue the arrest, the testimonies and Monica's decision follow from it (obs. 176–291).

The hinge is a frame. In chapter four the present Felix falls asleep in a carriage and dreams of his first carriage ride (obs. 097). The memory that follows is Isaac Walker's. In chapter eight he wakes in the same carriage, and the narration corrects itself from "Isaac" to "Felix" (obs. 176). The volume thus gives the identity in the narration, from the person's own point of view, and not only through others' beliefs.

The volume closes on a second reveal, Monica's. She names herself to the council and asks for help (obs. 290, 291). The frontispiece of the weeping Monica and the closing line frame the book (obs. 300, 291).

### 6.3 The black chalice and the father's case

The black chalice, open since V05, is resolved at the level of meaning. It is the novel's name for Venedict Rain's design: a tool that analyses the mana in blood to read hereditary information (obs. 077, 266). Porter used the name in his hidden note as a code that a reader of his novels would understand (obs. 071, 076, 079).

The motive is given in three ways:

- as Monica's conclusion: the tool could show that the present prince is not the king's son (obs. 078);
- as Porter's and Isaac's (obs. 197, 199);
- in the narration, which states that Hilda had hidden Clockford's part in Rain's death (obs. 265).

The prologue adds that powerful people worked against Rain in the background, that the seizure was hurried from above, and that Sams denounced him (obs. 001, 002, 005). Clockford never speaks of it in his own point of view, in the bishop scene or the secret episode. The reading registers Clockford's responsibility for the execution as given by the narration and the characters' agreed conclusion, and the chalice motive as their conclusion (SW-C-0064). The patron 閣下 to whom Sams sold Rain remains unidentified (SW-C-0039).

Hilda's prologue shows that she took the designs as well as the pot (obs. 003, 004). In chapter twelve she gives the designs to Monica (obs. 267), and they become the means of the planned rescue (obs. 268).

### 6.4 Clockford

Clockford is seen acting four times:

- He offers Bishop Labiana a pistol and has him killed when he aims it (obs. 026, 027).
- He sets a trap for the traitor in his collection and kills Emmanuel with it (obs. 190, 191).
- In memory, he chooses a scarred orphan as a substitute, cuts a scar into him, calls his grandson "that thing", hides the boy's death and stabs Arthur (obs. 106, 110, 123, 145, 156).
- When the substitute turns, he discards him and recasts the whole affair as an Imperial crime (obs. 205, 209).

Mary's image of him playing many boards alone and discarding any piece fits these acts (obs. 029). He is also given reasons. He refuses a foreign drug as a poison to the land (obs. 024). In the secret episode his father proposed peace during the war fifty years ago and was killed by Ridill's own war party, and Ridill lost the following year (obs. 294). He now judges that Ridill would win a war (obs. 295).

The volume leaves his motive for war at that. His statement that he feels nothing but attachment is his own (obs. 293). Mary's comparison of the discarded boy with his own end is her prophecy, not a fact (obs. 296).

### 6.5 The Empire and the bargain

The Black Lion Emperor speaks for himself at length (obs. 054–066). His argument is:

- Large wars have stopped because mages have multiplied, and a war now would mean mass death and land poisoned by mana (obs. 058).
- He wants to avoid war by accusing Felix on his accession, without Imperial blood (obs. 059).
- If attacked, he will retaliate (obs. 066).

He also admits that the Empire is weak, with coups common (obs. 065). His counterpart's case is given in the secret episode: Clockford judges that this weakness means Ridill would win (obs. 295). The volume sets the two leaders' arguments side by side and does not decide between them.

Monica's bargain is made by threat. She tells him she would let him leave alive only if he agreed, and that she could kill him by treating him as a chess piece (obs. 061, 062). He laughs because he once said the same to a tribal chief (S14:E000488). Her stake is the loss of her treasures; his terms bind her, if war comes, to end it by killing her own royal family (obs. 063, 064). The reading records the bargain as the volume's main new obligation (SW-C-0066).

### 6.6 Monica: from bystander to actor

The volume opens with Monica deciding to keep the truth to herself, and the narrator immediately marks that decision as temporary (obs. 017). By the end she has done the following:

- She has threatened an emperor (obs. 061).
- She has revealed herself as the Silent Witch to Porter (obs. 084), and through Nero to Isaac (obs. 259).
- She has refused the "most correct" course, full exposure, which would clear her father and have Isaac executed (obs. 182, 253).
- She has asked a craftsman, a spirit, her friends and her council for help (obs. 271, 282, 283, 291).

Porter's self-description as a bystander is the foil (obs. 081). Monica says that she too could do nothing when her father was burned, but that she can do more now (obs. 084).

Two scenes give the cost:

- The father's name spoken to Felix is called a curse by Monica herself and by the frontispiece (obs. 181, 300).
- The drawer of treasures, the farewell to Lana and the uniform she will not wear again set the price of the reveal in the loss of Monica Norton (obs. 274, 283).

Her motive is stated in her own words: she wants to save Isaac, and to know him (obs. 235, 241). She cannot name what he is to her (obs. 240). The reading registers the wish and the inability to name it, and no more.


### 6.7 Isaac Walker

The memory chapters build Isaac in a pattern of failed protection:

- He promises his father to protect his mother and brother, and cannot (obs. 100, 104).
- He resolves to protect the prince "this time", and cannot (obs. 126, 144).
- He blames himself for the prince's death (obs. 146).

His adoption of the name follows from the prince's fear of being forgotten (obs. 114, 148). The narration itself judges it a childish and foolish wish (obs. 149). The adult Isaac describes himself as already dead (obs. 258) and plans to be executed as a villain, to make the prince's tragedy beautiful (obs. 260).

Against this the volume sets the prince's own last wish: that Isaac find something he loves for himself (obs. 140, 168). Isaac finds it only once, in the Silent Witch's magic (obs. 174, 175). The S12 epigraph prints that thought before the book begins (obs. 007). The scene retells V01's prologue from the watcher's side: the wyverns, the gate of light and the words of a man who has found what he can lose himself in (V01 obs. 001–003; SW-C-0006).

The diary contradicts something in his understanding of the prince's death (obs. 247). The text does not say what. The reading records the contradiction and not its content.

He is also ruthless. He suborns Emmanuel to kill his grandfather and frame a dead man (obs. 090, 091). He would sacrifice what he must for the throne (obs. 177). He plans to block Lionel's escape if Randall turns (obs. 167). He nearly strangles Elliott (obs. 220). Monica is the one person he cannot bring himself to harm (obs. 202). The reading keeps both halves on record (SW-C-0065).

### 6.8 Others

- **Cyril** learns that the prince he served was never the prince, calls himself a clown, and recovers by holding to his own choice and his duty (obs. 227, 229). He studies spirit language for Romaria (obs. 275).

- **Emmanuel** is used by Felix and killed by Clockford (obs. 088–094, 190, 191).

### 6.9 Names, stars and remembering

The volume turns on remembering a name:

- The prince wishes to become a star so as not to be forgotten (obs. 114).
- Isaac takes the name to carve it in history (obs. 148, 166).
- Monica binds Felix to remember her father's name, and calls it a curse (obs. 180, 181).
- Isaac is glad that the Silent Witch will remember his own name (obs. 261).
- Nero forgets it and is told it (obs. 259).
- Porter says Isaac's story will not be told (obs. 200).
- The secret episode's English title is "The star's future" (S30).

The volume does not resolve whether the name or the person should be remembered. It sets the prince's wish for Isaac's happiness against Isaac's wish for the prince's fame.

## 8. Alternative readings, absence and contradiction

### 8.1 "The disclosure is Monica's inference"

One could read the identity as resting on Monica's reasoning in chapter two (obs. 040–043). It does not rest there. The volume gives it:

- in the narration, from inside the person's own memory, framed by his sleep and waking (obs. 097, 176);
- in Elliott's independent account of the boy he recognized (obs. 219, 220);
- in Clockford's own words in that memory (obs. 147);
- in the card (section 7.5).

Monica's account is wrong in one particular: she supposes the prince died "of some cause" and the page's death was faked to hide it. The memory shows how he died (obs. 143). The reading therefore registers the identity as disclosed (SW-C-0063). It keeps Monica's account as her inference where it goes beyond the memory, notably her supposition that Clockford killed Arthur to silence him, which the memory confirms (obs. 043, 156).

### 8.2 "Clockford killed Venedict Rain"

Monica, Porter and Isaac reach this conclusion, and the narration says Hilda hid Clockford's part in Rain's death (obs. 078, 197–199, 265). Against treating it as fully established:

- Clockford never speaks of Rain;
- the mechanism (Sams's denunciation, 閣下's purchase) still runs through an unidentified patron (obs. 005; SW-C-0039).

The reading registers Clockford's part as narrated and the chalice motive as the characters' conclusion (SW-C-0064), and keeps 閣下 open.

### 8.3 Normative evaluation: the bargain

Criterion: whether the volume presents Monica's threat and her acceptance of a regicide clause as justified. The emperor's argument is given in full and the narration grants that it is reasonable (S14:E000438). Monica herself finds the clause unbearable and accepts it because she has already threatened him (obs. 064; S14:E000545–E000546). The volume shows the threat as effective and the cost as hers. It does not ask whether a Sage may bargain over her own kingdom's royal family.

### 8.4 Normative evaluation: rescue over justice

Criterion: whether the volume weighs clearing Monica's father, and punishing those responsible, against saving the man whose existence caused his death. Monica names full exposure as "the most correct way" and declines it (obs. 253). Louis states the opposite principle, that power makes truth (obs. 252). The volume grants her motive, the prince's unknown last wish and her wish to know Isaac (obs. 241, 247). It leaves the question of her father's justice open for the Supreme Council.

### 8.5 Meaningful absences

- The last sentence of the prince's diary is withheld (obs. 246).
- What Monica asks Mary, whom she wants to meet, and what she fetches from the castle are withheld (obs. 255, 273).
- What Monica asked Heidi to find is withheld until the result (obs. 069, 234).
- 閣下 is not advanced.
- The person Mary does not want to die and the hidden star are not advanced.
- The king does not appear.
- Lionel appears only in others' speech.

### 8.6 The afterword, the jacket and the cards

The afterword calls the reveal scene one the author long wanted to write. It says the robe is not in the web version and came from a comic illustration (S41:E000016–E000017); the reading draws nothing about the web version from this. It announces an anime adaptation in progress (S41:E000041). The jacket calls the volume the eighth act, heading towards the end (obs. 299). The cards are listed in section 7.5. All are paratext.

### 8.7 Apparent contradictions

- **The fire.** The gardener (V07) said Isaac Walker died in the fire. V08 shows the bodies were Arthur and the prince. The gardener reported the cover story; this is not a contradiction.
- **The official story.** It dates the substitution to just before the end of the winter break; the memory dates it about ten years ago. The official story is false within the fiction (obs. 211, 231).
- **Sams.** Sams came to Clockford's house "about ten years ago" (V07) and Arthur was killed then (obs. 156). These agree.
- **Isaac's age.** He was "just seven" when taken (S16:E000320) and "ten this year" two years later (S18:E000013). These are consistent if he was taken late in his seventh year.
