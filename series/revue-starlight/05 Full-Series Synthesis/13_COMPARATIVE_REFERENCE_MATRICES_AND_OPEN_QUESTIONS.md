# 『少女☆歌劇 レヴュースタァライト』
## Comparative Reference Matrices and Open Questions
### Reusable character, relationship, Revue, motif, and philosophical reference

**Function:** This document exports the conclusions developed in Documents 01–12 into compact forms suitable for future cross-series, cross-character, and methodological comparison. It is not the primary home of any major argument. For the reasoning behind a table entry, follow the internal cross-reference.

**Spoiler scope:** Complete television series, all three OVAs, sequel film, credits, and post-credit audition.

---

# I. How to use this document

The matrices are designed for four kinds of use.

## 1. Within-corpus comparison

Examples:

- Karen and Junna as two different anti-finality characters;
- Nana and Hikari as two different protective controllers;
- Claudine and Futaba as two characters who transform fan-like admiration into co-performance;
- Mahiru and Kaoruko as two characters threatened when a loved person becomes less dependent.

## 2. Cross-series comparison

The matrices support comparison with works about:

- idols;
- theater;
- bands;
- school clubs;
- yuri-coded relationships;
- artistic rivalry;
- memory and repetition;
- institutional competition;
- audience and public image.

## 3. Retrieval

Use source codes and primary-home references to locate the full argument.

## 4. Hypothesis generation

The open questions identify points that remain underdetermined or require other franchise continuities.

### Caution

The tables compress.

Compression can make:

- development look static;
- ambivalence look resolved;
- relationship intensity look morally classified;
- metaphor look like fixed code.

Use the matrices as index, not substitute for close reading.

# V. Compact character export profiles

## Karen

- **Desire:** another stage capable of answering Hikari and reaching everyone.
- **Fear:** ending without next line.
- **Ethical problem:** constitutive relation becoming terminal vocation.
- **Final formulation:** `愛城華恋は 舞台に一人` + `ひかりに負けたくない`.

## Hikari

- **Desire:** reciprocal performance without extraction or fan-like self-erasure.
- **Fear:** loss and captured gaze.
- **Ethical problem:** protection becoming unilateral control.
- **Final formulation:** `神楽ひかり 私がスタァだ`; wait and communicate rather than disappear.

## Mahiru

- **Desire:** live onstage and make people smile.
- **Fear:** no longer being needed.
- **Ethical problem:** care requiring dependency.
- **Final formulation:** afraid but decided; desired Hikari-stage can end without becoming meaningless.

## Futaba

- **Desire:** personal road and co-rivalry.
- **Fear:** permanent fan/caretaker status.
- **Ethical problem:** personal ambition disguised as service.
- **Final formulation:** `今度はあたしが わがまま言う番`.

## Kaoruko

- **Desire:** world-class bloom and unique relational centrality.
- **Fear:** abandonment and ordinary domestication of desire.
- **Ethical problem:** possessive inheritance of another person's attention.
- **Final formulation:** different road accepted without emotional indifference.

## Junna

- **Desire:** protagonist status authored through work and language.
- **Fear:** being permanently “not enough” or a remembered underdog.
- **Ethical problem:** external authority substituting for own voice.
- **Final formulation:** `私の道は 私が 切り開く`; `眩しい主役 星見純那`.

## Nana

- **Desire:** preserve precious collective life.
- **Fear:** irreversibility and loss of legibility.
- **Ethical problem:** memory becoming environmental/casting control.
- **Final formulation:** `私の再演は 今`; `またね`; present-tense `眩しい`.

## Maya

- **Desire:** highest stage and universal performance capacity.
- **Fear:** insufficiency and contaminating contingency.
- **Ethical problem:** ideal performer as self-erasure.
- **Final formulation:** perfection enlarged by greedy, jealous, cute, embodied Maya.

## Claudine

- **Desire:** surpass and be uniquely recognized by Maya.
- **Fear:** replaceability and permanent secondness.
- **Ethical problem:** rival relation becoming exclusive total purpose.
- **Final formulation:** another match, another Maya, another Claudine.

## Relationship diagnostic questions

For any comparison, ask:

1. **Who first gives whom a future?**
2. **What role makes the relation possible?**
3. **What role later becomes too small?**
4. **Who can refuse the other's interpretation?**
5. **Does admiration demand repetition or generate response?**
6. **Can the relation survive different institutions or geography?**
7. **What ordinary practices—not only climactic declarations—sustain it?**
8. **What audience does the pair create for itself?**
9. **What does the pair need from the ensemble?**
10. **What would make the mature form regress?**

## Six-outcome shorthand

When comparing Revues, keep six outcomes distinct:

- **MEC:** mechanical
- **EMO:** emotional
- **EPI:** epistemic
- **REL:** relational
- **SYM:** symbolic/theatrical
- **MUS:** musical

A Revue may have:

> MEC loss + EPI breakthrough + REL transformation.

This is common rather than exceptional in the corpus.

### Motif-use rule

Do not compare motifs only by object.

Compare:

1. who controls it;
2. whether it opens or closes action;
3. whether it is remembered, repeated, or transformed;
4. what relationship is active;
5. what music and editing do around it;
6. whether the later recurrence preserves or reverses the first function.

# XI. Performance-comparison prompts

Use these questions when comparing *Revue Starlight* to another performance-centered work.

# XIII. Open questions — genuine animated-corpus ambiguities

## 1. What is the exact ontology of the Revue space?

The animated corpus deliberately overlaps:

- diegetic mechanism;
- performed theatrical reality;
- subjective relation;
- impossible physical event.

A fully literal mechanics model remains underdetermined.

## 2. How much does the Giraffe learn?

He recognizes novelty and adapts.

It remains unclear whether he undergoes ethical development or simply reclassifies whatever produces better spectacle.

## 3. What is `キラめき` at the most literal level?

It is extractable, losable, re-producible, inaccessible, renewable, and spectrally visible in several senses.

The corpus supports conceptual plurality more strongly than one final metaphysical definition.

## 4. How sustainable are the final rivalries?

Maya/Claudine and Karen/Hikari acquire future-producing relations.

The animated corpus does not show enough adult duration to prove rivalry will not become another total purpose.

## 5. How much of Mahiru's movie coercion is ethically endorsed?

The Revue is dramatically successful.

The work also makes it frightening and excessive.

The exact normative balance remains intentionally uncomfortable.

## 6. How stable is Futaba/Kaoruko equality?

The film permits separate roads.

Their long-term division of practical labor, attention, and emotional authority remains open.

## 7. What does Nana finally choose between performing and creating?

The Royal Academy future preserves exploration.

The animated corpus does not force a permanent answer.

## 8. How romantic are the principal relationships in canonical naming?

The yuri coding is extensive.

The animated continuity rarely supplies conventional explicit romantic labels.

The disciplined position remains:

> strong textual intimacy and yuri compatibility, with pair-specific degrees and unresolved naming.

## 9. Does Karen pass the post-credit audition?

The film withholds the answer.

Any claim about success exceeds the animated text.

The uncertainty is thematically functional.

## 10. What does the intact final tomato ultimately mean?

Ripeness, body, role completion, consumption, and renewed organic material are all supported associations.

A singular secret-code solution is not.

## 1. Animated continuity only

This synthesis does not claim to exhaust:

- stage-play continuities;
- mobile-game stories;
- manga adaptations;
- later franchise material;
- creator interviews not included in the corpus.

## 2. OVA Japanese text

The available OVA subtitle layer is English.

Visual, musical, and performed-vocal analysis is strong.

Fine-grained Japanese lexical claims remain conservative.

## 3. Film transcript layer

The Japanese ASS is an analysis-oriented transcription and retrieval aid rather than an official publication-grade screenplay.

Load-bearing phonetic or timing claims should continue to be checked against audio.

## 4. Final-mix analysis

Technical music measurements describe the complete stereo mix, often including dialogue and effects.

They corroborate rather than replace listening and dramaturgical interpretation.

## 5. Credits image attribution

Some tiny messages or inset images do not always name sender/recipient within the same frame.

Relationship-specific attribution should remain conservative when the visual evidence is not explicit.

# XVI. Final comparative orientation

The most reusable full-series formulation is:

> **A Stage Girl is a person who becomes through roles without being exhausted by any one role.**

The most reusable relationship formulation is:

> **A mature bond remains constitutive after the beloved person exceeds the form through which she was first loved.**

The most reusable temporal formulation is:

> **Re-production preserves causal inheritance without granting the past ownership of the future's form.**

The most reusable audience formulation is:

> **To watch ethically is to desire another performance while permitting the performer to change what “another” means.**

And the most reusable formal formulation is:

> **The Revue is the form in which a relationship becomes performable—and therefore answerable.**

These principles distinguish *Revue Starlight* from works that treat:

- performance as mere self-expression;
- rivalry as only rank competition;
- memory as only comfort;
- graduation as only farewell;
- audience as passive witness;
- or role as the opposite of authenticity.

Its distinctive claim is that the self does not exist outside performance in some pure, untouched state.

The ethical and artistic challenge is to keep performance alive enough that the performer can still become someone the last role did not know how to contain.
