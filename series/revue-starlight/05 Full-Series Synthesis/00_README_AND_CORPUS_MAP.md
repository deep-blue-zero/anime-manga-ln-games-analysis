# 『少女☆歌劇 レヴュースタァライト』
## Definitive Animated-Corpus Synthesis
### Corpus guide, executive orientation, source policy, and document map

> **Complete spoiler warning:** This corpus discusses the full television series, all three OVAs, the entire sequel film, the movie credits, and the post-credit audition without spoiler restriction.

---

# I. Purpose

This package is the definitive multi-document synthesis of the animated *Revue Starlight* corpus examined for this project.

It is designed to function as:

- a full-series literary and thematic analysis;
- a character and relationship reference;
- an audiovisual and musical-dramaturgy reference;
- a Japanese-language and vocal-performance reference;
- a record of the Revue system, Giraffe, audience, Position Zero, and *Starlight* intertext;
- a comparative-analysis source for later work on anime, manga, games, performance fiction, idol narratives, yuri-coded relationship dramas, and stories about memory, repetition, institutions, and artistic vocation.

The corpus does not merely concatenate the earlier episode and movie-movement analyses.

It retrospectively integrates them while preserving developmental chronology:

> **Earlier reading → later pressure → revised full-series understanding.**

The television endings are treated as genuine resolutions.

The sequel film is read as a pressure-test of what happens when a successful resolution becomes another completed role.

---

# II. Mature series thesis

The complete animated corpus is about:

> **the necessity and danger of becoming through roles.**

A Stage Girl becomes legible through forms:

- performer;
- star;
- rival;
- fan;
- caretaker;
- Claire;
- Flora;
- childhood promise-partner;
- Top Star;
- member of the 99th class.

These roles are not merely false masks covering an authentic self.

They help create the person.

Karen becomes a Stage Girl because Hikari gives theater a relational direction.

Junna's desire for protagonist status protects her against interchangeability.

Nana discovers collective creation through the first *Starlight*.

Maya makes discipline and visible excellence impossible to sentimentalize away.

Futaba's fanhood and care are real forms of love before they become roles too narrow for her.

The ethical and temporal danger begins when the role that once made a person possible claims the right to remain her final definition.

The television series answers tragedy through:

> **Encore.**

The ending happened.

Another scene can still be made.

The movie extends the answer:

> **The finished self can also become material for another self.**

The strongest master formulation is:

> **A Stage Girl is not the role she performs, the center she wins, the person she loves, the script she inherits, the radiance others see, or the ending that once made her complete. She is the performer who can turn all of those inheritances into material for another stage.**

This is the corpus's mature meaning of:

> **再生産 — re-production.**

Re-production is not change for its own sake.

It is:

> **continuity in which the past remains causally active without possessing the future's form.**

- editing;

- choreography;

## Tier 1B — Japanese textual evidence

Use:

- Japanese subtitle/dialogue layers;
- corrected transcript material;
- lyrics;
- title cards;
- onscreen Japanese text.

These govern exact claims about:

- wording;
- tense;
- pronouns;
- address;
- register;
- recurrent lexical forms.

## Tier 1C — Canonical epilogue evidence

The movie credits and post-credit scene are narrative evidence because they contain:

- career destinations;
- travel;
- messages;
- first stages;
- changed plans;
- the ordinary audition that concludes Karen's arc.

## Tier 2 — Navigation materials

Contact sheets, dialogue indices, manifests, and shot maps are retrieval aids.

They do not replace direct inspection of the relevant scene.

## Tier 3 — Analytical derivatives

Optional measurements include:

- approximate BPM;
- RMS/loudness contours;
- spectral brightness;
- onset density;
- broad tonal estimates.

They are corroborative rather than primary.

# VIII. Evidence-state labels

- **AF — Audiovisual Fact:** directly visible or audible event.
- **JT — Japanese Textual Fact:** directly established by Japanese dialogue, lyric, title, or onscreen text.
- **PF — Performance Fact:** directly audible/visible delivery feature.
- **MF — Musical Fact:** directly audible musical structure or change.
- **FR — Formal Recurrence:** demonstrable return of image, line, melody, composition, object, or action.
- **SI — Strong Inference:** supported by multiple independent evidence streams.
- **IT — Interpretive Thesis:** higher-order explanation organizing several scenes.
- **SP — Speculation:** plausible but underdetermined.

The synthesis repeatedly distinguishes:

- character belief from series endorsement;
- mechanical result from relational result;
- recurrence from replay;
- admiration from consent;
- intensity from reciprocity;
- visual similarity from fixed symbolic meaning.

# XII. Short glossary

## Revue

A heightened theatrical performance in which a relationship or self-conception becomes performable and therefore answerable. A Revue may have mechanical rules, but its decisive outcome can be emotional, epistemic, relational, symbolic, or musical rather than simply competitive.

## Position Zero / ポジション・ゼロ

A stage coordinate that develops from singular institutional center into certification, shared threshold, insufficient ending condition, and nonfinal punctuation.

## `キラめき`

Radiance: artistic vitality, desire, visibility, affective access to performance, and an energy the audition can extract but relationships can re-produce. The corpus supports conceptual plurality rather than one simple physics model.

## `再演`

Replay / re-performance. Most importantly associated with Nana's effort to preserve a precious known stage through recurrence under her authorship.

## `再生産`

Re-production: continuity through transformed creation. The object expands across the corpus from radiance, to destiny/stage, to the self.

## `次の舞台`

The next stage: not merely another institution or role, but the next performance a changing person can enter without the previous role dictating the result.

## *Starlight*

At least three layers:

1. the inherited internal play;
2. Karen/Hikari's `私達のスタァライト`;
3. the broader artistic practice of transforming life and relationship into stage.

## Theatrical death

The ending of a role or relationship form that can no longer generate a future. It is not automatically biological death, failure, or relational annihilation.

## Generative emptiness

The temporary open state after a role has been completely performed and before the next role is known.

# XV. Core distinctions at a glance

The following distinctions prevent the most common interpretive collapses.

## Role versus person

A role can create a person without exhausting her.

The problem is not performance. It is the role claiming final jurisdiction.

## Dependence versus possession

People are genuinely made through one another.

Dependence becomes possessive when the needed person must remain in the role that makes her necessary.

## Memory versus archive

Memory carries influence forward.

Archive becomes coercive when it defines which past version counts as the real person.

## Rivalry versus hierarchy

Rivalry can generate another performance.

Hierarchy becomes finalizing when one result determines permanent worth.

## Repetition versus replay

Return, rehearsal, reprise, and another match are not automatically `再演`.

Replay becomes dangerous when recurrence requires the performer to remain the same.

## Ending versus annihilation

A role, stage, or relationship form can end while the person, relation, and inheritance remain meaningful.

## Audience versus ownership

The audience is necessary to theater.

Watching becomes ownership when the performer is required to repeat the image first loved.

## Autonomy versus isolation

Self-authorship does not mean becoming uncaused or socially independent.

It means retaining the ability to answer, revise, refuse, leave, and enter another role.

# XVI. Interpretive limitations

This corpus is intentionally deep rather than exhaustive in every possible sense.

It does not provide:

- a complete production-history account;
- exhaustive external reception history;
- staff interview synthesis;
- a formal musicological score edition;
- a shot-by-shot transcript of every episode;
- a definitive resolution of every Revue metaphysics question.

Its strongest evidentiary areas are:

- Japanese dialogue and recurring language;
- character/relationship development;
- performed voice;
- Revue music and dramaturgy;
- visual recurrence;
- TV-to-film revision;
- credits/post-credit continuity.

Where the animated corpus remains ambiguous, the synthesis preserves ambiguity rather than filling it from outside franchise knowledge.

# XVIII. Final orientation

The television series asks:

> Can a finished tragedy produce another story?

The movie asks:

> Can a finished role produce another self?

The complete animated corpus answers both through the same operation:

> **Let the ending remain true. Carry what it made possible. Refuse its claim to be the only possible next form.**

That is why *Revue Starlight* can end at an ordinary audition.

No cosmic guarantee remains.

Karen can pass.

Karen can fail.

Another stage still exists.

The deepest final proposition is not that the stage never ends.

It is:

> **the performer can survive the curtain.**
