# 星見純那と大場なな
## Memory, Replay, and Authorship
### Loving the past without assigning the next role

# I. Executive thesis

Junna Hoshimi and Nana Daiba form the complete animated corpus's most sustained study of **the ethics of knowing another person**.

At first, they appear to be an asymmetrical pair built from familiar contrasts:

- Junna is anxious, ambitious, verbal, analytical, and still climbing;
- Nana is warm, capable, observant, practical, and apparently secure;
- Junna wants to become protagonist;
- Nana appears content to support and create for others.

The television series gradually reveals that the apparent security belongs to the more dangerous character.

Nana has watched more.

She remembers more.

She has repeated the year.

She has accumulated versions of everyone that the others themselves do not remember living.

Her intimacy is therefore structurally one-sided. Nana knows the girls across repetitions; they know only the current Nana.

This asymmetry gives her enormous tenderness and enormous epistemic power.

She can anticipate needs, preserve habits, feed everyone, photograph them, remember lines, and produce the stage they love.

She can also decide that she knows:

- which stage was best;
- which configuration was most beautiful;
- which Junna is “my Junna”;
- which future would betray the person she loved watching.

The TV series first frames Nana's problem at the scale of time.

Her `私の再演` converts collective creation into privately governed recurrence. She wants to protect:

- the first 99th-class *Starlight*;
- the people she loves;
- the happiness she fears cannot be recreated.

Episode 9 breaks the supernatural replay by teaching her that:

> `同じ私達も 同じ舞台もない`.

Junna offers:

> `私達で 次のスタァライト`.

The television resolution is real. Nana accepts that the past can become material for another shared performance rather than a world everyone must inhabit again.

The movie then discovers the next contradiction hidden inside that success.

Nana can accept change conceptually and still fail to recognize it when the person changing is Junna.

She has aestheticized a particular Junna:

- foolish;
- passionate;
- reaching toward a protagonist role she may never obtain;
- beautiful because the star remains painfully distant.

Nana says:

> `君は 美しかった`
>
> `君は眩しかったよ 星見純那`.

Junna catches the decisive wound:

> `どうして 過去形なのよ？`

The relationship's central violence is grammatical.

Nana has already moved Junna into the archive.

Photographs surround her.

The camera/replay logic returns, now not as a repeated year but as a gaze that recognizes the living girl through a completed image.

Nana eventually says:

> `私の純那ちゃんは そんな役じゃない！`
>
> `私の純那ちゃんじゃない`.

This is the intimate residue of `私の再演`.

The world is no longer looping.

Nana's interpretation of Junna is.

Junna's counter-development occurs through language.

She begins as the girl who borrows the authority of writers and aphorisms. The quotations are not empty decoration. They genuinely gave her concepts and courage.

But Nana's attack forces the distinction between:

- words that support selfhood;
- words that substitute for self-authorship.

Junna finally says:

> `他人の言葉じゃ だめ！`

Then produces language nobody can lend her:

> `届かぬ 足りぬはもう飽きた`
>
> `足掻いて 藻掻いて 主役を食らう`.

She rejects the assigned role:

> `あなたに与えられた役なんか 要らない`

and claims:

> `私の道は 私が 切り開く！`

Her `折れないペン` does not mean literature has been abandoned.

It means the reader has become author.

The Revue ends by repairing tense and temporality.

Past:

> `眩しかった`.

Present:

> `眩しい主役 星見純那だ！`

Future:

> `いつかまた 新しい舞台で`.

Finally Nana looks again and says:

> `やっぱり… 眩しい`.

The complete relationship arc is therefore:

> **ambition observed → firstness preserved → time privately authored → change accepted in principle → beloved person archived → assigned role refused → memory retained without jurisdiction.**

Its mature form is not forgetting.

It is not detachment.

It is:

> **loving what the photographs preserve while accepting that the living performer does not owe the archive another audition.**

# II. Junna — ambition as a fight against interchangeability

## 1. The desire to be protagonist is a personhood claim

Junna's early desire can be misread as generic competitiveness.

It is more existential.

She wants:

> `私だけの星`

and to be the protagonist of her own fate.

The center matters because Junna fears becoming:

- competent but replaceable;
- hardworking but unseen;
- the person who understands the rules without becoming the one the story is about.

Her glasses, bow, study habits, quotations, and precise speech all support a self built through deliberate mediation.

Junna rarely trusts unformed desire to carry authority by itself.

She prepares.

She names sources.

She cites.

She aims from distance.

The bow is a fitting weapon not merely because it belongs to an intellectual archetype. It makes ambition visible as:

- target;
- trajectory;
- calculation;
- the painful space between present self and desired star.

## 2. Episode 2 — one-chance ontology

Junna's early danger lies in treating one defeat as final judgment upon personhood.

If this stage determines who gets to be protagonist, then losing may mean:

> I was never the person I tried to become.

Karen's intervention is therefore not an argument against Junna's ambition.

It is an argument against its temporal compression:

> `私達は 何度だって舞台に立てる`.

The series does not tell Junna to want less.

It gives her more time in which wanting can become method rather than emergency.

This matters enormously in the film.

Her later choice to study theater is not surrender.

It is Episode 2's anti-finality transformed into adult strategy.

## 3. Quotation as scaffold

Junna's quotations are sometimes used for comedy, but the full corpus treats them seriously.

They perform several functions:

- give shape to anxiety;
- make historical company available;
- turn emotion into argument;
- borrow confidence from language already tested by others;
- let Junna speak before she possesses a fully original formulation.

The problem is not that quotation is fake.

The problem appears when Junna must answer a question no quotation can answer for her:

> Who are you now, when the person who knows you best says your radiance belongs to the past?

At that point, even a quotation about self-creation cannot create Junna.

## 4. Junna's voice

Junna's lexical voice is among the most explicitly authored in the class.

It is:

- precise;
- explanatory;
- academically inflected;
- quick to name a source;
- capable of turning emotion into proposition.

Her performed voice often maintains control even under stress until a specific relational wound breaks the structure.

The movie's:

> `殺してみせろよ 大場なな！`

is therefore powerful partly because it departs so sharply from her ordinary register.

The roughness is not Junna becoming less intellectual.

It is Junna discovering a form of speech not guaranteed by borrowed authority.

The literary girl gains access to:

- anger;
- swagger;
- self-authored theatrical violence.

Her voice expands rather than changes species.

# III. Nana — care, firstness, and the desire to make time hospitable

## 1. Nana is not secretly uncaring

The replay becomes ethically disturbing because the care it grows from is genuine.

Nana cooks.

Nana watches.

Nana creates.

Nana remembers.

She understands that performance is not only the star under light but:

- costume;
- script;
- rehearsal;
- food;
- timing;
- morale;
- backstage work;
- the social environment in which others can perform.

This makes Nana one of the corpus's most complete theater people.

It also gives her the practical capacity to turn protection into administration.

Her danger is not indifference to others' needs.

It is confidence that care gives her enough knowledge to determine them.

## 2. The first 99th-class *Starlight*

The first production gives Nana something she had not possessed:

- collective authorship;
- irreplaceable firstness;
- belonging achieved through work;
- a social role in which her many capacities cohere.

Nana does not merely love the show as audience.

She loves the person the production let her be.

That is why the ending is so threatening.

If the stage ends, the social self born through it may also seem temporary.

Replay becomes an attempt to preserve both:

- everyone;
- Nana's place among everyone.

## 3. Nana's ordinary voice and performed warmth

Nana's baseline voice is warm, rounded, gently accommodating, and socially smoothing.

Her repeated food language and `ばなな` identity make care audible as everyday rhythm.

This warmth is not a mask in the simple sense.

It is real.

The disturbing discovery of Episode 7 is that the same voice can coexist with absolute hidden control.

Nana does not need to become vocally cold in order to become dangerous.

This is one reason her preservationism is more unsettling than overt domination.

The coercion can sound like comfort because Nana sincerely believes the world she is building is safe.

## 4. Camera, photograph, and epistemic power

Nana's camera is not only a motif of nostalgia.

It gives her a mode of knowing.

The photograph says:

> this happened;
> this person looked like this;
> this moment can be retained.

That is valuable.

The danger begins when the image shifts from evidence of a past person into a standard against which the present person is judged.

The camera can preserve:

> who Junna was.

It cannot establish:

> who Junna is allowed to become.

The movie Hunt Revue makes this conflict literal by surrounding Junna with photographs while Nana speaks of her beauty in past tense.

# IV. Episode 7 — `私の再演` and the privatization of a shared stage

## 1. From `私達のスタァライト` to `私の再演`

The possessive shift is the ethical center of Nana's television arc.

The first production was:

> something everyone made.

The replay is:

> something Nana governs for everyone.

This distinction is why good intention cannot justify the system.

Nana says:

> `私の再演の中にいれば 何も怖くない`.

The safety is real in one sense.

The girls will not confront the unknown future Nana fears.

But safety requires:

- one rememberer;
- one author;
- one person permitted to know the recurrence;
- everyone else living inside a world whose terms they did not choose.

The shared stage has become benevolent sovereignty.

## 2. Firstness cannot be technically reproduced

Nana's replay is doomed even before Hikari disrupts it.

The event's firstness included:

- uncertainty;
- risk;
- people becoming something they had not predicted;
- Nana herself not knowing the result.

Once Nana remembers, the structure changes.

The external world may recur.

Nana's position inside it does not.

The replay therefore preserves scenery while altering relation.

This is the series' deepest criticism of nostalgia:

> **the more completely one controls the return, the less the return can possess the openness that made the original alive.**

## 3. Junna's position inside Nana's replay

Junna is especially important because Nana's love for the group is not emotionally uniform.

The later `私の純那ちゃん` reveals an intimacy in which Nana's observation of Junna is unusually charged.

Even before the movie, the television series shows that Nana knows versions of Junna that Junna does not know Nana has seen.

This creates the potential for an asymmetrical claim:

> I know what you are really like because I have watched more of you than you remember becoming.

Episode 9 begins correcting that asymmetry by forcing Nana to become visible in fear rather than only as observer and caretaker.

# V. Episode 9 — one-sided intimacy becomes co-authorship

## 1. `私の純那ちゃん`

The phrase first appears around the unfamiliar nickname and relationship configuration.

Nana objects that “my Junna” is not called that.

The moment can be funny, jealous, affectionate, and structurally revealing at once.

Nana possesses an internal model of Junna.

A new form of Junna can feel not merely surprising but incorrect.

The television episode does not yet take this problem to the film's full severity.

It plants the exact language the film later makes explicit.

## 2. Junna knows less of Nana than Nana knows of Junna

The relationship's hidden imbalance becomes visible when Junna recognizes how much Nana has watched and how little Nana has allowed herself to be known in return.

Nana's role as caretaker and observer gives the appearance of intimacy without equivalent exposure.

This is not fake relationship.

It is incomplete reciprocity.

Junna's importance lies in making Nana perform the fear underneath “everyone's Banana.”

The person who had governed the stage becomes someone who can be frightened, uncertain, and reached.

## 3. `同じ私達も 同じ舞台もない`

This line is the television series' best direct answer to replay.

It does not say:

> the past is gone and therefore meaningless.

It says:

> even if the external stage returns, the people occupying it are not the same.

The insight protects both memory and change.

Nana's replay is not wrong because repetition is impossible in a technical sense.

It is wrong because it demands that changed people be interpreted as the same `私達`.

## 4. Junna's invitation

> `私達で 次のスタァライト`.

Junna does not ask Nana to stop caring about the first production.

She asks Nana to move from:

- private author of recurrence;

to:

- participant in another shared creation.

This is a relationship offer as much as an artistic one.

Junna says, in effect:

> do not preserve me from outside;
> come make something with the person I am becoming.

## 5. Television resolution

The TV resolution produces several genuine changes:

- Nana accepts that the first stage cannot be first again;
- she permits the 100th *Starlight* to proceed;
- she becomes capable of present-tense caregiving;
- she joins the new nine-person story;
- Junna becomes someone who can contradict Nana rather than merely be watched by her.

The film does not erase these.

It finds the remaining point where the old structure still survives:

> Nana's aesthetic certainty about Junna.

# VI. OVA evidence — ordinary authorship after replay

The OVAs do not radically alter Junna/Nana, but they stabilize several important interpretations.

## 1. Junna's language becomes increasingly her own

OVA02 gives Junna space to articulate an insight about unequal stage time and radiance without immediately hiding behind a named authority.

The point is not that she no longer reads or quotes.

It is that her reading has begun producing formulations that belong to Junna.

The trajectory toward the movie's `折れないペン` is already visible.

## 2. Nana's care can exist in present tense

OVA03's practical caregiving is not replay.

Hikari is nervous now.

Nana offers help to this present situation rather than resetting the world to a prior ideal.

This matters because the film's later conflict should not be read as proof that all Nana care is secretly preservationist.

Nana has genuinely learned present-tense care.

Her specific blind spot concerns the beloved image of Junna.

## 3. Their relationship is embedded in the ensemble

The OVAs also prevent Junna/Nana from becoming a sealed dyad.

Both girls have:

- other friends;
- institutional roles;
- Class A/B responsibilities;
- different forms of knowledge;
- participation in group care.

This broader ecology is what makes their movie separation survivable.

They can say `またね` because neither falls into social nothingness when the pair's old form ends.

---

# VII. Movie career paths — the conflict appears before the Revue

Junna chooses further study.

Nana remains divided between performing and creating.

These choices already place them in different temporal positions.

## 1. Junna's route is anti-finality made institutional

Junna acknowledges that Maya and Claudine currently exceed her.

She does not conclude:

> therefore I am not a Stage Girl.

She chooses:

- time;
- study;
- analysis;
- a longer route toward performance.

The key qualification:

> `今は`

protects the future from being collapsed into present ranking.

This is Episode 2 fully metabolized.

## 2. Nana's route is multiple rather than absent

Nana loves:

- making stage;
- standing on stage.

The indecision is not refusal of change.

It is an inability to reduce a more complex artistic self to one career category.

The later credits' Royal Academy route is appropriate because it preserves exploration rather than prematurely finalizing the answer.

## 3. Why Nana misreads Junna

Nana sees Junna's study choice and hears:

> retreat from the beautiful impossible reaching that once defined her.

The interpretation contains real concern.

It also contains aesthetic possession.

Nana loved Junna's distance from the protagonist position.

That distance made Junna's struggle visible and moving.

If Junna changes the route, Nana risks losing the performance she loved watching.

This is why the movie makes Nana the antagonist of Junna's future without making her indifferent to Junna's success.

She loves Junna intensely.

That intensity has become the problem.

# VIII. The Revue of the Hunt

## 1. Nana offers a beautiful end

Nana frames Junna as:

- ripening fruit;
- a performer whose hope has been extinguished by distance;
- someone approaching fall;
- someone Nana can grant `美しい最期`.

This is preservationism transformed rather than repeated unchanged.

TV Nana refused ending.

Movie Nana accepts ending—but initially wants to author its aesthetic form.

The underlying claim remains:

> I know what fidelity to the Junna I love should look like.

## 2. Hunter and prey refuse stable assignment

Junna names the confrontation the Revue of the Hunt and threatens to capture Nana.

The stage cages Nana and turns the apparent executioner into prey.

This destabilization matters because the true object of the hunt is not one body.

It is the role each girl assigns the other.

Nana casts:

> beautiful doomed underdog.

Junna initially casts:

> enemy to defeat with inherited language.

Both roles must fail before the relationship can move.

## 3. The word-world and its collapse

Junna's quotations become stage architecture.

Words are not merely spoken.

They build the world in which Junna moves.

This makes the later collapse more than a rhetorical change.

The inherited textual structure physically ceases to carry her.

Nana says:

> `響かない`
>
> `感じない`
>
> `届かない`.

The critique is cruel and partly correct.

Junna cannot answer Nana's intimate misrecognition by proving that Goethe or Hesse endorsed perseverance.

No authority can tell Nana who present Junna is.

Junna must perform that person.

## 4. `君は 美しかった`

The Revue's most violent strike contains no weapon.

Nana's admiration is an elegy.

She describes Junna's old striving as:

- foolish;
- hot;
- desperate;
- beautiful;
- dazzling.

The line reveals what Nana loved:

> not only Junna,
> but Junna as the person who reaches toward a lead role Nana believes she cannot obtain.

This is why the praise becomes confinement.

Junna's inadequacy has become part of the aesthetic object.

If Junna finds a different route, the distance that made her beautiful by Nana's old criteria may change.

## 5. The photographs

The photographic field materializes archival intimacy.

Nana has evidence of Junna.

She has moments, expressions, versions.

The images are not lies.

The ethical error is using truthful fragments of the past to legislate the whole future.

The question:

> `あなた今まで何 見てたの？`

therefore attacks more than attention.

Junna asks whether Nana's extensive observation amounted to recognition.

A person can be watched in extraordinary detail and still not be seen when the observer's model becomes too complete to update.

## 6. `他人の言葉じゃ だめ！`

Junna's break with quotation is often misunderstood if read as anti-intellectual revelation.

She does not reject literature.

She rejects substitution.

The lines that follow retain:

- poetic cadence;
- metaphor;
- star language;
- literary compression.

Junna has internalized the tradition enough to write with it rather than only cite it.

Her declaration:

> `届かぬ 足りぬはもう飽きた`

rejects the deficit grammar through which she and Nana have both understood her.

Then:

> `足掻いて 藻掻いて 主役を食らう`.

The underdog does not wait for recognition.

She becomes hungry enough to consume the role.

## 7. `殺してみせろよ 大場なな！`

The roughness is a vocal event.

Junna has historically protected authority through:

- polish;
- citation;
- correctness.

The challenge sounds like nobody else's permission.

It does not mean literal suicidal desire.

Nana has offered the death of Junna's present role.

Junna answers:

> if you believe this role has no future, confront the living performer who refuses your ending.

## 8. Borrowed sword and unbreakable pen

Junna temporarily uses Nana's katana.

Nana asks for her sword back.

The visual rhyme with borrowed language is exact enough to support a strong inference.

Junna can borrow:

- words;
- weapon;
- tradition;
- method.

Borrowing is not shameful.

It cannot finally establish authorship.

Her `折れないペン` names the instrument whose authority comes from Junna's ability to write the present self.

## 9. `私の純那ちゃん`

Nana's panic reveals the deepest relationship script:

> `私の純那ちゃんは そんな役じゃない！`

> `私の純那ちゃんじゃない`.

The phrase is not merely romantic possessiveness or jealousy.

It is a theory of authenticity:

> the real Junna plays the role Nana recognizes.

Junna's answer is therefore exact:

> `あなたに与えられた役なんか 要らない`.

The issue is not whether Nana's remembered Junna was real.

The issue is whether Nana gets casting authority.

## 10. `私の道は 私が 切り開く`

Junna's road is not simply one available institutional choice.

She claims authorship of trajectory.

The stage Nana prepared is cut away.

This is the moment the relationship becomes capable of continuing.

Nana cannot meet the next Junna if she controls the set in which Junna must appear.

## 11. Present protagonist

> `この舞台の私は 今`
>
> `眩しい主役 星見純那だ！`

Junna does not claim objective supremacy over Maya or Claudine.

She claims present subjecthood.

This is Episode 2's mature resolution.

Junna can be:

- currently less experienced;
- still studying;
- still reaching;
- protagonist now.

Protagonist status stops being a scarce casting outcome and becomes authorship of one's relation to the current stage.


## 12. `私の再演は 今`

Nana's recognition that her replay may have ended “now” should not be interpreted as evidence that she secretly resumed the literal time loop.

The stronger reading is psychological and relational.

The supernatural recurrence ended in television.

The interpretive recurrence survived.

Nana kept replaying:

> the Junna whose beauty she already understood.

The movie therefore gives `再演` a final intimate scale.

The repeated object is no longer the year.

It is Nana's preferred reading of one person.

When Junna exceeds that reading, Nana's replay can finally end.

## 13. `またね`

The farewell is modest after the Revue's enormous violence.

That modesty is the point.

Nana's old “again” meant:

> return to the same stage under preserved conditions.

`またね` means:

> another encounter after uncontrolled time.

The girls do not guarantee:

- same institution;
- same role;
- same relationship hierarchy;
- same emotional state.

They guarantee openness to meeting.

This is the repaired form of recurrence.

## 14. `眩しい`

Nana's final present-tense correction completes the Revue.

Earlier:

> `眩しかった`.

Finally:

> `やっぱり… 眩しい`.

Nana does not renounce the old Junna.

She updates.

The living performer becomes visible again.

This is what the entire relationship needed from the beginning:

> not less looking,
> but a gaze capable of changing its mind.

# IX. Credits and post-film futures

The credits make the film's relational solution concrete.

Junna's initial university plan does not remain final.

She studies at the New York Musical & Drama Academy.

Nana goes to the Royal Academy of Theatre.

The details matter for several reasons.

## 1. Their opening plans were not destiny

The career interviews were self-authored statements, not prophecies.

A plan can be sincere and later change without becoming a lie.

This is exactly what the Revue teaches about roles.

Junna's route remains consistent with her desire for study and growth while becoming more directly theatrical.

Nana's route preserves her refusal to reduce herself too early to either:

- performer;
- creator.

The academy becomes a place where the binary can remain open.

## 2. Firstness returns without replay

Junna's future includes another first stage.

This is a direct answer to Nana's old firstness anxiety.

The original first stage cannot be re-created.

Another first can occur.

Firstness is not one historical object Nana must preserve.

It is a renewable quality of genuinely new encounter.

## 3. Separate futures prove the relationship's maturity

The girls do not need to enter the same institution to validate `またね`.

Their future relation is carried through:

- memory;
- artistic influence;
- possible reunion;
- the fact that each changed the other's self-conception.

The pair becomes portable without becoming abstract.

# X. Memory, archive, and the ethics of preservation

## 1. Memory is not the villain

The corpus repeatedly values memory.

Karen/Hikari's promise matters because both remember.

Nana's photographs matter because they testify to a real past.

The first *Starlight* matters because it happened once.

An anti-memory reading would contradict the entire work.

The danger is not preservation itself.

It is the shift from:

> this happened;

to:

> therefore this must happen again.

Or from:

> this is who you were;

to:

> therefore this is the role that counts as the real you.

## 2. Archive versus prison

The photographs remain after the movie Revue.

They are not burned.

This is the correct visual resolution.

The archive survives.

Its authority does not.

A healthy archive can:

- preserve evidence;
- carry grief;
- sustain gratitude;
- make influence visible;
- help later performers understand origin.

An unhealthy archive becomes prison when it:

- closes interpretation;
- replaces the current person;
- treats change as betrayal;
- gives the rememberer jurisdiction over the remembered.

## 3. Nana as intimate audience

Nana occupies several roles simultaneously:

- friend;
- admirer;
- photographer;
- creator;
- audience;
- director;
- protector.

The movie's critique of Nana is therefore also a critique of audiences who love a performer most intensely in one role.

The ethical question becomes:

> **Can the audience allow the performer to become less legible by the criteria through which she was first loved?**

Nana's eventual `眩しい` says yes.

But the answer requires grief.

## 4. Love relinquishing expertise

The most difficult kind of love in this corpus is not love that knows nothing.

It is love that knows a great deal and still relinquishes the claim to final expertise.

Nana's intimacy with Junna is deep.

The film does not deny it.

It asks Nana to accept:

> the person I know profoundly retains the right to become someone my knowledge cannot predict.

That is the ethical maturation of the relationship.

# XI. Reading, quotation, and authorship

## 1. Junna does not outgrow books

The film's self-authorship should not be flattened into a romantic cult of spontaneous originality.

Junna's own lines remain shaped by reading.

Her language retains:

- aphoristic compression;
- literary rhythm;
- metaphor;
- philosophical abstraction;
- familiarity with inherited forms.

The transformation is not:

> other people's words → pure unmediated self.

It is:

> citation → internalization → authorship.

## 2. The difference between source and authority

A source can give Junna:

- concepts;
- courage;
- companionship across time;
- language for problems she could not name.

Authority becomes dangerous when Junna uses the source to avoid taking responsibility for the present statement.

The movie's `他人の言葉じゃ だめ` means:

> no one else can be answerable for this declaration.

The final line must be Junna's because Junna must bear what it does to the relationship.

## 3. The pen and the series' larger theory of inherited script

Junna's pen repeats the corpus's treatment of *Starlight* itself.

The inherited play is not discarded.

It becomes material for:

- revision;
- sequel;
- new casting;
- another text.

Junna's literary development mirrors the series' theatrical development.

Both move from:

> faithful performance of inherited authority

into:

> responsible authorship using inherited material.

# XII. Voice, music, and staged relationship

The full technical treatment belongs in Document 09. Several pair-specific observations are essential here.

## 1. Junna's vocal rupture

The shift from attributed quotation to:

> `殺してみせろよ 大場なな！`

is not only lexical.

It is performed as a release from Junna's habitual mediation.

The roughness marks:

- anger;
- risk;
- self-ownership;
- refusal to remain the admired underdog.

The later return to calm `またね` shows that authorship does not require permanent aggression.

Junna can soften after her right to define herself has been theatrically established.

## 2. Nana's vocal collapse

Nana begins the Hunt Revue as poetic executioner.

She appears to possess the ending.

As Junna refuses the role, Nana's speech changes into panic:

> stop;
> lower the curtain;
> do not stand again.

Then `私の純那ちゃん` emerges.

This is the moment the warm caretaker and hidden sovereign become one audible person.

Nana's control is no longer abstract philosophy.

It is fear of losing the Junna her love already knows how to recognize.

## 3. Music and past tense

The Revue's active hunt gives way to a contracted, elegiac field around Nana's admiration before Junna explicitly asks why the tense is past.

The music begins burying Junna before the dialogue names the funeral.

This is a major “music knows first” case.

## 4. Music and self-authorship

When Junna says:

> `他人の言葉じゃ だめ！`

music expands dramatically.

The stage gives its greatest force not to the perfect quotation but to the refusal to keep searching for one.

## 5. Music contradicts Nana's curtain

Nana begs:

> `もう幕を降ろして！`

The composition is near maximal intensity.

The score refuses her authority to end the Revue.

Only after Junna destroys the assigned stage does the music withdraw enough for `またね`.

The correct curtain is not the one Nana imposes.

It is the one both girls can reach after recognition.

# XIII. Romantic and yuri coding

## 1. Explicit relationship language

The animated corpus explicitly gives Junna/Nana:

- intense attention;
- jealousy;
- possessive `私の純那ちゃん`;
- an invitation to make the next *Starlight* together;
- mutual tears;
- a pair-specific movie Revue;
- the promise of meeting again on another stage.

## 2. Strong yuri-compatible evidence

The relationship is strongly compatible with a yuri reading because it is:

- emotionally privileged;
- aesthetically charged;
- possessive;
- mutually transformative;
- organized around being uniquely seen;
- threatened by the possibility of another version of the beloved.

Nana's gaze toward Junna and Junna's ability to reach the vulnerable Nana carry intimacy beyond generic classmates.

## 3. Limits

The corpus does not provide a conventional explicit romantic confession or formal couple status.

Its principal vocabulary is:

- bonds;
- watching;
- “my Junna”;
- next *Starlight*;
- roles;
- brightness;
- future stage.

The most disciplined conclusion is:

> **Junna/Nana is a strongly yuri-coded, emotionally intimate relationship whose explicit dramatic grammar is memory, admiration, authorship, and the right to become unfamiliar.**

The movie also critiques the possessive form of that intimacy. A romantic reading should not convert `私の純那ちゃん` into uncomplicated proof of healthy exclusivity.

# XV. Contradiction audit

## Strongest supporting evidence

1. `私の再演` transforms shared production into private authorship.
2. `私の純那ちゃん` recurs from television into the movie.
3. Photographs surround Junna during Nana's past-tense admiration.
4. Junna explicitly objects to `眩しかった` as past tense.
5. `他人の言葉じゃ だめ` marks the shift from citation to authorship.
6. Junna rejects `あなたに与えられた役` and Nana's prepared stage.
7. Nana identifies the end of `私の再演` and corrects `眩しかった` to `眩しい`.
8. `またね` preserves relationship without pre-scripting reunion.

## Strongest complicating evidence

1. Nana's criticism of Junna's borrowed language and possible self-deception is partly correct.
2. Junna's self-authored declaration remains theatrically extreme and does not prove her career plan will succeed.
3. Nana's intimate knowledge has often genuinely helped Junna and the ensemble.
4. The film's credits still place both girls inside prestigious institutions; self-authorship remains institutionally mediated.
5. `私の純那ちゃん` also carries affection and comic intimacy, not only domination.

## Plausible alternative reading

A serious alternative is:

> Nana correctly perceives that Junna is rationalizing a retreat from professional performance; the Revue's aggression forces Junna to recover ambition, so Nana functions less as possessive archivist than as the critic who sees through Junna's diminished self-conception.

This reading has support:

- Junna admits present inadequacy;
- she relies heavily on quotations;
- the Revue produces a more aggressive artistic self;
- the credits revise her route toward a more directly theatrical academy.

## Why the present thesis remains stronger

Nana's critique is indeed partly correct, but the relationship problem cannot be reduced to career advice.

The decisive evidence is:

- past tense;
- photographs;
- `私の純那ちゃん`;
- `そんな役じゃない`;
- Nana's plea that Junna stop standing;
- Junna's explicit rejection of the role Nana assigns.

The film gives Nana useful perception and possessive overreach simultaneously.

Junna becomes more herself not because Nana correctly defines the right role, but because the conflict makes it impossible for Junna to keep borrowing either:

- authors' authority;
- Nana's image of her.

The strongest synthesis is therefore:

> **Nana's criticism catalyzes Junna's authorship, but Nana must relinquish the right to determine what that authorship should produce.**

# XVII. Final synthesis

Junna begins by wanting a star she can call her own.

Nana begins by making a stage everyone can call theirs.

The relationship becomes dangerous when Nana's ability to remember and create turns:

> ours

into:

> mine.

First:

> `私達のスタァライト`.

Then:

> `私の再演`.

The television series breaks the loop and gives them a new proposition:

> `私達で 次のスタァライト`.

That resolution is true.

The movie then asks whether Nana can apply it to the person she has watched most intensely.

Nana looks at Junna and says:

> you were beautiful.

The compliment becomes a funeral.

Junna hears the tense.

The photographs appear.

The person who preserved time has preserved Junna inside one aesthetic truth:

> foolish, passionate, desperate, reaching, dazzling.

Junna tries the words that always helped.

They do not reach.

She finally stops looking for the sentence another author can lend her.

> `他人の言葉じゃ だめ！`

Then the literary girl writes herself.

She is no longer content to remain beautiful because the protagonist is distant.

She will devour the protagonist.

She rejects the role Nana gives.

She cuts away Nana's prepared stage.

She names the present Junna:

> `眩しい主役 星見純那だ！`

The past is not destroyed.

The photographs remain.

Nana's love remains.

What ends is the archive's jurisdiction.

Nana recognizes:

> perhaps my replay ends now.

They do not promise to remain together in the same form.

They say:

> `またね`.

Another meeting.

After time.

After change.

After neither knows exactly who will arrive.

Then Nana looks again.

Not at the photograph.

At Junna.

> `やっぱり… 眩しい`.

That present tense is the mature form of their relationship.

It does not love less.

It knows less possessively.

And because Nana no longer knows the ending in advance, Junna can finally return someday as someone worth meeting again.

# XVIII. Supplemental synthesis — two forms of intimate spectatorship

Junna/Nana becomes even clearer when placed beside Maya/Claudine without collapsing the pairs.

Nana and Claudine are both unusually intimate audiences.

Both believe they can see something in the beloved performer that ordinary viewers cannot.

Both use exclusivist language.

Both want another person's radiance intensely.

The difference lies in the future each gaze permits.

## Nana's gaze

Nana's gaze begins from preservation.

It says:

> I know the performance in which you were most beautiful.

The danger is retrospective authority.

New Junna is evaluated through old Junna.

The unknown becomes evidence of decline.

## Claudine's gaze

Claudine's film gaze begins from dissatisfaction with the completed Maya she already knows.

It says:

> show me the part your perfect performance has not yet exposed.

The danger is invasive possessiveness.

But the direction is future-producing rather than archival.

## Why the distinction matters

The corpus does not divide:

- watching = bad;
- performing = good.

It distinguishes gazes by what they do to the performer's future.

A gaze becomes preservational when it asks:

> perform again the self I already know how to love.

A gaze becomes generative when it asks:

> become someone whose next performance may change what I thought I knew.

Nana reaches the generative form only at the end, when `眩しかった` becomes `眩しい` and `再演` becomes `またね`.

Her final gaze is not weaker.

It is less certain.

That uncertainty is the relationship's ethical achievement.

# XIX. Six-outcome summary of the complete Junna/Nana arc

## Mechanical

Junna loses her early TV Revue but survives it as Stage Girl. Nana's replay is broken in TV09. In the movie, Junna destroys Nana's prepared stage and refuses Nana's ending; the result is not a conventional Position Zero hierarchy.

## Emotional

Junna moves from fear of insufficiency through the pain of being mourned in advance into anger, authored pride, and quiet future openness. Nana moves from protective certainty through panic and grief into present-tense admiration.

## Epistemic

Junna learns that inherited language can support but not answer for her. Nana learns that extensive memory does not guarantee current recognition.

## Relational

Observer/observed becomes performer/performer. “My Junna” becomes two named people capable of separate next stages and another meeting.

## Symbolic/theatrical

Camera, photograph, quotation, borrowed katana, pen, curtain, and road turn questions of knowledge and authorship into visible stage machinery.

## Musical

The score contracts around Nana's elegy, expands when Junna rejects borrowed words, contradicts Nana's demand for the curtain, and withdraws only after self-authorship has made a legitimate ending possible.

This six-part outcome clarifies why the Revue cannot be summarized as:

> Junna proves Nana wrong.

Nana is right that Junna's current language is inadequate.

Junna is right that Nana's image cannot define the adequate replacement.

The relationship advances because each girl's partial truth becomes insufficient without the other's contradiction.
