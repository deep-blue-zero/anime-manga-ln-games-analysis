# 『少女☆歌劇 レヴュースタァライト』
## Japanese Dialogue, Lyric, and Motif Index
### Language-first retrieval index

# I. Purpose, source policy, and use

This file indexes short Japanese formulations whose wording, tense, pronoun, address, or recurrence materially affects the full-series interpretation.

It is a retrieval tool, not a replacement for the primary audiovisual scenes or the specialist documents.

Each entry identifies:

- the Japanese phrase;
- a concise working translation;
- speaker(s);
- source code;
- local function;
- major later transformation;
- primary analytical home.

## Source caution

The TV and film Japanese subtitle/transcript layers are high-value textual aids. Audio remains authoritative for timing, interruption, stress, pronunciation, and performed vocal state.

The available OVA subtitle layer is English. Exact OVA Japanese wording is not indexed unless the Japanese is confidently recoverable from audio.

## Translation caution

Working translations preserve analytical function rather than matching one official subtitle track.

Terms with deliberately broad ranges are often retained:

- `舞台` — stage, production, vocation, relational/theatrical site;
- `スタァ` — stylized “star” identity;
- `キラめき` — radiance, sparkle, artistic vitality;
- `役` — role, part, function, assigned identity;
- `再演` — replay/reenactment;
- `再生産` — re-production;
- `奪う` — take, seize, steal.

Source codes:

- `TV01–TV12`
- `OVA01–OVA03`
- `MOV-PRO`
- `MOV-M01–MOV-M08`
- `MOV-EPI`

# XVII. Translation and recurrence notes

## 1. `スタァ` versus `星`

`スタァ` is the stylized theatrical “star,” while `星` is the ordinary written star and the object within *Starlight*'s imagery. The corpus deliberately lets them overlap without making them identical.

## 2. `キラめき`

The mixed orthography is retained because the franchise term carries more than ordinary `煌めき`/sparkle. Translate contextually as radiance, sparkle, artistic vitality, or shine; do not force one English word in every scene.

## 3. `再生産`

Literal economic/social senses of reproduction are not the sole relevant meanings. In the corpus, “re-production” usefully preserves both:

- recurrence;
- transformed making-again.

## 4. `奪う`

Depending on context, this can mean take, seize, steal, or deprive. Its ethical force changes sharply between institutional extraction and reciprocal Revue offering.

## 5. `舞台`

Translate as stage when possible, but track whether it means:

- literal physical stage;
- performance opportunity;
- vocation;
- relationship-space;
- life in performance.

## 6. `役`

Translate as role/part. The corpus does not treat role as inherently false. The key question is whether the role remains revisable and answerable to the performer.

## 7. `眩しい`

Can mean dazzling, radiant, or too bright to look at. The visual/affective ambiguity is important: radiance inspires and can blind.

## 8. `また`

“Again” must be classified by form:

- replay;
- reunion;
- another match;
- another stage;
- another first.

Do not automatically map it to Nana's `再演`.

# XVIII. Index limitations and verification rule

This index prioritizes formulations already used as load-bearing evidence in the synthesis.

It is not a complete concordance of every subtitle line or lyric.

For publication-grade quotation:

1. return to the source audio and Japanese subtitle/dialogue layer;
2. confirm timing, speaker, and wording;
3. inspect surrounding lines;
4. distinguish spoken dialogue from lyric and onscreen text;
5. preserve uncertainty where the analysis-oriented movie ASS may contain nonofficial segmentation or labels.

The index should be updated if later source verification changes:

- punctuation;
- orthography;
- speaker allocation;
- title formatting;
- or exact lyric transcription.

Its governing principle is:

> **Exact language matters because small changes—`私` to `私達`, `眩しかった` to `眩しい`, role to person, memory to line—often carry the series' largest transformations.**
