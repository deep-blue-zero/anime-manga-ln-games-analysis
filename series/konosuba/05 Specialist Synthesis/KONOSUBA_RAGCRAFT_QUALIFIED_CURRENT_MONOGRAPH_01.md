## 1. Identity and office

Ragcraft / ラグクラフト names himself as Elroad's chancellor in MAIN10. Later he visibly changes into a black featureless form and identifies himself as a doppelganger and intelligence commander. That direct scene joins public official and exposed infiltrator. His separate disclosure during the DUST04 prison encounter supplies another situated act, not a second independent national infiltration (RG28-01, RG28-05, RG28-08).

Levi remains a separate prince with his own incentives and contrary decisions. Casino workers, police, guards, robbers and courtiers cannot all be assigned Ragcraft's intention merely because they operate in the same country. The claim that he maintained or repaired national finances deserves attention as an agent's account, while still requiring its attributed status.

## 2. Sequence and access

The public introduction combines deference, apparent command of a room and a reception argument from modest food to national poverty. Ragcraft initially mistakes Kazuma's brother address as the name of Prince Jatice. His claimed control of policy is then challenged sufficiently that he permits appeal to Levi. Subsequent dragon-risk encouragement and reward revision show policy being defended through selected burdens and national-necessity language (RG28-01–03).

The deal with the Demon Army becomes explicit; Aqua's false charges provoke his defense of the Demon King. Levi independently realigns despite his objections. Ragcraft then impersonates companions, takes a hostage and turns to revenge. DUST04 exposes how Zesta's bluff elicits disclosure in the prison sequence. These strands concern linked events with different focal access, not a multiplication of trials (RG28-04–09).

## 3. Values and a complicated occupational stake

His declared mission favors the Demon Army, but his career account values diligent work, financial repair and earned advancement. He describes repeated applications and decades of labor with little gambling, then admits having lost sight of the original spy purpose. This is substantial self-interpretation even though it is not a verified thirty-year employment audit (RG28-05).

The resulting grievance is not exhausted by a desire to kill protagonists. He wants to vent about work and thanks his captives for hearing him; he also wants Iris to suffer through the people she values. Public service, professional pride, factional loyalty and retaliatory enjoyment can coexist. None cancels the hostage threat or makes administrative achievement fictitious merely because the official is an infiltrator (RG28-04–06).

## 4. Affect and threatened self-command

Formal reception speech and restrained policy language are vulnerable to exposure, slander and loss of authority. He backs away during a forced audience, becomes openly upset defending the Demon King and reacts angrily when Zesta seems to know his identity. These are adverse cases for a perfectly composed master spy (RG28-02, RG28-04, RG28-08).

In captivity he shifts between explanation, gratitude, grievance and pleasure in anticipated helplessness. The transition can be understood as a threatened professional identity finding an audience and then a punitive outlet. A rival is straightforward frustration at a failed mission. Both remain plausible; the work-history material gives the first account extra specificity without proving his interior life beyond his speech.

## 5. Competence, institutions and bodily skill

Ragcraft can occupy office, direct officials, provide accommodation, negotiate policy, maintain a disguise and physically restrain Kazuma. The prison supplies keys, guards, confinement and a ward explained by Zesta. These are concrete institutional resources. Ward effects differ by location, as exorcism becomes effective after the door is passed; they are not an undifferentiated ability to suppress all magic (RG28-01, RG28-05, RG28-08–09).

His disguises are fallible. Ragcraft's impersonation of Kazuma lacks the behavior Darkness expects and includes an incongruous appeal that exposes the disguise. The captives escape through complementary biting, fire and rope work, with burnt hair and remaining handcuffs. His authority does not ensure universal compliance, perfect surveillance or secure detention. No source-given intelligence rating follows from either a successful disguise or a failed one (RG28-05–06).

## 6. Judgment, disclosure and error

The reception misidentification and prison revelation share a sensitivity to an apparently meaningful social cue. In prison Ragcraft interprets Zesta's accusation as knowledge of his secret. Zesta's quiet admission that he improvised from gambling resentment occurs outside Ragcraft's hearing. The error is not a knowingly chosen confession to a bluff he already recognizes (RG28-01, RG28-08).

His larger career account also presents competence becoming entangled with role absorption: work that makes an infiltrator successful can redirect what he defends. That candidate needs testing against ordinary career diligence, mission cover or self-excusing autobiography. The evidence supports the dilemma, not certainty that public service always overrides his faction or that every administrative act secretly serves the Demon King.

## 7. Relationships and separate interests

Levi credits the aid-cut proposal to Ragcraft but wants to avoid an arranged marriage for his own reasons. When Levi later reverses policy and apologizes, Ragcraft protests rather than controlling the outcome. Their relationship includes influence and conflict, not puppet and puppeteer (RG28-02, RG28-04, RG28-07).

Iris is a dangerous adversary and the intended recipient of emotional revenge. Kazuma and Darkness become captives with their own escape decisions; Darkness's fantasies do not authorize restraint. Dust is noticed only late in the prison encounter. Ragcraft's awkward departure after the belated recognition shows a local social failure, not a plan to target Dust from the outset. The better meal's apologetic meaning is Dust's guess, and Lolisa's claimed dream-service order is a bluff (RG28-05–06, RG28-08–09).

## 8. Ordinary labor and preferences

The reported career contains applications, promotions, responsibility, abstention from the local gambling economy and frustration at being treated as a chancellor while serving another mission. This is unusually rich occupational material for a late antagonist. It lets the monograph ask what the work has come to mean to him rather than using office as a generic villain costume (RG28-05).

Still, there is no watched full working day, household, friendship network, ordinary meal preference or independent national account. Public austerity is an argumentative choice in a reception, not proof of personal frugality everywhere. The creator's afterword speculation about decline without him is another plane, not a completed posthumous experiment (RG28-01, RG28-10).

## 9. Voice and impersonation

His public register explains, qualifies and invokes institutions. His exposed register retains explanation but gives more space to grievance and direct threat. Extended work-history speech differs from the rushed prison self-exposure. Voice fidelity must track counterpart, role and what he thinks has been discovered, rather than sprinkle formal expressions over an all-knowing monologue (RG28-01, RG28-05, RG28-08).

Imitating appearance or a companion's ordinary words does not reproduce relational conduct. The fake Kazuma fails partly because of what he does not do and what he asks of Darkness. The apparent Megumin encounter is his performance, not her sexual conduct. Printed disguise and speech cannot establish audio imitation accuracy or an unlimited ability to copy memory (RG28-05).

## 10. Comedy and harm

The spy who becomes an exhausted career administrator and the confessor fooled by a gambling priest's accusation create reversals rooted in specific goals. The comedy does not require the career to be false or the hostage situation harmless. His announced change from killing captives immediately to using them against Iris retains a serious object even when the scene permits an escape (RG28-05–09).

A serious transfer can retain institutional fluency, defensive affiliation, overdisclosure and revenge under threatened standing. It must translate only capacities explicitly supplied by a new scenario; a human administrator does not acquire shapechanging because the source character can do it. Neither transfer mode should quietly improve him into a neutral public servant or a flawlessly disciplined operative.

## 11. Contrary evidence and rival models

Pure mission discipline fails against disclosed identity, work absorption, emotional defense and missed opportunities for quiet control. Pure benevolent technocracy fails against risk shifting, hostage coercion and retaliatory enjoyment. Pure incompetence fails against office, influence and sustained administrative account. Pure impersonation mastery fails against the behavioral mismatch and captives' escape (RG28-01–09).

A conditional model can therefore separate role competence from social exposure and policy from personal retaliation. The strongest uncertainty is causal weighting: genuine professional investment, cover maintenance and self-justification can produce overlapping speech. A rule must remain falsifiable instead of naming all possible reactions as evidence of the same hidden pride.

## 12. Fate and responsible use

Kazuma hears a strike and later sees black liquid he identifies with Ragcraft; he does not watch the full encounter or detailed killing method. DUST04 later reports that Iris has destroyed him. Those observations and reports support an outcome without authorizing invented fight choreography. Levi's subsequent coercion of Lolisa is Levi's act; the fabricated sexual-debt story is Dust and Lolisa's tactic (RG28-07, RG28-09).

The differentiated public, captive and prison phases justify a scoped Tier3 attempt. They do not make duplicated events independent evidence, authenticate the entire career or complete the final simulation battery. New conditions may leave the next move underdetermined. A provisional generator must preserve that uncertainty along with actual errors and harms; source-known contrasts cannot award confidence, accuracy or evaluator readiness. The wider program remains open.
