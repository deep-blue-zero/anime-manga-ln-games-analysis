# Keele: the great wizard who cannot certify his beloved's happiness

## A reputation with a missing answer

Keele is the self-named lich who created the beginner dungeon near Axel. MAIN02 U01551-70 first presents a public account: an exceptional wizard falls in love with a noblewoman, becomes a national benefactor, receives a royal offer of a reward, and eventually abducts the woman and retreats underground. The account explicitly leaves both his original request66 and the eventual outcome68 unknown. That incompleteness matters. The publicly remembered villain is a story with an omitted decision, rather than a complete hostile biography that the analyst must simply reverse.

The present study returns to the known local Japanese EPUBs, including every paragraph and ruby occurrence in MAIN02 U015 and its preceding chapter. Later bounded references were received to distinguish the creator from his dungeon and from later characters who resemble him thematically. These are source-known readings by the same informed author, with no independent validation or new whole-edition credit. His unnamed beloved remains a separate subject whose mental life is not interchangeable with his testimony.

## Recognition is his decision

Kazuma and Aqua search a dead end for at least ten minutes U015272-273. The wall then opens from the other side274-275, and a voice asks whether a priest is present277. The travelers do not discover and successfully activate a hidden mechanism. The sequence gives the inhabitant an initiating contribution: the contact occurs when he recognizes an opportunity for the assistance he needs.

The room contains ordinary furniture281, a lamp that Kazuma lights286, and a robed skeletal host287. Keele greets them politely, correcting the greeting for his uncertainty about the outside time284. His cultivated manner is neither an attack nor proof of harmlessness in every possible situation. No present combat occurs. A dungeon creator's reported military past and his current encounter with visitors must remain different evidence channels.

## Taking possession of the villain's story

Keele introduces himself as the bad wizard who built the dungeon and carried away the noblewoman289. He does not demand an elaborate public rehabilitation before explaining himself. He then proudly supplies the missing request: that the person he loves, who is being mistreated, should become happy299-302. Kazuma summarizes a politically arranged position as the king's mistress, neglect by the king, and conflict with the other women at court303; Keele affirms the account305.

That exchange changes the interpretation of the public tale, while retaining its source. Keele is an interested participant narrating his own past. His agreement does not create a separately witnessed court scene, supply names for the king or the woman's parents, or independently establish every report of mistreatment. The self-description as a bad wizard can carry a playful acceptance of scandal and pride in defiance. It is insufficient evidence of either global moral self-condemnation or a stable pleasure in harming strangers.

## A reported acceptance, rather than an assumed romance

Keele says that he proposed after taking her away and immediately received an affirmative answer305. That is evidence for his account of her participation, stronger than silently assuming that an abduction became consensual because the story is romantic. It remains his account. The woman has no present speech in the received encounter, and the analyst cannot supply the precise words, timing, pressures, or alternatives of her decision.

He remembers fighting the kingdom during their escape and calls that time enjoyable305. The lightness of that recollection belongs to a survivor describing a shared adventure. It does not establish that every military opponent deserved harm, that every battle was won without cost, or that the woman experienced danger in precisely the way he did. Her possible agency needs protection from two opposite simplifications: treating her solely as stolen property and making her entirely knowable through the lover's retrospective praise.

## Protection that changes the protector

The account says that Keele was badly wounded while defending her and became a lich to continue protecting her323. The stated cause is relational responsibility under a severe constraint, rather than a direct display of power for its own sake. The earlier career of study and national service52-64/291-299 is also narrated around an unattainable relationship. These strands permit a substantial interpretation of the direction his remembered achievements take.

They do not establish a complete hierarchy across all duties or make romantic devotion a universal excuse. The source does not show the entire transformation, every tactical choice, or his conduct toward unrelated dependents. Becoming a lich to prolong protection and later asking for purification are not necessarily inconsistent. The purpose for which continued existence was desired has changed after the woman's death. A study can explain that change without inventing a general rule that any bereavement makes him abandon all other responsibilities.

## An affectionate image with an unanswered question

Keele points out the woman's carefully arranged remains on the bed306 and praises their appearance305. The scene presents continued attachment through his gesture and aesthetic attention. It stays non-graphic here. Exact age, a personal name for the woman, the date of her death, and the duration of his solitary waiting remain unestablished.

His testimony says that she smiled throughout the hardship without complaint327. Immediately afterward he wonders whether he actually made her happy328. That doubt is essential contrary evidence to the tidy interpretation that heroic escape has settled everything. He can remember apparent contentment and still lack the answer that matters to him. Neither his smile nor his account of her smiles gives the narrator direct access to her complete experience.

## Asking for the capacity he does not have

Keele asks Aqua to purify him315, recognizing that she has sufficient priestly power. He explains that he had been waiting to decay and woke on sensing extraordinary sacred power329. Those explanations belong to him; his statement about what an undead being cannot do is not an independently tested biological law. The actual request is specific and directed to someone he believes can help.

During Aqua's incantation319, he rests a hand on the woman's arm bones320. He later bows deeply338. These actions give the visitor encounter a movement from greeting and confident storytelling to dependence, thanks, and acceptance of a ritual. They do not authorize a fabricated confession of every past wrong or an invented final spoken farewell. His practiced politeness and humor survive a serious request; the scene need not choose between a comic voice and a wholly solemn replacement personality.

## What the ritual establishes

Aqua reports that the woman has already passed peacefully without regrets321. She expands the purification circle to include the room322 and tells Keele to ask Eris for another meeting, even under a different age or relationship335. She casts Sacred Turn Undead339. When the light fades, both Keele and the woman's remains are absent341. Disappearance is the observed result. A completed audience with Eris, granted reincarnation, or reunion is not shown.

The woman's remains disappearing does not retrospectively prove that she had been a trapped, unpurified ghost; Aqua has just said otherwise. Aqua's later response to Kazuma is explicitly less certain than a verified reunion350. Darkness's confidence that the woman's escape was happy400-401 is another situated interpretation, accompanied by a slightly sad smile. It gives Darkness a meaningful response to the story without transferring authority over the dead woman's mind to her.

## A gift and a place that outlast him

Kazuma reports that Keele gave them property stored in the dresser353. The transfer is presented as something he recounts, without an itemized witnessed handover or a valuation. It supports material generosity in the received account; it does not establish a particular fortune or a limitless funding rule for future simulations. Aqua's revived interest in dividing it354-356 also belongs to her, not to Keele's plan for every subsequent expenditure.

Later uses of his name mainly name the dungeon. Sena's MAIN03 U02211 accusation concerns new monsters there; Dust remembers the practice dungeon DUST01 U017351-353 and uses its approach as a location600. DUST03 U013402-403 considers it and reports an entry restriction associated with Vanir. None shows Keele restored to active residence or responsible for the later occupants. MAIN11 U015243 instead recalls his request for purification while Kazuma evaluates a different creature. That analogy preserves his narrative influence without turning the other creature into him or witnessing his afterlife.

## A substantial study with a limited voice

Keele's own Japanese speech offers a formal first-person presentation, polite time-conscious greeting, self-deprecating villain label, proud anecdote, gentle humor, and a direct request for help. The reported romance supplies no long corpus of ordinary banter with his beloved. A convincing reconstruction cannot substitute a theatrical undead dialect, a generic noble wizard, or an endless quotation of his introduction for that missing range.

The source earns a dedicated Tier2 monograph: it contains remembered ambition, public and private reputation, an attributed relationship decision, costly protection, material domestic attention, uncertainty, and an actual request with a performed consequence. A broader behavioral model is not yet earned. Devotion, retrospective self-presentation, the present need for release, and the genre's sympathetic reversal remain overlapping explanations. No independent prospective test separates them. The present disposition is proportional to this evidence, without a book-count quota, species ceiling, or promise of a future model merely because he has received a long study.
