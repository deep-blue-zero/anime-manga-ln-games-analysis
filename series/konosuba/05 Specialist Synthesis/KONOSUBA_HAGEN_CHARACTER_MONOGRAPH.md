# Hagen: household provision, social tact and unequal judgment

Status: initial scoped Tier2 monograph; no new reconstruction model. HG28 in the [cohort evidence index](../08%20Evidence%20and%20Indexes/KONOSUBA_ALDERP_MAXWELL_RAGCRAFT_COHORT28_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md) owns the named household joins and limits.

## 1. Identity

Hagen / ハーゲン is named as a Dustiness household servant in MAIN05, a head butler in MAIN07 and the old butler in YORI04. This route consolidates the existing census subject. He is not automatically the unnamed wedding guard, Norris, Alderp's insulting messenger, Heidel or Rionor's head butler (HG28-01–03).

## 2. Chronology

The emergency visit after the Crimson journey, threatening-letter handling and wedding logistics, and later complaint about borrowed maids form differentiated work situations. Actual speech and choices coexist with K's trust appraisal. The same household is not independent evidence of every unseen past act, but the recurrence is enough to move beyond a one-line servant role (HG28-01–03).

## 3. Wants and work

He tries to deliver a serious message, maintain household services, arrange the noon ceremony and recover scarce maid labor. The later request is practical rather than decorative: borrowing staff affects his own responsibilities. Household loyalty can be real while he has objectionable assumptions about people outside its status protections (HG28-01–03).

## 4. Affect and social handling

He recovers breath after being assaulted, checks Alderp with a cough in the wedding sequence and uses tact around the purified tea/water discussion. A stimulant warning becomes awkwardly public. His role permits both controlled face-saving speech and disclosure that embarrasses the recipient; tact is a repertoire, not flawless concealment (HG28-01–03).

## 5. Capacity and limits

He supplies messages, scheduling, escort and knowledge of staff availability. He does not possess limitless labor or an automatic solution to the family's finances. Arranging Aqua's service includes navigating an anticipated devil/goddess conflict, not controlling either supernatural actor. His actual assault/choking also shows a body vulnerable to the people he addresses (HG28-01–03).

## 6. Judgment and adverse conduct

The MAIN05 visit includes his own claim that without noble status Darkness might have sold her body, and insults directed at K's group. These words are not somebody else's rumor or an ideal caregiver's hidden kindness. The group has assaulted him, but that antecedent does not erase the status judgment. Later polished requests qualify a uniformly hostile account without retroactively repairing it (HG28-01, HG28-03).

## 7. Relationships

Darkness is an employer's daughter whom he serves and at times addresses through hierarchical expectations. K and Aqua are both useful contacts and people with whom he has conflict. Megumin's use of maids creates a resource dispute; she is not merely an obstacle to perfect household efficiency. No reciprocal intimacy or complete consent to every assignment is shown (HG28-01–03).

## 8. Ordinary life

Messages, tea, staffing, warnings and ceremony arrangements make ordinary work unusually visible. They support a personal account of provision under scarcity. His own home, leisure, tastes and nonhousehold friendships remain absent. It would be misleading to fill those gaps with a tireless servant archetype whose every preference is the family's preference (HG28-02–03).

## 9. Voice

Formal requests, deliberate compliments, a corrective cough and explicit caution serve particular interactions. The early insulting language is contrary evidence to a universally gentle voice. Reconstructing social tact does not require guessing an accent or making each line an ornate butler formula; no audio or L3 performance is established (HG28-01–03).

## 10. Humor and harm

The message carrier's rough reception, awkward stimulant warning and friction over borrowed maids produce comedy through unequal expectations. Actual physical mistreatment, humiliating status assumptions and finite work remain part of the scene. Neither later usefulness nor a comic interruption licenses treating every interaction as mutually welcome (HG28-01–03).

## 11. Counterevidence and rivals

Pure ideal care fails against the early insults. Pure elitist contempt fails to explain all practical help and tact. Unlimited competence fails against staff shortage and the need to ask outside actors. Routine office practice explains much of the behavior, making a monograph with explicit variations more defensible than a generator claiming a unique universal rule from every courteous request (HG28-01–03).

## 12. Disposition

Tier2 reflects recurrent, named own work plus meaningful contrary evidence. It does not impose a minor-character ceiling: richer evidence could support a model later. Presently, broader autonomous aims, handling of an unsupported commoner and durable repair after conflict remain weak. The distinct guard and butler identities stay unresolved where no source join exists. No first bodies, independent accuracy, grade or final-program closure are added.
