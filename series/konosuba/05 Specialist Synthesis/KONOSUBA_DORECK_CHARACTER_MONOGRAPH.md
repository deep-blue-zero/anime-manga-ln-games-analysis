## 2. Chronology and unequal information

At the castle, the apparent Doreck presents a polished personality, accepts a cookie from Feit and talks about admired freedom and a desired knightly life. Those are Gaibil's performed actions. The real client's later account may explain parts of the performance, but it does not make the proxy's grip, eating or social fluency the client's observed bodily abilities (U011 P404–452).

During the investigation, past illness and a recent vigorous public presence are reports. Dust considers possession, replacement and contract explanations before finding the real man. Restricted-food instructions and the earlier cookie are clues in Dust's reasoning; they do not independently diagnose Doreck. The bedside acknowledgement then establishes that the two are different (U012 P129–138; U013 P79–118,202–218).

Doreck first says what he wished and paid, then recounts his attachment, jealousy and relationship with Gaibil. Only after Dust approaches to capture him does he disclose their shared eye and ear. The arriving guards make the information imbalance consequential. Do not give Dust that knowledge earlier or expand a specified sensory link into complete mutual mind-reading (U013 P223–298).

The later stages are not a single completed transformation. Doreck interrupts Gaibil's death-seeking performance, asks the king to spare him according to his own subsequent account, cares for the recovering devil, and requests help developing his own strength and appeal. The narrative ends with an agreement and handshake, not its successful execution (U014 P536–558; U016 P14–34).

## 3. Motives, values and self-justification

Doreck explicitly wants marriage to Rionor and says that he offered his entire existence. Dust suggests health as the more obvious wish; Doreck rejects the implication that his preference is madness. The illness therefore constrains his available means without supplying a complete explanation of his priorities (U013 P223–231).

His account makes Rionor a source of movement, novelty and attention in a restricted life. He remembers looking from his room, being dragged into her activities and later hearing her stories. His interpretation turns a coercive outing and a frightening night of symptoms into the origin of love. Dust supplies a darker bodily interpretation. Neither the romance nor Dust's speculation establishes a clinical diagnosis or retroactive permission for the outing (P234–255).

The desire has a possessive side. He says that even a substitute version of himself is preferable to another person having Rionor. Their private bargain concerns the two men's rewards while leaving her uninformed. Calling the arrangement mutually beneficial does not resolve her agency or turn her into something they can dispose of (P269–288).

His later concern for Gaibil is not reducible to the original marriage wish. He calls the devil a valued friend and begs him not to die. In the final scene he still wants Rionor, but asks to become appealing through his own qualities. This changes the requested means; it does not demonstrate that he has relinquished possessiveness, achieved health or earned her acceptance (U014 P538–542; U016 P21–34).

## 4. Affect and its changing rewards

Jealousy is not merely a feeling Doreck wants removed. In his account, seeing Rionor smile at Line first produces exclusion and resentment. Reports of the proxy's closeness later produce jealousy mixed with excitement. Gaibil's response corroborates that the mixed emotion remains rewarding to their arrangement (U013 P259–286,309–310).

The bodily coughing, agitation and interrupted speech matter, but they do not justify importing a diagnostic category. Dust's comparisons with Darkness are his comic comparisons. The prose distinguishes what Doreck feels, what he says he feels, and Dust's dislike of the account.

The final envy is different in content. Doreck admires Gaibil's apparent willingness to take his blame and calls that performance impressive. Gaibil notices a pleasant quality in this envy. That supplies a represented relational change, without proving that all jealousy has become harmless or that the earlier reward structure disappeared (U016 P21–28).

## 5. Competence, embodiment and limits

The actual bedside Doreck is thin, coughing and limited in movement. He can explain the bargain, recognize Dust and manage the timing of disclosure. He later reaches the battle with Rionor's support, speaks against Gaibil's intended death, and stumbles when trying to stop his charge. These are real actions within bodily limits, not proof of passivity or unrestricted capacity (U013 P202–218,285–298; U014 P536–558).

Public administrative competence belongs primarily to Gaibil's performance and other people's evaluations. Gaibil proposes continuing work with Doreck's advice, but the proposed future work is not an observed record of Doreck governing. His father's former office and the king's intended appointment are not inherited personal achievement (U011 P283–296; U013 P332–343).

The final proposal begins with health management and light exercise. No completed training, medical recovery or stronger body is shown. A generator must bind his current capabilities rather than silently giving the final wish its desired result (U016 P30–34).

## 6. Conditional decisions

Two provisional propositions are earned for the [reconstruction model](../06%20Character%20Modeling%20and%20Reconstruction/KONOSUBA_DORECK_RECONSTRUCTION_MODEL.md). First, when his own presence is severely constrained and a proxy offers access to the relationship he wants, anticipated jealousy need not deter acceptance: he can value the access and part of the emotional experience. This applies to the acknowledged proxy phase, not every later opportunity. His final request explicitly seeks a different means.

Second, an immediate danger to the particular companion he values can prompt intervention even when that companion's performance serves his original wish. He speaks, seeks lenience and offers care; he does not merely express abstract gratitude. That is a bounded conflict between attachment and the bargain, not a universal altruism rule.

Both propositions require information, available alternatives, bodily capacity, relationship history and narrative phase. They do not decide Rionor's wishes, authorize deception or predict a unique action in every unfamiliar case. Possessiveness, dependence, erotic reward and friendship remain viable joint or rival causes.

## 7. Relationships and asymmetry

Rionor's youthful attention is formative in Doreck's telling, but her own meaning and present wishes are not contained in his memory. Her later physical support gets him to Gaibil; it does not establish a romantic commitment or an acceptance of the earlier substitute scheme.

Line is first the admired person's apparent favored companion, then a jealousy target, investigator, recipient of deliberately late information, and temporary source of physical care. Dust handing him water does not erase their opposed purposes (U013 P259–298).

Gaibil is contractor, substitute, emotional consumer, carer and eventually someone Doreck calls a friend. These roles can coexist without a single pure motive. Doreck's intercession returns care to the former carer. The king's lenience is subsequently reported through Doreck, so it is evidence of Doreck's account and current survival context rather than a separately witnessed royal hearing (U016 P14–25).

## 8. Ordinary life and history

Doreck describes an early routine of limited mobility, looking through a window and valuing visits. The king's provision of castle medical and priestly support is his recollection. Later dietary instructions, medicine, rest and restricted movement make daily maintenance part of the actual plot rather than a decorative illness label (U013 P85–112,231–257,312–313).

The source does not provide a full household budget, education, independent workday, friendships outside this relation, or a reliable current account of his parents. His father is the former chancellor in the king's report; the physician's remembered suggestion to call parents does not name a separate mother or establish present family contact (U011 P291–296; U013 P251,259).

The everyday care scenes matter to the later agreement. They also limit it: helping him become stronger would require continuing work the ending has only proposed.

## 9. Japanese written voice

The real Doreck uses 僕, polite forms and respectful address while acknowledging uncomfortable facts with striking calm. He corrects Line to the current Dust name. His composure can become emphatic excitement and then coughing, before returning to composed questions (U013 P209–231,274–298).

The public groom's voice is an impersonation by Gaibil. Similar content or face does not make it an interchangeable Doreck voice sample. The final private request is more self-directed in content while remaining a request for help; the handshake supplies action without requiring an invented triumphal speech (U016 P30–34).

These observations concern represented Japanese wording, register and addressee. They do not certify pronunciation, timing, an actor's performance or a fully tested idiolect in new situations.

## 10. Comedy and serious consequences

The reveal turns a presumed imprisoned innocent into a willing partner with an unexpected reward. The joke depends on frustrating Dust's expectations. It does not make the bride's uninformed involvement consensual or transform the contract into a harmless arrangement.

The later public story reverses the private explanation: Gaibil loudly claims brainwashing, listeners express sympathy, and the reported disposition is lenient. Doreck himself thanks him for taking the blame. An analysis must retain the discrepancy rather than adopting whichever version produces the cleanest victim or villain label (U014 P544–552; U015 P7–10; U016 P21–26).

The reunion and new agreement also have a comic reversal, with the patient warning the injured carer to rest. Care is performed while prior confinement, threats and destruction remain part of the history.

## 11. Counterevidence, rivals and unknowns

A jealousy-only account cannot explain why Doreck values its painful mixture or later intervenes for its producer. A passive-victim account conflicts with his willing acknowledgement and sensory-link disclosure. A pure self-development account conflicts with the original possessive bargain. A pure friendship account does not explain all of the earlier reward or deception.

Conversely, his concern for Gaibil does not isolate friendship from gratitude, dependence or preserving a valued source of experience. His final change may be durable growth or a new means to the old goal. The source ends before those alternatives can be separated by later behavior.

The source-known diagnostic work must therefore preserve failures and specify phase and alternatives. It cannot turn agreement with remembered outcomes into independent accuracy, a capability grade or increased confidence.
