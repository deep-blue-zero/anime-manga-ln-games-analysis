# Leen — a shared present, selective knowledge and the costs of belonging

Leen wants a life with people she can recognize and influence. She works, worries about money, enjoys company, changes her mind about another adventurer's ability and takes substantial risks alongside her companions. She also withholds information, helps impose a dangerous scheme after a refusal, strikes people and makes a socially damaging mistake about a possible friend. Her practical competence and particular warmth belong in the same account as those choices. The person she wants to bring back under pressure is the familiar, flawed Dust who tries to survive, rather than an idealized knight. That preference is one of the clearest statements of what this relationship means to her. (DUST05 U012 P587–600.)

This account uses seven later receiving packets with 2,394 known BASE context slots in thirteen members/seven accepted objects. The number includes other actors, letters, recollections, transitions, blanks and repetitions of earlier accepted receiving. It is neither an independent observation count nor a new whole-source pass. Earlier full reports and the ten rolling responsibilities retain their qualified scopes. The sources are known to the author; reconstruction eligibility and future reliability remain separate questions.

## A wizard with a place in a working party

Leen's name isリーン. Lean is a retrieval variant; Rain/レイン and Raina/ライナ remain different people. MAIN02 introduces a young-looking wizard in a blue cloak who says she can use intermediate magic. Kazuma's thought that she might be younger than him is his estimate. These returns do not establish her exact age, full family history, birthplace, qualification record or complete physical specification. A later resemblance to Rionor is explicitly a resemblance between individuals. Neither a shared face nor Dust's mistaken address makes Leen a princess. (MAIN02 C1 P450–453; DUST06 U012 P433–456.)

The first one-day party exchange gives Leen an ordinary professional position. Taylor claims the leader's role, Keith introduces his archery and Leen offers to protect the apparent novice Kazuma. The three are surprised that an Adventurer leads a group of advanced classes. Their early ranking rests partly on occupational labels and incomplete information. Taylor's promise of equal reward shares is a promise made in that situation; the passage does not print a final cash division. Leen treats goblin work as attractive paid work and initially speaks with easy confidence. (MAIN02 C1 P443–484.)

That confidence changes when the actual situation changes. Kazuma supplies Enemy Sense and party-sharing Concealment; Leen holds her mouth and later explicitly admits being frightened by the Beginner Killer. She then takes some of his luggage so he can move freely if they must run, and asks to rely on his skills. The practical benefit to the party is part of her stated reason. The act is real help without requiring a pure selfless motive or making her dependent on Kazuma in every later crisis. Taylor and Keith also take loads. (P501–540.)

Leen's caution matters before the goblin fight. She asks to inspect the unexpectedly large group before committing. Keith rushes out to avoid yielding all the achievement to Kazuma, Taylor follows, and Leen's feared numerical problem becomes visible. She protests that she had warned them. The scene records a competent judgment that her companions fail to use; it also keeps her fear and imperfect control over the situation. Leadership title, expertise and actual influence are different things. (P548–573.)

Her Wind Curtain is actually cast. Kazuma's quicker Wind Breath first deflects incoming arrows while she is still chanting; her completed spell then surrounds the party. Kazuma owns the water-and-freezing terrain tactic, and Taylor directly cuts goblins on the resulting favorable ground. Leen announces a stronger attack in the collective assault, but the narration does not identify every later offensive spell. A delayed cast is not absent capacity, and the victory is not an individual competence certificate for any one member. (P578–613.)

Leen's account of her magic-school teaching makes the experience a learning event. She says basic spells were presented as a waste of skill points, then laughs at what Kazuma has achieved with them. She apologizes for underestimating him, asks to see his card and is surprised by the combination of ordinary intellect and high luck. Her response permits revision of an earlier category. It does not make her a universal inventor, prove a completed qualification or establish the party's subsequent luck explanation as the true cause of success. (P614–625/P708–717.)

## A life with small pleasures and finite costs

Leen has uses for money beyond financing another person's adventure. In MASK she is eating vegetable sticks at an inn when Dust arrives. She immediately refuses more lending or a guarantee until an earlier loan is repaid; Dust later acknowledges owing her. The repeated bites are one continuing snack, not multiple independent preference samples. The debt relation has support, while the exact amount, original handover and repayment remain unshown in this scene. Calling her a vegetable lover follows Vanir's nickname and this particular activity; a universal diet would go beyond the evidence. (MASK U013 P335–390.)

Her financial caution also appears from her own viewpoint in the royal-substitution episode. Angry and confined, she considers throwing a pillow, then puts it back because she cannot be confident of paying for damaged furnishings. She is capable of stopping an immediate expression of frustration when the material consequence becomes salient. That restraint coexists with later willingness to impose physical risk on Dust. It does not establish a general rule against hurting people or damaging property. (DUST06 U012 P427–432.)

Her own attention is also a resource. Rionor's letter offers compensation and access to Dust's past. Leen's interest sharpens at the information offer, then at the photograph of a younger Dust with Rionor and Feit. The photo is actually handed over; the promised money is not. She says hidden information irritates her and elects to perform the role. The text repeatedly pairs denial of personal interest with bodily discomfort and intense attention. Jealousy is a serious reading of that construction, but its exact weight relative to curiosity, exclusion and irritation is unknown. (P469–518.)

Leisure has visible costs as well. In YORI04 Dust and Leen tease Kazuma and Megumin outside a theater. Leen agrees to attend the recommended show if Dust treats her; the pair go. Later they complain that the supposedly romantic experience frightened them and that attendance made them miss an emergency quest and its free refreshments. The outing is an actual shared activity under a stated payment condition. The ticket transaction and a stable taste for horror are not printed, and the later complaints are their account of what happened inside. The retired observer's enthusiasm for pairing them supplies another character's interpretation, rather than an official relationship status. (YORI04 U014 P371–385/P461–470.)

Guild company is itself pleasurable. After the original party's escape, the members laugh together and physically congratulate Kazuma. After the defense of Axel, they drink, worry about their absent friend and discuss how an elevated hero might fit into their ordinary group. Leen can enjoy social life without every interaction being a romantic bid. Her participation, particular smiles and one chosen snack do not fix a complete hierarchy of tastes. (MAIN02 C1 P693–727; DUST07 U014 P11–40.)

## The person she calls back

DUST05 gives Leen a strong response to the difference between Dust's present self and his former identity. Dust's altered focalization calls her a princess and treats himself as the devoted knight Line. Leen rejects the self-sacrificial performance. She demands the cunning, selfish, troublesome companion who would find a way to survive even in that situation. The demand recognizes his undesirable features as part of the person she wants alive. It is particular familiarity, not a preference for a perfectly noble partner. His private use of the name Line does not mean she has heard his complete history. (U012 P570–593.)

Leen then causes a head collision that hurts both of them and asks him to name himself. He answers Dust, while his narration still records residual fog afterward. The immediate change is concrete, but the scene does not establish a universal medical technique, complete recovery from every prior condition or a performed kiss from the preceding close approach. The collision is her forceful intervention. Dust owns the subsequent protective leap, exposure to Feit's breath and strike that returns Feit to her human form. Leen neither supplies his resistance nor performs his finishing action. (P594–624.)

Her knowledge expands at a precise point. Leen says she had suspected concealment but did not know that the white dragon was Feit. Dust asks for secrecy; she agrees, naming the risk that Feit could be targeted and the trouble disclosure would bring him. Later the group is given a cover account in which the dragon escaped and Feit arrived separately; Dust says Leen helps make it credible. Her choice to protect a secret is informed by a stated local reason. It does not give her all of his old political or personal knowledge, and cannot be moved backward to her earlier suspicion. (P643–650/P689–692.)

The same aftermath contains something she is not told. Leen asks what the fortune telling cost; Dust says it was put on credit. His narration tells the reader that he actually paid with material from Feit. Leen's question shows concern with practical costs, but she cannot inherit the reader's answer. The offered meal, large order and men checking their money likewise do not print a final settlement. A useful teammate can remain partially deceived and financially uncertain. (P693–712.)

## Her own choices under a borrowed identity

The royal-substitution episode makes several decisions visible from Leen's own viewpoint. She begins confined and wrongly treated by the household's arrangement, even though the maid explicitly says she knows Leen is a different person. The maid's account of Rionor's plan and bribery is an attributed explanation. It does not convert the initial seizure into Leen's voluntary participation. Rionor's written apology and promised benefits likewise do not undo it. (DUST06 U012 P427–498.)

Leen then decides to play the role after the information offer. Only afterward does she confront the packed schedule, unfamiliar etiquette and visible whip. She completes a dinner while uncertain whether she has performed well enough; the maid supplies a passing assessment. When Leen asks to cancel, the maid refuses and reminds her of surveillance. This is an actual withdrawal request, not a comic paraphrase of continuing consent. (P516–559.)

A later moment has a different choice structure. The maid offers information about the old elopement while qualifying how much of its detail comes from Rionor. Leen's irritation intensifies. The maid then suggests that outperforming Rionor could embarrass the princess and explicitly offers compensation and return to the inn if Leen still wants to decline. Leen refuses that offered exit and chooses to continue, giving a revenge rationale while denying jealousy. The late offer is not a completed payment or proof of every future option. Nevertheless, it must be retained: her final continuation is not accurately described as occurring with no stated exit. Equally, it cannot authorize the original capture or erase the earlier denied cancellation. (P563–595.)

The continuation supplies another private set of reasons. Leen is embarrassed by the costume and doubtful that she can imitate Rionor. She thinks about the ties created by the earlier affair, says leaving the others would sit badly with her, and considers the benefit to Dust in his homeland. Those are her own articulated reasons. Her earlier curiosity and resentment are not the only reasons she can have. She can even identify Rionor's easy treatment of people without regard to status as a real strength, while finding the rest of the performance difficult. (DUST-CONT U012 P436–510, especially P461–463/P498.)

Her performance is locally successful in her account: she adjusts her posture, addresses guards and produces the expected frivolous greeting. The guards visibly react, the butler praises her and Doreck appears troubled but welcoming. Leen concludes that they are fooled. These are useful observations and a plausible local inference; they do not give her access to every observer's mind or establish completion of the whole later mission. Lolisa's investigation, Taylor and Keith's escort, and the butler's instructions retain their own actors. (P464–510.)

The wedding crisis later makes actor identity and choice visible again. Dust returns after detention and shares his mansion account; Leen supplies the earlier letter and asks about Rionor. At the ceremony Dust initially calls the bride Rionor, but later discovers that she is Leen. Taylor urges evacuation when the danger changes. Dust lifts the bride without asking, and she protests before stabilizing herself. Later, identified as Leen, she takes his offered hand and chooses to board Feit. The earlier protest and later assent are different acts. Taylor supplies a spear and Keith a staff; the staff's ownership route remains uncertain. Useful joint action neither erases the initial protest nor makes the whole rescue her passive role. (DUST-CONT U014 P144–179/P300–339/P430–443.)

## A refusal, an inference and someone else's apology

Leen's encounter with Yunyun is a consequential social mistake. Her abrupt refusal sends Yunyun away crying. Leen says Dust's involvement makes her suspicious, and she describes herself as a novice who would not fit an admired, powerful archwizard. She reports a guild belief that Yunyun prefers solitude because she repeatedly helps parties and then avoids their thanks. That account helps explain why an approach she regards as implausible could be a sincere attempt by someone lonely. The refusal is real, and its hurt does not disappear because Leen supplies a rationale. (MASK U013 P348–382.)

Several readings remain possible. Leen may sincerely fear another of Dust's schemes and a humiliating mismatch. Her account may also make a blunt rejection appear more defensible afterward. The source does not fully disclose the private weighting. Her rhetorical claim that Dust would walk past a drowning child without personal gain is not a newly witnessed omission or a universal behavioral law. Her stated willingness to respond differently without his introduction is a counterfactual claim, not an observed second decision. (P364–382.)

Leen's tactical anticipation in the next exchange is sharper. Dust threatens to discipline her; she predicts that he will first lecture, prepares a spell before leaving and meets his actual beginning of a lecture with an attack. Vanir hears the scream and explosion and sees her triumphant afterward. The exact spell and injury are not printed, but the planned ambush has substantial support. Her familiarity with one companion's habits can be useful without making her universally perceptive about another person's need. (P383–394.)

The apparent apology and friendship request that follow belong to Vanir in Leen's form. His first-person narration and explicit reveal settle the actor. They cannot be used as Leen's admission of error, an accepted friendship or a completed repair. Vanir then transmits a message attributed to her about not exposing Yunyun to the party's rough associates. The unshown intervening conversation is mediated through him; its precise sincerity and selection remain uncertain. Yunyun feels some relief, but real Leen and Yunyun do not establish a new friendship in this received sequence. (P399–424.)

## Familiarity can help and can impose

Leen gives Dust material and emotional support, but she also uses closeness to override him. In the defense preparation she identifies a real strategic need: the party cannot quickly increase everyone's power, while Dust might recover old skill. Dust boasts that he will withstand any training before the actual target is disclosed. Keith then supplies a secret proposal that Leen and Taylor approve. They withhold it when Dust asks. Keith and Taylor physically stop him leaving; Leen diverts Feit with dessert; the group binds and transports him. His broad earlier statement does not erase the subsequent attempt to leave. (DUST07 U011 P715–785.)

Leen refuses to untie him, remains the rider and later treats a dangerous request to be put down as an opportunity to leave him at the target. Feit descends near the ground, the rope is cut and Dust drops painfully; the specific cutting hand is not named. Leen supplies the spear, reveals the survival task and leaves despite his renewed call for help. The scene does not show a continuous fall from great height to the ground, and the thrown weapon is not shown hitting him. These limits matter without making restraint, the drop or abandonment harmless. (P797–830.)

The training has a real practical result and real adverse consequences. Dust narrates recovering facility with the spear after a day of fighting. He also reports capture, clothing damage and unwanted contact, with a further sexual act narrowly avoided. Those events can be described without graphic detail. They must not be flattened into an entirely unperformed threat, a completed further act or an uncomplicated lesson. His later fear persists. The source supplies neither proof that the party knew the spearhead would fail nor a license for his own unperformed retaliatory threat. (P832–894/P913–947.)

Repair is partial and concrete. Taylor initiates an apology, money is provided and Dust verbally relents. Leen is part of the warmer response and offers future care for Feit. Later she actually pours a drink while acknowledging some wrongdoing; Lolisa and Feit perform their own food-sharing acts. The exact individual cash contributions, final spending and completion of every offered care task are not shown. Real apology and recipient response coexist with continuing fear and later conflict. (P901–925; U012 P275–294.)

Leen subsequently joins Lolisa in taking Keith outside after his insulting remark. Keith protests; an explosion, light and scream are heard, and the two return without him. The outside caster, exact spell and injury remain unspecified. Taylor and Dust choose to close their eyes and ears. Keith's remark and earlier bad choices do not erase his status as the recipient of this treatment. Leen's kindness and her power within the peer group are both part of the relationship. (U012 P295–305.)

## Skill, influence and the limits of the moment

Leen's competence is strongest when it is described at its actual conditions. Her caution and Wind Curtain in MAIN02 are useful. Her DUST07 Fireball strikes a powerful undead opponent but does not defeat him. Keith's arrows are blocked and Taylor's shield is damaged in the same encounter. These are failures of effectiveness against this target, not evidence that none can perform the relevant act. Leen also feels pity for the taunted opponent, then later hits Dust with her staff and tells the pair to stop making the enemy difficult to kill through mutual sympathy. Particular compassion does not become a universal restraint. (U012 P735–825.)

Feit supplies the saving flame and Dust the decapitation. Leen then publicly claims that her magic saved him and redirects the witnesses toward the remaining enemy. The actual flame sequence, her account and Dust's thanks support a deliberate cover for the ally's secret. Her exact private formulation is inferred through Dust's focalization, and the claim must not be counted as another actual cast. Even the apparent victory is provisional: the opponent is a Dullahan, survives decapitation and resumes the deception before Dust silences the head. (P826–890.)

Leen chooses to board Feit for the aerial fight and states her own ranged-support reason. Taylor gives Dust his shield; Feit supplies flight, and Dust uses that shield under pressure. Leen does not acquire the others' resistance to dragon fire, and Dust acts to avoid exposing her to breath. Her presence is useful and physically risky. (U013 P475–559.)

Her calm warning brings Dust out of an angry response to provocation. His recollection of an earlier party episode explains why he values the words; it is a separate remembered event, not a second present intervention. His interpretation that she does not remember it is not direct access to her memory. Leen then actually casts Freeze Gust. The cold works much better than she expects. Dust hypothesizes that the Demon King's defeat has weakened the target; that deduction cannot be moved backward into her plan. Feit supplies the plunge and Dust the decisive strike. Leen's surprise and fear remain visible alongside an important contribution. (P560–635.)

## Affection, address and the person speaking

Leen's chosen cheek kiss is actual. She frames it as advance thanks after Dust's protection promise and asks whether it motivates him. A later genuine approach, with closed eyes and a reference to the remainder, is interrupted by Feit before contact. Afterward real Leen stands to Dust's right. The apparent second invitation from the left belongs to Vanir in disguise; it is not evidence of Leen choosing to proceed in front of the observers. Her later shoulder touch and hand-taking are hers. Each act has its own recipient, moment and scope. None establishes a permanent contract or standing permission. (U012 P361–375; U014 P63–145.)

Her private support also has words. Leen says the companions are trying to encourage Dust because others do not recognize what he has done. Taylor's praise and Keith's offered drinks have their separate authors. Dust's sense of belonging is a real part of his narration, while the precise relationship state remains more open than an outsider's label of couple would imply. She can want the shared life without every tease being harmless or every disagreement hiding a single feeling. (U014 P131–148; YORI04 U014 P381/P462–471.)

Japanese pragmatics help distinguish these contexts. Leen's casualあたし, confident treatment of the apparent novice, direct criticism, hesitant request for reliance, repeated private denial and comparatively gentle encouragement perform different tasks. The sarcastic kindness of the training delivery has a different effect from the calming voice in the aerial fight. Her acting as Rionor is a performed persona, and Vanir's two impersonations are a different speaker. A recognizable sharp register is insufficient to decide truth, consent or affection. ## Interpretation and a reconstruction boundary

The source supports a substantial individual monograph and warrants developing a separate falsifiable reconstruction candidate. Leen has her own focalization, ordinary costs, work, fear, learning, social error, voluntary care, selective information practices and coercive choices. A generic responsible mage explains her useful support but poorly captures the combination of ignored caution, mistaken social ranking, secret-keeping, forced training and desire for the present flawed companion. A purely romantic or financial account also misses substantial conduct. These rivals remain serious because exact private weights and recurrence are unknown.

A differentiated model should test at least four proposals: attachment to a shared present with mutual reliance; practical use of available information and finite means; selective investigation and presentation of knowledge; and enforcement or repair shaped by peer position, anger and the current goal. Each needs actual support, contrary evidence, competing explanations, application limits and a possible falsifier. The model must be able to reproduce misjudgment and an imposed choice; it must also allow learning, refusal and useful care. It cannot make every utterance a knowing lie or every adverse act an optimal strategy.
