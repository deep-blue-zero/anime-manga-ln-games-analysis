# Maxwell: constrained service, appetite and coercive attachment

Status: initial scoped Tier3 monograph. The separate model contains provisional conditional candidates; MX28 and AL28 in the [cohort evidence index](../08%20Evidence%20and%20Indexes/KONOSUBA_ALDERP_MAXWELL_RAGCRAFT_COHORT28_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md) specify source planes and recoverable contexts. This is source-known analysis, without final maturity, independent accuracy or a capability grade.

## 1. Identity and embodiment

Maks / マクス in Alderp's private room is explicitly identified by Vanir as Maxwell / マクスウェル, a powerful devil he knows. The name join comes from MAIN07 I P73–80, not a generic similarity between devils or a reader's prior franchise knowledge. Alderp's low-grade classification is his mistake, not a second identity (MX28-01–03).

Alderp describes a beautiful young-looking form, flat expression and unsettling breath-like sound. Later Vanir reports a physically backward-facing head. These are separate attributed observations, not evidence of a neurological diagnosis. Printed 僕 and a human-looking form do not establish human biological sex. Wiz's general statement and YORI04's upper-devil discussion must retain their speakers and qualifications (MX28-05–06).

## 2. What is observed and what is reported

The main direct encounters are concentrated in the two private interludes. Maxwell speaks under repeated physical abuse, reports limitations, seeks work and reward, recognizes a changed contract state, refuses a fellow-devil killing order and eventually breaks Alderp's arms. Those are linked phases, not a large set of independent encounters (MX28-01–04).

Memory failures, past service, old friendship and the scale of his rank receive much of their detail from Alderp or Vanir. The curse's removal has a separate actual scene involving Aqua and Ignis; Maxwell reports its loss afterward. Future transport and unending torment are promised or explained, while later hell/wellbeing are Vanir's accounts. No continuous journey or indefinite treatment is watched (MX28-02, MX28-04–06; IG28-03).

## 3. Wants and positive valuation

He repeatedly asks for a wish or task and its reward. He says he likes Alderp's cruelty and the emotions it produces. The service relationship is therefore not explained by helpless obedience alone: the work and counterpart can offer a kind of satisfaction to him, even while nonpayment is exploitative (MX28-01).

After his release, his language of liking and staying close centers on access to Alderp's despair. The continued existence of the recipient is valuable because it sustains that appetite. This is a specific form of attachment with coercive consequences, not proof of reciprocal romance, ordinary caregiving or an entirely impersonal contract collector (MX28-04).

## 4. Affect and expression

The initially flat presentation coexists with enthusiasm for cruelty and repeated requests. Later actual delight becomes visible around inflicted pain and a perceived declaration of mutual liking. Alderp's desperate appeal is taken in a way that serves Maxwell's own preferred relationship (MX28-01, MX28-04).

A plausible account preserves expression, appetite and misunderstanding without assuming a stable absence of emotion. A rival is that the transition is primarily freedom from contract rather than a changed feeling. The sequence supports a contract-enabled change of action; it does not measure the relative contribution of release, payment claim, audience or accumulated frustration.

## 5. Typed competence and external limits

Maxwell says he cannot retrieve an object whose location is unknown or undo the asserted extraordinary seal. Church conditions weaken his power and the excessive light blocks an intended action. In the received encounters those are stated access/environmental limits, not watched failed casts or evidence that all his abilities vanish everywhere (MX28-01–02).

Memory alteration and consistency correction are described as capabilities, with prior reliance reported by Alderp and explained by Vanir. The scope does not establish unlimited range, every target's susceptibility or the exact cause of every legal mistake. Actual physical arm breaking demonstrates local harmful capacity. It does not measure all combat ability or make him physically untouchable (MX28-03–04).

## 6. Choice under contract and release

The apparent successful arrival of Darkness prompts Alderp to release the contract, although Maxwell explicitly says he has done nothing. Maxwell then refuses the killing demand against a fellow devil. This is his own boundary at a particular contract state; it is not a general vow never to harm or an independent demonstration of moral solidarity in every case (MX28-03).

His ensuing collection of payment and bodily harm show a different action set from the earlier cowering service. A conditional contract-sensitive account is stronger than either universal servility or unconstrained autonomy. Yet contract mechanics remain partly explained speech. A new simulation must state the terms and release state rather than invent a loophole whenever a preferred action is unavailable.

## 7. Relations: exploitation without erased agency

Alderp exploits apparent forgetting to evade payment and repeatedly kicks him. The same counterpart is also attractive to Maxwell because of cruelty. Their relationship cannot be represented as innocent victim and wholly external corruption, or as mutual consent merely because both at times value the arrangement. Alderp plainly fears and rejects the final prospect of prolonged torment (MX28-01–04).

Vanir supplies recognition, history and interpretation and benefits from the spectacle. Friendship is his involved account; it is not proof that Maxwell always follows Vanir's plans. Darkness is the named object of Alderp's demands, not Maxwell's demonstrated freely consenting companion. Vanir's impersonation must never be assigned to her or counted as Maxwell's delivery.

## 8. Ordinary life and continuity

Little independent mundane life is shown. He occupies Alderp's hidden room, responds to summons and waits for work/reward. Later social proposals and reports of doing well in hell provide attributed continuity, not a watched domestic routine, diet, leisure schedule or network of relationships (MX28-01, MX28-05–06).

This gap constrains the generator. It does not justify reducing him to a prop, but neither should a monograph invent hobbies or housework preferences to mimic a principal character's evidence density. Work-seeking, distinctive appetite and desired proximity are the grounded ordinary motivations available here.

## 9. Printed voice

The short clauses, repeated Alderp vocative, 僕 and recurrent ヒュー forms give the speech a distinctive printed organization. Requests for work and expressions of affection recur with a persistent address to the counterpart. The same simple form can deliver a capacity explanation or a severe threat; simplicity is not evidence of innocence (MX28-01–04).

The sound notation is not an audio recording or a diagnosed breathing disorder. Repeating it in every synthetic sentence would imitate a surface marker while discarding constraint, desire and contract state. A serious transfer can preserve direct address and unsettling mismatch of affectionate wording and harmful intention without importing a devil body into an ordinary human setting.

## 10. Humor and real consequences

The master's supposed cheap instrument is revealed as a much more powerful agent, and an apparent love exchange becomes a threat from which the master cannot bargain free. The joke depends on a mismatch of interpretation and control. Maxwell's smile and promise to preserve the target do not lessen the actual broken arms or intended suffering (MX28-03–04).

Affective warmth and harm therefore require separate evaluation. The same discipline applies to the victim's prior misconduct: explaining how Alderp's exploitation fails does not establish a necessary, proportionate or benevolent punishment. Vanir's amusement is an additional motive, not authorial confirmation that all outcomes were ethically required.

## 11. Rivals and contrary evidence

A mindless weapon account fails against preferences, requests, refusal and selective collection. A universally omnipotent correction engine fails against his stated limits and the observed collapse of Alderp's plans. A kind devoted companion account fails against delight in pain and the recipient's terror. A pure detached sadist account misses work, reward, sustained address and the particular relationship he expresses (MX28-01–04).

Forgetting could be broad impairment, selective failure, instability under summons, another actor's hostile description or a mixture; no controlled memory assessment distinguishes them. His recognition of a fellow devil and payment pursuit mean that "remembers nothing" is too strong. These unknowns should restrict precise forecasts, not be repaired with an invented diagnosis or perfect selective memory.

## 12. Scope of the provisional model

The own decisions support conditional candidates about acknowledged constraints, contract-sensitive action and attachment to rewarding cruelty. They warrant a Tier3 attempt despite concentrated appearances, with low confidence outside the linked encounters. Tier reflects useful agency and testable distinctions, not page-count parity or automatic promotion because he is a devil.
