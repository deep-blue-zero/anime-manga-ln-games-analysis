# Taylor — responsibility, revisable judgment and selective protection

Taylor makes leadership concrete by carrying loads, choosing a rear guard, lending his own shield, convening reports and asking what an approach would mean to its recipient. He also misjudges danger, follows a rash attack, idealizes motives, helps enforce a dangerous undisclosed training scheme and deliberately declines to intervene when a companion is taken outside. Those are choices of the same person. The useful procedural leader and the participant in harmful group action cannot be assigned to separate moral selves.

The present account uses the seven Leen/Keith/Taylor preparation receipts, with 2,394 known BASE context slots in thirteen members/seven accepted objects, together with earlier qualified receiving. Most slots are not Taylor's individual conduct. They include other actors, reports, private viewpoints, blanks and repetitions. Neither the total nor the number of analytical restatements is an independent observation count. All sources are known to the author. The account supports a scoped reconstruction candidate; it does not establish predictive accuracy, a whole-source rereading or a successor to the frozen principal suite.

## A leader whose role has practical content

Taylor's Japanese name isテイラー. MAIN02 introduces him as the party's leader and a Crusader carrying sword and shield. This is an occupational and relational position, not proof of exact age, faith, rank within a religious organization, health, family history or lifelong training. His later association with other people who hold similar classes does not merge their identities. The present sources do not provide a complete private biography. (MAIN02 C1 P443–458.)

His initial handling of Kazuma is polite enough but not free of a status assumption. The party is surprised that an ordinary Adventurer leads advanced classes. Taylor asks him to carry luggage and promises an equal quarter of the reward. The promise is a stated allocation; a final paid division is not printed. Leen introduces intermediate magic and Keith boasts of archery. Taylor's leadership starts inside that shared occupational hierarchy rather than above it. (P443–484.)

He supplies a route and map information, but his first appraisal of the solitary monster is wrong. Kazuma's Enemy Sense and shared Concealment provide information and an escape option that Taylor does not initially supply. Once the Beginner Killer's threat becomes clear, Leen takes luggage so Kazuma can move and use his skills, and Taylor and Keith take some too. The adjustment is actual cooperation under fear. It does not require them to have become permanently dependent on Kazuma or to possess the reader's full knowledge of his abilities. (P492–540.)

The same outing records a limit of Taylor's control. Kazuma reports a larger goblin group, Leen proposes checking its size, and Keith rushes out because he does not want Kazuma to take all the credit. Taylor follows. Leen's warning is not incorporated before the party meets more opponents than expected. A leader title does not establish that Taylor initiated this attack, but following it is still his choice. He then covers a companion and continues fighting. His later arrow wound is part of this same encounter, not a second independent example. (P548–573/P628–630.)

## Courage with finite equipment and dependence on others

Taylor's positive capacity is specific. He uses his sword against goblins once Kazuma's water-and-freezing tactic has altered the ground. Leen's Wind Curtain and Kazuma's immediate defense have separate causal roles. A collective victory does not give Taylor their spells, foresight or all the decisive work. Afterward, Kazuma thinks about a healing spell, but the text does not show him performing one on Taylor in that moment. (MAIN02 C1 P578–613/P628–631.)

When the Beginner Killer returns, Keith sees it before Kazuma's Enemy Sense registers it. Taylor chooses to cover the others' retreat. Keith agrees to stay because the outsider has worked hard for them; Leen takes Kazuma's hand and tries to escape with him. Kazuma refuses to abandon them and creates his own escape opportunity. Taylor's rear-guard choice is meaningful even though the scene does not print a successful final shield block by him. Risk acceptance, completed defensive work and the party's ultimate escape remain different evidentiary claims. (P641–686.)

The defense of Axel gives a less favorable physical result. Taylor offers to take the front and actually blocks the attacking opponent, but is driven aside and his shield is damaged. His statement that he is all right does not erase the impact or demonstrate unlimited durability. Keith's arrows are blocked, Leen's Fireball hits without finishing the opponent, and Feit's flame plus Dust's action produce an apparent victory that the later Dullahan reveal qualifies. Taylor is useful within an uneven group effort; he is not an invulnerable substitute for Darkness. (DUST07 U012 P770–790/P822–890.)

Later he restrains Keith from boarding and gives Dust his own shield. These are actual material and social contributions. Keith complains before accepting the ground assignment. The object transfer does not establish a replacement price, reimbursement or a universal lending rule. Nor does Taylor's ability to restrain Keith establish that he had an effective, harmless way to stop every earlier group decision. (DUST07 U013 P482–494.)

## Duty, evidence and a tendency to see admirable motives

Taylor can revise an appraisal. After the first outing he stops the recruitment talk because Kazuma has a place to return to. The group enjoys his company and jokes about a new party, but no permanent membership is settled. Taylor respects a particular existing affiliation here. That observation cannot become a rule that he always protects voluntary choice: the later forced training directly contests such a rule. (MAIN02 C1 P633–637/P702–746.)

The Dust continuation supplies an especially clear procedural contribution. The party hears overwhelmingly favorable public reports about Doreck. Feit's smell observation changes their appraisal, and they spend three days investigating. In the reports meeting, Taylor says he has not found bad rumors; Keith separately reports a missing investigator. Dust dismisses her possible fate as imprisonment after hearing an unflattering account of her motive, without checking. Taylor's report is narrower than a universal absence of adverse evidence, and Keith's contribution is not canceled by his reputation for envy. (DUST-CONT U012 P41–114.)

As the group jumps toward devil involvement, Taylor asks to hear every remaining report and judge after the information is assembled. Dust explicitly recognizes his usefulness as chair. The procedure does not by itself certify each witness, prove a devil's identity or choose correctly among possession, replacement and contract. Small's qualified report and Dust's stronger interpretation of her glance remain different access classes. Taylor offers a way to slow premature judgment without acquiring omniscience. (P115–142.)

He also changes the perspective from access to reception. Dust thinks he may be able to obtain a meeting with Doreck; Taylor asks what it would mean for a groom to receive the bride's rumored former lover just before the wedding. Dust acknowledges the problem. The group's imagined extortion is not a performed crime. Keith then objects to endangering Rionor, and Taylor agrees. Their concern is actual while risk is subsequently redirected toward Leen. It therefore supports selective perspective taking, not an impartial rule that every person's exposure receives equal weight. (P150–190.)

Taylor's moral appraisals can also outrun evidence. During the Axel defense he celebrates the defenders' apparent purity; Dust answers with material and personal motives. That exchange contests the idealization, rather than proving nobody cares about the town. In the continuation Taylor reads Dust's stated wedding wager as a pure wish for Rionor's happiness, only for Dust to explain the attraction of long odds. No stake is actually placed. Taylor's favorable reading is a local mistake about motive, not evidence that betting or marriage has already occurred. (DUST07 U012 P388–395; DUST-CONT U012 P25–38.)

The later ceremony distinguishes deliberation from emergency action. Taylor and Keith invite Dust's explanation after his return; the group hears his account rather than already knowing every hidden step. Once Gaibil expands and threatens the church, Taylor tells people to move away and then orders evacuation. He does not insist on completing a discussion before responding to immediate danger. Later he actually hands Dust a spear; its exact ownership and reimbursement are not established. His practical help and Keith's staff delivery precede other actors' aerial work. An unfamiliar tuxedo and visible social discomfort do not prevent these specific actions. (DUST-CONT U014 P144–179/P193/P300–321/P437–443.)

## Helping impose a danger, then making a limited repair

The training episode is central adverse evidence. Taylor thinks about demanding repetition to recover Dust's former skill, and joins approval of Keith's undisclosed target. Dust's broad assent comes before he knows what is proposed. When he tries to leave, Keith and Taylor grip his shoulders and invoke the earlier answer. The subsequent binding and aerial transport are a group operation; the passage does not identify every hand that ties a knot or cuts the rope. Taylor's directly narrated restraint and approval are sufficient evidence of involvement without assigning him all the unprinted mechanics. (DUST07 U011 P735–785.)

The result includes a drop close to the ground, abandonment despite Dust's plea, pursuit and actual unwanted contact described retrospectively after he escapes. The scene distinguishes that contact from a further act narrowly avoided. Dust's own unperformed retaliatory threats do not excuse what his companions did. Recovered spear skill is a real outcome, but cannot make the process voluntary, safe or justified. A model that always makes Taylor choose a clean protective alternative would fail to represent this event. (P797–889.)

Taylor initiates the later apology and physically presents a money bag. The group offers compensation; Leen offers future care for Feit; Dust relents. These are meaningful moves toward repair. The exact personal contribution of each member, final use of the money and completion of the offered care are not shown. Dust's fear persists, affects his interpretation of Leen's approach and leads him to seek distance. Neither accepting a bag nor resuming company establishes complete restoration. (P899–958.)

A subsequent dinner displays selective disengagement. Keith insults the others and is forcibly taken outside by Leen and Lolisa despite protesting. Dust and Taylor choose to ignore the event. Sounds of an explosion and scream follow, but the outside caster, exact spell and injury are not identified. Taylor's disapproval of Keith's earlier speech is not an obligation to assist that he necessarily honors; equally, the insult does not erase Keith's status as a recipient of coercion. His deliberate nonhelp is contrary evidence against a universal protector. (DUST07 U012 P275–315.)

Taylor can later restrain Keith, lend a shield and apologize for interrupting Dust and Leen. These are subsequent choices, not retrospective permissions for the training or removal. The final epilogue gives Taylor a personal apology and private praise of Dust. It does not narrate a comprehensive settlement of earlier physical and psychological costs. (DUST07 U013 P488–494; U014 P104–108/P133.)

## Particular companions and an ordinary social life

Taylor's relation to Dust is neither unquestioning admiration nor constant contempt. He proposes search work in DUST05 and participates in travel accommodation, but has restricted information about Dust and Feit. Leen learns Feit's identity at a particular later point, while the others receive a cover story. Taylor's later account of recovering from Serena's influence is a report about magical manipulation; it cannot be read as an uncoerced preference for the earlier condition. His meal promise and the group's cash check do not print the final bill. (DUST05 U011 P442–452; U012 P643–712; earlier exact receiving applies to the search window.)

He can ask about an invitation and correct a country assumption by pointing to the written text. That small act belongs with the more conspicuous investigation procedure. He can also repeat a gossip-based belief that Dust confessed to Leen and was accepted. Taylor's conviction does not supply mutual romantic agreement or eliminate the difference between the genuine woman and earlier disguises. (DUST-CONT U010 P25–64/P103–113.)

His friendship with Keith includes cooperation, disagreement and unequal intervention. They investigate together, help block Dust's exit together and act as escorts in Leen's account. Taylor rejects Keith's attempt to attribute jealousy to him and says he celebrates a companion's happiness. He also removes himself with Feit when a social moment becomes awkward. These acts support a more differentiated social role than either an inseparable pair of troublemakers or an infallible adult supervising children. (DUST-CONT U010 P103–110; U012 P84–95/P465–468; DUST07 U011 P941.)

Ordinary comfort matters. In the continuation Taylor joins the group's unease in an unusually luxurious inn; familiarity with battle armor does not translate into complete ease with status or costly surroundings. The Axel epilogue records worry that Dust's elevated reputation could distance him from ordinary friends. That is a relation under possible change, not knowledge of Kazuma's outcome or a completed abandonment. Drinking, shared meals, teasing and concern belong to his ordinary life without fixing favorite food, finances, romantic experience or a permanent private aspiration. (DUST-CONT U012 P1–16; DUST07 U014 P11–40.)

## Voice, comedy and the reconstruction boundary

Taylor often presents a measured proposition, obligation or practical correction in plain Japanese. His wait-for-all request and groom-perspective question are direct, organized speech; their social function comes from content and audience as well as grammar. His use of俺 is compatible with that measured style. More formal-sounding vocabulary, including religious or duty language, does not by itself make every sentence grammatical polite register. A contrast with Kazuma or Keith must likewise be established at the exact utterances rather than assigned from their roles. No acoustic, dialect, ruby or native-human performance claim is made here. (DUST-CONT U012 P118/P170; U014 P304–317.)

His comedy repeatedly depends on a gap between honorable presentation and local group conduct. A serious leader is helpful when assembling reports, too optimistic about motive in another scene, complicit in an ugly training method, and quietly absent from a companion's need for intervention. Treating every inconsistency as a nonliteral gag would remove the behavior a reconstruction must reproduce. Treating every honorable statement as a secretly cynical mask would remove the material aid and real corrective procedure. Some sincere good intentions, biased judgment and selective loyalty can coexist; the text does not identify their exact relative weights.

The evidence earns a substantial monograph and a conditional reconstruction candidate. A useful model should generate responsibility taking, deference to role, concrete group support, occasional information discipline and recipient perspective, while allowing idealization, conformity and deliberate nonhelp. A generic conscientious leader with ordinary in-group bias is a serious rival. The candidate is differentiated by the observed combinations and specified departure conditions, not by a claim that its latent mechanism has been uniquely proven. Its transfer to unfamiliar institutions, private romance, medical decisions and solitary long-term planning remains weak. Known-source hypothetical checks can expose contradictions or wording defects; they cannot retrospectively validate Taylor or raise confidence in the source interpretation.
