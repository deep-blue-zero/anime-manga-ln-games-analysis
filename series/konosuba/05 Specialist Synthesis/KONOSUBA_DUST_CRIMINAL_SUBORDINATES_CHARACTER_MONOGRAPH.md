# The chief's subordinates — separate preferences without invented individual biographies

## 1. What this route contains

Existing census396 holds the two-subordinate route encountered with the chief in DUST02 and partially re-encountered in DUST03. It is a mapping responsibility, not a claim that one composite man speaks every line. This monograph gives the substantial material its proper space while preserving exact local ownership. The [cohort index](../08%20Evidence%20and%20Indexes/KONOSUBA_DUST_CRIMINAL_TRIO_COHORT42_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md) supplies the event table and [provenance](../08%20Evidence%20and%20Indexes/KONOSUBA_DUST_CRIMINAL_TRIO_COHORT42_NATIVE_PROVENANCE.json) the actual native windows.

DUST01U017 shows the chief with one man. Dust kicks, strips and robs that subordinate. DUST02U015P347–348 explicitly recognizes the stripped man, establishing a positive return. The third man present in DUST02 is not thereby proved to be the earlier temporary warrior, road confection-maker, archer or any particular masked attacker. Those are separate unresolved joins. A local っす speaker and an オイラ speaker can be distinguished in alternating turns without turning those forms into permanent name tags.

## 2. Chronology and changing information

The DUST01 subordinate first helps discuss the Leen abduction. He knows the party's composition and the supposed ally's reception (U017P241–253). The earlier noblewoman attempt is explicitly recognized as part of their group history. No tag maps this man to one particular face or weapon in the six-masked-person scene. Being present in the larger group is different from owning every reported act.

The man is then incapacitated before the chief is questioned. Dust later reports that the subordinate knows the meeting location (P302); the precise information-extraction exchange is not directly depicted. DUST02's three-man encounter adds disagreement, surrender, unusual binding requests, a warning-reading contribution, rescue and physical protection. DUST03 adds collective and chief-local opposition around the lake. Repeated encounters need not produce a smooth moral reform, and none supplies unrestricted access to all three men's private history.

## 3. Different stated preferences

The U017 subordinate prefers the conjunction of mental and bodily youth, while the chief argues for a broader imagined category (P245–249). That difference is substantive. The subordinate's praise of a term introduced by the chief shows engagement with the argument; it does not show permanent conversion or erase the original contrast. Both men nevertheless discuss taking Leen to a noble. Instructions about gentleness constrain a harmful plan; they do not authorize it.

DUST02 separates two desired recipients during the binding exchange. The っす-side turn and オイラ turn at P385–386, with the later carrying turns at P440–441, support a local distinction between interest in the small worker and interest in ponytailed Leen. The recognition/blushing sequence may suggest a link to the stripped man, but it is contextual. It does not explicitly attach every earlier, later or stylistically similar line to him. A biography assembled by guessing those tags would claim more than the text gives.

## 4. Affect, humiliation and outward response

The DUST01 subordinate is kicked unconscious, stripped and used in Dust's sexual-coercion threat toward the chief. The narrative cut strongly implies an imposed aftermath, while leaving its exact act off-page (P260–299). Lack of spoken protest during unconsciousness is not assent. The chief's reaction cannot substitute for this man's own remembered experience.

DUST02's visible embarrassment and specific refusal or eagerness belong to the particular tagged moment. They do not retrospectively authorize Dust's earlier treatment. Conversely, the capacity to experience humiliation does not make the men harmless: their abduction discussion and later demands remain. Affect can be individual while its cross-scene owner remains unresolved. That is a reason to keep the evidence, rather than manufacture a psychologically neat account.

## 5. Capability and distributed knowledge

One subordinate supplies practical party and infiltration information in DUST01. Dust reports his knowledge of the rendezvous. In DUST02 a subordinate reads or reports the red warning at P417–428, but the precise reader is not securely mapped across the mixed speech forms. One form resembles っす while another uses やした. Counting that passage as a single man's demonstrated literacy or negligence would make the uncertainty disappear by fiat.

The earlier difficulty reading a comic at P324 is also local. Without establishing the same individual, script or relevant task, the warning passage is not a proven literacy contradiction. Nor does comic-book interest imply inability to understand danger. The rescue shows actual carrying, movement and shielding by the men together; it does not identify a unique magical skill or a full combat ranking for each subordinate.

## 6. Refusal, surrender and rescue

The men initially stand with the chief and have numerical advantage over Dust. All three disarm when he takes the sleeping worker as a feigned hostage (P365–376). The action is shared and actual. Dust's staging, her sleepy no-touch protest and the women's later criticisms remain separate from the men's response. The source does not independently reveal whether fear for her, a group rule, fear of Dust or another concern dominates each man.

After activation, the chief takes the small worker and the two subordinates carry Leen (P440–451). One local exchange preserves a wish to carry a particular recipient, while the other response redirects the task. These are choices, not interchangeable background motion. All three shield the women from falling rock (P477–478). Later rope-pulling follows Lolisa's conditional offer, and the contribution really occurs (P512–523). A promised dream reward remains promised; an observed rope action remains observed even if the motivation is mixed.

## 7. Group relations and moral asymmetry

The chief's authority is present in address and assignments, but it does not own every subordinate's thought. Shared predatory plans coexist with differences in preference, knowledge and response. The two subordinates' useful carrying cannot be reassigned to the chief, and his objections cannot automatically become theirs. Their relation to Lolisa includes apparent desire, susceptibility to reward and actual protection; none is a standing claim on her body or labor.

Leen is an intended target of earlier coercion and a later rescue recipient. Her rescue does not establish forgiveness, intimacy or retroactive consent to abduction. Dust is an aggressor and temporary collaborator. His favorable interpretation of protective conduct does not independently audit motives, while his contempt does not erase a contribution. These relations remain unequal and partly undescribed outside the episodes.

## 8. Work and ordinary interests

The abduction is work for money within the chief's account. Printed material, private preferences and hoped-for service are meaningful ordinary objects and goals, though ownership of each collection item is not fully mapped. The road group's references to the absent chief's collection and an Iris photo book belong to those speakers, not automatically to either of the dungeon subordinates.

DUST02U016P17 shows the trio declining cash. Dust expects another form of benefit; the text does not display its fulfillment. The budget mentioned by the worker is distinct from each man's personal income. No home, family, regular legitimate employment, savings, exact age or stable partnership is supplied. These gaps do not make the observed material trivial, but they limit a complete individual biography.

## 9. Japanese voices and attribution discipline

The dialogue uses rough colloquial address, deferential references to the chief, っす and related endings, and the self-reference オイラ. These forms help track adjacent turns when the exchange contrasts recipients or answers. They are not sufficient alone to join distant lines. The same speaker can vary register; a similar ending can be used by more than one person. Explicit narration and continuity are stronger attribution evidence than a single particle.

Some turns are strikingly ceremonious about gentleness or a supposed mission while participating in abduction. That pragmatic mismatch is part of the comedy and self-presentation. It does not make the underlying coercive plan fictitious. An English paraphrase should not smooth the men into either identical thugs or harmless enthusiasts. Written Japanese/ruby reception supports these bounded observations, with no acoustic or native-human generation judgment.

## 10. Comic reversals and costs

The preference debate interrupts a criminal plan, the binding request reverses an expected fear response, and reward-supported cooperation produces useful emergency work. These reversals are distinct mechanisms. The source also retains involuntary exposure, a hostage tactic, physical peril and a threatened destruction of valued materials. A later comic willingness in one situation cannot supply consent in an earlier one.

DUST03 presents more than ten guards and several collective voices. The chief is individually recognized closer up, but the source does not allocate every insult, health claim, objection or departure to these two men. Dust admits important persuasion stories are false. Fruit effects and his mind-control hypothesis do not become a verified uniform diagnosis for every guard. Lolisa's client recognition is collective, not an individualized roll call.

## 11. Alternative accounts and what would resolve them

A generic criminal-helper account captures group work and leverage but loses the recipient disagreement, local information and actual protection. A purely benevolent account cannot absorb the abduction plan. A uniform preference account fails against the chief/subordinate disagreement and the locally opposed carrying wishes. A wholly helpless account fails against refusal, concealment and effective aid.

Several richer individual accounts remain possible, but they depend on assigning the warning reader, stripped man and later desired-recipient turns to stable owners. An explicit name, direct recognition or independently tagged return could support that allocation. Repeated style alone cannot. If a later source separates them differently, this route can split with its original uncertainty retained; there is no need to rewrite the first record as if the identity had always been known.

## 12. Current responsibility

Tier2 treatment is earned for this substantial, source-bound subordinate route. The account deliberately remains asymmetric: particular local acts and contrasts are strong; person-wide motives and longitudinal mappings are weak. It is not a single composite personality and does not certify two disjoint global people. Two separate generative models would presently turn unresolved attribution into invented evidence, so none is authored.

No permanent low ceiling follows from being unnamed. Further tagged evidence can earn separate individual studies or a scoped model.
