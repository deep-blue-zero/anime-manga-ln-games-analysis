# Okina: expertise, secrecy and a harvest business

**オキナ / Okina** is the named client and instructor in the Core supplied scenario **このタケノコに美味しさを**, native **TRPG-CORE-S300–313**, printed **298–311**. His principal speech and actions lie at S303–305 and S312. He is presented as an elderly man who looks to be in his late sixties and has forty years of bamboo-harvest experience. Those are an appearance estimate and a stated career duration, not an exact birth date. The wagon's driver introduced by Luna is Okina himself; the text does not introduce an additional coachman.

The supplied scene sequence provides enough concentrated characterization for a scoped Tier2 monograph, with no reconstruction model. All meetings, player responses, rewards and outcomes remain authored scenario material. Reading the script does not perform the adventure, prove player agreement or count a new psychological trial.

## A livelihood with material constraints

Luna recruits novice adventurers to harvest Okina's grove after his back trouble prevents his usual work. The task has a deadline, a base payment and quality-dependent additions. The shoots are most valuable just before they begin moving; movement lowers both food quality and the corresponding benefit. Expertise here includes timing, equipment and selection, rather than a general claim to know everything about the forest (S300–303).

Okina owns the secret grove and lends the necessary baskets and spades. He explains that the tools can stop or put the shoots to sleep, tells the novices to secure them in the basket promptly, identifies warning signs and points out a usable route. These instructions support local practical competence. They do not name a performed Sleep spell, establish a universal technique for every plant or prove that the player's eventual harvest will succeed (S304–305).

The scenario describes a physically costly business. Okina rubs his sore back while smiling; he drives rather than accompanies the party through every hazardous area. The text neither diagnoses a chronic condition nor shows a cure. Allowing novices to undertake the labor is a chosen response to a specific constraint, mediated through the guild, with equipment and warnings rather than an invented personal apprenticeship.

## Secrecy, scheduling and access

The meeting is at three in the morning. Okina apologizes for the early hour and explains that the work must occur in the morning. He requests blindfolds to protect the grove's location. The darkness means removing them would not reveal much during the journey, but that explanation does not make the requirement fictitious (S303–304).

This is restricted access to a productive resource. It should not be reduced either to pure hospitality or to proof of malicious captivity. Luna introduces the client, the proposed task has an explicit refusal branch, and the equipment is loaned for a defined purpose. The text does not record what a real player group agrees to, how informed every PC feels or whether the secrecy would survive an alternative route. The branch is a design condition, not a consent measurement.

## Expertise with a consequential memory limit

Okina admits that he has added traps each year and no longer remembers where he placed them. The grove can therefore be well managed for harvest and dangerous to newcomers at the same time (S304). His warning is useful, but does not neutralize that danger. The already dead goblin in the pit is authorial scenario background, distinct from any later player casualty or kill (S306).

No clinical explanation for the forgotten placements is given. Forty years of work, apparent age and a memory lapse do not warrant a diagnosis. Nor does a local lapse establish total incompetence: he still knows harvest timing, supplies appropriate tools and describes signs of a shoot about to move. His expertise and the costs of his protective infrastructure coexist.

On hearing about goblins in the proposed return scene, he plans to add more traps (S312). That response protects his resource through the same means that already create an access hazard. It supports an attributable tension between security and safe access. The additional traps are a future plan, not a newly completed installation, and the text does not isolate whether deterrence, profit, habit or anger dominates his choice.

## Failed tourism and bounded hospitality

Okina says he prepared for experiential farming, but abandoned it because the early schedule did not appeal to customers (S304). The statement gives a reported unsuccessful business experiment. It does not enumerate actual dissatisfied guests or show a particular customer being harmed. The source offers no financial totals or independently verified loss.

He encourages tasting fresh shoots raw and explains that eating them reduces the settlement by the consumed amount. This is an offer within the transaction, not unconditional free food. Later he offers breakfast at his own expense after the work and appraisal (S305, S312). That later generosity qualifies a purely extractive reading without demonstrating universal altruism. Acceptance, actual eating, payment and any friendship beyond the job remain dependent on the scenario's branches.

## Relationships and separate professional roles

Luna arranges the job, introduces Okina and handles the guild-facing settlement. Okina provides the resource, instruction, transport and tools. An unnamed professional vegetable broker appraises the returned goods beside Luna (S312). Appraisal is not automatically Okina's private act or proof that Luna is the broker. The three functions can cooperate without becoming one person.

The goblins are intruders in authorial background; Okina's knowledge of their presence depends on the proposed report. His warnings against moving shoots express a practical safety boundary. They do not prove friendship with every novice, concern for every goblin or responsibility for every subsequent choice. The players can decline the job or retreat; no successful harvest is guaranteed by his confidence.

## Voice and interpretive limits

His scripted register mixes an early apology, practical explanations, gentle imperatives, **ワシ / わし** self-reference in the ending and an offered meal. It supports a locally affable instructor, with proprietary restrictions and fallible memory. A smile is not a complete motive, and the scenario's explanatory dialogue is selected to teach a procedure rather than sample his ordinary conversation without constraint.

Most competence claims concern one livelihood. Combat ability, family, tastes beyond the food offer, beliefs about the gods and behavior outside the job are unknown.
