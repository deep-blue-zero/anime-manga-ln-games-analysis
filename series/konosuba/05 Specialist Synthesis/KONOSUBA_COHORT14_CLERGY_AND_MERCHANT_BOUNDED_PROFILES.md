## The unnamed Axis church guide — practical service, sect hostility and available money

**Identity.** MAIN16 U016 P87 explicitly identifies the woman Kazuma is talking to as the person who guided him on his first church visit. The corresponding MAIN04 encounter is U019 P278–310: one female believer is sweeping the church floor, tells him where his companions are and directs his attention to the confessional. This is a source-grounded co-reference rather than a name inferred from job or appearance. The earlier precise-return gap in current census row170 can now be narrowed. Her personal name, rank and clerical qualification remain unestablished. Neither passage identifies her as Cecily or Tristan.

At the earlier visit she begins with an invitation that Kazuma takes personally, then calls it a joke and mocks him for taking it seriously. She says Zesta and the other members are away; her self-correction from playing to spreading Aqua's name makes the presentation itself noticeable. She still provides actionable help: the companions are inside, one is using the confessional and the other can be found farther in. Her statement that the church's priests are away and the visiting archpriest has been entrusted with the confessional is a report of assignment. It does not show her personally making that assignment, prove she is a lay member, or supply the visiting archpriest's actual consent. (MAIN04 U019 P278–304.)

Her hostility to Darkness has its own action. As Darkness passes, she sweeps the collected rubbish toward her, then offers a pseudo-apology that blames the Eris amulet for being mistaken for garbage. Darkness's reaction belongs to Darkness; it cannot grant permission on behalf of all recipients or make the imposition unreal. Children throwing stones at Darkness earlier in the scene are different actors. The guide notices that attack and asks Kazuma about it, and Kazuma chases the children away. It is inaccurate to give the guide the children's throwing hands. (P290–310.)

By MAIN16 she is guarding the church with a mace and shield while most believers are away. She says the others are hunting monsters on Zesta's instructions and quotes Aqua's request for a safe, quick route to the castle. This is her report and quotation, not a newly watched spell or kill by the absent leader. She wanted to go but lost the decision over who would stay. Her duty and disappointment are both present; a wish to be elsewhere does not mean she has left the post. (U016 P76–96.)

She can recognize a cost created by her own group without surrendering its public innocence. She first attributes the church's bad reputation to evil Eris believers, then says non-Axis townspeople are retaliating for the group's habitual misconduct. Kazuma points out the contradiction. This juxtaposition supports selective public framing and in-group defense, but does not establish that she has a single consistent private theory of all the incidents. She threatens would-be intruders with the mace; no mace assault is performed in this received exchange. (P81–89.)

There is, however, an actual stone impact in the later scene. She kicks a stone at P93; it hits Darkness's shin guard at P94. She shows no regret and ignores Darkness's distress while talking enthusiastically about Aqua at P97. Whether she intended that precise impact is not established. The kick must be distinguished from Kazuma's later reference to the earlier crowd throwing stones at Aqua. The guide denies personal knowledge and shifts that earlier blame to infiltrating Eris believers; Kazuma calls the explanation a lie. The received passages do not individually identify her as an earlier thrower at Aqua. Recording the limit on that accusation must not erase her current, directly narrated kick. (P93–107.)

Her religious response is specific. Aqua's name brings visible delight; she gives a detailed account of the visit and says recognition of Aqua's identity is obvious. Her explicit present knowledge at P103 cannot be moved backward to a time before it is supplied. Earlier she might recognize an unusual visitor without having this later knowledge. The source does not disclose exactly when or how she learned it. Her sincere excitement, church defense and practical information coexist with sect-directed contempt. No generic rule that faith makes her helpful to everyone follows.

Ordinary costs also matter to her. She says a failed market venture has left her short of money and describes an unsuccessful attempt to attract the well-equipped traveler. The financial loss and attempt are her retrospective account, not independently audited investments or a narrator-certified relationship. When Kazuma asks about fast horses she identifies the church's likely suitable animals, insults Darkness and is then met with an offer of substantial cash. Her response becomes a blessing. The following already received MAIN16 horse sequence confirms actual access, while the exact cash transfer, rental terms and full mechanism of any intended trap are not supplied here. A transaction can change her treatment without proving either universal greed or an ethical reconciliation. (P113–122; cohort13's separately complete U016 context supplies the later horse use.)

Her speech moves among courteous reception, joking provocation, sect moralization, sharp insult, devotional excitement and transactional warmth. The apology after sweeping rubbish has a different pragmatic force from a straightforward admission of accidental harm. Her careful public denial also differs from her admission of habitual group misconduct. Printed form needs recipient and event context; it does not itself prove kindness or deception. These BASE returns do not certify complete ruby, rhythm, acoustic performance or native-human Japanese generation.

**Present disposition.** This is a scoped Tier1 profile: two linked visits yield concrete work, information, resentment, belief, preference, financial pressure, harmful action and shifts of presentation. The evidence supports useful bounded individual interpretation but leaves most biography, other relationships and exact private weighting unknown. A generic partisan caretaker with money pressure is a serious rival. The scope is not a fixed ceiling: further dense evidence could warrant a monograph or a differentiated falsifiable model. No new model or hypothetical battery is automatically earned by resolving the alias route.

**What changes and what stays open.** The exact MAIN04 return closes a locator gap for the existing guide record; it creates no second guide and supplies no personal name. Her rubbish sweep, stone kick, mace threat and the mob's throwing retain their separate actors and action stages. The older cohort13 account and first bodies keep their historical boundary. The caravan leader, hotel greeter, children and other workers remain individual census-return responsibilities rather than being absorbed into her profile. Full census, broader artifacts and later synthesis/model/integration/release work continue.

## The Axel Eris priest — service claims, an enforced boundary and an embarrassed escalation

**Identity and scope.** The unnamed priest appears at the Axel Eris church in CONTEXP01 U015 P327–347. This priest is a different source-bound individual from the female Arcanretia priest who later tells Megumin they have not met. Role, sect and the similar personal insult cannot establish a cross-city identity. Gender, personal name, age, rank and a broader biography are not supplied in this received Axel exchange. Cecily's language about the priest's body is provocation, not an identity record.

The Axel priest's first response is a refusal grounded in an alleged history: Cecily has come again despite being told repeatedly to stay away. The priest opens the door, hears the group's account and gives a practical explanation of the church's work. Healing adventurers and providing meals consume their time, the priest says; food is not lacking, and theft would violate their service to Eris. The activity and resource claims are the priest's account rather than newly watched healing, an audited pantry or proof that every member is incapable of theft. The actual encounter shows a boundary asserted against repeated visits. It does not show the priest stealing Axis food. (U015 P327–331.)

The exchange then separates public composure from personal vulnerability. Cecily says she ate conspicuously in front of the priest, challenges the priest's account of indifference and supplies a disparaging explanation involving food and bodily development. The narrator directly records a moment of discomfort; Cecily's recollection of the earlier encounter remains Cecily's report. The priest defends the sect's ideal of honorable poverty and then answers angrily. This supports embarrassment and anger under targeted humiliation. It does not independently establish Cecily's nutritional explanation, deprivation level, body history or the priest's settled private self-image. (P332–337.)

When Megumin criticizes both clerics for quarrelling, the priest blushes and acknowledges shame. Megumin tentatively interprets the embarrassment as awareness of descending to Cecily's level; that precise inner explanation remains a narrator inference. Cecily immediately taunts the priest for being reprimanded and adds another insult. The priest threatens to take a mace to the Axis church. No such visit or mace strike is performed here. Megumin makes Cecily apologize and the party leaves; the priest neither independently grants forgiveness nor promises a repaired relationship in the received passage. (P338–347.)

The priest's defense of service, immediate anger, acknowledgment of embarrassment and threatened retaliation can coexist. A general image of a perpetually mild Eris cleric would suppress the actual response; a general image of an equally culpable sect combatant would suppress Cecily's arrival, continuing provocations and the prior no-visit boundary. The comic framing does not equalize every action or turn a threat into a completed assault. Cecily's later attempted graffiti is stopped by Yunyun and Iris, not by the priest. (P349–352.)

The priest's speech moves from formal refusal and doctrinal justification to an angry rebuke, a hesitant acknowledgment of shame and a conditional threat. Those changes are pragmatic evidence for this interaction; BASE text does not establish full ruby, vocal timing or a Japanese generation capability. **Present disposition: scoped Tier1 profile.** The scene supplies work, a prior boundary, affect, an individual reaction and limited corrective receptiveness, but gives little secure evidence about the priest's decisions away from harassment. An ordinary overburdened cleric reacting to a repeat nuisance is a substantial rival explanation. No distinct reconstruction model, fixed ceiling, hidden theft, performed retaliation or universal moral rule follows.

## The Arcanretia Eris priest — a denied story, available skill and an underfunded church

**Identity and chronology.** CONTEXP02 U016 P445 explicitly identifies a female priest at the Arcanretia Eris church. At P476 the priest tells Megumin they are meeting for the first time. This is positive evidence against casually joining her to the Axel priest whom Megumin already encountered in CONTEXP01. Zesta's and Cecily's familiarity with this woman supplies a local history of contact, not a personal name, a romance or an earlier relationship with Megumin. The priest says she is the church's sole current occupant and struggles even to support herself as the Axis church gains influence. Those staffing and financial statements are her own account. (P476–479.)

Zesta and Cecily arrive with a coordinated ambush despite Megumin's objection. Zesta publicly asserts that the priest confessed love to him, deliberately drawing the priest out. Cecily trips and restrains the priest while Zesta approaches. The priest protests the public statement, denies the invented confession and explains that prior insults and lectures arose from continuing nuisance behavior at her church. The source supplies the grievance; Zesta supplies the claim that hostility means hidden attraction. His interpretation cannot erase her denial or convert it into consent. The source does not independently show an earlier confession. (P434–461.)

The priest's response is more than verbal indignation. As Zesta threatens further unwanted contact and Cecily encourages him, the priest invokes Eris and casts Powered. The narration then directly describes improved physical ability, Cecily being thrown off and a punch delivered to Zesta's cheek. The cast and escape are performed. By contrast, the threatened forced contact is not shown completing before she escapes. The observed spell is evidence of usable skill in these local conditions; it does not establish that every future restraint can be escaped, every spell is available or the priest could have prevented the ambush in advance. (P464–467.)

Afterward the priest issues an angry threat to send the attackers to Aqua. That threat is not a performed killing, transport or new spell. Zesta responds by generalizing the priest's violence into a claim about Eris and her followers, while Cecily offers Megumin protection from the supposedly violent cleric. Their frame omits their own preceding ambush. Megumin's comic description of the clerics' brawl records exasperation but does not supply equal initiation or responsibility. The priest's resistance is intelligible as defense against the specific attack without requiring an invented universal ethical rule. (P468–475.)

The priest is not simply an interchangeable emblem of virtuous charity. On recognizing Megumin as a Crimson Demon, the priest trails off in a way that prompts Megumin's challenge. The text supports a socially legible hesitation and Megumin's reaction, while the exact unspoken judgment remains unknown. The priest then denies keeping a pet, explains the church's scarcity and prompts Megumin to offer a small later donation. The offer is not a completed donation. Zesta falsely recasts Megumin's report of a small dark creature as an attempt to scare the woman who sleeps alone at the church. The priest's suspicion of Megumin follows this supplied context; it is not evidence that she knowingly hides the creature or knows its entire origin. (P476–485.)

Later Zesta says the Eris church is probably innocent. An inn witness then reports a slime escaping with food, and Megumin rejects another attempt to blame Eris believers. These are meaningful limits on the original accusation; they do not show that the priest receives an apology, compensation or a reliable promise against another visit. Nor does Zesta's later qualification certify every possible action of every Eris adherent. (U018 P3–30.)

The Arcanretia priest moves from outrage and public denial to explanation, an actual spell under restraint, a defensive strike, renewed anger and a subdued account of material scarcity. Available power and difficult ordinary life coexist. Her grammar and invocations have different functions from Zesta's invented romantic frame; English paraphrase cannot certify full Japanese performance. **Present disposition: scoped Tier1 profile.** The scene earns individual treatment for agency, recipient refusal, competence, resource pressure and an imperfect social response. It does not yet separate a stable decision rule from the strong rival of an isolated caretaker under repeated harassment. Further evidence could earn a larger monograph or model; neither a model nor a permanently low tier follows automatically.

## The merchant association chair — commercial persuasion, practical limits and hospitality

**Identity.** The unnamed chair is the civic business representative in the MAIN08 festival negotiation and its subsequent board and reward references. The within-volume role continuity is explicit; no personal name or join to a caravan leader, inn operator or other official is supplied. The chair's direct acts must be separated from the board's unassigned voices, Darkness's permits, Cecily's assault, Kazuma's decisions and the workers' own participation.

The chair initially refuses Cecily's request for a joint festival. Cecily physically attacks the chair, and Kazuma intervenes to end the immediate assault. The chair then hears Kazuma's different proposal: commercial benefit from an energetic festival and competing religious participation. The negotiation changes when Kazuma asks who supplies the funds. The chair describes an existing arrangement in which the Eris church pays most, with nobles and merchants making up the difference. Kazuma suggests also making the Axis church contribute. The chair immediately accepts the proposal and offers Kazuma a paid advisory role; Kazuma accepts that role. The source therefore gives an actual agreement between these negotiators, not merely interest in a suggestion. It does not separately show each prospective funder's assent or payment at this point. (U013 P183–239.)

Profit is a concrete attraction, but the chair is not uniformly blind to practical costs. At a later board meeting, Kazuma proposes putting vendors in swimsuits. The chair worries that taking the idea too far will bring police intervention. Another director identifies that concern as the chair's and adds the risk that next year's festival could be restricted. Other board participants urge the immediate commercial gain. Kazuma proposes water sprinkling, Aqua's association with water and heat protection as a public justification. The board applauds; Darkness later receives that account and issues the permit. The chair's caution is locally about official consequences, not a demonstrated objection on behalf of vendors. Equally, collective approval does not identify the chair as author of every shouted compliment or as the person who grants legal permission. (U014 P247–276.)

The fireworks disagreement exposes a different limit on cooperation. The chair explains that the usual caster is away because of military activity and says the event cannot proceed with the available firepower. Kazuma insists on preserving the occasion and suggests a companion's Explosion. Board voices reject that idea as threatening the festival itself. Kazuma grabs the nearby chair, who demands a usable alternative and protests an adventurer's violence against a civilian. A call to surround Kazuma comes from an unidentified board voice; Kazuma later appears injured. The sequence supports a conflict involving the board and a consequence for Kazuma, but does not identify each blow or prove that the chair personally orders or performs the later beating. Darkness's subsequent permission for explosive potions is Darkness's act. (P280–307.)

The chair later helps host a gathering that keeps Kazuma involved. Kazuma arrives privately intending to resign, but has not yet told the room that intention. The chair pushes the frozen Kazuma toward a seat and introduces two familiar workers as members of the merchant community who run a small food business and want to express thanks for the clothing permission. The workers then offer hospitality and pour a drink, and the chair leads a toast to Kazuma's work and future sales. These are actual social actions and a commercially framed account. They do not establish that the chair already knew of the planned resignation, designed the whole gathering specifically to prevent it, knew every fact Kazuma knows about the workers, or guaranteed an unshown sexual service. The workers retain their own offered and performed actions. (P645–673.)

The closing scene returns to the promised fee. Darkness tells Kazuma that the chair brought a reward to the house and attributed the joint festival, swimsuit vendors and other proposals to him. Kazuma treats the visit as the apparent reason his conduct has become known. Darkness actually shows a bag containing a large sum. There is therefore more support than an unfulfilled initial promise: a recipient reports the chair's delivery and the money is visibly present. The delivery itself occurs offstage, however; the exact amount and Kazuma's acceptance or final disposition of the bag remain unshown in this return. The chair's disclosure has consequences for Kazuma without proving a deliberate betrayal or prior knowledge of his domestic situation. (U016 P500–509.)

The chair's social stance changes with the interaction: resistant gatekeeper, receptive negotiating partner, cautious board participant, target of renewed aggression, genial host and reported payer. Polite business language and an advisory title are instruments of this relationship, not proof of disinterested friendship. The repeated sales motive supports commercial interest; worries over police and unavailable magic prevent reducing every decision to an invariant rush for immediate revenue. Public financing and official permission are also real constraints rather than mere personality defects.

**Present disposition: scoped Tier1 profile.** The chair earns a bounded account with several linked decisions, a direct negotiation, a practical objection, disagreement and ordinary hospitality/payment. Its explanatory limit is that a generic commercially minded civic organizer fits much of the same evidence. The source does not yet securely differentiate the chair's wider tastes, private weighting, biography or conduct outside this festival. A larger monograph or a falsifiable reconstruction model would need that distinction rather than a longer paraphrase or a self-designed hypothetical success. This tier is revisable, and the earlier mid-negotiation limit remains a preserved historical receiving boundary rather than a reason to deny the now-read agreement.
