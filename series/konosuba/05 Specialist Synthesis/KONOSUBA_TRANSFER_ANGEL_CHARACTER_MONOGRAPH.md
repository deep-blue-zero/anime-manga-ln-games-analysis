## Acceptance is an act of authority

The angel accepts the choice and says that she will take over the work130. She continues the handover140 and turns to Kazuma with a gentle smile142 while Aqua's objections fail to stop the process. Courteous presentation and consequential coercion coexist. It would be too generous to treat politeness as proof of universally attentive care; it would be too strong to infer a private grudge, hidden ambition, or enjoyment of Aqua's distress.

Distinguishing these levels permits an account of her responsibility without granting universal authority over heaven's rules.

## A worker-centered interpretation

The replacement angel makes a decisive institutional answer, promises continuity, performs a calm ceremonial interaction, and is later reported to disclose risks with consequences for recruitment. Her personal ordinary life, attachments outside the office, responses to correction, and choices under competing loyalties remain weakly specified. The evidence supports a dedicated Tier2 study of those tensions, not a saintly bureaucrat or a malicious careerist.

No generative model is added at this stage. A conscientious disclosure practice, literal procedural compliance, the administrative role itself, and interested narratorial comparison still overlap. A model that simply makes her read every warning and approve every formally allowed choice would risk being a job template.
