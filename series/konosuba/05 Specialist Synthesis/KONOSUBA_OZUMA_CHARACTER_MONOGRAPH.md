# Ozuma — reciprocal help, injured reputation and excessive trust in capability

## Identity and access

ザトゥ・オズマ introduces himself at YORI-01 U024 P21, `text/part0022.html`. Ozuma is the working Roman label for this individual. He travels with Akyua, Megumi and Rakuresu; none is an alias of Kazuma's party. Similar names and a deliberately parallel party composition create misidentifications within the story. They do not supply identity, reincarnation or shared psychology. The [cohort evidence index](../08%20Evidence%20and%20Indexes/KONOSUBA_YORIMICHI01_VISITORS_COHORT43_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md) binds the complete 472-paragraph context and its source access. These findings use Kazuma's retrospective narration, direct exchanges and explicitly reported histories, with no independent reader or pristine holdout claim.

## Help before a bargain, and a bargain with obligations

Ozuma makes water for the choking stranger at P25–26 before obtaining information or a paid service. The conspicuously threatening appearance through which Kazuma initially reads him therefore does not exhaust his conduct. At the meal, Ozuma insists that the food is payment for information and that Megumi let the informant eat without being hurried (P76). This protects the recipient's part of the transaction rather than simply maximizing the party's return. He is capable of recognizing a small, ordinary obligation without a heroic audience.

The meal is also an epistemically vulnerable arrangement. Kazuma controls both his alias and a selective account of his own history. The visitors' sympathy responds to that account; it is not independent corroboration of its framing. Megumi later explains the party's decision to consult other accounts (P180–186). Her quoted explanation does not establish that Ozuma personally conducted every inquiry. His own next-day shouting, duel demand and death threat are actual acts (P149/156), not merely Kazuma's fear of his face.

Ozuma is about to strike the door when Kazuma threatens a property-damage charge; he stops (P173–175). The source does not first show him breaking the door. A practical consequence can inhibit the action while leaving anger and the dispute active. Calling this either fearless justice or harmless bluster loses evidence. His intervention when Megumi begins an apology also prevents her from being talked into capitulation (P194–196), but protective loyalty here coexists with the quartet's unfair comparisons and insults. Protection of a companion is not proof that the protected position is correct.

## Work, limited appraisal and useful danger attention

The visitors spend two weeks working locally, and guild voices acknowledge their useful work (P252–255). The source supplies praise and reported rescues, not an audited personal kill total. When a wounded man is brought in, Ozuma's silent headshake follows his inspection of the injury (P294). Aqua's subsequent healing reveals a mismatch between his current expectation and an extraordinary capability; it does not establish universal medical incompetence or that Akyua performed a failed spell.

Ozuma accepts Kazuma's improvised bear-and-wolf explanation with self-reproach (P330–334). He then participates in treating a silver arrow as evidence of foresight; Kazuma's narration explicitly undercuts that inference (P369–375). These are errors about explanation and intention. They must be distinguished from actual situational perception: Ozuma notices that the surviving wolves have not dispersed and urgently warns that they are attacking Darkness (P394–395). His warning supplies a timely cue while Kazuma is absorbed in reflection. Lower comparative combat power does not make Ozuma useless, and deference to the principal party does not erase his contribution.

## Gratitude, grief and the credibility error

At P422 Ozuma moves from witnessed capability to the claim that bad reports about Kazuma must be envious slander. This exceeds what the demonstration establishes. His later account supplies a particular valued relationship: Hans killed one of his companions, and Ozuma has trained and traveled for revenge (P430). He personally thanks Kazuma and bows for avenging that loss (P436–437). That history is his report, not a newly watched death, training sequence or completed independent revenge. It gives his gratitude an intelligible object without reducing all earlier conduct to concealed gratitude. He already arrived with more than one purpose; appreciation and a reputational grievance can coexist.

The ending keeps the adverse consequence of his new trust. He promises to challenge future defamatory talk (P458), then repeats an allegation the reader can recognize as grounded in actual party conduct (P463). The principals' collective prostration at P472 is narrated; a full confession, compensation, Ozuma's settled appraisal afterward and successful future rumor suppression are not. Power, gratitude and good conduct remain different evidential questions even when Ozuma momentarily treats them as mutually confirming.

## Relationships, ordinary aims and written voice

Ozuma helps an unknown listener, protects a paid informant's meal, interrupts pressure on Megumi, challenges Kazuma, joins work with his companions and thanks a benefactor. These recipient differences support more resolution than a single angry-warrior label. Akyua's biography, Rakuresu's destroyed territory and Megumi's grandfather are not collectively his personal history. The departure at P453 is narrated, but completion of each companion's larger project is unknown.

His self-introduction uses direct plain language and 俺; his stranger-directed request at P21 is intelligible and practical. The meal correction at P76 explains an exchange norm. His doorstep language becomes contracted, accusatory and threatening at P149/156, and his eventual thanks and deep bow change the interaction at P436–437. P422 combines rougher plain phrasing with カズマさん. A fixed polite/rough binary would miss this movement. These are printed linguistic functions, not acoustic performance or a universal vocabulary generator.

Ordinary eating, payment, minor assistance, public work, name-based reputation and a continuing journey belong in his account alongside revenge. Exact income, appetite hierarchy, household routines, romantic preferences and a fixed willingness to incur every cost are unshown. The comic doubles and reversals magnify anger and humiliation, but the deception, threats, useful labor, injury and bereavement retain their different moral stakes.

## Proportional conclusion

Ozuma earns a Tier 3 **source-concentrated provisional** treatment: the [model](../06%20Character%20Modeling%20and%20Reconstruction/KONOSUBA_OZUMA_RECONSTRUCTION_MODEL.md) makes only two defeasible claims about reciprocal obligation and capability-based credibility. It does not claim a mature full model, a uniquely identified mechanism or reliable unfamiliar transfer. Ordinary reciprocity, companion loyalty, generic gratitude and the story's reversal structure remain serious rivals. A later context that separates admired power from a concrete conflicting obligation could weaken or revise this account. This current known-source study changes no historical model, prediction or first response.
