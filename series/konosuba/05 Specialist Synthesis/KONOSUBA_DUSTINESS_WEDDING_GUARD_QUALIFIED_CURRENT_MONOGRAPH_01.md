## 1. Identity

Darkness recognizes the man as one of the guards who has served her house for a long time. His slightly wrinkled face and household role are described in her focalization. No explicit name joins him to Norris, Hagen, the earlier gatekeeper or a guard in another household. Preserving an unnamed agent is more accurate than supplying a familiar servant name (WG28).

## 2. Present and remembered past

The current encounter follows his audible refusal to admit Alderp. Darkness then recalls repeated childhood attempts to leave, retrieve a ball and be caught. She now interprets the recurring game as his way of playing with a lonely bereaved child. That retrospective understanding is significant but differs from a direct record of his old internal intention (WG28).

## 3. Values

He says his service belongs to the Dustiness house and that he plans to leave after the marriage. He might serve a man whom Darkness herself recognizes. The declaration treats service as chosen allegiance with conditions rather than automatic transfer with a bride. No actual resignation or acceptance of a new master is shown (WG28).

## 4. Affect

His controlled answers to threats contrast with his warmth before the dressed bride and his shy final admonition. Darkness's smile matters to him in what he actually says. The account need not invent romantic attachment, paternal identity or complete knowledge of her wishes to recognize personal affection (WG28).

## 5. Competence and limits

He maintains a room boundary despite a powerful man's anger. The childhood recollection credits quick detection and pursuit, while her revised reading allows that some apparent gullibility was play. Neither proves magical perception or present combat invincibility. The boundary holds locally because Alderp leaves, not because every future retaliation has been defeated (WG28).

## 6. Choice under pressure

He grounds refusal in the present household and the fact that Alderp is not yet his master. The threatened future employer does not displace that judgment. His stated intention to resign also changes the expected employment cost, though retaliation remains possible. This is a situated decision, not proof that he always resists any superior (WG28).

## 7. Relationship and asymmetry

Darkness thanks him and offers to prevent punishment; he declines to make that reassurance necessary for his expressed choice. Her childhood dependence and present appreciation give the relationship history. He nevertheless believes a false sexual rumor produced by Kazuma's voice imitation, showing that loyalty is compatible with mistaken knowledge about the person protected (WG28).

## 8. Ordinary life

The recollected ball game provides ordinary activity, repeated play and the child's loneliness. His continued employment is real context, but wages, home, leisure and other relationships remain unknown. No perfect lifelong guardian biography should be inferred from the affectionate current memory (WG28).

## 9. Voice

Calm, literal boundary statements, respectful admiration and an embarrassed warning occupy different registers. The pronoun 自分 appears in his stated service choice. He can be courteous without yielding. No audio or accent is supplied; a generic deferential-servant voice would erase the actual refusal (WG28).

## 10. Humor and seriousness

The final warning embarrasses Darkness because the supposed private conduct was Kazuma's counterfeit speech. The joke is an epistemic injury to her reputation, not evidence she performed it. Alderp's threat of abuse and punishment remains serious, and the guard's local success does not make the danger imaginary (WG28).

A purely impersonal rule enforcer account misses stated allegiance and the recalled play, while limitless benevolence exceeds what is known. A concentrated personal account is earned; a broad behavioral generator would overextend one present encounter and a focal recollection (WG28).
