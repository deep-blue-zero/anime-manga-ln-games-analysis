# The knee-injured veteran: refusal before proof and expansive repayment after recovery

## 1. An unnamed man with a specific bodily history

The older adventurer in YORI-03 U020 P496–519 and652–679 is identified through Kazuma’s remembered knee-arrow injury and the local continuation of that encounter. He is not named. A familiar older man, a drinking companion or another veteran does not become this subject merely by fitting the same social category. In particular, the wallet-wager opponent in YORI01 remains a different source route without a demonstrated identity bridge.

His character evidence is unusually dependent on chronology. Kazuma remembers a later original event, encounters a counterpart in the second temporary route, and encounters a counterpart again in the third. Only Kazuma carries the later memories. The counterpart who receives healing in the third route has not experienced his second-route refusal. Treating these as successive changes within one continuously informed mind would manufacture a conversion narrative the story does not show.

All locators below refer to U020. Its complete882-P paragraph/ruby projection was received in the current five-story reinspection. No fresh images, residuals, whole-edition or independent-test credit follows. The source was already known; these are retrospective fictional contrasts rather than new prospective observations.

## 2. What Kazuma remembers and what the man confirms

Kazuma recalls that Aqua eventually healed an adventurer whose knee had long been impaired, after which the man expressed great gratitude and converted497. In the remembered original chronology, that event would occur about six months later498. Kazuma sees an opportunity to obtain early leveling help by moving the benefit forward. This is his instrumental proposal, not an offer already made by the man.

At505, Kazuma points to the particular seated adventurer and describes the injury. Aqua later announces a more complete martial account512, including a Demon King army battle and high-level status. The man’s response516 confirms that knowledge of his knee is unexpectedly personal; it does not independently audit every element of Aqua’s introduction. The text gives no name, exact age, prior party, complete campaign history or private medical account.

## 3. A refusal that precedes any demonstrated benefit

The second-route man is drinking when Aqua approaches511. She announces her supernatural identity and proposed healing512. He politely declines because the approach looks suspicious513. When Aqua becomes more insistent and demands worship and conversion515, he refuses the religious solicitation516 and sends her away517. The knee remains unhealed in this encounter.

His choice is intelligible on its own terms. Kazuma believes the treatment would help, but the recipient has not received Kazuma’s private knowledge of the provider’s abilities. An accurate statement about an injury does not compel assent to a stranger’s pitch. Pain or limited mobility likewise does not make every proposed intervention, reciprocal demand or religious condition acceptable to its holder.

The scene therefore records an actual boundary. It supplies no proof that the man dislikes all religious people, would reject a neutral offer or knows that the spell would work. His refusal concerns this approach as presented. Aqua’s disappointed report518 and Kazuma’s recognition that she made the Axis association too conspicuous519 preserve the failed proposal rather than making the man an irrational obstacle to an otherwise perfect plan.

## 4. The third route changes the order of events

Kazuma changes tactics in the final route. After registration652, he prompts Aqua, who casts before the man agrees653–655. The man first protests the surprise656, then moves his leg, becomes incredulous and begins to cry657–658. These are observable changes following the treatment. His later satisfaction does not backdate permission to the earlier cast.

Kazuma immediately requests help gaining three levels659. The man readily agrees660 and asks who Aqua is. Her self-presentation663 receives a hesitant response665 rather than instant, fully articulated theological certainty. He then proposes going out at once, giving his own reason: he wants to try the restored knee665. The assistance is simultaneously repayment and an opportunity to recover practical bodily agency.

The leveling is performed. Kazuma reaches level4 that day667, and the next day’s account explains that the veteran reduced frogs to near death for the beginners679. The outcome is stronger evidence of usable adventuring competence than the earlier impressive introduction alone. It does not establish his precise level, unlimited combat power or responsibility for every part of the pair’s subsequent prosperity.

## 5. Repayment beyond the requested minimum

The man does not stop at the three-level request. Kazuma reports that he insists leveling alone is an inadequate return and pays for lodging671. The pair also receives immediate living funds675. These are actual benefits in the represented route, rather than offers left wholly unperformed. They make the veteran more than a resource whom Kazuma successfully activates once.

His stated inadequacy judgment matters. Kazuma asks for one bounded service, while the veteran gives the restored bodily function a value that exceeds that service. He actively expands the exchange. Gratitude, recovery’s personal value, religious interpretation and social generosity may all contribute; the text does not isolate a numerical exchange rate or reveal which would dominate if the interests conflicted.

Conversion is narrated at676 as part of this successful route. It should retain that access plane. The visible hesitation at665, the earlier second-route refusal and the narrator’s later conversion statement are different phases and information states. They do not support a general claim that this man believes every divine announcement or always requires conversion before helping someone.

## 6. Competence, resources and ordinary life

Before treatment, the old wound limits mobility despite the experience Kazuma attributes to him505. Afterward, he wants to test the knee and can actually support rapid leveling665–679. This is a change in access to an established capacity, rather than proof that an incapable man suddenly acquires a new heroic personality. Restored movement gives him an activity he wants for himself.

The ordinary setting includes a drink at the guild511. The later lodging and money demonstrate available resources on that occasion. Neither detail establishes addiction, great wealth, a permanent pension or willingness to finance every project. The story does not show his household, regular spending, eating preferences or subsequent living conditions. A reconstruction must not spend an invented fortune on behalf of an agreeable benefactor.

His help makes Kazuma’s new economic route possible; Kazuma then learns a craft and markets goods through Wiz681–724. Those later choices belong to other agents. The veteran should receive credit for the aid he gives without becoming the unseen planner, guarantor or financier of every success that follows.

## 7. Three unequal relationships

Aqua is first an intrusive claimant to supernatural authority and later the provider of a directly experienced benefit. Kazuma is the proposer who knows more than he reveals, requests a limited return and receives additional help. The veteran has less information about the time experiment than either the narrator or the reader. His choices should be interpreted from the situation he can encounter, without granting him knowledge of the original chronology or the other branches.

The relation also contains unequal bodily power. Aqua can intervene before being authorized; Kazuma can arrange the encounter around a private objective. The veteran’s refusal in one route and gratitude in another are both his own responses. Neither makes the initiating intervention fully consensual, and neither erases his later capacity to choose, propose and provide more than the request requires.

## 8. Affect and written voice

His initial refusal uses an ordinary polite 結構です513. Under renewed pressure, he becomes more direct about unwanted religious solicitation516. In the third route, surprise, an incredulous question, tears and broken affirmations656–665 convey the treatment’s personal weight. The shift is tied to something he perceives happening to his body, not simply to flattering attention from Aqua.

The source supplies a small amount of attributed dialogue and more of Kazuma’s report. It does not provide an extended private monologue or a complete idiolect. English hypothetical speech can preserve caution, surprise and a wish to repay, but exact vocabulary, accent and Japanese adequacy remain untested. His hesitation about Aqua’s identity665 should survive any tendency to give him a polished devotional speech immediately after the spell.

## 9. Comic use and serious agency

The first approach frustrates Kazuma’s plan because he treats a possible beneficiary as someone who should naturally accept the exchange. The second approach succeeds by reversing the order: treatment comes before negotiation. That comic efficiency depends on bypassing a decision the earlier counterpart actually exercised. A fidelity audit should retain both the successful result and that cost.

The bodily recovery and generous aid are not merely a joke’s disposable machinery. The veteran wants to try walking and fighting effectively again665. His judgment that the requested service underpays the benefit671 gives his response a valuation different from Kazuma’s minimal-cost calculation. The two men cooperate while understanding the exchange differently.

## 10. What a provisional generator can and cannot explain

Two uneven candidate mechanisms are available. Before experienced proof, an unsolicited pitch that combines knowledge of his injury with religious demands can elicit refusal despite a possible benefit. After a personally important restoration is experienced, he can judge a small requested return insufficient and take initiative in practical repayment. The second mechanism has support in both performed leveling and the additional material aid; the first rests on one directly presented refusal.

Generic suspicion and gratitude remain serious rivals. The provider’s manner, immediate bodily evidence, religious interpretation, available money and the request’s size change together. The temporary routes do not isolate their causal weights. A generator should therefore condition its proposals on perceived benefit, credible information, actual resources and the particular request. It should not guarantee payment, conversion, obedience to a healer or a uniform reaction to every injury.

The asymmetry is useful precisely because it excludes two simple caricatures: an inherently hostile skeptic who cannot accept help, and a permanently trusting donor who accepts every divine claim. A meaningful disconfirmation would concern the specified conditions and his own valuation, rather than treating any refusal of an excessive new demand as a failure to feel gratitude.

## 11. Current disposition

The veteran earns a dedicated monograph and assessment of an asymmetric provisional behavioral model. His refusals, experiential revision, renewed physical goal and explicitly expanded repayment support more than a generic label for an injured quest giver. They do not establish independent predictive reliability, a full life history or mature transfer outside the limited evidence.

The complete foundation must be reviewed and frozen before exact hypothetical inputs are authored. Any original first outputs must remain intact even if they invent prior consent, remember another branch, spend unlimited funds or produce a suspiciously tidy theological conversion. Internal diagnostics can expose those errors; they cannot turn the already known fiction into an untouched source test.

Open questions include his prior beliefs, financial limits, response to voluntary pre-treatment negotiation, relationships outside the guild and long-term consequences of restored mobility. The current interpretation preserves a man who sets a boundary, is acted on without prior permission in another route, and then actively decides that a valuable benefit deserves more repayment than its organizer requested.
