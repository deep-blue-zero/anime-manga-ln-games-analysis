# Keith — useful eyes, contested standing and choices with other people's costs

Keith notices things his companions need to know. He sees a returning threat before Kazuma's detection, contributes an investigative lead that Taylor did not find, supplies equipment for Leen and chooses to follow Dust into danger. He also rushes an attack to avoid being outshone, proposes a deliberately concealed training target, helps prevent Dust's withdrawal, mocks another person's distress and tries to disrupt an intimate moment. His useful work is real; so are the mistakes and costs of the social competition around it.

This is an individual account ofキース, the archer in Taylor's party. It does not join him to an unnamed rough party associate merely because another character mentions one. It draws on seven current preparation receipts with 2,394 known BASE context slots in thirteen members/seven accepted objects and earlier qualified corpus receiving. The slots include many other people, blanks, reports and repeated contexts. They are not independent observations of Keith, new whole-source coverage, or novel predictions. The source outcomes are known to the author. The account argues for a substantial monograph and a small conditional reconstruction candidate with serious rivals and weak unfamiliar-context transfer.

## Being useful and being seen to be useful

MAIN02 gives Keith a confident introduction as an archer. His Snipe claim is a statement of capacity at this point, while Taylor introduces leadership and Leen intermediate magic. The party's surprise at Kazuma leading advanced classes belongs to an initial appraisal based partly on class labels. Keith's exact age, full childhood, family resources, complete skill list and private long-term ambition are not established by this introduction. (MAIN02 C1 P443–458.)

The one-day outing soon tests the hierarchy. Kazuma actually supplies Enemy Sense and shared Concealment when a dangerous lone monster is near. Keith is afraid alongside his companions; he also takes luggage so Kazuma can move and use the skills they now need. Accepting that practical dependence is not the same as welcoming every loss of standing. When Kazuma identifies an unexpectedly large goblin group and Leen proposes checking its size, Keith says he does not want Kazuma to take all the credit and rushes out. Taylor follows, and Leen protests after the numerical danger becomes clear. Keith's initiative has a stated competitive reason and a concrete tactical cost. (P501–540/P548–573.)

This supports a context-sensitive account of recognition. Being useful in front of a newcomer matters to him, enough to displace caution on that occasion. It does not establish that he always chooses fame over survival or that every boast conceals incompetence. Later in the same outing he sees the returning Beginner Killer before Kazuma's detection registers it. Kazuma infers unusually good eyesight. No Farsight casting is printed at that moment, so the observation should not be upgraded to a specific performed spell. (P641–646.)

Taylor chooses to cover the retreat, and Keith agrees to stay because Kazuma has worked hard for them. The source gives him both fear and a reason to accept exposure anyway. Kazuma ultimately refuses to abandon them and supplies his own escape tactic. Keith's chosen support is real without an invented successful final arrow or an inference that gratitude has permanently removed rivalry. The rash charge and the rear-guard assent are successive choices within one expedition, not independent repetitions of one trait. (P657–686.)

## Perception, archery and material help have different limits

Keith's vision is repeatedly useful, but it is not complete surveillance. In the Axel defense he provides a count of the approaching trees. During the later wedding he reports using Farsight to look outside and describes the Axis group, cart and dispute with guards. Dust subsequently sees the cart and bottles, corroborating part of the account. These are different access classes: the original scene shows Keith noticing a threat, the wedding passage gives his own report of a skill use, and Dust later confirms material details. They do not prove all-seeing awareness or make every motive he attributes to someone true. (DUST07 U012 P388–389; DUST-CONT U014 P215–221/P460.)

His actual archery also has finite effects. Against the force approaching Axel he fires Snipe and then three more arrows; the opponent blocks all of them. The arrows are performed attempts, not absent capacity and not four victories. Taylor's damaged shield, Leen's ineffective Fireball and the opponent's continuing action make this a difficult shared combat. Feit and Dust own the later apparent finish, which is itself qualified by the surviving Dullahan. Keith neither receives their success by group membership nor becomes universally useless from this particular failure. (DUST07 U012 P770–790/P822–890.)

A different role is available when Dust goes after Rouzeri. Keith follows, explicitly unwilling to yield all the glory. Taylor restrains him from boarding, hands Dust a shield and asks Keith to remain. Keith complains, then accepts the ground assignment. The passage preserves both his wish to participate prominently and his ability to take a less conspicuous part. The source does not establish that this restraint was consensual, that he had no useful possible role aboard, or that every authority request will produce willing obedience. (DUST07 U013 P298–302/P482–494.)

At the continuation's wedding crisis Keith follows Taylor's instruction to move and withdraw, despite the plan having gone wrong. Later he actually hands Leen a staff while Taylor supplies Dust with a spear. Dust cannot tell whether the staff was borrowed or taken from a court magician. Its availability is observed; lawful ownership, theft, exact price, permission and repayment remain unresolved. Keith's equipment contribution is therefore real without a clean procurement certificate. His envious-sounding praise of Dust can coexist with helping him perform. (DUST-CONT U014 P300–321/P437–443.)

## Gathering information without becoming a neutral investigator

The continuation gives Keith work beyond shooting and taunting. He and Taylor gather information in the adventurers' guild during three days of investigation. Taylor reports no bad rumors about Doreck. Keith adds a different lead from thieves: a woman who had investigated him is now missing. The report also supplies a self-interested reason for her inquiry. Dust dismisses her possible fate as imprisonment without confirmation. Her motive does not establish what happened to her, and Keith's envy of Doreck does not make the report automatically false. (DUST-CONT U012 P75–112.)

Keith's irritation is audible in the same meeting. He repeats women's admiration for the prosperous groom and grumbles at it. Earlier he had been disappointed when Dust appeared free of lingering attachment to Rionor. These are local displays of envy and appetite for interesting trouble, rather than proof that he believes every rumor or lacks all concern. Taylor's request to hear every remaining report slows the group without erasing Keith's useful distinct lead. Possession, impersonation and contract remain alternatives after the reports; the group does not yet know the eventual arrangement. (U012 P25–40/P110–149.)

Keith also raises a recipient's actual exposure. When Rionor is proposed as a convenient route to Doreck, he objects that involving her could put her in danger; Taylor agrees. The objection changes the discussion, but the later solution puts pressure on Leen. Selective concern, fear of consequences and practical risk appraisal are rival explanations that can coexist. This scene does not support a universal commitment to obtaining every participant's informed consent, given his directly contrary training conduct. (U012 P179–190.)

## Concealed plans and a refusal he helps override

The training scheme is not a harmless shortcut justified by its result. As the party discusses recovering Dust's strength, Keith proposes a secret destination. Taylor and Leen approve before Dust is told what it is. Dust had agreed in broad terms earlier; when he tries to leave, Keith and Taylor grip his shoulders and invoke that answer. The group subsequently binds him and transports him by air, while Leen diverts Feit with a dessert promise. The narration does not identify every individual hand in the binding or rope cutting. Keith's own proposal and direct prevention of withdrawal are already sufficient adverse evidence. (DUST07 U011 P735–785.)

The scheme leaves Dust in a dangerous coercive situation despite his objections. He escapes after pursuit and actual unwanted contact, while a further act is narrowly avoided. The account need not repeat graphic details to preserve what occurred. His improved spear work is an outcome of the episode, not proof of prior informed agreement or acceptable treatment. Dust's own unperformed threats are not a defense of Keith's conduct. (P797–889.)

Taylor later initiates an apology and offers a money bag on behalf of the group. Keith's exact personal share is not printed. Dust accepts a limited accommodation, while fear persists and later affects his conduct around Leen. It would be inaccurate to give Keith either no connection to the repair attempt or a separately completed restitution. Participation, apology, money offered, money received, actual spending and psychological recovery are different claims. (P899–958.)

Keith then mocks Dust's distress at a later meal and insults the women. Leen and Lolisa forcibly take him outside despite his protest. Dust and Taylor deliberately ignore the event; an explosion, scream and flash follow, and the women return without him. The exact outside caster, spell and injury are not supplied. Keith's insulting speech is actual wrongdoing, and his lack of agreement to being taken is also actual. A reconstruction must not optimize him into tactfulness, or treat his victim status as a cancellation of responsibility for the earlier training. (DUST07 U012 P275–315.)

## Company, amusement and uneven social reading

Keith enjoys ordinary group activity. After the original outing he shares the laughter and admiration around Kazuma. He offers a later drink in the Dust07 epilogue, but no completed payment is printed. DUST05 gives search and travel cooperation, and later reports of recovery from Serena's influence; the reported manipulated state must not be treated as an unconstrained ordinary preference. A large meal order and the men checking money do not establish that Keith paid a particular final sum. (MAIN02 C1 P693–746; DUST05 U011 P442–452/U012 P698–712; DUST07 U014 P131.)

His relation to Dust often mixes genuine chosen help with enjoyment of embarrassment. In the continuation he tries to put his own jealousy into Taylor's mouth, only for Taylor to reject the attribution. He helps circulate and perform the story of an earlier confession. Such retellings change the audience of a private exchange; they do not certify that Leen and Dust have settled a formal relationship. Keith's ability to notice awkwardness can be used to aggravate it. (DUST-CONT U010 P94–113.)

The Dust07 epilogue supplies an especially clear boundary on access. The real Leen joins Dust and is interrupted by Feit before contact. Later the observers appear, Taylor apologizes, and Keith whispers that he intended to interfere. Dust explicitly does not hear the whisper. The next apparent willing Leen is Vanir in disguise, while the genuine woman remains to Dust's right. Keith's private aside cannot be made Dust's knowledge, and Vanir's performance cannot be made a second willing approach by the real Leen. The joke preserves a particular malicious intention and a separate actor's deception. (DUST07 U014 P65–125.)

Keith's interest in standing does not require him to hate every companion's success. He freely follows Dust during the defense, supplies a staff in the continuation and offers future company after teasing. Those are serious contrary observations to a pure sabotage account. Conversely, the helpful work does not establish that the interference was affectionate, welcomed or costless. The sources do not supply complete private motive weights. Friendship, dependence, envy, amusement and resentment remain overlapping explanations.

The luxurious inn and formal wedding also show social discomfort. Keith huddles with the others in an unfamiliar expensive setting and fidgets in ceremonial clothing. A rough familiar manner does not imply ease in every public audience. These scenes support context-specific embarrassment rather than a diagnosis, an exact wealth level or a global inability to perform formal tasks. His actual later evacuation and equipment contribution show that discomfort need not prevent useful action. (DUST-CONT U012 P1–16; U014 P193–204/P300–321/P440.)

## Voice, comedy and limits of the model

Keith's familiar speech often uses contractions, rough assertions and provocative questions. The proposal to hear Dust's plan, the complaint that the operation has gone wrong and the jealous praise of his appearance all fit that ordinary mode. Taylor's more organized, measured plain language is a useful local comparison; it does not create an across-the-board grammatical formal/plain opposition. Public presentation, rapid joking and words spoken under danger can vary without producing a second personality. The present BASE returns do not constitute a fresh ruby pass, acoustic performance test or native-human dialogue assessment. (DUST-CONT U010 P61/P103; U014 P157–159/P304–307/P437–443.)

Keith's recurring comic position is partly to expose competitive or inconvenient motives that others prefer to soften. Sometimes he names his own motive; sometimes he assigns it to someone else incorrectly. That gives him neither total self-knowledge nor unique access to everybody's real reasons. The narration also lets his rashness be costly and his skill be useful. Labeling every bad outcome a nonliteral gag would erase the failures a behavioral model has to allow.

A small reconstruction candidate is justified by the combination of audience-sensitive competition, real task contribution under fear, and chosen coalition action that can be helpful or coercive. Its serious rivals are generic competitive friendship, an archer's practical role, ordinary group conformity and scene-specific comic escalation. The observations do not uniquely identify a hidden psychological mechanism. The model should therefore be conditional, allow retreat or complaint, preserve information gaps and make no claim to reliable novel behavior in intimate relationships, unfamiliar institutions or long-term solitary work. A source-known hypothetical can test whether those distinctions survive generation; it cannot turn Keith into an independently validated predictive model.
