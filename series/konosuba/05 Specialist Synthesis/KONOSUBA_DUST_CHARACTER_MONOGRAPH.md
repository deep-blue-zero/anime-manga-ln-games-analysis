# Dust — a chosen ordinary life, useful skill, and unreliable protection

## Responsibility and evidence boundary

Dust is an independently motivated person whose current life in Axel has positive value. His former knightly identity explains particular skills, obligations, attachments, and concealments; it does not turn every current failure into a deliberate disguise. The useful account must preserve his opportunism and coercion alongside his practical intelligence, affection, and actual sacrifices. Neither the heroic past nor his unflattering self-description supplies the whole person.

This monograph draws on the already admitted and completed DUST01–07 and separate unnumbered DUST-CONT analyses, their source-specific corrections, and relevant MAIN01–17 and CONTEXP evidence. The current audit has received all eight Dust analytical reports. Direct Japanese rereception is confined to separately recorded member/paragraph windows; it is not a new complete traversal of every EPUB, ruby, image, or ancillary component. FD's game-novelization plane and other continuities remain qualified. Repeated scenes, alternate viewpoints, letters, dreams, retrospective accounts, and public heroic histories retain different evidential weights.

These are known sources. This is an expanded interpretation and a candidate basis for the separate operational model, not a new blind prediction or a revision of the seven original Dust questions. Those originals, their fourteen empty future fields, the principal models, and all historical scores remain exact. The supporting evidence index records targeted current ledger/event reconciliation and later same-author internal receiving. Broader census and full-ledger consolidation remain open; canonical adoption and publication require their separately recorded gates.

## The ordinary self is wanted

The contrast between knight and delinquent is not adequately described as a noble essence hiding beneath disposable bad manners. Dust wants the companions, leisure, familiarity, and relative freedom of his present home. He can value a quiet life without living it quietly. His misconduct often concerns things he actually wants now: money, service access, drink, entertainment, advantage over acquaintances, or an escape from embarrassment. An old professional capacity does not make those motives insincere. (DUST01, especially C01 and Final; DUST05 U012; DUST07 U014; DUST-CONT §§11–13.)

DUST05 makes the distinction unusually concrete. After the dragon strikes him, Dust misidentifies Leen as the princess and narrates himself as the knight ライン・シェイカー. His formal 私 and sacrificial language belong to that altered appraisal. Leen objects that the Dust she knows survives through selfish and underhanded methods; the painful headbutt is followed by his own current name, rough speech, and rejection of the old pattern of brooding. The restored Dust then performs real protection and takes burns for Leen. The source therefore separates a mistaken knight/princess identification from a later protective act by the present person. It does not establish a clinical disorder, two independently existing people, or a cure of all conflicting attachments. (DUST05 U012 P574–641, particularly P591/P599/P602.)

The old name should remain complete and source-bound. ライン・シェイカー is not a reason to merge Dust with the princess's attendant Rain/Rein, whose レイン name and role are distinct. Rionor / リオノール and Leen / リーン are also different people. Resemblance, disguise, temporary substitution, and a confused identification are events to explain, not identity equivalences. DUST06 gives the princess's name and an attributable substitution history; DUST07 supplies deliberate disclosure of Dust's past and the dragon's identity to Taylor and Keith. Their prior suspicion of noble or knightly origins is explicitly weaker than their newly learned Dragon Knight identification. Their subsequent attempt to pretend they recognized the Leen/princess switch fails. (DUST06 U010 P596–640; DUST07 U011 P419–453.)

## Concealment has relational costs and a permission structure

Dust does not merely avoid a weapon because he lacks the ability to use it. The recalled instruction permits the spear for Rionor or another person he truly wants to protect. The complete alternatives matter. In DUST07 he recalls that instruction, privately identifies Leen, and adds companions, friends, and the town. He then chooses training and disclosure. This is an actual change in his current use of an existing constraint, rather than an exception invented by the analyst after a convenient victory. (DUST03 recalled instruction; DUST07 U011 P390–427.)

Privacy is more than a wish to look impressive. Dust hides the identity of Faitifore, the child-presenting dragon partner, experiences rumors and social costs, and initially searches alone. He eventually makes a particular request for help and receives it. The later relief after telling Taylor and Keith suggests a real burden of concealment, while their qualified recognition exposes his overestimate of how successfully he had hidden his history. Disclosure to those companions does not give every citizen, client, or enemy the same information. (DUST05 U012 P107–428; DUST07 U011 P419–453.)

Rionor's continuing importance does not make Leen a substitute who can be modeled entirely through the princess. Leen acts under her own knowledge, objects to imposed roles, and supplies her own reasons for later continuation. Her DUST06 viewpoint distinguishes initial capture, refusal of withdrawal, a later usable exit, and her renewed decision. The unnumbered continuation gives her a separate account of embarrassment, competence, connection, and wanting to benefit Dust. Those reasons are reader-accessible evidence about Leen; they need not already be known to Dust. (DUST06 U012 P427–597; DUST-CONT U012 P436–510.)

## Skill is real and distributed

Dust can read equipment, change encounter geometry, recruit providers, exploit an opponent's options, and use trained weapons. Those capacities are not discredited simply because he also makes bad decisions. Conversely, technical effectiveness cannot certify his treatment of the people whose labor makes a plan possible. The DUST02 machine confrontation needs Keith's rope, Taylor's shield, Yunyun's cut, Dust's spear, and Leen's Lightning. Penetration alone is not the completed stop. Later joint battles similarly require actual provider access, material resources, and opportunities created by others. (DUST01 C04/Final; DUST02 Final; DUST06 climax; DUST07 U013; DUST-CONT U014.)

The visible contrast between his unusual physical capacity, weaker ordinary sword performance, and older spear skill matters more than a scalar competence score. Missing weapons, healing, armor, reach, transport, and opposition can change the outcome. Borrowing a noble persona through finite equipment does not itself establish superior force, and asking a provider for help does not mean that provider's power has become his own. The helmeted admirer lends equipment for a day under stated conditions; those terms are neither a donation nor an observed completed return. (DUST03 U014 P244–350; DUST07 U011 P404–441.)

Success also has several endpoints. An attack can penetrate without completing rescue; a false report can recruit help without proving the report true; a chosen intervention can work while creating debt or collateral damage. The eventual weakening of the DUST07 enemy is observed locally, but the connection to the distant main-series victory is Dust's inference. Rouzeri's fall is not an individually performed death check. The account should preserve those limits rather than grant the local hero omniscient credit.

## Particular care does not provide a reliable ethical override

Dust's affection has behavioral content. Gifts, labor, asking for help, searching, remaining under danger, protecting Leen after the identity confusion ends, and adjusting care for Faitifore all have actual costs. A mixed motive does not make such acts unreal. DUST07's final private recognition and actual hand pull also matter without completing a final romantic contract. The anticipated approach to a mouth is interrupted; the source does not show mouth contact. (DUST01 C02/Ep; DUST04 coat and care episodes; DUST05 U012 P314–723; DUST07 U014 P81–148.)

The converse is equally important: he sometimes knowingly obstructs or declines needed help. DUST02's forced attraction is contrary evidence about his regard for another person's refusal. In DUST03 he stops Yunyun's available rescue while guards are still being swallowed. DUST06 includes both deferred feasible inquiry in favor of drinks and teasing during the princess's immediate toad predicament, after which Yunyun performs the rescue. In DUST07 Dust and Taylor intentionally shut their eyes and ears during Keith's forced removal and the actual blast. These acts cannot be repaired by later protection, survival, amusement, or a claim that the danger needed first to cause bodily injury. (DUST02 C03 P149/P156–176/P420–424; DUST03 U013 P218–247; DUST06 §9; DUST07 U012 P295–304.)

DUST04 adds another direct contrary: Dust knowingly pledges a particular companion for continued gambling after hearing her refusal and a consent caution. He could stop the wager. No completed handover, physical injury, or later benign report is needed to preserve that coercive choice. (DUST04 U012 P661–686.)

The earlier broad protective-override account must therefore remain rejected as a general rule. Not every omission is an equally complete trial of the exact old N01 conjunction: some scenes leave a competing leisure option or feasible protective means unresolved. That qualification preserves honest missingness; it does not erase directly shown coercion or create a new pure-motive requirement. In the unnumbered continuation, direct questions and patient care coexist with tolerated royal coercion, delayed recovery, and ignoring another person's fear. Concern inside his narration is not a substitute for the omitted action. (DUST-CONT U011/U013/U014; original N01 and current qualifications.)

His companions also cause harm. Coerced training can produce useful skill while retaining hidden purpose, a blocked exit, restraint, fear, and unwanted contact. Taylor's apology and money are separate later acts, not retroactive assent. Likewise, manager permission cannot supply Lolisa's consent, and Faitifore's gratitude or partnership does not automatically authorize removal of body material. Treating Dust accurately requires the same actor-specific standards when he is recipient, instigator, accomplice, or helper.

## Money, rewards, and selective checking

Dust is neither an uninterested altruist nor uniformly incapable of checking a deal. He sometimes appropriates entrusted money despite knowing its ownership, commits before future access is secure, invents inducements, or changes moral appraisal when his share improves. DUST04's promised multiplication does not undo the appropriation or prove repayment. DUST05's increase in his actual income can explain part of his reversal about Serena without making his earlier priest-sample inference sound. (DUST04 U012; DUST05 U010 P520–637/U011 opening.)

There are genuine contrary cases to an indiscriminate underchecking rule. He asks what is wanted and what the price is, inquires about terms and future arrangements, and checks whether a gift may be used or sold. Some relevant terms are known to him even when the narrative withholds them from the reader. Vanir's whispered price in DUST05 is one such case: reader ignorance does not establish Dust's failure to ask or listen. The new model needs the salience and accessibility of each term, rather than an automatic failed-contract ending. (DUST05 U012 P389–428; DUST07 U011 P361–460; DUST-CONT U013 P208–351.)

The actual sword handover supplies a particularly strong mixed-motive case. Dust narrates a secret one-use defense against otherwise fatal magic; that is his knowledge claim, not a new all-spell experiment. He describes the sword both as protection and a connection to the older life, then throws it to Kazuma and asks for its return. Leen helps with a public profit cover and Dust feigns anger. His private wish is that the sword protect his friend, while possible future profit remains explicitly genuine. MAIN16 sees the same exchange from another side; MAIN17 performs later use. Neither the cover nor the protective purpose cancels the other, and the loan is not a gift to Leen or an observed completed return. (DUST07 U011 P1085–1115; MAIN16 C3 P250–264.)

Keep resource transfers separate. The princess's sword, the merchant's gift, a one-day armor loan, funds for rumors, entrusted allowance, compensation, food for Faitifore, provider fees, and guild defense debt have different owners and closure states. In DUST07 Vanir explicitly cancels the defense debt; a separate payment associated with Luna is reported later. The sword lent to Kazuma is actually useful in MAIN17, but later use does not newly witness the initial loan, complete its return, or settle every account. The unnumbered continuation leaves several bills, royalties, and returns unresolved. No total wealth reconstruction follows from the available episodes.

## Fallible inference and deliberate representation

Dust can sincerely misread a cue, knowingly lie, withhold a motive, and make a good inference. Those operations should not be merged into global unreliability. Romantic guesses, desire-compatible explanations, and the DUST07 epilogue's accessible right/left error are genuine vulnerabilities. A missing detail known only in another narrator's private scene is a different limitation. A lie he admits is not a false belief merely because he says it aloud. (DUST01 §9/I01; DUST04 §9; DUST07 §9/U014; DUST-CONT §9.)

The alternative viewpoints are especially useful. The admirer values and understands objects through his own private appraisal, correcting Dust's romance hypothesis without providing laboratory authentication or a universal mind-reading license. Leen's viewpoint supplies her own choices and uncertainties. The Doreck/Gaibil epilogue supplies a private new contract and reversed care while Dust is absent. Their statements about lenience or no human harm do not delete captivity and shock already shown. The shared-sense mechanism is disclosed only before a later capture; it is not an earlier accessible cue Dust ignored. (DUST01 I01; DUST06 U012; DUST-CONT U013 P294 onward/U016.)

The same-event joins also protect character attribution. MAIN02's party swap and its Dust retelling are one underlying event. MAIN13's bar intimidation and DUST05's corresponding scene supply different views of one encounter. MAIN14's Serena preference/reversal and the Dust-side version likewise cannot create two independent confirmations. Different reports and ledgers can improve access to the event without multiplying its occurrence.

## Written voice follows purpose and relationship

Dust's rough contractions, blunt address, derisive questions, boasts, threats, and quick repairs are useful written tendencies, not a catchphrase generator. He can ask careful terms in that rough register, offer help while sounding defiant, or acknowledge affection without becoming ceremonially polite. Strategic politeness can instead corner someone, obtain access, flatter a powerful provider, or maintain a borrowed persona. Neither register independently certifies care, truth, or consent. (DUST01–07 and DUST-CONT, respective Japanese voice sections.)

The knightly 私/です/ます and present 俺/俺様 have independently established role histories. DUST06 shows the awkward addition of a rough particle, a registration repair, and later more natural present self-reference; DUST05's altered-state transition and DUST-CONT's king-directed repair have their own causes. The model should choose the communicative purpose first, then a plausible form under that recipient and state. A pronoun alone cannot identify every speaker. Written elongation, ruby, and typography do not provide heard accent, pitch, timing, actor performance, or native-human certification.

## What is earned and what remains uncertain

The varied evidence supports a distinct source-informed reconstruction candidate. It supplies goals with actual costs, contrasting role and knowledge states, technical choices, ordinary pleasures, adverse actions, meaningful other perspectives, and sufficiently specific opportunities for disconfirmation. Its usefulness comes from representing those distinctions, rather than from the number of volumes or from making Dust resemble the principal-character template.

The remaining uncertainty is substantial: stable weights among attachment, pleasure, shame, money, and complicity are not identified; hypothetical transfer has no independent validation; future romantic status and several resource outcomes remain open; and full current ledger/individual-census consolidation remains open beyond this scoped artifact. The six preserved source-known internal responses do not establish independent predictive accuracy: the first contrast has an explicit-instruction rival, the ownership contrast leaves the bad choice underdetermined, and the information-access contrast produces no difference in chosen behavior. A credible simulation should retain competing branches when the record cannot determine the choice. It should not silently supply an ideal knight, a uniformly helpless delinquent, an omniscient narrator, or a universally protective friend.
