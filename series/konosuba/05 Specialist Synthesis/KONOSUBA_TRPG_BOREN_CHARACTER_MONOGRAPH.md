# Boren: earned strength, claimed authority and coercive command

**ボーレン / Bōren** is the named kobold antagonist in the Advanced supplied scenario **この地底戦車に強制停止を！**, native **TRPG-ADV-S298–311**, printed **296–309**. The preceding divider is native S297/printed295. This is an unperformed authored adventure, not either published replay or a newly witnessed main-novel episode. Shared kobold status does not establish an identity connection to the Fanbook's repeatedly appearing Kobold Hero; no such join is admitted here.

The scenario supplies a particularly dense context: an attributed work history, material ability, institutional ambition, recruitment and marriage pressure, tactical choices, self-serving moral language and a possible loss of control. These support a scoped Tier2 monograph. They do not isolate a portable decision generator or earn a reconstruction model, independent result or final tier.

## What the scenario establishes and what he claims

The synopsis describes Boren excavating and repairing an abandoned Noize underground tank, then attacking a Belzerg town to make the army recognize his strength (S298). His opening speech calls him the next army executive and the tank's heir. A conditional information result describes him as an army-affiliated kobold considered a candidate after the excavation, while also stating his ambition to take control of the army (S300–301). Membership, candidacy, self-promotion and achieved command of the whole army are separate claims.

His roughly two-meter muscular body has a material explanation. He says he spent years digging alone; the enemy-card prose also states that the excavation strengthened him (S300, S309). The corroborating authorial description supports that local causal history. It supplies neither an exact duration nor universal combat superiority. His card is level15 and grants particular earth-magic and protection capacities, not unlimited strength.

He boasts that the tank can exceed the Demon King and defeat a goddess (S301). Neither feat occurs in this scenario. The preview's invincible framing and the external barrier describe a conditional advantage sustained by a device. They cannot turn his ambition into achieved divine or royal power.

## Recruiting people he has already confined

The opening forces the PCs' defeat and confiscates their equipment and money, exempting clothing or other items without game data. Boren offers them a subordinate role but confines them regardless of their answer (S299–300). The book separately instructs the GM to disclose this opening to players in advance; that preparation instruction is not evidence that any actual players were informed or consented during this analysis.

Boren recognizes that immediate agreement after capture may not be sincere, and postpones a formal answer until the planned destruction of Belzerg (S301). The speech therefore represents strategic concern about commitment while retaining coercion. It does not establish respect for an available refusal, voluntary loyalty or a completed campaign. Treating every proposed follower as already willing would remove the central asymmetry of the scene.

His institutional ambition also differs from the guards' immediate tasks. Guard conversation can reveal information useful to escape. Those conversations do not certify that every soldier shares his private takeover goal or that Boren knows what has been overheard. Conditional PC checks and scripted disclosure remain different access routes.

## Refused proposals and the captives' own aims

The guest room contains an unarmed female adventurer party whose members refused Boren's marriage proposals and were confined. The Wizard recounts his response that there is time; the Warrior asks who the PCs are, the Thief identifies the golem obstacle and the Priest offers practical assistance if equipment can be recovered (S305). Their refusal, uncertainty and cooperative agency remain their own. They are not consenting prospective wives or rewards for either Boren or the PCs.

Boren orders that this party not be killed. The diversion branch uses that order to let them occupy guards throughout the climax (S306). This is a selective constraint that can preserve their survival while serving continued captivity. It neither establishes universal mercy nor licenses the proposals. The text provides no later marriage, reconciliation, forgiveness or durable change in his conduct.

## Defending the source of his advantage

At the control room Boren is initially surprised by the PCs and then regains composure. He stays beside the device, attacks with earth magic and covers the device if it is attacked (S307). This is a specific tactical priority. It does not prove that he never protects another person, but the only supplied cover target in this scene is the mechanism sustaining the tank's external defense.

When that target is threatened he calls for a fair fight. The timing gives the appeal a self-serving function: it asks opponents to preserve his material advantage. A full moral theory of honor is not supplied. His earlier forced defeat, confiscation and confinement also qualify a reading of the appeal as a consistent commitment to equal terms.

The battle can end through device destruction before every enemy is defeated. Enemy defeat, device destruction and removal of the external barrier therefore have different conditions. The printed earth-magic attack heading and melee/physical summary are retained as a source discrepancy; this study does not silently repair the card or claim an exhaustive numerical balance audit.

## The limit of command

After device loss, the tank ejects people and treats outside lifeforms as enemies. If Boren has survived, he orders it to attack the adventurers but is struck from behind by its drill; the instructions remove him and diverted guards from that battle (S308, S310). His earlier claim of mastery fails under the supplied loss-of-control condition. This is not a new actually played reversal or a demonstration of how he would behave after an unrelated defeat.

The text describes him being defeated or blown away, without establishing his permanent death in every branch. Bounty calculations and a possible drop do not independently certify a particular killing event. His proposed destruction of Belzerg is prevented in the supplied successful ending, but no exact city casualty list or general future peace history is established.

## Voice, ordinary life and unresolved psychology

Boren uses **私**, announces titles and prospective power, narrates his labor and shifts to an abrupt plea when the device is attacked. These are attributed scripted registers, with a reported quotation inside the Wizard's speech kept separate from his direct supplied lines. They are not acoustic evidence or a tested imitation of a real person.

Excavation labor and desired marriage are the main noncombat glimpses. Family, ordinary leisure, treatment of freely consenting equals, actual income and private emotional appraisal remain unknown. Ambition, fear of losing an advantage, desire for recognition and situational villain scripting can explain much of the same material. No precise ranking or stable threshold is isolated. His real labor and tactical skill should remain visible alongside coercion; neither usefulness nor genre comedy cancels the captives' refusal. All earlier model, forecast, first-response and score histories remain unchanged.
