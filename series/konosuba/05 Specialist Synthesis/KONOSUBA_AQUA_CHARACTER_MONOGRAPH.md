## 1. The problem Aqua poses

Aqua is repeatedly capable of doing what the party needs and repeatedly difficult to rely on. Those statements concern different things. Her extraordinary sacred powers, practical craft, susceptibility to immediate incentives, need for esteem and attachment to familiar people produce neither a secretly infallible goddess nor a uniformly incompetent companion. The series makes the reader experience the value of her presence while continuing to expose the costs of her decisions.

Her central tension is between an identity that ought to guarantee recognition and relationships that cannot be guaranteed by identity. Being a goddess does not make people believe her. Performing a real service does not ensure that she continues the practical work. Being wanted as a healer does not exhaust wanting to be wanted by the town or household. Wanting heaven does not mean that her life with these particular people has no value. The later movement of the narrative makes these distinctions more explicit; it does not replace vanity with pure benevolence.

The strongest longitudinal account therefore follows competing appraisals rather than a single trait. Aqua can see a sacred problem correctly and misjudge its social setting; accept a task and become distracted; value another person and choose an ill-fitted means of comfort; refuse money because a performance matters to her on other terms; or be stopped by a friend's consent when anticipated resistance would have made action easier. Her contradictions are often intelligible at the level of what she believes an act means. Their intelligibility does not remove their harm.

The reading's emotional progression also matters. Early routine makes her a fellow inhabitant of the world into which Kazuma forced her. Later absence makes that ordinary presence perceptible as a loss. By V17 her usefulness and her irritating familiarity are both part of the desired return. This is a development in the articulation and recognition of attachment, not proof that she has acquired complete practical judgment or that the relationship has become romantic. ## 2. A divine identity lived under ordinary constraints

Aqua's ordinary life is not merely an interval between proper divine acts. In MAIN-01 C1, construction, food, bathing and sleeping form a workable routine before Kazuma protests that they have lost the expected shape of an adventure. She can correctly tell him that food requires work and point out their lack of equipment. Her admission of forgetting the remote objective is a local goal-salience failure, not evidence that all manual work was beyond her or that her participation was fraudulent. Kazuma's disappointment supplies one viewpoint on a life that has become satisfying enough to continue. The present bounded principal recheck covers C1P195–253; it supports these particular observations, not a fresh reading of the entire early volume.

Art offers an especially useful limit on the usual greed shorthand. MAIN-01 C2P136–140 has attention and prospective payment available, yet Aqua rejects repeated paid performance in the name of her amateur artistic standard. This is an expressed value with a behavioral cost. Later she can perform outside the Dustiness estate to recover Darkness and refuse tips because that performance has a relational purpose. These scenes do not establish universal disdain for money: anticipated earnings, tavern debt, expensive alcohol, loans, gambling and household subsidy remain real. They show that money is not the only currency she recognizes. The artist, follower-seeking goddess, pet caretaker and practical worker organize effort differently.

This distinction prevents a false opposition between an authentic goddess and a false ordinary persona. Construction and water control become strategically important at the V09 fortress. Enjoying applause can support days of useful labor when esteem attaches to repair. Enjoying drinking or caring for Emperor Zel can also divert time and resources. The same person can have an intrinsic standard about her own art and respect another person's property poorly. Her reported restaurant feat in V10, for example, remains a compensation problem even if the feat is technically impressive. No artistic success authorizes the consumption or disappearance of someone else's goods.

Ordinary preferences also acquire particular objects. Her distress over a lost named game character in V12 is not answered simply by restoring equivalent progress. Komekko can receive Aqua's own pudding in V11 despite Aqua's appetite. Tea, food, games and household play are recurring activities, but a single meal or joke does not establish a permanent favorite, addiction, or general inability to taste. MAIN-09's favorite inexpensive tea is a stated preference even though purification spoils the served cup. MAIN-11's plain hot water for Kazuma is intentional role play, not another failed brewing attempt. ## 3. Great capacity does not answer the whole practical question

The series repeatedly separates a power's execution from the outcome of the larger task. The MAIN-01 Beldia sequence makes this visible at its largest scale. Aqua's water spell is real; it sweeps away the participants, including Aqua, and flows into town. The ensuing victory requires other phases: Kazuma obtains the head, Darkness bears the hit, and Aqua's final anti-undead action removes the body. The epilogue's receptionist attributes damaged houses to the water and Darkness reads the compensation figure. Those are an institutional report and a stated liability, not an observed bank settlement. Three hundred million reward against three hundred forty million compensation leaves a forty-million shortfall arithmetically. The analysis must carry both the useful spell and the civilian externality. MAIN-01 C4P459–574; EpP49–77.

MAIN-02 provides a still more exact decomposition. Before the Destroyer engagement, Aqua declines to guarantee that she can break its barrier. The actual `セイクリッド・スペルブレイク` at C5P296 is followed by the film shattering at P300; Kazuma's identification of the film remains hedged at P302. Later, his concern about transferring sacred mana through Wiz is a compatibility warning and prior-illness report, not a witnessed fatal experiment. The eventual neck-contact arrangement routes Aqua's mana through Kazuma to Megumin. Her large remaining reserve is evidence for that scene's resource availability, not an infinite-mana theorem. Destroying the legs, removing the dangerous stone, stopping movement, dealing with residual heat and evacuating are distinct problems. Neither Aqua nor any one spell solves the whole event alone. C5P110–143,260–345,548–659; EpP1–46.

Later failures should be charged to the stage actually shown. In V10 she promises detoxification but becomes drunk and asleep before it is needed; another church provides the remedy. That is a readiness and backup failure, not a demonstrated inability to detoxify while engaged. In V15 she approaches a patient and is blocked before casting: access fails. The first attempt to cure Kazuma later genuinely fails and is followed by a coordinated retry: execution and adaptation are both visible. In V14 repeated BreakSpell attempts are reported without change, while no curse is established; the absence of a demonstrated curse is not itself a known causal explanation for the result. Her cemetery Turn Undead also fails in that actual scene. An unestablished ontology should not be invented to preserve perfection.

These distinctions are supported by CP06 A01's PARTIAL result. Capacity and readiness receive different evidence; comparative clarity remains untested at the required level. They allow strong claims about demonstrated abilities and weak claims about universal availability. The exceptional V17 recreation of Kazuma's body is consequential precisely because it is exceptional. It cannot be generalized into unlimited replacement, retroactive certainty of survival, or a reason to downgrade the cost he believed he was accepting earlier. ## 4. When a sacred category becomes an action cue

Aqua's sacred expertise includes real perception, diagnosis and intervention. Her true claims about demons, undead and divinity can be dismissed because listeners have learned to discount her. That credibility problem is partly social injustice and partly the consequence of her own exaggeration, lying and quarrels. An observer should neither convert disbelief into proof of her error nor convert her actual divine status into authority over every proposition she asserts.

V04 is a particularly strong positive case. She makes useful investigative distinctions, continues purification under painful conditions and contributes to defeating the threat. Yet the scale of purification also damages the hot-spring system and affects Wiz. The initial objective, the sacred classification and the consequences for a community do not automatically coincide. MAIN-02's earlier cemetery shortcut gives the same problem a domestic afterlife. Aqua reports finding regular visits troublesome and installing a holy barrier; the receptionist later attributes displaced spirits and the haunted house to that act. Aqua eventually clears the house, but the repair does not erase the cause. The newly checked MAIN-02 C3P160–530 contains her confession, the guild's account, actual purification and the decision about residence. It does not newly show performance of regular maintenance after the decision.

The cemetery story also resists total cynicism. Aqua's early account of the child Anna is partly corroborated by the later grave inscription, although every biographical detail and her tentative account of the alcohol are not verified. A service can be genuine even when performed by someone who created the displacement that made it necessary. The household then benefits from both the intervention and continued grave care by Kazuma. The arrangement is causally compromised, not fictitious.

Conversely, sacred capacity can make mistreatment easier. V13 repeatedly shows the count's fear, despair and costly revival rather than harmless infinite play. Aqua acknowledges discomfort about tormenting the cute count and still attacks again after provocation. In V11 a peaceful release for Lucy is displaced by status competition. These scenes prevent a universal claim that sacred classification, pity or a companion's rebuke reliably brakes aggression. They also prevent the attractive generalization that every undead encounter is motivated by disinterested duty. ## 5. Recognition can sustain work, displace it, or fail to predict the choice

Aqua does not respond to every status cue in the same way. The V09 campaign first shows the destructive route: a planned delaying encounter becomes a contest over divinity and an excessive flood damages the fort. It then shows a productive route. Kazuma discovers that the construction work he had not watched closely is skilled work. Aqua's water control accelerates drying; repairs strengthen the wall, morale and the military's options. Darkness attaches the title `補修隊長` to a real responsibility. Recognition now rewards doing the actual job rather than abandoning it for a rival spectacle.

This is not simply praise causing competence. Practical activity is already under way before the title, and her previous craft supplies the means. Nor is every later service to be explained by a desire for applause. CP06 A03 establishes a more carefully bounded cross-volume recurrence at the source-named support-magic category: Mitsurugi's V16 F63 acknowledgment and useful support at F65 precede distinct actual V17 casts at C1P397 and C3P115. C2P75 is a recast request whose execution is not shown; C2P218 refers back to a cast, not a third fresh event. The formal result is CONFIRMED at moderate category/causal confidence, not unique experimental isolation of gratitude as the cause.

Negative evidence remains equally important. The V14 cemetery insult is followed by resumption of her own purification attempt despite anger; that local A02 FALSIFIED history remains. CP06 A02 is AMBIGUOUS, not a retrospective reversal of V14. Eligibility requires the challenged capacity/status and ongoing task to be established independently. Merely knowing or liking a person is not an acknowledged competing relational cue that immunizes the prediction. In V17 the final thanks is already under way before Kazuma's intellect insult; Aqua gives a brief reply and completes the thanks. Whether that insult fully challenges the relevant task capacity remains material. It cannot be excluded merely because the service had started or because the recipient is a friend.

The surviving claim is narrower and more interesting than either prestige makes her useless or appreciation fixes her. She can find useful work rewarding when its social meaning aligns with the work. Status conflict can also seize the goal. At other times a status cue does not control the observed choice, and sometimes the forecast's exact conjunction is not identifiable. ## 6. Particular affection does not become a general moral rule

The decisive contrast in V15 is Wiz. Aqua approaches a friendly undead officer as an apparent way to advance the return-home problem. She expects opposition; Wiz instead consents and expresses gratitude and love. Aqua rebukes her ready acceptance, hesitates and leaves without casting. There is no demonstrated power failure. The friend's trust changes what carrying out the role would mean. Kazuma calls this conscience, while the other observers' gaze is also present. The scene supports a relational limit on willingness without uniquely isolating private altruism from social pressure. MAIN-15 F119–168.

That restraint has force because the surrounding evidence is not uniformly kind. Aqua can purify Wiz painfully, give an undead friend numbing tea while trying to comfort her, neglect a plea while mining, or repeat an attack after local pity. She offers vegetables to a grieving Wiz in V13, but the completed transfer is unshown. Intention, fitted method and completion need separate descriptions. The V15 scene should therefore strengthen a particular moral conflict rather than establish a new saintly endpoint.

Likewise, Axis loyalty is not a moral certificate. V16 shows genuine devotion and actual danger when Serena threatens Aqua. It also shows binding, tree-suspension and rope-pulling, with imminent threatened execution and sexualized threat. No completed execution, death or sexual assault is shown. Calling the event merely threatened suspension would understate the physical act; calling it a completed killing would overstate it. A goddess's followers can value her and do grave wrong in that value's name. Their conduct cannot be made her unrestricted permission or a vindication of every sectarian judgment.

The moral scale is thus personal, categorical and situational. She can value a familiar undead friend against an impersonal purification role while treating another vulnerable undead being cruelly. She can give a child food and be exacting with household food. These are actual differences in appraisal and relationship, not proof that none of the care counts. The synthesis must retain the people who receive each choice and the costs to those who do not. ## 7. Kazuma: familiar company without a romance inference

Aqua and Kazuma are often mutually accurate about ordinary vulnerabilities. They can cooperate in mischief, flatter each other's evasions and improvise retaliation. Familiarity also makes practical care possible. In V13, after the frog disaster, he gives Aqua first use of the bath, helps her and heats the water; she objects to being won over by kindness from the person responsible. The repair remembers causation. Their direct denial of romantic interest in the same volume should be taken as their present expressions, not decoded into covert attraction or inflated into an eternal prophecy.

The relationship's endurance does not certify its safety. V11 legitimate recovery of property coexists with Kazuma's chosen humiliation of a crying Aqua. His polite question can function as a taunt. Later relief does not cancel the retained Gen03 K04 failure. Aqua's own attacks, control attempts and harmful pressure remain hers. The narrative's quick return to play is evidence of continued membership, not proof of consent to each preceding act.

V15 changes how Kazuma can understand her absence. Town rejection hurts Aqua partly because it denies the usefulness and belonging she normally expects. His initial food-based confidence about bringing her downstairs fails by nightfall, although she later appears on the balcony; she is not shown shut away all night. He can imagine the attractions of a quieter three-person household and still find it boring without her. Her account of a long heavenly life makes him confront prior friends and experiences he did not know, and his role in forcing the move. Her wish to return is neither newly invented in V15 nor an uncomplicated rejection of their shared life.

V16 makes the comparison reciprocal. Mitsurugi and Yunyun are useful companions and the travel is genuinely easier, yet Aqua misses Kazuma's responses and particular problem-solving. No general impossibility of substitutes follows. In V17 she proposes returning to shared home and labor, contributes to the campaign, and later gives private thanks. Kazuma's failure to bring her directly with his final wish remains a relational failure despite his claimed plan of later retrieval. The route and heavenly registration are corroborated later; his prior intention is not independently certified by that corroboration. Her distress and his punitive getting-carried-away explanation remain. An eventual ordinary reunion should not be used to erase the departure's agency or injury. ## 8. Leaving heaven, leaving home, and the incompleteness of the ending

Aqua's heavenly return objective has existed from the beginning. It loses salience in satisfying routine and regains urgency when institutional recognition supplies a concrete reason to act. V15 does not create the goal. Her message from heaven, barrier ideas, persuasion campaigns and final departure distinguish wanting, planning, recruiting and doing. She varies methods when Kazuma refuses. Some are strategic, some comic and coercive, some reckless. She does not already know how the final campaign will work.

Her erased `探してください` is a small but important limit on the fantasy of autonomous departure: she wants to leave and wants to be found. The erasure's embarrassed motive is inferred rather than witnessed. The next volume's stories, pets, Cecily and delays show other ties affecting her route. Anna's story request is an actual service situation, not merely a joke about someone who never cares. Its indirect departure language also means it should not be certified as a fully opposite J01 service case simply because it contains complaint and gossip.

By the final volume, a shared-life alternative is spoken more concretely. That does not prove a performed ordinary job or erase the old heavenly wish. Aqua can accept support, act decisively and still seek the familiar group's return. The ending retains friction, private gratitude and others' choices; it does not grant a final audited household constitution, universal access to heaven or a settled romantic future. The character's attachment is stronger than a generic insistence that all four are useful. It concerns a noisy, sometimes hurtful set of people who know her routines and whose absence has become a real cost.

The development is therefore a widening of the acknowledged stakes. The goddess initially made to inhabit ordinary constraints becomes a person whose separate history and present attachments must both count. The series does not require that she stop being vain, wasteful or impatient for this recognition to matter. Nor does recognition oblige an observer to forgive every failure in order to value her presence. V15 F307–354/Ep59–65; V16 I1/I4/F154–161; V17 C2P540–557, C3P456/498–503, F786.

## 10. Narration makes competence and vulnerability unevenly visible

Kazuma is an observer, participant and interested interpreter. His habitual evaluations of Aqua compete with acts he stages, reports he hears and intentions she expresses. V09 construction is partly a discovery about his former inattention. V15 makes him recognize a previous life he had failed to ask about. His ability to predict her food or money response is sometimes accurate and sometimes fails. Familiarity is considerable evidence of a relationship; it is not transparent access to another mind.

Source planes matter even in comedy. A clerk's liability report differs from witnessed flood damage. Aqua's account of installing a cemetery barrier is a confession, not a freshly shown maintenance scene. A distant conversation Kazuma says he cannot hear well should not become a precise acoustic observation. V14 healing resistance is his hedged inference: Serena's healing on Aqua is ineffective, Aqua has a real wound, and later self-healing is observed, but the private resistance act is not shown. In V17 C3P440, Darkness's sword broken halfway and a large scythe lie at the blood pool where Kazuma was killed. These are weapons, not a reaper's bodily remains; Kazuma's attribution to Aqua's retaliation441 is hedged and does not verify the reaper's death.

Comic amplification can make the speed or scale of an appraisal vivid without proving every private cause. The billions-of-followers boast and Darkness's few-hundred estimate are divergent claims, not an audited census. Approval from a crowd does not authorize likenesses of companions' private bodies. A strange successful conversation with Duke is real rapport through trivia and prejudice, not verified seduction merely because distant observers use a romantic frame.

This source discipline also protects Aqua from the reverse error: treating every successful act as a surprise exception to an independently established idiot essence. Her manual work, perception, prediction of Kazuma and sacred execution have repeated witnesses. Her knowledge can be locally right when the framing voice devalues it. Neither the narrator's affection nor his irritation should settle the analysis in advance.
