# Aulip: threatened rank, evasion and recourse to protection

Status: initial scoped Tier2 monograph, without a reconstruction model. AU28 in the [cohort evidence index](../08%20Evidence%20and%20Indexes/KONOSUBA_ALDERP_MAXWELL_RAGCRAFT_COHORT28_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md) owns the complete MASK U015 context and source-plane distinctions.

## 1. Identity

A retainer introduces Aulip / アウリープ as a retired baron formerly responsible for the town's finances. Rain identifies him as apparently related to Alderp and suspected of various crimes. Her kinship recollection and suspicion are not a complete genealogy or conviction. His name does not identify the separate MASK noble Undain or every wealthy target (AU28-01).

## 2. Evidence sequence

The street encounter moves from a retainer's attempted recruitment, through rejection and Aulip's abuse of the servant, to a summons, attempted evasion, blocked route and magic order. Vanir's nonlethal fight is later recollected. Aulip's fear at seeing the royal party is visible; his flight to police for protection is Iris's report (AU28-01–02).

## 3. Wants and entitlement

He expects rank to secure compliance and punish perceived disrespect. His own demand to identify and execute the rejecting people makes the entitlement concrete. The monetary mistress offer belongs to the retainer's speech, not Aulip's mouth. Correcting that attribution does not remove Aulip's responsibility for his own assault, threats and orders (AU28-01).

## 4. Affect and loss of control

He kicks a subordinate after the approach fails, then turns pale at a direct summons and later at the royal encounter. The sequence contrasts outward dominance over weaker people with fear of accountability to stronger institutions. Rain's description of desperation and Vanir's explanation of legal danger remain interpretations of those changes (AU28-01–02).

## 5. Resources and limits

Aulip has a carrying chair, retainer, knights and social standing. Knights question the street-magic order before casting upward. They subsequently draw swords in another phase. Their hesitation and selected direction mean the order does not equal fully controlled indiscriminate injury; civilian wounds are not shown (AU28-02).

## 6. Appraisal and choice

He claims illness and orders a later explanation after hearing that his personal attendance is required. When bystanders obstruct departure, he escalates to a display of magic. A strong local account links threatened accountability with evasion and coercion. A broader invariant rule would overfit one linked scene; genuine illness is not independently examined, while the sequence makes avoidance a reasonable interpretation (AU28-02).

## 7. Relationships and other agents

The retainer misjudges the adventurers and is then kicked. The woman rejects the offer and the queried bystander refuses presumed name knowledge; neither is merely a prop for Aulip's humiliation. Knights, Claire, Rain, Iris and Vanir act for different reasons. Aulip's commands cannot stand in for their entire psychology (AU28-01–02).

## 8. Ordinary material life

Former financial office and present elite transport provide context, not a measured record of administrative competence, spending, domestic life or friendships. The retainer's estimate of beginner income is his sales argument. The woman's higher expectations do not establish that she would consent at a sufficiently high price (AU28-01).

## 9. Speech and register

Aulip's own printed speech uses rank distinctions, insult, execution threat and urgent imperatives. The polished status introduction and financial proposal belong to another speaker. The switch from ordering commoners to an excuse before the household's authority is more informative than a generic arrogant-noble catchphrase (AU28-01–02).

## 10. Comic reversal and seriousness

A noble's promised benefit proves unimpressive to adventurers; later a threatening figure seeks institutional protection. Those reversals depend on unequal knowledge and local material expectations. The servant's actual kick and the risk of street magic remain meaningful. The lack of watched civilian injury is neither proof of kind intent nor a substitute for the knights' separate restraint (AU28-01–02).

## 11. Rivals and contrary evidence

Universal bravery fails against evasion and visible fear. Omnipotent rank fails against refusal, obstruction and the royal encounter. A merely harmless bluster account fails against the kick and magic order. A completely known criminal biography would exceed Rain's suspicion and Vanir's forecast; the direct present choices already carry enough adverse evidence without invented convictions (AU28-01–02).

## 12. Tier and unresolved scope

This dense encounter warrants a monograph because Aulip changes strategy, uses resources, harms another and faces failed control. It does not yet warrant an independent generative model. The actual completed legal outcome, durable correction, earlier finances and anonymous recurrences remain unproved. The historical census phrase assigning the sexual-financial offer directly to him is retained in history and explicitly corrected in the new scope. No model, grade, confidence gain or global closure follows.
