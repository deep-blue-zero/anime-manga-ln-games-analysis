# The YORI02 dryad: living dependents, unequal demands and a negotiated limit

## 1. A particular forest speaker

This is the dryad encountered near Axel in YORI-02 U016, not every plant spirit, the MAIN11 forest Princess, a Tranquility Girl or an independent second ghost. Her voice precedes the fleeing deer at P190–192, her upper body emerges from a tree at194–196, and Aqua corrects Kazuma's ghost identification at212. The narrated physical intervention is firmer evidence than either Kazuma's first category or an assumed personality associated with a beautiful forest maiden.

The current C44 receiving includes the complete390-P paragraph/ruby projection and the other nine prose stories. It is dependent reinspection of an already read local edition. No current image, nonparagraph-residual, whole-edition, native-human or independent-performance claim follows. All locators below use this U016 member. Earlier reading and model histories retain their own scopes.

## 2. Chronology and the translation boundary

The supposed dangerous ghost begins as an institutional report of interference with gatherers139; Megumin's dead-gatherer origin140 is conjecture. The deer chase follows Aqua's stone155–156, Darkness's Decoy works165–166, and Kazuma's arrows kill or fell deer173 before their attack on his tree179. The dryad's voice changes that immediate danger192. Kazuma offers thanks and a handshake203–204, then hears Aqua translate a dismissive answer206–209.

Her raw speech is represented with non-Japanese glyphs. The Japanese propositions in this scene are Aqua's mediation, explicitly marked as an interpretation207. Kazuma does not independently understand the language. Observable smiling, pointing, anger and vines therefore need separate treatment from the exact wording, truth and pragmatic tone of the translated account. An attractive appearance does not disprove harsh speech, and a hostile translation does not prove that every unseen nuance has been preserved.

## 3. What she values

Her attributed objection is that living wild vegetables should not be casually taken232. Her translated reference to plants as her dependents206/263 provides a local responsibility claim. It is not evidence of a universal, consistently applied doctrine about every organism. She threatens to make future gatherers fertilizer232 and proposes that humans attempt photosynthesis or bury themselves238. Those demands impose the burden of adjustment on people rather than reconciling their actual nutritional needs.

She also wants something for herself or her connected plants: the delicious, magic-rich water reported through wind spirits247, plus fertilizer263. Aqua's household water is Kazuma's inference250, and Aqua interestedly accepts that explanation252–256. The water's appeal is an attributed motive; the complete travel history and a controlled demonstration of its source are absent. Her resource interest and living-plant claim coexist. Neither may simply erase the other.

## 4. Affect, dignity and unequal treatment

The smiling tree apparition196/202 does not have a uniformly gentle interaction. Aqua reports contempt for Kazuma206/209 and Megumin225; the dryad herself becomes angry when the harvest complaint is raised230. When Darkness offers help as a lord's daughter231, she exploits the breadth of that offer in the fertilizer demand263–275. Darkness's refusal272/276 is a real limit, not proof that a noble should carry out any later specified task.

Her disappointed gestures and commands288–290 retain a bossy relation to the humans. They do not establish an invariant temperament outside this encounter. Aqua is simultaneously interpreter, resource holder and a participant being blamed, so the performance is not a neutral interview between a transparent speaker and a disinterested recorder.

## 5. Effective power with a ceiling

The initial voice is followed by deer retreat192/199. Later, vines restrain deer and assist Darkness324–325. These are useful local capacities. They do not establish control over all animals: the translated account describes a recurring conflict with the boss deer308, whose arrival creates a real emergency312–325/331. Calling plants dependents does not make the whole forest perfectly obedient.

Kazuma argues that the dryad controlled yesterday's unusually accurate garden attacks291–294. Knowledge of his defeat206 and the reused challenge308 support a connection, but the causal assignment remains his inference. No direct confession identifies every garden movement as her deliberate remote command. A model must not acquire an unlimited surveillance or puppet-control power merely to make the earlier joke tidy.

## 6. Choice under competing threats

The dryad rejects easy human access, asks for restitution and tries to direct Kazuma to fetch resources290. These choices occur while she appears secure. When deer return, she helps a human defender324–325. That cooperation is compatible with shared danger; it does not establish a new unconditional alliance, a reversal of every harvest objection or repentance for the threats.

She protests the dangerous spell through Aqua329–330. Megumin nevertheless casts362, blasting the deer and surrounding trees368. The dryad's renewed anger369/372 matters: the humans' escape is not the same outcome as the protection of her plants. Kazuma's account then records a negotiated arrangement373–375. The settlement follows persuasion after damage, rather than a spontaneous gift that proves limitless generosity.

## 7. Relationships and competing responsibilities

The dryad's claimed relation to plants is protective and hierarchical. Humans appear as harvesters, possible fertilizer suppliers, a magical-water source and temporary fighting partners. Darkness's noble duty231 does not automatically create a reciprocal duty in the dryad to accommodate every human practice. Conversely, the dryad's claimed representation of plants does not settle her authority to threaten people or require humiliating contributions.

Aqua's ability to translate enables contact without guaranteeing either control or agreement. The humans need her language access, while the dryad seeks her water. Kazuma's anger, Megumin's proposed spirit-slayer title296–297 and Darkness's experience-seeking add their own motives. The outcome cannot be credited solely to a generic benevolent negotiator or to one all-knowing forest agent.

## 8. Ordinary provision and an unperformed future

The resolution concerns repeat material work. Aqua is to provide water, and naturally expired wild vegetables are to be supplied373–374. The latter changes the relation between human food and the dryad's living-plant objection. No future watering trip or vegetable handover is shown. The guild's actual payment366–367 confirms institutional acceptance of this special resolution, not proof that every obligation was later kept.

This distinction also constrains the resource claim. She has not banned all possible plant-derived provision in the represented settlement, but the text does not establish an unlimited free-food service, a standard rate, an enforceable contract or a complete distinction between every form of harvesting and natural expiry. Those remain conditions for later inquiry.

## 9. Written voice and reader access

The contrast between a smiling forest maiden and Aqua's cutting interpretations is part of her presentation. The auditor can describe that effect and identify the mediator; it cannot certify the translated Japanese as the dryad's exact native idiolect. Nor should a hypothetical reconstruction give her omniscient knowledge of every human feeling or a conveniently fluent Japanese private monologue.

An appropriate representation could use an explicitly translated exchange and visible actions. Requests, refusals, resource conditions and plant states can then remain legible without claiming to reproduce an unheard accent or complete spirit grammar. Acoustic and native-human adequacy are untested.

## 10. Comedy and serious costs

The episode overturns Kazuma's assumption that ordinary plants and deer are safe. The dryad's rescue immediately becomes an affront to his pride; fertilizer changes meaning; the great human rescue spell damages the forest it passes through. These reversals depend on actual acts, incompatible goals and material consequences. They do not require treating the death threat as a completed killing or the refused demand as performed.

Her intervention matters to Kazuma's survival. Her threats and disregard for Darkness's limit matter too. Megumin's damage and Kazuma's threatened retaliation do not become harmless simply because the party receives a reward and closes with cheerful dialogue. Each actor retains responsibility for the phase they caused.

## 11. Rival accounts and the narrow reconstruction claim

A generic protector of dependents explains much. A resource collector may explain the journey and bargaining; comic reversal may explain why the supposedly gentle maiden speaks harshly. These rivals are not isolated by a single encounter. Still, the text constrains a narrower conditional account: the dryad contests taking connected living plants, while a usable arrangement involving wanted water and naturally expired plants can be accepted after conflict. “Always oppose humans,” “freely give any plant food” and “care only about maximum payment” each lose part of that local pattern.

Only this bounded constraint is proposed for a one-rule provisional model. Its transferred extension remains defeasible and must preserve contrary threats, infeasible proposals, imperfect control, mediated speech and the unperformed service. No second rule is added merely to describe her power, irritation or cooperation. A small source-known exercise cannot establish causal isolation, accuracy or broad personality reliability.

## 12. Current disposition and open questions

The dryad earns a substantial character monograph and a narrowly scoped provisional generator because her stated plant interest has a conditional consequence across refusal, resource seeking, fighting and settlement. This is a Tier3 artifact claim at that limited scope; maturity, independent discrimination and future-source validity remain open. The original outputs of any subsequent diagnostic must be retained even if they miss the condition or improve the source into an excessively fair bargain.

Unresolved questions include the exact raw utterances, personal history, garden-control mechanism, full reasons for the initial rescue, the reach of her plant obligations and whether the promised exchange succeeds. She is an agent with useful power and unequal demands, rather than a generic harmless ecological symbol. No census or full-program closure follows from this study.
