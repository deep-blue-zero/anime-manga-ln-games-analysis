## 2. Ordinary access is a consequential good

Royal comfort does not supply the experience Iris wants. MAIN06 C2P98-118 gives interest in school and festival activity, but also practical questions about customers, wages and profit. She is unfamiliar with a social arrangement, rather than unable to reason about it. The school suggestion meets a real war alarm and her own recognition that study alone is not enough atP128-130. The ordinary-life wish and public problem enter the same scene.

Her game request is especially precise. AtC2P234-254 she wants an opponent who tries, including the possibility of losing. Kazuma's refusal to throw matches matters because rank ordinarily blocks reciprocal play. She initially wins enough that he protests the stopping point. Later his false artifact description wins a handicap. Rain corrects it, Iris objects to the unfairness and demands a rematch; a subsequent distraction strategy then wins for her atP329. Genuine ability, exploitable ignorance, protest and learning coexist. The directly rechecked sequence atP285-307/319-335 also prevents converting her preference for a real opponent into a principled ban on tactical advantage. She explicitly calls the distraction calculated. The synthetic refusal of a ceremonial win is a plausible completion of its supplied setup, not a general fairness trait.

MAIN10 C2P270-300 extends this distinction into cooking. Iris asks to help, reports that the castle refuses such work, accepts bounded instructions and corrects Kazuma about checking whether an ingredient is alive. Neither combat excellence nor sheltered opportunity predicts the whole competence map. The ordinary task lets her contribute while still being a novice.

## 3. Recognition is more specific than attention

Kazuma's importance is partly that he gives Iris a relation in which royal rank does not settle every exchange. MAIN06 C2P543-546 explicitly identifies nondeference, openness and genuine competition as things she likes. Older-brother address refers to an informal relation she says she has lost with her actual brother. That attachment should be described at her stated age of12. Kazuma's wishful or sexualized speculations are his perspective, not proof of her present adult agreement.

MASK adds a qualification to an access-only account. Iris says she wants praise for having done well, has organized a public ruler performance and learns that its political premise is stale. Rather than simply accept an ordinary visit, she declines that available option because she wants an achievement of her own before reunion: MASK U015P137-170/210-223. This is personal aspiration, not merely separation distress or a demand for constant entertainment.

It can also distort judgment. A villain becomes attractive partly because he can complete the desired reveal. The observed civilian threat is real, so the motive is not wholly invented theater; nevertheless, the performance goal precedes finding the suitable opponent. Treating every such plan as pure public duty would hide the order.

The MAIN06 farewell prevents a uniformly gentle voice portrait. At finaleP143-194 she mentions the silver-haired thief chief, reacts to the interrupted first request, argues with Megumin over older-brother address and asserts that royalty is strong. The two begin a scuffle and guardians intervene. She finally asks Kazuma for another game. Kazuma hoped for a different request; his hope is not an actual utterance by Iris. Play, rivalry, rank language, embarrassment and a bounded familiar wish coexist without an adult settled relationship.

## 7. Relationships are mechanisms, not a supporting cast

Kazuma offers genuine play, stories, attention and recognition, while also transmitting misinformation and encouraging impulsive demands. Iris can value him without accurately judging every claim. Megumin offers peer adventure and real skill, but manipulates roles, conceals information and solicits the false-cover assurance. Yunyun supplies skilled help and wanted friendship, while preserving her own refusals and knowledge limits. Iris's desire for ordinary friendship is not a claim on either girl's unlimited labor.

Claire and Rain combine care with control. Their danger judgments can be sensible, and Iris's expressed wishes can still be overridden. Claire's affection may tempt indulgence rather than uniformly stricter discipline; Rain can correct politics and prices while bearing practical pay consequences. Neither is a generic interchangeable guardian.

Dust's flight has Iris's scoped willingness but does not retroactively authorize earlier mask intrusion. Her indirect thanks leaves him room not to confess. Vanir's temporary service answers an agreed role and his own incentives; his refusal of longer employment for a friend remains an independent choice. The wished-for good is reciprocal life with particular people, not abstract possession of whoever can entertain or protect her.

## 10. Comedy, comparison and the present interpretation

The comic mechanisms carry different evidential force. Genuine game frustration, a calculated distraction, a specific stop request and a false-witness offer are actual choices in their scenes. Competitive bursts and the retainers' amplified reactions can exaggerate intensity without deleting the choice or its cost. Extraordinary combat is a supplied in-world capacity, not automatically nonliteral license. Neither a comic court tactic nor a warm ending turns coercion, injury or misinformation into an event that did not happen.

A useful comparison with Yunyun is therefore a question, rather than an equivalence. Both value ordinary company and demonstrate real skill; Iris can deploy rank and money to change access, while Yunyun's invitation and role boundaries require their own source history. Their shared presence does not establish identical inhibition or loneliness. Comparison with Komekko remains deferred until the latter's scarcity, family position and own choices are semantically reviewed. Child agency is shared analytical responsibility, not evidence of a shared personality.

The current interpretation is a capable, developing agent who seeks reciprocal ordinary life and meaningful recognition while negotiating real obligations. She can reduce a wish, assert a boundary, use tactical advantage or recruit unequal influence. Duty, imitation, novelty and attachment remain competing explanations whose strength depends on the specific episode. The model should predict the kind of mistake possible under those conditions, while admitting where the evidence cannot choose a response.
