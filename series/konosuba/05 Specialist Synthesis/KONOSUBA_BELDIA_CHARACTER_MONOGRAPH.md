# Beldia: a defended home, selective chivalry and escalating retaliation

Status: cohort27 initial monograph, proposed scoped Tier3 with a separate provisional model. The [cohort27 evidence index](../08%20Evidence%20and%20Indexes/KONOSUBA_BELDIA_HANS_SERENA_COHORT27_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md) owns BE27 locators and their narrative planes. All sources were already admitted and known. The shared source recheck totals include other actors, blanks and repeated accounts; they are not independent decisions or validation trials. Semantic receiving and the containing publication remain pending at authorship.

## 1. Identity and narrative position

Beldia / ベルディア is the named Dullahan executive who occupies the castle near Axel and is purified in MAIN01. His name in the return encounter secures continuity with the earlier unnamed Dullahan complainant (BE27-01–04). Neither a generic Dullahan skill discussion nor another headless combatant is automatically this person. The DUST07 commander explicitly treats Beldia as a former superior and later reveals his own Dullahan nature; that establishes two agents, not Beldia's resurrection (BE27-12).

The executive's body and detachable head are parts of one agent. The head supplies a movable vantage point and later a point of dependence. His mount and undead troops have distinct roles; they do not become separate reconstructed minds from serving him. Conversely, their commander does not disappear into an anonymous army aggregate merely because he served Beldia.

## 2. Evidence and chronology

The MAIN01 grievance begins with repeated explosions against his apparently abandoned residence. Megumin's later description of an intentional lure is not the whole prior record: the established routine and Kazuma's participation precede that justification. Beldia complains, identifies a capable priest and curses the person who shields his intended target. His proposed route to a cure is deliberately conditional and dangerous. Aqua actually removes the curse; the subsequent quiet week is consistent with that removal, not a test of what an untreated curse would have done (BE27-01).

The return brings troops, renewed grievance and a threat against the town. The curse did not kill Darkness as he expected, and the bombardment continued. He praises what he takes to be her fidelity while condemning supposed abandonment, then orders lethal violence. Aqua's initial purification hurts without removing him; his knights are destroyed by Explosion; he then fights personally and kills three men. Water, separation from the head, damage to the armor and final purification belong to an interacting defeat sequence (BE27-02–05).

MASK supplies a prior, differently mediated episode. Vanir receives Wiz's account of a month-long curse affecting her party; later he hears Beldia offer curse removal, praise courage and request a draw. The exact completed cure and outcome are not watched in that passage. It is nevertheless substantial contrary evidence to a rule that Beldia must always fight to a lethal finish (BE27-06). DUST03 adds a projected past of castle upkeep and retaliation preparations; it is a mediated image of earlier conduct, not a new live encounter (BE27-07).

## 3. Motivations and valuations

A protected residence, freedom from humiliating interruption, acknowledged martial standing and revenge against identified offenders are better-supported goals than indiscriminate destruction as his only interest. The projected household scenes show him cleaning, ordering help, checking dust and repairing damage. His initial patience with what he imagines are novices is meaningful, even though the eventual solution remains a planned killing rather than a safe educational exercise (BE27-07).

The strategic assignment is not interchangeable with the grievance. Beldia himself says a fortune-teller account of a great light brought him to investigate (BE27-02, MAIN01C4P70). A later Mitsurugi report also connects his dispatch with that account; the earlier EXP03 reception report leaves destination and purpose unknown. These reports can explain why he is in the region without making every local inconvenience an intentional campaign against the economy (BE27-10–11). The received evidence does not establish an independently verified private ranking of military duty, household order and reputation.

## 4. Personality and affect

Beldia can be patient, proud, aggrieved, appreciative, confused and frightened in different conditions. Repeated attacks and an opponent's apparent disregard intensify his anger. He notices courage and technical surprises, yet recognition does not reliably inhibit violence. His pleasure at the defeat of his own assembled knights undermines the simpler claim that he uses troops only because he is too afraid to fight (BE27-02–03).

Darkness repeatedly supplies a sexualized captivity interpretation that he does not share; his confusion and relief at Kazuma's intervention are real contrary evidence to assigning that fantasy to him. That distinction does not establish sexual harmlessness. Wiz later reports repeated attempts to peer under her skirt with his detached head. Selective restraint and reported intrusive conduct can coexist in the same person (BE27-01, BE27-08).

## 5. Competence and dependence

His sword skill is directly demonstrated against a group and separately praised by Wiz. He uses his head as an elevated visual aid, frees both hands for fighting and rapidly recognizes that the apparent beginners are unusual. Calling the encounter easy would erase the three lethal strikes, Darkness's wounded body and the failure of early theft attempts (BE27-03–04, BE27-08).

His protection is conditional. Aqua's early TurnUndead spells cause visible effects but do not purge him. This is partial resistance, not a failure to cast and not immunity to every holy attack. Water becomes a decisive vulnerability under the later flood conditions. The broken armor and removed head matter before purification. No single event isolates the effect of every component, and retrospective descriptions of inexhaustible stamina or universally lethal curses do not constitute new tests of those claims (BE27-02–04, BE27-14).

## 6. Appraisal, choice and correction

Beldia does more than repeat an angry challenge. He assesses a priest's job and apparent level, questions whether Aqua is really a beginner after surviving her painful purification, reacts to a damaged plan, chooses direct combat after losing troops and uses defensive movement. The conditional cure route transforms grievance into leverage over another person's life. In the projected preparation he tries to make a challenge passable enough to draw the intended victim into his own killing position (BE27-01–03, BE27-07).

His flexibility has limits. Repeated provocation and martial self-presentation can bring him into costly engagement; losing his head to Steal creates a dependency he cannot repair through force at that moment. His polite request for its return is a local response to a changed constraint, not a general benevolence certificate. The earlier offer of a draw shows another possible exit, but its sincerity and completion remain less directly observed than the MAIN01 battle (BE27-04, BE27-06).

## 7. Differentiated relationships

Megumin is both the actual repeated attacker and the person he tries to force into the castle. Darkness first appears to him as an admirable protector, then as a remarkably durable and confusing opponent. Aqua changes the technical situation through curse removal and water/purification, while Kazuma initially appears weaker than he later proves tactically. These appraisals develop; reader knowledge of Aqua's divinity cannot be installed in Beldia before the scene supplies it (BE27-01–04).

Wiz's report rejects a close-friend assumption based on their common executive office. Vanir's prior encounter allows face-saving bargaining under threat. The DUST07 subordinate invokes Beldia's esteem and revenge while openly aspiring to his vacant rank; that is evidence of the subordinate's agency and report of Beldia's appraisal, not proof of universal loyal attachment among his troops (BE27-06, BE27-08, BE27-12).

## 8. Ordinary life and unguarded interests

The strongest ordinary-life material is the DUST03 projection: maintaining an inhabitable castle, cleaning and taking satisfaction in its condition. It supports a local preference for domestic order, qualified by the projection's proposed residual-memory mechanism. Later castle damage observed by Dust does not overwrite Kazuma's earlier impression of an apparently intact building (BE27-07).

The Demon King later reports that Beldia repeatedly forgot his head in the bath. This is useful adverse evidence against perfectly disciplined management, but remains an attributed recollection. Wiz's near-death image of Beldia calling her from beyond the river is not independently watched afterlife conduct or a new resurrection. Neither joke supplies a complete leisure biography (BE27-09, BE27-13).

## 9. Japanese voice and pragmatic shifts

The received dialogue moves between an offended demand for recognition, formal curse/challenge staging, technical appraisal, surprise and a dependent request. Address and accusation matter more than reproducing a catchphrase. His reprimands distinguish a purportedly honorable protector from the supposed companions who abandoned her; the inference is his, not a narrator-certified account of their conduct (BE27-01–04).

The translation-sensitive danger is to treat theatrical knight language as either empty performance or an ethical guarantee. Both remove information. Printed speech and the projected memory are not an audio performance; no accent or fixed delivery is inferred. A conventional threat or idiom is not automatically an L3 language dependency.

## 10. Comedy and seriousness

Detached-head football, broken expectations about captivity and the abruptly polite request make the conflict comic. They do not turn the curse, lethal strikes or repeated harassment into consensual play. The three men return through resurrection, but they were killed; financial compensation later does not undo the initial flood or eliminate the period of debt (BE27-01–05, BE27-14).

A serious transfer should preserve grievance, selective standards, tactical appraisal and changing dependence while using only stipulated capacities. It need not preserve literal detachable-head physics in a human analogue. Such an analogue is an informed change of setting, not a validated psychological result. H3 marks a local comic contingency; it is not a synonym for uncertainty, and fantasy embodiment alone is not H4.

## 11. Contrary evidence and competing interpretations

An always-honorable knight fails against peeping, hostage leverage and town-directed violence. A mindless aggressor fails against patience, household maintenance, conditional challenges and the prior draw proposal. A coward who never fights fails against his personal engagement after losing the knights. An invulnerable elite fails against the interacting water/head/armor defeat. A faultless planner fails against disrupted preparations and reported absent-mindedness (BE27-01–09).

Generic revenge, a stock proud antagonist and a narrator's comic framing remain serious rivals to a more differentiated mechanism. The separate model therefore makes only conditional proposals: grievance can alter restraint, appraisal can alter tactics and dependence can alter bargaining. It does not infer a hidden honorable motive to explain every harmful choice or claim that multiple retellings independently validate a rule.

## 12. Current interpretation and uncertainty

Beldia merits a dedicated account and a provisional generative model because the evidence includes an initial demand, changed constraints, tactical corrections, domestic material and contrary accounts from distinct perspectives. The evidence is asymmetric: combat appraisal and grievance are better identified than affection, long-term institutional loyalty, unprovoked civilian conduct or the complete past life of the knight.

The strongest conclusion is selective, condition-dependent restraint within a violent repertoire. Whether restraint lasts without a credible exit or recognized status remains open. Kazuma's YORI03 expectation that an unharassed Beldia would eventually leave is a counterfactual plan in an alternative restart segment, not an observed departure. New source-known hypotheticals can inspect consistency, but cannot provide blind accuracy, an evaluator grade or confidence gain. The wider census and KonoSuba program remain unfinished.
