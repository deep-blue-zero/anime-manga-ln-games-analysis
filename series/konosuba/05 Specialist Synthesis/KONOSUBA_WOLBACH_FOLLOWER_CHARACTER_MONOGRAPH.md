# The unnamed YORI02 Wolbach follower: reverence, leverage and failed superiority

## 1. Identity without a borrowed name

This is the hooded Chomusuke abductor in YORI02 U012. Megumin initially infers a man from his build131; the narrative subsequently calls him a man and depicts a one-horned, yellow-eyed devil-like appearance203/206. His own intended self-introduction is interrupted202–206. No personal name is supplied.

Wolbach is his declared master202, while Arnes and Hoost are servants whose failed missions he references200. He is therefore not assigned their names, complete minds, contracts or histories. Shared reverence and a similar retrieval task establish a relation between roles; they do not collapse this agent into the already studied actors.

## 2. Evidence and access

The complete283-P U012 paragraph/ruby projection is received in C44 alongside nine other YORI02 prose components. The narrator here is Megumin, just over a week after joining the party19. This is important access: she recognizes a possible connection before Kazuma understands it133–138, but her assessments of his abilities and the follower's danger remain her own.

Current images, nonparagraph residuals and whole-edition reinspection are not claimed. The local edition is source-known, and the study adds no independent validation. Every locator below is U012 unless specified otherwise.

## 3. The goal and its moral cost

The follower snatches Chomusuke129 and repeatedly uses reverent address136/141. He later explains that his plan was to seize Wolbach quickly and escape on foot176. His target is particular, rather than an arbitrary valuable cat. This account supports loyal pursuit but not the target's present consent, a known command from the master or a complete understanding of divided divinity.

His reverence does not make the method harmless. He abducts the creature, holds Darkness under a knife167–174, threatens her life208 and actually attacks216. Darkness's invented allegation of sexual demands180 is denied181/184 and must not be added as a true offense. That correction leaves the actual abduction, threats and claw strike intact.

## 4. Pride, frustration and presentation

The man corrects Kazuma's demeaning description of the cat136, objects to the false public accusation141 and becomes distressed as the situation escapes his plan159/176/185. His frustration has a comprehensible cause without excusing his conduct. He treats the encounter as an opportunity to display superiority200–203, and Aqua interrupts the display with a real attack204–206.

After the first Steal, he laughs and speaks as if the party's success explains the failures of his predecessors198–202. This is an adaptive appraisal rather than proof that he is either incapable of updating or now respects every opponent correctly. His final anger and embarrassment271 occur after the second theft has undone his new leverage. No inner monologue supplies a complete private account of either moment.

## 5. Competence and mistaken classifications

He reaches the square before the protagonists despite their shortcut168, giving Megumin a basis to infer unusual speed169–170. A strong ordinary escape capacity does not protect him from Kazuma's fabricated bounty story151, crowd obstruction154–159 or Steal190/258. The narrator explicitly notes that the first theft works against the usual difficulty of stealing from a high-level target194–195; it is not a generic proof that the follower is weak.

Darkness is an ineffective hostage for the first kind of leverage. His knife is taken193, she remains unusually unworried197, and his later claw strike does not produce the expected injury217–221. He also correctly identifies Aqua as a dangerous priest184, but a warning does not stop her Exorcism204. Useful recognition and failed control coexist.

## 6. Decisions as leverage changes

The crowd blocks the route, so he changes direction146. He has already acquired Darkness as a hostage by the next narrated scene167–174; the exact capture procedure is not shown. After losing the knife he retains physical control197/207 and escalates to claws208–216. These are successive attempts, not one immediately completed hostage resolution.

Megumin asks Aqua to stop further purification232. The follower then infers that Chomusuke can constrain the party235, discards Darkness237 and raises the bag238. He tries to leave239. That is a concrete change in leverage using newly received information. It does not establish that the creature is unharmed, agrees to travel or shares his purpose. He is drawn into answering a provocation257, and Kazuma actually steals the bag258–264 before Megumin casts281.

## 7. Relationships and competing knowledge

The claimed master relationship motivates pursuit but remains asymmetrical. The follower's language treats Chomusuke as someone to revere, while his actual handling places the small body in danger. Reverence is not identical to competent care, present comprehension or permission. Megumin's concern and the protagonists' rescue have their own mixed motives and conduct.

He disputes Darkness's false account181/184, yet still threatens and attacks her. Kazuma's false civilian warnings140/151 expose bystanders to a danger Megumin recognizes143. Those civilians are not informed allies whose risk was accepted. A study of the follower must retain both his harmful agency and the protagonists' misleading recruitment, rather than using one to erase the other.

## 8. Material limits and missing ordinary life

The bag, knife, legs, claws and hostage positions are the means the source actually gives him. His plans depend on routes, attention and the target's location. A claimed ability to escape quickly176 is checked by social obstruction and repeated theft, not by a measured universal speed hierarchy.

There is no observed domestic life, employment outside this mission, provisioning practice, leisure routine or ordinary close relation beyond the claimed master. Those absences constrain the monograph; they are not a reason to invent a civilian persona or permanently bar an unnamed antagonist from a future model. His practical constraints in this encounter are already substantial characterization.

## 9. Written voice and interruption

Reverent reference to the master136/176/202 alternates with threats208, wounded pride219 and a thwarted grand introduction202–206. The local recipient and tactical situation explain much of the variation. The narration does not provide enough to assign every devil a shared idiolect, infer a regional accent or certify native-human generative adequacy.

His denials are also part of his voice. Preserving181/184 prevents the narrator's joke about Darkness from being misattributed as his actual demand. His rebuttal235 to Aqua's proliferation claim is an uttered objection, not an independently verified universal demon biology.

## 10. Comedy without a harmlessness verdict

The abductor's carefully planned swift recovery becomes an improvised hostage farce. Darkness does not fit the expected victim role, Aqua interrupts his theatrical introduction, and the same stealing skill removes two different objects. These reversals rely on real attempts and material changes. They do not make the knife threat a consensual game.

Megumin feels valued when assigned the final firepower role251–262. That belonging is her development, not the follower's endorsement of the outcome. Explosion is cast281; no separate body, permanent extinction, custody, confession or trial follows in the text. A comic endpoint must not supply an unshown final metaphysical fate.

## 11. Rivals and reconstruction boundary

Generic mission loyalty, pride, opportunistic coercion and ordinary adaptation to a failed tactic all explain the sequence. The source distinguishes this agent's target, changing leverage and mistaken superiority. It does not yet establish which of those motives governs an unfamiliar ordinary setting, how he ranks loyalty against a genuine master refusal or what he would do without hostage opportunities.

The current disposition is Tier2, with a substantial individual monograph and no generated model or diagnostic. This limit follows from underidentified transfer conditions and the narrow mission context, not anonymity, short appearance or antagonist status. A later generator would need explicit rivals and contrary cases, with no borrowed rules from Hoost or Arnes.

## 12. Current account and open limits

The follower is a reverent pursuer and a harmful coercer, physically capable and repeatedly outmaneuvered. He changes tactics with information, but does not escape pride and faulty expectations. His particular target relation matters without proving shared memory or a justified right to retrieve Chomusuke.

Personal identity, the actual instruction behind the mission, exact knowledge of the target's state, life outside pursuit and final fate remain open. The study preserves actual threats, corrected false allegations, new leverage and both successful thefts. Earlier named-character models and originals remain unchanged; no new-person denominator or full-program closure follows.
