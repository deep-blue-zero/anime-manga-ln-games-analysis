## Sophie — correction, risk awareness and concrete support

**Identity and relation.** Sophie is ソフィ: Terry names her during the early argument, and Megumin explicitly joins the name to the woman who dodges the falling slime. Later the narrative names her spear support, and Yunyun names all three companions in the capital. The sharp eyes and apparently strong disposition are Megumin's physical impression, not proof of a stable private temperament. She is neither Leen, Lolisa nor a generic female slot whose consent stands for every teammate. (S01–S03/S08.)

Sophie first challenges Rex's contempt toward the girls, invoking their Crimson Demon identity and suggesting they could be stronger than he is. This is a real disagreement with the leader, yet its evidential basis is still a group reputation heuristic. She does not thereby know the exact spells, level, resources or preferences of either girl. Her correction can be useful without becoming infallible assessment. Terry's following heard claim is Terry's, while the girls' denial of upper magic does not prove complete inability (S01).

Her own response to the slime is physical and performed: she rolls clear of its fall. Yunyun kills it with Lightning and then burns the swarm. Sophie has dodged; she has not cast those spells or personally completed the whole clearance. Group admiration and firefighting do not resolve every unassigned line or hand. A description that merely makes her a person rescued by magic would omit her actual evasive action (S02).

After the expedition retreats, Sophie counters Rex's disparagement of the sword hero. She cites the report that he cut a wing despite injury and asks whether that enabled their escape. She then recommends waiting for high-level reinforcements against a foe described as commander class. This is an attributable argument about contribution and risk. It is not her direct observation of the frontal fight, certainty that aid will arrive, or a universal rule against risk. Her subsequent participation in dangerous action is real contrary evidence to a portrait of someone who always waits for rescue (S03).

On the cage expedition she supplies a hint about the large black creature and the demon's search. The target identification and promised benefit can be wrong even while her information is part of a practical plan. Her spear follows Rex's charge against the goblins. Terry remains near the mages and intercepts the ambush; those acts remain his. The nearby female recruitment voice offers the reassurance that the party is not all men. Sophie is the supported contextual candidate for that self-reference, but the passage does not explicitly attach a speaker tag to each recruitment line. It must not manufacture an independently confirmed declaration of all her personal terms (S04–S05).

Following the sudden Hoost assault, she returns in a group described as healed only enough to fight barely. She and Terry step forward in Rex's place and shield Yunyun. The narrator hears and infers their later fall, with impact sounds; that is less specific than a cleanly watched blow sequence. Her willingness to participate is not full bodily recovery or permission for every later injury. Her eventual encouragement to finish Hoost comes before Yunyun's inability is fully exposed. Saying that the crew is all right does not make the reassurance medically true or guarantee that someone can perform the requested act (S06–S07).

Ordinary continuity exists without a full biography. Sophie is prepared to travel at the capital departure; later she is among the named companions in a familiar guild encounter. The later adjacent woman and axeman are a qualified continuity join, with explicit Rex context, rather than new names independently spoken in every scene. A smile and group surprise provide limited affect evidence. They do not prove an intimate relationship, identical motives, collective memory, an independent choice of every relocation term or a completed profitable career (S08–S09).

Her speech can challenge Rex, offer a report-based counterargument, explain a hint and encourage a final action. The forms あたし and direct conversational questioning have different uses from anonymous shouted praise. There is no justified single fixed English catchphrase, complete ruby/audio claim or native-human language test. Comic group framing does not erase her practical contribution or make an overstated reassurance reliable.

**Disposition and gaps.** A scoped Tier1 profile currently fits the attributable evidence. Her practical judgment, disagreement, bodily risk and individual skill earn more than a collective cast list, while most private goals, ordinary preferences, other relationships and repeated choices outside the party task remain unknown. The rival of a competent teammate responding to strongly specified situations is powerful. A larger monograph or conditional model remains possible with further discriminating evidence. ## Terry — fallible information, transport and actual protection

**Identity and relation.** Terry is テリー, explicitly addressed by Rex after the heard account of Crimson Demon expertise. The cage driver and battle-axe guard are later named, as is the man who physically intercepts the attacker aimed at Yunyun. Yunyun supplies his name again at the later capital reunion. The man with an axe beside Rex in the final guild scene is a qualified continuity join; it does not create a new named actor or prove every nearby male line belongs to Terry (T01–T03/T07–T08).

His initial correction of Rex is based on what he has heard: every Crimson Demon supposedly has the advanced occupational and magical expertise described. When both girls deny upper magic, he is puzzled rather than omniscient. The literal answer leaves other spells possible. His account is a fallible piece of information, and neither Rex's dismissal nor later actual skill makes every universal part of the rumor true. The group admiration after Yunyun casts includes Terry, but the exact anonymous sentence about having said so earlier is not mechanically assigned to him (T01).

He performs logistical work that combat-only retelling would lose. Terry holds the reins while the horse pulls the large cage. This establishes driving in this encounter, not ownership of the horse, payment for all equipment, a universal provider role or a complete occupation. The collective capture plan cannot tell us who conceived, financed and assented to every term. He remains a choosing participant with unknown private reasons, not a resource conjured by Rex's leadership (T02).

His clearest protective action is directly narrated. While Rex and Sophie attack the goblins, Terry stays near the mages with his axe. After Rex calls the direction of danger, the beginner killer launches toward Yunyun and Terry steps into its path. He faces it long enough for her to cast Sleep. The warning, interception, time bought, spell and capture are separate contributions. Neither Terry alone nor the spell's finisher owns the entire success. His shouted challenge belongs to this tense confrontation, not a guarantee that he never fears anything (T03).

Hoost later overwhelms all three suddenly before they can ready weapons. Megumin cannot track the sequence. The unresponsive crew's transportation is not a death scene, and later medical appraisals must keep their sources and timing. Terry returns with the others after limited reported healing; he and Sophie move forward in place of the wounded Rex and shield Yunyun. Their collapse is partly inferred from sound, and Terry's later unconsciousness is explicit. Defeat against Hoost does not retrospectively undo protection against the other creature, nor does continued participation prove the absence of injury (T04–T05).

On regaining consciousness he tells Yunyun not to worry about them. That statement is his own encouragement, separate from Rex's request to wait and Sophie's more explicit instruction to finish the opponent. It precedes recognition of Yunyun's paralysis, so it is not a fully informed choice to be left helpless against a mobile foe. A reassurance is not proof of safety, recovery or permission for all subsequent costs. His wish to keep the operation going is intelligible without importing a universal self-sacrifice code (T06).

Terry is later visibly prepared for travel, then named at the capital reunion. Familiar smiles and surprise at Megumin's nonrecognition supply bounded social evidence. Rex's assertions about the party's status or earlier invitation do not independently disclose Terry's every ambition or membership decision. A collective move can be observed while its exact negotiations, earnings and division of credit remain unknown (T07–T08).

His small voice sample includes a hearsay explanation, uncertainty when it is challenged, a tense shout during actual defense and a weakened reassurance. These functions differ from the generic label of calm support. The male recruitment sentences around the capture remain collectively framed where speaker attribution is not explicit. Source Japanese forms and an English paraphrase cannot establish complete acoustic style or native-human reconstruction adequacy.

**Disposition and gaps.** Terry receives a scoped Tier1 profile for distinct information use, transport, protection, finite injury and companionship. The evidence does not yet isolate a repeated personal decision mechanism beyond task, role and strong situational cues. Most biography, private preferences, moral limits, independent work and relations outside this party remain unknown. Further specific choices can earn a monograph or model; neither supporting status nor the current absence of a model fixes his future ceiling. No individual hypothetical battery or accuracy claim is added now.
