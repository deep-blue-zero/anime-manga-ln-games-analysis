KonoSuba repeatedly gives someone a workable reason, a real ability and an incomplete account of what success will do. The resulting joke often depends on the ability working. Kazuma can remove the golem’s head; the head is heavy. Aqua can purify contaminated water; purification also changes the product people wanted. Megumin can supply extraordinary destructive power; the party must arrange transport, mana and appropriate targets around her chosen specialization. Darkness can protect people; enjoyment of some dangers does not make her invulnerable or make every humiliation welcome. Comedy emerges from the interaction of these truths, rather than from a world in which everyone is simply incapable.

## 1. A comic action has several kinds of result

The early golem experiment supplies a compact model of the series’ causal construction. Kazuma explicitly imports an RPG theft tactic, announces useful skill application and successfully steals the head. The golem stops. Only then does the stolen object crush his hand against the ground. His tactic did not fail at the level he modeled; his model omitted the body receiving the object. Aqua’s examination says there is not even a crack, contrary to his claim of fracture. The injury complaint and humiliation are real scene evidence; a broken arm is not. The later theft of the burning coronatite repeats the omission with temperature rather than weight. Wiz cools the object and Aqua heals him. These are complementary repair contributions, not evidence that Kazuma alone performed the successful operation. (MAIN02 C5 P430–452, P510–527.)

Several causal planes therefore need to remain visible: the desired immediate effect, the conditions enabling it, its material side effects, the social distribution of costs and the actor’s subsequent account. A head theft can be technically correct and physically foolish. A successful battle can leave a property bill. A true divine identity can be pragmatically unbelievable. A rescue can preserve someone’s life while violating another boundary. Describing only the final victory loses the mechanism; describing only the punchline loses the competence on which it depends.

This construction appears early in heroic framing itself. Kazuma moves before deliberation when he thinks a schoolgirl faces a large approaching vehicle. He subsequently asks whether she survived and treats that question as important. Aqua then reports that the vehicle was a slow tractor, that the girl would otherwise have been safe and that his own death came from shock. The source distinguishes his perceived danger and action from her later account. The undercut destroys his preferred heroic résumé; it does not retroactively establish that his intervention lacked a protective intention. Nor is the afterlife dialogue an independently inspected replay of the entire earthly incident. (MAIN01 Pr P24–60.)

The genre contract is similarly revised rather than abolished. Classes, skills, vulnerabilities, money, property and religious powers have effects sufficiently consistent for expectations to be tested. A joke can expose the incompleteness of “this is like a game” while preserving a useful game-derived hypothesis. The system needs enough stability for a successful exploit to surprise someone with its omitted consequence.

## 2. What each person is trying to preserve

The party’s incompatibility is partly a conflict among valued outcomes. Kazuma often wants a low-cost, low-risk route to comfort and recognition. Aqua values immediate pleasure, company, divine standing and personally meaningful service. Megumin values an aesthetic and specialist identity that ordinary efficiency measures cannot exhaust. Darkness values protection, useful membership and particular forms of exposure or punishment. These are recurring pressures, not four sufficient explanations for every choice. Their relative priority changes with actual knowledge, resource access, people present and what kind of harm or reward becomes concrete.

Megumin’s first account is unusually explicit. She does not merely report inability to diversify. She says Explosion alone is what she likes and loves while conceding the practical benefit of ordinary spells. Her commitment makes sense under her preference, even where it imposes costs on companions. That is different from saying every resulting choice is reasonable or morally harmless. The specialization also supports real technical growth, social pride, gratitude, guilt and identity-threatening reconsideration. (MAIN01 C1 P461–482; later MAIN16 C3 P312–324.)

Darkness’s recruitment similarly turns a deterrent into an attraction. Kazuma warns of bad treatment and dangerous service; she asks to be used as a shield and welcomes what he offers as reasons to refuse. Yet a prospective danger preference is not a complete consent model. She can welcome bodily risk, object to an unwanted name, dread exclusion and become sincerely embarrassed by an overheard expression of affection. The point of the reversal is that the same proposition has different value for the interlocutors. It is not that all propositions concerning humiliation have one value for her. (MAIN01 C1 P585–607; MAIN03 epilogue; MAIN16 C4 P23–40.)

Aqua’s desire for recognition can also select opposite outcomes. Divine rivalry disrupts a military delay in V09, whereas the repair-captain title rewards useful work she knows how to do. At the wall she gives practical instructions, dries water, accepts Darkness’s command structure and enjoys being called captain. Recognition is not inherently distracting. The causal question is what conduct the recognition makes worth continuing. The later failed service-language tendency sharpens this distinction: successful service and pleased role ownership do not guarantee that subsequent utterances become practical directives. (MAIN09 F P48–100; MAIN17 C1 P379–405.)

Kazuma’s pursuit of ease is equally poor as a universal refusal rule. Concrete entrusted help can become worth preparation, dirty tactics, personal expense or risk. Some apparent reluctance also asks for a feasible route rather than the disappearance of care. The costlier examples do not reveal a secretly pure hero whose complaints can be ignored. His favorable account of himself, appetite for prestige and capacity to exploit others remain behaviorally consequential. The comic character is maintained by this coexistence, not resolved by assigning every generous act an entirely selfish hidden cause or every selfish act a secretly benevolent purpose.

## 3. Coordination is an achievement with a price

The early party is funny because capabilities are extreme and costs are unevenly visible. One member can solve the immediate magical problem while another must supply positioning, timing, mana, transport, material repair or institutional cover. A final strike does not measure total contribution. Nor does Kazuma’s dense first-person tactical account prove that every success requires him, every member is always necessary or outside experts merely duplicate the party.

The [Competence Ledger](../03%20Longitudinal%20Ledgers/KONOSUBA_COMPETENCE_CONTEXT_LEDGER.md) and [Decision/Error Ledger](../03%20Longitudinal%20Ledgers/KONOSUBA_DECISION_ERROR_LEDGER.md) preserve these distinctions. In the mature record, readiness, access and coordination differ from possession of an ability. Armor can prevent a desired action or become too valued to risk. A spell can have the right target but insufficient resources. A newly learned method does not explain a boast made before it was learned. An offer, a preparation, a completed cast and a report of a result are different evidence.

V09 provides both successful and broken coordination. Aqua’s repair changes the strategic value of fortification. In another encounter Aqua, Darkness and Kazuma restore complementary roles, only for Megumin’s desire for conspicuous victory to interrupt the arrangement. Serious investment is not synchronized across the ensemble. The scene would lose its force if the whole party entered a single “competent mode” together. Darkness’s actual interposition and finite injuries also matter: her protection is not a joke-shaped absence of harm. (MAIN09 C2 P220–271; C3 P193–272; F P48–110.)

The final books retain outside capacities rather than graduating into all-purpose self-sufficiency. Yunyun’s transport and offensive phases, Mitsurugi’s combat, Wiz’s and Vanir’s distinct acts, and Eris’s information and reconstruction constrain the central party’s achievement. For example, Wiz kills and reanimates the dragon in V16; Vanir’s repair of his own arm is not the same event. In V17 a severely injured transported Mitsurugi does not receive a demonstrated treatment merely because a healer is present later. Comedy about the party must not silently fill that recovery gap. (MAIN16 C2 P94, P117–123, P214; MAIN17 F P747–751 and the accepted receiving qualifications.)

Successful coordination also has ordinary foundations. Repeated carrying, gift making, shared meals, games, baths, repairs and grievance routines establish knowledge of what another person can bear, wants or habitually does. These are not inferior evidence because they lack a boss fight. The household makes the eventual danger personally consequential and supplies practiced scripts for both help and exploitation.

## 4. Reversal depends on audience, status and institutions

A truth can fail because the speaker’s accumulated presentation has made it incredible. Aqua’s actual divinity repeatedly encounters the reputation earned by her behavior. This does not make her identity false or every audience irrational. It creates a conflict between proposition, source credibility and expected demeanor. Conversely, high-status or solemn language can make a weak account momentarily persuasive.

Courts, noble houses, churches, tourism and commerce extend this mechanism beyond private insult. The V03 truth device rejects flattering self-description while leaving narrower factual denials intact. It is not a complete moral oracle. V10’s “where” versus “which hand” wager exploits a restriction the display invites but the first question does not assert. The later corrected wager changes the practical agreement. A technically true statement can therefore be misleading without the source claiming language itself is meaningless. (MAIN03 C1 P219–265; MAIN10 C4 P181, P263–269 and C5 P459–464.)

Institutions supply both effective limits and exploitable performances. Police can make an ownership claim practically effective against a stronger occupant. Religious ritual can serve care, factional rivalry or personal retaliation. A noble title can secure access while damaging the bearer’s preferred peer identity. Tourist commodities make conventional fantasy-race behavior a performed commercial product. These jokes require specific incentives and procedures; they do not establish that all institutions are fraudulent or all social obligations arbitrary.

Money is especially useful because it carries the joke beyond the scene. Victory can generate debt; a gift’s cost can diverge from its relational value; a commercial success can change the desire to adventure. In V07 the wedding payment is **two billion Eris**, not twenty billion: MAIN07 C5 P475 says 二十億, consistent with two thousand coins valued at one million each. Older English analytical amounts remain historical and require this attributable correction. The magnitude is not merely decorative: Kazuma mobilizes resources that supported his planned comfortable future. His subsequent denial of being deeply attached does not erase the expense, while the expense does not prove a particular romantic contract.

The wedding’s coercive purchase-and-body-payment language remains ethically relevant. Darkness’s later pleasure or relief is neither retrospective consent to every preceding act nor evidence that ownership language accurately describes a legitimate relationship. The narrative can produce bathos through that mismatch without requiring the analyst to certify the most flattering account of the rescuer’s intention. (MAIN07 C5 P458–494.)

## 5. The corrector role keeps changing owners

Calling Kazuma the permanent straight man and the others permanent fools describes a frequent arrangement but misses much of the system. Aqua can correctly diagnose the bodily consequence of his exploit and ask whether the clever planner is actually foolish. Darkness can expose a false romantic script or assert whose discomfort matters. Megumin can see through a borrowed argument, yet also abandon a practical role for the specialist reward she values. Supporting characters can be sincerely competent, socially awkward, deceived or corrective in different exchanges.

The useful unit is the sequence: who establishes an expectation, who accepts it, who supplies contrary evidence and who pays for the correction? Kazuma’s “as planned” immediately before the golem head falls makes him both expectation setter and victim. Aqua’s grand title can be absurd in one turn and an accurate resource description in the next. Megumin’s familiar specialist ritual can be both a genuine shared activity and a transport burden. Darkness can be protective before attaching private enjoyment to the event. These temporal distinctions prevent a stock-role label from substituting for an explanation.

Ensemble humor also distributes knowledge. The reader can know a motive another speaker does not, or share Kazuma’s wrong interpretation until a later referent appears. The V13 courtship/job misunderstandings depend on exact recipients and omitted referents, not on an analyst declaring that all the women are in love or all the men oblivious. Vanir’s carefully bounded offers exploit listener inference and his own reward in humiliation. Such exchanges remain causal social acts even when their timing is amplified. (MAIN13 C3 P222–318, P394–452; F P118–141, P266–308, P345–360, P420–425.)

Repeated mechanisms consequently change meaning. A familiar insult after rescue can restore the household’s interactional pattern without annulling the rescue. A recurring catchphrase after a threatened loss of identity can express regained commitment. A second trap mistake is not automatically the first incident copied: what was known, what was detected and why the temptation prevailed must be separately located. Familiarity makes expectations available; variation determines the inference.

## 6. The narrator both reveals and manufactures incongruity

Kazuma supplies rapid causal explanation, game comparisons, bodily attention, hostile labels and elaborate self-defense. This makes him an excellent vehicle for revealing a contradiction and a poor universal authority on another person’s worth. “Useless” can compress a real failure while excluding the same person’s demonstrated domain competence. A narrated look can be an inference about someone’s thought. A later claim of having planned an outcome can be a reconstruction rather than contemporaneous motive evidence.

The [Narrator Ledger](../03%20Longitudinal%20Ledgers/KONOSUBA_KAZUMA_NARRATOR_LEDGER.md) therefore operates at the level of claims. The early theft experiment confirms his local skill model and disproves its completeness. The V03 bell qualifies self-description without invalidating everything he observes. V11 lets the reader compare the erased week with his later invented explanation. V17’s actual living heavenly access supports the feasibility of a retrieval method; it does not independently verify that his earlier departure was motivated by the prior intention he later claims. Repeated assertion by the same person is not a second source of intention. (MAIN17 F P699; Ep P54–60.)

Hedging also matters inside action comedy. In V17, Darkness’s sword is explicitly broken halfway, while a large scythe lies beside it at the blood pool where Kazuma was killed; the suggested Aqua retaliation is Kazuma’s inference, and a demonstrated death or bodily remains of the reaper is not supplied. Untagged cries cannot be assigned to a convenient speaker just because another passage later names someone persuading Megumin. The joke often benefits from uncertainty, but uncertainty does not authorize the analyst to complete the scene. (MAIN17 C3 P423–447, especially P427, P437–441.)

Alternative narrators and embedded reports constrain access further. Megumin’s V09 epilogue gives her affectionate uncertainty about whether a couple relation has been confirmed. Aqua’s represented version of Kazuma’s speech is not a new utterance by him. Eris’s final narration has its own information and appraisals. A mature comic analysis tracks the source of a proposition before deciding what the proposition proves.

## 7. Seriousness changes priorities without making the comedy unreal

The most informative serious moments do not uniformly make speech softer, conduct kinder or the group more sensible. A serious role can intensify theatricality, as Yunyun’s communal duty or Megumin’s “bad wizard” rescue does. Aqua’s sacred commitment can sustain painful labor, while a serious divine-status contest can worsen retaliation. Kazuma can respond to a credible lethal threat by retreating. Darkness can protect others while enjoying a dimension of the danger. Priority and accepted cost identify seriousness more reliably than solemn vocabulary.

The series also separates permission to act, capacity, actual action and aftermath. Kazuma asks Megumin what she wants before he knows her full history with Wolbach. Her technical mastery later makes the decisive act possible. Neither the tactical result nor the return of teasing resolves the emotional debt she names afterward. His awkward self-disclosure can help without proving that his consoling interpretation is the historical truth about Wolbach’s motive. (MAIN09 F P412–427, P473–484, P534–551, P752–815.)

The distinction becomes essential around bodily and sexual boundaries. In V12 Darkness’s imposed kiss precedes later restraint, Kazuma’s stop requests, his recognition of a free hand, his instruction to restrain it and his ensuing coaching. Initial imposition and later active participation are both actual parts of the sequence. Bodily arousal is not consent; later participation cannot consent retroactively; initial coercion cannot erase later exercised agency. No intercourse is established by the scene. This synthesis receives the current source-located qualification rather than preserving an exculpatory shorthand as the whole account. (MAIN12 C3 P434–537, particularly P473–477.)

Similarly, Kazuma’s V05 decision preserves a specialty Megumin deeply values while going **against her expressed intention** about the allocation. F P169–171 asks for advanced magic; P219 says めぐみんの意に反し before his Explosion investment. Her later joy does not turn the earlier operation into an agreed build decision. Care, understanding and paternalistic override can coexist. The comic restoration of an identity does not settle who had authority to decide.

The retained Gen03 K04 falsification is a particularly clear guard against benevolent reconstruction. Aqua is actually crying before Kazuma’s polite taunt at MAIN11 C2 P446. He later relents, but the later relief cannot supply an earlier brake that failed. New conditions may be proposed for a future model; they cannot be inserted into the retired forecast to rescue it. More generally, laughter after danger, resurrection after death or a successful apology does not remove the earlier choice from the evidence.

## 8. Literalness and language dependence are different questions

H1–H4 concerns what a comic passage warrants as behavior or world evidence. H1 supports direct acts, motives or consequences in their context. H2 retains a disposition while bounding amplified magnitude. H3 identifies a gag-contingent inference that has weak independent support as a durable trait. H4 marks actual nonliteral license. These are not grades of literary importance or a rule that a spectacular event must be metaphorical. Source-present resurrection, magic and material injury can be literal world events while unavailable in a realistic transfer.

A single sequence can require several dispositions. Darkness’s expressed objection is H1 even if the surrounding chase is H2 in intensity. Kazuma’s future boast is a real boast, not evidence that the future happened. A rhetorical imagined national program need not become actual political history. Where an older analysis attached H4 broadly to survivability or fantasy coincidence, the mature synthesis must ask whether the native scene actually treats the event as unreal; it cannot replace that question with “this would be impossible on Earth.”

L0–L3 asks a different question: what the principal joke needs from wording, pragmatics or Japanese form. A literal act may contain a form-dependent pun. A culturally unfamiliar institution may support a joke through ordinary equivalents. Structural portability does not establish psychological transferability: a repeated magical injury can remain a portable narrative joke while requiring major revisions to a realistic character’s risk calculation. Nor does L0–L2 mean the Japanese delivery is dispensable.

The completed Gen05 census is strong but bounded evidence for portability. Its **354 whole-premise units** comprise 175 V16 and 179 V17 units; **351 are clear L0–L2, one clear L3 and two unresolved**, giving a conservative portable range of **351–353/354**. All **81 soft-touched units** remain in the scored population. The diagnostic removal leaves 270–272/273; it is not the preferred denominator. The old Gen03 N354 and Gen04 N496 are different pools under different freezes and cannot be averaged with this one into a series-wide percentage. The 682 original discovery descriptions are provenance, not 682 separately scored jokes.

The exceptional cases remain substantive. V16 U054 turns 頭が足りない from apparent low intelligence into a physically absent rear skull. V16 U083 combines the written 行く/逝く opposition with independently portable malicious pet labels and hospitality in hell. V17 U145 combines the cute texture of ちゅんちゅん丸 with a portable naming nonanswer used to provoke an enemy. Both unresolved cases preserve rival whole-premise weightings. No new classification or translation experiment is performed here.

## 9. A failed language tendency is part of the humor model

Gen05 J01’s broad service-to-practical-directive tendency is **FALSIFIED at the V16–V17 checkpoint**, through two distinct fully eligible service opposites in V17. Actual support preparation precedes Aqua’s enjoyment; her performed buff and new anti-wraith assignment precede her torment-focused reply. In the separate reconstruction/remediation event, her practical directions precede the owned procedure and Eris’s clothing need; Aqua’s later replies give an excuse and refusal. The conduct is service, but the specified speech direction is not supplied. No fresh verbal acceptance or completed new remedy is required to keep a continuing provider role eligible. (PV17-017, C1 P379–405; PV17-079, F P621–635.)

Source identities and ordering have HIGH confidence. Primary pragmatic function has **MODERATE** confidence: a narrow procedural endpoint, an indirect appeal or management of another person’s exposure remains a real alternative, not a new eligibility floor. The failure does not assert that Aqua never gives useful instructions, or that all nine role/register branches fail. It rejects the frozen broad tendency under its actual repeated-opposite criterion. Anna’s departure complaint remains mixed because it can indirectly ask that the story be shortened. Kazuma’s wait command to the returning king remains mixed/compatible because delaying return can itself protect the separation plan. These are not recruited to manufacture a second failure.

This is a consequential refinement for humor. A person can remain responsible, perform the useful act, enjoy the role and speak chiefly about the social or bodily situation. The narrator and interlocutors can make the service comic precisely through that divergence. Practical action, practical utterance, role pride and ethical judgment need distinct variables. Replacing the failed prediction with “roles always determine speech” would remove the content that made it testable.

The compound K03 history gives a parallel warning. V16’s explicit sufficient-points/return proposal is a strong completion candidate; no invented numerical quota can exclude it. Continued descent follows an offered gear opportunity, leaving independently gratifying reward versus instrumental utility genuinely uncertain. V17 Ep P40 is an actual post-completion, praise-activated harem declaration and exposure, not a harmless absence of implementation. Prior scene-specific relational warnings matter without becoming universal exemptions. The formal P and older Gen04 local falsifications retain their own scopes. A mature account explains the ambiguity instead of making every reward either automatically corrupting or automatically task-supporting.

## 10. What can transfer, and what must remain constrained

The most reusable mechanism is a relation among valued purposes, perceived opportunities, available capacities and consequences distributed across systems. It can support new low-stakes scenes: a task solved at another person’s inconvenience, a truthful answer with misleading scope, a title that rewards the right work, a gift whose affectionate and patronizing functions coexist, or a familiar correction that restores contact. Such scenes need not repeat a canonical catastrophe or quote a catchphrase to feel continuous with the source.

Realistic transfer must change risk where healing, resurrection, magical protection or plentiful resources are absent. It must preserve the character’s actual knowledge of those changes. Transfer that simply deletes injury while retaining the same coercive or destructive choice sanitizes conduct; transfer that imports every comic magnitude makes the model grotesquely uncalibrated. Conversely, canonical mode must not erase actual spells or treat genuine specialist competence as delusion merely to make the cast ordinary.

Ordinary continuation should preserve neutral exchanges, practical ability and particular affiliation. Aqua can explain work; Megumin can budget and ask a direct question; Darkness can serve tea, protect and object; Kazuma can manage a small problem without a grand exploit. The less spectacular evidence stops the analyst from treating each conversational turn as an audition for the running gag. The comic system is richer because a familiar person can be genuinely helpful before, during or after being exasperating.
