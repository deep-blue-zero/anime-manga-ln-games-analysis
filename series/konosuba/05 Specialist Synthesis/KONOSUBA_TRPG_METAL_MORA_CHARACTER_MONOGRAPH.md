# メタルモーラー: a controlled weapon and an autonomous threat

**メタルモーラー / Metal Mōrā** is the individually designated underground tank in the Advanced supplied scenario, native **TRPG-ADV-S298–311**, printed **296–309**. The enemy description explicitly calls it an autonomous vehicle developed in Noize (S309). This is a source-defined artificial actor with a body, target processing, movement, spoken announcements and changing control conditions. It is not automatically the Mobile Fortress Destroyer or every Noize machine.

Its concentrated sequence supports a scoped Tier2 study of agency and constraints. Artificial status sets no tier ceiling. Conversely, a detailed machine should not receive invented human emotions, family, ethics or inner speech merely to fill a character checklist. The supplied states are enough for substantial analysis but do not earn a personality generator or reconstruction model. The scenario remains unplayed.

## Object history and separate control states

The synopsis says Boren excavated and repaired the abandoned tank. Its opening attack devastates a small Belzerg city in the authored background (S298–300). The reference description supplies Noize provenance and autonomous operation, while Boren claims inheritance and mastery. Those statements do not identify an individual engineer, reveal a creator's private motive or establish that his self-description is a legal transfer of ownership.

The important distinction is between the externally protected tank used under its control device and the autonomous threat after that device is destroyed. The control room is an internal object with its own HP and defenses. Its loss disables the external invincibility barrier, triggers emergency ejection and leaves the tank without Boren's effective control (S301, S307–308). The same tank persists across these states; the transition does not instantiate a second personality or prove a metaphysical identity change.

## Body, movement and access

The tank's drill permits underground travel and attacks. Its internal dungeon contains a prison, junction, supply store, guest room, passage and control room. These schematic connections are useful routes, not a complete engineering plan or a survey of every occupied compartment (S302–304). Omitted sleeping spaces may be elaborated by a GM; no additional fixed occupants follow simply from that permission.

The prison and security devices are parts of the supplied environment. They are not evidence that the tank itself consciously designed each trap or negotiated with every guard. Boren, subordinate soldiers, golems, the control device and the vehicle have separately specified functions. An HP-bearing device is not automatically a second sentient companion, while an autonomous vehicle is not reduced to Boren's mind just because he operates it.

After device destruction the machine announces the emergency escape mechanism, then detects life reactions ahead and declares external enemies will be eliminated (S300, S308). These lines support functional signaling and target classification. They do not establish its usual conversational pronoun, ability to explain motives, comprehension of every Japanese utterance or an emotional hatred of the people it attacks.

Detection is also source-bounded. The text gives the relevant lifeform response in this scene, not unlimited range, infallible identification or universal awareness of what occurred inside the dungeon. ## Power with explicit weaknesses

The opening uses Forced Defeat. The scenario instructs advance disclosure of that premise to players, and separately makes later identification easier because of the cheat's drawback (S299–302). That design cannot become a completed experiment showing that every possible character loses to the machine. It prescribes this opening.

The external barrier is generated internally and can be removed through device destruction. After removal, the scene gives both defenses as zero under the drawback, despite the general card's displayed defenses. The card's HP500 likewise coexists with the encounter's HP equal to100 times PC count; reduced-party rules remove specified actions/area behavior (S308–309). These are source-specific general and encounter contexts. No exhaustive rule reconciliation or numerical balance certification is claimed.

The card names a power-unit theft vulnerability alongside anti-theft protection, and lists possible salvage. This is a mechanical opportunity with an obstacle, not a witnessed theft or guaranteed drop. The drill, burrowing and body size describe capacities. No performed damage total or universal invulnerability follows from the preview's superlatives.

## Boren's failed mastery and other agents

In the exterior branch the tank defeats surviving Boren and guards, and a surviving Boren's attack order is answered by a drill blow from behind (S308, S310). The sequence undermines his claim to command it after control loss. The tank's action does not become an intentional joke, an ethical rejection of his coercion or a personal betrayal without evidence of those appraisals.

Surviving control-room golems retain damage and join the exterior battle. The female NPC party does not participate in round combat and cheers from hiding; this does not imply that the tank kills them. Individual guard continuity and the roster depend on earlier choices. The scenario's player-count formulas are not a global count of separate artificial or biological persons.

After successful defeat the tank becomes immobile wreckage and other enemies stop moving (S310). This gives a supplied operational dependency. It does not explain every golem's full control architecture or prove that all prior creatures cease to exist. No actual wreckage, party success or paid bounty is produced by reading the ending.
