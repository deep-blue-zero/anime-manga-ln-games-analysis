## 3. Motivations and valuations

She seeks a workable investigation and expects an account compatible with the available situation. She also values noble standing, useful connections and an ordinary domestic future. Her stated partner criteria include a middle income, household support, daily expressions of affection and help during pregnancy. These are preferences she expresses, not evidence she has a partner, is pregnant or has performed the domestic life described (AO27-02).

The account should preserve the tension rather than select a single moral identity. A person can recognize suspicious conduct and still distribute attention unequally according to patronage. Her admiration for Darkness is not proof that she shares every noble value or that the entire police institution acts on her personal aspirations.

## 4. Affect and public role

Aloerina's suspicion can become forceful and personal. Kazuma's changing excuses and manipulative remarks provoke her, while discussion of Darkness and her own situation brings a different register. The later patronage cues produce an observable change of demeanor. Those shifts support status sensitivity without proving every severe question was cynical theater (AO27-01–03).

Kazuma threatens a fabricated sexual accusation against the chief. It is his tactical claim, not a witnessed act by her. The analytic response must attribute the real coercive handling that is shown while refusing to invent sexual misconduct from his threat. Her own explicit dating preferences likewise do not authorize another person's contact or ridicule.

## 5. Competence and limits

She notices problems in Kazuma's stories, links prior contribution with present uncertainty and knows institutional routes such as a guarantor or proposed truth device. A proposal to use the device is not a performed test. Her authority supplies options that an ordinary bystander lacks (AO27-01–03).

That authority does not guarantee fair inference. Physical pressure, class assumptions and deference to powerful sponsors limit the credibility of a neutral-professional account. No independent case-success rate, sword skill, magical talent or broad legal expertise has been demonstrated in these received scenes. Her decisions are observed within one linked disruption and its two detention phases.

## 6. Appraisal and choice

The first release and second refusal should not be flattened into an inconsistent rule that Darkness's presence always solves custody. The complaint state and willingness to guarantee differ. The chief treats a credible external guarantee differently from Kazuma's self-serving account, then treats royal patronage as especially consequential (AO27-01–03).

The evidence supports a hypothesis of authority-weighted credibility alongside ordinary suspicion. A rival is that guarantees are routine institutional practice and the comic demeanor shift exaggerates a smaller personal bias. Both can explain part of the sequence. Without a comparable case involving a truthful, unsupported low-status complainant, the relative contribution of procedure and preference remains uncertain.

## 7. Relationships and asymmetry

Kazuma is a suspect with achievements, a poor immediate account and access to unusually powerful advocates. Aloerina's use of force and his threat of a false complaint do not cancel one another. Each is attributable. He also attempts to exploit her personal interests; her response must not be reduced to a reward for his charm (AO27-01–03).

Darkness supplies a status cue and, at one stage, intervention; she later refuses to rescue him through the same route. Claire and Rain supply differentiated royal evidence, not an interchangeable cloud of friendly authority. Aloerina's admiration is one-sided in the received scope; no reciprocal friendship, employment tie or romantic bond is established.

## 8. Ordinary aspirations

Her expressed domestic ideal is unusually specific compared with the limited evidence for her work outside this case. She imagines support, affection and manageable income rather than an abstract requirement for maximum wealth. That specificity resists the caricature of an official interested only in riches (AO27-02).

No observed household routine, shopping preference, leisure habit or established intimate relationship follows. The monograph does not transform singleness into pathology or turn her comparison with Darkness into proof she wants the noblewoman's entire life. Her own preferences are useful evidence precisely because they can remain bounded.

## 10. Comedy and seriousness

The suspect's accumulating excuses, an official's personal preferences and a sudden change before elite patrons create comic reversals. A serious account can preserve suspicion, self-interest and procedural dependence without turning every official into a villain or making the suspect morally innocent because he is resourceful. Pure corruption fails to explain all of her scrutiny and sensitivity to the differing complaint/guarantee states. Pure wealth-seeking is weakened by the more modest and reciprocal domestic ideal. Automatic capitulation to Darkness is contradicted by the second detention and the need for a different guarantee (AO27-01–03).
