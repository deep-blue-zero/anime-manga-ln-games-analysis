# Arnes: a loyal pursuer whose care, coercion and errors coexist — current qualified interpretation

This separately authored current version adopts the substantial actor-specific argument at its original C18 source boundary, while retaining the original monograph, tested model and private frozen foundations exactly. It distinguishes the current interpretation from its preserved experiment history and later dependent diagnostics. It adds no primary-source admission, independent observation, model rule, first response or capability grade.

## 1. Identity and narrative position

Arnes (アーネス) explicitly self-names as a loyal servant in EXP02 U023 P15–16 and identifies the creature on Megumin's belly as her intended master at P37. Megumin's initial fantasy that the visitor has come to serve her is corrected by Arnes herself. The direct name and intended-recipient exchange establish identity. Red hair and yellow eyes alone do not: the separately named speaking Wolbach shares those descriptive features. Arnes, Wolbach, Chomusuke, Hoost and the unnamed subordinates in later conversation require distinct subjects and access (A01–A03).

This substantial monograph supports a provisional Tier3 model with a separate evidence index and rule inventory. The reason is a varied extended sequence of decisions, not a required number of appearances. Negotiation, payment, temporary postponement, reinterpretation of resistance, rescue, retreat, deliberate harassment, hostage leverage, mistaken inference and attempted withdrawal all have analytical responsibility. A short span in series chronology does not impose a ceiling; these observations also do not make her complete inner life known.

The shared provenance records 3,275 known BASE context slots across fifteen members/four local native objects. It includes Sena, other characters, blank paragraphs, repeated accounts and contextual scenes. It is not a count of Arnes's independent choices or a new whole-source/ruby/image/admission pass. The twenty-six Arnes evidence rows identify actual source windows and unequal access. No native-human adequacy, blind test, independent accuracy or confidence increase is claimed.

## 2. Evidence chronology and access

The first visit begins ceremonially. Arnes bows, offers service and describes a freed master whose memory may not yet have returned. When Megumin misconstrues the intended subject, Arnes corrects her. She thanks the household for protection, invokes the master's will, continues asking after the creature turns away, objects to its new name and offers her available300kEris. Chomusuke's physical resistance leads her to postpone until the next day; she actually leaves a silver-filled bag (A01–A03).

On return, she challenges the foster-owner framing, discourages inquiry and accepts the creature from Yunyun. Chomusuke struggles free and returns. Arnes describes resistance as lost-memory wariness and tries again. Horn exposure changes the conversation from concealed identity and politeness to conditional threats. Yunyun fires a warning near her; Megumin throws the creature, and Arnes slides to catch it. A Crimson shot wounds her, followed by a false accusation about earlier nightly Explosions. She releases Chomusuke under pressure and flees. The earlier money remains with Megumin (A04–A09).

At Arcanretia she returns to the same objective, initially restraining action because the master likes Megumin, but attacks an interrupting Zesta. Reflection, a near miss, his stated rank and Cecily's presence reverse her confident stance. She retreats and is pursued. Zesta later says he deliberately grazed her with lethal magic because he enjoyed her tears; that is a later report with its own ethical responsibility, not another newly watched Arnes attack (A10–A12).

The caravan sequence changes the geometry. After actual attacks and exhaustion, Arnes kills a horse, claims to have observed the travelers and admits driving monsters toward them. She demands delivery or refund, then says repayment would not stop retrieval. She disables guards and uses the survivors and passengers as hostages against escape. Her correct assessment of Yunyun's depletion coexists with a wrong inference that Megumin cannot cast. She still catches a thrown Chomusuke, loses hold during a distant-power interruption, shows relief when Yunyun catches it and then faces Megumin's completed Explosion. She offers retreat and apologizes for the earlier dismissal; after refusal she attempts another spell and is overtaken (A13–A21).

Later EXP03 appearances of her name are primarily recollection, comparison and attributed claims. Hoost is explicitly a different devil; his own remaining lives and contract account do not automatically describe Arnes. Megumin's statement that a manifested body's destruction need not establish permanent demon death remains an important limit on the outcome (A21–A26).

## 3. Motivations and valuations

Retrieving the being she calls Wolbach-sama is a repeated, stated and enacted priority. It directs travel, deference, money, repeated approaches and concrete rescue. She says she will risk her life in service; her sliding and airborne catches give that attachment practical content. Her loyalty should not be erased merely because her methods become harmful. Conversely, calling an objective loyal does not establish that it serves the creature's present interests.

The strongest tension is between her invocation of the master's will and her response to its visible resistance. She first notices the rejection enough to allow another night, then later explains it away as memory loss and continues. This can be protection as she understands it while also overriding present refusal. The analysis need not choose between total insincerity and perfectly recipient-centered care. Neither her claim of remembered hierarchy nor Megumin's claim that the creature is content supplies complete internal access.

Other goals matter. She wants the paid transaction addressed, resents humiliation, enjoys Megumin's frustrated expression and seeks to prevent another failed encounter. The refund grievance is real as a matter of delivered money without delivery of the creature. It is not the entire later purpose: she says she will retrieve the master even if paid back. No actual refund occurs. Nor is there evidence here that she borrowed the300k; finite available money does not justify inventing personal debt (A03/A09/A14–A15).

## 4. Affect, self-presentation and vulnerability

Arnes moves from formal deference to the master, guarded politeness toward protectors, irritation at naming, shock at rejection, anger at injury, swagger against apparently weaker opponents and fear before credible superior power. Tears during flight, the reaction to the distant divine force and the later blue-faced response to Explosion are observed or narrated bodily expressions. None supplies a diagnosis or a universal cowardice trait.

Some inner readings remain the narrator's. Megumin thinks rejection hurts more than the scratch and interprets the release under Crimson pressure as concern for the master. Those interpretations fit other actions but are still interpretations. Arnes's actual catches and visible relief give stronger behavioral anchors than a single adjective about her shock. Her delight in an opponent's frustration is more directly supported by her own statement and demeanor during the admission of monster herding.

Her confidence is fallible. She calls Zesta a strange old man before his defense makes her revise that estimate, then still underestimates what a human priest can do. She ridicules Megumin's apparent inability, becomes more guarded at a change in demeanor and ultimately proposes withdrawal when confronted with completed high magic. Updating occurs late and unevenly, without making either her previous cruelty or her final danger disappear.

## 5. Competence, resources and finite means

Her capacity is substantial in context: flight, force sufficient to disable equipped adventurers, electrical and fire magic, evasion of reflected fire, and an admitted ability to drive weaker monsters through threatening power. The caravan's actual attacks and depletion complement that admission. She is not merely a loud obstacle whose threats never affect anyone. A horse dies, defenses break and some people are seriously injured (A13–A16).

The limits are equally concrete. Crimson villagers overwhelm her situation; a priest's reflection surprises her; a credible holy near miss prompts fear; divine power interrupts her spell; and an unfamiliar completed Explosion defeats her manifested presence. No amount of later praise for Megumin proves that Arnes had no capability or that every preceding choice was inevitable. Her fear can track a real disparity while her earlier assessment remains wrong.

Available cash is finite and actually transferred, not an unlimited narrative currency. She uses an initial concealed identity, but the horn exposure removes that advantage. She adjusts location, timing, targets and leverage on later attempts. These are meaningful means choices, yet their success is conditional. Observing several constrained non-casts does not reveal all possible spells, and correctly identifying one exhausted defender does not establish that nobody else can act.

## 6. Decision making, learning and mistakes

The repeated-failure sequence supports adaptation rather than a fixed script. She initially negotiates, pays and permits delay. Following refusal and conflict, she seeks opportunities without the same protectors, observes the traveling group, tires its defenses, disables guards and threatens other people to neutralize a creature-as-shield escape plan. Generic tactical learning is a serious rival to a uniquely Arnes-specific mechanism, but the changing actions themselves are attributable.

Her experimental-looking inference is particularly revealing. She says she watched Megumin and drove three attacks to make sure. Repeated non-use then becomes certainty that Megumin cannot cast. Yet the reader has seen constraints on an indiscriminate large spell: nearby people, village, town and crowd. Her observation is real; the generalization is wrong. The model should reproduce that possible failure instead of granting the antagonist the reader's explanation or optimizing her into an ideal investigator (A15/A18).

Her bluff judgment is also bounded. She decides Megumin cannot kill the master and approaches despite a threatened haircut. That does not stop her from catching the creature when it is thrown high. Megumin's subsequent willingness to cast with the creature present is an ugly contrary fact, not evidence that Arnes should automatically have known every extent of her adversary's recklessness. A partly correct reading of one threat does not make her a perfect interpreter of all future behavior.

## 7. Relationships, protection and recipient agency

With Chomusuke, care and possession cannot be collapsed. Arnes bows, pays, catches and visibly relaxes at safety, while repeatedly refusing to treat departure resistance as a settled current preference. Chomusuke's clinging, turning away, struggling, escaping and returning to Yunyun are its actions. The creature is not merely an object passed between speakers who thereby exhaust its interests.

With Megumin, she begins by thanking protection and later combines threatened harm, a genuine payment grievance, retaliation and negotiation. Megumin invents the earlier nocturnal culprit, keeps money, uses the creature as a shield, throws it and is willing to kill it in a proposed Explosion. Arnes's own serious violence remains separate. The account does not have to select one wholly innocent side to preserve these different responsibilities.

With Yunyun, Arnes encounters both protective force and practical intervention. Yunyun warns, shields, offers to advance the refund, resists surrender, stops Megumin's dangerous cast and catches Chomusuke. Those actions are not automatically the expression of Megumin's plan. Nor does Arnes's relief make Yunyun an agreed agent of her retrieval objective.

With Zesta and Cecily, her initial attack and their defense are distinct from their unjust blame and subsequent threats. Later reports of deliberately prolonged fear or further pursuit cannot be ethically erased because the pursued subject is a devil. Hoost shares a master name and is called a colleague in later dialogue, but neither that relation nor his account gives Arnes every thought, contract term or survival resource he has.

## 8. Ordinary life, money and social interaction

The material provides little unpressured recreation, domestic routine or ordinary work outside this pursuit. That gap should stay visible. Money, travel, visiting a door, politeness, waiting, naming and concern about a companion's attachment nevertheless provide ordinary mechanisms within the unusual plot. Her postponement is a specific concession, not a complete patient temperament inferred from one night.

She treats300k as a finite available sum and delivers it before taking the creature. The money becomes Megumin's teleport fare after Arnes flees, a choice justified by Megumin through a self-serving monster-loot analogy. That is not Arnes's consent to a gift or proof that the exchange was fulfilled. The later demand for repayment has a concrete history, but her insistence on retrieval even after hypothetical repayment shows the objective is not settled by financial repair alone.

Her correction of the master's name is socially important. She rejects both the familiar household name and the foster-parent description, preserving a relationship hierarchy that the creature's current conduct does not straightforwardly affirm. This can be modeled as attachment to an interpreted relationship without inventing a full religious doctrine, biological kinship or mind-control contract. Her own claim about discontinued soul services remains attributed world information rather than an established universal rule.

## 9. Japanese voice and situational register

The early voice uses elaborate deference toward 貴方様, 我が主 and ウォルバク様. The grammatical subject of the intended service matters: Megumin appropriates the grandeur, and Arnes corrects her. Later rougher forms, あたし, 貴様, imperatives and taunts mark changed social positioning after exposure and conflict. The same subject can be deferential to one recipient and coercive toward another in a single encounter.

Naming corrections and interrupted speeches do comic work, but an English reconstruction should not consist only of repeated honorifics or villainous laughter. Her eventual apology for calling Megumin all talk is a local revision under threat. It is not a confession to every earlier wrong, a promise of permanent reform or automatic abandonment of the master objective.

The current native recheck is BASE text with rt/rp removed. It does not certify every ruby, full voice plane, delivery choice or native reader's adequacy. English internal hypotheticals can explore perspective and register but do not become a Japanese-language test. A model that sounds smoothly courteous while omitting pressure, anger or errors would be a flattering replacement rather than faithful reconstruction.

## 10. Comedy, serious consequences and ethical fidelity

The false-master entrance, naming dispute, interrupted threats, startled retreat and mistaken skill judgment are comic. Actual paid resources, creature resistance, a killed horse, broken equipment, serious injuries and hostage threats remain real within that same narrative. Humor is not a permit to relabel harmful acts as harmless by default or to treat an eventual defeat as repair for those harmed.

Arnes is also subjected to injustice and cruelty. The earlier Explosion accusation is false, and Zesta's claimed enjoyment of near-lethal grazing has its own responsibility. These facts do not excuse her later herding, disabling and threats. The correct analytical move is attribution of each act and motive, not balancing harms into an acquittal score.

The most severe unperformed acts also need their proper scope. The threat to finish passengers if Megumin flees is not a completed massacre. Arnes's large fireball is interrupted before completion; Megumin's expectation of incineration is an inference. Megumin's threatened haircut is not executed, but her dangerous chant is actually begun and physically stopped. Keeping those distinctions makes the record more serious rather than less.

## 11. Contrary evidence, rivals and possible falsifiers

A generic loyal subordinate explains much of the pursuit and rescue. A generic coercive pursuer explains pressure and hostage leverage; loss aversion explains the refund demand; generic tactical adaptation explains observation and timing. These rivals are substantial. The model earns usefulness by stating where it expects a difference and by preserving uncertainties, not by announcing that any plausible line proves distinctive identification.

Specific contrary facts constrain stronger stories. She notices present resistance enough to postpone, so she does not always ignore it. She later reinterprets that resistance, so her claim to respect will is not a complete consent ethic. She updates after reflection and finally offers withdrawal, so she is not incapable of learning. She makes a consequential non-use-to-inability inference, so she is not an omniscient planner. She catches the creature despite a claimed bluff judgment, so adaptation does not erase an attachment-specific rescue priority.

Future evidence of freely accepting an equally clear current refusal without reinterpretation would challenge a strong retrieval-over-preference rule. Repeated avoidance of the non-use/inability error under comparable information would weaken the present epistemic proposition. A generous act outside the master objective could broaden the account; absence of such a scene is not proof that she cannot have one. Such evidence would require new attributable observations, not additional retellings of this arc or agreeable invented outputs.

## 12. Current interpretation and unresolved questions

Arnes is currently best reconstructed as a strongly attached pursuer who can offer material means, concede a limited delay and act to prevent immediate harm to the being she serves, while overriding its present resistance and harming others to obtain it. Defeat changes her tactics and fear changes her conduct, but neither makes learning complete. Her correct local assessment of one depleted defender sits beside a confident, costly mistake about another person's capability.

The manifested defeat is established at the narrative's stated access. Permanent extinction is not. Megumin explicitly leaves demon death uncertain; Hoost's later remaining-lives and contract statements concern him. Later affectionate or lonely statements by the speaking Wolbach cannot automatically be translated into a complete Arnes biography, a quantified survival mechanism or proof of shared present knowledge.

The separate provisional rules and the [published internal assessment](../06%20Character%20Modeling%20and%20Reconstruction/ARNES_EXPANDED_V1_EVIDENCE/ARNES_INTERNAL_ASSESSMENT_01.json) remain source-known historical diagnostic evidence. The same informed author forms the model, designs inputs, writes first outputs and assesses them. Original model, monograph, rule and first bytes remain preserved; this current argument neither repairs their outputs nor turns a later assessment into an old premise. Conditional plausibility under explicit instructions does not distinguish Arnes from every loyal or coercive pursuer, and it cannot demonstrate permanent consent-respecting reform or cure her source error about non-use.

The current interpretation keeps material payment, rescue, present resistance, hostage harm and fallible appraisal attributable to their own actors. Ordinary contexts, stronger rival discrimination and unresolved master/contract knowledge remain actual evidence gaps. No new rules, firsts, independent accuracy, capability grade or confidence gain follow. The current project entrypoint owns later whole-program progress, curation, evaluation and release.
