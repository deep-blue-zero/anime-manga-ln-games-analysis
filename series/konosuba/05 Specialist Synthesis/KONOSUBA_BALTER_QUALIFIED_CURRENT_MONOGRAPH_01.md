## 1. Identity

Alexei Barnes Balter / アレクセイ・バーネス・バルター is Alderp's son in the named MAIN03 introduction. He helps with territorial administration according to that account. He is neither Darkness's father Ignis nor a generic unnamed noble suitor. Alderp later describes selecting an attractive capable child as a possible body resource; that speech does not settle biological parentage or legal adoption (BA28-01, BA28-03).

## 2. Chronology and access

The interview contains favorable reports, actual concern, an initially unwanted match, changed interest, a technically one-sided duel and his concession. The continuation adds a mistaken romantic interpretation, a false pregnancy and proposed withdrawal. Later assistance to the successor is Kazuma's report. These planes are not multiple independently observed adult-life settings (BA28-01–03).

## 3. Valuations

He explicitly says he came to refuse his father's imposed interview. His interest changes after seeing Darkness's directness, embarrassment and treatment of a supposed servant as an equal. Attraction therefore has stated particular objects; it is not automatic filial obedience or merely a response to rank. His comments do not establish reciprocal attraction (BA28-01).

## 4. Emotion

Concern, envy, admiration and eventual relieved laughter vary across the encounter. During the duel his voice becomes desperate despite technical superiority. The admission of genuine attraction after concession is his own speech. It should not be converted into a durable exclusive love or proof that he understands Darkness's reasons for persisting (BA28-01).

## 5. Competence and capacity

Reports of study, charity and early knighthood are attributed. The duel directly shows quick disarming and repeated accurate hits while Darkness cannot strike him. He concedes because he will not keep hitting, not because she outperforms him technically. Her bruises are real; his initial reluctance does not establish that he never inflicts injury (BA28-01).

## 6. Judgment and revision

His initial refusal to raise a sword against a woman changes after her challenge and his revised interest. Later he reads endurance as strength and Kazuma's wager as confidence while winning, although Kazuma's actual fear of losing contradicts that account. The false pregnancy also changes his response. He can revise, but the information behind revision is fallible (BA28-01–02).

## 7. Relations and boundaries

Alderp's wish and scheme are not Balter's. His initial intention resists the arranged match, and his later offer to tell his father he declined gives Darkness an available exit in speech. No delivered message is watched. His attribution of mutual Kazuma/Darkness affection remains an interpretation rather than a settled relationship fact (BA28-02–03).

## 8. Ordinary life

The available work/study/charity material is mostly report; actual family labor and broader friendship are sparse. His expressed courtship criteria concern character and treatment across rank as well as attractiveness. There is no basis for completing his food, leisure or household preferences from a "good knight" archetype (BA28-01, BA28-03).

## 9. Voice

His printed 僕, hesitant courtesy, explicit reasoning and later urgent protests differ from Alderp's possessive imperatives. The duel confession and quiet admission of attraction show a shift within that register. No audio or accent is supplied. Copying courteous forms without his stated refusal threshold would miss the most informative choice (BA28-01).

## 10. Humor and harm

The technically dominant fighter experiences the encounter as defeat because he cannot endure continued hitting, while the recipient is dissatisfied that it stops. Kazuma understands another meaning behind Darkness's persistence. That mismatch is the joke; it does not erase bruises or justify treating every request for pain as general consent to unrelated treatment (BA28-01).

## 11. Counterevidence and rivals

Pure obedience fails against the initial refusal intention. Perfect empathy fails against the romance/wager errors and his limited understanding of the duel. Universal pacifism fails against actual repeated strikes. Generic chivalry explains part of the reluctance, but his specific attraction and chosen stopping point provide an individual account without yet yielding a robust cross-context rule (BA28-01–02).
