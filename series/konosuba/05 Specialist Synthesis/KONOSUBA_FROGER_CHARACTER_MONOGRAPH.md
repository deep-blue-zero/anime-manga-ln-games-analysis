# Froger: a bid for sovereignty made through a frog kingdom

Status: scoped Tier 2 monograph for フロガー in the Core TRPG edited replay. [Cohort 31 evidence and qualifications](../08%20Evidence%20and%20Indexes/KONOSUBA_TRPG_REPLAY_COHORT31_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md) govern this account. No generative model is accepted: a dense adversarial project does not identify a general decision mechanism across ordinary alternatives.

## Identity and source layers

Froger is the historical English retrieval label for フロガー, the frog-headed researcher and boss. Core S063 calls him a lower army researcher; S064 also gives a joking GM Thousand Spears designation. Neither establishes executive rank in the principal novels. Gilbert's later deliberate executive inflation is checked and not verified by the receptionist (S083). The Advanced Flame Giant Toad says Froger was its former master; it is a surviving subordinate, not Froger returned (ADV S045). Portrait interpretation, diary disclosure, direct enemy dialogue and GM-created details require separate attribution.

## Chronology

The diary/GM backstory makes him a former human adventurer who stole a wish-granting jeweled artifact and asked to become a king. The artifact mishears human words and makes him a frog king (Core S064). He joins the army, trains frogs and plans to replace the ruler (S065). The party discovers the room and diary before the throne encounter. Guards, threatened Hanako and actual combat follow; Chiho's attack defeats him and the GM stages the fall/slip (S067-080). The ending certifies defeat, not permanent death, resurrection or later control of the surviving toad.

## Motives and projected order

The wish and later speeches center on not remaining a human servant, commanding trained frogs and building an alternative order. The project threatens people and livestock. An amusement-park name is supplied by Gilbert and openly adopted by the GM before further atrocity plans are elaborated (S069). The name and plans therefore have a visible coauthoring history. A threatened human-powered attraction is not an already built park, and a planned attack on Axel is not an accomplished destruction.

## Affect

Froger objects to humans entering his educational facility and can dismiss their attempts to frame him as a restorable human. Gilbert's cheat frightens him and cancels the intended guard buff (S068-071). Fear after a mechanically imposed fumble does not measure a stable personality trait or settle the human-autonomy dispute. Temiren's handsome-human appeal fails; the GM also describes the former face as froglike. The source supports local pride, irritation and refusal, with little basis for an emotional history outside this confrontation.

## Competence and constraints

Research, frog training, dungeon design and force organization give substance to the project. The dungeon's tests are designed for frogs and have an unused alternate praise response (S066); the alternative is not an event the party performed. Guards learn to evade after swallowing, and the intended king buff can improve them (S069-070). That buff is actually canceled, Gilbert's attack initially fails to damage him, and later allied attacks overcome him. Competence is neither omnipotence nor mere boasting. A skill or GM ruling does not certify every numeric table in the book.

## Choices and rejected alternatives

He rejects Temiren's suggestion of becoming human again and rejects submission to a human ruler (S069-071). This gives a meaningful local alternative and refusal. It still leaves the transformation's mental effects, other options and stable preference outside the throne scene unknown. The misheard wish explains a magical result, not a psychological inability to state goals clearly in every setting. A global model would overfit the one sovereignty project and the GM's need for a climax opponent.

## Relationships

Froger commands guards and breeds or trains frogs; their improved tactics show a local organized relation. Hanako is a threatened captive on a metal plate with a pot below (S067), not an already killed meal. The party frees the cow and spares a remaining frog (S081). The Advanced survivor calls Froger its former master, but its own later agency does not turn it into the same character. The diary's insult to Aqua and Axis ownership of the artifact are openly added after Chiho's question, prompting Gilbert's renewed hostility (S066). They are ratified replay details with an improvisation history, not an independently fixed source test.

## Ordinary environment

A bedroom, books, desk, portrait and forbidden diary supply more than a battlefield silhouette (S063-064). Gilbert initially objects to invading privacy, while the others stage an accidental reading. The room and slimy bed can reveal a lived environment without authorizing a complete daily schedule, diet, social life or human family. The threatened farm raids are reported by Bo Kujo; the party has not observed every attack or every captive's fate.

## Japanese voice

The native フロガー fixes identity across direct dialogue and reported history. The boss's claims to rule and complaints about intrusion have a different function from the GM's exposition or Gilbert's taunts. The 王-to-frog-king wordplay belongs to the artifact's mishearing; it is not a direct observation of habitual Froger speech. Short pride and refusal patterns can be described, but there is too little neutral conversation for a general Japanese voice reconstruction, and no audio has been used.

## Comedy and serious stakes

A wish for kingship produces frog sovereignty; a throne can be performed like a swivel or pipe chair; players and GM build the villain's park language together. These comic conditions coexist with abduction, threatened cooking and actual damage. The useful analysis keeps the comic authoring mechanism visible while treating the fictional stakes as consequential. Serious transfer would need to reconsider the magical transformation and role-imposed battle conditions before inferring ordinary motives.

## Counterevidence and rivals

He is more than an incompetent punchline: training, defenses and early resistance materially affect the party. He is also less than an omniscient mastermind: the artifact mishears, the party invades, the buff is canceled and he loses. Rank inflation, the later survivor and the improvized insult cannot supply independent repeated observations of the same trait. Rule incentives, GM pacing and one antagonistic goal remain plausible explanations of many choices.

## Limits and disposition

Tier 2 is warranted by the biography, environment, project, alternatives, relationships and detailed defeat. A Tier 3 generative model is withheld because neutral work, sustained cooperation, ordinary resources and response to a credible noncombat opportunity remain too thin and too tied to this one GM-shaped crisis. His permanent fate is unresolved. No model, hypothetical first output, independent-observation count, accuracy grade or capability promotion is created for Froger.
