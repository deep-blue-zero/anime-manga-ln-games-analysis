# Village-peer medicine attribution: report of effect, not medical work

## Current qualification — C20-CR04

The cohort20 Funifura and Dodonko monographs and shared evidence index use the loose English phrase “medicine work” or “medicine-work testimony.” Read that phrase as a report about a potion's apparent effect. It does not establish Dodonko making medicine, gathering its ingredients, treating Funifura's brother or practicing a medical occupation. The competence and ordinary-life ledger wording receives the same qualification.

EXP02 U023 P433 says that the potion **Megumin made** apparently worked very well. The Japanese uses an apparent-result formulation, not a statement that Dodonko performed medical production. The current index assigns the farewell line to Dodonko. That is a contextual attribution within the Funifura/Dodonko exchange: the line refers to Funifura in the third person, and adjacent turns support the join. P433 itself has no explicit narrator name-tag. Accordingly, the index's word “explicitly” should not imply a directly named speaking turn.

Megumin's earlier suspicion that the illness was an excuse is stated at P436; Funifura denies going that far at P437. This later participant testimony qualifies the suspicion. It is not a new observed patient examination, independently verified cure, exact dose/accounting record or proof that all earlier financial pressure was harmless.

The actual ingredient work in EXP01 U037 P93–98/P113–122 belongs to Megumin and Arue: Arue catches a fleeing ingredient, helps the preparation, asks which part is needed and removes/cuts leeks. Those acts should not migrate to Dodonko. The chapter's report of a successful potion likewise does not make every participant a producer or clinician.
