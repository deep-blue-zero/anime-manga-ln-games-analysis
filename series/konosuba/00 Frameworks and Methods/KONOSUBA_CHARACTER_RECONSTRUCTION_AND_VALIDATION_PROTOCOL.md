# KONOSUBA - Character Reconstruction and Validation Protocol

## 1. Purpose

This protocol defines how sequential evidence becomes a usable character model and how that model must be tested before it is called mature.

The objective is not imitation by catchphrase or stereotype. It is a generative reconstruction capable of producing plausible behavior in situations that were never explicitly depicted.

A successful model should answer:

> Given this stimulus, relationship context, current emotional state, stake level, and genre register, what will the character notice, how will they appraise it, what motives will compete, what predictable distortions will enter judgment, what action will become attractive, how will the character speak while acting, and what conditions could override the normal response?

## 2. Mandatory subjects and promotion rule

Full models are mandatory for:

- Kazuma;
- Aqua;
- Megumin;
- Darkness.

Secondary characters may be promoted when the corpus contains enough evidence across **multiple contexts**, not merely many lines in one narrow role.

A mature full-model candidate should normally have evidence for:

- ordinary conversation;
- conflict;
- at least two distinct relationships;
- decision-making under nontrivial stakes;
- preferences or ordinary-life behavior;
- speech/register variation;
- emotional change;
- enough repeated behavior to separate trait from one-off gag.

If these conditions are not met, create a limited profile rather than pretending to have a generative model.

## 3. Reconstruction philosophy

### 3.1 Traits are summaries, not engines

"Proud," "lazy," "perverted," "stupid," "kind," or "explosive" may summarize evidence but do not predict behavior by themselves.

The model must identify **decision mechanisms**.

### 3.2 Separate trait, state, and context

A stable disposition can be temporarily suppressed by fear, exhaustion, guilt, affection, public scrutiny, resource scarcity, or serious danger.

### 3.3 Separate capacity from choice

A character can know better and still choose badly. Distinguish knowledge, reasoning, judgment, self-regulation, and value preference.

### 3.4 Relationship is part of the model

Behavior with Kazuma cannot be assumed to equal behavior with a stranger. Pair-specific trust, irritation, vulnerability knowledge, status, and ritualized conflict must condition simulation.

### 3.5 Genre register is a parameter

KONOSUBA uses farcical amplification. The underlying psychology may transfer across settings while the visible magnitude of reaction does not.

### 3.6 Negative evidence matters

When a high-confidence trigger occurs but the expected behavior does not, treat that as evidence. Do not only collect confirming examples.

## 4. Evidence hierarchy

Use the following general weighting, subject to context.

### E1 - Direct high-context behavior

Direct action or speech where motive/context is unusually clear.

### E2 - Repeated cross-context behavior

A mechanism appears independently across several scenes or volumes.

### E3 - Relationship-conditioned repetition

A pattern is reliably tied to a specific counterpart or relationship state.

### E4 - Seriousness-override evidence

A comic/default script is suppressed under meaningful stakes, revealing higher-order priorities.

### E5 - Ordinary-life evidence

Habits, preferences, boredom, chores, money, leisure, low-stakes conversation.

### E6 - Narrator-framed evidence

Kazuma's description or interpretation without independent corroboration. Useful, but mark viewpoint dependence.

### E7 - Gag-contingent evidence

Behavior primarily serving a local joke. Generalize only with recurrence or supporting evidence.

Evidence classes are not a simple numeric ranking. E5 evidence may be decisive for a mundane simulation even when E1 combat evidence is irrelevant.

## 5. Required components of a mature character model

### 5.1 Identity and self-conception

- Who does the character believe they are?
- What roles/statuses matter?
- What self-image do they protect?
- What contradictions exist between self-image and observed behavior?

### 5.2 Goal hierarchy

Distinguish:

- immediate appetites;
- status goals;
- comfort/security goals;
- relationship goals;
- identity commitments;
- moral/duty commitments;
- long-term ambitions.

The hierarchy must specify known override conditions rather than a flat list.

### 5.3 Attention model

What does the character notice disproportionately?

Examples of categories to test without assuming them in advance:

- money;
- praise;
- sexual cues;
- danger;
- status insult;
- magical opportunity;
- inconvenience;
- food;
- social embarrassment;
- another character's distress.

### 5.4 Appraisal model

How does the character interpret ambiguous events?

Track recurring attribution patterns, suspiciousness, optimism/pessimism, entitlement, victim framing, competitive framing, or other tendencies only when evidence supports them.

### 5.5 Reward and aversion structure

What feels:

- rewarding;
- humiliating;
- threatening;
- prestigious;
- comforting;
- boring;
- morally unacceptable?

### 5.6 Error generators

The model should identify recurring mechanisms that produce bad decisions, not merely list bad decisions.

Potential mechanisms include impulsivity, motivated reasoning, overconfidence, identity protection, poor risk assessment, fixation, resentment, short-termism, social misreading, or failure to update.

### 5.7 Competence map

Represent competence by domain and component:

- knowledge;
- reasoning;
- planning;
- practical judgment;
- execution;
- social perception;
- regulation;
- adaptability;
- crisis performance.

### 5.8 Affect transitions

Model common transitions such as:

`calm -> annoyed -> angry -> retaliatory`

or

`confident -> challenged -> boastful -> overcommitted -> panicked`

only after the sequential corpus supports them.

Track both triggers and recovery mechanisms.

### 5.9 Regulation and inhibition

What can stop the character from acting on an impulse?

Possible inhibitors include:

- fear of consequence;
- shame;
- financial cost;
- another person's disapproval;
- duty;
- affection;
- real danger;
- exhaustion;
- lack of audience;
- strategic calculation.

### 5.10 Decision heuristics

Convert recurring evidence into conditional rules such as:

> Under trigger X, if state S and relationship condition R are present, the character tends toward appraisal A and behavior B, unless override O is active.

Every important heuristic should include scope and exceptions.

### 5.11 Social scripts

Where evidence allows, distinguish behavior toward:

- party members;
- close friend/intimate counterpart;
- stranger;
- authority;
- subordinate/dependent person;
- admirer;
- rival;
- hostile actor;
- vulnerable person;
- person who insults them;
- person who offers status or reward.

Do not invent unsupported categories.

### 5.12 Relationship-conditioned variants

For each major dyad, model:

- default tone;
- trust;
- affection;
- annoyance;
- dependence;
- known weaknesses;
- tolerated aggression;
- forbidden boundaries;
- manipulation;
- repair;
- seriousness override.

### 5.13 Moral and seriousness boundaries

State what the character will not normally do and what conditions cause ordinary selfish, cowardly, lustful, lazy, vain, or obsessive behavior to be suppressed.

These limits are critical to preventing caricature.

### 5.14 Ordinary-life model

Include mundane behavior:

- money;
- food;
- work;
- chores;
- sleep;
- leisure;
- comfort;
- possessions;
- boredom;
- travel;
- waiting;
- inconvenience;
- small social obligations.

### 5.15 Language and voice grammar

The model should specify **patterns**, not a quote bank:

- register range;
- address style;
- sentence length/shape tendencies;
- complaint style;
- boast style;
- denial/pleading style;
- insult style;
- emotional interjections;
- rhetorical self-justification;
- pair-specific speech differences.

All claims remain subordinate to `KONOSUBA_JAPANESE_HUMOR_AND_VOICE_PROTOCOL.md`.

### 5.16 Humor amplification envelope

Define how the character's baseline disposition is amplified by KONOSUBA's comic register.

The model should explicitly distinguish:

- psychologically portable mechanism;
- canonical comic magnitude;
- physical/cartoon consequence that should not transfer literally;
- contexts where the work itself reduces amplification.

### 5.17 Uncertainty and competing hypotheses

A mature model may contain OPEN alternatives. Do not force false precision.

## 6. Two required operating modes

Every final model must support two modes.

### 6.1 Canonical-comic mode

Use when the character is placed in a scenario compatible with KONOSUBA's own farcical register.

Preserve:

- larger emotional swings where canon supports them;
- comic timing;
- routine interpersonal aggression permitted by the series;
- exaggerated consequences only when genre context allows them;
- established ensemble escalation patterns.

### 6.2 Cross-context transfer mode

Use when the character is placed in a more realistic or differently toned setting.

Preserve:

- goals;
- biases;
- preferences;
- relational tendencies;
- attention patterns;
- voice identity;
- decision mechanisms;
- seriousness boundaries.

Reduce or reinterpret:

- cartoon physics;
- gag-contingent humiliation;
- impossible physical consequences;
- extreme reactions that the evidence classifies as comic amplification rather than literal baseline.

The transfer model must not make the character bland. It should reduce **genre amplification**, not remove the underlying disposition.

## 7. Prospective validation protocol

### 7.1 Freeze before exposure

At V01-V03, V04-V06, V07-V09, V10-V12, and V13-V15 checkpoints, freeze current model claims before reading the next tranche.

### 7.2 Prediction schema

Each prediction records:

```text
prediction_id
model_generation
character
trigger/context
relationship condition
stake level
predicted appraisal
predicted dominant motive
predicted behavior
predicted linguistic/interaction tendency if relevant
confidence
disconfirming observation
source claims supporting prediction
```

### 7.3 Outcome schema

After later evidence appears:

```text
outcome
confirmed / partial / falsified / not tested / ambiguous
new evidence locator
failure diagnosis
claim transition
model revision
```

### 7.4 Failure diagnosis categories

When a prediction fails, consider:

- trigger was misidentified;
- missing state variable;
- relationship dependence;
- stake dependence;
- genuine character development;
- competence-domain mismatch;
- comic amplification mistaken for baseline;
- narrator framing mistaken for fact;
- language/pragmatic cue missed;
- model overfit to too few scenes;
- model was simply wrong.

Do not rescue a hypothesis by adding an unfalsifiable exception after every failure.

## 8. Checkpoint confidence

Confidence should be qualitative and evidence-based rather than pseudo-precise.

Suggested states:

- **LOW** - plausible but thin evidence or highly context-bound;
- **MODERATE** - replicated but important boundaries remain unclear;
- **HIGH** - repeated across distinct contexts and survives prospective tests;
- **VERY HIGH** - repeated, boundary-tested, relationship/stake conditions understood, and supported by withheld-source validation.

No claim reaches VERY HIGH before post-freeze validation unless the project explicitly documents why validation is impossible.

## 9. Simulation audit battery

Before a final model is promoted to canonical reconstruction status, test it across deliberately varied prompts.

### 9.1 Mundane inconvenience

Examples: long wait, lost item, delayed train, bad meal, minor household problem.

Purpose: test ordinary-life evidence and prevent quest-only modeling.

### 9.2 Resource conflict

Limited money, unfair division, desired luxury, debt, reward allocation.

Purpose: expose goal hierarchy and negotiation behavior.

### 9.3 Social novelty

Meeting someone admirable, obnoxious, shy, wealthy, hostile, or flattering.

Purpose: test social appraisal rather than scripted party interaction.

### 9.4 Public embarrassment

Distinguish private discomfort from status loss in front of an audience.

### 9.5 Moral pressure

A real person is being harmed and the situation cannot be dismissed as routine comic inconvenience.

Purpose: test seriousness overrides.

### 9.6 Fear and danger

Separate low-stakes discomfort, credible personal danger, and threat to someone valued.

### 9.7 Competence-domain shift

Place the character inside and outside domains where the corpus shows expertise.

### 9.8 Relationship conflict

Test ritualized argument versus genuinely serious conflict with a close counterpart.

### 9.9 Cross-universe novelty

Use a situation with no close canonical analogue. The model must reason from mechanisms rather than nearest-scene imitation.

## 10. Audit criteria for generated responses

For each simulation, reviewers should be able to answer:

1. Which evidence-backed motive produced the action?
2. Which bias/error generator operated?
3. Which relationship modifier mattered?
4. Was the humor amplification level appropriate?
5. Was a seriousness override ignored or invented?
6. Did the Japanese-derived voice model influence speech naturally rather than through catchphrase spam?
7. Could a superficially similar situation produce a different response, and does the model know why?
8. Is the model overconfident where evidence is thin?

A response that merely "sounds like the character" but cannot be causally defended fails the reconstruction standard.

## 11. Withheld-source validation

After V17 and after the main-series reconstruction suite is frozen, audit the available side material.

Suitable scenes from:

- `Yorimichi`;
- `Consulting the Masked Devil`;
- Dust V06-V07;

may be used to test the model, especially when they offer different narrator/viewpoint conditions or social contexts.

Record:

- whether the character was in scope;
- whether chronology makes the scene comparable;
- whether the main-series model predicted the behavior;
- whether a failure reflects model weakness, development, narrator difference, or source-specific comic framing.

Do not immediately absorb every validation failure. First determine whether the test is genuinely comparable.

## 13. Model maturity standard

A character is not "fully modeled" because the document is long.

A mature model must demonstrate:

- repeated cross-volume evidence;
- clear boundaries and exceptions;
- distinction between competence and judgment;
- relationship conditioning;
- serious versus comic operating modes;
- ordinary-life evidence;
- language/voice evidence;
- prospective predictive success;
- documented failures and revisions;
- successful tests in novel situations;
- no dependence on a handful of famous catchphrases or memes.

The desired end state is a model that can explain **what kind of mistake the character will make, why, when they will not make it, how they will speak while making or avoiding it, and what evidence could prove the model wrong**.
