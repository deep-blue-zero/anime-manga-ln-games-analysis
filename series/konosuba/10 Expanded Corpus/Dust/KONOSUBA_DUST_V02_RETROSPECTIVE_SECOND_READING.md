## 1. Narrative shape and chronology

The prologue is a deliberately incomplete preview of the later mixed-bath encounter. Pro P5/P16 recur as C03 P668/P669. It supplies a sexual opportunity and Dust's anticipatory rationalization before supplying the full setting, impaired attractions, private age/crime inhibition, collective rivalry, aborted proposals and magical retaliation. Counting the preview and later scene as two distinct behavioral events would be false replication. Reading only the preview would also omit a genuine private brake before Leen arrives.

C01 revisits the hydra battle through Dust's participation and death. Its overlap with accepted MAIN evidence must remain a dependent alternate perspective, while its exact speech, motives and social consequences are new evidence in this telling. The beginning gives Kazuma's practical coordination and the party's unequal roles; Dust's later self-account is less reliable than those observed steps. Rescue exhortation, competitive opportunity, actual death, an Eris encounter, resurrection, Leen's relief and Aqua's gratitude burden coexist. This is not a linear moral conversion: Dust can recognize life-saving care and still turn another person's pride into an escape problem.

C02 moves from a hidden-room discovery and ordinary job logistics into a temporary party. Keith and Taylor's unexplained exhaustion creates the need for replacements. Dust first mistakes the situation as an opportunity for fame and women; the source then makes recruitment fail in differentiated ways. Darkness has unusual task motives; Megumin has a real one-cast allocation problem; Aqua has real healing capacity but anticipated disruption; Yunyun has extensive actual spell competence; the succubus has limited direct combat but useful control spells. The successful temporary party is therefore a distributed accomplishment. The dragon resolution reveals cultivated behavior, calmness and practical knowledge without yet naming Dust's complete former identity.

C03 explicitly says several months have passed since the hydra episode (P15). Kazuma's old Arcanum experience is a prior reference, not the same journey being reenacted concurrently. The current trip later presupposes the Eris festival (C04 P347–348), which Dust did not attend personally. Within C02, the prepaid job is the day after tomorrow (P171), and the later job-day/jail reference allows an intervening day; it should not be compressed into an incorrectly dated next-day job. The trip has seven initial travelers, with Chris arriving later. Its apparent harem increasingly depends on a charm artifact and asymmetrical access to Vanir's explanation. The reader hears part of the warning that Dust misses. This creates dramatic irony rather than evidence that all parties knowingly consented to one arrangement.

C04 follows the bath on that evening, then the next day's near-noon conversation (P195). It converts tourist harassment into a religious-status and commercial-conflict scene. Chris's original public-good plan is abandoned after its risks are considered. The later costumed public appearance is an unplanned response to a demon alarm; it is not the successful execution of the rejected plan. Vanir's sales fraud, Dust's opportunistic participation and a pursuit create the final departure. Final P8 specifies an all-night escape before the party leaves town. The returned city has a progressively worsening motivational crisis, which reconnects the trip to C02's overlooked artifact. The closing group battle resolves that local danger, and Epi confirms an unofficial reward supported by a selectively edited record. It does not disclose Dust's full past to Leen: she falls asleep before he gives the content of the intended confession.

## 2. Dust: capable action without moral erasure

Dust's hydra sequence should retain both altruistic and gratifying causes. He urges action to save Kazuma and Darkness at C01 P37–41. His later charging decision is also framed by personal opportunity, bounty and female admiration (P43–49), and Leen's warning precedes the fatal result. Neither the rescue goal nor the self-enhancement can replace the other. His later willingness to confront danger does not make the original lapse imaginary. Eris's actual death greeting and Aqua's actual resurrection establish that this death is not merely a failed boast or dream.

The scene after resurrection supplies a particularly strong inhibition counterexample. Leen's tears and embrace interrupt his head-hit complaint; he privately thinks about staying alive for her and responds with apology and a head pat (C01 P161–170). This is a bounded relational brake. It does not establish a permanent tear-trigger law or erase later teasing, sexual threats and opportunism. The same volume repeatedly shows a wish to be valued by companions alongside reluctance to own the cost he creates.

In recruitment, the anti-caricature evidence is not that Dust's cruel methods were justified. The task really has composition constraints; his manner adds avoidable harm. His fake interview of Megumin is explicitly designed to pressure and hurt her (C02 P363–377, especially P371). His private concern for protecting Leen and managing a one-cast teammate is not an excuse for humiliation. He also lies that Vanir has already joined to induce Aqua to refuse (P433–442), then momentarily forgets that he still needs a fourth worker (P443–448). A plan that removes an unwanted candidate can still fail at the actual recruitment objective.

The goblin and dragon sequences show real practical ability. Dust can stall six enemies and notices that a spear would have made the job easier (C02 P633–638). Sleep and Lightning then do work he could not simply replace with swagger. At the dragon, his choice to protect the group is already made at P760–761, before Leen's later trust at P783 energizes him. His announced selfish escape at P774 is explicitly a pretext at P775 to send the others away. The dragon's removal is reported as treatment and negotiation, with the embedded sword and an actual bag of treasure as local support (P811–814); it is not a narrated dragon slaying. The familiar neighbor/dragon-knight story is told by Yunyun as information from Iris (P815–817). Dust's behavior and reactions make a hidden identity plausible, but do not constitute an explicit complete confession.

The later emergency combines responsibility, concealment and genuine protection. Dust orders distributed information gathering (Final P101); he and Leen revise the initial wait-and-see hypothesis when Luna's account shows progression. He carries three affected women, rejects seductive alternatives because sleeping could be fatal, and continues to carry them after considering a small residual danger if they were left behind (P223–250). He also wants to hide his own causal role once he recognizes the first device (P311–317). The edited diary is eventually an actual act, confirmed by Epi P8, not merely a passing fantasy. Rescue does not certify transparency.

His hostage bluff is causally effective and ethically ugly. At Final P365 he pretends to put a sword to his own succubus companion; no wound is narrated. The armed men surrender at P376 because they believe the threat. That does not make the companion's exposure consensual. Likewise the later book-burning bluff has no intention to destroy the valuable books, but he prolongs it because their reaction amuses him (P407–415). Those two tactics should remain distinguishable from actual injury, actual burning and harmless neutral negotiation.

The miniature Destroyer battle has explicit division of labor. Keith's rope arrows and the three men's pulling constrain movement; Taylor shields the casters; Yunyun severs two legs; the succubus supplies a dream incentive; Dust uses a borrowed spear and learned movement; Leen's Lightning completes the destruction (Final P505–546). Piercing the orb alone does not stop the machine (P541). Dust's poor landing and upskirt proposal afterward preserve his comic limitations without retroactively denying the skilled movement or the others' contributions. The final soles approach at P555; that line alone does not establish a particular severity of beating or death.

## 3. Leen: task competence, care, uncertainty and refusal

Leen performs both practical and relational work. She manages the wire task, job requirements, candidates, team fairness and the eventual comparison between the city's listlessness and Keith/Taylor's earlier symptoms. She is not always right: early explanatory guesses are revised as more information arrives. Her frightening expressions and threats are often focalized through Dust. They must not automatically be upgraded to completed attacks, nor generalized into consent to every risk because she stays in the party.

Her care after resurrection is directly shown, and her later loan management is concrete: C03 P51–55 says she uses the dragon profits to settle Dust's debts. This is not an unperformed proposal from the earlier post-hydra conversation. She still challenges his spending, bullying and new trouble. Her warning before the dragon and later trust are differently timed; the latter does not cause his first decision to protect the others.

The charm incident makes causal restraint especially important. Earlier ordinary contact and support coexist with abrupt intensified closeness after the artifact. The reader has Vanir's account of a gradual one-to-two-day effect; Dust misses it and repeatedly interprets changed behavior through an incorrect short-duration assumption. Leen's near-kiss is interrupted, not completed (C03 P535–536). Her later rivalry is not a reliable measure of an unaltered durable preference. After the bath, the avoidance of discussing it and a lack of objection to incidental touch do not prove informed consent, forgetting or the absence of embarrassment.

The final conversation is an unresolved relational offer, not a completed identity reveal. A drunk Leen changes from joking to a serious question about concealed matters (Epi P45–57). Dust considers her safe to tell, begins, then hears her sleeping (P60–64). He supports her unsteady body and carries her back to their friends (P56/P66–67). Her question, his willingness, her sleep and his care all matter. They cannot be collapsed into either 'she already knows his past' or 'he refuses all intimacy.' The underwear-sale line at P53 is a provocative evasion/self-report in this exchange, not an independently witnessed new theft.

## 4. Yunyun, the succubus and other companions

Yunyun's shyness depends on audience and knowledge. She is hesitant about entering a recruitment conversation, yet articulate with familiar Dust, capable of recognizing both a scam and Megumin's dangerous romantic implications, and forceful when refusing the fraudulent photo proceeds. Her ritual self-introduction is explained as a village practice (C02 P498), so it should not be read exclusively as spontaneous self-aggrandizement. Her practical magic is demonstrated rather than inferred from a character sheet. Her vulnerability to praise is visible; Dust explicitly plans to exploit it while asking for more work.

She is also capable of concealment for a personal aim. C03 P560 admits that she withheld the destination's problems because she feared the trip would be canceled. That is an observed counterexample to a one-note account of transparent innocence. Her later approach to Dust in C04 is resolved more narrowly than an uncomplicated romantic conversion: she is trying to create an experience with which to answer Megumin's romantic bragging (P540–541). That does not make all friendliness fake. It gives a locally stated purpose, while the precise residual magical state is not independently tested.

The doll should be described with its actual sequence and cost. Dust bought a smaller version of the desired toy with his remaining money on his return from the theater (C04 P537/P544–545), before Yunyun's later thanks. Guilt after that thanks prompts the present offering (P533–536). Her delight, conditional forgiveness of the bath and embrace of the doll are shown (P546–548). This gift is not a purchase made only after gratitude, not the original larger expensive toy, and not evidence that she knowingly accepted the earlier manipulation. Dust's recollection and generosity coexist with his failure to disclose the artifact.

The succubus is not merely a childlike visual role or an ineffective substitute. The public alias is ロリーサ; her true name and age are not supplied by that alias. She states a direct-combat limitation, yet Sleep saves Dust, dream knowledge supplies hypotheses, steam causes real species-sensitive harm, and dream incentives mobilize the captured men. Her conditions and goals are her own. She accepts the trip because of Vanir and helps with the seating conspiracy to secure her own arrangement; that is not unconditional devotion to Dust. Her tears during Vanir's sales crisis do not cause him to stop the scheme, although he extricates her from the immediate crowd. Her final reward share is reserved in Epi P17, not actually handed over in the shown scene; the promised dream reward that night remains future relative to Epi P18.

Keith and Taylor supply a useful correction to initial judgments. Their early listlessness can be mistaken for sexual or ordinary exhaustion. The later account of its abrupt onset in the hidden room, the broader town crisis and the diary/device evidence require a revised causal explanation. Their motor and tactical contributions in the final battle are real. They can be friends who share a bath and notice Dust's distress, opportunistic beneficiaries of his spending, and collaborators who attempt to distance themselves from illegal book proceeds. A single moral label cannot preserve those changes of task and incentive.

## 5. Aqua, Kazuma, Megumin, Darkness, Chris and Vanir

Aqua's local portrayal separates performed capacity from recognition and coordination. She actually restores Dust's life; Taylor credits her repair of the damaged body. She also performs the water-drawing art, receives Dust's thanks, and becomes increasingly demanding of accurate praise because she feels disrespected elsewhere (C01 P202–232). Dust's eventual escape by handing her to Kazuma does not make the restoration unreal. In C02 Dust explicitly recognizes that a healer would be useful but manipulates the supposed party composition to avoid her. That is evidence about his expectations and his tactic, not an actual Vanir/Aqua joint mission that fails.

Kazuma is a capable coordinator in the hydra scene, a friend who gives a travel ticket with enough context to indicate his own bad experience, and an opportunistic social actor who later volunteers Dust's money without consent. The ticket supplies no evidence that Kazuma personally caused Vanir's fraud or Dust's charm plan. His absence during the city crisis is later explained by a visit to the royal capital (Epi P12), not a refusal of help after an actual request. His later intervention against Megumin and Yunyun drinking, together with Darkness, is directly narrated at Epi P39. Dust's surprise is informative about his model of Kazuma, not a universal rule that Kazuma never disregards rules.

Megumin's application scene should retain both resource limits and humiliation. The one-cast problem is real for the goblin job. Her pride and rivalry do not erase the fact that Dust turns the meeting into a deliberately insulting interview. The threatened Blast is interrupted before a cast. Her tears in the hypothetical rejected-employer construction are not a demonstrated tear-brake episode in the current interview. Yunyun's later claim about having defeated 'Destroyer' is true only at the relevant miniature scale and in a shared effort. Megumin's initial disbelief is not proof that Yunyun imagined the event.

Darkness's unusual desires appear as specific local reactions to humiliation and a request for adventure away from administrative stress. They do not authorize every danger or establish that all outside roles disappear. Her protecting Kazuma in the hydra event and her later part in stopping underage drinking occupy different contexts from her recruitment motives. No forecast is scored from those appearances here.

Chris experiences actual religious discrimination, body-directed insults and institutional exclusion. Her badge can also create solidarity with the guards. The guards do not grant demonstrated source access: she says their detailed account is sufficient for her commission. Her resemblance to Eris, the suppressed-power remark, her reaction to praise and the aside that Dust has met Aqua many times give the reader access beyond Dust's persistent misunderstanding. Within this volume Dust does not explicitly solve and announce her full identity. Her costume is not simple proof that she is an impostor, and Dust's limited memory is not a reliable denial of what the reader already knows from the broader admitted corpus.

Vanir is neither a universal organizer nor a simple omniscient solution. He provides a dangerous artifact with strategically incomplete communication, seeks bad feelings, manipulates the public sale, disguises himself as Yunyun to attach himself to the fastest runner and refuses the subsequent physical rescue mission. He still provides important diagnostic information after hearing the dungeon account. His report of Wiz's loss to a compensation scam and Yunyun's care interpretation are distinct from the observed charred body and Dust's qualified impression that he is pleased to be valued. His later proposed punishment of the scammer is a plan, not a witnessed executed humiliation.

## 6. Comedy mechanisms and Japanese pragmatic evidence

The strongest repeating device is not 'everyone is foolish.' It is unequal knowledge plus a shift in whose purpose a useful act serves. Dust first helps, boasts or improvises; another character supplies a rule, debt, resource limitation or unanticipated reply. The joke often leaves the initial real capacity intact. A life-saving priest becomes an expensive gratitude interaction; a hidden treasure creates both commercial opportunity and a still-unexplained illness; a new party reveals useful specialists; a romantic artifact produces altered-state rivalry and an informational defeat; a fraud makes money and endangers its helpers; a rescuer's hostage bluff looks worse than the criminals' concern.

The hydra-to-miniature-battle contrast is a bounded learning pattern. Dust explicitly remembers his earlier carelessness at Final P537 and changes his movement under a different coordinated plan. The concluding tumble and sexual opportunism do not erase that specific correction. Conversely the resolution of the motivational crisis does not establish honesty: the diary is edited and public evidence is selected. Comedy and serious causal responsibility are simultaneous, rather than mutually exclusive source planes.

The diary is an actual embedded documentary passage at Final P277–286. It reports an earlier inventor's wishes, experiments and safety settings. Current events outside those quotations are not diary testimony. The exhausted characters' statements and the reader's access to the charm warning should be kept separate from objective medical diagnosis, free preference or full voluntary consent. Dreams are described, promised, marketed or reported; the principal narrative does not supply a separately realized sexual dream episode that validates every claim about them.

A source-first voice inventory accompanies this report. It has no formal L outcomes. Several examples are especially useful:

- Vanir's delayed explanation and `残念、再び我輩でした！` (C04 P508) manipulate turn expectation and speaker identity. - `エリス教徒のお客様` and the apparent service at C04 P122–130 create a courtesy frame followed by a hostile padding gift. The literal greeting is less informative than the gift's context. Religious solidarity is Dust's expectation, not the server's demonstrated purpose.

## 7. Specific risks for later coordinated receiving

The accompanying event cards retain stronger and weaker readings. The following precision points should remain visible in any later synthesis:

1. The prologue and mixed bath are one event. The later bath contains private age/crime restraint before Leen interrupts; later sexual proposals and collective punishment remain real contrary behavior.
2. Two artifacts must not be merged. Dust's earlier button press in the dungeon activates the motivation device; Vanir's later charm orb creates a separate romantic/knowledge problem.
3. Vanir's full charm warning is initially reader-accessible but not heard by Dust. His short-duration model is mistaken; altered women's behavior is not ordinary informed consent.
4. Yunyun's C04 dating behavior has a stated comparison/bragging aim. Dust's magical-resistance theory at P293 is his inference, not an independent test that all artifact effects ended by a measured time.
5. The doll is actually bought with the remaining money before the later thanks, then offered after guilt. It is smaller than the initially desired object. The purchase and presentation do not have exactly the same immediate trigger.
6. The costume publicity plan is abandoned. Chris runs toward a demon while still costumed; Dust later uses her as a scapegoat. This is not evidence that the intended peaceful program was executed successfully.
7. The apparent Yunyun chosen during the escape is Vanir. Dust's attempt to divert pursuers from weakened friends is still independently explicit and later helps the real Yunyun escape.
8. The motivational explanation revises earlier impressions. It is not demonstrated intrinsic immunity from having no motivation. The first-activator protection and distance settings are documentary claims that fit the current observations.
9. Early recovery does not establish a destroyed orb. Dust's hypothesis at Final P465/P469 is superseded by a functioning miniature machine and the unlit-eye orb. The eventual spear puncture alone also does not stop it.

12. The closing secret is not told. Leen is asleep before the content, while Dust's willingness and his care remain observable.
