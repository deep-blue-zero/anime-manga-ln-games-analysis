## 2. Architecture and the central movement

The volume moves through three attractive but incomplete stories: a harmless-looking plant offering comfort, an immense-looking reward offering easy money, and a dream offering consequence-free access. Each requires an account of material conditions that Dust or another character initially neglects. The last major movement gives a different use to fiction and performance: a knowingly constructed envoy persona enables an accepted, temporary outing for a person whose obligations Dust has actually witnessed.

The Anraku Girl chapter introduces the distinction between soothing appearance and a predatory transaction. Its fruit cannot simply be carried home; its protectors have different reasons for remaining; its vulnerable voice is strategic. The capital chapter places the same pressure on noble legend and reward. Iris's knight story contains romantic assumptions, while the ring's value cannot be separated from its origin. The guards' adventure turns heroic military history into housekeeping losses, then makes a supposedly bonding artifact violate privacy. Finally, the mask gives Dust a false explanation of his own experience, before a second mode lets him witness Iris's duty without controlling her.

The conclusion does not abolish obligation. Iris returns to the castle, the funding and marriage problem remain, and Dust keeps his public identity. The useful change is a bounded interval of freedom and an invitation to accept help. That outcome sits beside sustained harassment, deception and an actual rescue obstruction; it does not retroactively convert the volume into evidence that every bad act conceals kindness.
