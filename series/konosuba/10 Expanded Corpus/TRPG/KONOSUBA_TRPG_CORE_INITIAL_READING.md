# TRPG-CORE: negotiated adventure, comic competence and unequal source authority

This candidate records an actual ordered inspection of all 333 native page-images of *この素晴らしい世界に祝福を！TRPG*. The complete replay, the world guide’s continuous second-person fiction, the sample adventure and the eligible vignettes, teaching dialogue, character commentary and embedded examples were read in Japanese. Every page has its own view/read/legibility acknowledgment. Dense numerical sheets, reference tables and indexes were also inspected, at the individual scopes recorded in the 333-row register; this is not an exhaustive semantic audit of every rule interaction or numerical cell. No peer CORE annotations were received during this initial work.

The book offers several ways to inhabit KonoSuba without giving them equal evidentiary status. A played-session account makes decisions and dice outcomes part of a particular history. A world-guide story narrates an unnamed newcomer’s experience. A sample scenario prepares alternatives that no player has yet chosen. Character templates, rules and advertising balloons provide further possibilities and representations. Treating all these pages as additional main-novel events would destroy the distinction the book itself repeatedly makes between construction, declaration, adjudication, result and future intention.

## The replay’s causal engine is participation under conflicting appraisals

The replay’s four PCs have different reasons to enter and continue the adventure. 暁なつめ plays クラリオン・ヒート・ギルバート, a frightening-faced noble/Axis priest; 鰤／牙 plays スズキ・チホ, a reincarnated adventurer with a powerful weapon and an emphatic health story; 長月達平 plays 御手洗音姫, a warrior with death-return who strongly does not want to die; みかみてれん plays てみれん, a Crimson Demon mage whose bathhouse upbringing and romance-manga expectations affect her appraisals. These are not aliases for Kazuma, Aqua, Darkness and Megumin. Similar genre roles or cheats do not make the characters interchangeable.

The introductory construction matters because later danger does not simply reveal traits from nowhere. Otohime’s survival desire is already explicit even when a data field calls his goal aimless. Owning a return-from-death ability does not mean he wishes to use it. Temiren’s prince interpretation is chosen before Gilbert’s more useful contributions. Gilbert’s sectarian frame combines a declared mission with poor social reception, while Chiho’s health rhetoric remains vulnerable to actual damage. Backstory is co-authored and reported rather than independently witnessed: the shared fatal accident, Aqua’s alleged reward and earlier divine instructions have different access conditions from what the session later resolves.

The first quest is not accepted by a single necessary coordinator. Otohime narrows options out of fear, the remaining toad job provides a practical bottleneck, the receptionist supplies urgency, and companions offer reassurance and relational pressure (printed 023–025). Gilbert adds a purported religious command; a player’s joke that the original author said it does not authenticate an observed instruction from Aqua. Participation results from unequal information and mixed motives, not an ideal team instantly sharing one goal.

Otohime’s later request to hear that he is needed makes the social mechanism unusually explicit (049–051). The group can value him as a person and also need his lantern or the oath’s damage benefit. Those reasons coexist. Their coexistence neither makes all affection fraudulent nor makes all encouragement fully informed consent to every risk. His fear remains audible in the written dialogue after he agrees. The party repeatedly repairs willingness without rewriting his survival motive into fearless devotion.

## Combat preserves failed handoffs and asymmetrical competence

The initial frog fight makes reassurance answerable to consequences. Otohime is swallowed after an armor-related assurance, complains to Gilbert, and receives a narrowed defense of what was actually promised (037–039). The earlier clothing was described as armor-like; it should not become a certified metal construction. A hypothetical digestion consequence is explained, but that future damage does not occur merely because the rule was described. The frog is killed in time.

Otohime later chooses cover despite fear. Gilbert’s shield helps but is initially insufficient, and Chiho’s attack is needed to rescue him (040–043). These are costly, sequential handoffs. Reading the ultimate success backward would erase the actual swallowing and the failed protective margin. The account also allows Otohime to cover while inside a frog. That is an explicit local adjudication in an abstract game system, not evidence of an ordinary body physically crossing an intact stomach wall. Nor should every mechanical abstraction be labeled H4 comic license: the relevant distinction is rule resolution versus realistic spatial inference.

Gilbert’s frightening-face ability later cancels a dangerous buff, while his own physical attack disappoints (068). This separates effective support from raw damage. Temiren’s powerful magic has a once-per-scenario cheat and a continuing doubled MP cost; it is not unlimited capability. Chiho contributes decisive offense while depending on resources, positioning and allies. Otohime’s finishing damage can be small and still matter. Brief pride after a frog victory is corrected by the enemy scale; it is not a permanent cure of his fear or proof that he can replace an entire campaign’s requirements.

The final battle most clearly shows why the replay cannot be treated as passive behavioral sampling. Chiho deliberately chooses to roll an evasion she could otherwise expect to manage so that Otohime can have a protective showcase (076). Cover then leaves six damage, and Gilbert’s shield exactly cancels that amount (078). Chiho’s later 46-damage attack defeats Froger. Agency exists at both player and PC levels, and the player actively shapes the opportunity. This is valuable evidence of collaborative authorship, not an independent trial of a naturally occurring disposition.

A more dangerous sequence occurs when Otohime pushes Chiho out of an area attack and faces damage sufficient to kill him (072–073). Gilbert offers shielding, but Otohime chooses the paid death-return mechanic. The GM narrates his feelings reaching Aqua and a new skin emerging. That is the session’s supernatural explanation of the resolved cheat; it does not show main-novel Aqua arriving in person. The survival motive persists in his explicit refusal to die, even as he protects someone else. Apparent martyrdom, companion praise and faith awakening cannot remove the actual Blessing cost or establish universal immunity.

## Dungeon cooperation is creative, locally adjudicated and sometimes careless

The cave sequence distributes knowledge and usefulness. Otohime supplies light and furniture-based reach; the ordinary chair and stick solve an ostentatiously praised intelligence task (052–053). Temiren’s bathhouse background helps recognize acid (054). Purification, bodily effort and a local recovery ruling allow the group to continue (055–056). The GM permits Fishing in a fishless purified pool, a conspicuous local exception. The skill’s general entry permits the GM to deny unsuitable conditions; the replay’s successful exception must not silently become the universal rule.

Useful strength does not prevent an omitted precaution. Chiho breaks a chest without a declared trap check, and the bomb damages the group and destroys part of the possible treasure (057–060). Otohime’s armor reduces the damage to zero before the cover doubling, rather than subtracting defense from an already doubled amount. His rescue is effective, but it does not restore broken rubies or remove Temiren’s small injury. The actual two remaining rubies are distinct from a hypothetical five-ruby result and later newly obtained loot. Cosmetic slime shame and romance framing sharpen the scene without converting every embarrassment into a status effect.

The antagonist’s diary further separates access from action (061–064). Gilbert objects to reading another person’s private writing; the companions conspicuously claim to drop and read it accidentally. That intrusion is performed within the replay. Froger’s earlier theft, transformation and ambitions are reported through GM/diary narration. The king/frog history uses a specifically Japanese *kaeru* reconstruction: changing into a king is turned into the king of frogs. A proposed kiss cure is not attempted. The reported transformation is not a second observed event simply because the party learns about it.

The GM then openly adds a sectarian insult in response to a player’s suggestion. Gilbert’s priority changes immediately. This is a powerful demonstration of how fiction can be made motivationally effective at the table, but it cannot be presented as a pre-existing trigger independently predicting his response. Similarly, the player’s「わくわくカエルランド」becomes a GM-adopted grotesque plan in which humans are ridden. No amusement park has actually been built. The local joke supplies villain characterization while leaving its particular future institution unobserved.

## Reward, coercion and ordinary life remain part of the adventure

The replay does not divide cleanly into selfish comic banter followed by pure altruistic teamwork. Temiren’s request for affection becomes a headpat conditional on an Axis donation; Gilbert acknowledges the manipulation, and Chiho enables it because it is entertaining (045–046). Agreement is present, but the payment itself is not verified. Romance, humor and willing response do not retrospectively authorize every form of pressure or turn roleplayed contact into real-player contact.

The diary’s religious trigger and the client encounter also show limits. A baseless Eris accusation is stopped by the GM at the table (048). The farmer’s view of the missing cow resists a romance narrative. Otohime can lose track of the rescue goal and need reminding. None of these failures is canceled because the party ultimately succeeds.

The return provides a material reward while resisting heroic self-certification. The base quest pays 100 KE and the rescued cow adds 150 KE (080); the latter was not in the original scenario, but was added by the GM for the play that occurred (081). That improvisation is causally significant and explicitly attributed. Gilbert upgrades Froger’s rank in his report, while the receptionist considers self-proclaimed status and tactfully looks away. She does not confirm that he was a formal executive. An artifact is held for intended temple return; actual delivery is not observed.

Even the concluding celebration has a capability boundary. Gilbert wants Aqua’s familiar party trick but lacks the required「宴会」skill (082). Faith and enthusiasm produce a future learning plan, not instant expertise or a performed trick. Afterplay recovery for eligible surviving PCs is an inter-session procedure, not a free reset in the middle of an otherwise costly battle. Ordinary money, living standards, fatigue, equipment and knowledge remain conditions of continuing membership.

## The world-guide fiction implicates the reader in KonoSuba’s faulty welcomes

Printed 250–260 is a complete story framed around an unnamed「キミ」. A holy sword and a goddess-mediated arrival do not identify this newcomer as Mitsurugi. The exhausted sword-as-walking-stick opening strips the heroic gift of its promise of effortless survival. The narrator mocks an apparent ending and begins the real social adventure after the newcomer collapses. The second-person form invites the reader to feel both the attractive fantasy and the grievance it produces.

Aqua’s safe-start promise is contradicted by the newcomer’s deadly arrival and her small「あっやばっ」at the transfer (252). In the present she wears a humiliating sign and kneels, while Kazuma tells her to take an appropriate apologetic attitude. Her written「しゅみましぇん」shrinks divine dignity into childish pleading. The newcomer approaches in anger, but no punch is shown. Letting the matter go does not prove that the original risk was harmless. Nor should the apology’s exaggerated presentation be mistaken for a recording of an actor’s voice.

Kazuma offers practical assistance and then specifies a 500-Eris introduction fee (253). His paid guidance is not pure charity, but a charge is not automatically fraud. The newcomer can accurately point out his limited knowledge. Megumin’s grand entry reveals lunch-time loneliness she immediately qualifies, while Kazuma corrects the idea that her eyepatch seals dangerous power: it is decoration (254–255). The newcomer’s tactful naming praise works because it gives her status without repeating a direct insult. Language manages a relationship here rather than merely displaying a stock catchphrase.

The receptionist supplies an important outside view. She privately asks「大丈夫かしら」while continuing professional registration; Kazuma, Aqua and Megumin do not notice (256). This is not reassurance addressed to the newcomer and not proof that the Guild knows every earlier act. Aqua’s impressive statistics coexist with social concern about the guides. The 1000-Eris registration fee and the newcomer’s 3000 Eris are stated as ordinary Eris, not KE. The delayed tutorial follows real danger; the earlier near-death route is not made an appropriate training phase simply because the Guild can now explain quests.

Darkness’s introductory concern is genuinely mixed (258). She notices poor equipment and asks about the newcomer’s trouble, then becomes visibly interested in the account of being beaten. Kazuma signals that the questions have another attraction for her. Concern and pleasure-oriented attention coexist; neither cancels the other. Her suggested next-day toad quest is refused and not performed. Her enjoyment cannot consent on behalf of the newcomer or make an actual future injury safe.

The ending neither absorbs the reader into the quartet nor demonstrates their wholesale replacement. They share lunch but do not subsidize the poorer newcomer, then leave for work. Another novice priest asks to form a party, and the newcomer accepts with an explicit novice limitation (260). The future adventures are prospective. A new partnership is possible within the setting, but no comparable full campaign has been executed. The account is a compact social initiation whose asymmetrical assistance, self-interest and embarrassment remain intact.

## The sample adventure teaches failure-bearing possibilities

*このタケノコに美味しさを* (298–311) is an authored scenario for a possible group, with a novice harvest presented through Luna and the expert オキナ. It is not a transcript, a completed generated response or a newly observed main-party adventure. Its read-aloud blocks, background facts, checks and advice belong to different planes even when all use vivid present-tense prose.

The easy-work pitch conceals a three-a.m. meeting, a blindfolded route and an expert who forgot his own annually added traps. The absurdity is not merely decorative. It creates information risk and prevents the expert’s status from guaranteeing safety. The source explicitly allows quest refusal to end the scenario and early retreat to fail it; a GM is not instructed to retroactively force a successful harvest. The request’s deadline, borrowed tools, free-hand requirement and carrying limits constrain what the possible PCs can do.

Bamboo shoots are profitable before movement. Failed harvesting can create a rampaging missile, and a launched crop can be lost without loot. The basket and spade put bamboo shoots to sleep within this harvesting procedure; that is not a universal named Sleep spell. A dead goblin in a trap is authored background, not a player kill or automatically earned XP. The final ambush shares the crop hazard with the goblins; it does not certify that the hazard will always rescue the players. Checks and GM choices retain multiple outcomes.

The ending keeps ordinary incentives nonadditive. Eating trades money for food/XP value; food XP is restricted to the scenario’s ending meal rather than a universal remote consumption rule. Premium produce can pay 150 KE, ordinary produce 20 KE, and the basic commission scales with PC count. Proposed breakfast and future traps are not completed choices. Okina’s intention to add still more traps repeats the source of danger without proving the next expedition actually occurs. The plot’s humor comes from a working ecology whose absurd resources still impose causal costs.

## Templates, rules and commentary cannot silently become principal history

The five sample personas and the lifepath miniatures broaden the setting beyond a fixed quartet. Their ordinary work, loneliness, family pride, coercion, revenge and lack of grand ambition are available possibilities. A thief class is explicitly not criminal employment; an absence of a grand goal does not mean no motive; a coercion entry does not mean the coerced actor desires the task. Crimson families include poverty and ordinary nonmagical life rather than one uniform destiny. A sample mage’s drawn-out Fireball is not Megumin casting a forbidden extra spell.

The book also marks adaptation. The adventurer class has a low-prestige place in the fictional world but can be demanding for an expert player to build, while this game’s basic class acquisition is not simply the novel’s stat gate (132). The social drawback of a faith option is subject to GM judgment, not universal actual persecution. Optional goddess, pain, royal, villain and production cheats make explicit tradeoffs rather than certifying that every apparent source analogue possesses the exact game statistics. Resurrection of an incapacitated character and actual death are distinguished. Lifelike desire or consent does not follow from a defense bonus, an HP total or a cheat name.

Player knowledge is also not automatically PC knowledge. A master scene can inform the player while leaving the PC uninformed. A chosen leader can coordinate without becoming a necessary universal Kazuma substitute; the rules allow the players to choose and to reject an unaccepted decision. The GM is a participant with adjudicative responsibility, not a servant. Procedural reassurance about GM rulings does not make every factual inference or ethical treatment infallible.

One small internal reference problem is retained for receiving rather than silently repaired: a quest’s printed 271 reference points the BeginnerKiller reader to 289, whereas the actual enemy card is on 288 and the inspected index supports 288. This is a routing issue, not a new fictional contradiction or permission to rewrite the native source. The 333-page inventory allows a receiver to verify the two locations directly.

## Humor and language: selected evidence, not a census

The original pass preserved comic observations on 120 pages. Before any formal coding, those exact page notes were consolidated into 75 selected premise groups and 41 selected language/context witnesses and sealed. This is a purposeful register. It includes weak reference flavor, optional histories, staged commentary and unresolved linguistic material; its row count is not a source-wide denominator, main-J02 census or collection of independent trials. The complete assignments retain the source plane, soft eligibility, culture/context flags and original observation routes.

L class evaluates what materially makes the selected premise work. A frog swallowing a frightened protector is structurally intelligible without Japanese wordplay. The clergy’s tactful doubt is pragmatically sensitive. The reported king/frog transformation combines portable privacy/romance setup with central Japanese-form reconstruction, so the whole compound premise remains mixed. The drawn-out Fireball’s printed timing is form-dependent. Aqua’s apology combines portable status reversal with material written deformation. Cultural RPG knowledge, Japanese wording and source-authority uncertainty are separate variables.

H class answers another question. H1 marks substantially literal behavior at the stated plane; H2 retains a disposition while qualifying comic intensity; H3 marks behavior too dependent on a local punchline to generalize; H4 would mark nonliteral comic license. H3 is not a synonym for paratext, uncertain extrapolation or an expanded source. Pure rules and unused options can have no performed behavioral object and therefore retain explicit N/A rather than being forced into H3. No H4 is forced merely because cover is mechanically abstract. The local Chris advertising aside and Aqua’s appropriation of a chapter depend on their particular punchlines; this is a behavioral reason for H3, not a source-plane shortcut.

The voice appendix separates speakers and audiences. Players addressing Akatsuki as creator do not give their PCs creator knowledge. GM narration as Aqua’s divine feeling is not main Aqua dialogue. Wiz’s eternal-20 label is profile narration, not her spoken tagline. The receptionist’s quiet concern is private rather than companion-directed care. The untagged blonde armored corrector on 263 is kept cautiously described rather than made into a confidently tagged speaker. These limits prevent snippets from acquiring false before/after roles or false listener responses.

## What this inspection supports and leaves open

CORE supplies a substantial expanded-corpus account of negotiated play, fallible reassurance, costly protection, motivated cooperation, local improvisation and source-aware humor. It also illustrates main-character stances in specifically identified teaching or guide-fiction contexts. It does not certify every adaptation mechanic as main-novel world law, every possible branch as a performed act, every main-character likeness as a natural dialogue sample or every successful phase as full campaign substitution.

The current report and coding are initial private candidates for root receiving. Unresolved limits include the one label/form issue, an untagged illustrated speaker, the non-exhaustive dense-table/index audit, and the edited nature of the replay. Ordinary rules actually read can support narrow mechanic statements, but a later claim about an exception or interaction still needs its actual governing passages. No model, forecast, earlier failure, R1 response, four-source local score or frozen artifact was revised. The complete 333-page disposition record, original uncoded seal, selected registers, attribution overlays and proposed ledger rows permit substantive receiving without requiring a false new whole-book reread by the receiver.
