## 3. The V04 illustrated sequence: desires are not completed acts

The sequence frames a planned Alcanretia visit through Vanir's information and response. Wiz's commercial hopes and the individual party members' wishes receive exaggerated visual realizations. Explicit character-case labels identify Megumin, Darkness, Aqua, Kazuma and Yunyun; the final page places the wishes in an imagined/foreseen frame and says the wished-for outcomes will not occur. This ending controls the evidence class of the preceding images.

Megumin converts a formidable religious underground setting into an occasion for spectacular destruction. Darkness's confession scenario produces an imagined coercive experience rather than a performed assault. Aqua's recognition demands and the crowd's reaction supply status comedy. Kazuma's bath desire and Yunyun's company-oriented bath image have different relational rewards. Vanir's final anticipation turns every grand expectation into his enjoyment of their failure. These are intelligible character-conditioned wishes, but they are not independent observations that the trip fulfilled those wishes.

The sequence is useful for genre and representation: character salience determines the imagined reward, visual escalation makes that reward legible, and a narrator/commentator's superior information punctures it. It is poor evidence for literal frequency, duration, physical consequences or universal behavior outside the gag. Speaker assignments in small introductory balloons remain bounded where a unique tail/label has not been established; explicit case labels and the final framing support the narrower claims without an invented exact speaker.

The source index's V04 frontispiece label prevents double-counting the artbook collection as an additional event. A later main-edition component audit must link this route as a dependent representation rather than silently enrolling it in the frozen principal derivation history.

## 4. Special story: structure and chronology

The short story begins and ends in inflated self-introduction. Between those bookends Megumin tries to secure the title of Axel's strongest magician by treating unlike successes as one ranking. The morning card game, the shop visit, the Guild and rival encounter, the frog hunt and the immobile aftermath each change what a victory could mean. The story repeatedly gives a participant a locally defensible rule and then exposes the rule's missing conditions.

Publication chronology is fixed by the 2017 artbook. Fictional chronology is only broadly constrained: Kazuma's party has its mansion, knows Wiz/Vanir/Chris/Mitsurugi/Yunyun, and the magical resource system is familiar. None of those facts supplies an exact position between main-volume chapters. Root exposure is after V17, R1 freeze and four-book validation. The story is not assigned a precise main-series date by its placement next to V10 art.

Kazuma narrates in first person. He reports some present events, paraphrases earlier events, quotes participants' claims and supplies sardonic motive attributions. This source plane must remain visible even when his criticism is persuasive. There is no all-knowing neutral ranking simply because the narrator thinks a title is absurd.

## 5. Ordered causal reading of the complete story

**Printed118/B1–B3: claiming expertise while missing the interaction.** Megumin poses and recites her challenge; Kazuma and Aqua continue the card game. Aqua's turn succeeds through the game's stated features and Kazuma concedes, while Megumin's delayed request for attention exposes the failed performance. The narration's deliberately extreme game example is a comic representation, not a newly demonstrated combat spell. Darkness is reading incident reports. The reported wizard ambushes become connected to Megumin's own account of recent intimidation exploiting the prohibition on city magic. Reported incidents, her admission and a directly observed attack are different kinds of evidence.

Megumin's goal is recognition and a public title. The method she defends exploits the opponent's institutional constraint; it is not inability to reason. It is also not a sound general demonstration of superior combat capacity. Kazuma's objection concerns the relation between the proposed title and the route used to acquire it. The gap between a desired identity and a narrow success criterion drives the rest of the story.

**Printed119/B1–B3: winning at what, and being whose customer?** Megumin takes the party as witnesses. At Wiz's shop she says she has already beaten Vanir and Wiz: she cites firing Explosion while Vanir possessed Darkness, costing him one life, and a recent Explosion contest against Wiz. Both are her reports of prior events. The noncombat victories she next mentions concern Yunyun, whose rivalry she still wants to settle; no board-game win against Wiz is stated here. She wants to settle matters with her longstanding rival. Wiz's eager excuses show that a former fighter can avoid an unwanted challenge without lacking capacity. Vanir and Aqua turn a discussion of tea into a quarrel over resident status, payment and age. Familiarity permits insults; it does not remove real commercial interests or supply unconditional service.

Vanir's appearance and information remain interested interventions. His offered information demands a price. His later sales pitch is not authenticated simply because he is knowledgeable. Aqua's retorts puncture his authority in a way quite different from Kazuma's explanatory questions.

**Printed120/B1–B3: consequential power and exploitative victory.** Vanir first offers a tool for temporarily obtaining extraordinary magic power beyond one's limits, without yet disclosing the lethal cost. Kazuma immediately says he will buy it despite lacking money, then asks about side effects. Vanir replies that life or stamina is burned in place of magic power and that using it invariably kills the user. Kazuma explicitly withdraws with `いらんわ！`. Only then does Megumin declare that she wants to burn her soul and exceed the limit; Kazuma restrains her and objects to such haste toward death. Wiz, who has been pouring tea for Aqua and Darkness, says the object is not for sale and must not be used by a person. Vanir reminds Wiz of using it against him and proposes selling it to Megumin anyway. Aqua and Darkness are not shown criticizing this transaction. Megumin subsequently objects to being called suicidal, but does not supply a separate prudent-use plan. The two buying declarations therefore occur under different information, not simultaneous informed acceptance of lethal cost. No present sale, use or death is completed.

Megumin recounts obtaining Mitsurugi's surrender by exploiting his refusal to fight girls, taking his weapon and demanding acknowledgment. His reluctance and her leverage are causally important. The account does not prove that she out-fought him. Her triumph also carries a borrowed language of cleverness that Kazuma finds disquieting. The Guild list reflects the narrator's available acquaintances rather than a verified census of Axel's strongest inhabitants.

Chris asks the exclaiming Megumin whether she wants snacks and presents the plate; Aqua, rather than Megumin, takes it. Megumin says Chris's earlier absence had counted as a forfeit, but now that they have met she proposes `血みどろの殺し合い`—a bloody fight to the death. Chris protests and subsequently offers to count herself defeated rather than fight. Megumin's hyperbolic coercive proposal is not a performed bloody duel; the text does not say that bloodshed is unnecessary.

**Printed121/B1–B3: recognition, friendship and an efficient rival.** Aqua says being with Chris feels familiar and comforting. Kazuma and Chris startle. Aqua then supplies an ordinary job explanation, and the encounter resumes. Her noticed familiarity and her unpursued inference coexist. This is evidence against always-oblivious caricature; it does not certify a new explicit discovery of Chris's identity. Aqua's job suggestion and Chris's response retain their mundane social context.

Megumin is frustrated that she cannot find Yunyun when looking for her; the earlier absence-as-forfeit statement concerned Chris. They meet Yunyun, who has been staying home in case friends visit and is happy that Megumin came. Recognition of her loneliness briefly coexists with exploitation of their rivalry. A public magical introduction earns attention; the relative audience response matters to Megumin. Neither attention nor friendship is identical to combat power.

The rivals settle on one continuous frog hunt measured by kills using magic. Yunyun deliberately uses appropriate intermediate magic rather than waste advanced-magic resources. Her Fireball line reports a cumulative ninth toad; Lightning follows without a separate per-spell count. Yunyun, addressing Megumin, says she could still kill five more. After Light of Saber the narrator gives Yunyun's total as sixteen. Megumin has not fired a first Explosion or obtained nine kills. Yunyun is then explicitly described as unable to move from exhausted mana; this is her observed current state, not a universal sixteen-kill capacity ceiling. No completed preliminary victory or second contest is narrated. Yunyun pointedly asks Megumin whether Megumin had forgotten Yunyun’s intermediate-magic ability. Kazuma’s following narration separately offers Megumin’s forgetting that ability as a hedged explanation for Megumin’s secret cold sweat. Both Yunyun’s question and Kazuma’s qualified narrator inference remain visible; neither establishes Megumin’s private cause. Knowledge, technical skill, choice of force and available resources must be modeled separately.

**Printed122/B1–B3: collective support, literal terms and a title that creates exposure.** Megumin gathers a larger target population through Aqua and Darkness and receives additional mana from Kazuma, with Aqua identifying the tactic as Drain Touch. Yunyun objects that this is unfair teamwork. Megumin replies that the agreed terms specified magic, not solitary work. Her interpretation exploits an omitted condition; it does not retroactively prove that everyone shared it. After Explosion removes the collected targets, Yunyun concedes. No exact kill total for Megumin's single narrated Explosion is supplied.

The nominal winner is immobile. She boasts of being the strongest while dependent on allies for the decisive resource and target setup. The allies then propose their own challenges. Darkness actually pokes Megumin's cheek and solemnly proposes a one-on-one match for the title. Kazuma asks whether she is angry because her investigation of Megumin's mage-intimidation cases proved misdirected; that is his explanatory question, not her stated motive. Yunyun lifts her face slightly and smiles happily; Megumin apologizes and asks to wait until her magic returns. The final compressed narration mentions someone panting red-faced and someone fleeing in tears without fixing their identities or the exact acts. No detailed new duel result or injury/death is shown, but the actual cheek-poking and narrated aftermath must not be turned into a claim that nothing occurred. Kazuma closes with his own elevated title, a callback that makes his apparent corrective distance incomplete: he too enjoys the verbal status game.

## 6. Character mechanisms and resistant evidence

**Megumin:** the story links identity, attention, chosen specialization and competitive framing. She wants other people to recognize a preferred identity; a witness, an audience and an acknowledgment are therefore rewards in their own right. She can exploit institutions and rules, recruit resources and adapt her setup to an efficient rival's target count. None of this establishes universally good judgment. A title pursued through defaults, earlier reported noncombat wins and another person's refusal is unstable outside its local definitions. Her asymmetric force preference survives competent alternatives but is not identical to incapacity to understand those alternatives. Her plea after spending her mana is state-sensitive regulation, not an immutable brave/coward binary.

**Yunyun:** she combines longing for company with operationally good resource choice, public performative identity and a willingness to dispute unfair terms. Her efficient spell use and sixteen kills are shown, but no completed first victory over Megumin is narrated. Her eventual concession follows Megumin's single Explosion against a larger, ally-gathered target pool with supplied mana. That local loss does not demonstrate permanent inferiority. She can enjoy competence success and still be hurt by the solitary label. The relationship remains a source of both pain and contact; neither passivity nor unqualified mastery explains the sequence.

**Aqua:** an actual game victory and a fleeting perceptive observation resist a purely incompetent reconstruction. Her appetite, hospitality, commercial friction and practical assistance remain ordinary motives. A useful observation does not entail that she follows its hidden implication. The source does not provide a universal probability of recognition or a fresh validation result.

**Darkness:** her attention to papers, investigation and questioning, restraint of Megumin's abrupt departure from Wiz's shop, actual help gathering toads and final title challenge are causally useful. The text does not show her morally criticizing the item sale, and Kazuma rather than Darkness supplies the final investigative-motive hypothesis. Her contribution is counterevidence to a model consisting solely of desired suffering, but does not prove a universal seriousness override or a completed severe punishment.

**Kazuma:** he distinguishes kinds of victory and notices missing terms, yet helps supply the resources for Megumin's scheme and ends by imitating the title performance. He initially wants Vanir's powerful item, asks its side effects and rejects it after the lethal cost is disclosed; his ensuing restraint of the still-eager Megumin has a different information history from his first buying declaration. His participation qualifies any reading in which he stands wholly outside the comic system. Source-level self-consciousness is not immunity to status incentives.

**Wiz, Vanir, Chris and Mitsurugi:** the story supplies limited, relationship-conditioned evidence. Evasion of a challenge is not inability; interested commerce is not omniscient reliable advice; Chris's avoidance is not universal cowardice; Mitsurugi's reported concession follows a chivalric constraint rather than a demonstrated skill deficit. Full secondary models cannot be promoted on this short story alone.

## 9. Production testimony and the amplification envelope

The making page explicitly shows editorial selection between lively and quieter compositions, separated character layers, color placement and finishing. It supplies evidence of image construction, not an independent explanation of a character's internal life. The Q&A says expressive deformation and permissible range differ by character; preferred expressions are creator preferences, and the wish to draw serious fighting Darkness is not a claim that she never fights seriously in prose. Armor-design difficulty is a creator's technical admission, not a character skill deficit.

This material helps explain why illustrated energy or a pinup pose should not simply be transferred into a sober behavioral simulation. A character model needs the incentive and situation that could generate an expression. It cannot treat an illustrator's favorite face, a commemorative pose or an unrealized rough as a performed canonical choice.

## 10. Model implications and limits

The supported Stage C constraints are narrower than a reconstruction revision: separate title/recognition rewards from objective skill; preserve Yunyun's adequate-force competence and relationship-sensitive vulnerability; allow Aqua to notice a meaningful cue without resolving it; retain Darkness's informational and corrective roles; and model Kazuma as a participant in the status game he diagnoses. These are attributable expanded-source candidates. They do not silently repair, erase or rescore a frozen forecast or R1 failure.

The ensemble cannot be reproduced by adding four isolated traits. The winning spell depends on providers who later become challengers. Correction can become assistance, assistance can create leverage, and a boast can change other people's incentives. The final liability exists because the party shares knowledge of both the title claim and the resource state. A transferred model should preserve that interactional causal chain while recalibrating institutional consequences and available technology.
