## 2. Architecture, chronology and the central change

The central recurring problem is an assumed role becoming more extensive than the person accepted. An unpaid helper claims a public leisure entitlement; a senior adventurer becomes an involuntary shield and unlimited payer; a professional volunteer is treated as having volunteered her partner; a one-wing demonic bargain becomes prolonged bodily confinement. Some assignments are freely adopted, some are imposed, some are only proposed, and some are refused. The difference determines the meaning of the cooperation.

Competence is correspondingly distributed. Aqua's paper work is precise, Yunyun reasons technically about rain, Darkness makes legitimate ethical and civic distinctions, Angie kills and harvests correctly, and Ignis supplies both tactical resources and his own body. Kazuma contributes real alternative means, but also withholds information and mistakes a useful adviser position for a right to humiliate. Megumin has useful knowledge outside Explosion and genuine destructive control while remaining capable of selfish concealment and costly misjudgment. Neither a successful campaign nor an incompetent moment provides a complete character description.

The author expressly calls the chronology loose and asks readers to place the stories near the latter part of the main series (U021P0006). Internal connections are firmer: the fifth story returns to the first's acting-lord problems and the fourth's Angie encounter; the fourth reports Vanir's return after a week away following the third's departure (U018P0097). These links do not establish an exact date against every main chapter. In particular, a locally stated romantic position must not be forced into an exact late-main agreement without independent chronology.

The second story changes narrator to the retired female DarkStalker. Her limited first-person access is a substantial formal change, not an invitation to treat every favorable interpretation of Kazuma's household as truth. The other four stories use Kazuma. The fifth's letter and the stories' reports remain mediated; the afterword cartoon is contributor paratext. Mentioned books and an abbreviated retold fairytale are subordinate narrative references within the parent paragraph counts, not separately completed novels.
