## 7. Complete new prose: incentives make a dungeon, and a household

*ダンジョンマスターは世知辛い* is a four-page causal story, not four detached jokes. Kazuma initially wants to remain at home while Aqua and Megumin play a card game. Aqua's low-cost kobold strategy frustrates Megumin; Darkness interrupts with a call to do actual work and spills the cards. The card's borrowed-money/dead-lich mechanic is a **game rule**, not an actual lich dying in the room. Kazuma offers replacement packs and future food while trying to let Darkness undertake the obligation. The group's home conflict supplies the first model of administrative labor that the dungeon story later mirrors.

At the guild the new dungeon is a tower. Kazuma recognizes a destructive opportunity for Megumin, but the guild insists that exploration and recovery occur before demolishing it. Megumin's excitement about a tower and promise to check for people are real speech; neither makes destruction already completed or establishes perfect judgment. Aqua declines through dungeon-related fear. Kazuma's claim that his own party is badly suited for normal dungeon work is domain-specific: Aqua is useful against undead, Megumin's blast expenditure can incapacitate her, and Darkness's strikes are unreliable. His criticism cannot be a blanket statement of their lack of ability.

Vanir then recruits Kazuma as adviser to a Medusa who wants to grow a dungeon. The offered reward is a succubus's serious dream service. Kazuma's eventual work is therefore not pure altruism, and accepting the inducement does not prove he actually consumed the reward. Medusa wants to begin with novice adventurers and develop her establishment. She is initially suspicious of the frail human; Kazuma leverages Vanir's prestige, becomes teacher-like, and takes pleasure in being recognized. The owner also lied about her own level. Her deference is a developing social role, not proof that every technical recommendation is wise.

The central reversal is institutional. Kazuma proposes effective ambush, fire, collapse and induced rivalry; the monsters regard some methods as dishonorable, while Vanir repeatedly marvels at a human exceeding demonic cruelty. A real ticket, a deadline and apparent scarcity turn adventurers against each other. It is not supernatural mind control. Kazuma explicitly explains why an uncertain chance and an expiring opportunity are enough to keep them searching and then withdraw. Medusa copies his advice; Vanir treats the quarrel as a meal of negative feeling. Same behavior, different institutional purposes: teacher's cleverness, owner's defense, devil's appetite and adventurer's tempting prize.

The short diary-like entries do not provide exact calendar dates. Over time invaders adapt, reputation spreads and stronger visitors arrive. Resources must be conserved deeper in the tower; trivial enemies can become free experience rather than useful defense. Yet excessive traps would deter all visitors, so the dungeon needs an attraction/defense balance. Kazuma's advice about toilets exposes the mundane infrastructure hidden beneath a heroic dungeon premise. The outcome punctures Kazuma's initial party diagnosis without claiming that the original quartet secretly has no constraints. Yunyun, Darkness, Chris and Wiz form a very strong combination. Kazuma recognizes their power despite his habitually unflattering framing. The owner, alarmed and attached to her teacher, asks not to be abandoned. Kazuma gives assurances and retreats; he does not demonstrably rescue her.

At the end the invaders turn toward the outside. The guild's Explosion warning orders evacuation, enormous magic is perceived, and Medusa appeals to the goddess. **A completed tower-destroying cast and Medusa's death or escape are not narrated.** Vanir speaks conditionally about his dungeon blowing away and promises an underground next attempt. Kazuma ends by hoping his disciple returns safely. In the mansion Aqua has been angry at his absence; Megumin and Darkness are being scolded about the tower affair and competition with Yunyun. The card game offers consolation, not a permanent moral repair. The administrator who wanted comfortable confinement learns that running a dungeon brings dependent people, troublesome duties and exposure to outside disaster, much like his household.

Kazuma's voice moves from listless avoidance to analytical instruction and pride under deference, then risk management and self-protective exit. Vanir's grand diction and Medusa's teacher address create a ceremonial frame for deliberately grubby advice. Their shock is not an omniscient moral verdict on all humans; it is a local inversion. ## 10. Language, speaker and narrator safeguards

The book contains at least five kinds of represented voice: selected main-story quotations; newly scripted editorial character bubbles; new prose narration and dialogue; original comic bubbles/internal monologue; and actual creator/interviewer speech. The same name or pronoun does not erase those planes. A quote from a profile may lack the trigger and response window needed for a local behavioral contract. A creator describing Kazuma's appeal is not Kazuma uttering a self-description. A reader's question about costume construction is not the wearer's own belief.

Kazuma's roughness can do several jobs: practical instruction to Medusa, familiar teasing, defensive justification, dominance, narrator aside and opportunistic performance for Luna. The adviser sequence contains real analytical content as well as demonic-comparison insults. Plain speech alone does not tell us which function dominates. Aqua's divine self-naming, femininely assertive endings, wounded demands and ordinary practical comments likewise cannot be sorted into one fixed competence or civility score. Megumin's polite endings can frame indignation or rivalry, while her theatrical vocabulary can be shared genre performance. Darkness's dignified declaration, corrective insistence on the whole party and volunteered fantasy are different speech acts.

Written form and image interact.
