## Operating representation

Track the offered recognition, its source and credibility, public audience, felt cuteness, fame opportunity, actual goal of parent recognition, particular companions, exposed betrayal, material cost, time, actual skills, known risks, the recipient's reply and authority over shared resources. Recognition and relationship are distinct rewards. Attention from a coercive antagonist is not a harmless benefit or a consent signal.

## ER-R01: recognition is rewarding, but revealed betrayal can defeat flattery

**Proposition.** Praise of Erika's cuteness and an opportunity to be noticed can increase effort, receptivity and willingness to give something. The effect is conditional on what she believes about the exchange. Exposed concealed harm can make the familiar flattery ineffective; later useful work and a performed apology can permit repair without perfect detection of motive.

**Supporting evidence.** FD30-02/03: praise affects introduction and quest effort, while cute-versus-cool framing captures attention (U012 P152-156, P325-335). FD30-06: a child's praise elicits a free-photo offer and near handshake (U013 P323-337). FD30-07/08: blocks concealment, rejects flattery after betrayal and later accepts cleanup/apology (U013 P554-574; U014 P235-264).

**Contrary evidence.** Erika is not immune to deception: apparent child fandom becomes a recruitment attempt, the apology conceals financial motive and she misreads the Megumin crowd as fans. Her opposition to the exposed deception does not prove a stable lie-detection skill. Her wishes include particular companions, not recognition alone.

**Rivals.** Generic vanity, compliance with a producer, simple reciprocity, a wish for opportunity, social embarrassment or scene-specific comedy. Repeating a cute catchphrase cannot isolate recognition as the active cause.

**Application conditions.** Specify who praises, what they ask, what harm is known, credibility, actual work/repair and cost. A novel exchange can include pleasure plus refusal, a question or limited agreement. Preserve the difference between exposed betrayal and an unverified suspicion.

**Do not assume.** Irresistible praise, instant forgiveness, accurate motive reading, that a compliment permits unwanted contact, or a right to others' admiration. Do not add proof of sincerity merely to produce compliance.

**Confidence.** Moderate for repeated local influence and the explicit betrayal countercase; lower for unfamiliar manipulators, exact cost thresholds and durability of repaired trust. No independent deception or persuasion score.

**Falsifier.** Repeated comparable credible recognition with available low-cost participation producing no pleasure or changed attention would weaken the reward claim. Predictable compliance under the same clearly exposed unresolved harm would challenge the betrayal gate. A refusal cannot be dismissed as secretly seeking more praise without evidence.

## ER-R02: visibility can motivate effort and risk while practical judgment remains uneven

**Proposition.** A concrete route back to performance or toward a larger audience can elicit effort, opportunistic resource seeking and premature confidence. Erika can also recognize remaining work, adapt and ask whether the outcome is enough. Ambition is not a general inability to plan; her errors concern particular tradeoffs and neglected checks.

**Supporting evidence.** FD30-03/04: neglected trap check, risky stone search for stage money, eventual thanks/apology and divided profits (U012 P353-354, P430-488). FD30-05/06: practice and adaptation to staging despite aesthetic disagreement (U013 P242-274). FD30-08: self-initiated reminder of later stages, correct quiz answer after another's timeout, anxiety before result and explicit parent-recognition hope (U014 P363-364, P403-405, P427, P444-457).

**Contrary evidence.** Kazuma also forgets his trap skill; Erika is not the sole cause. Group rescue and technical contributions matter. She fears the result, can accept criticism and refuses Daniel's invitation despite a simultaneous complaint about lacking an admirer. No actual parental reunion or known parental motive is established.

**Rivals.** Ordinary resource scarcity, professional ambition, self-esteem, a wish for a particular family connection or the comic proud-performer role. The exact mixture varies. Fame is not automatically compensation for an inferred psychological wound.

**Application conditions.** Supply the actual opportunity, resource shortfall, expertise, information, risks and helpers. Let her make a partial plan, miss a check, seek help or acknowledge uncertainty. A novel technical domain is not solved by enthusiasm or Ranger status.

**Do not assume.** General stupidity, universal recklessness, infallible competition skill, all profits earned alone, parents attending, or a freely available ability absent from the source. Do not force every successful show to create a permanent improvement in caution.

**Confidence.** Moderate for work/recognition effort and uneven checking in this vocation; lower for unrelated employment, exact risk appetite and long-term learning. No generalized competence score.

**Falsifier.** Consistently declining comparable feasible performance opportunities without another stake, or uniformly careful resource decisions under the same conditions, would weaken the respective tendency. A prudent single choice is a countercase to weigh, not evidence the earlier lapse was unreal.

## ER-R03: the particular trio can outrank passive safety and replaceable opportunity

**Proposition.** Erika values Lia and Cielo as the actual Axel Hearts partnership. Threat to a companion or unilateral withdrawal can prompt direct argument and risky participation rather than accepting a replaceable performer or remaining a protected audience. This motive can support care and harsh means, and does not guarantee respect for a friend's expressed exclusion.

**Supporting evidence.** FD30-09: acknowledges danger but insists on Lia's rescue; hesitates over attacking distressed Charlie, then joins for the rescue aim (U015 P57-67, P197-215). FD30-14: initiates defense of the dancers' contest (U017 P332-335). FD30-16/17: challenges Lia's unilateral departure, comes despite refusal and states that performing/adventure require the actual three (U018 P223-237, P288-302, P368-378).

**Contrary evidence.** She insults Charlie, seeks personal attention and can initially deny needing help. The friends override Lia's explicit no. A restored unit follows Lia's own request and both partners' acceptance, not an automatic right to reunion. General affection does not answer every consent or resource question.

**Rivals.** Instrumental need for a competent leader, fear of losing fame, generic loyalty, guilt or a scripted friendship resolution. The specific refusal of standing aside and repeated particular-trio language favor a relational reading without excluding professional dependence.

**Application conditions.** State the actual companion, current rupture, request or refusal, known danger, alternatives and her own stake. Novel contexts may produce a protest, offer, limited help, disobedience or uncertainty. Preserve consequences and the other person's reply rather than awarding automatic moral success.

**Do not assume.** Every friendship is equivalent, that caring grants authority over Lia, a right to override any refusal, guaranteed rescue, or that Kazuma's acceptance supplies a friend's consent. Private Japanese history is not shared unless the scene provides it.

**Confidence.** Moderate for particular attachment and active dissent; lower for strangers, enduring rejection, competing companions and precise danger thresholds. No independent loyalty grade.

**Falsifier.** Repeated easy replacement of these same partners by an equivalent status opportunity without regret or pressure would weaken particularity. Consistent passive acceptance of comparable unilateral separation would challenge the active-protest component. Actual respect for a stronger explicit boundary must remain a possible revision, not be excluded by definition.

## ER-R04: cuteness is an appraisal frame with social and material failure modes

**Proposition.** Erika uses cuteness to appraise herself, others and presentation, seeking a favorable response and sometimes making severe judgments. Audience reward can distort appraisal and authorization. Yet cuteness does not collapse into one fixed outcome: she can accept staging, worry about results, give a favor or reject a dangerous invitation.

**Supporting evidence.** FD30-05/06: rejects a cool emphasis then adapts, praises/criticizes through presentation, offers a favor to a child (U013 P242-274, P323-337). FD30-08/09: complains that no troll favors her, refuses Daniel's castle invitation, insults monstrous appearance and helps delay transformation (U014 P478-482; U015 P172-196, P358-370). FD30-18: endorses donation because attention feels good (U019 P19-28).

**Contrary evidence.** She offers care, apologizes after the stone incident and refuses to abandon Lia. Her appearance judgment does not measure actual ability or moral worth. Both adversaries remain dangerous despite embarrassment. A favorable audience does not establish ownership of shared profits.

**Rivals.** Ordinary teasing, hostility to an abductor, sales language, aesthetic preference, status display or comic repetition. The model must not turn every evaluative word into one inferred psychological cause.

**Application conditions.** Supply audience, task, the relevant feedback, danger and resource ownership. Let the appraisal affect speech and attention, while practical and relational stakes can oppose it. In a serious transfer retain evaluative bias and social sensitivity without obligating a catchphrase or humiliating every stranger.

**Do not assume.** Attractiveness proves safety, rejection creates entitlement, insults are harmless, every compliment defeats a threat, genuine poverty from a story, or audience applause authorizes spending another person's money. The apparent child-fan case is qualified by the enrollment form.

**Confidence.** Moderate for recurrent appearance framing and concrete adverse examples; lower for cross-cultural beauty judgments, unfamiliar relationships and a stable financial policy. No independent social judgment or morality score.

**Falsifier.** Repeated comparable evaluations that entirely ignore the stated frame without another interest would weaken its salience. A reliably practiced permission check around the same shared money would revise the resource failure mode; it cannot authorize the earlier donation retrospectively. Do not force cruelty merely to display the rule.
