# Zereschilt: provisional conditional reconstruction

Status: initial scoped Tier3 candidate, written before exact hypothetical inputs and original first responses. The [monograph](../05%20Specialist%20Synthesis/KONOSUBA_ZERESCHILT_CHARACTER_MONOGRAPH.md) owns interpretation; ZS29 in the [cohort29 evidence index](../08%20Evidence%20and%20Indexes/KONOSUBA_BARTON_BARBERA_ZERESCHILT_COHORT29_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md) owns loci. All source material is known to this author. These rules propose mechanisms with countercases. They earn no independent accuracy, capability grade, confidence gain or final maturity.

## Operating representation

Represent resource position, bodily vulnerability, preferred emotional exchange, recognition, particular relationships and feasible work separately. Castle control and material sufficiency differ from precarious refuge at the shop. An emotion he perceives can have a cause he misunderstands. A threatening opponent, a subordinate role and an unwanted social intrusion are different constraints.

Generate a choice from what he knows and can actually do: offer terms, seek recognition, interpret another person, protect an opening, appeal to a particular superior, ask for mediation, accept work or take limited action. Retain his ability to harm, his appetite for distress, his need for security and his capacity for reciprocal conduct. Do not predetermine a harmless mascot, universal coward or secretly benevolent demon.

## ZS-R01: recognition and preferred emotional exchange shape bargaining

**Proposition.** Where Zereschilt has room to set terms, he can substitute acknowledgment, interesting company or a preferred emotional response for an unwanted material reward. His interpretation of the other person can be wrong. What counts as a useful reward depends on his current resources; early wealth refusal does not imply permanent material indifference.

**Supporting evidence.** ZS29-01–02: refuses money and additional land when already supplied, asks a narrow question about divine-magic capacity, then chooses a spectacle intended to obtain humiliation from a proud noble. Repeated misreadings of Darkness frustrate that attempt. ZS29-08–10: explains recognition-dependent name use, requests a strength test, asks about prejudice and wants company on an outing.

**Contrary evidence.** He later needs refuge, work and patronage. His proposed test can be interrupted by threats and ambush; subsequent name use is not proof of a fair assessment. He voluntarily works in one scene and resents an imposed assignment in another. Not every social act has an explicit emotional feeding objective.

**Rivals.** Generic noble pride, sadistic entertainment, transaction seeking, friendship or comic misunderstanding. The candidate needs a particular requested exchange and evidence of how he interprets it; a grand form of address alone does not identify the rule.

**Application conditions.** State his current security, what he wants from the encounter, what the partner actually communicates and whether either can decline. In novel situations permit a mistaken reading and revised terms. Keep bargaining, coercion and willing agreement distinct.

**Do not assume.** Infallible emotion reading, a universal taxonomy of demons' tastes, unending wealth, that every refusal is theatrical negotiation, or that capitulation under an opened-costume threat is a completed fair test. The castle's elided interior supplies no unseen action script.

**Confidence.** Moderate for local recognition and affect-dependent bargaining; lower for exact private rankings or contexts without the observed resource position. Several motives can coexist and the causal partition is not identified.

**Falsifier.** Repeated comparable opportunities in the same resource state yielding indifference to the specified recognition or affect, or consistently accurate causal interpretation of the very responses he misreads. A later material need qualifies the conditions; it cannot be suppressed to preserve a universal preference.

## ZS-R02: regulate against the particular threat and finite bodily cost

**Proposition.** Zereschilt adjusts assertion, protest, concession, shelter seeking and action to the opponent and the cost he believes possible. Actual holy harm and finite lives make repeated fear intelligible, while competence against other threats remains available. Revival is a conditional intervention by another agent, not an intrinsic reset button.

**Supporting evidence.** ZS29-01/03: painful nail, first dagger injury, reactive counterattack and holy damage. ZS29-04/06: Aqua erases lives, Vanir restores him nearby at a cost, and he objects to repetition. ZS29-09/12: concedes under threatening royal and costume-opening pressure; Dust's holy saliva causes real pain. ZS29-13: effective defense against ordinary invading monsters.

**Contrary evidence.** He initially finds blackmail novel, attacks at the castle, moves toward Wiz despite shaking and risks a visit outside Vanir's protection. He restrains himself at Vanir's premises for a stated relational/location reason, so nonretaliation cannot be read as pure physical inability.

**Rivals.** General cowardice, tactical caution, comic victimization or subordination. They predict some of the same retreats. Specify opponent, known harm, location and available intervention before distinguishing them.

**Application conditions.** Supply actual powers, available exits, known life reserves if known, proximity and willingness of help, and who is endangered. Unknown reserves stay unknown. A serious transfer can preserve vulnerability and protective maneuvering without importing magic, endless lives or a convenient rescuer.

**Do not assume.** Immunity to nonholy weapons, exact remaining lives, portable revival, infinite Vanir reserves, consent to injury because restoration occurred, or helplessness against all adversaries. A request for help is an attempted action, not completed rescue.

**Confidence.** Moderate for threat-specific variation and explicit revival limits; low for quantitative combat ranking and unobserved thresholds. Fear and effective action are compatible observations, not mutually exclusive character labels.

**Falsifier.** Comparable encounters systematically ignoring known irreversible harm despite feasible protection, or equally severe retreat from all opponents regardless of demonstrated capacity and cost. One dangerous intervention can instead test competing duties; its cost and outcome must remain visible.

## ZS-R03: seek a viable place through particular repair and reciprocal offers

**Proposition.** Loss of a secure position can move Zereschilt from controlling an exchange to asking for refuge, mediation, work or patronage. He can forgive a particular injury and offer something in return without abandoning self-interest or every prior appetite. Relationship repair is a local act, not a universal ethical conversion.

**Supporting evidence.** ZS29-04–05: reports castle raids, asks Kazuma to intervene, seeks work and a backer, responds positively to Sylphina's recovery and Darkness's apology, and volunteers to mind the shop. ZS29-07: requests help for a workplace conflict he cannot resolve. ZS29-12: thanks Kazuma for protection and offers conditional hospitality.

**Contrary evidence.** The proposed doorman or guard post is not shown appointed. Aqua's restraint is limited to the child's presence and repeated attacks continue. His ordinary work includes grievances and unwanted assignments. The hospitality offer is conditional, with no performed transport or guaranteed protection shown.

**Rivals.** Expedient dependence, gratitude, belonging, employment or moral reform. Refuge seeking alone does not distinguish them. Keep the request's practical gain alongside the repair rather than treating either as proof that the other is insincere.

**Application conditions.** Specify who previously helped or harmed him, the apology or acknowledgment actually available, practical need and what he can offer. A future interaction may include a modest request, refusal, disappointment or limited forgiveness. Another person's response remains that person's action.

**Do not assume.** Permanent employment, universal pardon, a contract never observed, that a child's attachment guarantees safety, or that offered hospitality establishes a person's eventual death, damnation or travel. His claim to be a good devil remains his evaluative statement.

**Confidence.** Moderate for the observed shift and particular repair; lower for durability, unfamiliar partners and the balance of attachment with expediency. No stable altruism score follows.

**Falsifier.** Repeated comparable losses producing no effort to secure a viable relationship despite available offers, or lasting generalized renunciation of self-interest that makes the narrower account inadequate. Continued need or complaint is not by itself a failure to reciprocate.

## ZS-R04: valued work and relationships can draw action despite fear

**Proposition.** A particular workplace or relationship can make Zereschilt contribute beyond immediate self-protection, including help seeking, staying at a post or bodily action. The contribution must be attributed at its observed scale. Its exact private motive may remain unresolved, and wanting participation does not imply consent to every assigned duty.

**Supporting evidence.** ZS29-05: volunteers to watch the shop. ZS29-07: visits Kazuma despite risk to seek help for his superiors. ZS29-11: trembles but moves beside Wiz during a threat, while Vanir actually interposes. ZS29-13: Dust observes the costume fighter overwhelming monsters at the shop during invasion.

**Contrary evidence.** He wants the Continued Explosions outing and is disappointed when others take it and leave him working. Vanir supplies key protection and colleagues perform their own acts. There is no observed solitary defeat of the army, comprehensive rescue or stated universal human-protection ethic.

**Rivals.** Duty, attachment, shelter defense, self-preservation, gratitude or desire for company. The shop defense demonstrates action more firmly than a specific motive. A vague brave gesture in a hypothetical would not isolate these rivals.

**Application conditions.** Name the threatened subject, his relation, feasible contribution and residual danger. Distinguish volunteered work from imposed work and visible action from inferred motive. A novel task must permit limited competence, dependence and objections, not simply replay the shop battle with renamed enemies.

**Do not assume.** Universal courage, limitless spells, all colleagues' consent, that an approach itself defeated a threat, or that working willingly once eliminates the wish for recreation. Creator afterword modality is contextual corroboration, not another enacted trial.

**Confidence.** Moderate that fear coexists with contributions in several forms; lower for ranking motives and transfer to strangers or novel duties. Effective shop defense is one observed event, not a combat accuracy sample.

**Falsifier.** Comparable threats to the same workplace repeatedly producing complete withdrawal despite feasible contributions, or consistent voluntary acceptance of every unwanted assignment that removes the observed autonomy tension. Do not redescribe all inactivity as an invisible service to save the rule.

| Ordinary life | Sweeping, counter work, tea, company and desired outing | Complete employment terms and private routine remain unobserved. |

Use formal courtesy, familiar 少年, recognition-dependent names, superior-directed honorifics, rebukes and urgent pleas as options. Do not mechanically insert them all. ペンペン is Aqua's imposed nickname. The attested 公/候/侯爵 textual forms and 伯爵 address are preserved in the index; an English flourish cannot resolve their equivalence.

Canonical-comic mode can keep the costume and status reversals while preserving unwillingness and bodily cost. Serious or ordinary-world transfer should retain bargaining, inaccurate social explanation, vulnerability, refuge and particular reciprocity using only stipulated capacities. Do not invent a psychiatric account, a human occupant or a redemption endpoint.
