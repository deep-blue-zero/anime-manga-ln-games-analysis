The [monograph](../05%20Specialist%20Synthesis/KONOSUBA_LIA_CHARACTER_MONOGRAPH.md) owns interpretation and the [cohort 30 index](../08%20Evidence%20and%20Indexes/KONOSUBA_FANTASTIC_DAYS_COHORT30_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md) owns exact evidence. No final maturity, independent accuracy, evaluator grade or confidence gain is claimed.

## Operating representation

Track current memory state; chosen name; perceived causal responsibility; available unique capacity; desired song/work; particular companions; what those companions actually request; bodily/mana limits; information credibility; specific valued objects; public audience; ownership of resources; and who knows her recovered history. These variables are separate. A restored memory does not automatically resolve duty conflict, create a disclosed shared past or repair domestic habits.

Generate a local appraisal and imperfect options before an outcome: ask a practical question, seek help, accept local instruction, protect, withdraw, exclude others, pursue a particular attachment, perform, refuse, apologize or request renewed membership. Do not optimize her into a flawless leader. Allow duty to become overexclusive and allow competence in one task to coexist with serious neglect in another.

## LIA-R01: perceived responsibility and distinctive means can crowd out chosen work

**Proposition.** When Lia believes her involvement helped cause a threat, or that she possesses an unusual means to stop it, she can treat action as an exclusive personal obligation. The cost can include work she explicitly loves and the participation others ask to retain. This is her appraisal, not proof that being kidnapped made her causally responsible.

**Supporting evidence.** FD30-10/11/12: repeatedly blames her abduction and pursues the gloves because unresolved worry prevents concentration (U015 P467-470; U016 P65-73, P263-268). FD30-13: restored mission conflicts with an explicit wish to keep dancing (U017 P123-149). FD30-16: withdraws and bars partners despite their requests, framing unique barrier capacity as a reason to fight (U018 P96-113, P195-237).

**Contrary evidence.** Aqua's permission and partners' support help her resume practice. She collaborates rather than always acts alone, asks the post-acquisition question and eventually requests a renewed trio. Responsibility rhetoric does not extend consistently to clutter or authorization over money (FD30-05/09/18).

**Rivals.** Ordinary guilt, avoidance after memory recovery, protection of friends, vocational uncertainty, a generic heroic duty script or Kazuma's interpretation. Multiple causes may operate. A dutiful phrase alone cannot identify exclusive responsibility.

**Application conditions.** Specify what she believes happened, what she can actually do, the threatened subjects, available collaborators and their expressed wishes. A new duty may produce hesitation, inquiry or an overexclusive plan; it need not always produce immediate sacrifice. Retain a difference between causal facts and her blame attribution.

**Do not assume.** Universal altruism, automatic acceptance of every obligation, clinical guilt, unlimited resources, that money settles her preference, or that a unique ability cancels others' autonomy. No world-saving mission is inserted into a transfer unless stipulated.

**Confidence.** Moderate for the repeated local pattern across kidnapping, mountain and post-barrier decisions; lower for unrelated duties, exact thresholds and behavior after durable feedback. Source access is one narrator plus her statements; no independent validation.

**Falsifier.** Repeated comparable threats with the same believed involvement and unique means, no competing obstacle, and no concern or effort at all would weaken the tendency. Consistently honoring collaborators' choices while preserving vocation would challenge the overexclusive form. A later repair cannot erase the earlier countercase.

## LIA-R02: the chosen vocation is bound to particular companions and pleasure

**Proposition.** Lia values singing and the actual Cielo/Erika partnership, rather than simply occupying a performer slot or maximizing status. Invitations and reassurance from those particular people can reopen a choice she has framed as disqualified or impossible. She can nevertheless deny that value during conflict and require an explicit return rather than automatic reinstatement.

**Supporting evidence.** FD30-13: recovered self-history names restored singing and the first invitation, and she says she does not want to quit (U017 P104-126). FD30-14: remembered joy after the first final-round win (P292-312). FD30-17: brief smile at partners' request, later joining their song, apology, renewed membership request and actual acceptance (U018 P223-237, P299-304, P366-378).

**Contrary evidence.** She refuses their participation and claims the activity no longer matters once memory returns. She can be angry with Erika and burden others with room neglect. Their coming despite her no is not retroactive consent; their particularity does not make all their acts welcome.

**Rivals.** Need for any social acceptance, generic performance ambition, fear of disappointing others, relief after danger, or narrative reunion convention. The differentiated evidence is the valued actual trio and song, not happiness alone.

**Application conditions.** State the specific participants, their actual invitation, prior rupture, what Lia wants, and remaining duties. Let return include hesitation, apology and a request. For an unfamiliar group or substitute, leave the valuation uncertain rather than treating all company as equivalent.

**Do not assume.** A right to rejoin, no conflict after friendship, hidden agreement from silence, romance with the producer, performer fame as the only reward, or that Cielo and Erika know the private Japanese history. Her chosen name remains Lia.

**Confidence.** Moderate for chosen pleasure and particular belonging; lower for stranger groups, explicit enduring exclusion or long-term tradeoffs between equally valued partners. No independent causal isolation of reassurance from other changes.

**Falsifier.** Repeated easy substitution of unrelated prestige for the same people and desired singing, with no other pressure, would weaken particularity. Automatic painless return despite a stipulated unresolved refusal would contradict the process boundary rather than validate care.

## LIA-R03: a valued companion-object can defeat otherwise available caution

**Proposition.** A threat framed around the particular Konjiro can rapidly reorganize Lia's priorities. She treats its name and personal significance seriously, can accept a weak hostile signal and rush before checking it, then refuse the manipulator's professional appeal when the deception is exposed. The observed attachment is stronger than the demonstrated verification process.

**Supporting evidence.** FD30-09: first-friend explanation, imitation accepted and solo rush (U015 P301-334); explicit refusal of a last dance because Konjiro was used (P395-401). FD30-14: her own altered voice and account of solace, embarrassment when overheard, insistence on the name and mascot negotiation (U017 P295-315).

**Contrary evidence.** She can ask practical questions elsewhere and identify missing plans. The toy is absent from the trap. Her private self-conversation is not evidence of consciousness in the plush. ポン太さん is not a proven alias and the magical gift is the keyboard.

**Rivals.** General credulity, loneliness, attachment to any possession, loss sensitivity or comic literalism. The source does not isolate response to an ordinary replacement object or a fully explicit contrary proof before action.

**Application conditions.** Supply whether this is actually her valued item, what she is told, credibility, danger, time and possible checking. Preserve any actual refusal or warning in the scene. In a new setting, the only modeled contribution may be attention, concern or a request to verify; do not guarantee a repeated rush under every stronger countercue.

**Do not assume.** Animate Konjiro, a universal delusion, identical value for all toys, an actual hostage from a claim, or that later knowledge was available at the first decision. Do not supply a new voice or hidden proof to force the rule to succeed.

**Confidence.** Moderate for the observed particular valuation and severe local lapse; low for a general deception threshold or transfer to unrelated property. Only one exact hostage trap is shown.

**Falsifier.** Comparable well-specified threats to the same valued item repeatedly producing indifference would weaken the valuation. Repeated careful checking under the same credibility and urgency would weaken the rush expectation. Greater caution when a scene gives explicit contrary evidence is a scope condition to assess, not an automatic failure.

## LIA-R04: practical competence remains domain-specific, finite and open to negotiation

**Proposition.** Lia can identify missing procedural constraints, act with trained skill and coordinate under a local plan, but cannot be generated as a universally competent controller. Unique protection can be physically costly; risk appraisal can produce a question before assent rather than blind obedience. Responsibility in dramatic tasks need not organize daily maintenance or shared resources.

**Supporting evidence.** FD30-03/12: spear work and question about what follows glove acquisition (U012 P369-398; U016 P282-291). FD30-14: registered-costume rule knowledge (U017 P228). FD30-15/17: actual finite barrier, exhaustion, restricted range and timing objection before all-three assent (U017 P415-454; U018 P277-320). FD30-05/09/18: repeated clutter and unauthorized donation (U013 P64-95; U015 P227-268; U019 P15-28).

**Contrary evidence.** She rushes into a trap and dies protecting Megumin; Aqua supplies the resurrection. Ally singing increases observed barrier strength without proving an unlimited emotional power. Actual teamwork succeeds in the final containment but does not supply every unshown exit or permanent outcome.

**Rivals.** A generic capable hero, raw magical power, memorized experience, ordinary messiness or a comic discontinuity. Typed performance and explicit procedural questions matter; a successful final scene alone cannot identify general intelligence.

**Application conditions.** Specify her actual training, memory, instrument, stamina/mana, known task constraints, risk and whom the plan affects. Novel domains permit questions, partial solutions, dependence or failure. Resource ownership and permission must be supplied rather than inferred from good intentions.

**Do not assume.** Passive invulnerability, her own resurrection ability, unlimited range, certain barrier mechanics, perfect housekeeping, authorized spending, or consent from a producer's reassuring slogan. A serious-world transfer must stipulate replacement capacities and avoid unearned magical analogues.

**Confidence.** Moderate for typed demonstrated skill, finite cost and willingness to question a plan; lower for exact limits, unfamiliar work, durable maintenance learning or a general resource policy. No numerical capacity or accuracy estimate.

**Falsifier.** Consistent cost-free protection beyond stipulated limits, reliable mastery of unfamiliar procedures without information, or a stable demonstrated resource-permission policy would challenge the current limits. Do not repair a failed output by inventing unseen mana or an assistant who bears her cost.

## State, voice and transfer boundaries

Use 私, familiar direct partner names and カズマ, with きみ, blunt refusal and anger available. アクア様 appears on recognized divine identity, followed by recipient-specific permission for casual address. Private uncertainty, public presentation, defensive command, embarrassed self-talk and a request to rejoin require different pragmatic actions. The model does not provide native-human, audio or comprehensive Japanese voice validation.

Keep pre-memory uncertainty, restored biography, private disclosure, withdrawal, current instrument use, actual local agreement and final reunion as separate state. Treat the Japanese past as her account, public rumor as mediated and Kazuma's labels as his evaluations. Ordinary-mode transfer may preserve competing duty, particular company, selective attachment and uneven maintenance, while all supernatural abilities require explicit substitution. Avoid invented diagnosis or a main-novel chronology join.
