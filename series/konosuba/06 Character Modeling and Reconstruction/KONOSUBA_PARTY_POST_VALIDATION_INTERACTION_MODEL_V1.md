# Party interaction: post-validation reconstruction V1

## 1. Authority and source boundary

This distinct Stage D model derives from the [expanded ensemble synthesis](../05%20Specialist%20Synthesis/KONOSUBA_EXPANDED_PARTY_ENSEMBLE_SYNTHESIS_V1.md) and [extended synthesis](../10%20Expanded%20Corpus/KONOSUBA_EXTENDED_CORPUS_SYNTHESIS.md). The [MAIN_ONLY_R1 party body](KONOSUBA_PARTY_INTERACTION_MODEL.md), exact eight forecasts, firsts and scores remain unchanged. Other narrators, game-derived fiction, tutorial scripts and TRPG branches retain separate authority. Current acceptance, diagnostic battery and exact publication are distinct records under the suite and current map.

## 2. Reconstruction purpose

Produce a bounded sequence of interactions among explicitly present participants. The state fixes each actor's chronology, knowledge, goals, body, resources, accepted scope, relationship and opportunity. Represent the task as dependencies: who can supply what, who knows the need, who agrees, who acts, what result is observed and what remains. A participant is not a role token guaranteed to obey. Stop when an unmodeled outsider's response or unperformed phase is needed.

## 3. Collective identity

Shared household and adventure history can make particular company valuable without one shared mind or a permanent equal commitment. The quartet can argue, leave, return under constraint, choose quiet ordinary life or recruit other people. Dust's party, shop workers and FD's coalition are comparisons, not interchangeable memberships. Do not infer a final relationship settlement or a universal quartet identity from one successful group act.

## 4. Stable interaction tendencies

Complementary capacities, reciprocal conflict, uneven recognition, particular attachment and mixed motives recur. Kazuma can organize useful phases, yet Chris, Luna, other providers or another member can initiate work. A group success can coexist with an actor's harmful or contrary choice. Seriousness can align some aims while intensifying another person's dangerous identity commitment. The model must retain both cooperation and selective failure.

## 5. Shared and private state

Maintain an actor-by-fact map: observation, received report, inference, mistaken belief, private premise and unknown. Familiarity can support an explicitly stated inference but not omniscience. A hidden objective does not become common knowledge because the group acts compatibly. Track current mana, equipment, fatigue, range, institutions and each person's authority separately. Chronological uncertainty limits the comparison rather than granting a favorable terminal state.

## 6. Goals

For each participant, state the current practical end and relevant private reward: ease, praise, specialty, protection, belonging, autonomy or material gain. A shared objective does not eliminate a credit contest or a provider's wish to leave. A score can reward visible output while undervaluing protection. An uninformed person's useful action may solve a different task in their own appraisal. Neither outcome proves that every actor accepted the organizer's complete goal.

## 7. Attention and information flow

Identify which person receives each warning, refusal, request, offer, result and new cost. A warning heard after initiation cannot inhibit that initiation. A later cue can still change continuation or recovery. Speaker and addressee matter; an untagged line remains unassigned. Institutional mediation, a supplier's terms or a newcomer's revised impression can shift who holds useful information without making that actor an omniscient coordinator.

## 8. Appraisal and permission

Compute each person's appraisal independently. Practical usefulness, friendship, arousal, feared loss, tolerance and agreement are separate variables. A still-unwanted act can be endured to protect another priority; that is not consent or incapacity by default. An apparently helpful imposed start remains imposed. A later freely accepted step changes permission for that step, not the earlier one. Wrongdoing can be modeled faithfully without being represented as an agreed handoff.

## 9. Reward and cost allocation

Track who receives credit, money, pleasure or safety and who supplies time, bodily risk, mana, property or responsibility. Do not distribute one pooled cost equally by assumption. A successful performance can leave unpaid fees, concealed money and an uncompleted promise to pay. Shared gratitude may coexist with one person's complaint or refused work. Material accounting and relationship appraisal remain distinct.

## 10. Error generators

Recurring failures include goal divergence, hidden premises, metric gaming, discounted refusal, missing provider acceptance, overconfidence in another's capacity, a successful first stage mistaken for completion and flattering credit assignment. No single person's foolishness explains every failed chain. The simulator itself can err by supplying a missing edge, selecting whichever actor fits a forecast after seeing the result or counting group success as absolution for a contrary individual act.

## 11. Competence and provider graph

Represent capacities as conditional edges, not a ranked intelligence list. A provider needs actual skill, access, readiness, information and willingness. Leen's regulation, Taylor's defense, Keith's sight/cover, Small's route knowledge and the child's assent in Dust are distinct contributions. Wiz's magic, Vanir's intervention, Lia's role and a supplier's tools likewise retain ownership and limits. A provider can refuse, charge or fail; their presence does not promise every later recovery.

## 12. Affect and interaction transitions

Praise can recruit work and competition; a joke can expose a withheld cost; an ignored request can lead to renewed protest or unwanted quiet; danger can invite protection or intensify risky resolve. State whose affect changes and what they received. A quiet person's dislike can remain. A later outburst after a new insult does not retroactively prove either prior enjoyment or absence of an earlier response opportunity.

## 13. Operation phase graph

Separate request, understanding, adoption, preparation, execution, immediate result, extraction, treatment, restitution and settlement. Local success does not close every edge. Ask who receives a remaining-dependency cue and has an opportunity to act, then record their actual attempt or omission. A stalled operation with correct costs is typed-consistent but does not itself fulfill a behavioral prediction that someone requests recovery.

## 14. Interaction procedure

Initialize independent states; fix the acting set and each received fact; identify the next task edge; generate a request, refusal, independent action or escalation from each material goal; update only facts actually communicated or observed; retain costs and unresolved outsider choices. A bounded next exchange can end with conflict. Do not force a harmonious solution or require Kazuma to approve every useful action.

## 15. Social scripts

Use practical requests, corrections, complaint/bargaining, status contest, teasing, vulnerable acknowledgment, explicit refusal, ordinary invitation and silent tolerated imposition as different scripts. Who corrects whom can change within a sequence. A useful question is not automatically the straight-person role; a practical speech act can be part of a harmful plan. A comic ritual does not settle consent to a new act.

## 16. Relationship variants

Particular dyads have their own trust, grievance, private history and permission. Kazuma/Aqua work and friction differ from Kazuma/Megumin negotiated intimacy, Darkness's protective/peer wishes or Chris's independent correction. Dust/Leen/Small relations do not inherit quartet scripts. Newcomer tutorial partners and optional TRPG samples are not aliases of familiar people. An outsider's competence and independent agenda remain part of their contribution.

## 17. Moral and seriousness boundaries

Serious harm does not synchronize the group into one ethical actor. One member can plead for restraint while another presses an unsafe course. Protective intent may coexist with coercion, and a recipient may remain quiet for a different protective priority. Preserve the exact individual choice even when another participant performs the group-level predicted act. Known injury and death do not become harmless because the sequence is comic or a later remedy exists.

## 18. Ordinary shared life

Generate meals, work, errands, games, leisure, expenses, chores, familiar disagreement and neutral cooperation as well as combat. Particular company can matter after practical ease is available elsewhere. A temporary arrangement is not automatically a durable replacement, and one argument is not permanent household collapse. The group can complete a small task without every member speaking or displaying a signature flaw.

## 19. Japanese interaction grammar

Assign each utterance to its actor, addressee and present purpose. Preserve Kazuma's practical/direct options, Aqua's expressive or task-fitting turns, Megumin's polite/technical/performance range and Darkness's practical/vulnerable/forceful variants. Do not mechanically rotate catchphrases or fix permanent fool/corrector roles. Silence is an observed behavior with limited interpretation, not an omitted assent line. Generated wording has no acoustic or native-human certification.

## 20. Humor envelope

Escalation can arise when individually intelligible goals interact badly, a metric misaligns reward, a solution transfers cost or authority changes owner. Keep ordinary competence and serious consequences. A farcical mode may amplify expression while leaving literal ownership, injury and refusal intact. Do not force a punchline, an equal share of blame or a free collective reset.

## 21. Canonical-comic mode

Retain actual source powers, known resource constraints, familiar aggression and appropriate comic intensity. Every magic-dependent edge still needs a supplied ability/provider at the chosen slice. An untold plan does not become known from genre familiarity, and an offered shield is not guaranteed safety. The actor who supplies the remedy owns that contribution.

## 22. Cross-context transfer mode

Recompute physics, institutions, risk, communication and work. Preserve independent goals, grievance, specialty and particular company rather than converting the quartet into generic cooperative colleagues. Absent magic can remove a role edge; it does not erase the person's aims. A shared modern setting does not supply new credentials or legal authority. A harmful attempt can remain plausible under ordinary consequences without being represented as successful agreed coordination.

## 23. Historical prediction responsibility

All prior group and individual forecasts, main-only firsts, four holdout score files and blanks remain exact. Gen03's tears brake stays retired; Gen05 J01's failure and D01C/M05AMB/M06P keep their scope. A typed consistency success is not another behavioral confirmation. New source-known models create no unseen Gen06 and cannot use a group-level pass to erase an actor-level adverse.

## 25. Stage D working commitments

Fix actor sets, facts, competing ends and adequate opportunity before first response. Score behavioral direction, typed integrity, execution and missingness separately.

- **SD1-E01, accepted fitted handoff:** Given a concrete next edge, explicitly accepted roles, actual compatible resources and no prebound changed goal, expect the named participants to attempt their fitted parts. Refusing or substituting unrelated isolated actions is locally adverse. Actual success is separate. MODERATE; R1-E01 and expanded provider sequences.
- **SD1-E02, tempting bad metric:** Given an independently defined metric that rewards visible output while undercrediting enabling work, preidentified tempted actors and opportunity, expect at least one of those actors to pursue the distorted reward or create interpersonal competition before correction. None doing so is group-level adverse; each actor's opposite remains individually visible. MODERATE to LOW; R1-E02 and expanded reputation/accounting evidence.
- **SD1-E03, unwanted quiet:** Do not predict objection merely from still-unwanted conduct. When an independently fixed higher-priority aim makes protest costly, quiet or yielding is a possible supported branch; the precise response selector remains OPEN. Preserve dislike and absent permission regardless. This is an uncertainty/typed agency contract, excluded from directional-success totals. DUST07's old E06 failure is retained.
- **SD1-E04, recovery cue:** Given an identified received cue to a real remaining dependency and a fixed capable actor set with opportunity, expect at least one dependency-directed request, proposal or attempt absent a prebound competing constraint. Everyone ignoring it is adverse. A correctly stalled trace alone is insufficient. MODERATE; R1-E03 and FD/Dust phase evidence.
- **SD1-E05, outside initiation:** Given a fixed feasible phase, accepted available outside provision and no actual Kazuma dependency, expect a named provider or participant to initiate a useful attempt without waiting solely for him. No attempt is adverse; full replacement campaign success is not predicted. MODERATE; R1-E07, MASK/Dust/FD and source-qualified Core mediation.
- **SD1-E06, serious divergence:** Given preidentified identity-committed actors, a tempting dangerous course, incomplete reassurance and separately informed corrective actors, expect some initial persistence and a distinct correction/alternative attempt. Either missing branch is adverse at that branch. Immediate unanimous safety is adverse to this bounded risk expectation. LOW to MODERATE; R1-E08/MAIN17 and expanded risk/provider comparisons.

## 26. Open areas

The priority between quiet and protest, exact durable membership decisions, leadership under unfamiliar institutions and thresholds for credit competition remain unresolved. Source-backed actors can choose differently under similar-looking premises; name the differing known state or acknowledge uncertainty. Do not use a disjunctive account to claim both directions were predicted. No calibrated misconduct prevalence or fully tested franchise-wide ensemble law is claimed.

## 28. Simulator failures and limits

Reject a shared mind, mandatory Kazuma organization, free provider labor, unshown assent, safety from survival, automatic synchronized seriousness, equal blame and a forced happy ending. Reject a trace that states all constraints correctly while claiming an unperformed behavioral handoff succeeded. Preserve actual first responses and all later causal/voice defects separately. Authorship does not complete the battery, independent evaluation, protected integration or release.
