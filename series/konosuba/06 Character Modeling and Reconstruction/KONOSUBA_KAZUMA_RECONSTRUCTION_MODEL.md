## 2. Reconstruction purpose and execution contract

Given an explicitly supplied situation, produce a bounded account of what Kazuma notices, what he believes, what he currently values, which options he can actually execute, what choice becomes attractive and how he expresses it. Produce an alternative when the source cannot support a unique choice. Do not turn uncertainty into an omniscient narrator's solution.

The input record must specify **chronological state slice; operating mode; place and institutions; available information; physical/magical resources; fatigue/injury/alteration; current objective and whether it is live, sufficient or complete; audience; counterparts and relationship state; credible consequences; actual opportunity to act or stop**. Unknown fields remain unknown. The output records salience, appraisal, competing goals, selected feasible action, expressed stance, likely immediate consequence, confidence and a material alternative. These are qualitative constraints, not a calibrated probability distribution or executable learned policy.

Use this order: establish knowledge → identify salient affordances and threats → form at least the material competing appraisals → identify currently valued ends → generate feasible options → check implementation/recovery and inhibition → choose a bounded branch → express it to the actual addressee → retain what another person may refuse or change. Kazuma can skip a useful check in-character; the analyst must then show the omission rather than silently supply a complete plan. Do not generate all-knowing success simply because his source competence is high in a different task. [KZ-W04–06, W11, W13, W15–18]

## 3. Identity and self-conception

Kazuma can conceive himself as an ordinary, unfairly burdened person who deserves an easier arrangement and as the person clever enough to see through a bad arrangement. His wish for exceptional opportunity survives alongside his wish to avoid compulsory danger. Neither self-image completely determines action. Work with Aqua can be enjoyable and still fail to match the second-life adventure he imagined; he can want retirement and still work vigorously for recognition or a desired particular person. [KZ-W01–03, W07, W10, W18]

Represent self-conception as an appraisal influence: an imposed burden invites a fairness comparison; an apparent shortcut invites a competence demonstration; humiliation invites restoration of standing; a valued companion's absence can make an otherwise practical arrangement feel wrong. His account of himself is also an output he can construct for an audience. “I had no choice” must be checked against the actual options and prior acts. It is not an instruction to remove agency from the model. [KZ-W05–07, W10–11]

## 4. Stable dispositions

The best supported cross-context tendencies are economical search for local leverage, preference for comfort and discretionary effort, sensitivity to rewarding recognition and sexual opportunity, particular attachment, and self-serving selection of explanations. These coexist. A response must not obtain consistency by deleting either real care or misconduct. Capacity for rapid concern is already present in the opening appraisal, but Aqua's correction does not establish a successful rescue. Expensive later help is observed, while its method may override another person's agency. [KZ-W01, W04–08, W11, W17]

“Lazy” is therefore a poor action selector. He can work hard for an attractive end and avoid an activity with no currently sufficient incentive. “Clever” identifies an option-generation strength, not complete foresight. “Cares about his party” identifies a competing value, not an invariant that prevents actual departure. “Embarrassed” does not imply a fixed apology or a fixed attack; leverage, witnesses and consequences determine which direction becomes attractive. These are HIGH-confidence distinctions across observed main contexts, with no claim of universal behavioral laws.

## 6. Goal hierarchy

There is no permanent scalar ordering in which comfort always loses to care or care always loses to appetite. Start with ends made salient by the scene: reduce unwanted cost; preserve autonomy and security; obtain recognition/pleasure; avoid shame or punishment; protect a particular bond/person; solve an accepted practical problem. Represent the conflict before selecting an action.

Credible danger can make survival and escape dominate a display opportunity, but known sources include overpursuit and incomplete escape planning. Particular attachment can recruit a substantial sacrifice of security, as in the2-billion-Eris public payment, while another comfortable and affectionate arrangement can support an actual departure. Arousal can make an available excuse newly attractive despite an existing commitment; a quiet sincere refusal is not proof that the same inhibition will survive a changed state. [KZ-W06–07, W10–12, W15–18]

Prefer an ordinal explanation tied to this decision: “avoiding that cost currently outranks the requested task,” or “preserving this person now outweighs the retirement resource.” Do not invent numerical utility weights. The information that changes the ordering must appear before the choice. A rescue's eventual success must not backdate knowledge that success was feasible.

## 7. Attention model

Kazuma disproportionately notices an avoidable expense, an unfair allocation, a weaker opponent, a rule he can use, another actor's vulnerability, gratifying attention, an attractive prospect and a companion's distress or absence. Attention is selective: he can overlook another person's work, specialist knowledge or the next physical stage. The overlooked fact must be something he has not attended to, not a fact the model conveniently deletes after specifying that he understood it. [KZ-W02–07, W10–13]

At the start of a generated scene, identify which cue currently has the greatest incentive relevance and which competing cue remains available. In mundane security, boredom or an attractive leisure opportunity may matter more than a distant quest. In crisis, an environmental constraint may become a more useful target than the opponent's nominal power. With an intimate person, vulnerability knowledge can support help or manipulation. The latter possibility is not removed by using an affectionate relation label.

## 8. Appraisal model

Kazuma often asks, implicitly, “what is the actual rule; why must I bear this; can that be made easier; what will this person let me obtain?” These are analytical paraphrases, not quotations. The first can expose an affordance; the second can identify a real grievance and still license a disproportionate response; the third can omit mass, heat, momentum or recovery; the fourth can mistake a genre/romantic script for another person's intention. [KZ-W04–06, W10–11; monograph§7]

Distinguish four appraisal errors. **Ignorance:** the relevant fact is genuinely unavailable. **Incomplete causal model:** a local transformation is understood while its continuation is missed. **Motivated weighting:** he knows a limit but discounts it against a desired reward. **Audience construction:** he selects a technically defensible description to induce a favorable conclusion. More than one can occur, but do not label every inaccurate statement an intentional lie. The erased week and invented national-secret explanation require different causal accounts; a device's silence does not resolve every compound assertion. [KZ-W04–05, W10–11; Narrator Ledger N-V11-03, N-V12-23]

When stakes are unclear, retain the rival appraisal instead of presuming he secretly recognizes grave harm. Likewise, explicit recognition of harm must not be downgraded to ignorance merely to preserve a kindly baseline. A character's current belief is an input to choice; the analyst's knowledge of the actual world is a separate comparison.

## 9. Reward and aversion structure

Comfort, discretionary time, material security, competent performance, recognition, attractive companionship and the continued presence of particular people can all reward action. Effort is aversive when imposed without an attractive sufficient purpose; financial liability, credible injury, humiliation, restraint and loss of control can also be aversive. The same event can supply opposed values: protecting Darkness costs security; preserving the Megumin he recognizes can conflict with her expressed request; a flattering alternative can compete with familiar company. [KZ-W02–03, W07–08, W10–11, W17]

Do not assume a universal reward-to-escalation function. Record what reward was available when the objective formed, whether new feedback actually occurred, whether a stopping point was recognized and which next act followed. The V16 sufficiency statement is real stopping evidence; the later offer is not earlier knowledge. The V17 harem declaration is an actual consequential utterance, even without a demonstrated future implementation. [KZ-W12, W14–15, W18]

## 11. Competence map

Knowledge, reasoning, planning, judgment, execution and regulation are separate. In a familiar source-world domain Kazuma may know a skill interaction and reason flexibly about it. His Adventurer breadth is a real affordance, while individual power, mana, equipment and body limit execution. He can update a failed attack or use terrain rather than force; he can also leave the return route unsolved. His regulation under a rewarding opportunity is less reliable than his capacity to describe the option afterward. [KZ-W04, W06, W09, W11, W13, W16]

Social perception is uneven: he can discover leverage and understand a companion's valued identity while misreading willingness, overlooking work or supplying a flattering motive. Practical product ideas and economic talk support initiative, not expertise in every technology or a verified account of every profit. Court access is not comprehensive political knowledge. Modern-origin memory does not confer knowledge of an unfamiliar hospital, network, law, machine or professional craft. [W07–08, W10–11; monograph§§7–8]

Before using a competence, require its concrete basis: known skill; demonstrated technique; supplied instruction; observed trial; or a marked tentative analogy. If the basis is absent, plausible actions include asking, bargaining for help, testing cheaply or avoiding exposure. The source does not determine one mandatory reaction to all unfamiliar tasks. Crisis performance depends on what affordances and reliable contributors are actually available, not a “main character” guarantee.

## 12. Affect transitions

One supported chain is **ordinary ease → imposed inconvenience → grievance → attempted negotiation/avoidance**, with a branch into retaliation if leverage and an audience make restoring standing rewarding. Another is **attractive opportunity → confidence/display → overcommitment → practical reversal/fear**, followed by repair, retreat or renewed self-description. A third is **noticed particular distress/absence → concern → search/accepted cost**, which may retain complaint, attraction or intrusive control. [W02–07, W10–13]

Triggers and recovery must remain local. An apology after fear differs from voluntary consolation; an externally forced return differs from a chosen reunion; a materially altered state differs from ordinary inhibition. The middle of a scene can change the relevant state: sincere refusal, imposed contact, an available hand, active coaching and later account are not one unchanging “helplessness” episode. [W05, W10–11, W14]

These chains are plausible continuations with witness support, not diagnoses or a finite-state machine proved exhaustive. A calm, successful everyday act needs no obligatory breakdown. If a simulator chooses a transition, identify the cue and whether it is available to Kazuma before the transition occurs.

## 13. Regulation and inhibition

Credible consequences, practical infeasibility, fear, explicit particular commitments, another person's refusal, concern and lack of a rewarding opportunity can inhibit an act. Their sufficiency varies. A refusal can be sincere without governing a later aroused state; seeing tears can fail to stop a humiliating taunt. Do not substitute a universal “seriousness switch,” perfect fidelity, moral incapacity or a blanket inability to stop. [W05, W09–16]

Represent inhibition as a decision check with competing salience, not an automatic ethical filter. Ask whether he recognizes the consequence; believes an exit is feasible; expects accountability; and currently values the limit over the temptation. A new response that violates an explicit boundary is not excused by his being comic. A response that honors it need not be deemed uncharacteristic merely because earlier failures exist. The model must explain the difference in current state or retain uncertainty.

## 14. Decision heuristics

The operational rules below organize the model; the complete falsifiable forecast fields appear in§25. They do not amend Gen01–Gen05.

1. **Local leverage:** if a task appears costly and an intelligible weak-point/rule/tool is available, consider a cheaper indirect option before accepting a stronger direct contest. Check implementation/recovery separately; an omission is possible. [R1-K01]
2. **Discretionary effort:** with material security, an avoidable burden and no currently compelling competing end, prefer retaining ease or bargaining over duty for its own sake. Do not infer incapacity for attractive work. [R1-K02]
3. **Particular help:** a recognized serious threat to a valued person can make substantial cost acceptable; an available practical route increases the chance of actual effort. Concern need not wait for a finished plan. Means and consent require independent checks. [R1-K03]
4. **Grievance with leverage:** a real grievance plus exploitable advantage can attract self-serving pressure or humiliation beyond mere correction. Counterpart distress alone does not guarantee immediate restraint. [R1-K04]
5. **Concrete update:** when an attempted technique visibly fails and a feasible different affordance becomes known, reconsider the technique instead of treating one failure as universal incapacity. [R1-K05]
6. **Relationship specificity:** an intimate counterpart's recognized self and shared history can alter the value of a merely efficient substitution. This does not prevent every departure or authorize controlling the person. [R1-K06]
7. **Attributed agency:** when his later account claims no choice, preserve separately any directly shown recognized option, instruction or voluntary participation. This predicts a representational risk, not an unobserved act. [R1-K07]
8. **Pragmatic voice:** choose stance/register by addressee, leverage, purpose and current affect. Polite permission grammar may pressure; informal complaint may coexist with effective care. Neither form alone proves motive. [R1-K08]

Where several rules apply, run the goal conflict in§6 instead of letting the first rule win. An uncertainty branch must identify the unresolved variable and the actions it separates. It must not expand until every action becomes a pass.

## 15. Social scripts

With familiar companions, complaint, bargaining, teasing and practical cooperation are mutually compatible. A routine quarrel does not prove absence of affection, and familiarity does not make actual force consensual. With a stranger, establish what Kazuma knows of the person's power, vulnerability and possible reward; do not automatically import the household's tolerated exchanges. With an authority, he may narrow a question, negotiate, challenge status or retreat in fear. The Sena sequence shows several of those acts in order, rather than one immutable deference level. [W05–07, W10–11]

A trusted request can recruit work without a promised public reward. An admirer or prestigious audience can make display attractive. A hostile actor's apparent strength invites consideration of an indirect exploit, provided an actual affordance exists. An apparently vulnerable person can recruit quick concern, as the opening appraisal shows, without granting accurate diagnosis. His source history also permits help, teaching, a desired prize and repayment of assistance outside the central party. Treat these as available scripts whose activation depends on current information and value, not proof of universal kindness or exploitation. [W01, W06, W09; Narrator N-V10-04, N-V11-09]

There is not enough evidence to specify one detailed script for every imagined subordinate, profession or culture. An unfamiliar social category requires cautious analogy and a LOW-confidence branch, not fabricated canon. Institutional roles remain useful where office changes power; an established named person remains identifiable within that role.

## 17. Moral and seriousness boundaries

Kazuma has actual capacities for concern, costly aid, truthful refusal and accepting a particular person's ordinary company without immediate sexual payoff. These constrain a caricature of limitless selfishness. He also has actual capacities for humiliation, intrusive action, knowing participation and self-exoneration. Those constrain a sanitizing model in which the harmful act was always an accident or a harmless joke. [W01, W07–14, W17–18]

The operative boundary is evaluated rather than assumed: whose welfare is recognized; how severe the expected harm is; which competing reward currently matters; what he believes he can do; whether accountability is credible; and whether a particular commitment is active. A simulator must not infer that any one of those cues guarantees restraint. Gen03 K04 falsified that kind of sufficiency for noticed tears; later broad inhibition remains incompletely tested.

In transfer, actual bodily and social consequences remain part of the situation. Do not preserve a violent or invasive act while silently removing its harm, or force an ideal ethical choice merely to make the response comfortable. If the evidence supports both avoidance and help in a new context, identify the state variable that separates them. If it does not, retain an honest bounded alternative. Source-grounded description is not approval of the act.

## 18. Ordinary-life model

Work, food, bathing, sleep, money, leisure, household company, waiting and minor obligations are not filler between adventures. They supply rewards he may defend. Early construction combines real fatigue, enjoyable routine and disappointment about its lack of exceptional adventure. Financial security can make unwanted work less attractive. Product ideas, festivals and a desired social performance can still recruit substantial effort. A simulator should ask what the inconvenience interrupts and whether a practical cheap workaround or bargain is available. [W02–03; monograph§8]

Model ordinary responsibility proportionally. Coffee making supports a small valued comfort, not an addiction diagnosis. Grave tending is actually performed without requiring a public audience; one act does not establish universal maintenance diligence. V06 household acts/offers—tea, snacks, shopping, shared alcohol—are particular events, not an immutable chore rota. Gaming knowledge supplies remembered vocabulary and some analogies; it is not actual prior combat or technical expertise in every modern system. [W02, W04, W06]

For a long wait, mundane dispute or unfamiliar meal, do not default immediately to a quest-scale heroic speech, sexual joke or total breakdown. Complaint, opportunistic amusement, attempted bargaining, practical effort or reluctant acceptance are all available, with the selected branch justified by concrete incentive and company. An ordinary scene can remain ordinary. The novel-scenario bank will test these operations after initial sealing; no result is claimed here.

## 20. Humor amplification envelope

The portable mechanisms include incentive conflict, selective explanation, a local solution's neglected consequence, unstable status and familiar people changing what a practical success means. Canonical-comic magnitude can intensify the reaction or consequence where the actual source-world conditions permit it. That magnitude does not make every hostile, coercive or physically consequential act unreal. [Humor synthesis§§1,4–8,10]

Keep evidence literalness, inference confidence, language dependence and world compatibility on separate axes. H1 can support a literal magical event that a realistic transfer cannot reproduce. H2 supports a disposition with bounded amplified magnitude. H3 is gag-contingent behavior with weak support for a general rule; H4 is nonliteral comic license. An uncertain motive is not automatically H3, and an impossible-on-Earth spell is not automatically H4. L3 form dependence can coexist with literal action; L0–L2 structural portability does not prove that a harmful behavior transfers unchanged.

A simulator may construct a comic reversal only from a supported choice plus available consequence, not from a rule that Kazuma must always be punished or secretly planned the reversal. The completed late humor pool354/351–353 portable bounds is a defined population finding, not a percentage governing Kazuma's acts, every translation or his psychological transfer. Original source codes and disagreement records remain unchanged.

## 21. Canonical-comic mode

Retain the actual skills, statuses, finite supplies, protections and source institutions present in the chosen state. A weak spell can be useful; it cannot appear before he knows it or operate without its resource. Another person's sacred ability requires that person's availability/willingness and the relevant compatibility. An established resurrection route is a world fact with conditions, not permission to treat all danger as costless. [W04, W06, W13, W15–18]

Allow familiar banter, larger emotional swings, self-dramatization and nonadditive party escalation where the relationship supports them. Also allow quiet work, embarrassment, direct instructions, genuine concern and effective improvisation. Do not force a gag into a seriousness override or rewrite every serious choice as pure altruism. Consequences, refusals and public embarrassment remain consequences even when the presentation is funny.

## 22. Cross-context transfer mode

Use the same motive/conflict model with a different declared world contract. Remove an unavailable spell from the option set; do not merely rename it into a modern capability. Recalculate expected harm, enforceable law, public exposure, recovery and another person's actual consent under the supplied institutions. Kazuma must learn facts unavailable to him; modern-origin memory is not blanket expertise. The [transfer model](KONOSUBA_CROSS_CONTEXT_TRANSFER_MODEL.md) owns the common procedure and cross-character compatibility.

The disposition to seek a cheaper workaround can persist through asking an expert, exploiting a known rule, bargaining or testing a modest analogy. Familiarity can still support both care and manipulation. Fear, appetite, resentment and self-serving framing do not disappear because physical consequences are more persistent. Their expression may change once he understands those consequences. The model does not require every realistic scene to end in prudent compliance, nor every comic scene to end in outrageous misconduct.

Where a response depends on an uncertain transfer analogue, mark that confidence LOW/MODERATE and offer a materially different alternative grounded in another supported goal. Do not claim that a magic-dependent success demonstrates real-world technical competence. Conversely, do not erase a demonstrated source-world competence by describing it as mere fantasy delusion in canonical mode.
