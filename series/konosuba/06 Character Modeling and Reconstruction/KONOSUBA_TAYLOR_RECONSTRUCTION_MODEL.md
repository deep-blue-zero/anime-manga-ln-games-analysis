## Conditional rules

### TAYLOR-R01 — Make responsibility practical through a role and a feasible contribution

**Proposition.** When he recognizes a group need and sees himself as leader or Crusader, Taylor can take a concrete burden: route choice, cover, coordination, an owned resource or an evacuation instruction. The role gives him a reason to act under fear, not superior knowledge or unlimited defensive capacity. His contribution can depend on a more skilled outsider and can be mistaken.

**Contrary evidence and rivals.** He follows Keith rash charge after Leen suggests checking numbers (MAIN02 P548–573), helps force training and deliberately ignores Keith removal. A generic conscientious leader, class-role convention and ordinary cooperation are strong rival accounts. One good rear-guard decision is not a standing duty to protect everyone. An offered or transferred resource does not certify exact ownership of every later object or completed reimbursement.

**Apply when.** The group need, his understood responsibility, time pressure and usable means are supplied. Separate the role he claims, authority others accept and what his body/equipment can actually do. An outsider demonstrated skill can change the feasible allocation.

**Do not assume when.** There is no established responsibility, an unfamiliar task requires unshown expertise, or the action needs unlimited strength, healing, foresight or money. Do not give him Darkness endurance, Kazuma tactics, Keith vision, another person spell, a completed reward division or a successful defense not narrated.

**Failure and departure branches.** He can follow a poor initiative, misjudge a monster, offer cover that is physically overmatched, ask for help or call retreat when the situation changes. He can lend his shield rather than insist on being the sole front fighter. A role can increase willingness to accept danger without ensuring that he has understood it.

**Confidence and falsifier.** Moderate for specific burden taking and finite contributions, low for a universal duty hierarchy. Repeated comparable recognized group needs with available low-cost means but no attempt to help would weaken this tendency. Repeated refusal to adapt after an outsider clear useful contribution would weaken the allocation branch. Incapacity or a misunderstood need is not a clean preference test.
