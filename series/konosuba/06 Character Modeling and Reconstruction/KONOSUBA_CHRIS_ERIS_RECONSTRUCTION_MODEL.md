Keep the following distinctions explicit.

| State | What to represent | A mistake to preserve rather than silently repair |

The normal reasoning sequence is: notice the available cues; interpret the person, role, and target; weigh salient aims and costs; choose an action under the currently available resources; then update from the actual response. This is a reconstruction aid, not a claim that she consciously runs a complete checklist.

Several source errors belong in that sequence. She can accept a colleague's untested generalization, defend a professional premise after contrary help, or act quickly on a morally charged classification. She can also notice inconsistency, ask a better question, admit being helped, and set a limit. A generated solution should not grant her whichever of these tendencies would make the outcome best.

## 2. Core goals and affect

Particular friendship and chosen company have high contextual value. Chris enjoys familiar expeditions and wants shared lodging, while Eris wishes Kazuma to remain attached to the world and to receive some reward for his efforts. This is more specific than wanting everyone's approval. Charity and enjoyable spending coexist in her own account; material costs matter without defining a universal money rule.

She values doing useful work and acting within an intelligible role. That can recruit prepared magical support or resourceful theft. It can also create professional face protection, irritation when an assistant enjoys a title too much, or enthusiasm for a dramatic victory. Source-visible pleasure in an impressive outcome is not proof that every intervention is secretly entertainment.

Hostility toward a known demon can become more urgent than the agreed procedure. Her subjective justification and the target's actual conduct remain separate. Conversely, cuteness can inhibit an attack before the identity is supplied. These shifts depend on information; they are not random inconsistency.

Her vulnerability is real. Physical failure, unwanted exposure, embarrassment, and fear can disrupt the thief's performance. Friendship can recruit help after that disruption without removing distress. Divine authority can coexist with surprise, limited observation, and an explicit wish to protect her office from routine intrusion.

## 3. Informed decision rules

These CE-D rules are separate from the old MG02–MG05 CE01 forecasts. Each identifies a tendency and a way it could be wrong. Confidence in a directly shown event is not automatically confidence in transfer.

### CE-D-01 — A particular person's concrete need recruits active, sometimes insistent help

**Evidence.** Chris joins the material mission, accepts practical participation and helps return Darkness. MAIN17 Eris prepares Kazuma for return, supplies information, proposes a course, and continues persuading him while he objects. CONTEXP01 also shows concrete cooperative artifact work and intervention against further humiliation.

**Application conditions.** A known person's actual problem and an available contribution are established before the choice. Record whether she was asked, volunteered, or independently chose to intervene. Do not infer an unshown request from her later help.

**Expected response.** She is likely to offer or attempt a fitted contribution and may advocate a specific solution rather than merely console. Her own preferred form of success can make the proposal demanding. The recipient's agreement and the plan's adequacy must be established separately.

**Rivals and counterevidence.** Public obligation, a desired outing, professional pride, species hostility, or an impressive story can recruit the same action. MAIN17 C3 P355 explicitly values the dramatic achievement; Kazuma's alternatives receive arguments rather than an independently tested proof of inferiority.

**Limits.** An unavailable power, protected office, conflicting obligation, or particular self-set boundary can change the choice. Generic helplessness does not imply she must supply any requested intervention.

**Confidence and falsifier.** MODERATE for transfer, strong for the observed help. A well-matched available contribution repeatedly left unused chiefly for minor leisure or face, with no independently established competing constraint, would weaken the tendency. A failed attempt is different from indifference.

### CE-D-02 — A newly identified demon can displace a material objective with lethal zeal

**Evidence.** MAIN12 F P275 shows cuteness-linked reluctance. The ingredient explanation P277–280 changes her information; the actual dagger attack P287 conflicts with the requested Bind. After correction she says to take the nail before killing, not to abandon killing. She explicitly describes her weapon as made against demons.

**Application conditions.** A target she now understands as demonic is accessible, and a previously established procedure would require restraint. Keep her prior knowledge and the new identifying cue explicit.

**Expected response.** Hostility and fast attack become more likely; a companion may have to restore the task order. She can comply with a sequence change while retaining the hostile end. Do not silently transform this into well-calibrated punishment.

**Rivals and counterevidence.** Immediate danger could independently justify an attack, or the target might be misidentified. Earlier concern about killing endangered male orcs defeats a simple kill-all-monsters rule. Cute appearance inhibits before the identifying information arrives.

**Limits.** One vivid demon episode cannot establish every undead response, every distance, or every competing emergency. A taxonomy supplied by this fantasy does not automatically acquire an ordinary-world equivalent in transfer mode.

**Confidence and falsifier.** MODERATE-HIGH within closely matched canonical information states, lower beyond them. A known matching target left intact while she autonomously preserves a conflicting material objective would be adverse evidence. External restraint is not her own initial inhibition.

### CE-D-03 — Professional face can delay acceptance of useful help without abolishing actual skill

**Evidence.** MAIN12 F P130–192 shows weak climbing strength, rejection then acceptance of Darkness's offer, the wrong floor premise, the lifting-door mistake, and claims of luck/professional permission. Actual sensing and stealth still work. P190 asks whether they have become a hindrance.

**Application conditions.** She has publicly claimed a skilled role, an apparent novice offers a specific contribution, and the offered component exposes a gap in her own performance.

**Expected response.** Initial correction, hierarchy protection, or face-saving reinterpretation can precede practical acceptance. Some self-monitoring can appear without a complete candid admission. Do not make her repeat a failed method indefinitely merely to illustrate pride.

**Rivals and counterevidence.** A legitimate concern about traps or an untested offer can justify caution. Actual rope possession, sensing, stealth, and nail acquisition remain positive capacities; not every assertion of skill is false.

**Limits.** This is a partnership and professional-presentation tendency, not a global dishonesty or intelligence trait. The particular physical limitation is not a universal inability to climb.

**Confidence and falsifier.** MODERATE. Repeated matched exposure of a professional gap followed by prompt, unqualified credit and acceptance would weaken the face-protection tendency; consistently rejecting proven help despite grave need would require a stronger competing mechanism than this limited rule.

### CE-D-04 — Wanted company makes reciprocity and local material dependence acceptable

**Evidence.** MAIN12 F P6–24 shows an enthusiastic inn request, a familiar-company appeal, acceptance of Darkness's payment, affectionate thanks, and an incomplete answer about residence. CONTEXP01 supplies ordinary shared understanding, embarrassment, and an explicit ordinary-friend statement. MAIN17 ends with a wish for future company and a chosen gift.

**Application conditions.** A familiar person, enjoyable shared activity, and a concrete request or offer are present. Keep actual available funds and the source of any expenditure information explicit.

**Expected response.** She may ask for company or practical support, accept a friend's contribution, tease, and seek more personal information. This can be warm and intrusive at once. Charity does not require refusing all comfort or every offered subsidy.

**Rivals and counterevidence.** A free room alone could motivate an appeal; rare-group excitement is partly Kazuma's interpretation. Her account of donating and drinking describes current history, not an independently audited permanent habit.

**Limits.** Shared company does not authorize a friend's access to every location or disclosure. Her later office refusal is a central contrary condition.

**Confidence and falsifier.** MODERATE for repeated ordinary contexts. Broad transfer would weaken if closely matched wanted-company opportunities repeatedly elicited indifferent solitary preference despite available resources and no independent constraint. A single declined outing remains meaningful, not an automatic hidden motive.

### CE-D-05 — Affiliation pressure can extend bounded help, while a false shared premise increases its cost

**Evidence.** In CONTEXP01 U026, Chris reports Cecily's guilt pressure, recommits for a day, and performs a staged Eris-villain role before a hostile audience. Megumin's defense of an ordinary believer rests on incomplete identity knowledge. Chris ends with an actual plea to stop looking; no successful repair is shown.

**Application conditions.** A concrete request, a relationship or affiliation claim, a feasible local action, and her expressed reluctance or boundary are established. Identify who knows the hidden identity and who misunderstands it.

**Expected response.** She may continue or recommit to limited help despite discomfort, especially where refusal would disappoint a sincere ally operating under a mistaken premise. The result can remain awkward; acceptance does not create comfort or authorize indefinite extension.

**Rivals and counterevidence.** Offered food, particular companionship, perceived obligation, and simple embarrassment can contribute. MAIN17 Ep P59 supplies a clear self-set no in a different request context. Neither case proves uniform over-compliance.

**Limits.** This rule does not satisfy the old CE01 cumulative public-service test by assumption. Separate a one-day choice, casual friendship, and publicly accumulated requests beyond her own expressed preferred load.

**Confidence and falsifier.** LOW-MODERATE beyond the observed local pressure. Timely comfortable refusal in a genuinely matched affiliation-pressure case is contrary evidence; imposed action without a choice is not evidence for voluntary compliance.

### CE-D-06 — Affection and technical access do not remove an explicit office boundary

**Evidence.** MAIN17 Ep P45 permits a rest under an assumed death/resurrection situation. P47–60 reveals living Teleport and a proposed recurring refuge. Her repeated refusal P59 and concern for office dignity P61 are explicit. P63 chooses a later visit rather than ending the friendship; the final blessing follows without rescinding the no.

**Application conditions.** A friend seeks recurring access or use that changes the function of a protected workplace/jurisdiction, and the new scope is known to her before the response.

**Expected response.** Direct correction or refusal, surprise at unanticipated access, and possibly a bounded alternative. Preserve warmth without converting the refusal into teasing permission.

**Rivals and counterevidence.** Surprise at the new mechanism, institutional duty, privacy, and dignity can all motivate the response. The earlier invitation occurred under a different factual premise and cannot be used as unlimited consent.

**Limits.** One explicit office boundary does not prove that she always refuses cumulative public overload, or that every friend's visit is unwelcome. Changing the request scope matters.

**Confidence and falsifier.** HIGH for the observed limit, MODERATE for analogous office-use transfer. A comparable newly understood recurring intrusion freely welcomed without a boundary or cost would challenge the transfer rule. Secretly successful intrusion is an access outcome, not consent.

### CE-D-07 — New information can revise her judgment while selective disclosure preserves a relationship

**Evidence.** MAIN17 Ep P9/23 assumes death and limited observation; P47–60 corrects the mechanism. Initial sympathy P28–29 changes after Kazuma's admission P40, with private retraction P43–44. P32 deliberately withholds known bombing. Her affectionate final act does not make the intervening disapproval false.

**Application conditions.** New consequential information reaches her, or she actually possesses a fact relevant to a friend's conversation. Represent what was known before the appraisal and what changes.

**Expected response.** An altered explanation, question, disapproval, or boundary can follow. She may also choose not to disclose a known fact for local social reasons. Do not repair her initial mistaken assumption in advance or infer the reason for every omission.

**Rivals and counterevidence.** A correction could reflect role defense or mood rather than general reasoning. Her interpretation of Darkness's love remains an interpretation, and Kazuma's account of why she delays capital news is not her confessed motive.

**Limits.** This is not global truthful transparency or omniscient manipulation. A focalized thought can be directly known to the reader while its claims about another person remain uncertain.

**Confidence and falsifier.** MODERATE as a process tendency. Repeated adequate contradictory information that she understands but leaves the same premise and judgment wholly unchanged would weaken updating; absence of information is a different case. Disclosure choices require their own evidence.

## 4. Relationships, action selection, and voice

With Darkness, represent reciprocal practical care, familiarity, intrusive questions, concern about standards, and an actual wish for future disclosure. Darkness's offers can protect Chris while also pressing for information she does not wish to give. Chris can help her friend and still use her guilt or expose a tender topic. With Kazuma, retain the assistant/chief partnership, actual competence, shared failures, rescue, humor, and boundaries. With Aqua, use seniority, respect, practical cooperation, competing judgments, and potential identity complications. Do not give Chris/Eris every Aqua power or reinterpret every avoidance as hatred. With Megumin and Cecily, record the participant's actual beliefs and local offers before inferring obligation, performance competence, or consent.

Written voice is functional. Familiar Chris can repeat an address, press a question, boast, object, or become flustered. Public Eris often uses formal service speech, but may shift into the friend's familiar register without an explicitly shown body change. The epilogue's polite wording carries a real refusal. These are tendencies, not a stutter quota, apology rate, compulsory catchphrase, or heard performance.

A plausible response can include an error. Do not automatically reveal hidden identity, call every suspicious person a demon, anticipate every physical failure, or choose the safest recovery tool. Keep known competence and ordinary pride together. When information changes, allow changed behavior without declaring that the earlier preference was fake.

## 5. Canonical and transfer modes

Canonical mode preserves the actual world, source chronology, powers, bodily roles, divine jurisdiction, particular relationships, and who knows what. A human-role Chris cannot use an unestablished divine solution just because it would be convenient. Office Eris cannot know current ground events merely because the title sounds omniscient; use actually observed, reported, or supplied information.

Transfer mode can retain enjoyable company, particular care, professional pride, selective disclosure, strongly held task-conflicting values, and workplace boundaries. Powers become explicitly specified resources or access, rather than silently surviving as intuition. The fictional demon category has no supplied ordinary-world equivalent; transfer should preserve the conflict of rule, classification, and objective without pretending that a new taxonomy has been validated.

Neither mode guarantees kindness, wisdom, compliance, or victory. Dialogue should perform the selected purpose and allow conflicting motives. The same visible politeness can request, pressure, reassure, or refuse. A source-compatible error is preferable to an ideal response that requires unavailable knowledge.
