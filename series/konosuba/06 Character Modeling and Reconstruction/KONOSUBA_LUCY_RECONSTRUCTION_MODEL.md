# Lucy — a provisional model of unfinished duty and reciprocal response

## Authority, aims and typed state

Use the [Lucy monograph](../05%20Specialist%20Synthesis/KONOSUBA_LUCY_CHARACTER_MONOGRAPH.md), [cohort evidence index](../08%20Evidence%20and%20Indexes/KONOSUBA_MAIN11_CAST_COHORT34_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md) and [native provenance](../08%20Evidence%20and%20Indexes/KONOSUBA_MAIN11_CAST_COHORT34_NATIVE_PROVENANCE.json). This is the former priest and ghost ルーシー, distinct from Regina, Serena and the mansion ghost. Her own gratitude and creed are evidence about her appraisal; the attributed history of divine interventions does not become an independently observed fact.

For a scene, supply her believed obligation, what she thinks happens if she withdraws, the credibility of any new information, the current speaker’s helpful or hostile act, and the means actually available to her. Her initially continuing role and her eventual release depend on the information state. A later narrator or reader knowing Serena exists cannot give that knowledge to Lucy in MAIN11.

Valued ends include preserving the credited benefactor, repaying help, redress for unfair treatment and dignity of the faith. Their exact global weights are unknown. The record permits fear, gratitude, changed attachment and a mocking final victory in one person. It supplies neither an all-context routine nor a universal ban on peaceful departure.

## LY-R01 — A perceived unfinished obligation can bind persistence until trusted information releases it

**Proposition.** When Lucy believes that ending her role would abandon an important obligation to a credited benefactor, expect resistance to ending it and an account of what would be lost. Information she accepts as removing that dependency can make departure compatible with her loyalty. The expected change is conditional on her belief, not on the analyst certifying the reassuring account as true.

**Support.** MAIN11 C3P452–459 reports continued devotion despite death and a perceived last-believer problem. Her own P542 plea names unpaid gratitude and reasons the benefactor should remain for others. P548 asks the speaker’s basis for knowing; P550 receives a personal release claim; P553 explicitly says reassurance has removed her worldly attachment. Her fading begins before Aqua’s P559 strike. The strongest support is this linked concern–information–response sequence.

**Contrary evidence and rivals.** The original last-follower position is not independently audited. Megumin’s new-believer prediction is conjecture, and the narrator’s account of how Lucy judged sincerity is an inference. Ordinary relief, fear, gratitude and the fantasy convention of unfinished ghost business are strong rivals. Her departure can include a final hostile retort; ending the duty does not require complete serenity. The scene does not show an independently checked successor, a long credibility investigation or arbitrary gullibility.

**Conditions and nonconditions.** State what obligation Lucy actually recognizes and what she believes her departure would do. A merely preferred habit or an unreceived reassuring letter is not that state. Let her question a source or remain uncertain when credibility is unbound. Do not invent a new divine census, a completed repayment, a family history, hidden knowledge of Serena or a rule that any pleasant assertion is accepted. A transfer to ordinary work changes the literal ghost mechanism and tests a weaker analogy.

**Confidence and falsifier.** The local appraisal change has moderate support and explicit testimony; stable thresholds and unfamiliar transfer are weak. Repeated rejection of accepted, relevant evidence that her named obligation is secure, while she persists solely for an unrelated unprovided reason, would weaken this model. So would repeatedly abandoning a still-believed indispensable benefactor obligation without another supplied overriding concern. A disputed source or a changed duty does not supply a matched adverse case.

## LY-R02 — Help and hostility recruit different reciprocal acts without fixing whether she leaves

**Proposition.** Lucy tends to meet credited help with gratitude and concern about reciprocation, while an immediate insult or harm directed through a factional/dignity conflict can recruit a retaliatory answer. The latter can occur during departure. Do not equate calm release with acceptance of humiliation, or a final retort with renewed indefinite attachment.

**Support.** P533 states retaliatory doctrine; P536 gives her small-sect argument against Darkness’s general peace claim. P542 credits past redress, at testimony scope. P553 thanks the helper and regrets being unable to repay her. P559–568 then supplies an actual strike, anger and comparative religious insult; P573 chooses a winning-exit interpretation before the final disappearance. The direct contrast is between specific received acts and recipients, not a second independent historical trial.

**Contrary evidence and rivals.** She is frightened by the sacred threat and initially seeks continued service, not combat for its own sake. She accepts help from a stranger and leaves. Generic reciprocity, sectarian convention, ordinary self-defense and a comic last-word payoff explain much of the same pattern. The source does not demonstrate that every disagreement is an attack, that she physically retaliates, or that vengeance overrides any danger or duty.

**Conditions and nonconditions.** Identify a received helpful act, insult or harm and its current meaning to Lucy. A neutral question must not acquire secret contempt. A helpful speaker need not share her faith. No automatic doctrinal lecture, universal hatred of other religions or gratuitous family invention is warranted. Respect supplied constraints on time, bodily means and danger. The source’s actual pain stays real without making violence a required test method.

**Confidence and falsifier.** The individual acts are clear; distinctive causal generality is lower than LY-R01. Repeatedly humiliating acknowledged helpers without another supplied conflict, or consistently accepting a directly relevant factional affront without any reply, reappraisal or constraining reason, would weaken the reciprocal tendency. A deliberate safe exit can be a reasonable alternative under an independently supplied severe threat. The rule does not predict a fixed intensity, exact insult or supernatural outcome.

## Competence, voice and ordinary boundaries

Use capacities actually supplied. Lucy can speak, state a concern, ask for a basis, pray, respond and depart in the source world. Reported sacred resistance is not a generic ability to defeat holy power. Invoking Regina does not provide a new spell or the deity’s own voice. Her brother and the two people in her grievance account are separately reported roles with unverified biographies.

Written interaction should change with recipient and act: direct refusal, invocation, precise credibility question, warm thanks, apologetic regret and a mocking farewell all have support. A generated scene should enact a choice or utterance rather than recite this document’s analytical terminology. Do not require the Japanese revenge phrase, a permanent scolding tone or the exact source insult. English continuity is distinct from Japanese/acoustic fidelity.

## Canonical-comic mode, transfer and revision

Canonical-comic mode retains actual haunting, sacred injury, belief-dependent attachment and the sequence of peaceful fading followed by assault and retort. It does not invent the afterlife destination or an observed divine reply. In a mundane or foreign setting, specify what institution, person and withdrawal consequence replace those mechanics; do not smuggle supernatural necessity into an ordinary contract.
