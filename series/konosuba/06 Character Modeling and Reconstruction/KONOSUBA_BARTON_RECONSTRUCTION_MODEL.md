## BT-R01: ecological interest can displace the practical question

**Proposition.** Faced with a living thing or biological puzzle, Barton tends to select interesting, demonstrable features for attention and explanation. That selection may answer the listener's task poorly even when the underlying distinction is useful. The error is often task alignment rather than complete absence of knowledge.

**Supporting evidence.** BT29-01: distinguishes his curiosity from weakness research and admits not knowing proposed explanations. BT29-02: lectures while captured, sketches during danger and recommends an empowering color change with valuable scales. BT29-05–06: explains absorption, traces, rarity and a petting approach while others seek immediate tactical help.

**Contrary evidence.** He can provide a practical hint, identify material and suggest a workable lure. He cannot derive an exact den and explicitly refuses the assumption. The animal appearing does not isolate the lure as its cause (BT29-02, BT29-05, BT29-09).

**Rivals.** A generic distracted academic, a comedy function, commercial material interest or deliberate obstruction. The candidate needs the observed priority mismatch and retained knowledge limits; verbosity alone does not identify it.

**Application conditions.** Supply what he knows, the actual requested outcome, available tools and risk. In a novel domain, let interest produce questions, observation or a provisional explanation, not invented authoritative facts. A useful knowledge answer and a useful plan must be evaluated separately.

**Do not assume.** Infallible ecology, the truth of every book claim, spell mastery, exact tracking, universal animal affection or that he always obstructs a rescue. Do not insert a new creature property just to make a digression clever.

**Confidence.** Moderate for recurring information-selection mismatches in the concentrated Continued Explosions scenes; lower outside biological work or when a precise danger threshold is required. No cross-volume reliability is established.

**Falsifier.** Comparable opportunities repeatedly producing direct task-relevant selection without the documented competing interest, or reliable mastery of the explicitly refused tracking inference. A practical answer in one urgent scene is a countercase to explain, not an automatic disproof of every weaker tendency.

## BT-R02: recognition can gate exchange without guaranteeing knowledge

**Proposition.** Scholarly acknowledgment is rewarding enough for Barton to correct, delay or ignore an exchange until addressed as professor. After public success he also advertises his standing and offers signatures. Immediate credible personal pressure can shorten that performance, but ordinary urgency does not reliably do so.

**Supporting evidence.** BT29-01: professor demand during questions. BT29-02: withholds a hint for the form of address, then gives it under threat. BT29-04: ignores greetings, answers the properly addressed greeting and announces the doctorate. BT29-05/09: repeats corrections even amid an encounter, and enjoys Aqua's interest.

**Contrary evidence.** He admits ignorance, thanks the companions, accepts advice and later becomes anxious when Iris's royal identity is revealed. He supplies information under pressure rather than defending the honorific at any cost. The requested title predates the reported doctorate.

**Rivals.** Ordinary professional etiquette, rude humor, a desire to teach or generic vanity. A title correction can be too conventional to identify a distinctive mechanism without its timing, interruption and coexistence with admitted limits.

**Application conditions.** State the address used, whether he can hear it, current standing, audience, time and credible consequences. Treat recognition as a competing motive. An unknown audience or unfamiliar institutional title must not become automatic deference or knowledge.

**Do not assume.** Every missing honorific causes a refusal, danger instantly removes vanity, a doctorate proves his theory, strangers know his fame, or he knowingly defied Iris before learning who she was. Barbera's affectionate address is a separate relationship pattern.

**Confidence.** Moderate for direct recurring address sensitivity and public self-presentation; lower for exact thresholds, unfamiliar authorities and whether a particular boast is sincere. No independent general status-sensitivity score is earned.

**Falsifier.** Repeated comparable unacknowledged exchanges handled without any status concern despite the same room for correction, or a consistent refusal to provide urgently needed information even under the specific personal pressure that previously interrupted the performance. Do not label every silence a title demand when hearing or information is uncertain.

## BT-R03: a particular relationship can motivate feedback-driven revision

**Proposition.** Barton can invest in Barbera and revise his approach after feedback, while continuing to interpret her through uncertain expectations. Price, prestige and advice are means within that relationship, not interchangeable rewards. The candidate predicts an effort to find another approach more readily than an accurate understanding of her mind.

**Supporting evidence.** BT29-01/03: rain request tied to the conditional date and a particular childhood connection. BT29-04: reports her initiative, gifts and refusal of the engagement ring, requests advice and takes Iris's idea despite condescension. BT29-06–08: rejects a replacement-partner suggestion, accepts the small personally found crystal and presents a ring.

**Contrary evidence.** His younger-brother explanation is conjectural. Advice comes from people who can also project or tease. Barbera's reply at the final scene is explicitly unheard, and a happy-looking exchange does not establish acceptance, engagement or marriage (BR29-01–03).

**Rivals.** Generic romantic persistence, wealth display, pride after rejection or an externally written happy ending. The evidence does not isolate durable respect for an explicit refusal, nor license a universal rule about women's gift preferences.

**Application conditions.** Specify what the recipient actually communicated, what Barton believes, the available feedback and costs. Preserve the difference between a conditional opportunity, rejection of one gift and rejection of further pursuit. In new contexts, uncertainty about the other person's motive must remain possible.

**Do not assume.** Hidden approval, a right to affection, the recipient's consent from spending, that a personal gift must succeed, or that a younger stranger can replace Barbera in his valuation. No invented final reply or performed marriage belongs in source reconstruction.

**Confidence.** Moderate for the local feedback-and-investment sequence, lower for its interpretation and unfamiliar relationship boundaries. Source access to Barbera is asymmetric. The model cannot supply the missing independent viewpoint.

**Falsifier.** Comparable explicit feedback repeatedly failing to change his means at all, or unimportant interchangeable status rewards consistently replacing the particular connection at no other cost. A continued mistaken interpretation can be a predicted failure mode; it must not be relabeled successful learning.

## BT-R04: selective care and responsibility can draw personal cost

**Proposition.** Favored creatures, valued companions and accepted involvement can inhibit an attractive goal and prompt a personally costly alternative. Barton can accept fear and seek repair while retaining selective concern. This is a conditional capacity, not a universal conservation or self-sacrifice ethic.

**Supporting evidence.** BT29-05–07: rejects killing the rare liger, tries to prevent the blast, volunteers as a visibly trembling decoy, offers apology/compensation and worries about renewed hunting. BT29-09–10: returns to help, objects to Darkness being caught in the blast and argues for a nonlethal resolution.

**Contrary evidence.** He initially puts companions at risk, resists retreat for a date, differentiates his beloved liger from a frog, and gives impractical advice. Compensation is offered rather than shown paid. His submission display does not calm the animal, and others secure the outcome.

**Rivals.** Species preference, romantic self-interest, guilt, academic identity or ordinary gratitude. More than one may operate. An approach to a favored animal is not automatically a moral sacrifice, and the narrator's guilt attribution is not direct confession.

**Application conditions.** State the threatened subject, his relation or preference, known harm, alternatives, his bodily ability and who bears each cost. A novel rescue can include a plea, limited contribution, failed plan or retreat. Preserve actual collaborator effort and residual danger.

**Do not assume.** Equal concern for every species, absence of fear, success from courage, unlimited funds, completed restitution, or that refusing killing eliminates all risk. Do not move the burden onto an invented helper and still call it his personal risk.

**Confidence.** Moderate for the demonstrated capacity to change role and accept cost; lower for how far it generalizes beyond the particular creature and companions. No quantitative moral ranking or independent courage score follows.

**Falsifier.** Repeated comparable threats to the same valued subjects producing complete disengagement despite feasible personal alternatives, or indiscriminate protection of every creature that contradicts his explicit selectivity. Do not rescue the rule by declaring every unwanted result secretly caring.
