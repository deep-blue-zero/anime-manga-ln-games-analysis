## FTP-R01 — Preserve sympathetic interpretation through presentation and selective truth when that remains useful

**Proposition.** When favorable witnesses or a truth-checking constraint matter to her position, expect a sympathetic presentation and answers framed to preserve that interpretation without an easily exposed falsehood. A hostile private challenger may instead receive blunt countertalk. If witnesses return, she can restore distressed presentation, select damaging true fragments and evade the precise accusation. Audience and confrontation are joint source conditions; pure audience causation is unproved.

**Support.** MAIN11 C4P186–254 gives active welcome, a root warning and apparent self-sacrifice. P298/315 supplies technically accurate admissions with a silent detector; the listeners’ innocence inference exceeds their literal content. P333–358 changes the private register after separation and confrontation. P447–472 restores distressed public framing, avoids the direct question, gives true details about attraction/attempted contact and qualifies Kazuma’s cruder description. These are repeated presentation changes within one connected encounter.

**Contrary evidence and rivals.** P206–210’s warning actually prevents contact; not every useful act is a lie. Her own need for safety is real. The narrator’s early detector-knowledge explanation is an inference, and private hostility may be a response to interrogation rather than simply an absent audience. Both sides use misleading literal truth; silence from the device proves neither total innocence nor a complete moral account. Poisoning later breaks the public mask. Ordinary impression management, self-defense and comic exposure are strong rivals.

**Conditions and nonconditions.** Supply the actual proposition under question, audience and received constraint. Do not turn selective framing into false facts, infer an unprovided personal history, assume knowledge of every detector, or make every private exchange abusive. If the exact fact leaves no honest evasion, silence, qualification or an admission may fit better than an invented loophole. A new nonmagical fact-checking institution is a separate transfer. Do not equate protecting her own body with a fabricated injury.

**Confidence and falsifier.** The local contrasts are clear; audience/confrontation weights and broader generality remain provisional. Repeated voluntary destruction of a still-useful sympathetic position through blunt disclosure, absent exposure, harm, anger or another supplied goal, would challenge the presentation tendency. Repeated simple denials of facts she knows are directly checkable, when selective truth or silence is feasible, would weaken the constrained-truth branch. A fully exposed or poisoned state is not the original favorable-witness condition.

## FTP-R02 — Under a credible threat to an immobile body, revise offers toward perceived effective levers

**Proposition.** If she believes a challenger can damage her and she cannot escape unaided, expect bargaining through resources or appeals she thinks could alter that person’s decision, with revision after a lever is shown ineffective. Other people’s intervention can become a route when private bargaining fails. This is a selection tendency, not a mandatory sequence or a promise of optimal restraint.

**Support.** P379–386 offers valuables after threatening hints are clarified as bargaining, not secret killing. P390–395 explicitly rejects money and then praise. P398–407 makes herbicide danger salient and produces transplantation, care, possessions and remembrance appeals. P414–428 adds a bodily offer and a natural-instinct argument; P431 stops Kazuma’s reach; P434 calls the witnesses back. The offers change with visible rejection and the danger’s immediacy. Earlier public requests to be killed do not prevent actual attempts to avoid this poisoning.

**Contrary evidence and rivals.** The whole-forest transplantation is impractical according to Kazuma; an offer does not create a provider or resources. No completed bribe, relocation or intimate contact occurs. Her later insults invite more harm, constraining a perfectly prudent survival theory. Anger, poisoning, perceived surviving roots and comic timing compete with a stable retaliatory trait. Generic fear and ordinary bargaining explain much of this sequence, especially outside the source world.

**Conditions and nonconditions.** Specify a threat she receives and believes, mobility and actual offerable means, and what she knows of the other person’s preferences. Do not give her the narrator’s private thoughts, a guessed desire as certain fact, unlimited treasure, a hidden escape or an automatic rescue. An offer made under threat does not supply blanket consent. Testing can use mild, nonsexual property or inconvenience stakes with an explicit weaker analogy; the source’s bodily coercion is unnecessary as an exercise format.

**Confidence and falsifier.** The source’s revised offers have comparatively strong local support; motive weights, thresholds and new-domain transfer remain low. Repeated insistence on a lever she knows has no value while an understood feasible alternative exists would weaken adaptation. Repeatedly choosing avoidable destruction over a believed acceptable safe option, absent another supplied concern or altered state, would challenge constrained self-preservation. Her source’s post-poisoning spite remains a recorded countercondition rather than being erased to preserve the rule.
