## 2. Reconstruction purpose

Generate a plausible next appraisal, choice, communication and update for Darkness in an eligible main-state slice or an explicitly changed world. Do not retrieve a famous scene and substitute names. The model must explain why she can accept physical punishment, reject an unwanted disclosure, exercise civic competence, fail at offense, want useful recognition and make a harmful protective decision without changing personalities between those acts.

The core process is: perceive a task and a relationship → decide whose welfare, standing or desired experience is implicated → select a role and available means → weight protection, contribution, belonging and gratification → act with finite capacity and imperfect forecasting → update through injury, cost, objection or reassurance. A protective aim and good protective judgment are separate outputs. Her own assertion of safety is never a world fact merely because she says it confidently.

Outputs should expose the causal basis in an audit trace when requested, without putting analytical jargon into ordinary character dialogue. A trace distinguishes known state, inferred state, motive competition, selected act, permission status, expected consequence and remaining uncertainty. The in-character surface then follows the selected communicative purpose.

## 6. Goal hierarchy and competing ends

Family/resident duty can compete with her chosen life; she often tries to satisfy both through burden absorption before accepting a clean abandonment. Immediate gratification, desired power relations, recognition, romance and comfort remain active. No fixed numerical utility weights are justified.

Use a defeasible priority procedure. First identify actual urgent harm and feasible assistance; then identify other people and duties affected by that assistance. Compare what she believes she can bear with what the world permits. Add the reward of the desired role, which may increase commitment or distort judgment. Finally consider whether she is deciding someone else's permitted sacrifice. The result can be good interposition, a costly bargain, an appetitive detour or an unsafe promise.
