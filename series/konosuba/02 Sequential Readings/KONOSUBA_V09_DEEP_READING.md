## 2. Volume-level thesis and narrative architecture

V09 makes **the personal meaning of a task** as consequential as the ability to perform it. The daily Explosion ritual becomes a military method and finally the medium through which Megumin confronts the person who gave that ritual its meaning. Aqua's apparently marginal construction experience supplies a missing capability that the fort's impressive fighters have not provided. Kazuma succeeds by recognizing and arranging those capacities, but his arrangements can still fail when he does not know what a target means to a companion. The same volume also shows the limits of affection as regulation: household care coexists with destructive rivalry, intrusive conduct and attacks continued amid enemy pleas. (Pr P0009–0044; C2 P0254–0271, P0592–0606; F P0043–0110, P0199–0258, P0286–0316, P0365–0544; Ep2 P0016–0034.)

The movement is from **shared ordinary practice**, through **badly organized competition**, to **effective distributed warfare**, then to a confrontation that technical organization cannot settle by itself. The intimate aftermath repeats that structure on a smaller scale. An apparently available opportunity does not become an emotionally sound action simply because both people can proceed. Kazuma must finally attend to Megumin's actual condition, and the reader must keep his earlier contrary choices in view. Victory settles the immediate confrontation; it does not supply Wolbach's undisclosed motive, establish complete grief recovery or settle the couple label. (F P0473–0544, P0612–0818, P0827–0868; Ep1 P0003–0007, P0051–0052.)

## 3. Chapter-by-chapter analytical reading

### 3.1 Prologue — a dependence that is also a chosen relationship

Kazuma expects leveling eventually to solve Megumin's post-cast collapse. She explains that additional points go into greater Explosion power, keeping the practical dependency intact. The scene makes specialization an allocation choice rather than an inability to understand alternatives. Carrying and appraisal are also shared pleasures. Kazuma's daily exposure gives him a discriminating vocabulary and perceptual skill that will matter when he recognizes enemy bombardment. (Pr P0009–0044; C4 P0048–0056, P0108–0116.)

Megumin's contrast between her former willingness to remain alone and her present appreciation of companions is explicit self-report, given during a continuing ritual. It supports development in belonging without implying that every companion cost now outranks the protected specialty. Her plan to report her achievement to the person who inspired it creates a deferred personal obligation. At this stage the reader should not supply the identity or later outcome from outside knowledge. (Pr P0027–0044.)

### 3.2 Chapter 1 — care and coercive farce occupy the same household

The private conversation inherited from V08 produces nervous delay and repeated domestic interruptions. Kazuma's repeated claim to be “cool” is immediately undermined by his own attention and behavior. Aqua's interruptions have visible mundane purposes; the text does not establish a covert romantic sabotage strategy. The house is active with food, a chick's shelter, gifts and conversation, and that ordinary density makes privacy difficult. (C1 P0006–0118.)

Megumin's handmade protective charms turn affection into an object and a wish for nobody to be lost. Darkness receives the most sturdily made one because she repeatedly shields the others. Kazuma keeps his close to his body. These actions support a valued household and a specific appreciation of contribution; they are not a new enacted permanent-separation test. (C1 P0061–0083, P0343–0374.)

The binding farce is a chain of decisions with material constraints. Kazuma chooses a powerful, long-lasting restraint and spends the resource that might later solve the problem. A requested experience changes into unwanted bodily distress. Concealment to avoid a feared misunderstanding worsens the practical predicament, and resentment continues the conflict after the original reason to hide has passed. Darkness can enjoy an aspect of the situation yet reject a humiliating proposed solution. Her explicit account concerns personal dignity, not merely aristocratic pride. Treating all this as either harmless fantasy or a single preference for pain would erase the changing appraisals that produce the scene. (C1 P0177–0228, P0235–0255, P0274–0281, P0295–0394.)

The resolution is also uneven. Kazuma does not respond adequately to every explicit statement of distress. An actual tear finally prompts practical release, followed by a reversal through social accountability. Megumin later accepts the factual explanation, but her subsequent reference to the episode shows that correct interpretation need not erase feeling. The farce therefore supplies evidence for both durable familiarity and mistreatment within it. (C1 P0417–0433, P0476–0485, P0537–0543.)

### 3.3 Chapter 2 — a bad metric, a genuine request and a protected identity

Economic comfort makes Kazuma's refusal to work coherent under his immediate priorities. Public contribution and romantic admiration fail to motivate him. A challenge to his strength succeeds because it changes the reward from useful work to status/payback. Aqua's account of how her own abilities counter his is technically specific; Darkness's defensive progress likewise defeats a single scalar ranking. (C2 P0021–0111.)

The ensuing kill-count contest measures credit rather than contribution. Kazuma optimizes that metric by taking openings others create, provoking teammates into pursuing him instead of managing the enemy. The increasing noise and distraction permit encirclement. His eventual recognition that their roles are not comparable produces a real cooperative plan, but Megumin interrupts it by taking the decisive shot. She kills the enemies, blasts companions away and leaves Darkness unconscious, then claims superior status. This is target destruction with failed friendly-risk control. Neither “the party wins” nor “she loves them” may erase the adverse outcome. (C2 P0120–0271.)

The frontline request then provides an unusually useful within-volume motivational contrast. Glamour, fame, Iris's possible approval and a private inducement do not settle Kazuma's decision. Megumin's bowed, concrete request concerning repeated attempts to take Chomusuke does. His later calculation that the party and Yunyun's escape capacity provide a workable chance follows that acceptance rather than fully explaining it away. This supports entrusted need recruiting effort before certainty. (C2 P0310–0373, P0478–0486, P0621–0658.)

The lie detector extracts relevant information but also becomes a social instrument. What it tests is the truth of particular utterances, not every motive, relationship or cosmological implication surrounding them. Megumin's first unsealing and Komekko's later action become reportable history; Aqua redirects the situation toward acknowledgment, and flattering Kazuma yields a comic correction. The presence of a truth device does not give the analyst permission to infer unspoken facts. (C2 P0376–0475.)

Preparation exposes a genuine prediction problem. Aqua transforms a useful object as an unrequested display and cannot reverse it. Megumin then treats Kazuma's imitation of Explosion as an identity challenge. She discards the remaining prepared item after he states its money and labor cost; only afterward does he explain its purpose in supporting her request. She acknowledges possible utility while maintaining her rejection. Kazuma subsequently ignites and detonates the discarded item. The exact frozen M02 condition includes concrete companion cost; it does not require bodily injury. This case must revise a broad cost-sensitive retaliation rule. The surviving altered item later helps them escape, but that fortunate outcome cannot retroactively make either earlier decision well planned. (C2 P0519–0536, P0559–0617; F P0383–0409.)

### 3.4 Chapter 3 — fantasy expectations encounter material limits

Kazuma's attempt to obtain extra protection is presented as an offer to escort departing soldiers. He learns that the frontline is more exposed than he assumed, but a public promise and face concerns complicate retreat. What he later reports to the party emphasizes acquiring a map rather than his original goal. This separates successful local information gathering from selective retelling and from an adequate initial risk model. (C3 P0006–0068.)

The bandit encounter reverses the desired genre script. The party is excited by a supposedly conventional fantasy event; noble and Crimson Demon recognition instead makes the bandits flee. Darkness's pursuit serves her desired scene more than the journey's actual schedule, leaving the party to camp. She acknowledges the cost and offers the watch. Yunyun's excitement and preparation have their own cost in lost sleep, which Megumin notices. Ordinary routines and physical state determine who is suited to watch as much as nominal class does. (C3 P0072–0165.)

The dragon zombie demonstrates several distinct kinds of competence. Kazuma's concealment does not cancel Aqua's undead attraction; he has to revise his approach when the creature is too large for his initial perceptual assumption. Darkness interposes despite fear and is overwhelmed, protecting others at the cost of armor and consciousness. Aqua's delayed waking is a deployment failure, while her engaged purification is immediately decisive. She then heals and carries Darkness. Resistance to penalty and real repair behavior coexist. Kazuma reorganizes light, load, buffs and movement for departure. Calling any one of them simply useful or useless would lose the causal arrangement. (C3 P0168–0272.)

The bath conversation develops Wolbach as a particular, considerate person before the battle identifies her role to Kazuma. He already has reason to know her Demon Army association, but warmth and familiarity lower its salience. Her suggestion about affinity with her other half is a character hypothesis, not established shared consciousness. Kazuma raises Chomusuke's flight and fire as possible identifying clues, but Wolbach dismisses them; his skepticism toward Megumin's divine claims and his self-serving denial of laziness further complicate the comparison. He also forgets to ask the woman's name. These are distinct epistemic limits. (C3 P0387–0437, P0453–0462.)

The surrounding conduct also prevents a sentimental simplification of Kazuma. Concern for trust and possible rejection initially inhibits him, yet he then privately resolves to override an assumed companion's resistance and directs hostile speech at the unexpected stranger. The imagined coercive act is not shown being carried out; the ideation and harassment are still part of the moral evidence. His newly “unrestrained” self-story will recur after the later care scene. (C3 P0336–0386; Ep2 P0016–0034.)

At the fort, Kazuma uses Dustiness authority to reverse threatened commoner subordination and secure better rooms. The companions join grievance theater, while Darkness bears the embarrassment and the command obligations attached to the publicly invoked status. His exploitation of hierarchy is practically intelligent without becoming a general commitment to fairness. Darkness's later correction makes the causal debt explicit: his display helped create the duty he now finds inconvenient. (C3 P0512–0544; C4 P0146–0152.)

### 3.5 Chapter 4 — an enemy system and an unknown relationship defeat the first plan

Kazuma and Aqua's prohibited-button play first supplies a miniature version of goal misalignment: soldiers' warnings become cues for mischief, and the pair interprets expulsion partly through the soldiers' tension. More substantively, Kazuma recognizes the wall damage as Explosion through his daily experience with Megumin. He then learns that his intended range advantage does not exist. Darkness's damaged armor carries forward as a real constraint on the decoy option. (C4 P0006–0022, P0048–0077.)

The first revised idea—attack after the enemy has expended Explosion—is intelligent but incomplete. Wolbach teleports away to a protected army in favorable terrain. Range, withdrawal, mana-recovery time, force concentration and the threatened wall form one effective system; nominally strong defenders cannot solve it by rushing the attacker. Kazuma's willingness to retreat reflects new information. His decision to help Megumin attempt an ambush follows her concrete request and uses his particular skills. (C4 P0117–0171.)

That ambush has explicit roles and contingency branches. Megumin also says she prefers a surprise attack to exposing Darkness to the enemy spell. Yet identifying Wolbach changes the task for people whose prior relationship Kazuma does not know. Mechanical preparation is not enough to predict whether the specialist can fire. Meanwhile Aqua's fear yields to the contest over divine status, and the delaying assignment becomes an excessive flood that harms the fort. Both failures are motivational and relational, not evidence that the relevant powers are weak. (C4 P0182–0228, P0234–0300; F P0007–0022.)

### 3.6 Final chapter — repair, effective warfare and a costly completion

Aqua's repair assignment initially follows coercive accountability, but its success depends on real prior skill. Kazuma admits he had not watched her construction work closely before; the revelation is partly a correction of his observation gap. Manual method and water-control drying restore a wall the military had treated as nearly lost. Darkness's symbolic captain title then attaches recognition to the service itself. Over days, repaired walls become stronger, morale returns and resupply/reinforcement become useful. The same status sensitivity that disrupted the ambush now stabilizes a productive role. (F P0016–0019, P0043–0110.)

Kazuma then borrows the enemy's attack-and-withdraw method. His concealment and judgment, Megumin's range, Yunyun's Teleport, Aqua's repair crew, Darkness's command/defensive role and the fort's other fighters form the effective unit. He explicitly anticipates retaliatory attacks and delegates mass defense. This is evidence against turning his known downstream blind spots into a universal inability to anticipate consequences. (F P0199–0258.)

Repeated success also produces a morally and motivationally adverse sequence. Megumin's growth reward becomes an aim in itself. Enemy dialogue includes pleas, surrender and claimed disarmament; the attacks continue. Kazuma helps choose targets before eventually restating the mission, while Yunyun objects to the apparent reversal of moral roles. The exact truth of every enemy's claimed family circumstance is not known, and the montage amplifies panic comically. Its ethical signals and the party's conduct cannot therefore be deleted. Tactical efficacy, appropriate stopping and humane judgment are distinct axes. (F P0268–0321.)

The enemy's interception exposes preparedness limits. Kazuma has left his larger collection of tools behind; one accidentally retained altered object buys time for Yunyun's escape and for physically drawing the unresponsive Megumin to safety. The outcome combines fast action, another person's competence and luck. It is not evidence that his earlier preparation anticipated every situation. Megumin apologizes for not firing but cannot yet explain the relationship. (F P0324–0428.)

Kazuma's next effective move is to ask what she wants while acknowledging that he does not know the history. He does not invent a painless option: other adventurers intend to kill the still-hostile opponent. He offers help if Megumin wants to settle the matter herself and uses familiar walking/scene-taking language to make that choice accessible. A negotiation with the other fighters creates space for it. Agency here operates within constrained alternatives; it is not a guarantee that the chosen outcome is emotionally harmless. (F P0456–0494.)

Wolbach refuses a nonaggression settlement and says reclaiming power would require destroying Chomusuke. Her reasons for the Demon Army allegiance remain unanswered. Megumin identifies an additive wording clue in the earlier name exchange and infers that her teacher remembers her despite the denials. She says she has mastered control without an incantation, gives quiet thanks, and then voices the spell name. **The incantation is omitted; the spell name is spoken.** The volume establishes this capacity here without dating its acquisition precisely. The signature act becomes both demonstration and destruction. (F P0499–0544.)

### 3.7 Final aftermath — recognition of distress, with contrary behavior preserved

The party declines the victory celebration because Megumin is distressed. Kazuma's possible sight of Wolbach's smile is explicitly uncertain and followed by a wish that it was real. It cannot certify forgiveness. Megumin's subsequent approach combines genuine affection with grief; reducing it to either alone ignores her prior declarations and later account. (F P0549–0598, P0606–0608, P0766–0783.)

Kazuma's self-regulation fluctuates visibly. He worries about changed household relations, is briefly relieved by interruption, resumes pursuit and dismisses those same concerns, then actually stops when he notices tears. Only then does he ask the obvious question of why she came. After hearing the origin story, he uses embarrassing self-disclosure and an intentionally ordinary, awkward comparison to challenge her self-condemnation. The consoling “match-pump” analogy is his reframe, not proof that Wolbach deliberately staged the original rescue. He explicitly declines to exploit the grief-driven situation, while desire, regret and hopes for a later voluntary relationship remain. (F P0612–0818.)

### 3.8 Epilogues — a second viewpoint and an exact forward boundary

Epilogue 1 shifts to **Megumin's first-person narration**. It independently confirms that she wanted the connection and that she does not know whether mutual declarations mean they are formally lovers. She becomes jealous of a proposed outing without knowing its full purpose, asks Kazuma's preference and resolves to grow her hair. The shift is a controlled check on his romantic uncertainty, not an omniscient solution to it. The return invitation also includes Yunyun; temporary campaign participation does not establish permanent party membership. (Ep1 P0003–0007, P0017–0026, P0029–0074.)

Epilogue 2 returns to Kazuma. Chomusuke's behavior prompts hopes about Wolbach and a wish that Megumin be forgiven; those hopes do not establish transformation, restored memories or identity continuity. Darkness hides Iris's letter, Kazuma identifies its sender from a repeated pattern, and his intrusive retrieval repeats the self-justification of a newly unrestrained self after the consolation scene. The letter's familiar salutation, concern and escort request are observable; Iris's private wish or conflict with duty is not. Kazuma tears the letter after reading of the betrothed prince. The analysis stops at that response. No subsequent journey, decision or reaction is supplied. (Ep2 P0003–0041.)

## 4. Major-character reconstruction updates

### 4.1 Satou Kazuma

**Replication and new evidence.** His high-value competence remains the construction of indirect solutions from rules, incentives, modest tools and other people's strengths. V09 extends the routine-to-crisis connection: repeated Explosion walks supply perceptual knowledge, and knowledge of a companion's identity later supplies a usable way to offer help. Concrete requests recruit effort more successfully than abstract duty or flattering inducements. He can also forecast a counterattack and deliberately assign it elsewhere. These observations strengthen the model's conditional competence, not a global intelligence ranking. (Pr P0009–0044; C2 P0478–0486; C4 P0048–0056; F P0199–0258, P0473–0494.)

**Contradictions, negative evidence and boundaries.** He optimizes the contest's bad metric, turns status into obligations for Darkness, neglects contingency equipment, contributes to attacks amid enemy pleas and repeatedly supplies self-exonerating interpretations of intrusive conduct. Warmth toward a personally known enemy produces reluctance that anonymous enemy appeals do not reliably evoke. He can understand another person's vulnerability and still initially exploit it; actual tears form a narrower observed interruption cue than all verbal requests. A simulation must retain these failures beside the successful rescues. (C1 P0417–0433; C2 P0172–0271; C3 P0336–0386; F P0286–0316, P0383–0389, P0434–0448, P0701–0815; Ep2 P0016–0034.)

**State change and confidence.** Affection toward Megumin becomes directly reciprocated in speech, and he offers care through a personally embarrassing disclosure. Formal relationship status remains unresolved in both viewpoints. Confidence is HIGH for indirect problem solving and concrete commitment, MODERATE-HIGH for reward/recognition-conditioned overreach, and MODERATE for the generality of the tear-triggered interruption. No enduring global moral conversion is established. (F P0719–0740, P0752–0847; Ep1 P0003–0007, P0051–0052.)

### 4.2 Aqua

**Replication and new evidence.** Engaged sacred/support power remains exceptional. V09 adds practiced construction and water control as a strategic competence, with continuity to everyday drying and earlier labor. The captain role converts public recognition into sustained task performance; manual work can be enjoyable mastery rather than intrinsically disliked effort. The skill is newly observed by Kazuma, not proven newly acquired in V09. (C3 P0238–0272; F P0043–0110.)

**Contradictions, negative evidence and boundaries.** She fails to wake promptly, violates warnings about the fur spirits, changes a prepared item without respecting its purpose, and lets divine-status conflict replace the ambush objective. Her later remediation is real but begins under instruction and becomes rewarding; it does not isolate an internal apology/accepted-blame transition. Continued susceptibility to flattering egg-related messages does not negate every earlier learned rule, but it does defeat a global improvement story. (C2 P0519–0536; C3 P0079–0092, P0193–0240, P0469; C4 P0256–0300; F P0016–0019; Ep2 P0011–0019.)

**State change and confidence.** She occupies a sustained, socially recognized repair-leader role for the operation. The better model distinguishes mastery, readiness, task ownership, reward alignment and accountability. Confidence is HIGH for demonstrated capacities and prestige sensitivity, MODERATE-HIGH for constructive recognition alignment, and MODERATE/OPEN for the complete accountability mechanism and the still-unmatched low-status maximal shortcut. (F P0061–0110; unchanged MG02-A03/A04 and V08 addendum.)

She deliberately preserves the exclusive build and its dependency; safe separated targets make it extraordinarily useful. V09 adds a distinct inhibition variable: a target who is the rescuer/teacher associated with that very identity. It also establishes control without the incantation, while retaining the spoken spell name. Technical mastery and emotional difficulty can coexist. (Pr P0009–0044; F P0007–0015, P0199–0242, P0365–0419, P0534–0544, P0766–0783.)

**Contradictions, negative evidence and boundaries.** Companion cost does not automatically constrain retaliation under core identity threat. Repeated military success also shifts her aim toward further growth despite the original task. By contrast, concern about Darkness receiving the enemy's spell is genuine care, but not a positive test of her own serious-friendly-fire choice. (C2 P0254–0271, P0592–0606; C4 P0206–0208; F P0286–0321.)

**State change and confidence.** She completes the confrontation she had imagined as a report of achievement, then struggles with what she describes as killing her benefactor. She states a detailed attraction to Kazuma's imperfect behavior, receives reciprocity and resolves a small ordinary-life preference change in her own viewpoint. Confidence is HIGH for protected specialization and context-dependent artillery, MODERATE for transfer of the valued-target mechanism beyond Wolbach, and MODERATE for serious companion-harm inhibition. ### 4.5 Secondary candidates and a bounded opponent profile

Yunyun's participation combines practical competence, strong value for inclusion, overpreparation, care and moral dissent. ## 8. Japanese voice and language findings

The Japanese was read before the glosses below were formulated. Each entry identifies the speaker, addressee, relationship, channel, short anchor, pragmatic mechanism and translation sensitivity. L0 is structurally portable; L1 depends on phrasing/timing; L2 on register/pragmatics; L3 on specifically Japanese form requiring reconstruction or annotation. The detailed Japanese voice ledger contains fifteen fuller evidence entries.

### 8.1 Shared practice becomes a relationship register

**Pr P0009–0044; C4 P0048–0056, P0108–0116.** Megumin and Kazuma, as habitual caster and evaluator/carrier, use the comic connoisseur role `爆裂ソムリエ`. The basic gloss is an Explosion “sommelier”: an apparently excessive evaluative identity attached to a daily routine. Its phrasing is L1, its shared role/pragmatics L2. Later recognition of the enemy's damage and precision gives the joke a practical consequence. This is dialogue and narrator observation, not proof that he has universal magical expertise.

### 8.2 One written form links protection and caretaking

**C1 P0061, P0081–0083, P0116–0118.** `お守り` first denotes a protective charm; a later use selects the reading of babysitting/caretaking through the ruby on 守. Megumin's teasing about looking after Kazuma reuses a written form that the domestic scene has just made emotionally important. The charm/care relationship remains situationally portable, but the same-script/different-reading effect is local **L3** and needs reconstruction or annotation. This finding depends on the original ruby, not just a romanized or English rendering.

### 8.3 Direct request differs from successful seduction

**C2 P0478–0486.** Megumin addresses Kazuma as a trusted companion with an apologetic, specific request, including `私と一緒に来てはもらえませんか？` (P0479). The gloss is a request to accompany her, framed through responsibility for Chomusuke. The lowered posture, specificity and relational stake distinguish it from the earlier inducements. L2 politeness and vulnerability accompany an H1 decision contrast. The model implication is about how need becomes legible, not a rule that a polite ending compels compliance.

### 8.4 Formal authority can be borrowed for grievance

**C3 P0512–0544.** Kazuma invokes Dustiness standing while addressing soldiers who have threatened him: `この御方をどなたと心得る！`, roughly “Do you know who this person is?” The shift from aggrieved commoner to spokesman for noble authority changes the social action performed by his speech. Darkness's visible discomfort and later attribution of the command obligation prevent reading this as pure protective nobility. The mechanism is mainly L2 hierarchy/mock authority, with L0 status reversal. The precise institutional position and audience matter more than a fixed “Kazuma register.”

### 8.5 Divine self-naming can intensify conflict

**C4 P0256–0300.** Aqua and Wolbach address one another as rival claimants to legitimate divinity. Aqua's `私の名はアクア` is a self-identifying assertion in a reciprocal legitimacy dispute; subsequent demands for apology intensify it. The scene moves from fear and a delaying assignment to status aggression. This is L2 stance and role, with L1 repetition. Serious investment in identity does not necessarily produce dignified speech or less theater.

### 8.6 A title can name useful responsibility

**F P0054–0063, P0083–0099.** Aqua's `補修隊長` identity is sustained by Darkness, helpers and Aqua herself. The gloss is repair captain: a nominal title with no pay or formal powers that nonetheless identifies real valued work. Professional directives and title uptake are L2, while the comic mismatch between nominal rank and strategic usefulness is L0. Calling the title “empty” would miss its effect; treating her confidence as empty boasting would miss the observed skill.

### 8.7 Additive wording supports an inference of recognition

**F P0179 and P0537; denials at P0188, P0372 and P0515.** Wolbach asks Yunyun whether her name is also not a nickname. Megumin later isolates the additive `も` and infers that Wolbach has a prior comparable name exchange in mind, despite denying recognition of her. The earlier wording and its emphasized recall are present in the primary text. The claim that Wolbach remembers Megumin remains **Megumin's inference**; the narrator does not enter Wolbach's mind to certify it.

This is chiefly **L2 pragmatic inference with exact-wording sensitivity**; L1 is a defensible narrower label. An ordinary additive such as “either” or “too” can preserve the clue and its later emphasis. Importance of a Japanese particle is not itself evidence of L3 dependence. The payoff is serious and relational, so it should not be used to inflate a count of Japanese-form-dependent comedy.

### 8.8 Gratitude and mastery are not mutually exclusive registers

**F P0534–0544.** Megumin addresses her teacher/opponent directly, explains control `詠唱がなくても`—without an incantation—and quietly says `ありがとう` before the voiced spell name. Personal thanks, technical demonstration and the signature attack occupy one sequence. The effect is L1/L2 sequencing and relational stance, not a claim that serious speech must become uniformly plain. Nor is it silent casting: the preparatory incantation is omitted, while the spell name is explicitly spoken.

### 8.9 Ordinary companion language offers a choice

**F P0473–0484.** Kazuma asks Megumin `どうしたい`, “what do you want to do?”, and offers help with the choice. The narration explicitly compares the manner to inviting her on an ordinary walk. He does not yet know her history, and his statement of the other adventurers' intention keeps the practical constraint visible. The voice effect is L2 familiarity, supported by the established shared routine; the offer is meaningful without solemn eloquence.

### 8.10 A duty declaration can break without becoming insincere

**F P0459–0467.** Yunyun attempts to speak as a member of the Crimson Demons obligated to oppose the Demon Army—`私達紅魔族`, “we Crimson Demons” (P0463)—while also stating what Wolbach's invitation meant to her. Her declaration breaks into visible distress. Collective role language and personal gratitude are both serious; neither automatically cancels the other. This is L2 role conflict and L1 disruption. The passage establishes conflicted commitment, not a demonstrated failed magical attack.

### 8.11 Particular affection exposes the limits of generic praise

**F P0724–0740.** Asked what he likes, Kazuma falls back on Explosion; Megumin immediately identifies the generic strategy. She then describes him as `善くも悪くもない` in a longer, nonidealizing account of ordinary inconsistency and help despite complaint. The phrase's basic gloss is neither straightforwardly good nor bad, but its local affectionate pragmatics matter. This is L1/L2, dialogue from a person in love, not an authoritative ethical acquittal by the novel. The surrounding contrary conduct remains evidence.

### 8.12 Self-disclosure, a second viewpoint and formal intimacy

**F P0784–0815.** Kazuma's embarrassing school/parent history is offered as consolation in an awkward, self-involving voice. His `マッチポンプ` analogy is a colloquial reframe; it does not establish an intentionally engineered rescue. **Ep1 P0003–0007, P0051–0052** makes the first-person “I” Megumin and explicitly preserves unconfirmed relationship status. **Ep2 P0036–0040** combines the formal letter opening with `お兄様` and an escort request. These are primarily L2 differences of relationship, role and channel. Iris's familiar address does not reveal an unspoken wish or settle the duty prediction.

## 12. Worldbuilding, institutions and genre parody

The siege is described through operating constraints rather than a simple stronger-versus-weaker ladder. Long range, mana expenditure, Teleport, protected terrain and fortification interact. Repair rate and supply access can change the strategic problem without first defeating the most powerful enemy. This is a source-specific worldbuilding claim, not an attempt to impose real-world military equivalence. (C4 P0065–0144; F P0043–0110, P0199–0258.)

Noble identification has material effects: it changes treatment at the gate and helps impose a command obligation. The party's use of status thus creates both benefits and costs. Bandit flight similarly reveals how inhabitants respond to known power rather than politely fulfilling the protagonists' desired genre scenario. (C3 P0099–0138, P0512–0544; C4 P0146–0152.)

Wolbach's interpretation of the name Satou invokes a heroic-lineage story that Kazuma privately treats as a mistaken use of a common name from his world. The joke concerns unequal cultural information; the source's demographic statement is not independently verified here. Nor does it establish actual ancestry. (F P0347–0363.)

The divided divine identity is presented through reported history, Aqua's observations, Wolbach's statements and Chomusuke's later behavior. Those sources establish a live explanatory problem, not a complete ontology. The reason for Wolbach's allegiance is explicitly deferred and remains unknown within this boundary. No side story or later volume may be used to complete it. (C3 P0403–0433; F P0148–0154, P0499–0529, P0561–0567, P0835–0868.)

## 13. Longitudinal implications before scoring

The previous model's broadest mechanisms survive: indirect problem construction, concrete commitment, context-specific powers, protected identity, protector contribution, valued household relationships and role-conditioned voice. A title can align an agent with a real task; a metric can misalign an entire group; an apparently well-designed attack can fail because its target has an undisclosed personal history.
