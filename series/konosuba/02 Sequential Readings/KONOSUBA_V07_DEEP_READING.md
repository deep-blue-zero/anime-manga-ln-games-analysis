## 2. Volume-level thesis

V07 is the strongest relationship-separation test in the project so far. V06 established that the current party/home ecology had become a valued end state. V07 removes Darkness from that ecology by a mechanism she herself believes morally compulsory, then asks whether the remaining party behaves as if her absence is a rational optimization.

It does not.

The main causal architecture is:

`hidden family/public burden -> Darkness attempts unilateral self-sacrifice -> party initially lacks a solvable route -> separation produces household/identity distress -> new information converts grief into actionable leverage -> distributed rescue -> ordinary peer membership restored`

The most important V07 conclusion is therefore not simply "the party cares about Darkness." It is more specific:

> **KONOSUBA's party attachment has become strong enough that a member's attempt to protect the others by removing herself is treated by the others as a problem to be solved, not as a legitimate efficient reorganization.**

V07 also prospectively validates several Gen 0.2 mechanisms at high diagnostic value:

- Kazuma's unusual value in under-structured, rule-heavy problems;
- concrete relational duty overriding comfort and asset preservation;
- actual separation activating attachment much more strongly than his replacement rhetoric predicts;
- Aqua's elite sacred-domain competence and salience-driven goal substitution;
- Megumin's structured-target Explosion utility and identity-challenge response;
- Darkness's defensive excellence, equal-peer belonging sensitivity, and negotiated/self-sacrificial handling of family/party incompatibility;
- routing as a system requirement whose source can vary;
- seriousness aligning speech toward the currently authoritative role rather than simply making speech plainer.

V07 also reveals a new Darkness failure mechanism: **protector paternalism through self-erasure**. Her willingness to absorb costs is admirable, but when combined with secrecy and a belief that she alone should decide whose burdens count, it can become causally destructive.

## 3. Narrative architecture

### 3.1 Prologue — ordinary antagonism after status revelation

The prologue deliberately returns Kazuma and Darkness to domestic friction. Kazuma is carefully maintaining Iris's ring while Darkness attacks the manner in which he does so. The scene matters because V06's royal/noble disclosures have not replaced the established peer script with deference.

Darkness herself recalls the distinctive fact that Kazuma learned she was Dustiness Ford Lalatina and still treated her primarily as Darkness. V07 will later make this ordinary treatment the thing she fears losing.

### 3.2 Chapter 1 — wealth/status inflation, the false dragon egg, and the hidden Darkness problem

Kazuma receives the Sylvia reward and public praise. His internal line `そう、俺の時代が。` marks an immediate recognition/status upswing. He purchases drinks for the guild and performs his newly elevated social position with unusual generosity.

Aqua is simultaneously pulled by a parallel status-reward mechanism. A merchant flatters the party as distinguished adventurers worthy of raising a dragon for the coming Demon King conflict. Aqua spends essentially all available personal funds on what is transparently a chicken egg, because the story provides several personally salient rewards at once:

`dragon prestige + heroic recognition + caretaker identity + rare-object excitement`.

Her own explanation is revealing: if a supposedly priceless dragon egg is being offered cheaply specifically because its seller wants *great adventurers* to raise it, then buying it becomes proof of status rather than merely a commodity choice.

The chapter also introduces Darkness's concealed urgency. Vanir predicts her coming ruin and explicitly says raw strength will not solve it. Darkness refuses the obvious advice to flee. Soon afterward an Alderp servant summons her, and she begins pushing the party toward the Kowloon Hydra with unusual urgency.

### 3.3 Chapter 2 — failed Hydra attempt, causal diagnosis, and model-building

The first Hydra engagement fails decisively. Aqua's passive purification disturbs the lake system and brings the monster into active conflict. Kazuma dies after being swallowed; Darkness deliberately allows herself to be swallowed to recover his body and cuts her way back out. Aqua resurrects him.

The post-failure sequence is analytically excellent because nobody simply repeats the same plan. The party identifies the Hydra's regeneration mechanism: damage alone is not sufficient because the monster spends mana to regrow its heads and can return to the lake to restore its strategic position.

Kazuma initially recommends handing the problem back to the royal knights. Aqua then correctly observes that her purification has changed the Hydra's incentives: because part of its lake has been purified, it will spend time re-polluting its territory rather than immediately ranging outward. This is genuine system/domain reasoning, not accidental usefulness.

The royal knights, however, are unavailable because the V06 castle-heist response has pulled them elsewhere. This is another example of past successful action creating later operational cost: the party's earlier clandestine win has reshaped the institutional environment.

Darkness and Megumin continue attacking the Hydra daily. Megumin's motive is not merely Explosion opportunity; the monster killed Kazuma, and revenge becomes relationship-conditioned. Darkness eventually begins going alone because she does not want Megumin exposed to increasing danger.

Kazuma first responds with refusal and mockery, but after observing worsening injuries he begins constructing a real solution. Aqua explicitly identifies his comparative advantage in a crude but accurate form: his strength is not raw power, but having many middling skills and finding underhanded solutions to unreasonable problems.

### 3.4 Chapter 2 climax — the Hydra operation as a Gen 0.2 systems test

The second Hydra engagement is one of the cleanest examples of Model Generation 0.2's routing theorem.

Kazuma recruits a large group of Axel adventurers and assigns functions:

1. Aqua wakes/lures the Hydra toward shore.
2. Front-line adventurers protect the rear.
3. Darkness uses Decoy and physically fixes the Hydra's attention.
4. Thief-class adventurers use wire to bind multiple necks together.
5. archers/others attach lines and heavy adventurers pull the Hydra away from the lake;
6. Kazuma uses concealment to approach, climbs onto the Hydra, and applies Drain Touch to attack the regeneration resource rather than the heads themselves;
7. Yunyun removes heads with `ライト・オブ・セイバー` as regeneration falters;
8. mass magic is applied once the target is immobilized and depleted;
9. Megumin becomes terminal artillery only after the tactical state is prepared.

This is not "Kazuma has a plan" in the generic sense. The plan is specifically a sequence of **state transitions**:

`lake-linked regenerating monster -> land-committed target -> neck-constrained target -> mana-depleted target -> regeneration failure -> safely separated terminal target`.

That directly confirms MG02-K01. It also confirms MG02-E01 because victory depends on routing/sequence structure. Kazuma is the router here precisely because the environment initially supplies none.

The battle also strongly confirms MG02-D03. Darkness's accuracy problem is irrelevant; the needed skill is to survive, hold aggro, and physically maintain space while others alter the state. Her specialization becomes elite under the matching task.

MG02-M03 is likewise strongly confirmed: once allies are safely separated and the target's regenerative escape route is blocked, Explosion is not a liability but an ideal terminal strike.

There is also a boundary repetition for Kazuma. While draining mana from the Hydra's back, he has not fully modeled what the enormous creature can do to a rider; it tries to scrape/crush him against the ground. Darkness catches/protects him beneath the monster. The scene resembles his established post-transition/embodied consequence blind spot without reducing the success of the larger systems model.

The social scale of the operation matters too. V07 shows that party competence is embedded in accumulated Axel social capital. The people Kazuma has drunk with, paid for, fought beside, and helped become operational resources. "Ensemble" is expanding beyond the four-person party.

## 4. Satou Kazuma — V07 reconstruction update

### 4.1 MG02-K01 receives a strong prospective confirmation

V07 supplies two separate unstructured problems where Kazuma does not attempt a conventional contest.

**Hydra:** he attacks the monster's regeneration state and environment rather than trying to outdamage it from the beginning.

**Dustiness/wedding:** he identifies the barriers as information, liquidity, consent, and institutional access. Once Vanir supplies debt information and a way to liquidate his intellectual-property portfolio, Kazuma converts the problem into a solvable transaction plus infiltration/rescue sequence.

His night entry into the Dustiness estate uses:

- Aqua buffs;
- Snipe plus hooked rope;
- Enemy Detection;
- stealth;
- `Tinder` + `Freeze` thermal shock on a window;
- voice mimicry under Aqua's performance buff;
- false servant messages to locate Darkness.

At the wedding he again uses the environment and another person's incentives rather than direct force: he dumps the twenty億 Eris payment physically onto the floor because Alderp and the guests will stop to collect it, buys a temporary movement corridor, and later mimics Alderp's voice to redirect guards.

K01 is therefore **CONFIRMED strongly at V07 level**.

### 4.2 Care exists before solvability, but action accelerates when leverage becomes concrete

Kazuma spends much of V07 claiming that there is nothing he can do. This can look like low commitment if only dialogue is considered. Behavior says otherwise.

Before a rescue plan exists, he:

- researches Alderp;
- keeps inventing products after Vanir's earlier advice that he might need them;
- becomes preoccupied with Darkness's unexplained behavior;
- notices the mansion feels unusually large/empty after she leaves;
- fails emotionally to accept the supposedly sensible replacement-frontliner solution.

Thus the correct mechanism is not "Kazuma cares only once a solution is easy." Rather:

> **attachment generates rumination and search behavior before solvability; visible leverage converts that latent search into decisive commitment.**

Vanir's intervention gives him the two missing variables: why Darkness owes the money and exactly how much money is required.

### 4.3 MG02-K02: concrete duty overrides asset preservation

Kazuma has spent several volumes treating money and intellectual property as routes to permanent comfort. V07 creates a direct test: he can preserve his future passive-income/merchant path or liquidate essentially all remaining inventions, rights, and accumulated reward capital to produce the twenty億 Eris needed to remove Darkness's debt leverage.

He sells.

This is stronger than ordinary quest loyalty because the cost attacks an explicitly established life plan. He does not merely risk death in a setting with resurrection; he destroys the economic architecture of his intended retirement.

`MG02-K02` is **CONFIRMED strongly**.

### 4.4 MG02-K04: actual separation overwhelms replacement rhetoric

Darkness's letter asks to leave the party and tells them to find a new frontliner. Kazuma tries to narrate this as rational social sorting: she is a noble from another world, they were lucky to have adventured together, and a new accurate frontliner would improve the party.

The experiment fails on several levels.

- The house feels physically too large and lonely.
- Megumin explicitly says something is missing.
- The recruitment process is sabotaged by Megumin's presentation and by everyone's comparisons to Darkness.
- Dust can perform a plausible front-line role, but his adequacy does not restore the missing relationship.
- News that Darkness will marry Alderp immediately reactivates Kazuma's investigation.
- Kazuma admits that he does not want Alderp to have her.

The epilogue provides the strongest possible closure. Darkness nervously asks, in awkwardly deferential language, whether she may join the party again. Aqua and Megumin answer first, but Darkness still looks to Kazuma. His `当たり前だろ。……お帰り` is answered by her `……た、ただいまっ!!`.

The party/home model is thus not metaphorical. Departure and return are narrated as leaving and returning **home**.

`MG02-K04` and `MG02-E02` are **CONFIRMED strongly**.

### 4.5 MG02-K03 gets support but not a clean dangerous replication

The Sylvia reward and public praise activate status reward immediately. Kazuma announces internally that his era has arrived, spends generously at the guild, and enjoys elevated treatment.

This supports the trigger side of MG02-K03, but V07 does not give as clean a status-induced *dangerous overextension* as V06's kobold pursuit. The Hydra and wedding risks are more strongly explained by relational commitment and problem-solving than by recognition pursuit.

V07 outcome: **PARTIAL SUPPORT**, leave the mechanism open for V08-V09 replication.

### 4.6 Darkness rescue and motive plurality

Kazuma's rescue is not evidence of one purified motive. V07 preserves several:

- attachment to Darkness;
- resentment toward Alderp;
- dislike of being structurally powerless due class/status difference;
- possessive/territorial irritation;
- obligation because Darkness previously paid costs caused by the party;
- desire to prove a solution exists;
- attraction/sexual tension;
- genuine fairness response to the debt circumstances.

The volume becomes stronger if these remain co-causal rather than being reduced to "he secretly loves her" or "he is only repaying a debt."

## 5. Aqua — V07 reconstruction update

### 5.1 MG02-A02 receives one of its cleanest confirmations

The false dragon egg is nearly a designed test of Aqua's salience system.

The instrumental question is whether the object is credible and worth the price. Instead, Aqua is captured by:

- being recognized as part of an elite adventuring party;
- raising a legendary dragon;
- future heroic utility;
- rare-object excitement;
- caretaker identity.

She spends all available personal money and then organizes significant daily behavior around incubating the egg. The "dragon" narrative becomes more rewarding than skeptical verification.

`MG02-A02` is **CONFIRMED strongly**.

### 5.2 Aqua's Darkness rescue efforts constrain the salience model

Aqua's performance orientation does not mean performance always replaces the goal. When Darkness leaves, Aqua repeatedly stages conspicuous magic outside the Dustiness estate in an attempt to lure her out, explicitly comparing the strategy to the Ama-no-Iwato myth. She refuses tips because she is not performing commercially; the performance is being used *instrumentally* for a relationship goal.

This is useful boundary evidence:

> performance salience is most dangerous when the reward competes with a weakly internalized assigned goal. When Aqua strongly owns the relationship goal, performance can become the means rather than the replacement objective.

### 5.3 Sacred/domain competence culminates in the Dustiness curse reveal

V07 gives an exceptionally strong `MG02-A01` confirmation at the end.

Darkness's father has been treated as terminally ill. Ordinary investigation has not identified poisoning. The farewell scene reaches apparent deathbed closure. Aqua then applies `セイクリッド・ブレイクスペル` and removes a powerful demonic curse essentially immediately.

This is important for three reasons:

1. the condition had defeated ordinary social/medical interpretation;
2. the intervention lies exactly inside Aqua's sacred specialization;
3. the result is not ambiguous or lucky—the father's "disease" was a curse connected to Alderp/Maxwell's manipulation.

Aqua once again appears absurdly overpowered when the problem is correctly typed.

`MG02-A01` is **CONFIRMED very strongly**.

### 5.4 Aqua's Hydra reasoning is genuinely competent

After the first Hydra failure, Aqua accurately explains that the purified lake segment will occupy the Hydra with re-pollution. She later contributes the lure function in the final plan.

This reinforces the Gen 0.2 claim that her competence is not restricted to reflexive spell output. She can reason about sacred-domain environmental consequences when the relevant causal variables are salient.

### 5.5 A03 and A04 remain unresolved

V07 mentions that Aqua had allowed cemetery purification duty to lapse until spirit incidents increased. This is useful ordinary reliability evidence, but it is not a clean test of either frozen prediction:

- `MG02-A03` requires confrontation with a harmful consequence and a test of face-saving versus undeniable causation;
- `MG02-A04` requires a repetitive low-status duty plus an attractive one-step maximal shortcut.

The V07 cemetery evidence is delay/forgetting, not a new shortcut experiment. Do not force confirmation.

Both remain **NOT_TESTED at clean V07 level**.

## 6. Megumin — V07 reconstruction update

### 6.1 Explosion remains relationship-conditioned rather than socially isolated

Megumin's desire to kill the Hydra is explicitly intensified because it killed Kazuma. Her continued attacks alongside Darkness combine ordinary Explosion appetite with revenge/attachment.

During the planned battle, however, she reliably waits for the tactical state Kazuma has built. This is another strong `MG02-M03` confirmation: target separation and sequencing transform Explosion from a liability into ideal terminal artillery.

### 6.2 External challenge branch of MG02-M01 is directly confirmed

At the wedding, Alderp argues that Megumin's charged Explosion must be a bluff because nobody would be stupid enough to fire Explosion in the middle of town.

Megumin treats this explicitly as a challenge to her identity:

`この私が怖気づくと！ 爆裂魔法を撃つ事を怖気づくと、本気で言っているんですね！ ... その挑戦を受けましょう!!`

This is almost exactly Trigger A of MG02-M01: outsider questions her willingness/Explosion identity -> defensive challenge acceptance.

She ultimately fires into the sky, which preserves the threat, causes collateral window damage, but does not deliberately blast the wedding crowd. The identity response is real; the output remains socially constrained enough to serve escape.

`MG02-M01` Trigger A: **CONFIRMED strongly**. The relational-guilt inversion branch is not newly tested in V07.

### 6.3 Serious rescue increases rather than reduces theatricality

Megumin's entrance is:

`悪い魔法使いが来ましたよ。悪い魔法使いの本能に従い、花嫁を攫いに来ました`

This is high-value support for the revised J01 rule. Seriousness does not make her plain. The authoritative role she adopts is **the villainous/romantic "bad wizard" who steals the bride**, so seriousness recruits theatrical identity rather than suppressing it.

### 6.4 Relationship action is costly and distributed

Megumin independently chooses criminal exposure to retrieve Darkness. She recruits Yunyun by calling her `我が親友`, correctly predicting that belonging recognition will overcome Yunyun's fear of legal trouble.

This shows two things at once:

- Megumin herself treats party membership as worth major external cost;
- she possesses a precise enough model of Yunyun's belonging reward to activate her reliably.

### 6.5 M02 and M04 remain narrower than generic care

V07 provides suggestive M02 evidence because Megumin redirects the Explosion upward rather than actually using it against the people surrounding her friends. But the exact frozen trigger is insult/challenge where retaliation would impose a concrete companion cost. Since she **does** accept the challenge and partially externalizes collateral damage, the cleanest V07 adjudication is **PARTIAL SUPPORT**, not full confirmation.

`MG02-M04` remains **NOT_TESTED**. No V07 scene recreates a situation where the best tactical Explosion use directly subjects a valued companion to serious harm.

## 7. Darkness / Dustiness Ford Lalatina — V07 reconstruction update

### 7.1 V07 is the definitive test of MG02-D04 so far

The central conflict is exactly the predicted trigger:

- Dustiness family/public obligation;
- dying father;
- enormous debt;
- Alderp's coercive marriage condition;
- party membership and ordinary life.

Darkness does not casually choose one identity. She constructs a self-sacrificial workaround:

1. defeat the Hydra to obtain enough reward to reduce the debt if possible;
2. conceal the reason so companions do not use their resources for her;
3. leave the party when the financial/noble conflict becomes unavoidable;
4. marry Alderp in exchange for debt cancellation;
5. intend to use the marriage position to investigate/limit Alderp from inside;
6. preserve memories of the party as the emotional resource that makes the sacrifice tolerable.

This is almost a textbook confirmation of the Gen 0.2 prediction that she tries negotiation/self-sacrifice/procedural workaround before abandoning either identity.

Even after Kazuma offers money, she refuses partly on distributive-fairness grounds: a noble should not consume commoners' life-earned resources to satisfy a noble-family debt. Her error is not lack of duty. It is **taking unilateral ownership of everyone else's right to choose what they are willing to sacrifice.**

`MG02-D04`: **CONFIRMED very strongly**.

### 7.2 New failure engine: protector paternalism / self-erasure

V07 establishes a Darkness error mechanism that should be tracked independently from masochism:

`responsibility cue -> assumes others must be protected from cost/information -> hides burden -> removes self as liability -> creates larger relational and operational damage`

This can look noble because the motive is prosocial. It is still a decision error.

Her companions would rather knowingly share the cost than be protected through abandonment. Darkness's model of what counts as "not burdening them" is therefore systematically incomplete because it excludes **their preference for her presence**.

### 7.3 D02: belonging threat produces the deepest ordinary vulnerability

Darkness can endure being swallowed by the Hydra, cut herself out, hold the monster's weight, and eroticize some forms of physical domination. None of those situations produces the particular uncertainty seen when she asks whether the party will accept her back.

Her letter tries to formalize her own removal. Her internal wedding viewpoint repeatedly asks whether the others might let her return someday. In Epilogue 1 she uses awkward, uncharacteristic politeness:

`もう一度、私を仲間にしてもらえますか……？`

Even after Aqua and Megumin immediately say she belongs, she still needs Kazuma's answer.

`当たり前だろ。……お帰り`

`……た、ただいまっ!!`

This is exceptionally strong evidence that equal-peer membership is a protected psychological variable.

`MG02-D02`: **CONFIRMED strongly for belonging sensitivity**. The narrow comparative claim that pain *alone* is ineffective coercion remains only indirectly supported rather than cleanly retested.

### 7.4 D03: defensive specialization is decisive against the Hydra

Darkness's value in the successful Hydra plan is not incidental. Her Decoy ability and extreme endurance are the anchor around which every other state transition is built. She later physically supports the Hydra's body long enough to prevent Kazuma being crushed.

The masochistic reward does not cancel competence. Both are active.

`MG02-D03`: **CONFIRMED strongly**.

### 7.5 D01: protection extends across body, family, public, and companions

V07 broadens the protector system again:

- voluntarily enters Hydra to recover Kazuma's corpse;
- goes alone to prevent Megumin taking worsening Hydra risk;
- assumes financial liability for citizens harmed by Destroyer/Aqua flood consequences;
- accepts marriage to protect family/public interests;
- tries to keep companions from spending their resources on her.

The volume therefore supports D01 while also showing why protector duty needs a **procedural/consent constraint** in the reconstruction model. Protection can become harmful if she interprets "I protect you" as "I decide for you what costs you are allowed to bear."

## 8. Kazuma ↔ Darkness — relationship reconstruction

V07 is the highest-density Kazuma/Darkness volume so far, but its relationship should not be flattened into a simple romance label.

### 8.1 Equal treatment is foundational

Darkness's attraction to Kazuma is partly inseparable from his refusal to reclassify her socially after learning her aristocratic identity. He is rude, invasive, sexually interested, retaliatory, and often unfair—but he continues to interact with **Darkness**, not with an untouchable Dustiness heiress.

That equality has enormous value to someone whose status continually creates distance.

### 8.2 Sexual interest is real but constrained by future relationship meaning

The bedroom encounter produces unusually explicit mutual sexual tension. Kazuma is strongly tempted. Darkness provocatively offers that they could "become adults together." Yet the scene does not cross the line.

For Kazuma, this is useful evidence against both extremes:

- he is not merely a harmless asexual teaser;
- he is also not someone whose sexual salience automatically defeats all relational consequence modeling.

The encounter destabilizes into argument because their normal interaction grammar remains active even at high intimacy.

### 8.3 The rescue is both debt repayment and chosen attachment

Kazuma repeatedly describes Darkness's hidden prior payments as a debt he owes. That is genuinely part of the motive. It cannot explain the whole behavior, because he also:

- misses her presence;
- reacts possessively to the marriage;
- researches Alderp;
- liquidates future income;
- commits crimes in public;
- repeatedly denies that he "likes" her while behaving as though permanent loss is unacceptable.

Darkness likewise frames her departure as a way to protect them, yet internally hopes she might someday be admitted back.

The relationship is therefore reciprocal but asymmetric in self-description: both are much more willing to absorb costs for the other than to state a clean claim about what the relationship is.

### 8.4 Return restores the old conflict immediately

The epilogue does not convert rescue into sentimental harmony. Within minutes, Aqua and Megumin turn Kazuma's "body payment" wording into sexual accusation; Darkness weaponizes the bedroom incident; Kazuma counterattacks by revealing that Darkness herself made the provocative offer; the group discovers that the interrupted marriage may still have made Darkness legally divorced and Kazuma gives her `バツネス` as a new insult.

This is not anticlimax. It is evidence for **affection without idealization**: a crisis can prove attachment without permanently changing the interaction temperature.

## 9. Aqua ↔ Darkness, Megumin ↔ Darkness, and town reciprocity

### Aqua ↔ Darkness
Aqua's rescue behavior is inefficient but persistent. Her Ama-no-Iwato-inspired public performances outside the mansion are socially absurd and tactically weak, but relationship-directed. At the wedding, she uses her legitimate clerical role as the insertion route and then openly sabotages the ceremony from the altar.

### Megumin ↔ Darkness
Megumin refuses the premise that Darkness can simply resign by letter. She threatens noble property, recruits Yunyun, enters the wedding as an armed magical deterrent, and calls Darkness `仲間` when embarrassment makes the word difficult to say directly.

### Axel adventurers ↔ party/Darkness
The town's adventurers become a distributed rescue network. At the Hydra fight they accept Kazuma's coordination. At the wedding, Alderp offers money and stable employment to anyone who will stop the fugitives; many simply pretend not to hear. Dust and Keith then manufacture a confrontation and other adventurers pile into Alderp's guards.

This matters for later modeling because the party is no longer socially isolated. Repeated reciprocal interaction has created **community capital** that can substitute for formal authority.

## 10. Vanir, Alderp, Maxwell — incentive intelligence and hidden causal infrastructure

### 10.1 Vanir as an external router

Vanir's intervention is not altruism. His incentives align with Kazuma's because Alderp's collapse, Maxwell's recovery, profitable intellectual property, and rich negative emotion all benefit him.

Yet functionally, he supplies exactly what Kazuma lacks:

- hidden causal information;
- debt amount;
- liquidity through purchase of all remaining IP;
- confidence that a solution exists.

This is another E01-compatible case where the routing/structure source lies outside Kazuma himself.

### 10.2 Darkness's debt is largely the hidden cost of prior party victories

Vanir reveals that Dustiness assets had already absorbed much of the compensation burden from the Destroyer/flood damage. When agricultural residents were abandoned by Alderp after Destroyer's passage, they appealed to the Dustiness family, which borrowed additional money to compensate them.

This is a major retrospective institutional correction:

> the party's apparent "we paid some repair bills and moved on" understanding understated how much of the socialized cost Darkness's family had quietly absorbed.

Darkness's later self-sacrifice is therefore tied to a long-running policy of shielding ordinary citizens and the party from costs.

### 10.3 Maxwell explains the otherwise implausible institutional failure

The interlude reveals that Alderp has been using Maxwell, a demon capable of twisting truth/causal consistency (`辻褄合わせ`, `真実を捻じ曲げる`), to conceal wrongdoing and manipulate outcomes. Darkness later describes her earlier compliance as if she had been hypnotized; after Maxwell is removed, evidence of Alderp's crimes suddenly becomes visible.

This prevents the analysis from misclassifying all prior institutional irrationality as ordinary human incompetence. There is a supernatural causal layer.

Vanir eventually forces contractual closure, Maxwell takes Alderp to hell as payment, and Alderp's assets become available for restitution.

## 11. Chris / Eris — identity deduction and Kazuma social inference

Epilogue 2 gives Kazuma one of his strongest social-pattern inferences.

He notices overlapping behaviors and address rules:

- Chris calls Aqua `さん` while treating Darkness/Megumin differently;
- Eris calls Aqua `先輩` and Darkness without an honorific because Darkness is her close friend;
- hair/eye color and names overlap;
- the same habitual expression and cheek-scratching behavior recur.

Rather than directly accuse Chris, Kazuma uses a leading question: `そういえばエリス様...` and asks where the recovered artifact was hidden. Chris answers reflexively as Eris before realizing the trap.

This is meaningful competence evidence. Kazuma's social perception is not globally poor; it becomes strong when he has repeated behavioral observations and can test a hypothesis cheaply.

The identity reveal should now be routed into the relationship/narrator/competence ledgers, but not overgeneralized into "Kazuma reads people well"—he remains uneven in affective interpretation and self-motive accounting.

## 12. Decision/error analysis

### Kazuma
**Strong decisions**
- reframes Hydra from damage contest to state-control problem;
- recruits social network instead of limiting solution to four-person party;
- researches Alderp before criminal intervention;
- waits for missing debt amount rather than pretending solvability;
- sells IP once the required number is known;
- uses transaction/incentive chaos at wedding instead of direct aristocratic contest;
- reflexively tests the Chris/Eris hypothesis with a low-cost verbal trap.

**Failure/boundary mechanisms**
- status reward after Sylvia encourages grandstanding/generosity; only partial K03 replication;
- Hydra-riding again exposes imperfect modeling of the post-transition physical state;
- sexual salience strongly distorts the Darkness bedroom interaction but does not fully override future relational cost.

### Aqua
**Strong decisions**
- provides accurate Hydra domain reasoning;
- performs the lure/support functions;
- uses magic performance strategically to try to retrieve Darkness;
- instantly removes the demonic curse when properly exposed.

**Failures**
- accepts implausible dragon-egg framing because recognition/rarity/caretaking reward dominates verification;
- allows egg fixation to reshape task availability;

## 15. Japanese voice and register

### 15.1 Darkness: formal self-sacrifice versus peer vulnerability

Darkness's wedding/internal noble mode is controlled, responsibility-centered and status-conscious. Her post-return request is striking because she applies hesitant politeness to people who ordinarily receive blunt peer speech:

`もう一度、私を仲間にしてもらえますか……？`

The awkward `です` register does not indicate distance preference. It indicates **belonging insecurity**: she temporarily treats admission as something the others have authority to grant.

Kazuma's `お帰り` and her `ただいま` collapse that distance immediately.

### 15.2 Megumin: seriousness can heighten theatricality

`悪い魔法使いが来ましたよ。悪い魔法使いの本能に従い、花嫁を攫いに来ました`

This is a clean J01 demonstration. The serious rescue selects a story-role that fits Megumin's self-presentation, so the language becomes theatrical and controlled rather than plain.

The later Alderp challenge similarly activates proud, formal challenge acceptance.

### 15.3 Kazuma: compressed attachment denial

At the wedding Kazuma pays twenty億 and then still corrects Alderp:

`別に大好きなんて事はねーよ！ な、仲間だからっ！ 大事な仲間ってだけだからっ！`

The stammered correction is valuable because the *action* carries more attachment than the label he is willing to use. V07 repeatedly separates behavior from explicit relational naming.

### 15.4 Aqua: ritual role used subversively

Aqua's wedding blessing begins inside the priestly formula but deliberately contaminates it with personal judgment and desire to take Darkness home. This is not failure to know the ritual. It is role appropriation: she knows the structure well enough to sabotage it from inside.

### 15.5 Chris/Eris identity is partly sociolinguistic

Kazuma notices address asymmetries—who receives `さん`, who receives `先輩`, and who is treated as an intimate without honorifics. The identity reveal therefore uses Japanese relationship grammar as evidence, even though the final joke/mechanism is portable.

### 15.6 Translation dependence

Major V07 humor remains predominantly L0-L2:

- L0: situation/causal reversal, rescue, failed separation, social reciprocity;
- L1: phrasing/rhythm and role-parody;
- L2: register/honorific/address behavior, noble versus peer speech;

## 24. V07 governing synthesis

V07 turns the series' "dysfunctional party" premise into a stronger relational claim:

> **By this point, dysfunction is part of the relationship rather than evidence against it. The members may optimize badly, insult one another, conceal motives, create collateral damage and resist emotional clarity; but when efficiency requires losing a person, the group increasingly treats the efficient answer as unacceptable.**

Darkness's attempt to protect everyone by disappearing is therefore not defeated because the others discover she was secretly irrational about duty. It is defeated because her calculation omits something the first six volumes have made real: **the others assign positive value to Darkness herself, not merely to the defensive function she performs.**

That is precisely the kind of hidden variable a reconstruction model must contain if it is to predict these characters rather than merely imitate their jokes.
