# De Lugo: preservation under humiliation and contested authority

Two admitted V03 scenes support a bounded military and institutional reconstruction. Two own diagnostics, current readiness and the directed provisional-council episode were read in full preparation. Private de Lugo cognition is locally available; a continuous later career and civilian life are not. The mature V2 analysis keeps literary responsibility. <a id="dl01"></a>

**DL01 — Select the evacuation or council state with its actual constraints.** At Brest on June 19 de Lugo receives the capital's collapse news while implementing a planned continental evacuation. His private anger, humiliation and wish to stand with comrades coexist with a commander's need to preserve usable forces. In the provisional council he is exhausted by factional reports and quarrels but waits for an opportunity to assert an operational agenda through his office. (D01-D02; E-COUNCIL.)

| Unit | Constraint he knows | Conditional behavior | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evacuation | Remaining troops/resources, secret concentration, delayed elite arrivals and exposure risk | Preserve a future fighting core; check progress; set a bounded departure and later rendezvous possibility | No universal ten-hour rule or guaranteed evacuation outcome |
| Council | Legal office authority versus older officers' seniority and factional resistance | Read material, endure quarrels, choose timing, declare a concrete agenda and ask readiness | A declaration does not establish actual compliance |

The provisional shared mechanism is preservation of a capacity to act despite emotionally costly constraints. It does not imply calm without grief or command without resistance. The units are local developmental states, not enough to supply every later decision, relationship or change in political aims.

<a id="dl02"></a>

**DL02 — Five channels distinguish inner cost from public command.** Private cognition supplies evacuation shame, anger, counterfactual regret, material assessment, the conflict over waiting troops, council fatigue and deliberate patience. Observable behavior includes watching loading with a strained expression, drinking the tea, returning to papers and speaking into a noisy room. Public speech is unusually narrow here: progress questions, a departure/loading order, an agenda declaration and a force-concentration query. External interpretation includes his assessments of subordinates, opponents and factional reception. Institutional consequences are the ordered departure schedule and attempted control of council work. (D01-D02; E-COUNCIL.)

His appraisal of what Republic forces can still do is a local judgment with evidence, not a promise of final victory. His estimate that senior officers will resent him belongs to his cognition; it does not disclose every individual officer's private position. The council's unnamed answer is another speaker's turn. No named reciprocal emotional bond is invented from this group encounter.

Private intensity should not be copied verbatim into delivered command. He recognizes the need to contain a personal urge to fight alongside comrades because others share the same burden. The source therefore supports a meaningful difference between what he feels and what he says, without making every public restraint an effortless or universal trait.

<a id="dl03"></a>

**DL03 — Material loss makes homeland defense and continued resistance different objectives.** De Lugo knows why the theoretical possibility of rebuilding a front is inadequate: the breach is vast, trained forces and heavy artillery are lost, and production capacity cannot sustain the old attritional contest. He thinks about earlier allied intervention, then tells himself that continued regret will not repair the present situation. (D01.)

The attention transition is counterfactual lost opportunity → current means → a possible future force assembled from remaining troops and colonial resources. He wants to preserve the national capacity to fight, even while evacuating the homeland contradicts his preferred image of honorable service. That conflict explains the decision better than either cowardly escape or glorious refusal to retreat.

The model should require actual material evidence for a matching decision. It cannot infer that every bad situation merits abandonment or that patriotic feeling always defeats resource assessment. Nor can it treat his harsh private standard for military service as a universal wish for death. The witnessed response is an emotionally difficult preservation plan and a shift back to operational progress.

<a id="dl04"></a>

**DL04 — Waiting for valued troops competes with keeping the concentration secret.** De Lugo wants every available soldier, particularly experienced elite mages who would expand future options. He also knows that delay increases disclosure risk and that pursued arrivals may lead the enemy to the evacuation force. Waiting can protect people and capability while endangering the assembled core. Abandonment has psychological costs as well as military costs. (D01.)

After the ten-hour arrival estimate, he orders departure in ten hours, calls for maximum loading before then and notes that mages may join at sea. This is a local compromise among time, secrecy, arrivals and mobility. It is not a fixed personal countdown rule, unconditional rescue commitment or proof that the rendezvous actually occurred.

For a new evacuation, identify the arrival estimate, enemy discovery risk, transport capacity and realistic means of later reunion. Change any of those conditions and the appropriate deadline can change. The mechanism is a bounded trade, not loyalty measured only by how long he waits. Outcome assessment must remain separate from the ordered plan.

<a id="dl05"></a>

**DL05 — Fatigue does not remove deliberate council patience.** In the provisional council de Lugo drinks his tea, looks back at paperwork and is privately worn down by reports full of self-praise and colleague-blaming rather than useful battle information. He regards the meeting's quarrels as a threat to action, yet stays because he judges that an opportunity to act can emerge. He waits for timing before declaring the recapture operation for discussion. (D02; E-COUNCIL.)

The causal sequence is unusable institutional talk → frustration and recognition of a possible opening → deliberate endurance → a specific agenda intervention. This preserves both impatience and patience. Neither a naturally serene chair nor a constantly erupting autocrat captures the source. He can choose to endure what he despises because the task gives endurance a purpose.

His criticism of report content is a local source-owned judgment. It cannot establish that every colonial officer is incompetent or that all objections lack substance. Future use should ask whether waiting provides a plausible opening and whether the proposed agenda addresses the actual task; otherwise the same patience may not be justified.

<a id="dl06"></a>

**DL06 — Legal office and social seniority create distinct kinds of power.** De Lugo occupies the senior place through his defense and army office, whose wartime responsibility gives command authority. His earlier military rank and age would place him below many officers in the room by seniority. The source explicitly separates office power from the likelihood of willing compliance by older officers. (D02; E-COUNCIL.)

His plain agenda declaration and question about army concentration attempt to exercise that office rather than solve the social hierarchy by rank alone. The other speaker's request to repeat what he said demonstrates an interaction, but the logged window does not establish settled compliance, a named conspiracy or the full outcome of the meeting.

For an analogue, establish legal responsibility, command scope, counterpart seniority and audience. Formal entitlement can permit a directive without guaranteeing uptake. A younger officer's title alone cannot produce de Lugo's institutional situation, and this typed group relation cannot be converted into individually named friendships, enemies or reciprocal feelings.

<a id="dl07"></a>

**DL07 — Ordinary texture exists within strained work.** Tea, paper review, a sigh and a strained observation of loading make the tasks embodied rather than purely abstract. His effort to preserve usable units, refusal to spend all attention on irrecoverable chances and willingness to endure a frustrating meeting supply constructive professional actions. They coexist with humiliation, anger, grief and resentment. (D01-D02; E-COUNCIL.)

This small range is enough to resist a one-note vengeful caricature. It is not enough for preferred beverages, leisure rituals, family attachment or life after work. The tea is an observed council act, not a favorite. His imagined future resistance remains a plan under constraints, not an ordinary peacetime horizon whose details can be filled freely.

No error/repair comparable to Calandro's late decapitation recognition is directly witnessed here. The available correction is attentional: he halts an unproductive counterfactual and returns to current progress. The model must not invent a confessed tactical mistake or later learning sequence to fill a generic error slot.

**DL09 — Falsifiers must match the preservation and authority conditions.** Actual evacuation despite personal fighting impulse defeats glory-only persistence. A departure deadline plus possible later reunion defeats both endless waiting and indifferent abandonment. Deliberate council patience defeats immediate anger as the only response. Legal command paired with seniority friction defeats automatic obedience. The return from regret to progress defeats a mind permanently trapped in counterfactual mourning. (D01-D02; E-COUNCIL.)

Future tests should compare current means against the preservation objective; the arrival/disclosure trade against the departure decision; a realistic meeting opening against timed agenda assertion; and formal office against actual uptake. A contradiction under matched conditions should revise the mechanism. An outcome known only later cannot become his earlier knowledge.
