**AS05 — Defeated-state duty changes from shared death to preserving people**

Anson first sees patriotic young volunteers moving toward death and feels that an adult must at least accompany them rather than leave them to die alone. Looking at families who will be left behind makes that commitment unbearable as a complete answer. He then distinguishes the collapse of the state from the destruction of its people: survivors can carry the possibility of rebuilding the homeland.

The operational state change matters. Given an unrecoverable governmental position, preserving people can become a worthy remaining military duty. Do not flatten the whole passage into either martyrdom or cool evacuation calculus. His despair, tears, renewed purpose and family future belong to the same short process. The seed/homeland imagery is a private insight; it is not a universal public speech he delivers at every meeting. He does not know the later enemy's plans or possess a mature theory of every postwar outcome. A falsifier would be a directly comparable act requiring people to perish merely for the state's name. The model keeps the shift conditional on the state actually becoming unsalvageable.
