**KL02 — Five channels make the private-access limit concrete.** Klam's private cognition is unavailable throughout. Behavior includes visible hesitation, confusion, rapid recovery, gestures while leading, command-tank movement and risk-aware conversation. Public speech includes authority and purpose questions, statements of preparation, senior safety pleas, tactical orders and formal acceptance. <a id="kl03"></a>

**KL03 — Preparation precedes initiative, while purpose conditions uptake.** The telephone call initially surprises Klam. He asks whether Zettour is issuing an order and whether the action is to rescue friendly troops. Once that purpose is stated he requests the plan, then says he has thoroughly studied the earlier mobile-war proposal and consulted other commanders as requested. (K01.)

The causal sequence is surprising authority claim → purpose clarification → already prepared plan and coordination → an observable increase in readiness to act. The preparation statement supports more than impulsive compliance. The question about rescue supports a task-sensitive uptake; it does not prove that rescue is his only possible objective or that every senior proposal will be accepted.

Zettour explicitly describes an inspection/request authority whose recipient may refuse. Klam's later participation must therefore retain the institutional ambiguity instead of being rewritten as ordinary obedience to a superior's direct command. His rank alone does not disclose the legal relationship. For a new proposal, establish both authority and purpose before extending the mechanism.

<a id="kl04"></a>

**KL04 — Safety objection is compatible with readiness to lead.** At departure Klam offers to take the force out himself and urges Zettour to stay back. He questions whether the senior's exposure is serious or a performance, proposes one of his division's armored vehicles and expresses concern about casualties when infantry transport is discussed. When dismounting at enemy contact is clarified, he recalls the relevant battle report. (K02.)

These are concrete professional questions about protection and execution. They do not establish reluctance to fight. The same commander later leads from a tank and issues vigorous orders. A model of simple timidity would miss the difference between armored command and exposing a senior in an open vehicle.

Zettour's private institutional reason for staying visible, and his withheld assessment of scarce tactical alternatives, remain his own knowledge. Klam is not secretly coauthoring that maneuver merely because the reader knows it. Nor is the senior's explanation proof that every casualty concern was resolved or that the proposed method is universally safe. The clarification changes what was publicly explained, not the whole risk structure.

<a id="kl05"></a>

**KL05 — Forceful surface commands distinguish bypass from required engagement.** On the June 18 advance Klam orders the division to ignore and bypass encountered defensive troops, preserving movement toward the rescue objective. When enemy armor is encountered, he prepares engagement while warning of antitank weapons. Tank elements move ahead to meet the threat, and his own command vehicle remains in gun range. (K03.)

The observed sequence supports an objective-conditioned command rule: avoid being fixed by incidental opposition, but respond to a mobile armored threat that cannot be similarly passed. It does not support indiscriminate attack on everything, reckless absence of precautions or withdrawal from every danger. His energetic gestures and commands are observable behavior, not proof of private delight in violence.

He asks Zettour to move back and then explicitly distinguishes his tank's armor from the senior's vehicle. The warning is specific to unequal protection. It coexists with his own continued combat participation; concern for safety is not a universal demand that every commander avoid the front. A new case must check route, threat and protection rather than repeat the same order blindly.

<a id="kl06"></a>

**KL06 — Acceptance follows clarification without becoming prior omniscience.** Klam relays the air-fleet warning of enemy repositioning and challenges the risk comparison. He asks whether encirclement has already been achieved; Zettour says only a possibility has been gained. Klam then asks what the senior intends and objects that any previously agreed pincer plan should have been communicated to them. Zettour says no such prior plan existed. (K04.)

The exchange supplies a knowledge correction and an information-sharing demand, not a secretly known joint plan. The question about completed encirclement is a tested possibility; it need not become a fixed false belief. The model should preserve what he asks, what is answered and when the information becomes available. It cannot borrow Zettour's faith in staff common training as Klam's full prior knowledge.

His formal response explicitly answers a request and uses an interception formula. Zettour appreciates its preservation of command-chain ambiguity. This is source-supported institutional skill in delivered speech. The acceptance does not erase the earlier objections or certify that the intended encirclement eventually succeeded. Request wording remains meaningful even under pressure and operational urgency.

<a id="kl07"></a>

**KL07 — Brave banter and reported injury have different evidential roles.** After accepting, Klam jokes that the senior should be prepared to attack Valhalla as marshal and general. The exchange supports an ability to speak with gallows humor under shared risk and temporarily familiar senior conversation. It does not report actual promotion, death, a settled desire to die or a civilian belief about the afterlife. (K04.)

The subsequent notice that Klam has been wounded and evacuated, with Brigadier Schultz assuming command, is delivered by Grantz. It establishes a reported institutional event at the window's boundary. It is not Klam's speech, private response or a license to infer severity, recovery, future service or ultimate fate. The model stops where the evidence stops.

No independent tactical confession or private learning process is witnessed. The relevant correction available to the model is public clarification of a possible encirclement and absent prior coordination. Risk questions, formal assent and a joke can coexist without turning the injury into proof of either recklessness or vindicated caution.
