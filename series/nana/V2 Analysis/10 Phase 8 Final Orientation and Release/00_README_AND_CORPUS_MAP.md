# 『NANA』 — README and Corpus Map
## Source boundaries, mature thesis, reader orientation, and evidence-routing guide

## 1. What this corpus is

This directory is the reader-facing orientation layer for the **NANA V2 Japanese-original deep-reading and multi-document synthesis**. It was built sequentially from the manga rather than from a predetermined full-series thesis. The project first read the Japanese tankōbon volume by volume under publication-order spoiler discipline, then stabilized evidence routes, then produced specialist syntheses, and only after those layers had survived counterreading, late-volume revision, the continuation through Chapter 84, and the *NANA 7.8* paratext pass was this README written.

The corpus is therefore designed to preserve two things at once:

1. a coherent literary interpretation of the complete extant narrative available to this project; and
2. an auditable route from high-level interpretation back to the Japanese manga page that supports it.

The governing provenance rule is:

> **Every load-bearing analytical claim should be traceable from synthesis → canonical analytical artifact → evidence entry → primary-source locator → original Japanese page.**

The corpus should not be treated as a substitute for the manga. It is an analytical map of what the supplied sources support, where the evidence is strong, where later material revised earlier readings, and where the hiatus leaves questions genuinely unresolved.

## 2. Source boundary

### Governing primary source

The governing literary source is the **original Japanese manga as supplied in Japanese tankōbon image EPUBs, Volumes 1–21**. The complete manga page—not OCR, not translation, and not an extracted dialogue fragment—is the source authority for:

- dialogue and narration;
- thought text and handwritten text;
- pronouns, address, sentence endings, and register;
- panel order and page turns;
- facial expression and gesture;
- clothing, props, interiors, and spatial relations;
- photographic and media imagery;
- and other formal features that participate in meaning.

The canonical sequential analyses are `NANA_V01_DEEP_READING.md` through `NANA_V21_DEEP_READING.md`.

### Chapters 81–84

The corpus continues narratively through **Chapters 81–84**, but these chapters do not have the same linguistic provenance as Volumes 1–21. Chapters 81–83 and most of Chapter 84 are preserved through supplied English fan translations; six missing printed pages of Chapter 84 were restored from a Spanish fan translation. The continuation is therefore usable for:

- events;
- chronology;
- characterization;
- relationship state;
- panel sequence;
- objects and spaces;
- visual composition;
- future narration;
- and most thematic interpretation.

It is **not** sufficient to establish original-Japanese pronouns, honorifics, sentence-final register, exact lexical echoes, or fine pragmatic distinctions. No Japanese wording is reconstructed backward from those translations. The canonical continuation artifact is `NANA_CH081_084_CONTINUATION_DEEP_READING.md`.

This means the corpus is **narratively complete through the extant Chapter 84 boundary, but original-language complete only through Volume 21**.

### `NANA 7.8`

`Nana 7.8 - Premium Fan Book [Japanese].pdf` is official 2003 paratext, not continuation of the primary narrative. Its contents are separated into evidentiary layers: direct Yazawa commentary, documentary facts, editorial profiles and framing, cultural-reference material, reader reception, and playful metatext. It may illuminate the manga's early architecture and publication culture, but it does not overrule later manga evidence or settle post-2003 developments.

The paratext synthesis is `13_NANA_7_8_PARATEXT_PUBLICATION_CONTEXT_AND_RETROSPECTIVE_REVISION.md`.

### Materials outside the governing corpus

The anime and other adaptations are **not** used as evidence in this synthesis. They may be studied later in adaptation-specific work, but they must not silently replace manga evidence. External historical material appears only where explicitly identified, chiefly in Document 12, and is used to contextualize rather than rewrite the fiction.

## 3. Mature full-series thesis

*NANA* begins as two parallel stories about young women moving toward Tokyo while trying to author themselves through love, work, music, and imagined adulthood. It becomes a story of chosen domesticity in Apartment 707, then a wider relational ensemble organized around BLAST and Trapnest, then a material test of romantic fantasy through pregnancy, housing, money, work, marriage, and childcare, and finally a story in which professional success industrializes private life while retrospective narration converts the characters' present into memory before they know what will be lost.

Its central problem is not reducible to **“Who should end up with whom?”** The work repeatedly asks a more difficult question:

> **How can people remain deeply attached without turning another person into property, function, refuge, provider, rescuer, audience, proof of worth, or destiny?**

The series does not answer by idealizing self-sufficiency. Nana's autonomy is real, but isolation is not freedom. Hachi's domestic desire is real, but being needed cannot safely become the sole proof that she deserves a place in another person's life. Ren and Nana's love is genuine, but romantic singularity cannot make death, sacrifice, jealousy, or fusion into reliable guarantees of permanence. Takumi's competence and provision are real forms of care, but competence becomes ethically dangerous when it is converted into jurisdiction over another person's body, information, time, or future. Nobu's development increasingly requires him to distinguish love from rescue and reclamation. Yasu most consistently models care that returns decision-space to the other person, though even his protection is not free of paternalism. Reira's exceptional voice gives her meaning and institutional power while also making her vulnerable to being treated as indispensable output. Shin's precocious social and sexual experience cannot erase the fact that he is a minor placed in adult systems.

The mature direction of the corpus is therefore best described as **interdependence with refusal intact**: attachment capable of tolerating separateness, plurality, privacy, pace, exit, and voluntary return.

The Nana/Hachi relationship is the structural center of that argument. Their bond is a materially real domestic partnership, chosen family, mutually transformative attachment, and explicitly romantic/queer-coded field of possibility. It receives narrative and formal privileges normally associated with central romance, while the manga never completes the final step of establishing a conventional mutual sexual/romantic couple category. The most accurate compressed formulation remains:

> **Structurally romantic; explicitly queer-coded; domestically and familially real; categorically unfinished.**

The final extant movement sharpens this rather than resolving it. After Ren's death, Hachi becomes practical shelter for a grieving community. Nana begins to show behavioral movement again, but eating, complaining, singing, or participating cannot by themselves prove psychological recovery. The closing Chapter 84 question turns Hachi's deepest insecurity into the final unresolved relational problem: if Nana appears no longer broken enough to require rescue, does Hachi still believe she has a legitimate place beside her?

That gives the extant corpus one of its clearest final questions:

> **Can belonging remain valid when necessity decreases?**

The future-narration layer makes the question more painful but not more settled. The manga directly establishes that future Nana is alive and singing abroad; her absence therefore cannot itself be treated as evidence of death. Hachi and the others continue to preserve places, rituals, objects, photographs, memories, and routes of return. Yet the causal bridge from Chapter 84 to Nana's later disappearance has not been published. The exact reason Nana leaves, the duration and conditions of that absence, her knowledge of the search for her, the final state of Hachi's marriage, and the eventual social form of Nana/Hachi remain unresolved.

The corpus therefore adopts the following hiatus rule:

> **The extant manga establishes trajectory without destination.**

The publication hiatus is not interpreted as intended thematic closure, and no unwritten ending is promoted into canon.

## 4. Evidence and epistemic conventions

The project separates the **kind of claim** from the **confidence attached to the claim**.

Evidence classes are:

- **TF — Textual fact:** directly shown or unambiguously stated.
- **SI — Strong inference:** multiple independent signals make serious alternatives unlikely.
- **TI — Thematic interpretation:** literary synthesis built from textual evidence.
- **CB — Character belief:** what a character believes; not automatically narrative fact.
- **UA — Unresolved ambiguity:** intentionally or evidentially unsettled.
- **FP — Prospective/foreshadowing signal:** suggests a later development without establishing it.
- **RC — Retrospective correction:** later analyzed evidence changes an earlier reading.
- **VJ — Value judgment:** explicit normative or critical evaluation by the analysis.

The separate A–E epistemic scale is:

- **A — directly established**
- **B — strongly established**
- **C — strongly implied**
- **D — plausible inference**
- **E — speculation/theory**

A directly quoted belief can therefore be `CB/A` as evidence that the character believes it while remaining weak evidence that the belief is objectively true. This distinction is essential for Hachi's interpretations of Takumi and Reira, future-narrator guilt, characters' accounts of one another's motives, and other places where subjective conviction is narratively important without becoming omniscient fact.

Read `00 → 14`, then descend only into the specialist document relevant to the question. This is the best route for later cross-series comparisons, character modeling, and scenario analysis.

Read `00 → 01 → 02 → 03 → 04 → 05 → 06 → 07 → 08 → 09 → 10 → 11 → 12 → 13 → 14`.

Read `00 → 04 → 02 → 03 → 06 → 07 → 09 → 10 → 11 → 14`.

This preserves the distinction between relationship form, each woman's independent psychology, material conditions, ethics, future narration, language, and visual grammar.

### Evidence/audit route

Begin with the mature claim in `14` or the relevant specialist document, move to `16` for a compact retrieval route, then to `15` or the cited volume artifact, and finally to the original Japanese page.

### Japanese-language route

Read `10`, then use `16` to locate Japanese lexical anchors and the owning volume evidence entry. Do not use Chapters 81–84 for original-Japanese microanalysis.

```text

→ NANA_VXX_DEEP_READING.md
→ NANA_VXX_E###
```

For example, a Nana/Hachi claim may route as:

```text

→ NANA_V03_DEEP_READING.md
→ NANA_V03_E002
→ Chapter 5
→ EPUB spine 50
→ 「あたしのナナへの憧れは かなり恋に近いものだったと思います」
```

Document 16 is the preferred entry point when searching by Japanese phrase, character, relationship, motif, object, or thematic system. Document 15 is the preferred entry point when asking how a conclusion changed across publication order or whether an older volume-level claim was later strengthened, narrowed, or corrected.

A locator is never guessed. If a printed page number was not independently visible and verified, the corpus uses the verified EPUB spine-page index instead.

## 8. Naming and terminology conventions

**大崎ナナ / Osaki Nana** is normally written as **Nana** when unambiguous. **小松奈々 / Komatsu Nana** is normally written as **Hachi** once Nana and Hachi share the narrative, because that reflects the work's relational naming system and prevents confusion between the two protagonists.

**BLAST** refers to BLACK STONES. **Trapnest** is retained in its standard romanized form. **707** means Apartment/Room 707 and may denote the physical apartment, the household formed there, or the later ritual/archive space depending on context; the surrounding prose should specify which level is meant.

**Future Nana** and **future Hachi** refer to the later temporal layer shown or narrated by the manga. “Future narrator” does not mean omniscient narrator.

**Structural romance** means that a relationship receives narrative, formal, emotional, and developmental privileges conventionally assigned to a central romance. It does **not** by itself establish conventional couple status, mutual sexual desire, or a final romantic endpoint.

**Chosen family**, **domestic partnership**, **romantic/erotic coding**, **possession**, and **categorical romance** are kept separate. The Nana/Hachi analysis depends on preserving those distinctions rather than collapsing them into either “only friends” or “secretly already a conventional couple.”

**Care** means an act or structure that can genuinely benefit another person. **Jurisdiction** means the presumed right to decide for that person. The fact that an act provides real care does not automatically establish legitimate jurisdiction.

**Dependency** is not treated as synonymous with pathology. The ethical question is whether dependence remains compatible with agency, plurality, refusal, and exit.

## 9. What the corpus does and does not claim

The corpus does claim, at high confidence, that future Nana is alive; Nana/Hachi carries explicit romantic and queer coding; Hachi genuinely loves Takumi; Takumi's provision and care coexist with serious sexual and relational violations; Hachi chooses continuation of her pregnancy before Takumi's resources settle the feasible household form; Ren's death does not fulfill the earlier death-together fantasy; 707 is material infrastructure rather than merely an abstract symbol of found family; and late *NANA* increasingly values distributed care over solitary rescue.

The corpus does **not** claim that the cause of Nana's disappearance is known, that the hiatus endpoint is an intended ending, that Nana/Hachi has been canonically established as a conventional sexual couple, that Hachi's final romantic choice is settled, that Takumi's feelings can be reduced to either “he does not love her” or “love excuses harm,” that Ren intentionally dies, that future absence proves death, or that editorial/paratextual wording in *NANA 7.8* overrides later manga evidence.

When the extant text cannot decide, the corpus preserves the question instead of manufacturing closure.

## 10. Final orientation

The most useful single sentence for carrying the corpus forward is:

> **『NANA』 is a story about people trying to build durable lives from love, friendship, art, work, family, money, bodies, and public roles, while learning that permanence cannot safely be secured by turning another person into something one owns, needs, rescues, provides for, performs for, or cannot live without.**

Its mature alternative is not detachment. It is connection that survives the other person's separateness.

That is why the unfinished ending matters so much. The work has already shown that love can be real without being sufficient, that homes can survive as routes of return after households dissolve, that people can be alive and still absent, that care can coexist with domination, that memory can preserve and distort at once, and that being needed is not the same thing as belonging.

The extant narrative stops before it can answer whether its characters can fully live by what they have begun to learn.

The corpus therefore ends where the manga does: not with a manufactured final pairing or a solved future, but with a disciplined distinction between **what has been established, what has been strongly implied, and what remains unwritten**.

## 11. Corpus construction history and release status

The analytical corpus was built in eight deliberately separated stages. **Phase 0** established the source inventory, integrity assumptions, and governing method. **Phase 1** performed the sequential volume-by-volume reading and then extended that reading through the extant continuation. **Phase 2** repaired legacy provenance gaps and froze the evidence ledger so later synthesis would not silently invent locators. **Phase 3** established the large structural argument and the two protagonist studies, culminating in the Nana/Hachi relationship synthesis. **Phase 4** widened the frame to the ensemble, material adulthood, and music/celebrity institutions. **Phase 5** separated ethics, narration, Japanese language, manga form, and gender/social context so that no single interpretive vocabulary would be forced to carry questions it could not answer alone. **Phase 6** admitted *NANA 7.8* only after the manga-derived conclusions had stabilized. **Phase 7** compressed the corpus into matrices and retrieval indexes. **Phase 8** wrote this orientation layer, audited the complete analytical tree, generated manifests and checksums, and froze the delivery package.

That construction order matters. The later documents are not meant to erase the earlier spoiler-bounded readings. A Volume 3 inference that was reasonable before Volume 15 may later become incomplete, but the project records that change as retrospective correction rather than pretending the earlier uncertainty never existed. Likewise, *NANA 7.8* is not allowed to retroactively dictate interpretations that the later manga complicates. The corpus treats literary understanding as cumulative revision rather than as a single timeless answer.

### v1.0 archival freeze

The **v1.0 analytical release is immutable**. Its final audit found no unresolved evidence-ID references in the numbered synthesis, no missing evidence-to-locator routes in the sequential corpus, no source/artifact checksum mismatch in the frozen Volume 1–21 evidence ledger, no exact long-form argument duplication across Documents 00–16, no known naming/romanization variant conflict under the corpus conventions, and no residual chat-format or draft-marker markup in the canonical analytical files.

One corrective action is disclosed rather than hidden. The originally generated Phase-5 copy of `10_JAPANESE_VOICE_ADDRESS_RELATIONAL_LANGUAGE_AND_LEXICAL_SYSTEM.md` was no longer recoverable from the active local workspace or Drive mirror when Phase 8 began. Before release, Document 10 was reconstructed from the frozen Japanese volume evidence, the mature neighboring syntheses, and the completed lexical/source index; its cited evidence routes were revalidated, and the file itself carries an explicit reconstruction note in YAML. The v1.0 checksum therefore governs this reconstructed canonical release copy rather than the earlier unavailable artifact.

The final archive contains **analysis and audit material only**. Copyrighted manga EPUB/CBZ files and the *NANA 7.8* PDF are not redistributed. Their identities are preserved in `SOURCE_INVENTORY.md` through filenames, hashes, provenance notes, and direct-revalidation status where the source payload remained mounted during Phase 8.

Later discoveries must be handled as versioned revisions rather than silent edits. The clearest trigger would be stronger primary evidence—especially original Japanese pages for Chapters 81–84, newly published canonical manga material, or an official source that materially changes an existing claim. Such a change should create **v1.1 or later**, retain v1.0 as a historical analytical state, and identify exactly which claims, locators, or language-sensitive conclusions changed.

The purpose of this policy is not bureaucratic neatness. It protects the distinction on which the whole project depends:

> **new evidence may change interpretation, but interpretation must never pretend the evidence was always what we now know it to be.**
