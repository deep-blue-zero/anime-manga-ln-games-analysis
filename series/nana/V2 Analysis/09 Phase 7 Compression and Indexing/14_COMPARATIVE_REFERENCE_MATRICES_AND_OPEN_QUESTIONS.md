# 『NANA』
## Comparative Reference Matrices and Open Questions
### Compression layer for cross-character, cross-relationship, and cross-series analysis

## 1. Purpose and governing synthesis

This document is the compression layer of the *NANA* V2 corpus. It does not replace the specialist arguments in Documents 01–13, and it does not reopen the sequential manga reading. Its purpose is to preserve the mature conclusions in a form that can be reused quickly for later comparative work without flattening the work into a list of character traits or relationship labels.

The most stable full-series synthesis is:

> **NANA is a story about young adults trying to make durable lives from love, friendship, work, art, family, money, bodies, and public roles, while gradually learning that no relationship, identity, or institution can safely guarantee permanence by converting another person into property, function, refuge, audience, provider, rescuer, or destiny.**

The series begins with two women seeking forms of self-authorship through migration and attachment. It develops those desires through the materially bounded intimacy of Apartment 707, the professionalization of BLAST and Trapnest, pregnancy and marriage, public celebrity, grief, and future retrospection. Across those layers, the work repeatedly tests the same distinction:

> **connection is necessary; ownership is not the same thing as connection.**

The mature ethical movement is not toward self-sufficiency. Nana, Hachi, Ren, Takumi, Nobu, Yasu, Reira, Shin, and the wider cast all demonstrate that people are made through relationships and institutions. The more defensible endpoint is **revisable interdependence**: attachment in which the other person remains capable of refusal, separate priorities, other relationships, changing roles, and voluntary return.

The extant corpus does not complete that movement. Ren's death destroys the old future; Nana survives into the depicted future but is absent from the 707 network; Hachi waits; the group searches; Chapters 81–84 show immediate post-death maintenance but not the bridge to later separation. The hiatus therefore leaves a highly legible trajectory and a deliberately incomplete destination.

# 2. Strongest series-level conclusions

The following propositions are the most reusable conclusions after the sequential reading, specialist synthesis, paratext pass, and adversarial checks.

1. **The two Nanas are dual protagonists from the work's early conception.** Their same-name convergence is not a later thematic accident. *NANA 7.8* confirms that the paired architecture and future-retrospective form were already conscious by early 2003.
2. **Apartment 707 is the central material architecture of early intimacy.** It succeeds not because it erases boundaries but because it combines separate rooms, privacy, shared costs, mutual aid, food, music, and elective access.
3. **The work's central relational problem is permanence under freedom.** Characters repeatedly want voluntary love to become certain enough that loss, refusal, rival attachments, work, mortality, or change can no longer threaten it.
4. **Possession and care are not opposites.** Many of the most possessive actions originate in sincere care; the ethical question is whether care preserves the other's agency or converts need into jurisdiction.
5. **Hachi's domesticity is a genuine vocation, not merely surrender.** Its ethical vulnerability lies in the way usefulness and being needed can become conditions of worth.
6. **Nana's independence is genuine and defensive simultaneously.** Her growth is not from dependence to independence but from refusal-based autonomy toward reciprocal dependence without engulfment.
7. **Takumi's competence and his coercion are both real.** Material provision, planning, protection, and family commitment do not erase sexual violence, information control, or the tendency to treat operational competence as authority over other people's decisions.
8. **Nobu's maturation is a movement from rescue fantasy toward accompaniment.** He increasingly learns that love may require remaining present without reclaiming or overruling the person he loves.
9. **Yasu provides the clearest recurring model of care oriented toward restoring agency rather than preserving dependency, though he is not free of paternalism or unilateral intervention.**
10. **Music is never merely escape or self-expression.** It is vocation, livelihood, identity, group responsibility, public image, bargaining power, memory, refuge, and sometimes institutional captivity.
11. **Celebrity expands visibility while reducing ordinary freedom.** Public images can be authentic and still become detachable assets used by labels, media, fans, advertisers, and strangers.
12. **Future narration is witness, not omniscience.** Future Hachi and future Nana possess later knowledge and later regret, but their interpretations do not become objective explanations of causality.
13. **Future Nana is alive in the depicted future.** Her absence from the 707 network is established; its full cause, duration, and final meaning are not.
14. **Ren's death does not fulfill the earlier death-together fantasy.** It destroys that fantasy by forcing the surviving characters to live through asymmetrical loss.
15. **The Nana/Hachi bond is structurally romantic, explicitly queer-coded, domestically and familially real, and categorically unfinished.** Friendship and chosen family are true descriptions but do not exhaust the text's romantic grammar; conventional mutual couple status is not established.
16. **Gender roles in NANA function as scripts, resources, pleasures, constraints, and bargaining positions at once.** The mature question is whether a person can inhabit a role without surrendering the right to revise or refuse what is done in its name.
17. **The manga's objects and spaces acquire meaning historically rather than symbolically by dictionary.** Keys, rings, photographs, rooms, clothes, hands, phones, food, and stages change meaning with access, observer, body, and accumulated history.
18. **The work repeatedly replaces fantasies of total solution with maintenance.** Cooking, waiting, letters, work, medication, hospital visits, rehearsal, ordinary conversation, and small acts of presence become more ethically credible than heroic rescue.
19. **The late series increasingly values plurality.** A person may love more than one person in different ways, belong to more than one home, be artist and partner and parent, need care from several people, and continue changing without one bond invalidating the others.
20. **The hiatus is an epistemic boundary, not a thematic answer.** The extant manga allows strong claims about direction and unresolved problems; it does not authorize invented end states.

# 4. Attachment, autonomy, dependency, care, and possession matrix

| Character | What security looks like early | Dependency form | Possessive/control tendency | More mature relational possibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nana** | Someone chooses her and does not leave | Ren, Hachi, Yasu, BLAST, stage, home distributed across different needs | Jealousy, tests, collar/pet grammar, desire for irreplaceable priority | Freely renewed connection among people who retain separate lives |
| **Hachi** | Being chosen, needed, missed, and useful | Lovers, home, family role, caregiving network | Rescue, mediation, making herself indispensable | Belonging that survives reduced necessity |
| **Ren** | Nana and music remain one continuous life | Nana, Trapnest, drugs, artistic identity | Fusion, death-together language, sacrifice as proof | Love that admits multiple obligations and survival beyond merger |
| **Takumi** | Systems remain functional because he can solve them | Work, Reira/Trapnest, Hachi/family, professional control | Decision substitution, money, information, sexual access, scheduling | Care that distinguishes provision from authority over the person |
| **Nobu** | Love should reveal the right path | Hachi, BLAST, moral self-image | Rescue fantasy and counterfactual claim | Presence without possession or opportunistic reclamation |
| **Yasu** | People have somewhere stable to return | BLAST/Nana and chosen responsibility | Protective intervention, information management | Support that returns choice to the person concerned |
| **Reira** | Someone sees the person beneath the voice | Takumi, Trapnest, performance, later Shin | Emotional sanctuary demands; difficulty tolerating unavailable love | Reciprocal adult relation not founded on idealization or rescue |
| **Shin** | Being useful, desirable, paid, or wanted proves value | Sexual economy, BLAST, Reira, chosen family | Provocation and emotional testing more than structural control | Accountability, patience, sobriety, care for others without performance of invulnerability |

A recurring comparative principle emerges:

> **The series does not condemn dependency. It condemns the conversion of dependency into title.**

Needing another person is often treated as unavoidable and human. The ethical danger begins when need is used to establish a right to their body, future, exclusivity, labor, silence, availability, or identity.

# 9. Major ethical-conflict matrix

| Conflict | Strongest series formulation | Typical false binary |
|---|---|---|
| **Autonomy vs dependency** | Mature autonomy includes chosen dependence; it is not isolation | independent / needy |
| **Care vs control** | Care becomes domination when vulnerability is converted into jurisdiction | caring / abusive as mutually exclusive categories |
| **Consent vs relationship continuity** | Consent is encounter-specific; later love, marriage, or prior sex does not retroactively authorize an earlier act | loving relationship / sexual violence |
| **Protection vs agency** | Protection is ethically stronger when it preserves refusal and decision space | protect / abandon |
| **Material provision vs freedom** | Resources expand feasible choices but can also raise exit costs and authority asymmetries | provider / oppressor |
| **Possession vs commitment** | Commitment seeks continuity through renewed choice; possession seeks certainty by reducing choice | devoted / detached |
| **Truth vs privacy** | Truth can be coercively acquired, decontextualized, or weaponized; privacy is not synonymous with deception | honest / secretive |
| **Art vs industry** | Professionalization can enlarge artistic agency while increasing institutional claims on the artist | authentic art / corrupt commerce |
| **Fandom vs entitlement** | Nonreciprocal attachment is legitimate when restraint survives lack of access | fan / stalker as fixed social types |
| **Gender role vs self-authorship** | A role can be sincerely desired and structurally unequal at the same time | liberated / traditional |
| **Grief vs recovery** | Functioning, singing, eating, working, or joking can coexist with severe unresolved grief | recovered / broken |
| **Waiting vs arrested life** | Waiting can preserve a place for return without coercion, but can also suspend the waiter's future | loyalty / moving on |

The common test underneath these conflicts is personhood:

> **Does the relationship or institution still permit the person to answer back, refuse, change, leave, return, hold other loyalties, and remain legible outside the function they perform for someone else?**

# 10. Strongest counterreadings and how far they survive

## 10.1 “NANA is fundamentally a romance about choosing the correct partner”

This survives only weakly. Romantic choices matter enormously, but the work repeatedly makes the consequences depend on money, pregnancy, work, fame, family, addiction, grief, and prior attachments. The strongest organizing question becomes how relationships coexist with personhood, not which candidate wins.

## 10.2 “Nana is the liberated woman; Hachi is the traditional woman”

Rejected as a stable binary. Nana's refusal to follow Ren is authentic autonomy, but she also seeks binding certainty in love. Hachi's domesticity is conventionally feminine, but she deliberately authors it and later makes domestic competence portable across networks and paid craft. Both women combine agency and dependency in different forms.

## 10.3 “Hachi simply chose the wrong man”

Too reductive. Nobu is a genuine love and ethically preferable in some dimensions, but he is not a magical counterfactual solution to pregnancy, money, communication, or Hachi's own attachment patterns. Takumi offers real recognition, resources, family structure, and genuine affection while also committing severe violations.

## 10.4 “Takumi's love proves he is misunderstood” / “Takumi's abuse proves none of his care is real”

Both fail. The corpus requires simultaneous recognition of genuine attachment and genuine coercion. His strongest positive and negative traits are structurally related: he sees problems clearly, acts decisively, and too often treats the ability to solve as the right to decide.

## 10.5 “Nana/Hachi are only friends”

Insufficient. Friendship and chosen family are textually true, but the accumulated first-love language, `恋` vocabulary, counterfactual heterosexual framing, jealousy, pair objects, love-letter naming, hero/heroine reciprocity, red-thread/ring grammar, future direct address, and principal separation/reunion structure exceed an ordinary-friendship description.

## 10.6 “Nana/Hachi are secretly a settled canonical lesbian couple”

Also insufficient. The manga does not establish mutual conventional romantic declaration, sexual partnership, or final couple status. Queer ambiguity should not be resolved in advance by converting structural romance into categorical certainty.

## 10.7 “Chosen family explains the Nana/Hachi bond completely”

Partially true but incomplete. Chosen family explains household, belonging, ritual, and incorporation into a wider social network; it does not exhaust the dyad-specific romantic grammar.

## 10.8 “Ren is Nana's only real love, so Hachi is secondary”

False as a structural hierarchy. Ren is Nana's explicit romantic/sexual/artistic love and cannot be replaced by Hachi. Hachi nonetheless occupies a different central axis: home, recognition, future address, restart, and the work's dual-protagonist architecture.

## 10.9 “The industry corrupts authentic art”

Too simple. BLAST actively seeks professional success; Trapnest originates in real artistic desire. The sharper issue is that institutions can increase artistic reach while accumulating claims over schedule, body, privacy, image, and output.

## 10.10 “The future narration tells us the ending”

Rejected. It tells us later conditions, later memories, and selected survivals. It does not provide the missing causal bridge or final relational configuration.

## 10.11 “Nana's post-Ren singing proves recovery”

Not supported. Behavioral animation can coexist with surrender, dissociation, or severe grief; Yasu explicitly warns against reading visible movement too quickly as recovery.

## 10.12 “NANA 7.8 reveals what Yazawa intended all along”

Only in limited architectural respects. It confirms the paired-protagonist conception, early retrospective form, fate-conscious framing, multiple viewpoints, and material-cultural interests. It also explicitly warns against total predestination by acknowledging that characters may respond differently than anticipated. It cannot answer post-Volume-7 outcomes.

# 11. Confidence-graded propositions

For comparative reuse, the corpus should use the following scale:

- **A — directly established:** explicit event, directly depicted future condition, verified original-language formulation, or documentary fact.
- **B — strongly established synthesis:** multiple independent A-level routes converge on the same interpretation.
- **C — strong inference:** best explanation of several routes but not directly stated.
- **D — plausible unresolved hypothesis:** compatible with the evidence but underdetermined.
- **E — speculation / prohibited as settled claim:** requires missing events, unshown causality, or categorical closure the extant corpus does not provide.

### A-level propositions

- Nana and Hachi are dual protagonists with deliberately parallel architecture.
- Nana survives into the depicted future.
- Ren dies after the March 2002 crash; the death is not established as intentional suicide.
- Hachi chooses to continue the pregnancy before Takumi's material support fully determines the feasible household structure.
- Takumi commits non-consensual sexual acts against Hachi in the analyzed encounters.
- Nana/Hachi contains explicit romantic and queer coding.
- Shin is a minor; adult responsibility in the Shin/Reira relation remains Reira's.
- 707 is materially structured through shared cost, separate rooms, privacy, and mutual aid.
- Future Hachi, future Nana, and the 707 network remain oriented toward a missing relationship and possible return.

### B-level propositions

- The mature ethical center of the series is interdependence with refusal intact.
- Hachi's deepest recurring vulnerability is confusing being needed with secure belonging.
- Nana's deepest recurring autonomy contradiction is wanting free choice to produce permanent certainty.
- Takumi's characteristic danger is competence becoming jurisdiction.
- Nobu matures from rescue toward accompaniment.
- Yasu is the strongest recurring autonomy-supportive caregiver in the main ensemble.
- Celebrity creates more public visibility and less ordinary bodily/privacy freedom.
- Chosen family is essential to Nana/Hachi but does not exhaust it.
- Ren's death revises rather than fulfills the earlier fusion/death fantasy.

### C-level propositions

- Nana's later disappearance probably arises from several interacting factors rather than a single cause.
- The late corpus increasingly privileges plurality over exclusive totalization as a relational ideal.
- The anticipated Nana/Hachi reunion, if written consistently with the extant ethical movement, would likely require permission and recognition rather than restoration of the old roles.
- Hachi's future family arrangement may preserve legal or social continuity while containing unresolved marital distance.

### D-level hypotheses

- Nana may be in Britain or another English-speaking environment indicated by the available geographic clues.
- Hachi and Takumi may eventually separate, renegotiate, or remain in an unconventional family structure.
- Nana/Hachi may eventually adopt a social form beyond ordinary friendship.
- Nobu/Hachi may experience later romantic reconsideration.

### E-level claims that must not be frozen as canon

- Nana dies off-panel before the future scenes.
- Hachi's failure to save Nana is the proven cause of Nana's disappearance.
- Nana/Hachi are definitively the endgame conventional couple.
- Hachi definitively leaves Takumi for Nobu.
- Nana's apparent post-Ren activity proves complete recovery.
- Ren intentionally crashes in order to die.
- *NANA 7.8* provides a hidden authorial solution to the unfinished manga.

# 12. Questions answerable despite the hiatus

The hiatus does **not** make the entire work indeterminate. The extant corpus already answers the following questions strongly enough for stable comparative use:

1. **Are Nana and Hachi the work's principal dual protagonists?** Yes.
2. **Is Nana/Hachi unusually central beyond ordinary friendship?** Yes; structurally, domestically, emotionally, and through explicit romantic/queer coding.
3. **Does the manga establish Nana/Hachi as a conventional romantic/sexual couple?** No.
4. **Does Nana love Ren genuinely?** Yes; romantically, sexually, artistically, and domestically.
5. **Does Hachi love Takumi in ways not reducible to coercion or money?** Yes.
6. **Do Takumi's later affection and marriage erase the non-consensual acts?** No.
7. **Was Hachi's pregnancy decision simply imposed by Takumi?** No; continuation is already her decision before his resources reshape feasibility.
8. **Is 707 merely a symbol of friendship?** No; it is a concrete economic and spatial design whose symbolism grows from material practice.
9. **Is the series anti-work or anti-industry?** No; it values vocation and professional competence while criticizing institutional capture.
10. **Is future Nana alive?** Yes.
11. **Does future narration establish the cause of Nana's absence?** No.
12. **Does Ren's death complete a romantic death pact?** No; it creates an asymmetrical survivor problem.
13. **Is Hachi's domesticity narratively dismissed as immaturity?** No; it develops into a major form of labor, care, craft, and social infrastructure.
14. **Is Nana's independence equivalent to not needing people?** No.
15. **Does the manga treat care as automatically virtuous?** No; it repeatedly distinguishes care that preserves agency from care that claims jurisdiction.
16. **Is gender historically relevant to the feasible choices?** Yes, while not mechanically determining individual desire.
17. **Did Yazawa plan at least some future-retrospective architecture early?** Yes, according to direct 2003 commentary.
18. **Does early planning mean every character outcome was fixed?** No; the same commentary explicitly leaves character responses contingent.

# 14. Open comparative questions that remain analytically useful

Some questions are worth preserving even if later chapters never answer them because they organize the work comparatively.

### 14.1 Nana and Hachi

The deepest extant question is not simply whether they love each other. It is whether their bond can survive the loss of the old proofs of love: living together, being needed, matching, rescuing, possessing privileged access, or occupying the same daily world.

> **Can belonging remain valid when necessity decreases?**

That question is useful far beyond romance. It describes the transition from dependence-based security toward chosen relation among autonomous adults.

### 14.2 Hachi and Takumi

The unresolved question is not whether the relationship contains love or abuse; both are already established. It is whether a family structure built under asymmetrical money, information, sexual power, and work obligations can become genuinely revisable from within.

> **Can provision stop functioning as authority?**

### 14.3 Nana after Ren

The unresolved question is not whether she can “get over” Ren. The manga's late ethic rejects that simplification.

> **Can survival become something Nana authors for herself rather than something she owes the dead and the living?**

### 14.4 Reira

Reira's comparative question is whether a person whose voice has become economically and emotionally indispensable can ever become ordinary enough to participate in reciprocal life.

> **Can a person cease being infrastructure without ceasing to matter?**

### 14.5 Shin

Shin's mature question is whether adulthood can be reconstructed after being performed too early.

> **Can responsibility be learned without continuing to punish the child who had to imitate it?**

### 14.6 The ensemble

The full cast increasingly points toward one social question:

> **Can a network remain intimate without requiring one person, one couple, one band, one home, or one caregiver to carry the entire burden of belonging?**

This is the strongest bridge from *NANA* into comparative analysis with other ensemble dramas.

# 16. Final comparative synthesis

*NANA* is unusually useful for comparative analysis because it refuses to let one vocabulary explain adult life.

Romance is not enough because marriage can contain love and coercion.

Friendship is not enough because Nana/Hachi carries domestic, familial, jealous, romantic, and future-oriented functions that the friendship label cannot exhaust.

Autonomy is not enough because Nana's strongest self-authored acts do not eliminate her need for others.

Dependency is not enough because Hachi can be highly relational and still make consequential, self-authored decisions.

Care is not enough because care can preserve personhood or become jurisdiction.

Money is not enough because material provision can create genuine security while intensifying inequality.

Art is not enough because vocation can liberate and enclose the same person.

Truth is not enough because photographs, publicity, and confession can be accurate while decontextualizing a life.

Family is not enough because legal, biological, domestic, and felt kinship repeatedly diverge.

Gender is not enough because social scripts are both lived constraints and materials people deliberately use.

Memory is not enough because future narrators remember vividly while revising, idealizing, regretting, and sometimes misunderstanding the past.

Even love is not enough if love requires another person to stop being separate.

The strongest mature formulation is therefore:

> **NANA treats adulthood as the construction of a plural life in which love, home, art, work, family, memory, and care can remain meaningful without any one of them acquiring total jurisdiction over the person.**

The series' late movement toward hands, waiting, distributed care, revisable roles, ordinary maintenance, and voluntary return does not abolish longing for permanence. It makes the cost of trying to manufacture permanence increasingly visible.

That is also why the unfinished state matters without becoming the work's “meaning.” The extant manga has already shown enough to distinguish binding from connecting, rescue from accompaniment, provision from authority, memory from omniscience, public image from personhood, and being needed from being allowed to stay.

What it has not shown is whether the characters can finally live those distinctions.

That is the boundary Phase 7 must preserve.
