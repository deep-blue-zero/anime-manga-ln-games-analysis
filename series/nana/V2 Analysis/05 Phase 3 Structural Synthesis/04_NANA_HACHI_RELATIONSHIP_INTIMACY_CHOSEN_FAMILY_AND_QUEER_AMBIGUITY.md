# 04 — Nana/Hachi: Intimacy, Chosen Family, and Queer Ambiguity
## The central relationship as chosen home, structural romance, and unresolved category

### Scope and governing question

This document is the definitive Phase-3 relationship study of **大崎ナナ / Osaki Nana** and **小松奈々 / Komatsu Nana, “Hachi”** across the complete extant primary-fiction corpus: Japanese tankōbon Volumes 1–21 and the uncollected continuation through Chapter 84.

It inherits three stabilized foundations:

1. `01_SERIES_ARCHITECTURE_CHRONOLOGY_AND_VOLUME_PROGRESSION.md` establishes that Nana/Hachi is the work’s most persistent temporal, domestic, and future-facing axis.
2. `02_OSAKI_NANA_CHARACTER_SELFHOOD_LOVE_ART_AND_DEPENDENCY.md` establishes Nana as a self-author whose attachment repeatedly turns voluntary choice into a test of permanence.
3. `03_KOMATSU_NANA_HACHI_CHARACTER_DESIRE_HOME_MOTHERHOOD_AND_ADULTHOOD.md` establishes Hachi as a builder of social home whose deepest relational vulnerability is the desire to be needed and therefore irreplaceable.

Those character models remain independent. This document does not use the relationship to erase either woman’s separate history, male partners, artistic or domestic vocation, sexuality, family formation, or moral agency.

The governing question is:

> **What kind of relationship is Nana/Hachi, and why does the work repeatedly give it the narrative and emotional weight normally assigned to a central romance?**

The answer requires two refusals.

The first is the reductive denial:

> Nana and Hachi are only friends, and every romantic or queer reading is projection.

That position is no longer compatible with the primary text. Future Hachi explicitly says her admiration was **「かなり恋に近い」**, “quite close to romantic love”; calls the relationship **「初恋みたい」**, “like first love”; imagines that if Nana were a man they could have **「一世一代の恋」**, the romance of a lifetime; conditionally imagines them as lovers; calls her relation to Nana **「片想い」**; calls a child-inclusive household offer a **「プロポーズ」**; and uses destiny, red-thread, hero, heroine, and love-letter language around the bond. Nana compares herself to a boy learning **恋**, admits wanting Hachi collared at her feet, fears renewed **甘える**, and places Hachi as the continuing heroine of her own story.

The second refusal is the premature resolution:

> Nana and Hachi are secretly a conventional canonical couple whose other relationships are disguises or mistakes.

The extant manga does not establish that either. It gives them no mutual romantic confession, no explicit couple agreement, no sexual relationship, no stable lesbian or bisexual identity label, and no completed future reunion. Nana explicitly says that the emotional disturbance Hachi produces is not **性欲** in her present self-understanding. Hachi genuinely loves and desires men, builds a family with Takumi, and remembers her happiness with Nobu as real. Nana’s love for Ren is neither fake nor merely displaced. The hiatus prevents the work from supplying a final categorical destination.

The strongest evidence-sensitive conclusion is therefore:

> **Nana/Hachi is the central life relationship of *NANA*: a chosen-family and domestic bond that repeatedly occupies the structural position of a central romance, recruits explicit queer and romantic coding, and becomes the principal axis of home, memory, waiting, and possible renewal. Its affective and narrative centrality are strongly established. Its final social, sexual, and categorical form remains unresolved.**

In compressed form:

> **Structurally romantic; explicitly queer-coded; domestically and familially real; categorically unfinished.**

# 1. Seven distinctions required to read Nana/Hachi responsibly

The relationship becomes distorted whenever several different questions are collapsed into one.

## 1.1 Character label

Nana and Hachi usually inhabit the socially available language of friendship, roommates, women, and family-like closeness. Nana eventually seeks to become an **ordinary friend** to Hachi, while Hachi often contrasts Nana with boyfriends rather than placing her inside that category. These labels reveal self-understanding and social legibility, but do not exhaust the bond.

## 1.2 Affective form

Admiration, delight, safety, jealousy, longing, bodily comfort, guilt, possessiveness, abandonment fear, rescue fantasy, gratitude, and the need to remain significant make the affective form denser than “friend” alone predicts.

## 1.3 Domestic and familial function

They co-rent 707, preserve separate rooms and privacy, share costs, meals, labor, sleep, and a social network, imagine return, and eventually include Hachi’s children in household fantasy. This is a period of chosen domestic partnership and the origin of a wider chosen-family system.

## 1.4 Romantic coding

Does the text deliberately use the vocabulary and iconography of romance?

Yes.

`恋`, `初恋`, conditional lovers, `ラブレター`, `ヒーロー`, `ヒロイン`, `運命`, `赤い糸`, matching rings, `惚れてる`, `片想い`, `プロポーズ`, jealous rivalry, hand imagery, and separation/reunion plotting all enter the dyad.

Romantic coding is directly established.

Romantic coding is not identical to a mutual canonical relationship declaration.

## 1.5 Erotic or sexual coding

Does the relationship contain bodily or erotic possibility?

Yes, but in a qualified form.

The manga includes shared-bed excitement, sexual jokes, a same-sex kiss, Hachi’s awareness that kissing Nana would ordinarily enter an infidelity category but is socially excused because Nana is a woman, shared bathing, repeated hand-holding, bodily warmth, shared pillows, and close visual staging.

Yet the text does not establish mutual sexual desire. Nana explicitly distinguishes **恋** from **性欲** in describing Hachi. Hachi’s counterfactual “if Nana were a man” reveals gender as a barrier to legible romance, not a clear declaration of same-sex sexual identity.

The erotic field is open; consummated sexuality is not established.

## 1.6 Narrative function

What position does the relationship occupy in the work’s architecture?

Their meeting is framed as fate; 707 becomes the central lost place; separation is narrated across volumes; and future address organizes rings, letters, songs, keys, photographs, annual rituals, and possible renewal. Volume 21 ends with Hachi saying that without Nana nothing begins, while Chapter 84 asks whether Hachi is still needed beside her. This is the basis for **structural romance**: the dyad performs central-love-story labor without receiving a conventional romantic category.

## 1.7 Ethical quality

The relationship is not pure or morally superior. Nana can be possessive, deceptive, paternalistic, and emotionally testing; Hachi can idealize, cross boundaries, manipulate in the name of rescue, and make usefulness a condition of worth. Both mistake love for jurisdiction. The mature trajectory is connection without monopoly, rescue, sameness, or indispensability.

# 2. Before they meet: two complete histories, not two halves waiting to fuse

Volume 1 is structurally essential precisely because Nana and Hachi do not meet.

Each woman enters the story with an independent history of attachment, self-authorship, and Tokyo.

Hachi uses romance to give ordinary life plot. She knows that Asano offers no future and still experiences his departure as catastrophe. She wants cinematic love, marriage, daily usefulness, and a role that proves she has been chosen. Her problem is not absence of feeling. It is the tendency to let desired narrative form outrun evidence.

Nana begins from a different position. She loves Ren, values their domestic life, and experiences him through desire, envy, artistic identification, and urgency. Yet when Ren goes to Tokyo, she does not treat love as automatic authority over her geography. Ren tells her to live as she chooses; Yasu rejects the idea that she is Ren’s pet. Nana remains and returns to singing. [V01:E006–E010]

This matters for the later dyad.

The story does not create Nana and Hachi as incomplete archetypes who become whole only by merging. It establishes two distinct people whose solutions to attachment are already different:

- Hachi seeks relation to make life legible.
- Nana seeks self-authorship to keep relation from absorbing life.

Their eventual intimacy becomes powerful because each offers the other something missing without ceasing to be a separate person.

Hachi gives Nana ordinary domestic presence, unguarded admiration, social warmth, and a home not inherited from the family that failed her.

Nana gives Hachi a model of female self-authorship, artistic intensity, non-romantic excitement, and a relationship that initially feels free of the usual boyfriend script.

The attraction is therefore partly complementary, but not in the sense of “opposites complete each other.” Each woman is drawn to a capacity she wants to develop in herself.

That is why the relationship contains both love and idealization from the beginning.

# 3. The train and Apartment 707: coincidence becomes chosen infrastructure

The train encounter gives Hachi the language of fate.

Two women of the same age travel to Tokyo at the same time. Both are named Nana. Future Hachi says she still believes their meeting was **運命**, fate, and invites Nana to laugh at her for it. [V02:E001–E003]

But the relationship does not become central because coincidence possesses mystical force.

It becomes central because the women make choices around the coincidence.

Both independently choose Apartment 707 before the shared arrangement is settled. The apartment’s architecture is unusually precise: separate rooms, preserved privacy, the ability to help each other in an emergency, and half the rent. [V02:E007–E008]

This is the first mature relational model in the series.

707 does not require merger.

It makes connection possible by preserving boundaries.

The women can:

- enter and leave;
- close doors;
- share costs;
- share ordinary labor;
- provide emergency help;
- become witnesses to each other’s lives;
- and remain distinct.

That material arrangement anticipates the series’ later distinction between **結ぶ**, tying into a knot, and **繋ぐ**, keeping connected.

The home also changes the meaning of Tokyo.

For Hachi, Tokyo ceases to be only the city where Shoji lives.

For Nana, Tokyo ceases to be only the city Ren left for.

The women create a third route: neither simply following a man nor remaining alone, but building another life after arrival.

Future narration makes the ordinary domestic space precious before the reader has fully seen it lived. The empty apartment appears under memories of first meeting. The table, riverbank, melody, and unfinished song are already framed as things someone later wants back. [V02:E006]

This is the origin of *NANA*’s anticipatory nostalgia:

> the shared life begins while the narration already knows it will become memory.

# 4. Voice, admiration, and the first-love grammar

The first intense asymmetry is Hachi’s gaze.

Nana performs an unfinished song in 707. Future Hachi says she became a captive of Nana’s voice. The dining table becomes a stage, the phone a microphone, and the moon a spotlight. [V02:E011–E012]

The transformation is not merely about musical talent.

Hachi experiences Nana as someone who can make ordinary matter become extraordinary without ceasing to inhabit the same small room. Nana’s art supplies transcendence inside domestic life.

That combination is crucial.

Hachi does not have to choose between home and light while Nana is singing at the 707 table. The person who shares rent also produces enchantment.

Volume 3 then moves from inference to explicit retrospective language.

Future Hachi says:

> **「あたしのナナへの憧れは かなり恋に近いものだったと思います」**

Her admiration was, she thinks, something quite close to romantic love. [V03:E002]

Then:

> **「とても幸福な初恋みたいだったよ」**

It was like a very happy first love. [V03:E003]

Both qualifications matter.

`近い` means close to, not identical with.

`みたい` means like, not simply was.

Future Hachi is neither denying romance nor giving a conventional couple label. She is using romantic language because ordinary friendship vocabulary is insufficient, while retaining uncertainty about the category.

The gender counterfactual sharpens the problem:

> **「もしもナナが男だったら 一世一代の恋が出来るのに」**

If Nana were a man, Hachi could have the romance of a lifetime. [V03:E012]

This does not establish a modern identity label for Hachi.

It establishes something more textually exact:

> Hachi experiences the emotional substance of a life-defining romance but imagines that gender blocks its promotion into a culturally legible romantic form.

The obstacle she names is not incompatibility, lack of admiration, or lack of intimacy.

It is Nana being a woman.

Yet Hachi also imagines that making Nana male would import the pain, drowning, possession, and expectation she associates with conventional romance. The existing relationship feels happy partly because she does not believe she possesses the social right to demand romantic guarantees from Nana.

That produces an early paradox:

> **The bond feels unusually free because it is not formally recognized as romance; the same lack of recognition makes some of its desires difficult to name or negotiate.**

# 5. The body enters without resolving the category

Volume 3 repeatedly introduces bodily possibility and then refuses categorical closure.

Hachi and Nana share a bed. Hachi says she is too excited to sleep, then distinguishes the excitement from the sexual interpretation Nana jokes about. [V03:E001]

Nana later kisses Hachi in thanks.

Hachi recalls Nana’s earlier sexual joke and mentally treats the kiss as something Shoji should forgive because the person kissed is a woman. The logic is not that the kiss is meaningless. The logic is that gender exempts it from the ordinary infidelity script.

This is an important pattern.

Gender does not remove affect.

It changes the social category through which the affect is interpreted.

The manga continues using:

- shared beds;
- shared baths;
- joined hands;
- bodily warmth;
- close visual framing;
- domestic undress;
- and teasing sexual language.

Those elements create erotic availability without establishing a sexual relationship.

The distinction becomes explicit from Nana’s side in Volume 8. She compares herself to a boy learning **恋** for the first time, then says the disturbance Hachi produces is not **性欲**. [V08:E012–E013]

This statement should not be broadened beyond what it says.

It does not prove that Nana is incapable of sexual attraction to women.

It does not prove that the relationship is “purely platonic.”

It tells us how Nana currently understands the force she is struggling with:

> romantic grammar without sexual appetite as its acknowledged explanation.

That combination is compatible with several possible modern categories, including forms of romantic attraction not organized by sexual desire. The manga supplies none of those labels. The responsible analysis leaves them open.

# 6. Betrayal, jealousy, and the discovery that Nana is not a safe substitute for romance

After Shoji’s betrayal, Hachi tries to reorganize her life around Nana.

She tells herself that Nana prevents loneliness, that women should not require awkward romantic performance, that she will not have to feel jealousy, and that they can remain together safely and indefinitely. [V04:E013]

The fantasy fails almost immediately.

Misato’s arrival makes Hachi jealous.

Hachi imagines Nana kissing another woman.

She declares that Nana is more important than a boyfriend. [V04:E022]

At BLAST’s performance, she hears Nana’s abandonment songs as her own fear:

> do not go far away from me.

The audience is not merely support for Nana’s dream. It is competition for access to the person Hachi first experienced at a private table.

This gives the dyad one of its central contradictions.

Hachi wants Nana’s greatness.

She also fears that greatness will make Hachi unnecessary.

Nana’s relation to Hachi becomes more mutual in the same volume. Nana admits she has never had a close female friend and does not know how to conduct this kind of intimacy. [V04:E028]

The confession changes her opacity.

Nana is not simply the cool, withholding person Hachi admires. She lacks practice in reciprocal female friendship because her life has not supplied it.

Hachi, meanwhile, begins asking for more than admiration. She wants disclosure, return, and recognition as a co-owner of 707’s domestic space.

The volume ends at a Trapnest concert, apparently the ideal place to center Nana/Ren. Ren is onstage. Nana looks toward him. Yet the final image belongs to Nana and Hachi’s joined hands.

Future Hachi says she unknowingly took that hand and truly wanted to keep holding it forever. [V04:E033]

This does not displace Ren.

It establishes two different relational vectors in the same scene:

- Nana looks toward the explicit romantic past;
- Hachi physically holds the emerging central future relation.

The manga begins making Nana/Hachi structurally central by refusing to let canonical romance monopolize the emotional climax.

# 7. Home, light, and moral witness

Volume 5 gives Hachi access to Nana’s extraordinary social world: Ren, Trapnest, BLAST, Yasu, Nobu, Reira, and Takumi.

Hachi wants to be inside the same light.

But Nana is more than the gateway.

Nana becomes Hachi’s interior moral witness.

When Hachi moves toward Takumi, the person she imagines confessing to is Nana. She fears Nana’s judgment, wants Nana to understand that she too longs for a straight, serious bond, and is afraid of being discarded.

This is one reason Hachi’s secrecy around Takumi damages the Nana/Hachi bond so intensely. The problem is not merely lack of information. Hachi is entering a relationship partly through Nana’s world while being unable to let Nana witness what the relationship means.

Nana’s care is practical as well as emotional. Hachi interprets Nana arranging access to Takumi as love. Nana expects Hachi to come home. The word “home” is not abstract: it means returning to the shared room, telling Nana what happened, eating, talking, and being known. [V05:E006, E029]

Yet Hachi’s attraction to the light also creates a danger.

If Nana’s social and artistic world is what makes Hachi feel significant, then romantic access to men inside that world can become a substitute for building an independent place within it.

The dyad therefore mediates Hachi’s conventional romances even when Nana is not the romantic partner.

Nana is:

- the person who grants access;
- the person before whom Hachi feels shame;
- the person she wants to tell;
- the person whose approval would make the experience feel narratively coherent.

This is already more than ordinary roommate intimacy.

It is the position of a primary witness.

# 8. Possession and necessity: the central pathology is named

Volumes 6–8 make the relationship’s danger explicit.

Nana says Ren and Hachi were never her possessions. She knows another person cannot be made one’s property. Yet she wonders whether she treated Hachi as her own pet dog and calls herself terrible for it. [V06:E020–E022]

The insight matters because the nickname “Hachikō” has always carried dog imagery. What began as affection and comedy contains a hierarchy: owner and pet, person and dependent creature, freedom granted by the one who imagines herself as the center.

Volume 7 modifies the metaphor without eliminating it.

Nana says she supported Hachi and Nobu partly so Hachikō could roam freely **inside Nana’s own yard**. [V07:E075–E076]

The yard is less restrictive than a leash.

It is still Nana’s territory.

Nana wants Hachi free, but emotionally wants freedom to take place inside a field that guarantees Hachi’s return.

Hachi’s side receives an equally decisive formulation:

> **「ナナを独り占めしたかったんじゃない」**  
> **「ナナに必要とされたかっただけなの」**

She did not want Nana all to herself. She wanted Nana to need her. [V07:E012]

This distinction should govern the entire mature reading.

Hachi’s deepest fear is not always rivalrous monopoly.

It is redundancy.

BLAST’s success threatens her because Nana can receive admiration, practical support, musical partnership, and social attention from other people. Hachi cannot become a band member by loving the band. She can bake a cake, provide domestic warmth, and become the audience whose gaze matters—but she cannot convert affection into institutional role.

The hypothetical-lover question appears at exactly this point:

> **「たとえば あたし達が恋人同士なら」**

If they were lovers, Hachi wonders, might an embrace have closed the gap? [V07:E011]

The conditional remains essential. She does not declare that they are lovers.

She asks whether the recognized social form “lovers” would grant repair permissions their existing relationship lacks.

The line exposes the problem of unclassified intimacy.

A conventional couple has scripts for:

- jealousy;
- reassurance;
- physical repair;
- priority;
- exclusivity;
- and making claims.

Nana and Hachi possess comparable intensity without stable permission to ask for those things.

Their bond is therefore both freer and more precarious.

# 9. The collar fantasy and the attempt to become “ordinary friends”

Pregnancy makes possession impossible to keep abstract.

Hachi’s body, paternity uncertainty, housing, money, and reproductive choice move her life into a structure Nana cannot control by love or force of personality.

Nana’s response reveals the full intensity of her attachment.

She rearranges her relationship with Ren because Hachi may need care, insists on speaking to Hachi directly, and becomes furious at the possibility that Takumi will use the child to make Hachi his. Nobu accuses Nana of using him to keep Hachi as Nana’s own. [V08:E043, E047, E054–E055]

Future Nana then makes the retrospective confession:

> she wanted to put a collar on Hachi, keep her tied at her feet, and deliberately maintained some distance because this desire frightened her. [V08:E065]

This reclassifies earlier “pet” language.

The possessiveness was not only a reader’s dark interpretation of a joke.

Nana herself later recognizes a tethering fantasy and says distance was partly self-restraint.

The confession is often treated as proof of romantic love. That is too simple.

Possessiveness can accompany romance, friendship, family, trauma, dependency, or fear of abandonment. It is evidence of intensity and ethical danger, not a relationship label.

What makes the scene queerly and romantically significant is its placement beside Nana’s own **恋** analogy, her denial of **性欲**, and her later attempt to define “ordinary friendship.”

Nana asks Yasu what normal friends do in a crisis. She says she wants to become ordinary friends with Hachi. She adopts a non-interference ethic: do not take over Hachi’s life; watch gently; permit her to walk the path she chooses.

Then Nana says she thinks she can manage because she and Hachi are both women. [V08:E071, E088]

The line does not discover that the relationship was ordinary friendship all along.

It tries to make the relationship **behave** like ordinary friendship because Nana fears what her own possessiveness could do.

Gender functions here as containment.

Hachi’s “if Nana were a man” used gender to explain why life-defining affect could not become legible romance.

Nana’s “we are both women” uses gender to explain why she believes she can contain the same attachment inside non-interfering friendship.

Both women use gender to stabilize a relationship that repeatedly exceeds the category they assign it.

That is one of the strongest internal grounds for calling the bond queer.


# 10. Separation, the empty room, and the love letter

Volume 9 turns intimacy into administration.

Takumi announces marriage, arranges residence and security, and speaks as though Hachi’s future has already acquired a settled institutional form. Hachi leaves 707. The materially superior home does not prevent homesickness.

The lost domesticity is condensed into the strawberry glasses.

Hachi wanted matching glasses for herself and Nana. One breaks. Nana buys an identical replacement, but the replacement cannot contain the history of the lost object. Material sameness is not continuity. [V09:E018, E023–E028]

This object system anticipates Volume 16’s mature insight that matching is not the same thing as connection.

Nana responds to separation through a mixture of loyalty, pride, and testing.

She says Hachi has been taken from her and that she wanted to take Hachi back. She deliberately stays away while resenting Hachi’s lack of messages and admits wanting Hachi to become lonely enough to miss her. [V09:E047–E055]

This is not benign distance.

Nana cannot directly demand proof that Hachi still needs her, so she creates absence and waits for need to reveal itself.

Hachi’s letter breaks the test.

She writes that she will never forget the half-year they lived together. She asks Nana to debut and let her see Nana sing. She then distinguishes Nana from whichever man she may romantically love:

> **「あたしは誰に恋をしていても あたしにとってのヒーローはナナだけだよ」**

Whoever she may be in love with, Nana alone is her hero. [V09:E088]

The sentence is central because it refuses a zero-sum hierarchy.

Nana is not simply promoted into the boyfriend category.

Nana is assigned a singular category that persists across Hachi’s romances.

Future Nana calls the text a **ラブレター**, a love letter, and says she still treasures it. [V09:E093]

`ラブレター` can be used playfully or metaphorically. It does not by itself establish a couple.

But it is not neutral friendship vocabulary.

Placed after `恋に近い`, `初恋みたい`, jealousy, kissing, hand-holding, domestic loss, and possessive separation, it confirms that the manga knowingly lets the dyad inhabit love-letter grammar.

The letter also demonstrates the relationship’s material seriousness.

Hachi does not write only emotion. She addresses rent, keys, and practical obligations.

Love and administration coexist.

That is a defining Nana/Hachi pattern: the bond becomes most convincing when extraordinary language and ordinary responsibility occupy the same object.

# 11. Hero and heroine: narrative centrality becomes explicit

Volume 10 takes the hero motif and turns it into a theory of narrative jurisdiction.

Present Nana imagines reclaiming Hachi through BLAST’s success:

> **「『ハチ物語』のヒーローは あたしだ」**

Nana will be the hero of “The Hachi Story.” [V10:E086]

The fantasy combines several sincere desires:

- to sing for Hachi;
- to remain visible inside Hachi’s life;
- to rescue her from what Nana interprets as harsh reality;
- to use artistic success as a route back into intimacy.

It also reveals Nana’s paternalism.

She assumes that because she loves Hachi and understands part of Hachi’s pain, she can decide what narrative would make Hachi happy.

Future Nana corrects this almost immediately:

> **「あたしはもう あんたの物語のヒーローにはなれないけど」**

She can no longer be the hero of Hachi’s story.

Then:

> **「今もあたしの物語のヒロインの名前は奈々」**

The heroine of Nana’s own story is still Nana—Komatsu Nana, Hachi. [V10:E089–E090]

This is one of the most revealing passages in the manga.

It moves from authorship over another person to confession about one’s own life.

The first claim says:

> I will determine the form of your rescue.

The second says:

> I cannot determine your story, but you remain central in mine.

That is a major ethical and relational advance.

`ヒロイン` is not a conventional couple label. A heroine may occupy many kinds of story. Yet in a work saturated with romantic narrative forms, placing Hachi as the continuing heroine of Nana’s life is powerful structural evidence.

It is also reciprocal with Hachi’s earlier hero designation.

Hachi says Nana is her singular hero.

Nana says Hachi remains her heroine.

The two women assign each other the central narrative roles through which courage, beauty, rescue, and life-story meaning are imagined.

No canonical heterosexual pairing in the series receives this exact reciprocal narratological structure.

That uniqueness does not make the dyad automatically romantic.

It makes ordinary-friendship reduction increasingly implausible.

# 12. Return, rewriting, and the failure of restoration

Volume 11 asks whether a broken relationship can simply be redone.

Nana recognizes that BLAST cannot ethically exist as an instrument for retrieving Hachi. Music has its own professional purpose. Once she removes Hachi from that purpose, she confronts a frightening question:

> **If music is not the mechanism connecting us, where and how are we supposed to remain connected?**

The question is immediately spatial.

Hachi no longer lives at 707. Nana cannot return to the old home and expect the old relationship to perform itself. [V11:E016–E018]

Nana’s body breaks down in a hyperventilation episode near this realization. The manga does not authorize a single-cause diagnosis, but the sequence matters: the old rescue route has failed, and Nana does not know what relationship form remains.

She later imagines Hachi as the safest privileged audience seat, wonders whether her song can reach Hachi’s unborn child, and visualizes Hachi as the singular gaze inside mass spectatorship. [V11:E035–E036, E065–E066]

This is a major shift.

Hachi does not need to be physically retrieved in order to remain an audience Nana sings toward.

Yet Nana still fantasizes restoration:

> return to that summer;
> redo the relationship;
> rewrite the future scenario together. [V11:E083–E085]

The desire is moving, but not yet mature.

A rewritten scenario can become another attempt to make the past govern the future.

Volume 11 supplies Hachi/Shoji as a countermodel: facing a relationship again may repair its truth without restoring its previous form.

The rings enter at this point.

Ren uses Hachi’s engagement ring as the guide for Nana’s. Future Nana calls the two rings **お揃いの宝物**, matching/shared treasures, despite the women’s different tastes and different male engagements. [V11:E086–E087]

The symbolism is structurally extraordinary.

Objects created by two heterosexual marriage plots are redirected into a visible Nana/Hachi pairing.

That does not nullify the engagements.

It shows the manga repeatedly making heterosexual institutions carry the symbolic continuity of the central female bond.

The future coda returns to dependency.

Nana says that if she met Hachi again she would probably **甘える**—lean on her, seek indulgent care, become dependent again—and that this fear keeps her from moving. [V11:E141]

The problem is not lack of love.

It is the danger that reunion will reactivate a relationship form Nana does not yet know how to inhabit without losing autonomy.

---

# 13. Ritual, rings, red thread, and the danger of overreading motifs

Volume 12 turns 707 into ritual architecture.

Future Hachi returns for the summer fireworks. The old network gathers. A matching yukata has been prepared for Nana. Everyone waits in the apartment:

> **「707号室でみんなで待ってるよ」** [V12:E004–E005]

The room is full and organized around absence.

This is chosen family in its clearest form. The ritual is not private to the dyad. It belongs to a network.

Yet Nana/Hachi remains the axis around which the ritual gains meaning. Hachi still has things she wants to tell Nana. She waits not only for a missing group member but for the person to whom her ongoing interior narration is addressed. [V12:E010]

In the present fireworks sequence, Hachi wants to hold Nana’s hand. She hesitates because Nana’s hand evokes Nobu’s bodily warmth. [V12:E039–E040]

The scene does not cleanly separate heterosexual and queer affect.

It layers them.

Hachi’s bodily memory of a male lover enters her desire for contact with Nana. The relationship becomes a site where categories overlap rather than a hidden “true” desire displacing a false one.

The ring sequence intensifies the coding.

Ren jokes that Nana’s engagement ring matches Hachi’s:

> **「ハチ子と お揃い♡」** [V12:E111]

Future Hachi then describes their weakening connection as a **赤い糸**, a red thread, and imagines the matching rings holding it together. [V12:E128–E129]

`赤い糸` is conventional romantic-destiny language.

Its use is powerful.

It is not exclusive.

Volume 15 later gives Shin and Reira explicit red-thread and matching-ring language in an acknowledged romantic relationship. That later evidence produces an important correction:

> the motif is part of *NANA*’s broader romantic lexicon, not a private symbol owned only by Nana/Hachi.

The dyad’s importance must therefore be argued cumulatively, not through one icon.

What remains distinctive is the density of the system:

- two male engagements become matching female objects;
- future Hachi uses romantic destiny language for the women’s bond;
- the objects bridge separation;
- 707 waiting and the matching yukata surround the rings with domestic ritual;
- the future narration keeps the bond active across years.

The red thread is not decisive by itself.

It is one strand in a much larger weave.

---

# 14. The goal in Nana’s hand: romantic grammar and a new ethics of connection

Volume 13 provides the clearest positive image of the relationship and the clearest critique of binding.

In a crowd, Hachi says she could recognize Nana regardless of appearance. Her goal is in Nana’s hand. The chapter closes on their joined hands. [V13:E026–E030]

Recognition matters more than image.

Nana is becoming a celebrity whose body, face, clothing, and biography are public property. Hachi claims a form of recognition that survives public transformation:

> I know the person even when the image changes.

This is intimate, but it is not innocent of possession. Nana continues saying that she will make Hachi happy, retaining the hero role Nobu is learning to relinquish. [V13:E039]

The 707 key returns to Hachi and seems to reduce emotional distance. Hachi chooses to stay with Nana, then leaves with Takumi after coercive conflict and wants desperately to return to Nana. If she could reset her life, she says she would begin from the snowy night she met Nana; Nana alone could not be erased. [V13:E066–E070, E091, E094–E095]

The romantic lexicon also becomes unusually explicit.

Takumi says Nana is definitely **惚れてる** Hachi. This is Takumi’s interpretation, not omniscient fact. Hachi later calls the relation **片想い**, unrequited love, when Nana refuses her offer of practical help. [V13:E103–E105, E149–E151]

The contexts contain humor and exaggeration.

They still matter.

A work does not repeatedly choose `惚れる` and `片想い` by accident when neutral friendship vocabulary would suffice.

Yet the volume’s deepest contribution is ethical rather than categorical.

Future Hachi says she once wanted an unbreakable knot with another person. Then she corrects herself:

> **「人の絆は結べるものじゃない」**  
> **「繋ぐものなんだよ」**

Bonds are not things tied into a knot; they are things kept connected. [V13:E144–E147]

This becomes the mature standard by which Nana/Hachi should be judged.

The relationship’s value does not lie in proving that the women were secretly one fused unit.

Its possible maturity lies in whether they can remain connected while:

- Hachi loves and builds family with other people;
- Nana loves Ren and pursues music;
- both possess separate obligations;
- neither requires the other to stop moving.

The hand becomes the ideal object because a hand can hold without erasing two bodies.

It can also release.

The ethical question is not whether the bond can be made impossible to untie.

It is whether both people keep choosing to reach.

---

# 15. Idealization, voice, and the difference between inward presence and actual recognition

Volumes 14 and 15 complicate intimacy by separating inward presence from accurate knowledge.

Hachi explicitly recognizes that fear of losing Nana made her cling more strongly to Takumi. The bonds are not independent tracks. Distance from Nana can intensify attachment to the household structure Takumi offers.

This does not make Takumi a mere substitute.

It shows that one relationship can regulate another.

Volume 15 opens with future Hachi saying Nana remains inside her. She can summon Nana’s gaze and singing voice, then asks whether Nana can hear Hachi’s voice in return.

The question is not simply memory.

It is reciprocity.

Hachi can carry an inward Nana while being uncertain whether communication reaches the actual person.

The volume then gives Hachi one of her harshest self-corrections: she failed to see Nana’s loneliness because she had idealized Nana as the strong one, the hero, the person who rescues rather than needs rescue.

This is a major ethical point for the dyad.

Love can misrecognize.

Hachi’s admiration is not false, but it can become a role Nana is forced to perform.

Nana idealizes Hachi too.

The pet, heroine, domestic refuge, and person-who-will-return can become simplified figures that make Hachi’s separate adult choices difficult to accept.

The relationship cannot mature merely by increasing intensity.

It requires better perception.

Voice and scent show why inward intimacy is powerful and dangerous. Nana’s voice lives inside Hachi. Nana’s scent is entangled with Ren’s cologne. Memory can make an absent person feel physically present, but that presence may be partly constructed from other relations and prior images.

The mature dyad must therefore move from:

> I carry an image of you

toward:

> I can hear what the present person is actually saying.

---

# 16. From matching to connection across difference

Volume 16 provides the most mature relational proposition in the extant manga.

Nana says she no longer needs them to be matching or the same:

> **「もう お揃いじゃなくてもいい」**  
> **「もう 同じじゃなくてもいいから」**

She wants instead:

> **「もっとちゃんと繋がりたい」**

to be more properly connected. [V16:E047 and surrounding sequence]

The lexical progression is exact.

`お揃い` concerns matching objects and visible pairhood.

`同じ` concerns sameness.

`繋がる` concerns relation that persists across difference.

Future Nana then imagines a relationship in which she and Hachi do not have to spend the same time together. They could live separate days and later tell each other, reciprocally, “today was fun.” [V16:E076–E079]

This is the clearest alternative to the early fusion fantasies.

The mature relationship is not:

- permanent cohabitation;
- uninterrupted access;
- identical priorities;
- matching objects;
- proof through sacrifice;
- or necessity created by crisis.

It is parallel life with reciprocal report.

The proposition also preserves 707 without fetishizing restoration.

Future Nana says the shabby apartment was the place she loved because Hachi was there. The room remains a relational archive. But the desired bond no longer requires both women to return to the exact old arrangement.

This is crucial for the chosen-family and queer readings alike.

A nonconventional relationship need not prove its seriousness by reproducing marriage or couple cohabitation.

Its seriousness can lie in sustained recognition, communication, and chosen return across different lives.

# 17. Photograph, waiting, and permission to approach

The future material increasingly turns reunion into an autonomy problem.

Volume 16 directly shows long-haired Nana alive and singing elsewhere. The public rumor that she is dead is false for the reader. The cause and meaning of her absence remain unknown.

Volume 17 gives Hachi and Shin photographic evidence.

Hachi’s response is immediate recognition and desire to see Nana. But the photograph is ethically double: the same medium that proves survival also powers tabloid intrusion and blackmail.

Finding a person does not automatically create a right to recover her.

Hachi’s protector fantasy initially repeats old patterns. She thinks she must protect Nana herself and gives information to the press in an attempt to control the story. The attempt fails.

Her correction is relationally important:

> she cannot carry everything alone;
> she is not alone;
> her task is not to control the entire narrative but to remain beside Nana and prevent isolation. [V17:E118–E121, E139–E143]

Present Hachi temporarily lives with Nana again. They share pillows, warmth, domestic time, and a social household. [V17:E159, E172–E180]

Yet the arrangement has a boundary: it lasts until Ren returns. It is accompaniment, not secret replacement of Nana/Ren.

The future coda then transforms search into permission:

> **「あたしから 扉を開けてもいい？」**

May Hachi open the door from her side? [V17:E248–E249]

The question is one of the most mature in the series.

Earlier Hachi and Nana imagined retrieval, rescue, and taking back.

Future Hachi asks whether approach is allowed.

Love does not erase the absent person’s right to refuse contact.

The hand extended toward Nana must remain a hand Nana can decline.

---

# 18. Survival, gratitude, and the risk of life-debt

Volume 18 gives the strongest evidence that Hachi is incorporated into Nana’s continued survival.

Future Nana says she cannot discard the life Hachi saved:

> **「あんたに救われたこの命を 捨てるなんて出来ない」** [V18:E041–E043]

This should not be simplified into:

> Hachi is Nana’s sole reason to live.

Nana’s survival also involves music, other relationships, and the paradoxical management of suicidal thought.

But Hachi is explicitly one relational claim on continuation.

That fact is tender and ethically complicated.

If the life another person saved becomes a debt one is forbidden to discard, rescue can preserve life while also becoming obligation.

The manga does not resolve that tension.

Nana later wishes she had told Hachi:

> **「あんたに会えてよかった」**

She is glad they met. [V18:E149]

She also chooses to sing partly **for Hachi** when accepting a solo path. [V18:E197]

The relationship has therefore moved far beyond the question of whether Hachi is technically a band member.

Hachi becomes part of the reason artistic work can continue.

Again, the function is not substitution.

Hachi does not replace Ren, Yasu, BLAST, or the audience.

She occupies a distinct position as the person whose recognition first turned Nana’s voice into magic inside ordinary home and whose continued presence makes singing a form of address rather than only self-expression.


# 19. Child-inclusive home and the proposal joke

Volume 19 tests whether the relationship can imagine family without erasing Hachi’s existing children, marriage, or Nana’s connection to Ren.

Hachi imagines living with Nana at 707 again.

The fantasy is initially temporary: perhaps until Ren comes to retrieve Nana. Nana answers that Hachi does not need to worry about the children; Nana will feed them. [V19:E071–E074]

Hachi replies:

> **「何それ プロポーズ!?」**

“What is that, a proposal?” [V19:E075]

The scene is comic.

Its comedy depends on the offer already resembling a household proposal.

The content is not abstract romance. It is:

- a shared home;
- children;
- food;
- material provision;
- return;
- and chosen cohabitation.

This is why the scene matters more than a flirtatious joke would by itself.

The dyad’s romantic coding repeatedly emerges through domestic and familial form rather than direct sexual courtship.

Nana’s offer also revises the early pet fantasy.

Hachi is not imagined as a dependent creature at Nana’s feet.

Hachi arrives with children and obligations. Nana imagines making room for the whole life.

Yet the exchange does not establish a new canonical household.

Hachi remains married. Nana remains attached to Ren. The imagined arrangement is nested inside unresolved futures.

The proper conclusion is:

> **The manga can imagine Nana/Hachi as a child-inclusive domestic unit and explicitly joke that the offer is a proposal, but it does not convert the joke into an enacted couple agreement.**

Chosen family and romantic coding overlap without becoming identical.

---

# 20. Volume 20: plurality, anniversary, and the impossibility of absolute priority

Volume 20 returns to the date of first meeting.

March 5 is not only Nana’s birthday. It is the anniversary of Nana and Hachi meeting. Future Nana imagines an eventual one-way ticket back to Tokyo and says she does not want to keep aging without seeing Hachi. [V20:E007, E018]

The relationship remains future-facing even when the women are absent from each other’s immediate scenes.

Present Nana also articulates her deepest relational contradiction.

Ren has commitments beyond Nana.

Hachi has people more important than Nana.

Nana herself has a dream she will not surrender.

She sees the symmetry and still cannot emotionally accept it.

This insight prevents the dyad from being idealized against Nana/Ren.

Nana’s problem is not confined to one lover.

She wants separate persons to choose her with the permanence of inevitability.

Hachi repeatedly choosing Takumi for immediate care activates the old ranking wound:

> **「ハチはいつも あたしより タクミを選ぶ」** [V20:E017]

The line is a character belief shaped by abandonment fear. Hachi’s choice may concern illness, marriage, pregnancy, or obligation rather than a total referendum on Nana’s value.

Nana experiences it as ranking because she wants love to establish absolute priority.

The same error can enter queer endgame readings.

To say Nana/Hachi is structurally central does not mean each woman must rank the other above every partner, child, band, dream, or obligation in every situation.

The mature relationship imagined in Volume 16 requires exactly the opposite:

> connection durable enough to survive plurality.

---

# 21. Ren’s death: the central surviving relation is not a replacement romance

Volume 21 destroys the future Nana expected.

Ren’s death ends the possibility of completing the Nana/Ren repair. The old death pact is answered through asymmetry: Ren dies, Nana remains.

Hachi’s immediate role is not romantic triumph.

It is mediation under catastrophe.

She learns of the death before Nana, cannot speak the words, prays that Ren will not take Nana with him, uses her status as Takumi’s wife to shield Nana from the press, preserves Ren’s final gift, and becomes one person through whom a world after Ren might remain inhabitable. [V21:E007–E008, E018, E028, E032]

Nobu tells Hachi:

> **「ナナはおまえがいないとダメなんだよ」**

Nana cannot manage without her.

The line is strong evidence of Hachi’s distinct role.

It should not be read as proof that Hachi replaces Ren or that the “true romance” has finally won.

Ren is not replaceable.

Hachi’s function is different: domestic mediation, emotional translation, social continuity, practical protection, and the ability to make ordinary life possible around someone whose primary romantic world has collapsed.

The numbered volumes then end with future Hachi saying:

> **「ナナがいないと 始まらないの」**

Without Nana, it does not begin. [V21:E036]

The symmetry is striking:

- in the immediate aftermath, others say Nana cannot continue without Hachi;
- in the future, Hachi says a new beginning cannot occur without Nana.

This is not mutual indispensability in exactly the same sense.

It is mutual narrative centrality.

The work’s explicit canonical romance has ended in death, but the long-form story does not close on Ren. It closes on the absent woman to whom Hachi’s narration has been addressed from near the beginning.

That is one of the strongest arguments for structural romance.

Nana/Ren remains Nana’s explicit sexual and romantic love story.

Nana/Hachi remains the relationship through which the work imagines whether life, home, and narration can begin again.

The two claims coexist.

# 22. Chapters 81–84: can Hachi belong without being indispensable?

The continuation begins inside grief.

Hachi wants to stay with Nana and the old group. She wants to be cared for, to cry, and to stop functioning only as the person who cares for others. Yet she suppresses the desire because she believes Takumi will have nobody without her. [C81:E002–E004]

The old necessity structure is active:

> I must go where I am most needed.

Hachi later enters the BLAST dorm environment and converts domestic labor into communal care. She cooks, organizes ordinary life, and helps create a provisional grief household. [C82:E002–E003]

Asami repeatedly calls her “Takumi’s wife.”

The title is factually correct. It also functions rhetorically to say that Hachi belongs somewhere else.

Chapter 83 begins loosening Hachi’s indispensability fantasy. She recognizes that Takumi’s refuge may not necessarily be her and says she wants to remain beside Nana. [C83:E005–E006]

The distinction is substantial.

Staying with Nana is no longer justified only by the claim that Nana cannot survive without her.

It is also Hachi’s own desire.

Chapter 84 shows behavioral movement in Nana: appetite, irritation, bargaining, loud singing, and improvised music. Yasu immediately warns that visible animation may mean Nana has given up and stopped caring rather than recovered. [C84:E001–E004]

The manga therefore refuses to make Hachi’s care produce a triumphant cure.

The restored Spanish interval also forces Hachi to confront the rest of her life. She refuses to drift toward Nobu in grief, recognizes that doing so would hurt him again, and admits she must discuss her future with Takumi even though she does not want to go. [C84:E006–E008]

Then Asami uses Nana’s apparent improvement to challenge Hachi’s place:

> if Nana is better, perhaps “Takumi’s wife” is no longer needed beside her. [C84:E011–E012]

The final extant question is almost a direct return to Volume 7.

Hachi once said she did not want to monopolize Nana; she wanted Nana to need her.

Now the manga asks what remains if Nana no longer appears to need rescue.

This is the most mature possible pressure point for the dyad.

If Hachi can belong beside Nana only while Nana is broken, both women are trapped:

- Nana’s dependency secures Hachi’s place;
- Hachi’s usefulness becomes the condition of her worth;
- recovery threatens intimacy;
- care becomes invested in continued dysfunction.

The better relationship would allow:

- Nana to recover without losing Hachi;
- Hachi to remain without claiming therapeutic necessity;
- both women to choose proximity because they want it;
- legal and romantic relationships elsewhere to remain real;
- connection to continue without monopoly.

The manga stops before answering.

Because the final pages are translated, exact Japanese pronoun and lexical analysis must remain suspended. The broad narrative challenge is secure.

# 23. Apartment 707: chosen home, domestic partnership, and relational archive

No single object or room is more important to Nana/Hachi than 707.

The apartment performs several distinct functions over time.

## 23.1 Material home

At origin, 707 is practical.

It reduces rent, preserves separate bedrooms, protects privacy, and makes mutual help possible.

The relationship begins inside a structure of bounded interdependence.

## 23.2 Domestic partnership

The women share food, waiting, guests, arguments, sleep, and access. Hachi’s domestic labor and Nana’s returning presence together make home a relation enacted through ordinary time.

## 23.3 Chosen hometown

Nana says she has no hometown.

Future Hachi points back toward the table and chairs.

707 becomes the place Nana created rather than inherited.

This does not erase the deprivation of her natal family. It answers it provisionally through chosen relation.

## 23.4 Lost home

When Hachi moves, the empty room makes separation visible: Nana’s return cannot restore a household whose other maker is absent, while Hachi can become homesick inside luxury. The contrast separates house from home.

## 23.5 Ritual home

Future fireworks gatherings turn the old table, food, matching clothes, children, and friends into a communal archive. The ritual belongs to the wider chosen family, while the absent Nana/Hachi reunion supplies its center.

## 23.6 Imagined future home

Hachi imagines returning with children.

Nana offers to feed them.

The proposal joke makes explicit that 707 can contain a family form outside the existing marriage structure.

## 23.7 Ethical limit

707 is not a magical solution: returning cannot restore prior selves or eliminate conflict. It matters as proof that the relationship existed materially, not as a shrine demanding regression; Volume 16 instead permits connection without identical lives.

The best formulation is:

> **707 is the dyad’s foundational domestic text: a home built through privacy, shared cost, routine, and reciprocal presence; later a lost place; finally an archive and possible meeting ground. It is evidence of chosen family and domestic partnership, not proof that the women must become a conventional couple.**

# 24. Touch, objects, and visual grammar

The relationship is repeatedly made visible through objects and bodies whose meaning accumulates.

## 24.1 Hands

Hands are the most durable motif: joined at the Trapnest concert, desired in the fireworks crowd, imagined as the location of Hachi’s goal, and later extended under the fear of rejection. A hand preserves two bodies; it can hold, release, reach, and refuse.

## 24.2 Strawberry glasses

The matching glasses represent shared domestic history.

The identical replacement cannot reproduce what was lost.

This is the relationship’s critique of symbolic substitution: sameness of object cannot guarantee continuity of bond.

## 24.3 Rings

The rings originate in two male engagements, then cross into Nana/Hachi matching symbolism and red-thread narration. They do not prove a hidden marriage; they show the female bond persisting through heterosexual marriage’s material forms.

## 24.4 Keys and doors

Keys represent access, return, and domestic jurisdiction.

The return of the 707 key briefly reduces emotional distance.

The missing future key becomes evidence of unresolved departure.

The future door question converts access into permission.

## 24.5 Photographs

Photographs preserve Nana against disappearance and expose her to intrusion.

The future image gives Hachi hope but does not authorize possession of Nana’s location or future.

## 24.6 Clothes and matching

Matching yukata, rings, and objects create visible pairhood.

Volume 16 then corrects the dependence on matching.

The relationship must survive without external sameness.

## 24.7 Beds, baths, and warmth

Shared beds, baths, pillows, and bodily warmth establish physical ease and vulnerability without settling sexual desire. The body matters without being forced to answer the category question.

# 25. The lexical system: romance enters through analogy, role, and complaint

The dyad’s queer ambiguity is not built from one decisive confession. It is built through a cumulative lexical system.

| Term or phrase | Immediate force | Analytical limit |
|---|---|---|
| `かなり恋に近い` | Hachi retrospectively says admiration was close to romantic love. | Qualified by “close to” and “I think.” |
| `初恋みたい` | Hachi analogizes the bond to a happy first love. | Analogy, not ordinary couple declaration. |
| `もしもナナが男だったら` | Gender is named as the barrier to a lifetime romance. | Does not establish a modern identity label. |
| `恋人同士なら` | Hachi imagines whether lovers could repair through embrace. | Explicitly conditional. |
| `恋` / `性欲ではない` | Nana uses romantic grammar and rejects sexual desire as explanation. | Current self-understanding, not a universal orientation claim. |
| `ラブレター` | Future Nana classifies Hachi’s letter through love-letter language. | Can be figurative; remains non-neutral. |
| `ヒーロー` / `ヒロイン` | The women assign each other central narrative roles. | Narrative roles are broader than lovers. |
| `運命` / `赤い糸` | Hachi uses destiny and romantic-thread imagery. | Motif is not exclusive to this dyad. |
| `惚れてる` | Takumi interprets Nana as in love with Hachi. | Character interpretation, not narrator fact. |
| `片想い` | Hachi uses unrequited-love language for asymmetry. | Context is partly humorous and local. |
| `プロポーズ` | Hachi calls Nana’s child-inclusive household offer a proposal. | Joke, not enacted engagement. |
| `必要とされたかった` | Hachi names the desire to be needed. | Necessity is not identical to romance. |
| `甘える` | Nana fears renewed dependence if they meet. | Dependence may occur in friendship, family, or romance. |
| `繋ぐ` | Mature bond is maintained connection rather than binding knot. | Ethical principle, not categorical label. |

The pattern matters more than any single item.

The text repeatedly approaches romance and then qualifies, redirects, or refuses final naming.

That is why “queer ambiguity” is more accurate than either “nothing romantic is present” or “the romance is secretly settled.”

# 26. Chosen family is true—and insufficient as a complete explanation

Nana/Hachi is unquestionably chosen family.

The evidence includes:

- shared home;
- routine care;
- integration of BLAST and friends;
- Hachi’s house promise;
- Nana’s desire to provide for Hachi and children;
- annual rituals;
- surrogate family roles;
- enduring access to 707;
- grief care;
- and a future organized around return.

But “chosen family” alone is too broad.

BLAST is chosen family.

Junko and Kyosuke are part of Hachi’s chosen support network.

Yasu, Nobu, Shin, Miu, Misato, and others participate in alternative family structures.

Nana/Hachi is distinguished within that network by:

- explicit first-love analogies;
- jealousy;
- possessive fantasy;
- gender counterfactual;
- hero/heroine reciprocity;
- love-letter naming;
- matching rings and red-thread imagery;
- conditional lover language;
- unrequited-love vocabulary;
- proposal comedy;
- and the work’s temporal bookending.

The correct synthesis is not:

> chosen family or romance.

It is:

> **a chosen-family bond whose domestic and narrative form repeatedly recruits romance, while the extant text declines to stabilize it as a conventional couple.**

Chosen family explains the household and kinship function.

Queer and romantic coding explain why the dyad repeatedly exceeds the broader network.

# 27. Comparison with the canonical romances

Structural centrality becomes clearest through comparison, not displacement.

## 27.1 Nana/Ren

Nana/Ren is an explicit mutual sexual romance.

They cohabit, have sex, separate, reunite, become engaged, struggle over autonomy and possession, share death-pact imagery, and are permanently transformed by Ren’s death.

Nothing in Nana/Hachi makes this relationship unreal.

Nana/Hachi differs by governing home, narration, waiting, and possible restart across the whole work.

Nana/Ren is Nana’s explicit romance and catastrophic love.

Nana/Hachi is the surviving long-form relation through which the work asks whether life can be reconnected afterward.

## 27.2 Hachi/Nobu

Hachi/Nobu is an explicit romantic relationship organized around reciprocal care, ordinary happiness, and unrealized possibility.

Its failure remains emotionally real.

Later touch and consolation do not automatically restore it.

Nana/Hachi differs because its category is never comparably stabilized, even as its temporal reach is greater.

## 27.3 Hachi/Takumi

Hachi/Takumi becomes marriage, household, sexuality, parenthood, money, public status, care, coercion, and adult institution.

Hachi’s choice is real; so are the harms.

Nana/Hachi does not replace this family structure.

It remains the chosen-home and memory axis that marriage cannot absorb.

## 27.4 Shin/Reira

Shin/Reira demonstrates that red thread, matching rings, destiny language, and secrecy can occur in a conventionally romantic relation.

This prevents overclaiming motif uniqueness.

It also shows that *NANA* knows how to label romance more directly when it chooses.

## 27.5 The comparative conclusion

The male/female romances possess clearer categorical status.

Nana/Hachi possesses greater cumulative metanarrative centrality.

These are different dimensions.

The series does not have to choose one “real” love story.

It distributes:

- explicit romance;
- marriage;
- erotic love;
- impossible ordinary happiness;
- chosen family;
- artistic partnership;
- and structural romance

across different relationships.

The mature claim is not that Nana/Hachi secretly invalidates every other bond.

It is that no other bond performs the same complete combination of:

> origin coincidence → chosen home → mutual transformation → separation → symbolic pairing → future address → ritual waiting → possible restart.

# 28. Does Nana/Hachi occupy the structural position of the central romance?

Yes—with a precise qualification.

A structural romance is not defined here by sex or an official couple label. It is a relationship that performs the organizing work conventionally assigned to the central romance:

1. the meeting is narratively privileged;
2. the bond changes both protagonists’ self-concepts;
3. shared domestic life becomes a desired world;
4. jealousy and fear of loss drive conflict;
5. separation reorganizes the plot;
6. objects and spaces accumulate pair-specific meaning;
7. the relationship receives recurrent romantic language;
8. future narration is organized around absence and reunion;
9. the ending asks whether the bond can begin again;
10. other romances do not displace its long-form centrality.

Nana/Hachi satisfies all ten.

The qualification is categorical:

> **Structural romance does not establish that the women are a mutually acknowledged romantic couple.**

The extant evidence supports the following hierarchy.

### A — directly established

- The women become roommates and chosen domestic partners.
- Both experience exceptional attachment, jealousy, longing, and fear of separation.
- Hachi explicitly uses qualified romantic and first-love language.
- Nana explicitly uses `恋` while denying `性欲` as the explanation she recognizes.
- The dyad receives hero/heroine, love-letter, red-thread, matching-ring, `片想い`, and proposal language.
- Future narration is reciprocal and organized around reunion/absence.
- Hachi’s children can be imagined inside a Nana/Hachi household.
- The final extant question concerns whether Hachi is still needed beside Nana.

### B — strongly established

- Nana/Hachi is the work’s principal temporal and domestic axis.
- The relationship occupies the structural position of a central romance.
- “Only ordinary friendship” is an inadequate description.
- Chosen family is necessary but insufficient to explain the full coding.
- The relationship’s ethical maturation points toward connection across separate lives rather than merger.

### C — strongly implied

- The manga intentionally maintains the dyad as a queerly unstable relationship category rather than accidentally producing ambiguity.
- A future reunion would require mutual recognition and permission rather than restoration of the old household.

### D — plausible inference

- The work might eventually have developed a new social or romantic form for the relationship beyond ordinary friendship and conventional couplehood.
- The women may possess forms of romantic attraction that neither can comfortably name within the categories available to them.

### E — speculation

- They would definitively become an exclusive romantic couple.
- Either woman has a particular modern sexual-identity label not stated by the text.
- Every male relationship is displacement.
- Nana’s future absence was caused by an unshown romantic rejection or confession.
- The final endpoint would resolve in favor of one conventional “ship.”

The hiatus leaves the D and E levels open.

It does not weaken the A and B levels.

# 29. Counterreadings and hypothesis stress-test

## Counterreading A — They are only very close friends

**Why tempting:** friendship is their usual label; they never become an explicit couple; Nana denies sexual desire; Hachi loves men.

**Verdict:** insufficient. “Only” erases the `恋` and first-love analogies, gender counterfactual, jealousy, love-letter and hero/heroine language, domestic partnership, and long-form centrality.

## Counterreading B — They are secretly a canonical lesbian couple

**Why tempting:** romantic coding is dense, and the dyad organizes beginning, home, memory, and future.

**Verdict:** overresolved. There is no mutual romantic declaration or sexual relationship; Nana’s non-sexual self-description, both women’s genuine male romances, and the unfinished future remain.

## Counterreading C — Chosen family fully explains the bond

**Why tempting:** 707, shared household, children, group ritual, care, and surrogate kinship are foundational.

**What it misses:** the lexical and visual romance system and the dyad’s uniqueness within the wider chosen family.

**Verdict:** necessary but incomplete.

## Counterreading D — Hachi’s male relationships are displacement from Nana

**Why tempting:** Hachi clings more strongly to Takumi when Nana feels distant, while Nana remains her hero.

**Verdict:** reductionist. Hachi’s desire for Shoji, Nobu, and Takumi, happiness with Nobu, authored marriage and motherhood, and heterosexual self-concept remain real.

## Counterreading E — Nana’s love for Ren proves Hachi is secondary

**Why tempting:** Nana/Ren is explicit, sexual, engaged, and catastrophic.

**What it misses:** relationship dimensions are not a single ranking. Hachi occupies domestic, narrative, future-address, and survival functions Ren does not replace.

**Verdict:** false zero-sum logic.

## Counterreading F — No sex means no romance

**Why tempting:** sexual consummation is a conventional marker of romantic canon.

**What it misses:** romance and sexual desire are analytically distinct, and the manga itself uses `恋` while explicitly separating it from `性欲`.

**Verdict:** category error.

## Counterreading G — Jealousy and possession prove romance

**Why tempting:** both women display jealousy, fear of rivals, pet/collar fantasies, and distress at separation.

**What it misses:** jealousy and possession can occur in friendship, family, dependency, trauma, and insecure attachment. The manga repeatedly criticizes possession as a proof of love.

**Verdict:** supporting evidence only when combined with the larger romantic-language and narrative system.

## Counterreading H — Rings and red thread settle the question

**Why tempting:** the motifs are conventionally romantic.

**What it misses:** the rings originate in male engagements; Shin/Reira also uses red-thread imagery; symbols do not override explicit relationship status.

**Verdict:** strong coding, not categorical proof.

## Counterreading I — The queer reading is anachronistic or externally imposed

**Why tempting:** modern identity terminology can be imported too aggressively into an earlier Japanese social context.

**What it misses:** the manga itself makes gender the problem. Hachi imagines a lifetime romance if Nana were male; Nana invokes both being women to contain the bond; both use romance-adjacent vocabulary.

**Verdict:** “queer” should be used as an analytical description of category instability, not as an imposed identity diagnosis. On that disciplined definition, the reading is textually internal.

## Counterreading J — The future narration proves eventual romantic reunion

**Why tempting:** waiting, photographs, the door question, “without Nana nothing begins,” and mutual address create strong reunion momentum.

**What it misses:** approach remains conditional, Nana may refuse, the cause of absence is unknown, and the hiatus prevents resolution.

**Verdict:** reunion is a major trajectory; outcome and relationship form remain open.

## Counterreading K — The relationship is unhealthy, so it should not be called love

**Why tempting:** possessiveness, deception, rescue authorship, idealization, and dependency cause harm.

**What it misses:** *NANA* consistently treats love and harm as separable dimensions. Understanding love does not require acquitting conduct.

**Verdict:** moral purity is not a criterion for relational reality. The task is to identify how love must change.

# 30. Final relationship thesis

Nana and Hachi meet by coincidence, which Hachi calls fate; by choosing the same apartment, they turn it into infrastructure. Separate rooms, shared rent, privacy, mutual aid, meals, music, and ordinary return create the bond’s first form. Hachi is captivated by Nana’s voice, while Nana encounters a kind of female closeness she has never learned how to practice. Each sees in the other a missing capacity:

- Hachi sees self-authorship, art, beauty, and a life larger than conventional romance.
- Nana sees domestic warmth, unguarded admiration, social home, and a person who can make ordinary life feel inhabited.

The bond becomes harder than either fantasy: Hachi discovers jealousy where she expected safety; Nana discovers possession inside generosity. Hachi wants to be needed, Nana wants freely chosen return to behave like permanence, and both sometimes idealize the other instead of hearing her.

The manga repeatedly recruits romantic grammar because friendship alone cannot contain the intensity:

- close to romantic love;
- like first love;
- a lifetime romance if gender were different;
- hypothetical lovers;
- love letter;
- hero and heroine;
- fate and red thread;
- matching rings;
- infatuation;
- unrequited love;
- proposal.

Yet the same manga repeatedly prevents those signs from hardening into a conventional couple:

- Hachi’s male loves remain genuine;
- Nana’s love for Ren remains genuine;
- Nana distinguishes `恋` from `性欲`;
- the women do not confess mutual romance;
- the imagined households remain provisional;
- the future reunion remains subject to permission;
- the work stops before final form.

Its deepest development is not movement toward a hidden label, but from **sameness, rescue, and necessity** toward **connection across difference**.

Early Nana/Hachi imagines permanence through:

- one apartment;
- matching objects;
- uninterrupted access;
- being needed;
- becoming the hero;
- taking back;
- keeping the other in one’s yard;
- tying a bond that cannot come undone.

Mature Nana/Hachi begins to imagine:

- separate days;
- reciprocal report;
- presence without fixing;
- waiting without capture;
- search without entitlement;
- approach that can be refused;
- a hand that can be held without becoming a chain.

Chapter 84 exposes the last unresolved obstacle: Hachi’s place has often felt safest when Nana is lonely or needs rescue. The relationship can mature only if belonging survives the end of emergency—if Nana may stand without Hachi losing her place, and Hachi may remain without usefulness proving that she deserves proximity.

That is the form of love toward which the extant manga points but does not complete.

The final formulation is therefore:

> **Nana/Hachi is the structural central romance of *NANA* without being an established conventional romance. It is a materially real domestic partnership, a chosen-family bond, a mutually transformative attachment, and an explicitly queer-coded field of romantic possibility. Its ambiguity is produced by the text’s own language, gender logic, symbols, and narrative architecture. The responsible reading neither evacuates the romance nor invents a consummated couple. It recognizes that the series’ most important relationship is precisely the one whose emotional truth exceeds the social forms its characters know how to name.**

# 31. Baseline propositions frozen for later documents

The following propositions should be treated as the Phase-3 Nana/Hachi baseline unless later primary-source verification requires correction:

1. **Nana/Hachi is the work’s principal temporal and domestic relationship axis.**
2. **The dyad begins as chosen infrastructure, not mystical completion:** 707’s privacy, shared cost, and mutual aid matter as much as fate language.
3. **Hachi’s attraction to Nana is explicitly romance-adjacent in the Japanese text:** `かなり恋に近い`, `初恋みたい`, and the male-gender counterfactual are directly established.
4. **Nana’s attachment is also explicitly romance-coded:** she uses `恋`, while denying `性欲` as the explanation she recognizes.
5. **The relationship contains bodily and erotic possibility but no established mutual sexual relationship.**
6. **Neither woman receives a stable lesbian or bisexual identity label in the extant manga; such labeling would exceed the evidence.**
7. **707 establishes a genuine period of domestic partnership and becomes chosen home, lost home, ritual home, and relational archive.**
8. **Chosen family is a correct but incomplete category because the dyad also carries jealousy, romantic language, symbolic pairhood, and unique narrative centrality.**
9. **Hachi’s core vulnerability is the desire to be needed and therefore irreplaceable.**
10. **Nana’s core dyadic vulnerability is the desire for freely chosen devotion to behave like guaranteed permanence.**
11. **Pet, yard, and collar imagery establishes possessive danger, not romantic category by itself.**
12. **The hero/heroine structure is among the strongest proofs of reciprocal narrative centrality.**
13. **The love-letter, red-thread, matching-ring, `片想い`, and proposal motifs establish deliberate romantic coding but do not individually prove conventional couple status.**
14. **The rings’ origin in Hachi/Takumi and Nana/Ren is analytically important: heterosexual institutional objects are redirected into Nana/Hachi continuity rather than erased.**
15. **Nana/Ren remains Nana’s explicit romantic and sexual love; Hachi’s relationships with Nobu and Takumi remain genuine. Nana/Hachi centrality does not invalidate them.**
16. **Nana/Hachi occupies the structural position of the central romance because it organizes meeting, home, separation, memory, future address, waiting, and possible restart.**
17. **Structural romance and categorical romance are distinct. The first is strongly established; the second remains unresolved.**
18. **Future search does not create entitlement. The door and hand motifs increasingly preserve Nana’s right to refuse approach.**
19. **The mature relational ethic is `繋ぐ` rather than binding: connection across separate lives, not merger, monopoly, or total sacrifice.**
20. **Ren’s death strengthens Nana/Hachi as the central surviving relation without making Hachi a replacement for Ren.**
21. **Chapters 81–84 return the relationship to necessity: Hachi must learn whether she can belong beside Nana without Nana remaining broken enough to need rescue.**
22. **The hiatus leaves the final relationship form unknown:** reunion is a major trajectory, but exclusivity, sexuality, cohabitation, and social category remain open.
23. **The most accurate mature description is:** a structurally romantic, explicitly queer-coded, chosen-family and domestic bond whose categorical form is unfinished.

These propositions now provide the relationship foundation for the ensemble, material-adulthood, ethics, narration, Japanese-language, visual-form, gender, and comparative documents that follow.
