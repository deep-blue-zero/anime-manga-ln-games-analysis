# 06 - Home, Family, Pregnancy, Motherhood, Money, Work, and Material Adulthood
## The material conditions under which love becomes possible, constrained, or transformed

# 1. Governing thesis

A great deal of NANA can be summarized badly by asking which person a character loves most.

The manga repeatedly makes that question insufficient.

People need somewhere to live. Rent has to be paid. A pregnancy advances according to bodily time whether the people involved have resolved their feelings or not. A temporary worker can become too sick to work. A twenty-year-old guitarist can love someone and still lack the money to raise a child. A rich musician can provide extraordinary security while his wealth also gives him disproportionate control over the shape of a household. A celebrity can earn more money and simultaneously lose ordinary freedom of movement. A room can remain emotionally home after nobody lives there. A marriage can be legally real, materially useful, emotionally contradictory, and still not answer every question about love. A person can perform hours of economically valuable domestic labor without receiving a wage. A child can belong to a family by decision, naming, residence, care, and future labor even when biological paternity remains unresolved.

For that reason, the material logic of NANA is not a secondary realism layer underneath a romance manga. It is one of the principal mechanisms by which the manga tests romantic belief.

The series' mature material proposition is:

> Love becomes adult when it has to inhabit space, time, bodies, money, work schedules, law, privacy systems, and obligations to third parties.

This does not mean that material conditions determine emotional truth. NANA consistently resists that reduction as well. Takumi's money does not prove that Hachi loves him. Nobu's lack of money does not prove that his love is inadequate. Apartment 707 is not authentic because it is cheap, and the Shirokane residence is not false because it is expensive. Hachi's decision to continue her pregnancy is not created by Takumi's wealth, although his wealth changes what continuation can look like. Celebrity labor does not make marriage insincere, although it can determine when a marriage is announced, where a couple lives, and who is allowed to know about it.

The better model is a feasible-set model:

> Feelings generate desired futures; material structures determine which futures can actually be attempted, how costly they are, who bears those costs, and how much bargaining power each person possesses while attempting them.

This is why the question governing this document is not "Who does she love more?"

It is:

> What choices are materially possible, and what does each choice cost?

The answer changes across the series. Early NANA treats adulthood as reaching Tokyo, finding work, paying rent, and building a home with another person. Middle NANA turns pregnancy into a collision among body, money, marriage, career, and housing. Later NANA shows celebrity converting privacy into infrastructure, family into public information, and home into a security problem. The future timeline finally distributes family, work, children, travel, memory, and care across more than one household and even more than one country.

The resulting adult world is neither the independent individual nor the sealed nuclear family.

It is a network of material interdependence.

# 2. Method: relationships as arrangements

This document treats intimate relationships as arrangements that require resources and repeated practices.

For each major domestic or family structure, the relevant questions are concrete:

- Who has legal or practical access?
- Who has a key?
- Who pays?
- Who cooks, cleans, shops, hosts, waits, transports, nurses, or manages appointments?
- Who can leave without losing housing or income?
- Who can return?
- Whose work schedule governs everyone else's time?
- Who has privacy?
- Who can invite friends?
- Who controls information about the household?
- Which bodies carry reproductive or caregiving costs?
- Which forms of labor are paid, and which are treated as natural extensions of love?
- What outside institutions - landlords, employers, agencies, record companies, hospitals, family registries, tabloids, security staff - acquire power over private life?

This is deliberately not the principal ethical adjudication document. Document 07 will address consent, coercion, care, possession, and autonomy directly. Here the task is prior and descriptive: identify the structures inside which those ethical questions become meaningful.

The source hierarchy remains the one established by the project method. Japanese manga evidence governs Volumes 1-21. The continuation through Chapters 81-84 is usable for narrative, visual, material, and relational analysis under its documented English/Spanish fan-translation provenance; it is not promoted into original-Japanese lexical evidence. NANA 7.8 remains excluded until the dedicated supplemental-material phase.

# 3. Apartment 707: cheap architecture becomes social infrastructure

## 3.1 The founding material design

Apartment 707 is powerful precisely because it begins without mystical properties.

The train gives Hachi a language of fate. The apartment gives fate a floor plan.

Both women independently choose the apartment before the shared-living arrangement is settled. More importantly, the landlord's logic is explicit: the arrangement can preserve each woman's privacy while allowing mutual help in an emergency and dividing costs. [NANA_V02_E007-E008]

That is already a miniature social philosophy.

707 is not founded on marriage, blood, romantic exclusivity, band membership, or financial dependency on one provider. It begins as a compromise among:

- affordability;
- separate rooms;
- privacy;
- shared costs;
- practical mutual aid;
- and voluntary proximity.

The material design allows intimacy without requiring fusion.

This matters because the rest of the series repeatedly tests arrangements that are wealthier, safer, more official, or more glamorous but less permeable and less mutually authored.

The apartment also gives Hachi's domestic competence a non-romantic field in which to operate. Her earlier fantasy with Shoji had imagined happiness partly as caring for a boyfriend's daily needs. [NANA_V02_E005] At 707, cooking, decorating, shopping, hosting, waiting, and maintaining the room become contributions to a household that is not organized by heterosexual couplehood.

That is one reason 707 becomes such an important alternative model of family.

## 3.2 The right to the home has to be negotiated

A home becomes more real, not less real, when its occupants begin disagreeing about it.

By Volume 4, Hachi asserts a right to participate in decisions about overnight guests. Nana can no longer treat the apartment as a private extension of her own social world. The ensuing conflict is a domestic-government problem: two people with equal claims must negotiate what shared residence permits.

The result is analytically important because it prevents the project from romanticizing early 707 as effortless harmony. Its value comes from a structure in which boundaries can be fought over and repaired without either resident becoming the other's dependent.

Volume 5 then adds expectation of return. Nana calls to ask where Hachi is, why she is late, and when she is coming home. [NANA_V05_E029] The room has acquired a temporal routine. Hachi's absence leaves a gap because her presence is now built into ordinary life.

The series thereby makes a useful distinction:

> A residence is a place one can occupy. A home is a place where another person's ordinary return has become expected.

## 3.3 The home becomes permeable to outside relationships

Volumes 5-8 show the limits of the founding architecture.

Takumi enters 707 first as a celebrity guest and sexual partner, then as someone who can eat Hachi's cooking, wait, sleep there, bring groceries, use Hachi's phone, and eventually intervene in the pregnancy crisis. Nana and Ren's renewed relationship also changes who sleeps where and which household each person treats as primary on a given night.

The issue is not simply jealousy. It is access.

Who has a legitimate right to enter? Who can remain overnight? Who speaks for a resident? Does a guest's competence authorize him to take over decisions? When Hachi tells Takumi not to come while Misato is staying, she exercises household boundary authority. [NANA_V07_E007] When Takumi later arrives during the pregnancy crisis and begins managing the situation, the home becomes an infrastructure through which care and control can be exercised simultaneously.

The apartment's meaning therefore shifts from shelter to jurisdictional space.

## 3.4 Pregnancy turns 707 into a sickroom and decision room

Volume 8 is the point at which domestic space and bodily reality become inseparable.

The positive pregnancy test appears at home. Vomiting, exhaustion, secrecy, uncertainty, and the inability to work become visible inside the apartment. Nana changes her own schedule because she thinks Hachi is ill. Misato worries about leaving Hachi alone. Takumi's physical presence matters because Hachi is sick enough that someone being there has practical value.

The apartment is no longer just where relationships happen. It is where a body requires care.

That distinction matters for the entire later series. Domestic intimacy is repeatedly measured through banal competencies: who notices that someone has eaten, who gets food, who drives, who stays through illness, who prepares a room, who can provide privacy, and who has the time to do any of those things.

## 3.5 After cohabitation, 707 becomes archive

Volume 9 produces one of the series' clearest material definitions of relational loss.

Hachi's room is emptied. She continues paying rent temporarily and arranges return of the key. [NANA_V09_E089] Nana still possesses access and can return with her own key even though she no longer knows Hachi's routine. [NANA_V09_E065]

The objects remain.

The old bed, the strawberry glasses, the table, walls, and accumulated traces of prior life turn the apartment into evidence of a relationship that no longer has the same daily form. The fact that later harm can also occur inside that space prevents nostalgia from making the archive innocent. A remembered home can preserve both tenderness and injury.

This is the point where 707 ceases to be adequately described as "where the Nanas live."

It becomes:

> a material memory system whose access survives the household configuration that produced it.

## 3.6 Celebrity makes the old home unsafe

Volume 10 introduces a correction nostalgia cannot solve: reporters can find 707. [NANA_V10_E040]

The apartment's early strengths - accessibility, permeability, ordinary urban location, lack of elaborate security - become vulnerabilities once Nana is a public figure. The very openness that once made found family easy now makes privacy difficult.

This is one of the series' most important material reversals:

> The same architecture can support autonomy at one life stage and fail to protect it at another.

The home has not become emotionally false. The external environment has changed.

That is why the later manga cannot simply restore the Volume 2 household and call the problem solved.

## 3.7 Ritual home, fallback, and open threshold

From Volumes 11 onward, 707 develops several functions at once.

It is a reunion site. It is a fallback Hachi can imagine if her marriage fails. It is a place she deliberately refuses to use merely as an escape from difficult confrontation. It is a space she continues cleaning even after legal marriage and relocation. [NANA_V15_E143] It becomes the location of annual fireworks and Christmas rituals. Future Hachi says the old network waits there for Nana. [NANA_V12_E004]

By Volume 17, the empty apartment still receives mail addressed to Hachi under her married name, and Nana's missing key becomes evidence sustaining the hope of return. [NANA_V17_E006, E009-E011]

By Volume 18, Nana herself can return to 707 alone. The stripped-down room lets her recover a form of solitude that is not identical to abandonment. [NANA_V18_E205-E212]

This is a major evolution. The apartment's relational history becomes strong enough to support solitary self-recognition. It no longer requires reenacting the original cohabitation in order to function as home.

The most mature formulation is therefore:

> 707 evolves from affordable shared housing into chosen home, contested household, archive, fallback, ritual site, receiving address, and open threshold.

No single later function cancels the earlier ones.

# 4. House is not home: competing domestic systems

NANA repeatedly places materially superior residences beside emotionally stronger but less secure homes. It does not resolve the comparison by declaring poverty authentic and wealth corrupt. Instead, it separates several variables that romantic fiction often combines.

A residence can offer:

- physical safety;
- financial security;
- privacy;
- social permeability;
- emotional recognition;
- legal legitimacy;
- resident authorship;
- proximity to work;
- and the right to return.

No major home in NANA maximizes all of them.

## 4.1 The Shirokane household: real home under unequal control

The Shirokane residence is the clearest test of simplistic symbolism.

At first it looks like anti-707. It is expensive, heavily secured, socially isolated, associated with Takumi's money, and built around pregnancy and celebrity privacy. In Volume 9, the security system is explicitly explained as protection for family during Takumi's frequent absences. [NANA_V09_E072] Hachi cooks, welcomes him home, participates in plans for the child's room, and also experiences homesickness. [NANA_V09_E075-E084]

All of those facts matter.

Calling Shirokane a prison misses Hachi's active household project. Calling it a simple dream home misses the structural asymmetry.

By Volume 10, Hachi explicitly says she wants a family and has decided to build happiness with Takumi and the child. [NANA_V10_E073-E074] By Volume 14, she regards the house as the place where she feels most settled and wants to make it genuinely home. By Volume 19, however, the physically large residence feels suddenly cramped because Hachi experiences the future as having to fit inside its already-established shape. [NANA_V19_E085-E088]

The contradiction is not accidental.

Shirokane is:

> a chosen home whose material stability is produced by an unequal distribution of money, ownership, information, and work power.

That makes it both protective and constraining without requiring either quality to be fake.

## 4.2 Luxury cannot substitute for accumulated belonging

The manga repeatedly stages luxury against home-memory.

Hachi can enter Takumi's expensive hotel world and still discover that she lacks money for ordinary transportation home. [NANA_V05_E035] The encounter makes class difference concrete: access to a rich person's room is not ownership of his resources.

Nana and Ren later receive a vast celebrity apartment. Its scale is explicit, and Nana dismisses much of the luxury as irrelevant compared with being able to live there at all. [NANA_V15_E094-E096] The decisive domestic moment is not the floor area. It is Ren offering to leave if Nana wants solitude, followed by Nana choosing co-presence.

The implication is consistent:

> material comfort can support a home, but home begins where occupancy becomes mutually authored.

## 4.3 Family homes reveal unequal starting positions

The Komatsu household, the Terashima ryokan, Takumi's childhood house, Shin's natal home, and the Uehara household show that "family home" is not a single category.

Hachi comes from an ordinary, noisy, durable family structure. Its familiarity is so normal to her that Nana's longing for a lively family atmosphere surprises and moves her. Nana explicitly says she lacks a hometown; future Hachi later answers that claim with the preserved material world of 707. [NANA_V03_E004-E005]

Nobu's family ryokan is home, workplace, inheritance, and economic safety net simultaneously. His artistic rebellion is real, but Volume 16 clarifies that it occurs with a fallback many other characters do not possess.

Takumi's childhood house is the inverse. It contains family members but fails the basic protective functions associated with home. An unreliable alcoholic father, serious maternal illness, an overburdened sister, early newspaper delivery, self-feeding, and financial desperation train Takumi to treat competence and money as protections against collapse. [NANA_V18_E225, E236-E240]

Shin's later return to the fact that his family house exists while his room no longer does makes exclusion architectural: the family system continues without preserving a place for the child who left.

The Uehara household shows another failure mode. The building can remain standing while publicity destroys its privacy function. Media attention makes a home economically and socially permeable against the residents' wishes.

Taken together, these homes produce a structural rule:

> The existence of a house does not prove the existence of refuge, and the absence of blood or legal kin does not prevent refuge from being built elsewhere.

# 5. Pregnancy: when choice acquires a body, clock, and budget

Pregnancy is the largest single material-adulthood hinge in the series because it forces domains that had previously been separable into the same decision.

Hachi must confront:

- bodily symptoms;
- medical time;
- abortion as an available option;
- uncertainty of paternity;
- inability to work normally;
- housing;
- income;
- possible single parenthood;
- Nobu's career and resources;
- Takumi's resources;
- social recognition;
- marriage;
- publicity;
- and the future labor of raising a child.

The manga does not permit a purely romantic solution because the body will not wait for relational clarity.

## 5.1 Hachi chooses continuation before Takumi solves the economics

The sequencing is crucial.

Hachi initially wants the test to be wrong and considers abortion. Medical examination confirms pregnancy at roughly six and a half weeks, and the clinic makes the time-sensitive alternatives explicit. [NANA_V08_E021-E022]

The ultrasound then changes Hachi's experience of the pregnancy. Her own account is that she no longer wants an abortion followed by acting as though nothing happened. She considers giving birth and raising the child herself. [NANA_V08_E074-E077]

Only then does the material problem become decisive: she has little money, and morning sickness makes even her existing work difficult. [NANA_V08_E078]

This means the correct causal formulation is not:

> Takumi pays, therefore Hachi keeps the baby.

It is:

> Hachi wants to continue the pregnancy; Takumi's material capacity changes which forms of continuation appear feasible.

That distinction preserves both Hachi's reproductive agency and the power created by unequal resources.

## 5.2 Nobu's contraception is already economic reasoning

Nobu's use of condoms is not presented merely as generic sexual responsibility. He explicitly links contraception to his inability to finance a child. [NANA_V08_E049-E050]

The pregnancy therefore tests a young-adult relationship against a future neither partner is materially prepared to carry.

Nobu's love can be genuine. He can refuse to disclaim possible paternity. He can also be twenty years old, entering a precarious music career, and lack the capacity Hachi imagines a household requiring.

Junko's point that love alone cannot raise a child is not an anti-romantic slogan. It is a statement about labor and resources. [NANA_V08_E082]

## 5.3 Takumi's resources are real care infrastructure

Takumi can offer things Nobu cannot immediately offer:

- money;
- secure housing;
- transportation;
- pregnancy knowledge derived from family experience;
- food and practical care;
- willingness to support and acknowledge the child despite uncertain paternity;
- access to professional and legal systems;
- and enough income that Hachi does not have to solve pregnancy through immediate wage labor.

Those are real goods.

The analysis does not become more critical by pretending they do not matter.

At the same time, useful resources produce leverage. The more completely one person can solve the material obstacles surrounding another person's chosen future, the more costly refusal can become.

This is the structural problem Document 07 will later examine ethically. Here the essential material point is:

> economic competence changes bargaining conditions even when the help is sincerely useful.

## 5.4 Pregnancy is labor before birth

Later volumes normalize what the crisis volume initially dramatizes.

Pregnancy becomes repeated clinic visits, nutrition, weight management, restricted clothing, altered mobility, public concealment, sleep, physical discomfort, and planning. Hachi's body performs continuous labor even when she is not in paid employment.

Celebrity adds another layer: a pregnancy connected to Takumi becomes information that agencies and magazines can monetize. By Volume 15, the press publicly reports the marriage and pregnancy, converting a bodily condition into entertainment-industry news. [NANA_V15_E137-E138]

The private body therefore becomes a public economic object without Hachi herself becoming an entertainer.

# 6. Marriage, legal family, and the difference between form and life

NANA treats marriage neither as the final proof of love nor as an empty bourgeois shell.

It is a legal and social technology with real consequences.

Marriage affects:

- names;
- housing legitimacy;
- parental recognition;
- publicity strategy;
- access to family networks;
- who can speak publicly for whom;
- household expectations;
- inheritance and registration systems;
- and the social interpretation of proximity.

## 6.1 Marriage becomes infrastructure during the pregnancy crisis

Takumi's initial marriage proposal is explicitly connected to hidden-child risk, public image, and media management rather than being introduced as a pure declaration of romantic destiny. [NANA_V08_E091-E092]

That does not make the marriage unreal.

It establishes one of the series' mature principles:

> an intimate institution can be emotionally meaningful and instrumentally useful at the same time.

Hachi later insists that she is not marrying Takumi merely because of the child. [NANA_V10_E035] She also actively resists postponement when industry considerations delay legal registration. [NANA_V12_E054-E057]

When registration finally occurs, the manga emphasizes paperwork. Hachi becomes legally married because documents are submitted. [NANA_V15_E036] Love has to pass through bureaucracy before the state recognizes a household.

## 6.2 Names are material social positions

Hachi rehearses the married name Ichinose Nana before it becomes legally active. Later, mail addressed to her under that name arrives at 707. [NANA_V12_E076; NANA_V17_E006]

The name is more than symbolism. It can grant credibility and access because Takumi's public status is legible to strangers. At Ren's funeral, Hachi's status as "Takumi's wife" becomes social capital she can use to shield Nana from the press. [NANA_V21_E028]

The same category can also be used against her. In Chapters 82-84, Asami invokes "Takumi's wife" as a reason Hachi's presence in the BLAST grief household appears anomalous.

Legal identity therefore does not merely describe belonging. It can authorize, protect, restrict, and rhetorically assign a person to a place.

## 6.3 Household, legal kin, and felt family are not identical

Volume 15 makes the distinction literal through the family register. Nana and Ren's marriage paperwork exposes adoption and birth information Nana did not possess as lived memory. [NANA_V15_E113-E116]

This yields three different family systems:

- household: who actually lives together;
- legal kinship: who the state records as related;
- felt family: whom the character experiences as family.

NANA repeatedly shows these systems overlapping without becoming identical.

707 can function as family without legal kinship. A legal parent can fail to provide home. Shin can wish he had been born as Hachi's child despite no biological relation. Nana can lack a usable natal home while possessing chosen family. Hachi and Takumi can decide that the unborn child belongs to the family they are building even when biological paternity remains unresolved. [NANA_V21_E020]

The series thereby treats family as both status and practice.

# 7. Money and the feasible set of adult lives

Money in NANA rarely functions as a crude indicator of moral worth.

It functions as capacity.

It determines who can move, wait, quit, travel, hide, raise a child, hire help, absorb scandal, or survive a period without work.

## 7.1 Hachi learns economic adulthood before the pregnancy

The material trajectory begins early.

Hachi does not simply rush to Tokyo for Shoji. She remains home long enough to save money for the move. [NANA_V02_E002] She later develops a concrete savings plan around the imagined future house. [NANA_V03_E006]

Employment then refuses to become an uncomplicated adulthood badge. Hachi is fired for workplace irresponsibility in Volume 5 and rejected from several subsequent interviews. [NANA_V05_E017, E038] She later finds short-term work she can actually enjoy. [NANA_V06_E002]

This sequence is important because it keeps two truths together:

- Hachi is capable of work and increasing practical competence;
- her labor-market position remains fragile.

Pregnancy does not create that precarity from nothing. It exposes it.

## 7.2 Celebrity proximity is not wealth ownership

Volume 5 offers an almost schematic class image: Hachi can spend the night in an expensive hotel room with Takumi and still lack money for ordinary transportation. [NANA_V05_E035]

The difference between access and ownership matters throughout the series.

Hachi can move through celebrity spaces because of Nana, Takumi, or the wider music network without possessing the financial security of the people who normally inhabit them. The glamour is real. So is the class asymmetry.

That asymmetry helps explain why Takumi's adult competence is attractive before it becomes a household structure. He moves through hotels, cars, schedules, privacy systems, and expensive environments without hesitation. Hachi encounters not just a man but a form of material mastery.

## 7.3 Wealth expands options and concentrates leverage

The one-million-yen monthly residence of Volume 9 makes the comparison impossible to ignore. Takumi can pay for security, privacy, child preparation, travel, and ordinary household continuity without requiring Hachi's wage income.

The key analytical sentence is:

> money changes the feasible set of lives.

It does not decide which life is morally best or emotionally truest.

This is why "Nobu loves Hachi more" and "Takumi can support the baby" are not competing claims of the same type. The first is an interpretation of feeling. The second describes material capacity.

Adult choice occurs where the two domains meet.

## 7.4 Economic dependence can coexist with chosen contribution

Hachi's intended breadwinner/homemaker arrangement is explicit. She imagines Takumi working and earning while she raises the child. [NANA_V12_E078-E079]

The arrangement is materially dependent and strongly gendered. It is also something Hachi actively wants to make meaningful.

Her anxiety about being merely someone Takumi spends money on shows that she does not experience dependence as pure luxury. [NANA_V12_E062] She answers financial provision with domestic work, pregnancy labor, emotional maintenance, hosting, child planning, and the attempt to make the house restful enough that Takumi wants to return.

The series does not assign a wage to this labor, and the analysis should not invent one. But its substitution value is obvious: food, cleaning, scheduling, emotional atmosphere, pregnancy management, childcare, and social coordination would otherwise require time or purchased services.

The correct description is therefore not "Hachi does not work."

It is:

> much of Hachi's work becomes unpaid household production inside a marriage whose cash income is overwhelmingly generated by Takumi.

## 7.5 Future Hachi regains identifiable paid skill

The future timeline materially revises any claim that Hachi's adulthood terminates in homemaking.

Volume 17 shows Miu following through on teaching her kimono dressing. [NANA_V17_E221] Volume 18 then establishes that future Hachi has paid or at minimum occupationally recognized kimono-dressing work and is especially busy at New Year. [NANA_V18_E017-E018]

The evidence does not prove complete financial independence, and it should not be exaggerated into a career biography the manga never supplies.

What it does establish is important enough:

> Hachi's domestic and aesthetic competencies become portable adult skills usable beyond the marital household.

# 8. Work: time, identity, and the hidden governor of relationships

Work in NANA is rarely just background occupation.

It governs when people can meet, where they can live, what they can disclose, whether they can travel, how quickly they can recover, and which private crises become collective emergencies.

## 8.1 Takumi: work as constitutional hierarchy

Takumi says directly that work is his first priority and occupies more than ninety percent of his mental life. [NANA_V10_E113]

The statement is not merely character flavor. The entire household eventually has to live inside it.

Marriage timing, travel, absence, fatherhood, privacy strategy, and even crisis response are shaped by Trapnest's schedule. His childhood history makes this understandable in material terms: he learns early that unreliable adults and unstable earnings can destroy a household, so adult competence becomes a moral grammar. [NANA_V18_E236-E248]

That history explains why provision matters so deeply to him.

It does not make the consequences disappear. If one partner's work is treated as structurally immovable, the other partner's time becomes the adjustment mechanism.

Hachi waits, reschedules, performs domestic continuity, and absorbs absence.

## 8.2 Celebrity labor can produce more money and less freedom

BLAST and Trapnest progressively demonstrate an adult paradox:

> professional success can expand economic autonomy while reducing ordinary mobility.

Contracts create income. Salaries and royalties make artistic life materially sustainable. At the same time, management acquires claims on schedule, image, location, relationships, publicity, and security.

By Volume 11, Nana can plausibly imagine supporting Hachi and the child with music income. Yet she also becomes less free to move through public space. The BLAST dorm can supply food, laundry, sleep, and security while still feeling like a cage because its primary logic is institutional management rather than resident authorship.

The point is not that success is false freedom.

It is that different kinds of freedom trade against one another.

## 8.3 Work identities survive relationship crisis

The ensemble repeatedly uses work as continuity.

Nobu's songwriting remains a capacity even when BLAST is suspended. Reira's voice makes her both artist and economically indispensable labor power. Nana's celebrity work can drift away from the singing she values most. Yasu's participation in BLAST carries opportunity costs against his alternative professional path. Shin's increased musical income reduces the material need that had supported compensated sex. Yuri's adult-video contract shows the opposite problem: paid sexual labor creates obligations and reputational permanence that cannot simply be abandoned because private desire changes.

These examples belong fully in Document 08 at the industry level, but their household consequence is already clear:

> adult relationships are partly negotiations among work identities that cannot be suspended every time intimacy demands attention.

## 8.4 Grief does not abolish labor

Ren's death makes this especially stark.

Death produces work: police contact, body identification, transport, funeral planning, lodging, management communication, cancellation, press handling, and public statements.

Nana tries to cling to her next work obligation rather than stop for farewell, and Yasu forces the human event back into priority. [NANA_V21_E019]

The continuation extends the principle. Reira cannot yet work. Takumi appears to cope partly by working. Reira's mother cannot simply take over care because she cannot take enough time away from employment. Asami's position is threatened if she does not return to work. [NANA_C84_E005, E007, E011]

Grief therefore unfolds inside labor systems rather than outside them.

# 9. Domestic labor: care is made out of tasks

Hachi's most continuous form of competence is domestic.

That should not be romanticized into a claim that caring labor is naturally feminine. Nor should it be dismissed as merely decorative because it is unpaid.

Across the series Hachi repeatedly turns feeling into tasks:

- cooking;
- shopping;
- cleaning;
- decorating;
- hosting;
- remembering birthdays and holidays;
- arranging meetings;
- monitoring pregnancy;
- making a household pleasant;
- caring for sick people;
- feeding friends;
- maintaining 707 after moving out;
- supporting children;
- and later converting kimono knowledge into outside work.

This is one of the series' clearest bridges between emotion and material life.

## 9.1 Cooking is not merely a femininity sign

Food can be romantic, familial, strategic, communal, or emergency care depending on context.

Hachi cooking for Takumi participates in wifehood and in her attempt to make Shirokane a place he wants to return to. Hachi cooking for the 707 network produces chosen-family continuity. After Ren's death, cooking in the BLAST dorm becomes grief infrastructure rather than courtship.

That final transformation is particularly important.

Chapter 82 shows Hachi preparing breakfast inside a temporary dormitory. Chapter 84 turns sukiyaki, cheap meat, appetite, and joking into small mechanisms that keep ordinary time moving. [NANA_C82_E003; NANA_C84_E001]

The domestic act no longer belongs to a husband or boyfriend.

It belongs to a community in crisis.

## 9.2 Maintenance can preserve a relationship without restoring its old form

Hachi continuing to clean 707 is the purest example. [NANA_V15_E143]

She does not move back permanently. The old cohabitation is not restored. The maintenance itself preserves possibility.

Similarly, future Hachi's Christmas tree and fireworks rituals are forms of domestic labor directed toward an absent person. The work does not guarantee return. It keeps a material place in which return would still be legible.

This is care without control at the level of space:

> maintain the room; do not force the person into it.

# 10. Privacy, security, and mobility as household infrastructure

NANA's celebrity plot changes the meaning of material adulthood by making privacy expensive.

Ordinary people can usually treat shopping, commuting, clinic visits, family contact, and opening a front door as background actions. For celebrity-linked characters, these become logistical problems.

## 10.1 Security protects and isolates

Shirokane's multilayer security exists for a reason. [NANA_V09_E072]

Reporters can locate 707. Hachi's pregnancy and marriage can become public information. Nana and Ren's movements can affect careers and press narratives. Shin and Reira cannot assume that a private meeting will remain private.

Protection therefore requires:

- controlled addresses;
- staff;
- managed transportation;
- private entrances;
- secrecy;
- scheduled meetings;
- and restrictions on ordinary social permeability.

The problem is that the same mechanisms that protect a household can make it feel like confinement.

This is why the series repeatedly returns to a difficult home equation:

> safety without permeability can become isolation; permeability without safety can become exposure.

No residence solves the equation permanently.

## 10.2 Ordinary life becomes a scarce good

Volume 15 makes the cost visible in trivial activities. Hachi insists that she still wants to shop, take cooking lessons, visit Jackson's, and clean 707 despite reporters. Her refusal to spend her entire life hiding is a claim to ordinary urban adulthood, not just stubbornness. [NANA_V15_E143-E144]

Privacy is therefore not simply secrecy.

It is infrastructure that allows ordinary life to remain ordinary.

## 10.3 Mobility reveals adult obligation

Travel repeatedly translates feeling into practical constraint.

A person cannot simply "go after" someone without transport, time, money, childcare, school schedules, work leave, and an address.

The future search for Nana makes this explicit. Hachi's responsibilities include children and work; transnational movement must be coordinated with school calendars and other adults. [NANA_V19_E004-E005]

The romantic gesture of pursuit becomes an adult logistics problem.

That is not a diminishment of feeling. It is what feeling looks like after life becomes populated with obligations.

# 11. Motherhood, fatherhood, and family as sustained practice

The pregnancy arc asks who the father is biologically. The later series becomes increasingly interested in a different question:

> Who will do the work of being family?

## 11.1 Biological paternity remains unresolved where the text leaves it unresolved

The project must preserve that boundary.

Hachi's pregnancy may be biologically Takumi's or Nobu's. The manga does not authorize certainty at the relevant evidence horizon.

But social parenthood is not left equally indeterminate.

Hachi and Takumi decide to treat the child as theirs. By Volume 21 Hachi states the principle directly: the child who will be born is hers and Takumi's because the two of them decided that together. [NANA_V21_E020]

That claim does not erase Nobu's possible biological connection or the ethical cost Hachi recognizes. [NANA_V21_E021]

It establishes a different category:

> parenthood can be materially authored through decision, residence, support, naming, caregiving, and future responsibility.

## 11.2 The series separates maternal feeling from maternal labor

Volume 8 explicitly debates maternal instinct. Hachi invokes it. Nana doubts that she possesses it. A doctor provides a biological explanation that the project correctly retains as character discourse rather than narrative scientific fact.

The later volumes shift the question away from instinct and toward practice.

Hachi manages pregnancy, plans a household, anticipates raising the child, organizes care, and in the future is directly addressed as Mama. [NANA_V12_E008]

The narrative therefore does not need a universal theory of maternal instinct to make motherhood materially real.

Motherhood appears as repeated work.

## 11.3 Chosen family expands the child-bearing household

Shin's wish that he could have been born as Hachi's child reveals how strongly maternal care operates beyond biology. Nana's later willingness to imagine feeding Hachi's children inside a shared 707 future similarly expands the household beyond a conventional couple. [NANA_V19_E073-E074]

The future timeline goes further. Children, grandparents, Hachi, Takumi, Nobu, Reira, the old music network, Britain, and the search for Nana form a distributed family ecology.

The manga's material family is therefore not reducible to one apartment, one marriage, or one bloodline.

# 12. The future: adulthood becomes distributed rather than solved

The future sequences do not present a neat domestic end state.

They show a network.

Hachi remains legally married to Takumi in the supplied future material and says she does not intend to divorce at that point. Takumi remains physically involved in family life and performs ordinary parental care. [NANA_V19_E017-E021] A boy named Ren appears in the future household. [NANA_V20_E003] Satsuki exists in the wider family network. Hachi has skilled work. Children can stay with grandparents. Nobu remains connected to the family ryokan and to music. Nana is alive elsewhere. The search for her requires international travel and coordination.

This future is significant because it refuses the idea that adulthood culminates in one perfectly unified home.

Instead:

- legal marriage persists;
- households can be geographically distributed;
- children connect adults across spaces;
- paid work continues;
- old chosen-family spaces remain active;
- 707 survives as ritual home;
- and Nana remains a missing relational center whose absence no current household can simply absorb.

The mature spatial model of NANA is therefore polycentric.

A person may simultaneously possess:

- a legal household;
- a natal family connection;
- a professional home;
- a ritual home;
- a remembered origin place;
- and a relational destination.

The tragedy is not that adulthood failed to produce a single home.

The more difficult truth is that no single home could ever contain every bond the characters acquire.

# 13. Major corrections to reductive readings

## 13.1 "707 is the true home and Shirokane is the false home"

Too simple.

707 is the most symbolically central chosen home, but it becomes physically unsafe under celebrity exposure and no longer functions as the protagonists' ordinary residence. Shirokane is genuinely chosen by Hachi and supports pregnancy, privacy, and family life while also concentrating material power and limiting permeability.

The manga's question is not which building is morally pure.

It is what forms of safety, authorship, permeability, and recognition each space makes possible.

## 13.2 "Hachi chooses Takumi because he is rich"

Insufficient.

Hachi's decision to continue the pregnancy precedes Takumi's material solution. She has genuine desire for Takumi, genuine domestic aspirations, and genuine fear about Nobu's future. At the same time, Takumi's resources make one family arrangement far more materially feasible.

Money is causal without being total.

## 13.3 "Money should not matter if love is real"

The manga rejects this repeatedly.

Children require resources. Housing costs money. Work absence has consequences. Publicity can threaten income. Travel requires time and money. A person's lack of resources can constrain a choice without disproving love.

NANA's realism lies in refusing to make economic constraint evidence of emotional inferiority.

## 13.4 "Hachi becomes a housewife, so she stops developing"

Unsupported.

Her development moves through paid work, unemployment, temporary labor, reproductive labor, unpaid household labor, care coordination, and later skilled kimono-dressing work. Her economic dependence is real, but so is the growth of practical competence.

The better question is how different forms of labor affect bargaining power and self-worth.

## 13.5 "Providing money is either love or control"

False binary.

Provision can be loving, necessary, strategic, self-protective, controlling, or several of these at once. Takumi's capacity to solve material problems is one of his genuine strengths and one source of relational power.

Document 07 will judge specific uses of that power. Document 06 establishes why the power exists.

## 13.6 "Marriage settles family"

No.

Marriage makes family legible to law and society. It can produce names, legitimacy, social capital, and household structure. It does not erase chosen family, possible biological ambiguity, old attachments, or the difference between being legally related and feeling at home.

## 13.7 "Home is where one permanently lives"

The series makes this untenable.

707 remains home after residence ends. Hotels can provide shelter without home. A family house can be unsafe. A celebrity apartment can become home only through chosen occupancy. A dorm can become provisional home through practiced return. A pub can be a performance site without being a residence. The future family can be distributed across several locations.

Home in NANA is best understood as:

> repeated material recognition: a place or network where a person can return, be expected, be fed, be remembered, and remain legible without having to earn existence from zero each time.

# 15. Frozen Phase-4 propositions for later synthesis

The following propositions should be treated as the stable material baseline unless later source correction requires revision.

1. Apartment 707 is materially founded on privacy, mutual aid, and shared economic burden, not on romantic or legal obligation. [NANA_V02_E007-E008]
2. 707 becomes home through repeated practice, expectation of return, negotiated boundaries, and accumulated objects rather than through ownership alone.
3. After cohabitation ends, 707 continues as archive, fallback, ritual site, receiving address, and possible route of return. [NANA_V09_E065, E089; NANA_V12_E004; NANA_V15_E143; NANA_V17_E009]
4. Celebrity exposure makes the early openness of 707 materially unsafe without making its emotional home-status false. [NANA_V10_E040]
5. Shirokane is a genuine Hachi-authored family project as well as a residence structured by Takumi's disproportionate wealth, work power, and security control. [NANA_V09_E072-E084; NANA_V10_E073-E074]
6. Greater luxury does not reliably produce greater home. Resident authorship, co-presence, permeability, and recognition remain separate variables.
7. Hachi's decision to continue the pregnancy precedes and is analytically distinct from Takumi's offer of material support. [NANA_V08_E074-E079]
8. Pregnancy narrows Hachi's feasible options through body, medical time, work incapacity, money, and future childcare even though reproductive choice remains hers.
9. Nobu's contraception is explicitly linked to his inability to finance parenthood, making sexual responsibility an economic-adulthood question. [NANA_V08_E049-E050]
10. Takumi's wealth creates real care infrastructure and real relational leverage; neither fact cancels the other.
11. Marriage in NANA is both intimate and infrastructural: it creates legal status, names, publicity consequences, household recognition, and social capital. [NANA_V08_E091-E092; NANA_V15_E036]
12. Household, legal kinship, biological relation, and felt family must remain analytically distinct.
13. Hachi's motherhood is materially demonstrated through reproductive, domestic, and future caregiving labor rather than requiring a universal theory of maternal instinct.
14. Hachi performs substantial unpaid household production after marriage; describing her as simply not working would erase cooking, maintenance, pregnancy, childcare planning, hosting, and care coordination.
15. Future Hachi's kimono-dressing work establishes portable occupational competence without proving complete financial independence. [NANA_V17_E221; NANA_V18_E017-E018]
16. Work schedules function as a constitutional layer of adult relationships, especially in celebrity households.
17. Celebrity success can increase income while reducing privacy, spontaneous mobility, and control over time.
18. Privacy is material infrastructure. Without it, ordinary actions such as shopping, commuting, visiting friends, or receiving medical care become public-risk events.
19. Family can be materially authored through decision, residence, support, naming, and caregiving even when biological paternity remains unresolved. [NANA_V21_E020-E021]
20. The future family system is distributed across households, generations, work, travel, and countries; the series does not resolve adulthood into one permanent domestic center.
21. After Ren's death, Hachi's domestic competence becomes communal grief infrastructure rather than merely wifehood. [NANA_C82_E003; NANA_C84_E001]
22. The continuation reinforces that caregiving is constrained by employment availability: Reira's mother cannot simply replace Takumi at the hospital because work limits her time. [NANA_C84_E007]
23. A household label can be both materially empowering and socially restrictive: "Takumi's wife" gives Hachi authority in some contexts and becomes a reason others question her place in another. [NANA_V21_E028; NANA_C84_E011]
24. The series' material question is not whether love or money matters more. It is how love operates when money, work, bodies, law, privacy, and third-party obligations make some futures easier to build than others.

# 17. Handoff to Documents 07 and 08

This document deliberately stops before two later syntheses.

Document 07 should take the material asymmetries established here and ask their ethical consequences. It should not have to re-prove that Takumi has disproportionate economic power, that Hachi's pregnancy narrows options, that housing access can be controlled, or that care can depend on resources. Those structures are now frozen as description. The ethical document can ask when provision becomes coercive, when protection becomes jurisdiction, how consent interacts with dependence, and how possession differs from care.

Document 08 should take the work and privacy structures established here and expand them into the music/celebrity system. It should not need to repeat the entire domestic history of 707 or Shirokane. Its task is to explain how BLAST, Trapnest, Gaia, management, contracts, sales, tabloids, fandom, touring, publicity, and market timing industrialize private life.

The material synthesis therefore leaves the project with a simple but demanding baseline:

> In NANA, adulthood is not the moment when feeling becomes less important. It is the moment when feeling becomes inseparable from the material world required to sustain it.

A home has to be paid for and maintained. A child has to be carried and raised. A marriage has to be registered and lived. Work consumes time. Fame changes privacy. Money creates capacity. Dependence creates vulnerability. Care takes labor. Travel requires logistics. Old rooms preserve memory. People can belong to more than one household and still fail to feel at home.

The series' deepest material realism lies in refusing both romantic cynicism and romantic exemption.

Love is not reducible to infrastructure.

Love is also never free of infrastructure.
