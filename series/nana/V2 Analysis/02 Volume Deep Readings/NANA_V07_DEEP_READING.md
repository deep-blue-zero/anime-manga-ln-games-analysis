# 『NANA』Volume 7 Deep Reading
## Needed, not possessed: belonging, reciprocal love, professional separation, and the evidence that cannot be painted over

**Canonical artifact:** `NANA_V07_DEEP_READING.md`  
**Cumulative scope:** Volumes 1–7 only.  
**Spoiler boundary:** no Volume 8+ material is used to settle Volume 7 ambiguities.  
**Method:** `NANA_ANALYTICAL_METHOD_V2`.

---

## 1. Central thesis and volume role

Volume 6 made possession explicit. Nana recognized that Ren and Hachi were not her property even while admitting that another person's separateness could make her desperately lonely. Ren had wished Nana were his. Takumi told Hachi to remain his. Shin argued that people eventually want 「相手の全部」—the whole beloved.

Volume 7 shifts the problem from **possession** to **necessity**.

Future Hachi gives the distinction herself:

> 「ナナを独り占めしたかったんじゃない」  
> 「ナナに必要とされたかっただけなの」

She did not want Nana all to herself. She wanted Nana to **need** her.

This is the volume's governing psychological correction. Hachi's fear is not primarily that Nana might care about other people. It is that Hachi's particular place might become redundant.

BLAST's success activates that fear. The audience grows; Gaia Music watches; Nana's charisma becomes commercially legible. Hachi describes herself as disappearing into the growing cheers. She understands that Nana should prioritize record-company business over private promises and professional music over uninterrupted cohabitation at 707. Then she thinks:

> 「でもそれならあたしは もういらないじゃない」

Then Nana no longer needs her.

That is a **character belief**, not an objective fact. Volume 7 strongly contradicts it. Nana remains moved by Hachi's care, returns to 707, endorses Hachi/Nobu, and privately reveals that her support for Hachi/Nobu was partly:

> 「ハチ公を自分の庭で放し飼いにする為」

to let Hachikō roam free **in Nana's own yard**.

Volume 6 asked whether Nana treated Hachi like a pet. Volume 7 does not simply resolve the problem. Nana revises the fantasy: Hachi may roam freely, but Nana still imagines the field of freedom as **her territory**. Moral insight changes the form of possessiveness without abolishing attachment.

At the same time, Nobu offers Hachi something that answers her fear almost perfectly: he explicitly needs her support. He rejects his old belief that a man should merely support a woman and asks Hachi to support him too. Hachi discovers that love can mean wanting to give—kindness, presence, help, an embrace—rather than only receiving those things from men.

This is genuine maturation. Yet Hachi immediately calls Nobu the 「本物の王子様」, the true prince who awakens her from the Takumi dream. She has not stopped narrating romance as fairy tale. She has transferred the fairy tale to a relationship that currently contains more reciprocity.

The volume then refuses a clean “new romance erases old error” ending. Hachi removes the Trapnest poster, but its sun-faded outline remains. She repaints the wall white and feels like a criminal performing 「証拠隠滅」, destruction of evidence. The final narration states that mistakes committed and wounds received do not disappear merely because they are covered.

And then the entire volume returns to Nana:

> 「あたしは今もナナの名前を呼び続けている」  
> 「どんなに痛くても」  
> 「応えてもらえるまで」

Future Hachi still calls Nana's name, however painful, until Nana answers.

The central question therefore becomes:

> **Can I still know that I matter when the person I love no longer needs me in the same way?**

## 3. Narrative architecture and causal close read

### Chapter 21: success as separation

BLAST's audience is growing. Hachi experiences the audience heat as almost vertiginous. On p.17 the visual structure is decisive: Nana occupies the stage as an increasingly singular public figure while Hachi is absorbed into spectators. Hachi says she feels she may disappear inside the expanding cheers.

This revises the Volume 5 “same light” thesis. Hachi wanted to inhabit Nana's extraordinary world. Volume 7 reveals that once Nana's light is shared by thousands, Hachi needs a different answer to the question “what makes **my** presence matter?”

Gaia personnel observe BLAST. Nana's previously private charisma becomes professional vocabulary and potential market value. The person whose performance once transformed a dining table into a stage is becoming institutionally legible.

Future Hachi says Nana's songs still encourage her to live. The paradox is important: Nana's career itself sustains Hachi, yet the career also activates Hachi's fear of being displaced.

Hachi says it is natural for Nana to prioritize record-company business over promises with Hachi, and natural to choose the dream called work over life with Hachi. She says she understands it all. Then she translates rational understanding into attachment panic: “then you don't need me anymore.”

Misato intensifies the anxiety because she is a visibly useful fan—devoted, selfless, continually present at lives. Hachi knows this and becomes tired of her own resentful jealousy.

Hachi sends Takumi a practical boundary: because Misato is staying at 707, he should not come to the apartment. Yet after avoiding Nobu she becomes lonely, receives Takumi's call, cries and says 「会いたい」. The volume repeatedly preserves Hachi's agency inside relationships whose power is unequal.

The homemade Black Stones cake then becomes Hachi's alternate form of participation. She cannot play an instrument, but she can bake. Nana is moved; later the band shares and photographs the cake. Hachi's domestic labor enters BLAST space even when Hachi cannot enter BLAST institutionally.

Chapter 21 closes with the crucial future-address passage:

> 「たとえば あたし達が恋人同士なら」

Suppose Nana and Hachi were lovers—would the gap between them have been small enough to fill by embracing?

The conditional 「なら」 must remain intact. Hachi does not say they **were** lovers. She asks whether the social form “lovers” would have provided permissions for repair that intense friendship did not.

Then comes the thesis:

> 「ナナを独り占めしたかったんじゃない」  
> 「ナナに必要とされたかっただけなの」

The problem is not monopoly. It is necessity.

### Chapter 22: belonging without membership

Future Hachi states that no matter how close she is to BLAST, no matter how often she goes to the studio, she can never literally be a member. Increasingly confronting that fact feels like **fear**, not merely loneliness.

The distinction is material. Friendship is affective; band membership has instruments, labor, contracts, schedules and institutional roles. Love cannot turn Hachi into a bassist or guitarist.

Hachi responds by creating a witness identity. She says no one can surpass her love for BLAST's members and music, and that this is something she can swear for life.

Her bond with Nobu deepens partly through art. She tells him that she has liked his songs from the first time she heard them. They startled and moved her. This is strong evidence against a rebound-only explanation of Hachi/Nobu.

Takumi's presence meanwhile becomes more complicated. Hachi sees how exhausted he is and interprets the fact that he came anyway as effort. Then she recognizes the reciprocal instrumentalization she had previously ignored: she once thought she was merely Takumi's convenient woman, available when he wanted sex, but she may also be using him as a convenient man who holds her when she is lonely. The status asymmetry remains; her agency also remains.

Hachi's short-term work prompts a direct statement of domestic vocation. She says that even if she never finds a deeply meaningful job, she could be happiest cooking every day for someone she loves. The qualifier 「あたし的に」—for me—matters. The manga does not present domestic desire merely as something she must outgrow.

But Hachi does not imagine that life with Takumi. She says marriage with him is impossible and that she should end the current unsatisfactory arrangement and have a 「まっとうな恋愛」, a proper/legible romantic relationship. Underneath this remains her fear of aging without a purpose and dying alone.

The Nobu scenes then place the “real self” problem in the foreground. Hachi tells him that he must be 「美化」ing her and would be 「幻滅」ed by the real Hachi. Nobu does not answer “you're perfect.” He says: then disillusion me. Knowledge, not reassurance, becomes the test.

The Yasu/Reira backstory gives a powerful comparator. Yasu admits jealousy made him less able to support Reira properly when they were together. Yet in the present he can say Reira no longer needs his support. He can relinquish necessity without treating reduced dependence as abandonment. Hachi cannot yet do that with Nana.

Reira gives the volume's major art/personhood formulation. She sometimes wishes she could be a singing machine without desire or jealousy, but knows that if she threw away emotion she could not sing well. The painful human self is also the artistic self.

By the end of the chapter Hachi understands that she must choose: say goodbye to Takumi or remove herself from Nobu's arms. She cannot indefinitely accept intimacy from both while avoiding the decision.

### Chapter 23: choice, disclosure, and the “real prince”

Hachi and Nobu kiss and walk hand-in-hand. His hand feels warm and immediately reminds Hachi of Nana's hand. This is formal evidence that Nana's touch has become part of Hachi's template for secure intimacy. It does not make Nobu a substitute or Nana a secretly settled romantic partner; it shows how deeply the Nana relationship has entered Hachi's bodily memory.

Hachi then states that her desire for Nobu existed before the final decision. She had walked along the river wanting to be his girlfriend, wanting to touch him and be held.

As BLAST's contract process advances, Shin's performed adulthood collides with law. Yasu reminds him that he is fifteen and needs a guardian's signature. Sexual experience, commercial self-pricing and punk appearance do not erase legal minority.

Hachi tells Nobu she intends to end Takumi and admits that she had once wanted to hide the whole Takumi episode so she could become Nobu's girlfriend without exposing it. She calls this unfair. Nobu explicitly refuses 「力ずくで奪う」—taking her by force—and says he will not intensify the triangle while she remains emotionally caught between two men.

He is not free of possessive vocabulary. He says that if Hachi herself leaves Takumi and chooses to become 「おれの女」, he will make her happy. The ethical difference is therefore not pure language; it is whether claim-language is constrained by Hachi's choice.

Nobu tells her to settle her feelings and come to him, adding 「信じて待ってる」—he will trust her and wait.

Hachi experiences this through fairy-tale grammar. She wonders whether she is dreaming and calls it a dream that will not end. This is not evidence that Nobu is unreal. It is evidence that Hachi's narrativizing mind survives her maturation.

Back at 707, Hachi impulsively joins Nana in the bath. Future Hachi then tells Nana that the claw-foot tub is no longer present in her future “here.” The material fact is established; its cause is not.

Hachi decides to establish 「ケジメ」 with Takumi. When she calls, Takumi raises pregnancy as a possibility; Hachi emphatically says 「違うよ！」. No pregnancy is established in Volume 7.

Then Hachi finally says the sentence she could not say in Volume 6:

> 「もうして来ないで！」  
> 「さようなら！」

Do not contact me again. Goodbye.

She is astonished by her own agency: 「言えるじゃん あたし！」.

Immediately afterward, Hachi constructs a total story in which Takumi is an “unloving man” who could not possibly be hurt. This remains **her interpretation**. Later, the reader sees Takumi apparently attempt to call from a withheld number only to be blocked by Hachi's anonymous-call rejection setting. This means Hachi's conclusion “he never even called back” is factually incomplete.

But the later information also cuts the other way. Takumi describes Hachi as merely being angry on her own, rather than clearly recognizing her explicit goodbye as a breakup. At the Volume 7 endpoint, this is enough to flag a boundary-recognition problem without importing later conduct.

Hachi then calls Nobu the 「本物の王子様」 whose kiss wakes her from the false Takumi dream. She has corrected one fantasy by means of another. Genuine ethical growth and persistent romantic idealization coexist.

### Chapter 24: reciprocal love and unerasable traces

The Trapnest poster is gone, but the sun-faded outline remains on the wall. The material surface remembers what Hachi wants the room to forget.

Nobu explains why Komatsu Nana remains “Hachi” even in romance: the name “Nana” evokes Osaki Nana for him. A nickname Nana created inside 707 has become socially durable enough that Hachi's boyfriend uses it. Osaki Nana has altered Hachi's social identity at the level of ordinary address.

Nobu then tells Hachi about high-school Nana: rarely smiling, barely speaking, isolated and intimidating. When conversation failed, Nobu decided 「言葉でコミュニケーションが取れないなら 音楽だ！」. He gave Nana CDs. Punk—specifically the Sex Pistols—became the first successful bridge.

This recasts music. Before industry, celebrity or even BLAST, music was a technology for reaching a lonely person.

Nobu also says he eventually realized Ren was not the invincible figure he had imagined and that Ren may have been “saved” by Nana as much as Nana was saved by Ren. The relationship becomes mutual rescue rather than one-way protection.

The most important Nobu development follows. He says he used to assume 「男は女を支えてやるもんだ」, that men are supposed to support women, and performed strength before girlfriends. Now he thinks this may have been a major mistake. He wants Hachi to support him too.

This directly answers Hachi's Volume 7 fear. She worries Nana no longer needs her. Nobu says he needs Hachi's support.

Hachi then experiences a crucial internal change. Although she describes herself as someone who wants to 「甘える」—be indulged/depend—she suddenly wants to be kind to Nobu, stay beside him, help him and hold him. She recognizes that until now she had mostly imagined men doing those things for her.

This is perhaps the strongest early evidence of Hachi moving from love-as-receipt toward love-as-reciprocal-practice.

Yet the “need” axis remains double-edged. A partner's need gives Hachi security. Future analysis must ask whether she can continue loving when the loved person becomes less dependent.

Nana returns to 707 because Ren is busy. Hachi tells her about Nobu. Nana says Nobu has always been straightforward and shares Hachi's values. When Hachi says she has fallen deeply for him, Nana answers 「当然じゃない？」 and does not interrogate her. Hachi's fear of Nana's moral judgment is overturned. Nana functions as witness rather than prosecutor.

Junko and Kyosuke then provide an external stress test. They describe older Hachi as expressing love too one-sidedly and sometimes loving the role of the boyfriend more clearly than the concrete person. Junko also notes Hachi's habit of running from hurt, putting a lid on it and refusing to face the partner directly. Her recent behavior—telling Nobu the shameful truth and explicitly ending Takumi—does look like progress.

The Nana/Nobu scene then produces one of the volume's most important Nana thoughts. Nobu thanks Nana for quietly supporting Hachi/Nobu. Nana says it was not for his sake.

> 「ハチ公を自分の庭で放し飼いにする為」

She supported the arrangement so Hachikō could roam freely **in Nana's own yard**.

The wording is almost a perfect compromise between freedom and possession: not caged, but not outside Nana's territory. Nana recognizes Hachi's autonomy while still wanting that autonomy to remain inside Nana's relational orbit.

Shin later provides an internal counterreading of Nana/Ren. Where Hachi once romanticized them as “destined partners,” Shin finds Nana's possessiveness toward Ren frightening and wonders whether he is the distorted one for feeling that way. The manga deliberately gives both descriptions space.

Shin also explains why he pushed Hachi/Nobu: he wanted them to show him an answer. If men and women are ultimately 「欲望の対象物」, objects of one another's desire, can 「愛」 really connect them? Nobu thinks so. Shin says he cannot easily test the answer himself.

Gaia then offers BLAST a 「仮契約」, provisional contract. A full contract depends on a concrete CD-debut plan.

Professionalization exposes Shin's legal status. Because he is fifteen, a guardian must sign. The industry simultaneously plans to conceal his true identity/profile and pass him as eighteen. It needs his childhood legally while erasing it publicly.

The guardian conversation is devastating. The adult responsible for Shin says that despite raising him for fifteen years and despite the lack of blood relation, he has never felt affection for Shinichi—not once, though he says he tried.

This validates one of Shin's bleakest earlier claims and retrospectively strengthens Nana's refusal to sentimentalize “parents always care.” It also clarifies why Shin is so sensitive to the difference between material provision and love.

Finally, Hachi and Misato repaint the room. Misato sees a brighter white wall. Hachi feels like a criminal destroying evidence. She wonders whether trying to seal the memories means she is still doing something wrong.

The final page answers: mistakes committed and wounds received do not disappear when sealed over. And future Hachi is still calling Nana's name until she receives an answer.

## 4. Nana Osaki — character-state update

Nana's public success now has private costs she does not intentionally create. The larger BLAST becomes, the harder it is for Hachi to know what distinguishes her from fans, industry professionals and bandmates.

Hachi's belief that Nana no longer needs her is contradicted by Nana's own interiority. The “own yard” metaphor shows intense continuing attachment.

The important revision is that Nana's non-possession ethic is **partial rather than complete**. Volume 6 showed moral recognition: Hachi is not property. Volume 7 shows the residual fantasy: Hachi may be free, but Nana wants that freedom bounded by continued proximity.

Nana's support for Hachi/Nobu is therefore mixed in a psychologically convincing way. She sincerely believes Nobu and Hachi fit. She also benefits if Hachi's romantic future remains inside BLAST's social field.

The high-school backstory deepens Nana's artistic origin. Punk and recorded music were interpersonal language before they were professional identity. Nana's later charisma grows from a practice that first helped her escape isolation.

Nana also continues moving between Ren's home and 707. Her location changes more quickly than her attachment to Hachi. This distinction will matter.

## 5. Komatsu Nana / Hachi — character-state update

Hachi's central fear is now best described as **loss of necessity**. She wants evidence that she performs a role no one else can simply replace.

Her BLAST anxiety reflects a real structural problem: she can love the band without ever becoming a member. She responds by claiming the role of lifelong witness/fan.

Hachi's agency strengthens considerably. She recognizes mutual instrumentalization with Takumi, tells Nobu the shameful truth, chooses a fork, states an explicit breakup boundary to Takumi and begins wanting to care for Nobu rather than only receive care.

Her domestic vocation becomes more explicit and should be treated seriously. She really may prefer marriage, food, home and daily care to professional identity. The analytical issue is not to discredit that desire but to track the material dependence it can create.

Her Nobu attachment is not adequately explained by rebound. She admires his music and had wanted him before the final break with Takumi.

Yet Hachi's fairy-tale grammar remains active. The “real prince” line means relational maturation has not abolished romantic idealization.

The final wall sequence shows another maturation: Hachi increasingly understands that moral growth requires integrating the past rather than making visible evidence disappear.

## 6. Other materially affected characters

### Nobu

Nobu becomes a serious alternative model of masculinity. He rejects taking Hachi by force, accepts the possibility of disillusionment, waits for her decision, and explicitly wants mutual support rather than unilateral male providership.

He still uses 「おれの女」. The appropriate contrast with Takumi is therefore not “no possession versus possession,” but **claim constrained by choice versus claim that may discount another person's stated boundary**.

### Takumi

Takumi's professional competence remains real and totalizing. His belief that only he can maximize Reira's voice is worth tracking beside his relational certainty.

After Hachi's explicit breakup, he apparently attempts to call. This complicates Hachi's “he never cared enough even to call” belief. His description of Hachi as simply angry, however, suggests he may not recognize the breakup with the finality she intended.

### Shin

Shin's philosophical maturity and legal childhood collide. He asks whether love can connect people beyond desire, yet says he cannot test the answer himself. His guardian's declaration of lifelong non-affection gives his bleakness a concrete origin.

### Reira

Reira wants escape from desire and jealousy but knows emotion is necessary to sing. This is a major art/personhood contradiction.

### Yasu

Yasu can acknowledge that Reira no longer needs his support. This makes him a valuable comparator to Hachi. He also admits past jealousy impaired his own care, preventing idealization of him as perfect caretaker.

### Junko/Kyosuke

Their external analysis of Hachi's old relationship behavior supports the claim that Volume 7 marks genuine growth in facing rather than covering interpersonal conflict.

## 7. Relationship-state changes

### Nana/Hachi
**State:** possession anxiety becomes necessity anxiety.

Hachi explicitly distinguishes wanting Nana all to herself from wanting Nana to need her. The hypothetical-lover passage strengthens romance-adjacent textuality but remains conditional. Nana's “own yard” thought proves continuing attachment and continuing possessiveness.

### Hachi/Nobu
**State:** mutual romantic relationship is directly established.

Hachi's feelings predate the final decision. Nobu respects her need to settle Takumi, accepts disillusionment and waits. Hachi discovers reciprocal care. Fairy-tale idealization remains.

### Hachi/Takumi
**State:** Hachi explicitly ends the relationship.

She recognizes her own instrumental use, denies that Takumi fits her marriage future, and states a clear no-contact goodbye. Takumi's post-breakup interpretation remains unresolved and somewhat concerning.

### Hachi/BLAST
This becomes a major dossier. Hachi is not a member but defines herself as lifelong lover/witness of the music and people.

### Nana/Nobu
Nana endorses the match for both Hachi's happiness and Nana's own desire to keep Hachi close to her world.

### Shin/Reira
The sexual-commercial connection continues, but Shin's larger concern becomes whether love can transcend objectification.

### BLAST/Gaia
The relationship advances to provisional contract.

## 8. Japanese voice, address, and translation-sensitive language

The volume's key opposition is **独り占め** versus **必要とされる**. Hachi rejects monopolization as the best description of her wish and instead uses a passive construction: she wanted **to be needed by Nana**. Her security is organized around how the other person evaluates her importance.

「恋人同士なら」 retains conditional force. It should never be flattened into “we were lovers.”

「まっとうな恋愛」 is more normative than “real romance.” Hachi wants a relationship she can regard as morally/socially coherent.

「美化 / 幻滅」 gives Hachi/Nobu a knowledge axis: idealization versus disillusionment.

Nobu's 「力ずくで奪う」 explicitly rejects force and echoes earlier “take/seize” vocabulary. His later 「おれの女」 proves his romance is not linguistically free of possession; the difference is consent/choice.

「支える / 甘える」 maps the new care structure. Nobu wants Hachi's support. Hachi, who identifies as someone who wants to 甘える, discovers pleasure in supporting.

Nana's 「放し飼い / 自分の庭」 is one of the series' most compact expressions of bounded freedom.

Shin's 「欲望の対象物」 deliberately reduces persons to objects, setting up his question about whether 愛 can do something categorically different.

The ending's 「証拠隠滅」, 「犯した過ち」, 「受けた傷口」 and 「塞ぐ」 moves from crime to injury. Hachi is both someone who has made mistakes and someone who has been wounded.

Finally, 「応えてもらえるまで」 makes future Hachi's address reciprocal: she is not only remembering Nana; she wants an answer.

## 9. Narration, time, memory, and information distribution

Future Hachi becomes an increasingly sophisticated interpreter of her younger self.

She supplies:
- the necessity/monopoly distinction;
- the hypothetical-lover question;
- lifelong BLAST attachment;
- the continuing call to Nana.

None gives her omniscience about Nana.

Future material facts also accumulate. The claw-foot bathtub is gone. We know the object is absent; we do not know why.

The Takumi phone sequence is a clean information-asymmetry example. Hachi thinks he never called back. The reader later sees an apparent call blocked by anonymous-call rejection. Character belief is therefore not fact.

The final wall sequence makes memory physical. Object removal produces trace; trace produces repainting; repainting produces guilt; guilt produces the proposition that covering does not erase.

## 10. Visual/formal analysis

The live pages place Nana above and Hachi inside the crowd, formalizing the fear of disappearance.

The Black Stones cake functions as a material proxy for Hachi: her care enters BLAST when she cannot become a band member.

Nobu's warm hand is explicitly compared to Nana's. The new romance is bodily understood through an earlier Nana/Hachi memory.

The missing claw-foot tub turns former domestic intimacy into future absence.

The removed poster's outline is a remarkably precise trace image: the object is absent, but the environment has been changed by having contained it.

White paint makes Misato see brightness and Hachi see evidence destruction. The same visual act has two moral meanings.

The stack of CDs in Nobu's backstory shows music before spectacle: communication before industry.

The contract-room pages put Shin's child status inside adult bureaucratic space.

Caller-ID blocking turns turn-of-the-century mobile technology into relationship architecture.

## 11. Home and domestic-space ledger

707 remains home while no longer guaranteeing that Hachi is needed.

Hachi's cake, cooking and decorating continue to make domestic labor a form of participation and care.

Nana returns when Ren is busy, proving 707 remains available to her but also confirming that her residence is now fluid.

The room repainting is a direct attempt to make domestic space match a revised personal story. The manga refuses the fantasy that space can be made historically innocent.

Nana's “own yard” metaphor also turns home into relational territory: she wants Hachi free but still inside her orbit.

## 12. Material and economic adulthood

Hachi continues short-term retail labor and explicitly does not currently seek vocational self-definition.

Her domestic aspiration would create real economic dependence on a partner; this should be tracked without dismissing the aspiration.

BLAST's provisional contract introduces contracts, legal identity and professional obligations.

Shin's minor status requires guardian authorization, while Gaia plans to conceal his age/profile. Legal protection and market image-management coexist.

Hachi's breakup with Takumi is unambiguous relational boundary-setting. The later call attempt does not undo it.

Complicated by Shin's internal counterreading of the same possessiveness as frightening.

## 21. Volume thesis and questions carried forward

Volume 7 appears to give Hachi a solution. She explicitly ends a relationship she regards as unworkable and chooses a man she genuinely likes. Nobu wants her support. Hachi discovers that love can mean giving care rather than only receiving it.

That is real progress.

But the volume does not let a new romance erase the prior self.

The poster is removed. The wall remembers. The wall is repainted. Hachi feels like she is destroying evidence. Future Hachi says wrongdoing and wounds remain even after they are covered.

The same principle applies to Nana. Nana recognizes that Hachi is not property, yet imagines Hachi “free” inside Nana's yard. Moral understanding changes the form of possession without eliminating the attachment underneath it.

And it applies to BLAST. Nana's professional dream is coming true, while Hachi feels herself disappear in the crowd. Shin's legal childhood is exposed because the band is becoming professional, then the industry immediately proposes hiding it again.

The strongest compact thesis is therefore:

> **Volume 7 shifts *NANA* from possession to necessity. Hachi clarifies that she does not primarily want to own Nana; she wants to matter indispensably to her. BLAST's professional ascent threatens that necessity, while Nobu's reciprocal love offers Hachi a role in which she can support as well as be supported. This is genuine maturation, but Hachi wraps it in a new prince fantasy, and Nana's support for the relationship is revealed to contain a wish to keep Hachi “free” inside Nana's own yard. The volume finally rejects the fantasy of clean replacement: posters can be removed and walls repainted, but mistakes, wounds and attachments remain as traces. Across those traces, future Hachi is still calling Nana's name and waiting for an answer.**
