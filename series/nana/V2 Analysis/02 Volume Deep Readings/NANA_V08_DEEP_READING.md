# 『NANA』Volume 8 Deep Reading
## The limits of self-authorship: pregnancy, material choice, information control, and Nana's attempt to love Hachi without owning her

**Canonical artifact:** `NANA_V08_DEEP_READING.md`  
**Cumulative scope:** Volumes 1–8 only.

# 1. Central thesis and position in the larger narrative

Volume 7 ended with an apparently decisive movement toward self-authorship. Hachi stopped allowing the Takumi/Nobu triangle to remain undefined, told Takumi not to contact her again, and chose Nobu. Nana, meanwhile, recognized that loving Hachi could not ethically mean keeping her under literal control. The language of Volume 7 therefore seemed to point toward a difficult but intelligible principle:

> **A person has to choose her own life, and love has to learn to survive that choice.**

Volume 8 does not reject that principle.

It asks what the principle costs once choice stops being abstract.

Pregnancy turns self-authorship into a problem of body, time, money, employment, nausea, paternity uncertainty, medical deadlines, housing, career, public reputation, legal recognition, information access, and the willingness of other people to support the choice. The volume's deepest corrective comes from future Nana herself:

> she still believes that one's life depends on oneself;

but she has learned that nobody can always be that strong, and that accepting this fact has made her kinder.

That is the conceptual center of Volume 8.

The earlier story often made autonomy sound like an internal achievement. Nana did not follow Ren because she needed a life authored on her own terms. Hachi gradually learned that drifting among romantic scripts was not the same as choosing. Shin was told that even a damaged family history did not eliminate the possibility of finding his own reason to exist.

Volume 8 adds the missing material sentence:

> **Choice is only as free as the world in which a person has to carry it out.**

Hachi can decide whether she wants to continue the pregnancy. She cannot make nausea disappear. She cannot conjure money. She cannot remove uncertainty about biological paternity. She cannot make Nobu financially ready to raise a child. She cannot make Takumi's resources irrelevant once those resources make one option materially survivable. She cannot stop other people from interpreting silence, access, and who is physically present as evidence about what she has chosen.

The volume consequently gives Takumi his most analytically important form so far.

His power is not that he offers fake goods.

He offers **real goods**:

- practical competence;
- money;
- mobility;
- pregnancy knowledge;
- food and drink;
- willingness to acknowledge and support the child regardless of paternity;
- and finally marriage as a legal/public solution.

At the same time, the very same crisis reveals his tendency to preempt other people's process. Hachi explicitly tells him not to tell Nobu. He calls Nobu anyway. He uses Hachi's phone. He discloses the pregnancy and uncertain paternity before Hachi can decide how to tell anyone. He becomes the gatekeeper through whom Nana and Nobu receive information. He repeatedly occupies 707 after Hachi has previously ended the relationship. He is able to make a viable future appear at exactly the moment when everyone else's options look materially precarious.

This produces a much more serious model than “Takumi is responsible” or “Takumi is manipulative.”

> **Takumi's care and his control often occur in the same gesture.**

He solves problems quickly because he is willing to take control of the situation quickly.

That trait can be genuinely useful.

It can also override the agency of the people whose problem he is solving.

Hachi's decision to continue the pregnancy is especially important because Volume 8 does **not** support the claim that Takumi makes that decision for her. Hachi initially thinks about abortion. At the clinic, the ultrasound converts pregnancy from feared possibility into embodied reality for her. She then decides that she does not want to erase the pregnancy as though nothing happened. She considers raising the child alone and only then confronts the economic fact that she has little money and cannot even perform temporary work while severe morning sickness is disabling her.

Takumi's support therefore does not create Hachi's desire to keep the pregnancy.

It changes what that desire can become materially.

The Nobu relationship collapses in the same space between desire and feasibility. Hachi genuinely loves Nobu. Nobu has behaved responsibly about contraception and refuses to evade the possibility that the child could be his. But he is twenty, has little money, and is on the threshold of a musical career. Hachi cannot bring herself to ask him to abandon that future for uncertain paternity. Nobu, meanwhile, sees Takumi already inside 707, already controlling the phone, already discussing recognition of the child. He interprets those facts as evidence that Hachi never really cut Takumi off.

The tragedy is not that Nobu and Hachi do not care.

It is that **the information architecture makes each person's care difficult for the other to see**.

Volume 8 simultaneously makes Nana/Hachi's classification problem more explicit than ever. Nana narrates Hachi's emotional effect on her through the language of **恋**, comparing herself to a boy learning love for the first time, and then immediately tells Ren that the conflict is **not sexual desire**. Later, future Nana admits that she wanted to put a collar on Hachi and keep her tied at her feet, and that she deliberately maintained a little distance because she was frightened of this desire. When the pregnancy crisis exposes the impossibility of owning Hachi's life, Nana asks Yasu how a “normal friend” is supposed to behave and says she wants to become an ordinary friend to Hachi. She finally adopts Yasu's ethic: do not interfere; watch gently; allow Hachi to walk whatever life she chooses.

Then Nana says she thinks she can manage it because she and Hachi are **both women**.

The line does not resolve the relationship category.

It exposes Nana's attempt to resolve it for herself.

Volume 8 therefore provides the strongest evidence yet for the careful formulation:

> **Nana experiences Hachi through explicitly romance-coded language while currently understanding the attachment as nonsexual and trying to contain it within the category of female friendship.**

The volume closes by refusing to let this ethical aspiration become sentimental purity. Future Nana admits that she cannot become the perfectly selfless person who simply says, “as long as you're happy, that's enough.” She wants, instead, to appear strong and resilient in Hachi's eyes—like an impossibly perfect manga hero.

That framing matters because Volume 8 begins and ends with the two Nanas imagining futures for one another.

Hachi opens the volume imagining a house she meant to build for Nana: a house by the sea, with a large garden, a system kitchen, an underground studio, fashionable clothes, and a room Nana could return to again and again whenever men made her cry.

Nana closes the volume imagining the person she wanted Hachi to see when Hachi looked at her.

Between those two future addresses lies the pregnancy crisis.

The volume's deepest question is therefore larger than reproduction:

> **What does it mean to love someone while accepting that love cannot supply all of the material conditions, information, strength, or freedom that the person's life requires?**

# 3. Narrative architecture and causal close read

## 3.1 The volume opens with a house Hachi wanted to build for Nana

Future Hachi begins by apologizing to Nana because she could not keep a promise Nana may not even remember.

The promise was architectural.

Hachi meant to build Nana a large house on high ground overlooking the sea. It would have:

- a large garden;
- a modern system kitchen;
- an underground studio;
- a closet continuously stocked with fashionable clothes;
- and, most importantly, a place Nana could return to whenever a man made her cry.

The fantasy matters because it clarifies Hachi's domestic imagination.

Her desire for a household has never been only heterosexual marriage. Even when Hachi imagines financial adulthood and permanent home ownership, Nana has a designed place inside the structure.

The wording about Nana repeatedly **returning** after relationships fail makes the house something more than a gift. It is a promise of unconditional re-entry:

> **Whatever happens elsewhere, there will be a room where you still belong.**

The future narrator says the promise was not fulfilled.

That is an established future fact.

Why it was not fulfilled remains unknown.

## 3.2 Nana leaves home because singing locally is no longer enough

The volume then returns to Nana's earlier decision to leave her hometown.

She is careful to tell Yasu that she is not going to Tokyo **for Ren**.

Yasu says he understands.

Nana insists he does not understand anything.

What she cannot say is more important than what she does say.

She wants Yasu to come with her.

But she cannot ask because Yasu has chosen his own life and Nobu has a privileged future of his own. Nana's ethic of autonomy appears before the move itself:

> she wants to be chosen voluntarily;

> she does not want to demand that other people abandon their futures for her.

The result is familiar Nana contradiction. She respects autonomy so strongly that she cannot state the dependency she actually feels.

Then, unable to tolerate the restraint, she erupts and effectively tells Yasu that if he feels that way, **he should come**.

Yasu reminds her that she can sing where she is.

Nana's reply defines her ambition:

there is no Dome or Budokan there.

The dream is not merely expression.

It is scale.

## 3.3 Nana explicitly rejects the “pure artist” fantasy

Nana's career manifesto is among the clearest in the first eight volumes.

She wants:

- a huge stage;
- enormous numbers of spotlights;
- enormous applause;
- and more money than she could spend.

Then she rejects the idea that she is an “princess” who would be happy merely singing.

Her key formulation is:

> **song is a means for living.**

This materially revises any reading of Nana as a romantic artist untouched by money.

She wants artistic transcendence and economic success together.

That does not make the art cynical.

It makes music a vocation rather than a fantasy purified from material life.

## 3.4 Nana's twentieth birthday is Hachi's entrance into her life

Nana remembers the date exactly:

**March 5, 2001**, her twentieth birthday.

That is the day she met Hachi.

The precision is structurally revealing. Nana's entry into legal adulthood is narratively synchronized with Hachi's entry into her emotional life.

The relationship is not a decorative subplot added to Nana's Tokyo career.

Nana's adult self-history marks its beginning by Hachi's arrival.

## 3.5 Nana sees Hachi's flaws and still calls her “unsoiled”

Nana's description of Hachi is harshly affectionate.

Hachi is:

- selfish;
- a crybaby;
- dependent;
- extraordinarily romance-oriented;
- changeable;
- already involved with several men within months of arriving in Tokyo.

Then Nana says that despite all of this, Hachi remains somehow **「少しも汚れない」**—not stained, unspoiled.

This is important because Hachi has repeatedly feared that Nana and Nobu idealize her.

Volume 8 shows that Nana is not ignorant of Hachi's flaws.

But Nana does apply a moral-aesthetic exception to them.

She can see behavior Hachi herself experiences as compromising while continuing to experience Hachi as fundamentally uncorrupted.

That is a form of idealization, but not blindness.

## 3.6 Hachi's “pet” function is affective labor

Nana says Hachi is like the band's pet.

The language remains ethically complicated after the earlier dog/collar metaphors.

But Nana also explains what the metaphor means socially.

When Hachi is present and smiling:

- the space brightens;
- the studio becomes more animated;
- the live environment gains energy.

Nana says this can feel more meaningful than adding another technically skilled member.

This directly corrects Hachi's Volume 7 fear that because she can never be a formal member of BLAST, she is unnecessary.

From Nana's point of view, Hachi performs a function no instrumental role can substitute:

> **she changes the emotional atmosphere of the group.**

The labor is affective rather than contractual.

Hachi cannot see it because professionalization makes only formal membership legible to her.

## 3.7 Nana says Hachi's behavior has typhoon-force over her emotions

Nana tells the absent Hachi that Hachi probably does not realize how much power her every action now has over Nana's feelings.

Then comes one of the strongest relationship-classification passages yet:

> Nana says she is like a boy who has learned **恋** for the first time.

Ren immediately interprets the analogy sexually and asks whether Nana wants to sleep with Hachi.

Nana denies it forcefully.

She says the conflict is real, but it is **not sexual desire**.

This pair of statements has to be preserved together.

The text gives us direct romance-coded language and direct nonsexual self-classification in the same scene.

The strongest prospective reading is therefore not:

> Nana is secretly canonically in love with Hachi in a conventional sexual-romantic sense.

Nor is it:

> Nana and Hachi are merely ordinary friends and queer interpretation is projection.

It is:

> **Nana herself experiences Hachi through the grammar of first love while explicitly denying sexual desire as the force she is struggling against.**

The categorical instability exists inside the character.

## 3.8 Ren reunion has drifted into “commuting wife” domesticity

Nana recognizes with irritation that she never intended to restore the old relationship so thoroughly, yet has somehow begun behaving like a **通い妻**, a wife who regularly goes to the man's home.

This is another case of lived relationship outrunning declared intention.

Nana did not formally choose:

> return to old domestic partnership.

Repeated practice has nonetheless begun creating that appearance.

Even during renewed sexual intimacy, she remains emotionally dysregulated enough to wish somebody could “adjust” her.

Ren's presence is real comfort and real desire.

It does not stabilize every part of Nana's attachment system.

## 3.9 Positive pregnancy test / contraceptive pill: reproductive futures become a visual pair

Hachi takes a pregnancy test.

It is positive.

The volume then places future Nana's retrospective narration near Hachi's bodily shock. Future Nana says that living fiercely allowed her to gain many things and lose something irreplaceable, though the exact referents remain unresolved. Her one lingering concern is addressed to Hachi:

> **Are you smiling now?**

The chapter then turns to Nana's contraceptive pill pack.

Nana knows Ren wants children.

She also knows that having a child would not automatically make singing impossible.

Her own explanation is simpler and more vulnerable:

> she probably did not have confidence that she could become a mother.

The two reproductive objects—positive test and contraceptive pills—therefore stage two different forms of female bodily authorship without telling us that one is morally superior.

## 3.10 Hachi initially keeps the pregnancy from Nana in order not to burden her

Misato urges Hachi to tell Nana because Hachi is ill and will soon be alone.

Hachi refuses.

BLAST is approaching an important debut phase. Hachi does not want to create unnecessary anxiety.

This is a notable reversal from the Volume 7 need structure.

Hachi previously wanted desperately to be needed by Nana.

Now, when **Hachi herself** needs support, she hides the need so that Nana can focus on her dream.

That is growth toward caregiving.

It is also a dangerous form of isolation.

The adult Hachi is learning not to make every pain somebody else's obligation, but she has not yet learned that mutuality can include asking for help.

## 3.11 The clinic separates diagnosis from reproductive decision

The medical encounter is structured around choice.

The doctor asks about Hachi's last menstrual period and asks whether, if she is pregnant, she hopes to give birth.

The pregnancy is confirmed at approximately six and a half weeks.

The ultrasound gives Hachi a visual object: a gestational sac and a tiny point she understands as her baby.

The doctor then provides two distinct medical paths:

- if she continues the pregnancy, return for follow-up;
- if she chooses abortion, earlier intervention is physically preferable.

This is important because Hachi's later decision emerges after a medical encounter that makes both continuation and abortion explicit possibilities.

The manga does not present continuation as automatic.

## 3.12 Hachi hides the pregnancy and is injured by Nana's abstract moral certainty

Nana decides to return home because she believes Hachi is ill.

That action is immediate counterevidence to Hachi's belief that Nana no longer needs or prioritizes her.

Yet Hachi continues hiding the pregnancy.

The resulting conversation becomes painfully ironic.

Discussing Shin and irresponsible parents, Nana says that people who cannot raise children properly should not have them.

Hachi, secretly pregnant and already unsure whether she can raise a child, hears the statement as a judgment on herself.

She defends the idea that wanting to give birth and raise one's child is natural.

Nana asks:

> Why is that natural?

Hachi reaches for **母性本能**, maternal instinct.

The word exposes a genuine difference between them.

Hachi assumes maternal desire as intuitive social common sense.

Nana experiences it as something she cannot simply locate inside herself.

## 3.13 Nana asks a doctor whether maternal instinct is universal

Nana later asks her own gynecologist whether everybody normally possesses maternal instinct.

The doctor's answer invokes a biological-essentialist model: women possess the instinct even when dormant, and pregnancy or childbirth may awaken it.

That claim should be classified as **period/character discourse**, not scientific fact supplied by the manga.

What matters for Nana is the question it generates.

She wonders whether, if she became pregnant with Ren's child at this exact moment—when professional debut would make pregnancy deeply inconvenient—she might nevertheless want to give birth for reasons that were not reducible to Ren's desire.

Nana is not “anti-child.”

She is unsure what motherhood would ask her to become.

## 3.14 Nobu's care and abortion time-pressure are placed on the same page

Hachi receives a message from Nobu after rehearsal: he heard she feels ill, and if she is awake he will come see her.

The doctor’s earlier warning about the time-sensitive physical implications of abortion is placed in the same emotional sequence.

The effect is important.

Hachi is not choosing between an abstract “good man” and an abstract “bad man.”

She is choosing while:

- a genuinely caring boyfriend reaches toward her;
- a pregnancy is developing in real time;
- she does not know paternity;
- and she has not told him what is happening.

Romance and reproductive time operate on incompatible clocks.

## 3.15 Hachi expects Nana at the door and receives Takumi

The doorbell rings.

Hachi initially thinks Nana has come home.

The answer is Takumi:

> **“Me.”**

The threshold is one of the volume's most important formal moments.

Hachi's desired/support figure and her former lover are exchanged at the door before she knows what the consequences will be.

Takumi asks her to open it.

Hachi tells him to leave.

This occurs after Hachi explicitly ended their relationship in Volume 7.

The prior boundary therefore matters to the current scene.

Takumi does not treat it as sufficient reason to go.

## 3.16 Takumi's first response to the pregnancy crisis is practical competence

Hachi is vomiting and physically unwell.

Takumi recognizes the seriousness of what he sees.

He asks whether she needs a doctor and helps her physically.

Hachi immediately says she will not give birth, tells him not to feel responsible, says the child may be Nobu's, and asks him again to leave.

Takumi's next behavior gives the volume its key ethical contradiction.

He acts as though the crisis cannot be left in Hachi's hands alone.

That instinct produces real care.

It also produces the first major information violation.

## 3.17 Hachi says not to tell Nobu; Takumi tells Nobu

Takumi searches Hachi's phone contacts.

Hachi explicitly tells him:

> do not tell Nobu.

Takumi calls Nobu anyway, using Hachi's phone.

He discloses:

- that Hachi is pregnant;
- that paternity is uncertain;
- and that the child may be his or Nobu's.

This is not ambiguous.

Hachi set an information boundary.

Takumi overrode it.

The fact that Nobu has a legitimate moral interest in possible paternity does not erase Hachi's autonomy over **how** the disclosure occurs.

Takumi has made the decision himself.

## 3.18 Takumi's offer is materially radical

The same call produces a fact that cannot be dismissed as mere manipulation.

Takumi says that if Hachi wants to give birth, he is willing to legally acknowledge and support the child **regardless of which man is biologically the father**.

This is a substantial offer.

At the current point:

- Hachi has unstable temporary employment;
- Nobu says he does not have the money to support a child;
- BLAST's career is uncertain;
- and Hachi has severe morning sickness.

Takumi can supply an actual material structure.

The ethical problem is the route by which he introduces it:

> a male-to-male negotiation conducted through Hachi's phone after she explicitly asked him not to disclose the pregnancy.

Care and control have become one event.

## 3.19 Future Nana corrects her own autonomy ideology

The volume then inserts one of the most important retrospective Nana passages so far.

Future Nana tells Hachi that she still believes one's life depends upon oneself.

The belief has not been abandoned.

What changed is Nana's estimate of human strength.

She has learned that nobody can always become strong enough to carry everything alone.

She says that accepting this made her gentler than she was at the time of the events.

This is not a repudiation of autonomy.

It is a maturation of it:

> **self-authorship without compassion for weakness can become another form of cruelty.**

## 3.20 “Trapnest” becomes narrator-language for entrapment

The narration glosses Trapnest as a nest with a trap: once inside, escape by one's own power becomes difficult.

The narrator then associates the name with a man who has a strong desire to control.

This should not be promoted into omniscient proof of Takumi's inner motive.

It is nevertheless an unusually explicit interpretive frame supplied by the narrative voice itself.

The band name becomes available as relationship metaphor at precisely the point when Hachi's crisis is becoming materially dependent on Takumi's resources.

## 3.21 Nana chooses Hachi over visiting Ren when she thinks Hachi is ill

Nana messages Ren that Hachi is unwell and says she cannot come see him for a while.

This matters because Hachi's Volume 7 fear was:

> Nana no longer needs me.

Volume 8 repeatedly supplies the opposite evidence.

When Nana believes Hachi needs care, she rearranges her own romantic life.

Hachi's problem is not that Nana does not care.

It is that Hachi has concealed the actual thing Nana would need to understand.

## 3.22 Reira and Shin formulate trust against destiny

Reira struggles with lyrics because she fears her love-story writing will be thin and damage a good song.

Shin answers that someone capable of crying with that depth is unlikely to produce emotionally empty lyrics.

Later Reira writes images of joined hands, red thread, and a vow-ring, then explicitly rejects destiny as her governing philosophy.

People, she says, are connected through **trust**, whatever their relationship category.

The placement is crucial.

Hachi and Nobu's relationship is simultaneously collapsing not because love is absent, but because trust cannot survive an information environment neither of them fully controls.

The manga's “red thread versus trust” contrast therefore operates as commentary on the main romantic crisis.

## 3.23 Takumi forces the paternity crisis into a binary decision

Nobu initially cannot respond.

Takumi pushes him:

> take responsibility, or withdraw.

He emphasizes that the fetus continues developing while they hesitate.

The argument has real material force. Reproductive time does not wait for emotional readiness.

But Takumi again converts complexity into a decision structure he controls.

There are more possibilities than his binary suggests:

- further paternity clarification later;
- Hachi making a decision before either man's permanent role is fixed;
- Nobu participating without immediate total life commitment;
- legal and familial arrangements not yet discussed.

Takumi's style is to collapse ambiguity into action.

That can be effective.

It can also define the field before other people have finished thinking.

## 3.24 Nana demands Hachi's voice; Takumi remains the gatekeeper

Nana learns that Takumi is at 707 and using Hachi's phone.

She becomes furious.

She insists Hachi is currently Nobu's girlfriend and demands to speak directly to her.

Takumi does not immediately transfer the phone.

He says Hachi is unable to talk.

Then, in almost comic tonal contrast, he asks Nana to bring grapefruit and carbonated water because Hachi's morning sickness is severe.

Again:

> the same man is controlling communication and caring competently for Hachi's body.

The scene resists purification in either direction.

## 3.25 Nobu's contraception is material responsibility, not prudishness

Nana asks Nobu whether he used condoms with Hachi.

Nobu says he did every time, from before penetration.

He becomes angry at the implication that this makes him overly serious and explains why:

> if a child were conceived, he has no money to support one.

This materially deepens Nobu's characterization.

His contraception is part of his ethics of responsibility.

It also means the characters perceive Takumi's biological paternity as more likely.

But paternity remains unresolved.

Consistent condom use reduces probability; it does not establish certainty.

## 3.26 Nobu refuses easy responsibility-evasion

Nana begins to reason that if Takumi is probably the father, Hachi would abort anyway and Nobu need not bear responsibility.

Nobu rejects the move.

He says that because he had sex with Hachi, the possibility that the child is his is not zero.

This is an ethically significant refusal to hide behind statistical convenience.

Nobu is materially underprepared.

He is not morally unserious.

## 3.27 Takumi's presence creates a false story about Hachi's choice

Nobu and Nana reason from what they can see:

- Hachi is pregnant;
- Takumi is in her apartment;
- Takumi knows the pregnancy;
- Takumi is speaking for her;
- Hachi has not told Nobu directly.

They infer that Hachi must have gone to Takumi because she knows or believes the child is his and chose him as the person to consult.

But the reader knows that Hachi did not invite Takumi as her reproductive adviser.

He arrived after a prior breakup boundary and disclosed the pregnancy despite Hachi's instructions.

This is one of Volume 8's clearest tragedies of information distribution:

> **the facts visible to Nobu create a story that is reasonable and wrong.**

## 3.28 Nana accuses Takumi of using the child to possess Hachi; Nobu turns the accusation back on Nana

Nana says Takumi's willingness to recognize the child is a tactic to monopolize Hachi—to use the child to make Hachi his.

This is **Nana's belief**, not established Takumi motive.

Nobu immediately exposes why Nana's certainty is psychologically dangerous.

He tells Nana that she is doing something similar:

> trying to use Nobu in order to keep Hachi as her own.

The accusation directly attacks Nana's Volume 7 “my yard” strategy.

Nobu then delivers the cruelest practical challenge:

> if Nana cares that much, she should become the father herself.

The line forces Nana's possessive love against the material realities of parenthood.

Wanting Hachi is not the same as being able to occupy every role Hachi's life requires.

## 3.29 Nana cannot imagine loving Takumi's child

When Nobu tells Nana that even if she tried to become the responsible parent figure, he cannot imagine Nana lovingly caring for Takumi's child, Nana answers plainly:

> she could not.

Then she cries that she does not want Hachi to give birth.

This is one of Nana's least purified moments.

Her reaction mixes:

- fear for Hachi;
- hatred of Takumi;
- jealousy;
- reproductive panic;
- and the impossibility of separating the child from the man she sees as taking Hachi away.

The analysis should not convert this into Nana's final ethical view of children.

It is a crisis response.

## 3.30 Takumi's pregnancy knowledge comes from family experience

While Nana and Nobu are in crisis, Takumi remains with Hachi.

He knows enough about severe morning sickness and pregnancy complications to respond practically, in part because he remembers his older sister's pregnancy.

He brings this knowledge into the room without romantic idealization.

He also teases Hachi harshly about her unstable work, loneliness-driven sex, and pregnancy.

The language can be insulting.

Yet his central stabilizing statement is significant:

> whatever the father's identity, **Hachi is definitely the mother**.

This moves the conversation away from male competition and back toward Hachi's actual embodied position.

For a Hachi drowning in paternity uncertainty, this is both harsh and grounding.

## 3.31 Takumi says the choice is Hachi's

When Nobu finally arrives, Takumi says:

> **Hachi chooses.**

There is no need for two fathers.

This sentence is important counterevidence against a simplistic model in which Takumi openly believes he has the right to make the reproductive decision for Hachi.

He does not say that.

The more precise problem is:

> he verbally locates ultimate choice in Hachi while repeatedly controlling the informational and practical conditions surrounding the choice.

That is a subtler form of power.

## 3.32 Nobu asks to hear Hachi's truth from Hachi

Nobu asks whether the pregnancy is real.

He says he wants to hear it from **her mouth**.

He reminds Hachi that he knows she had only recently cut off Takumi and that the child could be his.

Hachi apologizes.

Nobu asks why she is apologizing.

The first response is compassion rather than condemnation.

Then the visible situation overwhelms him.

He asks whether the relationship with Takumi had never really ended.

That conclusion is understandable from his perspective and wrong from the reader's fuller perspective.

## 3.33 Nobu begs for an explanation; Hachi can only apologize

Nobu eventually says, in effect:

> explain it;

> even if it is a lie;

> he will believe everything.

This is not a rational demand for forensic proof.

It is a plea for evidence that Hachi still chooses **him** strongly enough to author an explanation.

Hachi cannot do it.

Her repeated apology becomes communicatively catastrophic.

To Hachi, apology means:

> I have hurt you and I do not know how to ask you to sacrifice your life for this crisis.

To Nobu, silence appears to mean:

> there is no counter-story because Takumi really is the person she chose.

The breakup is therefore produced not by absence of love but by incompatible interpretations of the same silence.

## 3.34 Future Nana admits the collar fantasy was stronger than she let herself know

After the Hachi/Nobu rupture, future Nana addresses Hachi again.

The confession is much stronger than Volume 7's pet/yard language.

Nana says that even if she put a **collar** on Hachi, she wanted to keep Hachi tied at her own feet.

She says she was frightened by this part of herself and deliberately maintained a little distance.

This retrospectively refines several earlier behaviors:

- Nana's teasing “pet” metaphor;
- the “my yard” image;
- her support for Hachi/Nobu;
- her tendency to withdraw instead of directly asking Hachi to stay.

The ethical significance lies in the distance.

Nana knows enough to fear turning desire into control.

She does not know how to stop wanting control internally.

Future Nana adds that she still does not make friends well and remains a little afraid.

This is a long-term character fact without a disclosed causal endpoint.

## 3.35 Ren is not the person Nana wants beside her in this crisis

A quiet transitional page shows Black Stones cigarettes while Nana narrates that she no longer has the emotional capacity to use small excuses about beds or forgotten wallets.

When she is unbearably lonely, she says, the person she wants beside her is **not Ren**.

The immediate subsequent refuge is Yasu.

The safest reading is therefore not “she means Hachi.”

It is:

> **Ren is not Nana's sole attachment regulator; when crisis exceeds what romantic reunion can hold, Nana turns elsewhere.**

This strengthens the distributed-attachment model already established around Ren, Hachi, and Yasu.

## 3.36 Both Nanas seek older, steadier witnesses when 707 cannot contain the crisis

Chapter 28 creates a striking parallel.

Hachi, barely able to breathe emotionally, ends up at Junko and Kyosuke's place and pours out the story.

Nana goes to Yasu.

The protagonists do not first solve the crisis by confronting each other.

Each seeks a more stable relational witness:

- Hachi → Junko/Kyosuke;
- Nana → Yasu.

This is important for the home ledger.

707 remains symbolically central.

But it cannot currently perform every function the two women need.

## 3.37 Nana asks how “normal friends” behave

With Yasu, Nana finally articulates the classification problem directly.

She cannot fully explain why she wants Hachi so much or why she hates Takumi so much.

She tells Yasu that Nobu said something is wrong with her.

Then she asks:

> **What do ordinary friends do at a time like this?**

And:

> **I want to become ordinary friends with Hachi.**

This is revealing because Nana does not say:

> I discovered my feelings are actually ordinary friendship.

She says she wants to make the relationship **behave** like ordinary friendship.

The desired category is normative before it is descriptive.

## 3.38 Junko and Kyosuke split Takumi into two plausible readings

Hachi's crisis narration receives an external stress test.

Kyosuke hears that Takumi is willing to acknowledge and support the child despite paternity uncertainty and initially sees impressive decisiveness.

Junko sees a self-centered, intimidating man.

She is especially disturbed by how he used Hachi's phone and disclosed the pregnancy to Nobu.

This disagreement is analytically useful because both readings are supported by real evidence.

Takumi's decisiveness **does** solve problems.

The same decisiveness **does** override process.

The text itself contains observers who weight the same conduct differently.

## 3.39 Pregnancy becomes explicitly economic at Junko's table

Junko and Kyosuke force the conversation away from romance.

What does legal recognition mean?

Can Takumi afford support?

What is his job?

Would recognition merely mean child support without marriage?

Hachi initially says that would be enough.

She does not expect Takumi to marry her.

This is important because Hachi's later reaction to the marriage proposal should not be treated as a plan she entered the conversation already demanding.

Her immediate objective is:

> make continuing the pregnancy materially possible.

## 3.40 Hachi's decision to continue the pregnancy predates Takumi's offer

Hachi reconstructs her decision carefully.

At first she wanted the pregnancy test to be wrong.

Once it was positive, she thought she should have an abortion quickly and secretly restore ordinary life.

Then the clinic changed the experience.

Seeing the ultrasound made the pregnancy feel concretely like **her child**.

She decided she did not want to abort and pretend nothing had happened.

She felt that she was the only person who could protect the life in her body.

She considered giving birth and raising the child alone.

Only then did material reality overwhelm the idea:

- little money;
- unstable work;
- severe nausea;
- inability to keep earning.

Takumi's offer therefore arrives **after** Hachi's desire to continue the pregnancy has formed.

This is decisive evidence against the claim that Takumi persuades or forces her into motherhood in Volume 8.

## 3.41 Takumi's support is welcome because it makes an already desired choice feasible

Hachi tells Junko that Takumi's offer surprised her and made her happy.

She worries that relying upon it may be wrong.

But it changes the decision from:

> an emotionally desired pregnancy with no viable support structure

into:

> a pregnancy with a powerful adult willing to recognize and finance the child.

This is where material security becomes morally ambiguous.

Money is not corruption in itself.

But the person who can supply the money gains leverage precisely because the money solves a real problem.

## 3.42 Hachi experiences keeping the child as betrayal of Nobu's care

Hachi says Nobu always used contraception and treated her as precious.

Now she believes she is trampling all of that care by depending on Takumi and giving birth.

This is not evidence that Hachi no longer loves Nobu.

The opposite is closer to the text.

Her guilt is so intense because Nobu's goodness remains emotionally real.

She is choosing an outcome that effectively destroys a relationship she values.

## 3.43 Junko/Kyosuke explain why Hachi could not ask Nobu to become the father

Kyosuke says Nobu wanted Hachi to show enough love to explain herself.

Junko introduces the material problem Nobu himself already recognized:

> love alone cannot raise a child.

If Nobu took responsibility for uncertain paternity, he might have to:

- abandon or delay BLAST;
- give up the professional future he is approaching;
- return to the family inn;
- reorganize his life at twenty.

Junko's reconstruction suggests Hachi's silence contains a form of protection:

> she does not believe she has the right to ask Nobu for that sacrifice.

This does not make the silence harmless.

It makes it morally intelligible.

## 3.44 Hachi chooses the child at the cost of her own “ordinary bride” dream

Junko and Kyosuke later note the irony.

Hachi's long-standing dream was an ordinary marriage.

If she had aborted and stayed with Nobu, that dream might plausibly have become much easier to realize.

Instead she decides to continue the pregnancy even though she currently expects Takumi's support without marriage and understands that the arrangement may resemble being a mistress rather than building the conventional family she wanted.

This decisively complicates the claim:

> Hachi always chooses conventional security over authentic love.

In Volume 8, Hachi sacrifices the conventional romantic future she wanted in order to keep the pregnancy.

## 3.45 Junko makes the dependency cost explicit

Junko asks whether Hachi understands what it means to raise a child on the support of a man who will not marry her.

She says this could destroy the possibility of ordinary family life and bind Hachi to his influence indefinitely.

Her most severe warning is that if Hachi accepts the arrangement knowingly, she may be accepting a life in which Takumi can continue to control or manipulate her.

This is **Junko's warning**, not guaranteed future fact.

It is materially grounded enough to matter.

The pregnancy creates a durable legal/economic tie even if romance does not.

## 3.46 Hachi chooses continuation despite the cost

Junko asks whether Hachi can accept that Nobu may never forgive her and that even if the child later proved biologically Nobu's, Takumi might still be the legal/social father.

Hachi says yes.

Junko then tells her that if she truly wants to give birth with that level of resolve, Junko will not oppose her.

She tells Hachi to remember the decision for life.

This is one of the strongest agency moments in the volume.

The choice is not cheerful.

It is made after external challenge.

## 3.47 Takumi is already back inside 707 when Hachi returns

Hachi returns to 707 after speaking with Junko and Kyosuke.

Takumi is there with groceries and grapefruit juice.

He asks where she went and says he was worried.

Hachi had expected him to have left.

The domestic image is almost deliberately ordinary:

- groceries;
- juice;
- table;
- concern;
- work schedule.

That ordinariness is important.

Takumi's power over Hachi is not produced only through spectacle, sex, or celebrity.

He can also occupy the role of competent adult in an ordinary room.

## 3.48 Nana chooses Yasu's non-interference as a friendship ethic

Nana eventually tells Yasu what she wants to do.

Whatever life Hachi chooses, and whoever she chooses it with, Nana wants to stop interfering and simply watch over her kindly—like Yasu does for Nana.

This is Nana's clearest ethical aspiration so far.

It does not erase her earlier collar fantasy.

It is an answer to it.

Yasu warns that the practice is much harder than the sentence.

Nana thanks him and says she will manage.

Then comes the categorical stabilizer:

> **because she and Hachi are both women.**

The line can be read as Nana trying to place an attachment that has felt like **恋** into a socially intelligible form where non-interference seems possible.

It strengthens queer analysis precisely because it should not be overresolved.

## 3.49 Hachi explicitly chooses to give birth and raise the child as Takumi's

At the end of the volume, Takumi asks Hachi for the decision because he is about to begin an intensive recording period and cannot work while the crisis remains unresolved.

Hachi answers:

> she wants to give birth and raise the child as **Takumi's child**, if that is not a burden to him.

The distinction is essential:

This is a **social/legal-family decision**.

It is not a biological-paternity finding.

Biological paternity remains unresolved at the Volume 8 boundary.

## 3.50 Takumi proposes marriage as public/legal management

Takumi's reply is:

> **Let's get married.**

His first explanation is not a confession of romantic love.

It is practical and reputational.

A hidden child would be damaging if exposed. Ordinary marriage to a non-celebrity is much easier to manage publicly and would attract less destructive press.

This is archetypal Takumi reasoning:

> convert an emotionally chaotic problem into a manageable structure.

Marriage becomes:

- legal order;
- public image protection;
- child legitimacy;
- administrative simplification;
- material security.

None of those goods is fake.

Their presence does not tell us what Takumi feels romantically.

Hachi reacts with shock and tears.

Volume 8 ends before she gives an explicit verbal acceptance.

Therefore:

> **proposal = established; acceptance/engagement = not yet established at this spoiler boundary.**

## 3.51 Future Nana refuses the fantasy of perfectly selfless love

The last page returns to Nana's future address to Hachi.

Nana repeats the aspiration:

> whatever life Hachi walks, if Hachi is happy, that should be enough.

Then she admits she cannot become such a perfectly accomplished person from the bottom of her heart.

Instead she wanted the Nana reflected in Hachi's eyes to be:

> **strong and supple/resilient**—like an implausibly perfect manga hero.

This closes the volume on performance rather than purity.

Nana's ethics are not:

> I no longer feel jealousy.

They are:

> **I want to behave in a way that protects Hachi from the worst part of my jealousy.**

That may be the most mature form of love *NANA* has articulated so far.

# 4. Nana Osaki — character-state update

## 4.1 Ambition becomes explicitly material

Nana wants money, scale, stage, audience, and professional success.

Her formulation that singing is a **means of living** should permanently prevent the analysis from treating her artistry as economically disinterested.

She is not corrupted when money becomes part of music.

Money was always part of the dream.

## 4.2 Nana's autonomy ethic contains a hidden demand to be voluntarily chosen

Her difficulty asking Yasu to accompany her clarifies a long-standing defense.

Nana does not want to compel another person to abandon a life for her.

But she desperately wants the person to volunteer.

This means autonomy can become a test:

> **If I do not ask and you still choose me, then I know I matter.**

The structure is emotionally dangerous because other people cannot always answer needs Nana refuses to state.

## 4.3 Hachi is integrated into Nana's adult self-history

Nana remembers meeting Hachi on her twentieth birthday.

This gives Hachi unusual autobiographical weight.

Her adult Tokyo life begins with two events at once:

- professional movement;
- Hachi.

## 4.4 Nana's Hachi idealization is selective, not naïve

Nana knows Hachi is selfish, dependent, romance-driven and changeable.

She nevertheless calls Hachi fundamentally unspoiled.

The distinction matters because Hachi's fear—“Nana loves a fake pure Hachi”—is partly right and partly wrong.

Nana knows many flaws.

What she idealizes is Hachi's **moral core**.

## 4.5 The pet metaphor gains functional meaning and ethical danger simultaneously

Nana says Hachi's smiling presence animates BLAST more than a technically skilled new member might.

That validates Hachi's affective importance.

But the same language belongs to the cumulative dog/pet/collar system.

Nana's tenderness and her wish to possess cannot be cleanly separated.

## 4.6 “First love” is explicit; sexual desire is explicitly denied

The relationship dossier must now record both statements at high confidence.

Nana compares herself to a boy newly learning **恋**.

She denies that the struggle is **性欲**.

This is stronger and more precise than generic “queer subtext.”

Nana herself lacks an ordinary category for the attachment.

## 4.7 Ren is not enough to regulate Nana's loneliness

Renewed domestic/sexual intimacy with Ren does not eliminate Nana's need for Yasu or Hachi.

The crisis sends her to Yasu rather than toward deeper fusion with Ren.

The distributed-attachment model is decisively strengthened.

## 4.8 Nana's reproductive position is uncertainty, not rejection

Nana uses contraception and is afraid she lacks confidence to become a mother.

She also actively wonders whether pregnancy could awaken a desire to give birth even during career crisis.

The correct current model is:

> career commitment + reproductive self-control + maternal uncertainty.

## 4.9 Nana's abstract harshness toward weak parents is later corrected by her future self

Nana tells Misato that people should be able to find their own reason to exist and says irresponsible people should not give birth.

The future narrator later says she still believes in self-authorship but has learned that people cannot always be that strong.

This is a genuine internal philosophical development.

## 4.10 The collar confession reclassifies earlier distance

Future Nana says she deliberately kept a little distance because she feared how strongly she wanted to keep Hachi tied to her.

That means Nana's withdrawal should not always be read as low attachment.

Sometimes distance is the defense against **too much** attachment.

## 4.11 “Ordinary friendship” is an ethical project

Nana asks Yasu how normal friends behave.

She wants to become a normal friend to Hachi.

The phrase is aspirational.

It implies that Nana knows her existing attachment does not feel normal to her.

## 4.12 Nana chooses Yasu as a model of loving without interference

This is one of the most important Yasu effects on Nana.

He does not teach her not to care.

He models how to care without making his own need the other person's obligation.

Nana's final future narration reveals that she never becomes perfectly free of possessive feeling.

The ethical achievement is therefore behavioral restraint rather than emotional purification.

# 5. Komatsu Nana / Hachi — character-state update

## 5.1 Hachi's home fantasy has always contained Nana

The imagined seaside house is crucial.

Hachi's domestic vocation is not reducible to “find husband, become wife.”

Her imagined adult home contains a permanent place for Nana's repeated return.

That places Nana inside Hachi's deepest architecture of permanence.

## 5.2 Hachi moves from wanting to be needed toward refusing to burden Nana

This is genuine development from Volume 7.

Hachi hides the pregnancy partly because she does not want to interfere with BLAST's debut.

But growth contains a new danger:

> **care becomes concealment.**

The adult task will require learning when asking for help is not selfishness.

## 5.3 Pregnancy initially produces avoidance, not immediate maternal certainty

Hachi first wants the test to be wrong.

Then she thinks about a fast abortion and returning to ordinary life.

This is important evidence against any reading that treats motherhood as automatic destiny.

## 5.4 The ultrasound changes Hachi's moral perception

The medical image does not provide an abstract philosophical argument.

It makes pregnancy embodied for Hachi.

Her language changes from:

> a frightening condition

to:

> **my child.**

That subjective shift becomes the basis of her decision.

It should be described as Hachi's moral experience, not universalized into a rule about what pregnancy means for every woman.

## 5.5 Hachi's choice precedes Takumi's support

This is perhaps the most important agency finding in Volume 8.

She decides she does not want abortion before Takumi offers recognition/support.

Takumi changes feasibility, not desire.

## 5.6 Hachi seriously considers single motherhood

She asks whether she can give birth and raise the child alone.

The answer becomes material rather than moral:

- she lacks money;
- temporary work is precarious;
- morning sickness already prevents working.

This gives Hachi's decision unusual adult realism.

## 5.7 Hachi does not “choose Takumi because she loves him more than Nobu”

Volume 8 strongly rejects that simplification.

Hachi's love for Nobu remains explicit in her guilt and in Junko/Kyosuke's reading.

What she chooses first is the pregnancy.

Then she chooses the support structure she believes can sustain it.

That support structure is Takumi.

## 5.8 Hachi's silence toward Nobu is ethically damaging but not empty

Nobu needs her to explain.

Hachi cannot.

The silence hurts him badly and contributes directly to rupture.

But the silence also contains a belief that she cannot ask Nobu to reorganize his entire young life around uncertain paternity.

The correct judgment is mixed:

> protective intention + communicative failure.

## 5.9 Hachi sacrifices the exact ordinary-marriage future she wanted

This is a major cumulative revision.

Hachi has spent years imagining happiness through becoming an ordinary bride.

At the Volume 8 decision point, continuing the pregnancy appears likely to destroy the Nobu future that most closely resembles that dream.

She chooses the child anyway.

That is not evidence of perfect maturity.

It is evidence that Hachi can choose something she values more than the romantic fantasy she has spent the series pursuing.

## 5.10 Hachi accepts a durable dependency knowingly

Junko explicitly warns that Takumi's support may bind Hachi to him and compromise ordinary family life.

Hachi still chooses continuation.

This means later analysis must not describe all material dependence as though Hachi entered it without understanding that dependence creates power.

She understands at least part of the cost here.

## 5.11 Hachi's Volume 8 reproductive decision is explicit; biological paternity is not

Her final statement is precise:

> give birth and raise the child **as Takumi's**.

That is an authored social decision.

It is not evidence that she knows Takumi is biologically the father.

# 6. Other materially affected characters

## Nobu — responsibility without resources

Volume 8 significantly strengthens Nobu.

He reports consistent condom use because he knows he cannot afford to raise a child. When the pregnancy appears anyway, he refuses to use that precaution as a moral escape hatch. He says the possibility the child is his is not zero.

He also wants the truth directly from Hachi rather than from Takumi.

His failure is not lack of care.

It is that hurt converts incomplete evidence into the belief that Hachi never truly ended Takumi.

His plea that he would believe even a lie shows how desperately he wants Hachi to demonstrate preference for him.

## Takumi — competence as power

Takumi is the volume's most difficult ethical character because the same abilities can be read as virtues and risks.

He is:

- decisive;
- materially capable;
- observant about pregnancy;
- willing to assume social/legal responsibility;
- good at converting uncertainty into plans.

He is also:

- willing to override Hachi's disclosure boundary;
- willing to speak for her;
- comfortable entering the domestic space after a breakup;
- inclined to force binary decisions;
- and able to make his solution become the default because his resources are vastly greater.

The correct Volume 8 model is neither demonization nor exoneration.

It is **power through competence**.

## Yasu — the model Nana consciously chooses

Yasu's defining action is listening.

Nana tells him things she finds ugly in herself.

He does not convert listening into ownership or demand.

Nana explicitly decides she wants to treat Hachi the way Yasu treats Nana.

This makes Yasu's relationship ethic a direct causal influence on Nana's development.

## Ren — autonomy teacher, incomplete refuge

Nana uses Ren's earlier autonomy principle when speaking about Shin.

Future Nana later softens that principle through recognition of weakness.

Ren also remains Nana's lover without becoming the person she wants beside her in every crisis.

## Junko — adversarial friendship

Junko refuses to let Hachi use sympathy as acquittal.

She interrogates:

- money;
- marriage;
- legal recognition;
- future dependency;
- Nobu;
- responsibility for unprotected sex;
- and the durability of Hachi's decision.

Then she supports Hachi once Hachi has answered.

That is one of the strongest models of friendship in the volume:

> challenge before endorsement; support after agency is demonstrated.

## Kyosuke — material realism without moral panic

Kyosuke is more willing than Junko to recognize Takumi's willingness to assume responsibility as admirable.

He also interprets Nobu's emotional need accurately: Nobu wanted Hachi to show enough desire for him to explain.

His perspective prevents Junko's harsher reading from becoming the text's only external verdict.

## Shin and Reira — trust as an alternative to fate

Their lyric exchange becomes a thematic counterpoint rather than the volume's principal plot.

Reira rejects red-thread destiny and says relationships are connected through trust.

The line lands beside Hachi/Nobu's collapse and therefore gives the volume a clean alternative to fairy-tale certainty:

> **connection has to be maintained informationally, not merely believed cosmically.**

**Volume 8 state:** explicit romance-coded self-description; explicit denial of sexual desire; future admission of collar/tether fantasy; deliberate distance as self-restraint; conscious attempt to convert attachment into non-interfering friendship.

Most important new evidence:

**Current formulation:**

## Hachi / Takumi

**Previous state:** Hachi explicitly ended relationship; Takumi had apparently attempted further contact.

## Nana / Ren

Renewed romance remains genuine.

## Nana / Yasu

The relationship becomes explicitly exemplary.

## Hachi / Junko

The friendship matures into a deliberative institution.

Hachi goes to Junko not merely to be comforted but to have her decision challenged.

## Shin / Reira

Their bond continues to mix sexual/romantic intimacy with the prior ethical age/power concern, while their current thematic function centers on trust, vulnerability, and artistic expression.

# 8. Japanese voice, address, and translation-sensitive language

## 8.1 生きる為の手段

Nana's formulation that song is **「生きる為の手段」** is stronger than “music is important to me.”

**手段** means means/instrument/method.

The line strips away the romantic fantasy that a true artist should be indifferent to money and practical survival.

## 8.2 恋 versus 性欲

This is the volume's most important relationship-language pair.

Nana uses **恋** to describe what Hachi does to her emotionally.

She rejects **性欲** as the explanation.

Any translation or analysis that preserves one and ignores the other distorts the scene.

## 8.3 汚れない

Nana's **汚れない** description of Hachi carries moral/aesthetic force.

Hachi can behave in ways she experiences as shameful while Nana continues to see an uncorrupted core.

This is important for the idealization dossier.

## 8.4 ペット / 首輪 / 繋ぐ / 足元 / 距離

The cumulative pet lexicon becomes much darker and more explicit.

The chain now includes:

- pet;
- dog;
- yard;
- collar;
- tethering;
- keeping at one's feet;
- maintaining deliberate distance.

This is no longer an incidental nickname joke.

It is a coherent vocabulary through which Nana thinks about attachment and possession.

## 8.5 母性本能

**母性本能** becomes an explicit contested concept.

Hachi invokes it as ordinary common sense.

Nana does not intuitively recognize it.

A doctor supplies a period-essentialist explanation.

The manga presents multiple social understandings rather than proving a biological proposition.

## 8.6 認知

**認知** in the pregnancy context is legal/social recognition of paternity.

It is not identical to biological proof, romantic love, marriage, or cohabitation.

This distinction is essential to the Takumi offer.

## 8.7 責任

“Responsibility” appears in competing forms:

- Nobu's contraception;
- Nobu refusing to declare himself irrelevant;
- Takumi's recognition offer;
- Hachi's wish to protect the child;
- Junko's demand that Hachi remember the decision.

The volume refuses one single definition.

## 8.8 自分次第 / 強い / 優しい

The future-Nana correction links autonomy to compassion.

**自分次第** survives.

What changes is the model of strength.

Recognizing that people cannot always be strong makes Nana **優しい**, gentler.

## 8.9 信頼 versus 運命

Reira's rejection of destiny in favor of trust is one of the volume's clearest conceptual contrasts.

**赤い糸** and the vow-ring are romantic symbols.

**信頼** is relational practice.

## 8.10 弁解 / 信じる

Nobu's plea for Hachi to **explain** and his willingness to **believe** exposes the crisis as epistemic.

He does not merely need affection.

He needs a story from Hachi that lets him interpret the visible facts differently.

## 8.11 覚悟

Junko repeatedly moves Hachi toward **覚悟**—resolve/readiness to bear the consequence of a decision.

This is not the same as certainty.

Hachi can have resolve while still grieving.

## 8.12 干渉 / 見守る

Nana's new friendship ethic is explicitly behavioral:

- do not interfere;
- watch over kindly.

These verbs matter more than any claim that she has stopped feeling possessive.

## 8.13 女同士

Nana's **女同士だから** is categorically important.

She uses shared gender as a reason she believes she can manage ordinary friendship boundaries with Hachi.

The line should be read as Nana's self-classification effort, not an external final verdict about the relationship.

## 8.14 強くしなやか

Future Nana wants to appear **strong and supple/resilient** in Hachi's eyes.

This is different from hard, invulnerable strength.

**しなやか** implies the capacity to bend without breaking.

It is an especially fitting endpoint after future Nana's admission that human beings cannot always be simply strong.

Established:

# 10. Visual and formal analysis

## 10.1 The imagined house by the sea frames pregnancy with domestic futurity

Volume 8 does not begin with the pregnancy test.

It begins with architecture.

The sea, high ground, garden, kitchen, studio, closet, and return-room establish the question:

> **What kind of life can hold the people we love?**

Pregnancy later forces Hachi to answer the same question materially for a child.

## 10.2 Pregnancy test and contraceptive pills form a reproductive diptych

Hachi's positive test and Nana's pill pack are near-opposite objects:

- pregnancy discovered;
- pregnancy actively prevented.

The juxtaposition prevents one woman's reproductive position from becoming the universal female position.

## 10.3 The ultrasound makes scale morally powerful

The image is tiny.

That smallness is the point.

A black circle and tiny point become enough for Hachi to experience the pregnancy differently.

Visual perception precedes the verbal decision.

## 10.4 The door replaces Nana with Takumi

Hachi expects Nana.

Takumi is outside.

The door is therefore not only physical access.

It is relational substitution.

The person Hachi has concealed the pregnancy from is absent; the person she explicitly broke with enters.

## 10.5 The phone becomes an instrument of power

Takumi uses Hachi's device to locate Nobu and disclose the pregnancy.

The phone is not neutral communication technology.

It determines:

- who knows;
- who speaks first;
- who controls framing;
- whose voice reaches whom.

Mobile-phone culture is therefore part of the ethical architecture.

## 10.6 Nobu's message and the abortion warning collide

Care and medical time-pressure occupy the same sequence.

The page refuses to let romantic feeling suspend bodily chronology.

## 10.7 Reira's red thread appears after trust has already failed elsewhere

The romantic iconography of joined hands/red thread/ring is immediately revised by Reira's statement that she believes in trust rather than fate.

Placed beside Hachi/Nobu rupture, the motif functions as a formal counterargument to romantic destiny.

## 10.8 The collar confession uses black space

Future Nana's admission that she wanted Hachi tethered appears against large dark fields and isolated domestic imagery.

The visual emptiness makes the fantasy feel less comic than the old “Hachikō” nickname.

The pet metaphor has lost innocence.

## 10.9 The empty 707 becomes an evidence archive

Photos, cigarette packs, bed space, and the absence of people carry information after speech fails.

The apartment increasingly stores the residue of relationships rather than guaranteeing their repair.

## 10.10 Two tables produce two kinds of adult decision

At Junko/Kyosuke's table, Hachi's pregnancy is discussed through:

- money;
- work;
- legal recognition;
- marriage;
- Nobu's future;
- responsibility.

At the final table with Takumi, the decision becomes administrative:

- child as Takumi's;
- recording schedule;
- marriage;
- media consequences.

Neither table resembles fairy-tale romance.

That is precisely why the final proposal is so revealing.

## 10.11 The volume closes in open landscape rather than enclosed domestic space

Future Nana is shown moving through a wide landscape with a dog while describing the person she wanted to be in Hachi's eyes.

After a volume dominated by rooms, beds, doors, phones, and constrained choices, the final space opens outward.

The image stages the freedom Nana wants to grant Hachi and cannot emotionally inhabit perfectly.

## Hachi's ideal home

The opening fantasy defines home as:

It combines Hachi's domestic vocation with Nana's music/fashion identity rather than subordinating Nana to household femininity.

## 707 as sickroom

Pregnancy turns 707 into a place where bodily vulnerability becomes visible.

## 707 as contested access

Takumi's presence after breakup makes home-boundary questions concrete.

Who may enter?

Who gets to stay?

Who uses whose phone?

Who gets to speak for the resident?

## 707 as evidence archive

After Hachi/Nobu rupture, the apartment's objects retain relationship traces.

Home stores information people cannot say aloud.

## Junko/Kyosuke as alternative domestic refuge

Hachi seeks a different household when 707 becomes unbearable.

That home offers deliberation rather than romance.

## Yasu as non-spatial home function

Nana's refuge is relational more than architectural.

Yasu provides a safe place for morally ugly feelings without turning them into claims.

# 12. Material and economic adulthood

Pregnancy makes Volume 8 the most materially adult volume so far.

## Hachi's employment is structurally fragile

Temporary work does not provide enough security for pregnancy and childcare.

Morning sickness immediately demonstrates how bodily change can destroy even the limited earning capacity Hachi had.

## Nobu's contraception is economic planning

He uses condoms because he knows he cannot finance a child.

This is a direct connection between sexual behavior and material adulthood.

## Love alone cannot finance parenthood

Junko says the principle aloud.

The volume makes it structurally visible through Nobu, Hachi, and BLAST's uncertain career.

## Takumi's wealth creates options

His offer matters because he can actually fulfill it.

This is a key power principle:

> **material competence becomes relational leverage when other people lack alternatives.**

## Marriage becomes legal/public infrastructure

Takumi's proposal is first justified through:

- celebrity reputation;
- legitimacy of the child;
- media management;
- reducing scandal.

Marriage is therefore simultaneously intimate institution and public-relations technology.

## Hachi's old dream is sacrificed rather than fulfilled

At the decision point, “ordinary bride” no longer describes the future she expects.

Continuing the pregnancy appears to lead away from the ordinary domestic ideal she once imagined with Nobu.

That makes the choice more costly and more autonomous than the simple “Hachi wants marriage” model allows.

# 14. Gender, sexuality, reproduction, and social scripts

## 14.1 Maternal instinct is contested inside the text

Hachi treats it as obvious.

Nana does not.

A doctor explains it biologically.

The manga does not require us to adopt the doctor's claim as scientific truth.

What matters is the social pressure encoded in the question:

> **What is a woman supposed to feel when pregnant?**

## 14.2 Nana refuses the equation motherhood = end of career

She explicitly knows women can have children and continue singing.

Her fear is not simply career impossibility.

It is confidence in herself as a mother.

## 14.3 Hachi's reproductive choice is neither purely romantic nor purely instinctive

Her decision includes:

- fear;
- ultrasound embodiment;
- sense of moral responsibility;
- desire to protect;
- money;
- physical incapacity to work;
- social support.

Reducing it to “maternal instinct” erases the structure the manga carefully builds.

## 14.4 Paternity uncertainty redistributes gendered authority

The woman carries the pregnancy.

The men debate responsibility.

Takumi's intervention demonstrates how quickly paternity uncertainty can allow male actors to dominate the information flow around a woman's body.

The volume partly resists this by repeatedly returning to Hachi's choice.

## 14.5 Nana's “both women” line is a relationship-classification act

Nana uses gender difference from heterosexual romance to imagine that she and Hachi can become normal friends.

The statement should not be read as cancelling the earlier **恋** analogy.

The contradiction is the evidence.

# 15. Ethics, consent, coercion, care, and power

## 15.1 No Volume 8 sexual encounter warrants retrospective assault classification

Volume 8's principal ethical crisis is reproductive/informational, not a new sexual-consent event.

Prior encounter classifications remain unchanged at this boundary.

## 15.2 Hachi's information boundary is explicit

She tells Takumi not to tell Nobu.

He tells Nobu.

That is a clear autonomy violation.

## 15.3 Legitimate interest does not erase method

Nobu may have a legitimate interest in a pregnancy that could be biologically his.

That does not mean Takumi has unrestricted authority to decide how Hachi will disclose it.

The ethical problem is the unilateral method.

## 15.4 Takumi's care is genuine in function even when motive is unresolved

He:

- helps Hachi physically;
- knows pregnancy symptoms;
- brings food/drink;
- offers recognition;
- offers money;
- proposes a legal/public structure.

The analysis should not deny real benefits simply because his control tendencies are troubling.

## 15.5 Real benefit can increase power

The more useful Takumi's solution is, the harder it becomes for Hachi to choose a future independent of him.

This is not proof that he deliberately engineered every dependency.

It is a structural fact about unequal resources.

## 15.6 Nana's anti-Takumi certainty must remain character belief

Nana believes Takumi intends to use the child to own Hachi.

Prior possessive language makes this plausible.

The volume does not provide direct access to Takumi's inner motive sufficient to promote it to fact.

## 15.7 Nobu is ethically serious but communicatively overwhelmed

He refuses to evade possible paternity and asks Hachi directly for truth.

He then draws a false conclusion from silence.

Moral seriousness does not make a person immune to misrecognition.

## 15.8 Hachi's decision is constrained but real

The existence of constraint does not eliminate agency.

The correct formulation is:

> **Hachi makes a real choice among unequally available options.**

This is stronger than either “pure free choice” or “no choice.”

## 15.9 Nana's friendship ethic is restraint, not emotional cleansing

Nana still wants Hachi intensely.

Her ethical achievement is to choose non-interference despite that desire.

Future Nana admits the selflessness remains incomplete.

# 17. Counterreadings, limitations, and hypothesis stress test

## Hypothesis A — “Hachi chooses Takumi over Nobu because she loves Takumi more.”

**Status: strongly weakened.**

Hachi's love for Nobu remains explicit. The decision sequence is:

1. pregnancy becomes embodied;
2. Hachi decides she does not want abortion;
3. she considers raising the child alone;
4. material incapacity becomes obvious;
5. Takumi offers support;
6. Hachi chooses the support structure that makes continuation viable.

That is not equivalent to a romantic preference ranking.

## Hypothesis B — “Takumi makes Hachi keep the pregnancy.”

**Status: contradicted by Volume 8.**

Hachi's desire to continue forms before Takumi's support offer.

His resources change feasibility.

## Hypothesis C — “Takumi is simply admirable because he takes responsibility.”

**Status: substantially complicated.**

His responsibility is real.

So are:

- overriding Hachi's explicit disclosure instruction;
- using her phone;
- controlling information flow;
- forcing binary decisions;
- speaking for her.

## Hypothesis D — “Takumi is only manipulating and offers no genuine care.”

**Status: unsupported in this simple form.**

The volume gives concrete care behaviors and a serious recognition/support offer.

Motive remains partly unresolved.

## Hypothesis E — “Nobu loses Hachi because he is immature or irresponsible.”

**Status: strongly weakened.**

His contraception and refusal to evade paternity show the opposite.

His limitations are primarily resource and communication limits.

## Hypothesis F — “Nana/Hachi is now proven sexual/romantic canon.”

**Status: too strong.**

Nana explicitly invokes **恋** but explicitly denies **性欲**.

The volume makes the ambiguity more textual, not less.

## Hypothesis G — “Nana has overcome possessiveness.”

**Status: contradicted.**

Future Nana admits wanting Hachi collared and tethered.

What develops is self-restraint.

## Hypothesis H — “Nana's non-interference speech is pure selfless love.”

**Status: weakened by the ending.**

Future Nana says she cannot entirely become the person who only wants Hachi's happiness.

Her ethic is aspirational performance.

## Hypothesis I — “Hachi's motherhood is explained by maternal instinct.”

**Status: inadequate.**

The decision is produced through embodied perception, values, material reality and support.

The doctor's universalizing claim is character/period discourse.

## Hypothesis J — “Hachi simply returns to conservative marriage ideology.”

**Status: contradicted in the simple form.**

Before Takumi proposes marriage, Hachi knowingly chooses a path she expects may sacrifice ordinary marriage with Nobu and leave her supported without marriage.

## Hypothesis K — “Pregnancy reveals Nana as anti-child.”

**Status: too strong.**

Nana's own motherhood position is uncertainty; her hostile reaction to Hachi's pregnancy is entangled with Takumi hatred and possessive attachment.

# 20. Cumulative delta after Volume 8

## Revised — self-authorship

Earlier:

> your life is yours to choose.

After Volume 8:

> **your life is yours to choose, but choices occur inside bodies, economies, institutions and relationships that distribute power unequally.**

Future Nana explicitly retains the first proposition while adding compassion for weakness.

## Strengthened — Nana/Hachi centrality

Volume 8 provides the strongest direct relationship-language yet:

- Nana uses **恋** analogy;
- denies **性欲**;
- future Hachi imagines a permanent home for Nana;
- future Nana admits collar/tether desire;
- Nana asks how ordinary friendship works;
- future Nana still defines desired self through Hachi's gaze.

The relationship is more difficult to classify, not less.

## Revised — Nana's possessiveness

Volume 7 “my yard” now looks milder than Nana's later self-description.

Future Nana says she wanted Hachi tethered at her feet and deliberately created distance to contain herself.

This reclassifies some Nana withdrawal as defensive self-restraint.

## Strengthened — Hachi's agency

Hachi:

- hides need to protect Nana's career;
- experiences the clinic independently;
- decides against abortion before Takumi's offer;
- considers single motherhood;
- submits the decision to Junko's challenge;
- chooses continuation knowing the cost.

Agency is real.

It is materially constrained.

## Revised — Hachi's domesticity

The “ordinary bride” model is no longer enough.

Hachi's imagined home includes Nana, and her reproductive decision sacrifices the easiest path toward ordinary marriage with Nobu.

Domestic vocation is broader than conventional romantic conformity.

## Strengthened — Nobu's responsibility

Contraception, financial realism, refusal to evade possible paternity, and desire to hear Hachi directly all strengthen Nobu's ethical seriousness.

The rupture should not be explained as simple immaturity.

## Revised — Hachi/Nobu rupture

The relationship collapses through a compound mechanism:

> pregnancy + uncertain paternity + resource inequality + Takumi's unauthorized disclosure + visible Takumi presence + Hachi's silence + Nobu's false but reasonable inference.

No single “romantic preference” explains it.

## Strengthened/complicated — Takumi

Takumi becomes more caring and more controlling in the same volume.

That is not contradiction to be simplified away.

It is the emerging structure of his power.

## Strengthened — material adulthood

The manga now states directly that love alone cannot raise a child.

Money, health, work and legal structure become part of romantic ethics.

## Strengthened — trust over destiny

Reira's trust language supplies a counter-model to romantic fate exactly when Hachi/Nobu demonstrates how love can fail without workable information and trust.

## Reclassified — Nana's “ordinary friendship” aspiration

Nana does not discover that the attachment was ordinary friendship all along.

She attempts to **make it behave** like ordinary friendship because she fears her own possessiveness.

This is a behavioral ethical project, not a categorical revelation.

# 21. Volume thesis and questions carried forward

Volume 8 begins with Hachi imagining a house that could always take Nana back.

It ends with Nana imagining the person she wanted to be when Hachi looked at her.

Between those two future addresses, Hachi becomes pregnant and almost every major abstraction the series has built is forced into material form.

Freedom becomes money.

Love becomes disclosure.

Responsibility becomes contraception, recognition, groceries, work schedules and who can afford a child.

Possession becomes access to a phone.

Home becomes the right to enter a room.

Trust becomes the story another person is able to believe from incomplete evidence.

Motherhood becomes neither destiny nor a slogan but a choice Hachi reaches through fear, bodily perception, moral attachment and economic limitation.

Takumi becomes more dangerous analytically because he is useful.

He does not merely impose himself through force of personality. He can solve real problems that nobody else in the immediate circle can solve as easily. His willingness to recognize the child regardless of paternity materially expands Hachi's options. His unilateral disclosure and communication control simultaneously narrow Hachi's authorship of the process.

Nobu becomes more tragic because he is responsible.

He did what a young man without the resources for parenthood is supposed to do: used contraception consistently. When contraception fails to make him irrelevant with certainty, he refuses to pretend otherwise. Yet he still loses access to Hachi's actual story because the visible evidence tells him she returned to Takumi.

Hachi becomes more adult because she chooses something that destroys the easiest version of the future she always said she wanted.

She does not choose pregnancy because Takumi promises marriage; he has not promised it yet. She chooses it even while expecting that his support may leave her outside ordinary family life. The marriage proposal comes afterward, as Takumi's solution to legitimacy and media risk.

And Nana receives the volume's deepest philosophical correction.

She still believes people should author their own lives.

She has learned that people cannot always be strong enough to do it alone.

Her answer is not to abandon autonomy.

It is to become gentler around its limits.

That produces the strongest compact thesis for Volume 8:

> **Volume 8 is where *NANA* stops allowing autonomy to remain an abstract virtue. Pregnancy turns “your life is yours” into a question of body, time, money, work, information, paternity and who possesses the resources to make a choice feasible. Hachi's decision to continue the pregnancy is genuinely hers, but the future available to that decision depends on Takumi's material capacity; Takumi's competence therefore becomes inseparable from his power to control process and information. Nobu's love fails not because it is false but because love cannot by itself solve the material and epistemic crisis. In parallel, Nana discovers that loving Hachi without owning her requires a form of friendship she does not naturally know how to practice. Future Nana's correction supplies the volume's mature principle: self-authorship remains real, but human beings cannot always be strong enough to carry it alone.**

### Questions carried into Volume 9

5. How should his willingness to recognize/support the child be weighed against his violation of Hachi's information boundary?
6. Was Nana correct that Takumi's motive is possession, or is that interpretation partly projection from Nana's own possessiveness?
7. Can Hachi and Nobu recover enough accurate information to understand what actually happened?
8. Can Nobu distinguish Hachi's silence from romantic preference for Takumi?
9. Will Hachi ever explain that she had already ended Takumi before his uninvited crisis intervention?
10. Can Nana actually practice the non-interference ethic she learned from Yasu?
11. What will “ordinary friendship” mean when Nana has already described Hachi through 恋 and collar/tether imagery?
12. Why does future Hachi say she failed to build the promised house for Nana?
13. What did future Nana gain, and what irreplaceable thing did she lose?
14. Why is future Nana still asking whether Hachi is smiling?
15. What happens to Hachi's long-standing dream of ordinary marriage now that pregnancy has made domesticity a legal/economic problem rather than a fantasy?
16. How will BLAST's professionalization interact with Hachi's pregnancy and Nobu's emotional state?
17. How will Nana's own reproductive uncertainty develop alongside Hachi's decision to become a mother?
18. Can trust, rather than destiny or possession, become a sustainable organizing principle for any of these relationships?
