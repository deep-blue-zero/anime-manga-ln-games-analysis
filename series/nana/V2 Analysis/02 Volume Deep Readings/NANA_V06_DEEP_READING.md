# 『NANA』Volume 6 Deep Reading
## Love as possession, love as non-possession: fulfilled wishes, competing claims, the emptying of 707, and Hachi's search for someone who knows the unidealized self

# 1. Central thesis and position in the larger narrative

Volume 6 is the first volume in which *NANA* turns one of its deepest relational problems into an explicit argument spoken by the characters themselves:

> **What is the difference between loving someone and wanting to possess them?**

The problem has existed from the beginning. Nana refused to follow Ren to Tokyo because loving him did not mean surrendering the authorship of her life. Hachi repeatedly wanted romantic partners to become secure homes. Shoji's betrayal demonstrated that a recognized relationship does not create ownership over another person's future. Apartment 707 established a different domestic model: two young women can share a life while retaining separate rooms, separate locks, separate money, separate obligations, and distinct selves.

Volume 6 makes the latent tension verbal.

Shin says that once people become accustomed to another person, they begin to want **「相手の全部」**—the whole of that person—and calls this **「人のサガ」**, human nature. Nobu tries to oppose that instinct with an ideal of **「本物の愛」**, a “real love” in which one asks for nothing and simply watches over the beloved kindly, only to admit that jealousy makes the ideal difficult to live. Takumi tells Hachi **「誰にも渡したくない」** and **「ずっとおれのものでいてよ」**: he does not want to give her to anyone and wants her to remain his. Ren, in a remembered conversation, admits that there are moments when he desperately wishes Nana were his.

Most importantly, Nana tells Yasu that Ren and Hachi were never her **「所有物」**. She knows that making another person **「自分のもの」** is impossible. She understands the thought is wrong. But the impossibility can make her unbearably **寂しい**.

Then she turns the metaphor against herself:

> **「あたし ハチ公の事 本気で自分の飼い犬みたいに思ってたのかな」**

Did I seriously think of Hachikō as if she were my own pet dog?

The private nickname that has functioned as comedy, affection, intimacy, and domestic language is suddenly revealed to contain a possible possessive fantasy.

The manga is not declaring Nana's affection false. It is doing something more difficult:

> **genuine attachment can contain an ethically troubling wish to keep the other person.**

Nana's importance is that she recognizes the wish as a problem.

The volume's second governing movement begins with Tanabata. Future Hachi remembers that she possessed more wishes than she could count. The group writes wishes, drinks, eats and laughs. Hachi believes that although wishing alone cannot make a dream real, each fulfilled dream should make her progressively happier.

Then Takumi fulfills one of the wishes she has been afraid to admit she still holds: he comes to 707 exactly as promised.

Future Hachi's answer follows:

> **「夢が叶う事と 幸せになる事は  
> どうして別ものなんだろう」**

Why are having a dream come true and becoming happy different things?

That is the architectural center of Volume 6.

Takumi returns. Hachi's dream comes true. She is not simply happy.

Nobu loves her. That should be another dream. It produces joy and guilt at once.

Nana has Ren again. She is not simply secure.

BLAST receives meaningful industry interest. Nana's professional dream is becoming materially possible. That brings pressure, rivalry and distance from 707.

The volume therefore does not argue that wishes are foolish. It argues that **desire is simpler than the life created after desire succeeds**.

A wish contains only the wanted endpoint.

A life contains everyone else who must live with the consequence.

Volume 6's deepest Hachi development emerges from that complication. She increasingly suspects that Nobu and Nana love an idealized version of her, while Takumi knows the self she considers **「どうしようもなく空っぽ」**—hopelessly empty. By the end of the volume, she calls him:

> **「こんなあたしを許してくれる 唯一の共犯者みたい」**

like the only **accomplice** who can accept this version of her.

This does not make the Takumi relationship healthy. It explains why leaving it is psychologically difficult.

Nobu offers a form of love Hachi thinks she *ought* to deserve.

Takumi offers recognition of the self she is ashamed she actually is.

The volume ends by making the relational problem spatial. Nana leaves 707 for Ren. Hachi avoids the studio because she cannot face Nobu. Takumi enters 707 intermittently without becoming part of its original reciprocal domestic structure. The table remains where it has always been, but the people who made it home increasingly sleep somewhere else.

Future Hachi's enduring wish is not primarily to see Takumi or Nobu.

It is to see Nana again across that table.

Volume 6 begins with wishes tied to bamboo branches and ends with a wish that has survived into the narrator's future.

# 3. Narrative close reading

## 3.1 Tanabata: the fantasy that happiness can be accumulated

Future Hachi opens the volume with:

> **「あの頃  
> 願い事だけは 数えきれない程あった」**

At that time, she had more wishes than she could count.

The date is July 7, 2001—Tanabata. The cultural form is perfectly suited to Hachi: private wishes are written down, decorated, externalized and placed where other people can see them.

Yet the chapter does not begin with romance alone. Hachi is working short-term food-demonstration jobs at a department store. She cooks samples, talks to customers, performs small-scale sales labor and is paid quickly enough to keep her immediate finances moving.

What matters is her verdict:

> **「意外にも楽しいんだ これが！」**

To her surprise, she enjoys it.

That is a useful correction to any model in which Hachi can only feel alive through romance or glamorous aspiration. She can enjoy ordinary work when it suits her social energy and produces immediate human feedback.

That night, 707 is full. Nana, Hachi, Nobu, Shin, Yasu and the others eat, drink, write wishes and look toward the sky. The Milky Way is hidden by cloud.

The cosmic spectacle fails.

The domestic evening succeeds.

Future Hachi says they stay up late having fun anyway.

Then she formulates the younger self's belief:

> **「ただ願うだけで夢は叶わない事くらい  
> もう分かってたけど」**

She understood that wishing alone cannot make a dream real.

But:

> **「夢がひとつ叶う毎に  
> 幸せになって行ける気がしてた」**

She felt that every dream that came true should make her happier.

This is the assumption the volume breaks apart.

## 3.2 Takumi's promised return becomes a test of Hachi's sexual shame

Hachi has not forgotten Takumi's promise:

> **「真っ先に会いに行くから」**  
> **「また飯作って食わせてよ」**

He said he would see her first and asked her to cook for him again.

Hachi oscillates between **来る** and **来ない**.

She shops “just in case.”

She cooks too much food.

She lies to Nana that nobody is coming.

But the real stakes appear in her interior narration.

If Takumi never returns, perhaps she can finally let go.

If he does return—if he is actually the kind of man who keeps the promise—then some of her **後ろめたさ**, her guilty shame about sleeping with him, will disappear.

That is psychologically precise.

Hachi is trying to use later continuity to validate earlier sexual behavior.

If the night becomes part of a continuing relationship, then perhaps she was not merely foolish.

This logic will reappear later in a more self-critical form when Hachi wonders whether she only calls the feeling “love” because she wants to **正当化** having slept with him.

---

## 3.3 The male afterparty: consent and disrespect must be separated

At the Trapnest afterparty, Hachi's private sexual experience becomes male social knowledge.

Ren objects to Takumi's conduct because Hachi is an important friend of “his woman.”

Takumi answers:

> **「別に無理矢理やったわけじゃねえぞ  
> 合意の上でだ」**

He did not force her. It was consensual.

That statement is consistent with Hachi's own Volume 5 interior evidence, where she recognized where the evening was going, admitted that some part of her wanted it and deliberately permitted herself the experience. We therefore do not need Takumi's boast as the sole proof of consent.

But his next line is ethically different:

> **「シッポ振って喜んでたよ？」**

She was practically wagging her tail with delight.

The public version of Hachi's sexual vulnerability becomes masculine bragging.

This begins an important Takumi split:

> private affection versus public sexual crudity;  
> actual time spent with Hachi versus speaking about her as an acquisition.

The fact that the sex was consensual does not make this social framing respectful.

---

## 3.4 03:05: a wish walks through the door

At approximately **03:05 on Monday, July 9**, the doorbell rings.

Takumi has come.

The fulfillment is staged less like a romantic climax than like an intrusion from one reality into another.

Hachi opens the door.

Takumi embraces and kisses her.

Nana sees.

Yazawa largely removes dialogue.

The sequence becomes faces, bodies, gaze and threshold: Hachi, Takumi, Nana, retreat, closing door.

The moral consequence is carried by spatial composition.

A romantic wish enters the apartment.

A domestic relationship exits the room.

Then future Hachi asks:

> **「ねえ ナナ」**

and:

> **「夢が叶う事と 幸せになる事は  
> どうして別ものなんだろう」**

Why are having a dream come true and becoming happy different things?

The event Hachi wanted occurs exactly when another relationship is visibly hurt by it.

Fulfillment is no longer private.

---

## 3.5 Shin and Nobu: love as non-extraction, love as impossible ideal

Chapter 18 begins with one of the richest conversations in the series so far.

Nobu is furious about Takumi and defensive of Hachi.

Shin rejects the moral classification Nobu is tempted to make:

> **「僕はべつに  
> 僕と寝たがる女の人達を  
> ケツが軽いなんて思ってないよ」**

He does not think women who want to sleep with him are “easy.”

He may think they are:

> **「アサハカ」**

foolish.

But:

> **「みんなそれだけ寂しいんだよ」**

They are all that lonely.

Then Shin turns the same idea toward Hachi: perhaps she was lonely after breaking up with her boyfriend.

The remarkable thing is not that Shin is morally correct in every respect. He is not. It is that a fifteen-year-old whose own sexuality has become commercialized refuses the simple equation:

> sexual availability = bad woman.

He then explains why Hachi affects him differently.

The women around Shin want things from him:

- his body;
- his heart;
- his attention.

Hachi:

> **「何も求めずに  
> ただ優しくしてくれる」**

asks nothing and is simply kind.

Therefore:

> **「そーゆーのって  
> 愛を感じるじゃない」**

That feels like love.

This is the volume's first explicit model:

> **love as non-extraction.**

Then Shin immediately complicates it.

Once people become accustomed to another person, he says:

> **「相手の全部を欲しくなる」**

they begin to want all of that person.

That is:

> **「人のサガ」**

human nature.

His advice to Nobu:

> **「欲しいなら奪っちゃいなよ」**

If you want her, take her.

Nobu tries to formulate the opposite ethic: perhaps **本物の愛**, “real love,” means asking nothing, simply watching over the beloved kindly.

Yet Takumi makes him jealous enough that he cannot live his own ideal.

The scene therefore does not divide the two neatly into cynic and romantic.

Shin understands non-extractive care.

Nobu believes in selfless love.

Both acknowledge desire's tendency to become a claim.

Later, when Shin tells Nobu what he possesses that Takumi does not, he gives the answer in one word:

> **「愛」**

Love.

Volume 6's question is whether love is enough when timing, shame, sex, status and self-image complicate the person receiving it.

---

## 3.6 Nana leaves her own home and names the possession underneath love

While Hachi and Takumi remain in 707, Nana calls Yasu at roughly four in the morning.

She says:

> **「タクミが家に来てるんだ」**

Takumi is at the house.

She thought she would be in the way.

This is generous on its surface. Nana voluntarily removes herself from her own home so Hachi can have privacy.

It hurts enough that she needs Yasu immediately.

Then Nana says:

> **「なんで  
> あたしの大事なもんは  
> みんなトラネスに取られちゃうのかな」**

Why do all the things precious to me get taken by Trapnest?

Hachi has now joined Ren in the same emotional category:

> **あたしの大事なもん**.

Nana then corrects herself:

> **「レンもハチ公も  
> べつに元々あたしの所有物なわけじゃない」**

Ren and Hachikō were never her possessions.

She knows:

> **「誰かを自分のものにするなんて  
> 絶対無理」**

making someone “your own” is absolutely impossible.

But knowing does not abolish the feeling.

Sometimes it makes her unbearably:

> **寂しい**

lonely.

Then:

> **「あたし ハチ公の事  
> 本気で自分の飼い犬みたいに思ってたのかな」**

Did I really think of Hachikō like my own pet dog?

> **「最低だよ」**

I'm awful.

This is moral reflexivity.

Nana does not merely feel possessive.

She observes herself feeling it, judges it and recognizes the beloved's separateness as ethically non-negotiable.

The volume's argument is therefore not “love is possession.”

It is:

> **love can generate possessive desire because another person's freedom makes loss possible.**

## 3.7 Yasu becomes explicitly parental infrastructure

Nana tells Yasu:

> **「あんたに甘え過ぎだね」**

She depends on him too much.

Yasu answers that if she understands this, she should hurry up and:

> **「親離れ」**

separate from the parent.

The joke clarifies a relationship Hachi has repeatedly tried to read romantically.

Yasu functions partly as a parental attachment figure:

- crisis receiver;
- regulator;
- adult competence;
- safe witness;
- person Nana can call at four in the morning.

This does not reduce Nana/Yasu to literal parent/child. It specifies the kind of dependency Nana herself can recognize.

Nana's attachment system is increasingly distributed:

- Ren receives romantic/erotic longing;
- Hachi receives domestic intimacy, friendship, jealousy and possessive fear;
- Yasu receives emergency reliance and quasi-parental trust.

No single relationship supplies everything.

That is becoming central to *NANA*'s understanding of adult intimacy.

---

## 3.8 Ren wanted Nana to be his too

Yasu remembers Ren speaking before the move to Tokyo.

Ren admitted:

> **「ナナがおれのもんだったらいいのに」**

I wish Nana were mine.

There are times:

> **「どうしようもなく思う」**

when he cannot help feeling it.

This is the volume's comparative method at work.

Nana wants to keep Ren and Hachi.

Ren wants Nana.

Takumi wants Hachi to remain his.

Shin says humans eventually want the whole beloved.

Possessive longing is not framed as a uniquely male pathology or uniquely female insecurity.

The ethical question is:

> **what does the person do when the beloved acts independently?**

Ren did not force Nana to follow him.

Nana knows she cannot own Hachi.

Takumi's future relation to his possessive language remains unresolved at the Volume 6 endpoint.

---

## 3.9 Takumi becomes ordinary enough to become more dangerous to Hachi emotionally

Back in 707, Takumi does something Hachi did not expect.

He stays.

Hachi remembers that after their first night she only pretended to sleep because it hurt when he left so quickly. This time she realizes that when Takumi has time, he remains.

They talk.

He asks about her romantic history.

He explains that his own relationships tend to fail because:

- work comes first;
- messages go unanswered;
- promises get broken;
- by the time he visits, women say they no longer know him.

Hachi responds differently.

She is already a Trapnest fan.

The work is meaningful to her.

Therefore she can say:

> of course the band comes first.

Takumi tells her she is unusually understanding.

That is an important asymmetry.

Hachi's fandom pre-trains her to tolerate conditions that might otherwise read as neglect.

It also gives her a flattering identity:

> **I am the woman who truly understands his work.**

This is not necessarily false.

It may also become a technology through which Hachi lowers her own expectations.

The volume eventually asks whether her patience is **信頼** or **諦め**.

---

## 3.10 「ずっとおれのものでいてよ」

Takumi tells Hachi:

> **「誰にも渡したくない」**

I don't want to give you to anyone.

And:

> **「ずっとおれのものでいてよ」**

Stay mine forever.

At the Volume 6 endpoint, this occurs during affectionate, apparently consensual intimacy. Hachi likes being wanted.

The line should not therefore be retroactively turned into proof of later coercion.

But it must be recorded exactly.

It belongs in the possession ledger.

Nana, Ren and Takumi now share versions of the same emotional grammar:

> I know another person is separate from me.  
> I still want the person to remain mine.

The long-term ethical distinction will depend on behavior under conflict, not the mere existence of possessive feeling.

---

## 3.11 BLAST approaches professionalization while Reira explains its cost

Gaia Music has seen BLAST's demo/live material and wants to observe the next performance.

This is not a debut contract yet.

But the dream is now institutionally legible.

A major gate has opened.

At almost the same point, Reira explains what professional visibility has cost her.

She is half American. Her father died when she was six. The agency suppresses her mixed heritage because it prefers the audience to explain her beauty and vocal power within a commercially useful image of “one of us.”

She is also repeatedly told not to do or say things that would break that image.

Her protest is simple:

> **「あたしはあたしなのに」**

I'm still me.

Then:

> **「人間って疲れる」**

Being human is exhausting.

Yazawa cuts from this to Hachi doing rainy department-store food-sales work, frying and serving food while the smell sticks to her hair and clothes.

The class juxtaposition is powerful.

Reira's labor problem:

> too much visibility.

Hachi's labor problem:

> too little visibility.

Both women's bodies are part of work.

One must preserve a marketable fantasy.

The other is ordinary enough to become almost invisible.

## 3.12 Nana supports Hachi in a way she does not emotionally feel

Hachi eventually tells Nana about Takumi.

Nana already knows.

Nana says there is nothing to apologize for.

Hachi confesses her shame: what if sleeping with a musician she admired makes her pathetic or stupid?

Nana refuses that moral framing.

If Hachi genuinely fell for Takumi, celebrity status does not make the emotion fake.

She would dislike a purely trophy-like attitude—sleeping with a celebrity merely to say she had—but Nana saw Hachi cry in Takumi's arms.

Nana then encourages Hachi.

She interprets Takumi's return as possible evidence of genuine interest.

This is an extraordinary act of friendship precisely because the reader knows Nana's private emotional state.

She feels that Trapnest has taken another precious person.

She nevertheless gives Hachi the interpretation most likely to support Hachi's freedom.

Nana's feelings say:

> mine.

Her behavior says:

> you are free.

That is the ethical center of her Volume 6 arc.

## 3.13 Hachi finally asks whether she loves Takumi at all

Nobu's feelings become explicit.

He tells Hachi:

> **「好きだ」**

The confession is simple enough to contrast sharply with Takumi's celebrity aura and possessive language.

Hachi's reaction is not mild.

> **「どうしよう  
> うれしい」**

What do I do? I'm happy.

Then she recognizes that Nobu's words make her far happier than Takumi's “stay mine forever,” whose seriousness she cannot judge.

She thinks that if things had not already become complicated with Takumi, she would absolutely have fallen for Nobu and wanted to become his girlfriend.

This is decisive prospective evidence.

Nobu is not simply the “healthy alternative” critics might prefer.

Hachi herself experiences him as a real romantic possibility.

And that recognition forces the next question:

> **「あたし本当に  
> タクミの事好きなのかな」**

Do I really love Takumi?

Then:

> **「タクミと寝た事  
> 正当化したいだけなんじゃないかな」**

Am I only trying to justify the fact that I slept with him?

> **「もう分からない」**

I don't know anymore.

Hachi has now formulated her own adversarial reading.

She understands that people can construct “love” retrospectively because “I was in love” makes a sexual decision easier to narrate morally.

The manga does not tell us this is definitively the entire explanation.

The importance lies in Hachi being able to ask.

---

## 3.14 The cigarette pack and the unchosen fork

After Nobu's confession, Takumi's Gitanes cigarette pack remains as a material reminder of the other relationship.

Hachi thinks Nobu's words made her happier.

She thinks she would have wanted him.

But:

> **「でももしまたタクミが会いに来てくれたら  
> あたしは結局またタクミと寝るんだ」**

If Takumi comes to see her again, she believes she will probably sleep with him again.

> **「きっと拒めない」**

She probably will not be able to refuse.

This is important but must be handled carefully.

It is **not** automatic evidence that a future sexual act is nonconsensual.

It is evidence that Hachi experiences weak boundary control around a person she desires and feels ashamed of desiring.

Her next question is more important:

> **「あたしいったい何が欲しいんだろう」**

What do I actually want?

Then:

> **「こんな流されてばかりじゃ  
> 何も掴めない」**

If she keeps being carried by events, she will never grasp anything.

Hachi's central problem is no longer lack of available choices.

It is difficulty authoring them.

## 3.15 Nana moves to Ren and 707 begins to empty

The next morning Nana leaves for Ren's home.

She leaves Hachi a note telling her to lock the door and check who is outside before opening it.

The instruction is mundane and caring.

The apartment is empty.

Hachi tells herself it is temporary. She can still see Nana at rehearsal.

Only two weeks.

Then:

> **「二週間たてば  
> 戻って来てくれるよね？」**

She asks whether Nana will come back after two weeks.

Future Hachi then says:

> **「今でも あのテーブル越しに  
> ナナの姿を願わない日はない」**

Even now, there is no day when she does not wish to see Nana across that table.

She calls Nana's name in her heart:

> **「何度も  
> 何度でも」**

again and again.

The reason for the future absence remains unresolved and must stay unresolved.

But the emotional hierarchy is unmistakable.

707 has become a place capable of representing a person.

The table becomes Nana's missing position.

## 3.16 Two weeks: romance changes the group without formally breaking it

Nana does not return for two weeks.

Hachi continues working and attends rehearsal consistently.

Nobu escorts her home.

Takumi sends nothing.

Nana and Ren temporarily live together.

Yet the domestic scenes deliberately refuse “romance solved everything.”

Ren suggests travel.

Nana refuses because BLAST has an important performance and she must rehearse.

That is a significant continuation of her original self-authorship.

Being with Ren does not eliminate the band.

The same domestic sequence introduces reproductive difference.

Ren notices that Nana is taking contraceptive pills again.

Nana responds sharply enough to establish a core fact:

> she is actively preventing pregnancy and does not treat renewed intimacy as an automatic route into motherhood.

This is the first explicit reproductive-autonomy marker in Nana's own romantic arc.

---

## 3.17 Takumi's silence starts to become narratively useful to Hachi

Nana encourages Hachi to contact Takumi.

Hachi resists.

She does not want Takumi to come merely because somebody else pressured him.

Then she admits something stronger.

If Takumi never contacts her again, perhaps that would be easier.

Why?

Because then:

> **「全部タクミのせいにして 新しい恋をする」**

she could make everything Takumi's fault and begin a new love.

Hachi catches herself constructing a morally flattering story:

> deceived woman;  
> cruel man;  
> clean new beginning.

She knows the story would erase her own agency.

She also knows it would be unfair to use Nobu as the emotional reward for having been discarded by Takumi.

Most of all, she fears Nobu seeing her as:

> **「そんな軽い女」**

that kind of easy woman.

The triangle has become a crisis of recognition.

Hachi does not simply ask:

> Who do I want?

She asks:

> **Who could still love me if he knew how badly I think of myself?**

## 3.18 Takumi returns exactly when Hachi is moving toward Nobu

After rehearsal, Hachi and Nobu walk together.

Then Hachi sees a black Mercedes.

Takumi is waiting.

The timing is brutal.

Hachi thinks:

> **「今更来るなら  
> せめてメールの一本くらい入れて欲しい」**

If he is going to come now, he could at least send one message.

She calls him:

> **「気まぐれで勝手な男」**

a capricious, selfish man.

Takumi has waited roughly an hour.

Then he takes control of the social transition with casual fluency. Hachi explains that Nobu was escorting her because Nana is staying with Ren and she is afraid to walk alone at night. Takumi thanks Nobu and says good night, positioning himself beside Hachi.

No contest was declared.

Takumi's social confidence simply acts as though access has already been granted.

Hachi immediately understands the cost.

> **「最低だ あたし」**

I'm the worst.

She knows that she allowed herself to enjoy Nobu's attention while leaving the Takumi situation unresolved.

She knows Nobu has been hurt.

The insight arrives after the injury.

Again the series distinguishes:

> moral perception from behavioral competence.

## 3.19 Hachi wants to end Takumi and cannot say the sentence

Inside 707, Takumi settles with astonishing domestic ease.

He drinks.

Hachi prepares food.

He shares her drink.

The apartment that began as Nana/Hachi's negotiated home now repeatedly becomes the site of Hachi/Takumi intimacy.

Hachi internally reaches a decision.

To Takumi, she thinks, she is probably:

> **「やりたい時にやれる  
> 都合のいい女」**

a convenient woman he can have sex with when he wants.

Therefore:

> **「終わらせるなら今のうちだ」**

If she is going to end it, now is the time.

The sentence she wants is:

> **「もう来ないで」**

Don't come again.

Then:

> can she actually say it?

She cannot.

Takumi's ordinary presence—sharing the drink, sitting in the room, accepting food, acting as though his presence is natural—collapses the boundary she rehearsed internally.

The later sexual encounter must therefore be classified carefully.

The pages do **not** show Hachi saying no and being ignored.

They do **not** show physical force.

They show substantial internal ambivalence and inability to terminate the relationship.

The correct prospective finding is:

> **apparently consensual sexual participation under significant relational ambivalence and weak boundary assertion; not depicted as coercive in Volume 6, but not evidence that Hachi is satisfied with the relationship's terms.**

Consent to a sexual act and autonomous authorship of the surrounding relationship are different questions.

## 3.20 「唯一の共犯者」 — why Takumi feels safe to Hachi

After sex, Hachi gives the most revealing explanation yet for Takumi's psychological power.

She knows Takumi is a hopeless man.

And Takumi knows something about her.

Nana and Nobu, in Hachi's view, still imagine a purer Hachi than the person she believes herself to be.

Takumi knows:

> **「こんなどうしようもなく  
> 空っぽなあたし」**

this hopelessly empty version of her.

And he stays.

That makes him feel like:

> **「唯一の共犯者」**

the only accomplice who can accept/forgive her.

**共犯者** is not “soulmate.”

It means an accomplice, someone implicated in the same wrongdoing.

Hachi is not imagining Takumi as the person who rescues her from shame.

She imagines him as someone with whom shame can be shared.

That is why Nobu's sincere affection can paradoxically feel more frightening.

Nobu's love asks Hachi to believe she deserves something good.

Takumi's presence allows Hachi to believe:

> I can be foolish, sexual, inconsistent, empty and morally compromised—and still be wanted.

The dangerous possibility is visible without importing any later event:

> **self-acceptance can become attached to the person who validates the self one most despises.**

## 3.21 Trust or resignation?

Takumi eventually leaves for work.

Hachi messages Nana happily that he came and spent the remaining part of his break with her.

But Nana does not return even after Trapnest's break ends.

Hachi wants to see Nana at the studio but avoids it because she cannot face Nobu.

Takumi still sends no messages.

And Hachi discovers she can tolerate the silence.

Perhaps he will eventually return.

Then:

> **「それが信頼なのか  
> 諦めなのかは  
> 自分でもよく分からなかったけど」**

She does not know whether this is **trust** or **resignation**.

This is one of the volume's most important distinctions.

The “understanding girlfriend” can look admirable:

- I respect his work;
- I do not demand constant contact;
- I let him be free.

But the same behavior can arise from:

- fear of asking;
- low expectations;
- anticipated abandonment;
- emotional surrender.

The outward behavior does not tell us which interior state exists.

Hachi herself cannot yet distinguish them.

## 3.22 The volume returns to Nana through song

Misato arrives near the end, excited by the things a fan can be excited about:

- Nana and Ren;
- BLAST;
- the approaching live.

Her perspective contrasts sharply with Hachi's.

For Misato, these are beautiful narratives.

For Hachi, they have become ordinary emotional consequence.

Hachi wonders whether Nobu will still smile at her.

The thought nearly makes her cry.

Then the volume remembers the song Nobu wrote.

Future Hachi says that now she understands what Nana was wishing when she sang it:

> **「声をふり絞って  
> あたしの耳に届くように」**

straining her voice so it would reach Hachi's ears.

This remains future Hachi's retrospective interpretation, not omniscient proof of Nana's interior state.

But the structural effect is unmistakable.

Nobu wrote the song.

Nana sang it.

Hachi becomes the remembered addressee.

The romantic triangle folds back into the Nana/Hachi axis through music.

# 4. Nana Osaki — cumulative character update

Volume 6 provides Nana with unusually direct self-analysis.

## Possessiveness is now explicit

The model “Nana has abandonment anxiety” is no longer precise enough.

Her own vocabulary gives us the mechanism:

> attachment can become a wish for ownership.

She knows this is wrong.

She feels it anyway.

Her independence and possessiveness are therefore not opposites.

Nana values freedom because she fiercely guards her own.

Other people's freedom frightens her because freedom includes the power to leave.

This produces one of her strongest contradictions:

> **she demands autonomy for herself while emotionally wishing that the people she most needs could somehow be exempt from the same autonomy.**

Her moral seriousness lies in refusing to turn that wish automatically into a right.

## Hachikō acquires a second meaning

“Hachikō” has meant affection, teasing and domestic private language.

Nana's own **飼い犬** question adds:

> possible possession.

The nickname should henceforth be tracked as a double sign.

## Nana acts better than she feels

Her most important ethical action is not confessing jealousy.

It is what she does afterward.

She does not punish Hachi.

She does not claim priority.

She does not demand that Takumi disappear.

She supports Hachi's hope.

Nana's feelings say:

> mine.

Her conduct says:

> free.

## Yasu is a dependency structure she can name

**甘える / 親離れ** moves the Yasu relationship out of vague “dependable male friend” territory.

He is a person Nana uses for crisis regulation.

The relationship is loving, but its form is dependency-coded rather than simply romantic.

## Ren does not erase vocation

Even while living temporarily with Ren, Nana prioritizes BLAST practice.

This is strong evidence that reunion has not restored the pre-Tokyo household hierarchy.

## Reproductive autonomy appears

The contraceptive-pill scene makes Nana's bodily authorship explicit.

Sexual reunion does not equal agreement about children.

## Leaving 707 becomes consequential

Nana's departure is initially practical and temporary.

Its emotional result is larger.

By the end of the volume, future Hachi has turned the empty place across the table into a lifelong wish.

# 5. Hachi — cumulative character update

Volume 6 is one of the strongest arguments against reading Hachi as merely unreflective.

## She enjoys ordinary competence

Her food-demonstration work is not glamorous and she likes it.

The V5 “light” thesis therefore needs precision:

> Hachi does not reject ordinary life; she undervalues ordinary competence when compared with the symbolic intensity of romance and celebrity.

## She recognizes narrative self-justification

Hachi asks whether she only calls Takumi “love” because she wants to justify sex already had.

That is sophisticated self-criticism.

## Nobu makes her happy in a different register

Takumi's attention intoxicates.

Nobu's confession produces uncomplicated **うれしい**.

That difference matters.

## Moral exposure frightens her

With Nobu, Hachi fears not rejection but revelation.

What if he sees the sexual, opportunistic, confused Hachi and withdraws the idealized affection?

Her wish becomes:

> to be loved without having to remain morally legible as “good.”

## Celebrity remains constitutive of Takumi's attraction

Hachi explicitly says she cannot separate him from **トラネスのタクミ**.

As one ordinary man, her attraction may be much weaker.

The V5 “home + light” reading is therefore strongly confirmed.

## Consent and boundaries separate

Volume 6's Takumi encounters are not depicted as forced.

But Hachi struggles to convert inner disagreement into outer boundaries.

The relational problem is not absence of all sexual desire.

It is weakness of self-authorship.

## 共犯者 is the deepest Takumi insight so far

Takumi feels psychologically safe because Hachi thinks he already knows the morally compromised self.

That is different from being safely known.

Hachi may not yet perceive the distinction.

## 信頼 and 諦め blur

The mature-looking woman who “understands” a busy man can be secure or resigned.

Hachi cannot tell which she is becoming.

# 6. Other major characters

## Nobu

Nobu is now explicitly in love with Hachi.

His virtues are real:

- sincerity;
- emotional compatibility;
- directness;
- concern;
- relative lack of status power.

His “real love” ethic is not yet fully lived.

Jealousy destabilizes the ideal.

Hachi also fears that he idealizes her.

This makes “Nobu = uncomplicated healthy alternative” too simple.

## Shin

Shin is one of Volume 6's accidental philosophers.

He rejects slut-shaming.

He understands loneliness behind sexual behavior.

He recognizes non-extractive kindness as love.

He identifies the human desire to possess the beloved.

Yet his fluency must not erase the established fact that he is fifteen and engaged in commercial sex with adult women.

Precocity is not safety.

## Reira

Reira's image is institutionally manufactured.

Her mixed heritage is concealed.

Her conduct is managed.

Her private loneliness/curiosity intersects with Shin's commercial sexual logic.

The Reira/Shin sequence is complicated because Shin has real agency—he deliberately creates a reason to see her again and asks for payment—while the adult/minor power problem remains ethically serious.

## Ren

Ren becomes Nana's mirror in possessive longing.

He also becomes a genuine temporary domestic partner again.

But Nana's continued commitment to BLAST and active contraception prevent “reunion = restored old life.”

## Yasu

Yasu is emotional infrastructure.

His crucial function is to give Nana a place to confess possessive feeling without acting it out against Hachi.

That is a high-value form of care.

# 8. Japanese voice and translation-sensitive language

## Nana: rough speech, precise moral nouns

Nana's ordinary voice remains direct, rough and contracted.

But her possession confession becomes abstract and exact:

- **所有物**
- **自分のもの**
- **寂しい**
- **飼い犬**
- **最低**

Her deepest wounds often emerge not through long exposition but through compressed moral sentences.

## Hachi: self-prosecution

Hachi repeatedly uses words against herself:

- **最低**
- **ズルい**
- **軽い女**
- **空っぽ**
- **正当化**
- **流される**
- **どうしようもない**

She frequently prosecutes herself before anyone else can.

This makes her a rich narrator and an unreliable judge of her own worth.

## Takumi: audience-dependent register

Public Takumi:

> **「シッポ振って喜んでたよ？」**

Private Takumi:

> **「ほんと かわいいね おまえ」**  
> **「誰にも渡したくない」**  
> **「ずっとおれのものでいてよ」**

The register changes with audience.

Future analysis should therefore track not only what Takumi says, but **who is present when he says it**.

## Shin: prematurely adult abstraction

Shin uses language such as:

- **人のサガ**
- **愛**
- **欲望の対象物**

alongside commercial price negotiation.

This makes him sound older than his age while simultaneously reminding us how much adult sexuality has structured his adolescence.

## Nobu: simple language, direct affect

Nobu's decisive word is simply:

> **好きだ**

The simplicity is part of his force.

The problem is that emotional sincerity does not make the surrounding situation simple.

## 共犯者

The strongest noun in the volume.

It does not mean soulmate.

It makes intimacy a form of shared implication.

## 信頼 / 諦め

The final lexical opposition deserves longitudinal tracking.

Both can look like patience.

Only one assumes a relationship in which expectations still have legitimate force.

# 9. Narration, time and information

Future Hachi becomes still more interpretive.

At Tanabata, the younger self believes fulfilled wishes accumulate into happiness.

The future narrator already knows otherwise.

At Chapter 18's end, future Hachi apologizes for not understanding Nana and for failing to notice she was hurting her.

This is strong evidence of later Hachi's guilt.

It is **not** neutral proof that Hachi objectively caused all later harm.

The table passage similarly establishes a future absence emotionally without explaining its cause.

And the final song passage establishes future Hachi's interpretation of Nana's performance, not omniscient access to Nana's mind.

The volume is therefore increasingly built from:

> lived event + later interpretation + unresolved causal gap.

Information also remains asymmetrical:

- Hachi does not know the full intensity of Nana's jealousy.
- Nana does not know all of Hachi's self-loathing.
- Nobu does not know exactly how Hachi interprets Takumi.
- Takumi does not appear to know the full Nobu situation.
- the reader knows more than every participant.

The tragedy is not a lack of care.

It is care acting through incomplete models of other people.

# 11. Home and domestic-space ledger

707 changes materially in Volume 6.

**Who belongs?** Nana and Hachi remain the household core.

**Who enters?** Takumi becomes a sexual/domestic guest unlike the original BLAST gathering. He eats Hachi's food, drinks, waits and sleeps there without entering the apartment's original reciprocal economy.

**Who leaves?** Nana first leaves to give privacy, then stays with Ren.

**Who waits?** Hachi waits for both Takumi and Nana. Takumi's absence becomes possible trust/resignation. Nana's absence becomes future longing.

**Who cooks?** Hachi continues using food as care. Takumi's enjoyment of her cooking binds her genuine domestic competence to celebrity-sexual intimacy.

**Who controls access?** Nana's departing note specifically tells Hachi to lock the door and confirm who is outside.

This is poignantly literal in a volume increasingly concerned with who gains entry to the home and to the self.

By the end:

> **707 remains home, but home is no longer sufficient to keep its founders physically together.**

- foolish fan;
- easy woman;
- morally unserious.

Nana and Ren's pill conversation introduces reproductive autonomy without assuming that sex, cohabitation and children form one automatic sequence.

Possessive longing crosses gender: Nana, Ren and Takumi all participate in it.

Shin's age remains ethically decisive regardless of his fluency, agency or commercial sophistication.

# 15. Ethics, consent, coercion, care and power

## Takumi/Hachi consent audit

**Prior encounter, discussed in V6:** Takumi calls it consensual, and Hachi's own V5 interiority independently supports deliberate participation.

**First V6 707 encounter:** mutual kissing/embracing and affectionate after-sex intimacy; no refusal shown. Presented as consensual.

**Late V6 encounter:** Hachi internally wants to end the relationship but cannot say “don't come again.” No verbal/physical refusal is shown as ignored. Best classification: **apparently consensual sex under significant relational ambivalence and weak boundary assertion**.

Two principles must remain distinct:

> consent to a sexual act ≠ consent to every relationship term.

and:

> inability to author a relationship clearly ≠ automatic absence of consent to a specific act.

Takumi's crude public language about Hachi is ethically worse than the sexual evidence requires; it objectifies an intimate experience for male social performance.

Nana's jealousy is not admirable, but her response demonstrates moral restraint.

Nobu's sincere love does not make him perfectly non-possessive.

Shin's idea that Hachi's kindness feels like love because she wants nothing from him is a powerful ethical intuition, but sustainable relationships also require the right to make legitimate requests. Total non-demand cannot be the entire definition of love.

# 17. Hypothesis stress test

## “Hachi is simply boy-crazy”
**Substantially weakened.**

She now explicitly distinguishes:

- celebrity fascination;
- emotional compatibility;
- sex;
- love;
- retroactive justification.

Her deeper problem is self-authorship under competing desires.

## “Hachi's deepest desire is only home”
**Complicated.**

Home remains central.

But she also seeks light, recognition, desire and a person who can know her shame.

## “Nana is simply independent”
**Decisively inadequate.**

Her autonomy is genuine.

So are possession, abandonment fear, Yasu dependency and renewed cohabitation with Ren.

## “Nana/Hachi is ordinary friendship”
**Strongly weakened.**

Direct possessive jealousy, future table longing, pet-dog self-accusation and the closing song all place the relationship outside ordinary friendship grammar.

Mutual canonical romance remains unestablished.

## “Takumi is only a casual womanizer who does not care about Hachi”
**Complicated.**

Evidence against the simple version:

- he keeps his promise;
- stays when he has time;
- eats Hachi's cooking;
- explains his work/love pattern;
- waits for her;
- repeatedly returns.

Evidence requiring caution:

- crude public sexual talk;
- undefined terms;
- poor communication;
- possessive language;
- work-first priorities;
- Hachi's own doubt.

The correct V6 status is ambiguity.

## “Nobu is the uncomplicated healthy alternative”
**Complicated.**

His sincerity is real.

So are jealousy, idealization and insecurity before Takumi.

## “Shin is so sexually sophisticated that he is simply autonomous”
**Substantially weakened.**

Agency does not erase age, commercialized sexuality or adult-client power.

## “707 is a stable refuge insulated from adult life”
**Weakened.**

Other intimate relationships now redistribute the household physically.

A home can be emotionally real and materially unstable.

# 21. Volume thesis and unresolved questions

Volume 6 begins with Tanabata.

Hachi has innumerable wishes.

Almost every major character has one:

- Hachi wants Takumi to return.
- Nana wants Ren and Hachi not to disappear.
- Ren wants Nana.
- Nobu wants Hachi.
- Shin wants to understand what love is.
- BLAST wants professional recognition.

Then the wishes begin to come true.

The story becomes less simple.

That is not cynicism.

It is adulthood.

A wish contains only an endpoint.

Life continues after the endpoint.

Takumi comes to Hachi, but Nana is hurt and Nobu loves her.

Nana gets Ren, but must negotiate BLAST, cohabitation, contraception and the fear of losing Hachi.

Nobu confesses, but Hachi's shame makes accepting sincere love frightening.

BLAST gains industry attention, but professional success will expose the band to the machinery Reira already finds exhausting.

The central question therefore changes from:

> What do I want?

to:

> **What kind of relationship with other people's freedom does my desire require?**

Nana gives the most mature answer in the volume.

She wants Hachi and Ren to be hers.

She knows they cannot be.

The impossibility makes her lonely.

She does not therefore acquire a right to control them.

Hachi's problem is different.

She has not yet learned to distinguish:

- being wanted from being chosen;
- being accepted from being safely known;
- sexual desire from relationship desire;
- patience from resignation;
- shame-free recognition from complicity.

Nobu's love makes her happier than Takumi's possessive language.

Yet Takumi feels safer to her in one crucial respect:

> he already knows the self she considers morally compromised.

The strongest compact thesis is therefore:

> **Volume 6 turns *NANA*'s problem of love, possession, dependence and home into explicit character philosophy. It argues that no beloved can become property, but knowing this does not abolish the desire to keep them. Nana responds to that contradiction with painful moral restraint. Hachi oscillates between the lover who idealizes her and the lover she experiences as an accomplice to the self she is ashamed of. Meanwhile 707 begins to empty, revealing that a home can remain emotionally central even while the people who created it are being pulled into other lives.**

### Open questions carried into Volume 7

1. Will Nana actually return to living at 707?
2. Can Nana preserve Hachi's freedom without converting private jealousy into resentment?
3. Will Ren's possessive longing remain merely a feeling?
4. What does Takumi mean by repeated returns and possession language?
5. Can Hachi ask Takumi for explicit relationship terms?
6. Is Hachi's patience **信頼** or **諦め**?
7. Can Hachi accept Nobu's love without preserving Nobu's idealized image of her?
8. How will Nobu behave after Takumi's direct intrusion into the triangle?
9. What do Shin and Reira actually want from one another beyond transaction?
10. How will Gaia Music's interest change BLAST?
11. How reliable is future Hachi's interpretation of Nana's song?
12. Why does future Hachi continue to wish for Nana across the 707 table?
13. What does “home” mean once the people most associated with it begin choosing other beds and other households?
