# 『NANA』Volume 1 Deep Dive
## Two lives before intersection: romance, self-authorship, departure, and the first meaning of Tokyo

> **Migration / Phase-2 provenance note:** This is the archival Markdown version of the original legacy analysis. The historical analytical body is preserved. During Phase 2, the governing Japanese EPUB was reopened and a deliberately limited set of load-bearing evidence IDs and verified spine-page locators was appended below. No printed page number has been inferred where it was not visibly present.

**Primary source:** 『NANA』Volume 1, original Japanese page images, read sequentially through the validated 191-page EPUB spine.

**Analytical boundary:** Volume 1 only. *NANA 7.8*, the anime, later manga developments, and retrospective series knowledge are not used to decide what these characters “really” are.

One terminology point: I will generally call **Komatsu Nana** by her actual name in this volume rather than “Hachi.” That later nickname is so culturally fused with the character that it is easy to let the future version of her colonize the young woman Yazawa first introduces.

---

# 1. Position in the larger narrative

Volume 1 does something structurally unusual and, I think, crucial.

**The two Nanas do not meet.**

Instead, Yazawa devotes the volume to two substantial origin narratives:

- **小松奈々 / Komatsu Nana**
- **大崎ナナ / Osaki Nana**

Only after we have watched each woman independently experience love, sex, work, friendship, separation, and the gravitational pull of Tokyo will the story permit them to enter one another's lives.

That is more than exposition.

It establishes a methodological instruction for reading the entire manga:

> **Neither Nana exists to complete the other.**

Each arrives at their eventual intersection carrying an already developed history, relational vocabulary, wound structure, conception of adulthood, and idea about what a future ought to look like.

Volume 1 is therefore best read as a **diptych**.

The two halves repeatedly rhyme without simply duplicating one another:

- both begin through first-person autobiographical narration;
- both locate the heroine geographically before psychologically;
- both are young women approaching independent adulthood;
- both work;
- both become entangled with men who leave for Tokyo;
- both experience love as something incapable of remaining outside material reality;
- both must decide whether following a man constitutes love, dependence, or surrender;
- both imagine their futures through a story larger than their present surroundings.

Yet their answers are strikingly different.

Komatsu Nana instinctively tries to make **love give life direction**.

Osaki Nana instinctively tries to ensure that **love does not take direction away from her**.

Neither position is presented as complete.

That incompleteness is the volume's foundation.

---

# 2. Narrative close read

## I. Komatsu Nana: an ordinary girl looking for an extraordinary story

The first Nana introduces herself with almost aggressive ordinariness.

She tells us that her hometown is surrounded by mountains, neither particularly rural nor urban, and has nothing especially worth sightseeing. She is the middle of three siblings, raised by parents who are neither rich nor poor and who more or less let her grow up on her own:

> 「金持ちでも貧乏でもない親に  
> ほったらかされてスクスクと育ち」

This is important.

Nothing in her childhood immediately explains her romantic behavior through catastrophe.

There is no great abandonment trauma disclosed here. No abuse. No destitution. No grand destiny.

She is **ordinary**.

And that ordinariness may itself help explain why romantic imagination becomes so powerful for her.

Komatsu Nana lives in a world that has not yet supplied her with an obvious exceptional identity. Romance can do that. To be dramatically loved is to become the protagonist of something.

### Asano: romance as catastrophe because romance has become cosmology

We encounter her already involved with Asano, an older married man.

Then comes one of Volume 1's most revealing formal gestures.

Asano tells her he is going to Tokyo.

Yazawa gives the word enormous visual space:

> **東京？**

The landscape is snowy and nearly empty. One geographical noun becomes the emotional event.

Asano is being transferred. The relationship effectively ends.

His parting formulation is devastating precisely because it is so moderate:

> 「今まで楽しかったよ  
> ありがとう」

“It was fun. Thank you.”

For him, this can be spoken as a concluded episode.

For Nana, it feels like apocalypse.

Her narration invokes the famous 1999 Nostradamus “Great King of Terror” prophecy:

> 「恐怖の大王があたしの頭上めがけて落ちて来た」

The apocalypse has arrived several months early and landed directly on her.

The joke is funny because Nana is melodramatic.

But Yazawa does not therefore make the feeling fake.

Nana knows perfectly well:

> 「この恋に未来なんかない」

This love had no future.

Her emotional devastation does not arise because she literally believed Asano would divorce his wife and build a life with her. It comes from something subtler: **knowing intellectually that a relationship has no future does not stop a person from organizing her present around it.**

That distinction matters.

Komatsu Nana's problem is not simple ignorance.

She often understands a great deal *after* the fact and sometimes even during it.

Her difficulty is making understanding govern desire.

---

## II. Junko as the first counter-narrator

Junko is immediately more important than “the sensible best friend.”

She functions as an internal corrective to Nana's tendency to narrativize herself.

Nana produces stories:

- this man likes me;
- this encounter might mean something;
- perhaps this is fate;
- perhaps romance will finally work correctly.

Junko continually asks the boring but necessary questions:

- What actually happened?
- What did he say?
- What are you assuming?
- What are you doing?
- What happens next?

This gives the first half a productive double consciousness.

We are not forced to choose between:

> Nana is a hopeless romantic.

and:

> Nana is an emotionally perceptive young woman.

She is both.

She often sees people quite well. She simply becomes much less reliable when desire gives her an interpretation she prefers.

Junko therefore performs a role that will be worth tracking:

> **She separates feeling from evidence.**

That is one of the earliest ethical disciplines in *NANA*.

---

## III. Shoji: from the possibility of friendship to romantic adulthood

Shoji enters through Nana's wider friendship network, and their relationship initially allows the manga to explore whether men and women can be friends without desire rewriting the arrangement.

This is important because Nana's history makes male attention difficult for her to interpret neutrally.

Shoji is different from Asano.

The relationship is far more reciprocal.

They talk, argue, spend time together, become genuinely emotionally important to one another, and eventually cross from friendship into romance and sex.

But the manga does not present this as:

> Nana finally finds the correct boyfriend, therefore the problem is solved.

Instead, it immediately asks what becoming a couple materially means.

Shoji is headed to Tokyo.

Nana remains behind.

The romantic achievement produces a logistical problem.

### Sex and “becoming a woman”

Nana's retrospective narration after their first sexual relationship is revealing:

> 「幸せな恋がしたかった」  
> “I wanted a happy love.”

She wanted something:

> 「映画みたいに  
> ロマンチックで  
> ドラマチックな恋」

romantic and dramatic, like a movie.

Then:

> 「十九の夏  
> 初めて女になって」

“In the summer when I was nineteen, I became a woman for the first time…”

The phrase itself is worth tracking. Sex is linguistically framed through a conventional coming-of-age formula: sexual initiation equals “becoming a woman.”

But Nana immediately destabilizes the supposed lesson.

She says she thought she had learned that men were not something so sweet:

> 「はずだったのだが」

—or so she thought.

This tiny retrospective turn is characteristic.

The older narrating consciousness can see the irony that the experiencing Nana cannot.

Sex has not magically given her adult wisdom.

She has acquired experience without acquiring immunity.

That distinction is one of the volume's better observations about adulthood.

---

## IV. Sweetness immediately undermined

One of the best page-level juxtapositions in Komatsu Nana's section occurs around her consummation with Shoji.

Nana describes herself as almost melting in the excessively sweet taste of happiness:

> 「甘ったるい幸福の味で  
> 体中がとろけそうだ」

And almost alongside this emotional completion, Junko worries about whether the two of them will actually be all right.

Then Yazawa brutally reduces the problem to a short phrase occupying the page:

> **でも 遠恋**

“But—long-distance love.”

The effect is almost comic editing.

Romance announces:

> We have arrived.

Material reality says:

> No, now the difficult part begins.

This is going to be one of the most important structural instincts of *NANA* if Volume 1 is representative:

> **The manga repeatedly allows characters to achieve the emotional moment they wanted, then asks what Tuesday morning looks like.**

That is a very different romantic sensibility from stories where confession or sexual consummation functions as resolution.

---

## V. Nana refuses to simply become Shoji's dependent

There is already evidence in Volume 1 against reading Komatsu Nana as nothing more than helpless romantic dependency.

She wants to go to Tokyo.

Shoji is there.

But she does not simply get on a train because her boyfriend exists at the destination.

She works.

She saves.

She wants enough practical footing that going to Tokyo will also be **her movement**, not merely being absorbed into his life.

That impulse is not as developed as Osaki Nana's artistic independence, but it matters.

Komatsu Nana contains both:

> **I want to be with you.**

and:

> **I do not want my existence there to be only your responsibility.**

The tension between those desires is already more interesting than “dependent versus independent.”

---

## VI. The false security of romantic completion

The end of Komatsu Nana's half makes another intelligent choice.

She is working at a video rental store. An attractive older coworker, Nakamura, enters her awareness. She wonders about his interest.

Then she checks herself.

Then she calls Shoji and cheerfully announces that she is doing great:

> 「あ 章司？  
> うん！ 超元気！」

This is not necessarily evidence that she is about to betray Shoji.

It is more revealing than that.

Her cognitive pattern remains intact.

Male attention still produces possibility.

Romance with Shoji has not cured the mechanism by which Nana uses being noticed to generate a potential narrative.

Volume 1 therefore closes her origin without pretending that love has solved the personality that entered love.

---

# 3. Osaki Nana: a girl who already knows what it costs to need someone

The transition to Osaki Nana is one of the most effective structural contrasts in the volume.

Komatsu Nana begins:

> ordinary hometown; ordinary family; ordinary schooling.

Osaki Nana begins:

> 「あたしは自分の生まれ故郷を知らない」

“I don't know my birthplace.”

She has never seen her father's face.

She can no longer remember her mother's.

At four years old she came to the seaside town where her grandmother raised her.

The parallel construction matters enormously.

Both women narrate themselves through place and family.

One says, essentially:

> Nothing particularly unusual happened to me.

The other says:

> I don't even possess the basic coordinates with which most people begin a life story.

That is the first fundamental asymmetry between them.

---

## I. “Polishing fragments of a dream”

Osaki Nana describes her present life through work:

> 「バイトに明け暮れながら  
> 夢のかけらを磨いている」

While spending her days working part-time, she is “polishing fragments of a dream.”

Komatsu Nana's imagination is predominantly romantic.

Osaki Nana's is already vocational.

And Yazawa's first major visual introduction of her is not domestic.

It is **performance**.

Microphone.

Crowd.

Stage.

Black clothing.

Voice.

> 「今晩は ブラストです！」

“Good evening. We're BLAST!”

The character is presented first as someone who **makes herself publicly audible**.

That is a striking contrast with a childhood in which she has almost no reliable inherited identity.

If family cannot tell Nana who she is, performance can become a method of authorship.

---

# 4. Nana, Ren, and the home that is also a band

By the time we enter Osaki Nana's story, her relationship with Ren is already deeply developed.

They are:

- lovers;
- sexual partners;
- cohabitants;
- bandmates;
- members of the same social world.

That multidimensionality matters.

The relationship cannot be cleanly separated into “romance” and “career,” because their domestic life grew inside the same musical community.

Their home visually advertises them together:

> **REN・NANA**

Names become architecture.

Boots, bed, bath, cigarettes, clothing, instruments and rehearsals all exist in one shared ecosystem.

So when Ren eventually leaves, he is not merely ending—or suspending—a romance.

His departure destabilizes simultaneously:

> **lover + home + band + future.**

This is why reducing Nana's reaction to “girlfriend is sad boyfriend is moving” would miss most of what the chapter has constructed.

---

# 5. Nana and Ren's language: intimacy without politeness

Their bath conversation is one of the first strong demonstrations of Osaki Nana's voice.

Ren remembers what she wore when they met.

He teases her about her former reputation.

Nana fires back:

> 「やってねえって！  
> 濡れ衣だよ！」

“I didn't do it! That's a false accusation!”

And then, with complete sexual directness:

> 「あたしの処女はてめえが奪ったんだろ！」

“You're the one who took my virginity, asshole!”

The important thing is not merely that she “swears.”

Her voice combines:

- あたし as first-person pronoun;
- rough contraction;
- てめえ;
- direct sexual vocabulary;
- confrontational sentence endings;
- complete lack of demure euphemism.

Yet this is taking place inside affectionate banter.

So roughness ≠ absence of tenderness.

That distinction should remain central to her voice dossier.

### A provisional linguistic contrast

**Komatsu Nana** performs emotion outwardly.

Her speech and narration expand feelings into stories.

**Osaki Nana** often compresses vulnerability into roughness.

Her speech converts exposure into attack, joke, or challenge.

That is only a Volume 1 provisional model, but the contrast is already pronounced.

---

# 6. Money is already inside Nana and Ren's romance

The same domestic conversation also gives a seemingly small detail that matters a great deal.

Nana explains why she kept working despite unpleasant treatment:

> 「金が欲しかった」

She wanted money.

Her grandmother gave her essentially none.

This is an important difference from Komatsu Nana.

Komatsu's movement toward economic independence is developmental.

Osaki's has been closer to necessity.

She has already learned that money means:

- freedom of movement;
- distance from family authority;
- ability to sustain herself;
- capacity to pursue music.

So when Ren's career opportunity appears, “Why doesn't she just go with him?” is not merely a romantic question.

For Nana, following someone risks becoming materially and professionally legible as:

> **his woman.**

And that is a category she instinctively resists.

---

# 7. Reproduction enters before the manga becomes “adult”

The volume also gives Nana a remarkably direct line during sexual/domestic banter:

> 「今は子育てよりライブがしたい」

“Right now I'd rather play live than raise children.”

The immediate scene has comic energy, but the statement should not be discarded as a joke.

It establishes very early that Nana thinks about sex alongside consequences.

Pregnancy and childcare represent competing demands on the body and future of a working female musician.

And Nana's current answer is extremely clear:

> **music first.**

This is one of our earliest pieces of evidence for how she understands adulthood.

She does not reject intimacy.

She rejects the assumption that intimacy should automatically reorganize her life around domestic reproduction.

---

# 8. Ren goes to Tokyo

Then the entire structure breaks.

Ren tells her:

> 「おれ 東京行くから」

“I'm going to Tokyo.”

Again, Yazawa uses enormous negative space.

Again, **Tokyo** is not initially drawn as glittering aspiration.

It arrives as a sentence that removes someone.

And Ren's follow-up is perhaps even more important:

> 「おまえは おまえの好きに生きりゃいいさ」

“You should live however you want.”

That sentence deserves to remain unresolved.

It can mean:

> I love you enough not to control you.

It can also sound like:

> I am not asking you to come.

It can mean:

> Your dream matters independently of mine.

It can also feel like:

> I have already constructed my future without requiring your presence in it.

This is the first major instance in *NANA* where **freedom and abandonment become linguistically adjacent**.

Ren grants Nana autonomy.

Nana experiences the loss that autonomy does not repair.

That is much more interesting than deciding that either person is simply correct.

---

# 9. Ren does not merely leave Nana; he leaves BLAST

The supporting men sharpen the ambiguity.

### Nobu

Nobu experiences Ren's decision emotionally.

He wants the band preserved.

He worries about Nana.

He asks the obvious brutal question:

> Is Ren abandoning Nana?

Nobu interprets love communally.

The band matters because the people matter.

Its dissolution feels like relational betrayal.

### Yasu

Yasu is much more structurally minded.

He understands:

- careers;
- opportunity;
- professionalism;
- practical consequences;
- the danger of forcing Nana to organize her life around Ren.

His position implicitly distinguishes:

> **loving someone**

from

> **being entitled to stop their professional movement.**

Neither man's interpretation completely settles Ren.

Instead, Yazawa triangulates the problem.

Ren's departure can simultaneously be:

- artistically rational;
- professionally necessary;
- nonpossessive toward Nana;
- devastating to Nana;
- destructive to BLAST;
- experienced by Nobu as betrayal.

The story does not need one of these descriptions to cancel the others.

---

# 10. Nana refuses to follow

This is the defining decision of Osaki Nana's Volume 1 arc.

She loves Ren.

The chapter leaves very little doubt about that.

But she does not immediately relocate as his appendage.

Instead she continues singing.

This is genuine independence.

It is also, potentially, defensive.

Those possibilities should remain separate.

### Established

Nana wants a musical identity of her own.

### Strongly supported

She refuses to define success as merely staying physically beside Ren.

### Plausible but not yet proven

Part of that independence may also protect her from having to say something much more vulnerable:

> **Choose me.**

That distinction will be important.

Self-sufficiency can be both virtue and armor.

Volume 1 is not yet entitled to tell us which predominates.

---

# 11. “We were over”

The later narration reduces Nana and Ren's rupture to an extraordinarily simple statement:

> 「あたし達は終わった」

“We were over.”

The finality is important because what we have actually watched is much messier than that sentence.

Love has not obviously evaporated.

Shared history has not disappeared.

Nana has not discovered that Ren was secretly meaningless.

Instead, **circumstance has made the relationship's previous form impossible**.

This is one of the volume's most adult observations.

A relationship can end without its emotional truth becoming false.

The past does not have to be exposed as illusion in order for the future to become impossible.

---

# 12. Nana's answer is performance

After losing the shared Ren/Nana/BLAST structure, Nana does not immediately replace it with another relationship.

She sings.

She rehearses.

She participates in rebuilding musical life.

Eventually, Tokyo begins drawing her as well—but importantly, **not simply because she has surrendered and followed Ren.**

Her trajectory toward Tokyo increasingly belongs to her own musical future.

This makes the two Nana halves rhyme beautifully.

Komatsu Nana says, roughly:

> I want to be with the man I love, but I need to arrive on my own feet.

Osaki Nana says:

> I love this man, but if I go to Tokyo, I cannot go merely as his woman.

Different psychological structures.

Similar problem:

> **How do you move toward someone without disappearing into them?**

---

# 13. Character state: Komatsu Nana

Volume 1 establishes Komatsu Nana as much more complicated than “boy-crazy.”

## What she wants

Most obviously, she wants:

- romantic love;
- emotional excitement;
- to be chosen;
- a happy relationship;
- eventually proximity to Shoji.

But beneath this is a broader desire:

> **She wants her life to feel meaningful in narrative form.**

Romance gives ordinary experience plot.

A glance can become possibility.

An encounter can become destiny.

A boyfriend can make the future feel organized.

## What she fears

At this stage:

- being left;
- romance ending abruptly;
- being unwanted;
- being alone while the person she loves continues elsewhere;
- perhaps being a burden.

## What she does not yet understand

She repeatedly mistakes:

> **intensity**

for:

> **stability.**

A feeling being real does not mean the relationship has infrastructure.

That is exactly what Asano demonstrates.

And long-distance Shoji immediately begins teaching the same lesson under less exploitative circumstances.

## Her contradiction

She is simultaneously:

- emotionally dependent;
- romantically idealizing;
- impulsive;

and:

- proud;
- capable of work;
- self-aware;
- unwilling simply to have Shoji finance her migration.

That contradiction is not a flaw in the writing.

It is the character.

---

# 14. Character state: Osaki Nana

## What she wants

Nana wants:

- Ren;
- music;
- a band;
- adulthood on her own terms;
- enough economic independence to pursue it;
- recognition through artistic identity rather than through another person.

## What she fears

The volume does not give us a clinical confession, but behavior strongly supports fear of:

- abandonment;
- dependency;
- being reduced to somebody else's attachment;
- losing the artistic self she has built.

## Her defensive strategy

Where Komatsu Nana creates stories, Osaki Nana creates hardness.

She uses:

- rough language;
- sexual candor;
- humor;
- defiance;
- work;
- aesthetic control;
- artistic ambition.

These are genuine parts of her.

They may also protect a much less secure attachment structure underneath.

## Her contradiction

Her central contradiction is already visible:

> **She wants Ren deeply enough that his departure tears apart her world, but she wants to remain herself deeply enough that she refuses to solve the pain by simply following him.**

That is far more interesting than “independent punk girl.”

---

# 15. Other major characters

## Junko — affection without indulgence

Junko's importance is epistemic.

She loves Nana but does not validate every story Nana tells herself.

She represents the proposition:

> Friendship does not require agreeing with your friend's interpretation.

This gives her care an unusually adult quality.

She can be sharp precisely because she treats Nana as someone capable of hearing unwelcome reality.

## Shoji — reciprocity does not abolish logistical reality

Shoji is an important correction after Asano because his relationship with Nana is much more reciprocal.

But Yazawa refuses to let “better boyfriend” become “problem solved.”

Tokyo still exists.

Money still exists.

Distance still exists.

Sex still creates vulnerability.

Romantic commitment still has to survive ordinary time.

## Asano — different stakes inside the same relationship

Asano's role is short but foundational.

The relationship matters much more to Nana than to him.

His polite closure—essentially “I had fun, thank you”—makes the asymmetry devastatingly clear.

It also raises an ethical issue that should remain visible: Nana is a teenage schoolgirl involved with an older married adult.

Her agency is real.

That does not make the asymmetry disappear.

## Ren — autonomy without reassurance

Ren is not presented as simply cruel.

His departure makes professional sense.

His refusal to command Nana to follow can be read as respect.

But he also speaks from a position where he has already decided his own movement.

The burden of interpreting what that means for “us” falls largely on Nana.

His distinctive relational problem in Volume 1 may therefore be:

> **He refuses possession more easily than he communicates reassurance.**

Provisional, but worth tracking.

## Yasu — material realism

Yasu already occupies the position of the man most capable of separating:

- emotional desire;
- professional reality;
- immediate hurt;
- long-term consequences.

His calmness should not be mistaken for lack of care.

It is a different care language.

## Nobu — loyalty as emotional continuity

Nobu wants the band and its relationships preserved.

His pain over Ren's departure is almost the inverse of Yasu's acceptance.

Where Yasu asks:

> What can realistically continue?

Nobu asks:

> Why should people who love one another permit this to end?

That distinction will be useful.

# 16. Relationship-state analysis

## Komatsu Nana / Asano

**State:** asymmetrical affair → abrupt termination.

What Nana receives:

- romantic excitement;
- adult attention;
- the experience of being chosen.

What she does not receive:

- future;
- equal structural power;
- integration into his life.

The relationship establishes one of Volume 1's first distinctions:

> **being desired is not the same thing as being given a place in someone's future.**

## Komatsu Nana / Shoji

**State:** friendship → mutual attachment → sexual/romantic relationship → long-distance test.

The critical difference from Asano is reciprocity.

The critical continuity is Nana's tendency to make romance carry extraordinary expectations.

Shoji may be a better relationship.

Nana is still Nana.

## Osaki Nana / Ren

**State:** lovers + cohabitants + bandmates → professional divergence → separation.

This relationship already contains:

- love;
- sexual intimacy;
- teasing;
- domestic routine;
- artistic collaboration;
- shared identity;
- professional inequality/opportunity;
- competing futures.

Neither “true love” nor “toxic relationship” is an adequate Volume 1 description.

The relationship's primary problem is **integration**:

> Can two ambitious people remain one domestic pair when their professional futures cease to point in the same direction?

Volume 1's answer is:

> not in the form they currently possess.

## Nana / BLAST

This is already a relationship structure, not just employment.

BLAST supplies:

- social identity;
- artistic authorship;
- friendship;
- a future;
- a community not inherited from family.

Ren leaving therefore destabilizes Nana's chosen family as well as romance.

# 17. Japanese voice and language

The linguistic differentiation is already unusually strong.

## Komatsu Nana

First person:

> **あたし**

Her register is:

- contemporary casual;
- emotionally open;
- hyperbolic;
- self-dramatizing;
- frequently comic;
- highly relational.

Her narration loves narrative escalation.

A boyfriend leaving becomes Nostradamus.

Romantic fulfillment becomes body-melting sweetness.

The crucial nuance is that retrospective Nana can mock present Nana without completely disowning her.

Forms such as:

> 「はずだったのだが」

create distance between:

**girl living the scene**  
and  
**woman narrating the girl.**

That distinction should be tracked aggressively.

## Osaki Nana

Also:

> **あたし**

But the shared pronoun masks major stylistic difference.

Her voice is:

- clipped;
- rough;
- confrontational;
- sexually explicit;
- frequently masculine-coded in lexical choice;
- direct.

Forms like:

> てめえ  
> ～だろ  
> やってねえ

matter.

But I would **not** summarize this as “she talks like a man.”

Her visual presentation is intensely stylized and feminine on her own terms.

The more interesting effect is:

> **Nana decouples femininity from linguistic demureness.**

Her clothing, makeup, body, sexuality and speech all belong to the same authored persona, but they do not obey a conventional package of “feminine” behavioral expectations.

## Ren

His speech is casual masculine Japanese:

> おれ  
> おまえ

with comparatively direct declarative syntax.

The line:

> 「おまえは おまえの好きに生きりゃいいさ」

is almost a manifesto of his relational posture.

It gives autonomy without producing emotional security.

## Yasu

Provisional profile:

- measured;
- practical;
- less reactive;
- structurally minded.

His language tends to reduce emotional crisis to the decisions actually available.

## Nobu

More affectively transparent.

His speech makes loyalty audible.

He is quicker to say the painful interpretation everyone else may be avoiding.

# 18. Visual and formal analysis

Volume 1's visual construction may be as important as its plot.

## I. The autobiographical openings

Both Nana sections open through a combination of:

- place;
- first-person narration;
- nearly documentary geography.

Before romance or fashion tells us who they are, **location does**.

This matters because Tokyo will soon become the force that destabilizes those locations.

## II. The two visual vocabularies

### Komatsu Nana

Her pages favor:

- floral tones;
- cute clothing;
- busy social spaces;
- comedic distortions;
- expressive faces;
- shopping and dating environments;
- romantic iconography.

Her life is visually porous.

Other people are constantly getting into the frame.

### Osaki Nana

Her pages lean much harder on:

- black;
- negative space;
- live-house darkness;
- microphone and stage geometry;
- leather;
- plaid;
- boots;
- cigarettes;
- industrial/domestic punk spaces.

The stylistic darkness is not merely fashion branding.

It gives absence physical space.

---

# 19. Snow and separation

Both halves repeatedly place major relational rupture inside snow.

This is worth tracking.

Snow produces an interesting emotional effect:

- visually beautiful;
- acoustically imagined as quiet;
- physically cold;
- spatially empty.

The breakup does not look ugly.

It looks almost sacred.

That prevents the manga from making emotional pain aesthetically chaotic.

Instead, departure becomes something characters can later remember beautifully.

This may be one of the first mechanisms by which *NANA* generates what we have called **anticipatory nostalgia**:

> the present already looks like a memory while it is happening.

That interpretation is provisional, but Volume 1 gives real evidence for it.

---

# 20. Tokyo: first as separation, only later as destination

This may be Volume 1's most important recurring word.

Before either heroine's adult Tokyo story begins, **Tokyo takes people away**.

For Komatsu Nana:

- Asano → Tokyo.
- Shoji → Tokyo.

For Osaki Nana:

- Ren → Tokyo.

That repetition is too strong to treat as incidental.

Tokyo is initially not:

> freedom!

or:

> glamorous adulthood!

It is a force that reorganizes relationships.

Geography becomes destiny because modern adulthood distributes people according to:

- work;
- education;
- music;
- opportunity.

One provisional series-level question emerges:

> **What happens when love wants permanence but adulthood is geographically mobile?**

Volume 1 does not answer it.

It makes it unavoidable.

---

# 21. Home

The contrast is equally strong here.

## Komatsu Nana

She possesses a conventional family home.

Yet that home is strangely narratively unimportant.

Her imagination is oriented outward toward romantic attachment.

She has a house before she has a psychologically compelling sense of **home**.

## Osaki Nana

Her inherited family structure is fractured.

But she and Ren have made an intensely legible shared space.

Their names are literally displayed together.

Domestic routine and musical life overlap.

Paradoxically:

> the woman with the weaker family origin has created the more emotionally charged home.

And therefore has more to lose when Ren leaves.

This distinction between:

**house**  
and  
**home**

is already worth adding to the longitudinal ledger.

---

# 22. Material and social adulthood

Volume 1 makes adulthood concrete remarkably early.

The key mechanisms are not taxes or corporate employment.

They are:

- moving;
- saving;
- part-time work;
- rent/living arrangements;
- career decisions;
- school;
- transportation;
- sex;
- pregnancy possibility;
- childcare;
- professional music.

Both Nanas discover the same fact by different routes:

> **Love exists inside logistics.**

You can genuinely love someone and still have to decide:

- where to live;
- who pays;
- whose career moves;
- who follows;
- what happens to your own work.

That may be the major reason the manga already feels “adult” despite its shōjo demographic location.

Its adulthood is not simply sexual explicitness.

It is **consequence architecture**.

---

# 23. Gender and young adulthood

Volume 1 also quietly establishes a gendered mobility problem.

Men repeatedly receive external reasons to move:

- transfer;
- school;
- professional debut.

The women then confront a secondary question:

> Do I follow?

But both heroines resist making that the only formulation.

Komatsu Nana:

> I need money and footing of my own.

Osaki Nana:

> I need an artistic reason and identity of my own.

That does **not** mean they reject love.

It means love cannot automatically legitimize self-erasure.

At the same time, the manga does not romanticize independence as painless.

Osaki Nana's refusal to follow Ren hurts enormously.

Autonomy has cost.

That complication is essential.

---

# 24. Fashion as character authorship

The two Nanas already occupy radically different visual femininities.

## Komatsu Nana

Her fashion communicates:

- cuteness;
- romantic possibility;
- conventional young femininity;
- adaptation to context;
- pleasure in clothes themselves.

She dresses partly to participate in the social world.

## Osaki Nana

Her fashion operates more like a declaration.

Leather, plaid, jewelry, boots, dark cosmetics and punk references create a coherent authored self.

Her appearance says:

> **I have already decided how I will be seen.**

Given her weak inherited family identity, that is psychologically significant.

Fashion is not superficial ornament.

It is one of the ways she gives herself continuity.

---

# 25. Ethical and psychological questions

## I. The Asano affair

The volume allows Komatsu Nana agency.

She desires him.

She participates.

She romanticizes the affair.

That does not erase the ethical asymmetry:

- he is older;
- married;
- she is still a schoolgirl;
- he has an established adult life into which she is not integrated.

His departure reveals the difference in stakes.

Understanding why Nana entered the relationship should not become acquittal of the adult's responsibility.

## II. Is Komatsu Nana simply immature?

No.

Immaturity is part of her characterization, but it is insufficient.

She already knows:

- some relationships have no future;
- men can disappoint;
- sex does not guarantee permanence;
- money matters;
- she should not simply become Shoji's financial burden.

Her problem is not lack of information.

It is the gap between:

> **what she knows**

and

> **what she wants badly enough to believe anyway.**

That is a much more durable psychological problem.

## III. Is Osaki Nana simply independent?

Also no.

Her independence is genuine.

But genuine virtues can become defensive.

The manga gives us enough evidence to ask, though not yet conclusively answer:

> Does Nana preserve autonomy partly because openly needing Ren would give him power to abandon her again?

That remains a **plausible inference**, not established fact.

## IV. Is Ren abandoning Nana?

Volume 1 deliberately refuses a simple verdict.

Nobu says, effectively, yes.

Ren's behavior can be framed as:

- professional advancement;
- respect for Nana's freedom;
- refusal to demand sacrifice.

Nana's pain makes it equally clear that:

> not controlling someone does not automatically mean you have cared adequately for the relationship.

This is a sophisticated distinction.

**Non-possession is not the whole of love.**

---

# 26. Narration, time and memory

Both halves are already being remembered.

That matters.

We are not simply watching nineteen- or twenty-year-old consciousness unfold in real time.

We are hearing a narrator who has acquired enough distance to say things like:

> I thought I had learned.

or:

> we were over.

This produces an immediate split:

**experienced present**  
versus  
**remembered present**.

And because the manga repeatedly gives major events unusually beautiful page compositions—snow, empty space, silhouettes, trains, sea—the past can become aesthetically precious even when it was miserable.

That gives Volume 1 a strange temporal texture:

> **The characters are young enough to believe everything is beginning, while the narration already knows that beginnings become memories.**

That may become one of the work's central formal signatures.

# 27. Motif and vocabulary ledger

These are **tracking candidates**, not fully interpreted symbols.

### 東京 / Tokyo

Already extremely important.

Current function:

> opportunity elsewhere → separation → possible future destination.

### 恋 / love-romance

Especially strong in Komatsu Nana's self-narration.

Often carries cinematic or narrative expectations.

### 幸せ / happiness

Komatsu Nana explicitly imagines romantic happiness as something sensorially “sweet.”

We should watch whether happiness later becomes:

- romance;
- stability;
- home;
- career;
- family;
- memory.

### 夢 / dream

Most strikingly explicit in Osaki Nana:

> 「夢のかけら」

fragments of a dream.

Her dream has vocational form.

### Home / house

Not yet heavily lexicalized enough for conclusions, but spatial contrast is strong.

### Snow

Currently associated with relational rupture and memory.

### Trains / stations / roads

Mobility separates before it reunites.

### Clothing

Used not just decoratively but autobiographically. Ren remembers Nana through the red dress she wore when they met.

At Volume 1 alone this has **no demonstrated symbolic meaning**.

I am logging it and nothing more.

That is precisely the sort of recurrence where later material should determine whether interpretation becomes justified.

# 28. Epistemic ledger after Volume 1

## A — directly established

- Komatsu Nana comes from an ordinary provincial family and regards her upbringing as largely uneventful.
- She becomes involved with the married Asano, whose transfer to Tokyo ends their relationship.
- She later develops a reciprocal romantic and sexual relationship with Shoji.
- Shoji relocates to Tokyo, creating a long-distance relationship.
- Nana works and saves rather than simply relocating as Shoji's dependent.
- Osaki Nana does not know her birthplace, never knew her father, and was abandoned by her mother at a very young age.
- She was raised by her grandmother and worked for money because she had little financial support.
- She is BLAST's vocalist.
- She and Ren are lovers, cohabitants and bandmates.
- Ren chooses a professional opportunity in Tokyo.
- Nana does not simply follow him.
- She continues pursuing music.
- Nana explicitly prioritizes live performance over raising children at this point in her life.

## B — strongly established

- Komatsu Nana habitually turns male attention into romantic possibility.
- Romance provides her with a powerful source of self-narration and future direction.
- Osaki Nana's artistic identity is not subordinate to her romantic relationship.
- Ren's departure damages Nana simultaneously as lover, cohabitant and musician.
- Tokyo functions throughout the volume as a force reorganizing relationships.

## C — strongly implied

- Osaki Nana's aggressive self-sufficiency contains a defensive dimension as well as genuine autonomy.
- Komatsu Nana worries, at least implicitly, about becoming merely a burden to Shoji.
- The two Nana narratives are intentionally constructed as thematic mirrors before the women meet.

## D — plausible inference

- Komatsu Nana's hunger for romantic narrative partly compensates for an otherwise extremely ordinary sense of self.
- Osaki Nana's strong self-authorship partly responds to the lack of stable identity supplied by family.
- Ren's respect for Nana's autonomy is emotionally inadequate in ways he may not recognize.

## E — speculation, to be withheld

- Any final judgment about Nana/Ren.
- Any claim about what the number 707 ultimately signifies.
- Any later romantic or queer reading involving the two Nanas.
- Any conclusion about the ultimate meaning of Tokyo.
- Any claim that either heroine has already “learned her lesson.”

Volume 1 does not earn those conclusions yet.

# 29. Baseline character dossiers

## Komatsu Nana — Volume 1 state

**Core desire:** romantic happiness and a future that feels emotionally meaningful.  
**Primary vulnerability:** being chosen too easily becomes evidence of destiny.  
**Strengths:** warmth, sociability, emotional openness, work capacity, genuine self-reflection.  
**Defensive tendency:** narrativization—turn uncomfortable uncertainty into a romance story.  
**Relationship to money:** newly learning that economic footing permits relational dignity.  
**Relationship to home:** conventional family home exists, but emotional imagination points outward.  
**Voice:** exuberant, colloquial, hyperbolic, retrospectively self-ironic.  
**Major contradiction:** craves dependence while resisting becoming a burden.

## Osaki Nana — Volume 1 state

**Core desire:** music, Ren, and an adult identity in which neither annihilates the other.  
**Primary vulnerability:** loss/abandonment.  
**Strengths:** determination, work ethic, artistic commitment, self-authorship, directness.  
**Defensive tendency:** hardness—rough speech, pride, independence, refusal to ask too openly.  
**Relationship to money:** necessity and autonomy.  
**Relationship to home:** chosen domesticity with Ren carries enormous emotional weight.  
**Voice:** rough, direct, sexually candid, stylistically controlled.  
**Major contradiction:** needs intensely while insisting on the right not to be defined by what she needs.

# 30. Cumulative revision

Because this is Volume 1, there is nothing yet to revise.

Instead, it establishes the baselines against which later change should be measured.

Most importantly, we should resist two simplifications from the outset:

> **Komatsu Nana = dependent.**  
> **Osaki Nana = independent.**

Volume 1 itself makes both inadequate.

Komatsu Nana already wants enough economic autonomy to arrive in Tokyo on her own footing.

Osaki Nana's independence is inseparable from the fact that losing Ren devastates her.

The much more useful continuum is:

> **How does each woman negotiate the conflict between attachment and self-authorship?**

That question is already alive before they ever enter the same room.

---

# 31. Volume thesis

Volume 1 is fundamentally about **departure before encounter**.

Three men leave for Tokyo, and each departure forces a young woman to discover that love does not answer the question of how a life should be organized.

Komatsu Nana approaches adulthood believing that the right romance might give her life the drama, happiness and direction she wants. Experience repeatedly shows her that being loved, being desired, and being given a durable place in someone's future are different things.

Osaki Nana has already built a powerful authored identity around music, style and independence. Ren's departure reveals the price of that identity: she can refuse to become an accessory to the man she loves, but autonomy does not protect her from the pain of losing the home they made together.

The two origin stories therefore arrive at complementary problems:

> **Komatsu Nana must learn how to love without asking love to author her entire life.**

> **Osaki Nana must learn whether preserving authorship requires refusing to admit how deeply another person has become part of her life.**

And looming over both is Tokyo.

Not yet as dream city.

Not yet as home.

First, simply as the place people go when the life they are building can no longer remain where it began.

That is an exceptionally strong foundation for the eventual meeting of the two Nanas, because Yazawa has already made clear that when they finally encounter one another, neither will be an empty half waiting to be completed. Each will arrive with a different answer to the same unresolved question:

> **How can I belong to someone without ceasing to belong to myself?**

That, rather than romance alone or music alone, is the principal problem I would carry forward from Volume 1 into Volume 2.
