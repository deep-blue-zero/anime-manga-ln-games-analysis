# 『NANA』Volume 10 Deep Reading
## When private life becomes public story: heroes, heroines, scandal, and the marriage Hachi chooses

**Canonical artifact:** `NANA_V10_DEEP_READING.md`  
**Cumulative scope:** Volumes 1–10 only.  
**Spoiler boundary:** No Volume 11+ material, anime material, later paratext, or remembered later-series outcome is used to settle unresolved questions here.  
**Primary-source policy:** the rendered Japanese manga page is the governing evidence; dialogue, composition, gesture, page turn, silence, typography, and visual juxtaposition are read together.

---

## 1. Central thesis and volume role

Volume 9 asked who gets to author intimacy once intimacy becomes **administrative, evidentiary, and public**. Volume 10 makes that problem explicit by turning almost every major relationship into a **story somebody is telling**.

The volume repeatedly uses the vocabulary of narrative itself:

- **物語 / monogatari** — story;
- **ヒーロー** — hero;
- **ヒロイン** — heroine;
- media narratives that turn partial facts into legible characters;
- Hachi and Nobu imagining whether they still exist inside one another's stories;
- Nana explicitly planning how BLAST can keep appearing in **「ハチの物語」**;
- and future Nana correcting the role she once assigned herself.

The strongest structural movement is this:

> present Nana: **「『ハチ物語』のヒーローは あたしだ」**  
> future Nana: **「あたしはもう あんたの物語のヒーローにはなれないけど」**  
> future Nana: **「今もあたしの物語のヒロインの名前は奈々」**

In the present, Nana imagines herself as the heroic agent who will reclaim Hachi by becoming so dazzling that Hachi must return. In the future narration, she relinquishes that fantasy of being Hachi's hero while preserving something even more intimate: Hachi—Komatsu Nana—remains the heroine of **Nana Osaki's** story.

That reversal is Volume 10's deepest correction.

It tells us that **love is not the same thing as authorship over another person's life**.

The opening page already prepares this conclusion. The narration rejects the cliché that people who fight are necessarily close. A quarrel is ultimately an ego collision; exposing one's true feelings does not guarantee mutual understanding. Being wounded may be unavoidable, but one must at least try not to wound the people around oneself.

That ethical proposition governs the entire volume.

Nana loves Hachi enormously. She becomes desperate to sing for her, publicly calls to her through television, makes BLAST's success part of a plan to become Hachi's hero, and later says that the bond she formed with Hachi grew stronger than her bond with Ren. Yet the volume also has Nana confess that she was so fixated on **showing** Hachi her singing that she did not ask how Hachi was doing. She did not want to hear about the child or Takumi because she was jealous.

> **The failure of Nana's hero plan is not insufficient love. It is insufficient listening.**

Hachi undergoes the opposite clarification. Volume 9 left a meaningful authorship gap between Takumi's declaration—**「結婚する事にしたから おれ達」**—and Hachi's own more provisional language. Volume 10 closes that gap. Hachi tells Shin that she wants to have the child despite paternity uncertainty and wants to believe a happy family can be built through effort. She tells Junko that she really does love Takumi. She later states without ambiguity:

> **「あたしは自分の家庭を持ちたいの！」**  
> **「何があろうとタクミと子供と3人で幸せを築いて行くって決めたんだから！」**

That is her decision.

It should not be minimized into “Takumi decided for her.”

But neither should this new agency be used to sanitize the relationship. The Chapter 29 rape/sexual assault identified in Volume 9 is not revisited, named, apologized for, or ethically processed here. Takumi's domestic affection, financial provision, ring, concern for the child, and practical competence remain real; they do not retroactively transform that prior violation into consent. Volume 10 therefore adds **Hachi's authorship** without erasing the longitudinal sexual-ethics ledger.

The volume also refuses to make her choice of Takumi rewrite Nobu out of emotional history. Hachi tells Junko that the full-moon night from the Nobu period was probably the happiest moment of her life, when it felt as though she had everything she wanted and the future glittered without cloud. Nobu, meanwhile, imagines that he has already disappeared from Hachi's story. The reader knows he is wrong: Hachi has just asked Shin whether Nobu is well.

This is one of Volume 10's most important literary principles:

> **A later choice does not automatically falsify an earlier happiness.**

The celebrity plot develops the same argument at institutional scale. The media takes real relationships and reorganizes them into profitable narrative:

- Ren and Nana become a scandal;
- Nana becomes a suspected fame-seeker;
- Yasu becomes a violent “dark lawyer” stereotype;
- Room 707 becomes a broadcast location;
- BLAST's private history becomes public content.

Gaia does not need to have invented the scandal in order to become complicit. The volume carefully distinguishes **creating** a leak from **leveraging** it. Yasu reconstructs a plausible media-industry process: the television station likely approached Gaia before broadcast; Gaia appears to have permitted its name and footage to be used on terms that made the segment double as BLAST promotion. Later Gaia executives explicitly discuss the danger of turning scandal into **祭り上げ / elevation-by-hype**, while other departments exploit the sudden attention.

The volume's media thesis is therefore more sophisticated than “the evil label fabricated everything.” It is:

> **once private life becomes monetizable narrative, institutions can profit from a story without having authored its first sentence.**

Nana responds to humiliation by becoming more strategic. She says she has thrown away her “stupid pride,” will use Ren's fame if necessary, and will rise high enough that nobody can speak down to her. Yasu gives Nobu the harder adult formulation: the world is not built so that **正義 / justice** always wins. If he does not want to lose, he must become **したたか**—shrewd, resilient, strategically hard to defeat—and a little **ずる賢い**, cunning.

This is not the series endorsing dishonesty without limit. It is the series distinguishing moral purity from efficacy inside institutions that are already compromised.

Ren faces the same adulthood from the other side. He refuses to treat Trapnest as merely Takumi's castle or his own disposable job. Trapnest is now a large project involving management, record companies, and many businesses. He calls a band an **運命共同体 / community of shared fate** and says the people he must protect now are not simply the companions he left behind. Later he admits that his love for Nana has not changed, but he is no longer capable of the reckless self-sacrifice Yasu displays. Reira tells him that this is not necessarily coldness: as people live, their burdens grow heavier, and they need someone with whom they can carry them.

That formulation connects almost every relationship in the volume.

Hachi is choosing a household because she wants a future that can be built. Ren is discovering that adult love has to carry institutional burdens. Nana is discovering that being someone's hero is not the same as hearing her. Nobu is learning that musical integrity has to survive commercial structure. Shin is trying to turn money and distance into communication with Reira. Yasu's care is becoming materially costly enough to threaten his second profession.

And beneath all of it runs the same question:

> **Can a relationship remain real after it becomes a role inside somebody else's story?**

Volume 10's answer is severe but not cynical:

> A relationship survives not because the story told about it is pure, but because the people inside it keep revising the roles they assign one another.

## 3. Narrative architecture and causal close read

### 3.1 Chapter 33 opens by rejecting the fantasy that confession automatically creates understanding

Volume 10 begins not with a new plot event, but with an ethical proposition written over Hachi's letter:

> fighting is ultimately an ego collision;  
> showing one's true feelings does not necessarily make people understand one another;  
> living without being hurt may be impossible;  
> but one should try to live without hurting the people around oneself.

The placement matters. Volume 9 ended by elevating Hachi's handwritten letter into the most durable counter-record of the relationship. Volume 10 immediately refuses the naïve conclusion that honesty itself solves relational conflict.

The problem is no longer merely **say what you feel**.

It is:

> **what do you do with another person's truth once you have heard it?**

Hachi acts first. She emails Nana after abruptly ending a call, apologizes, says she needs to speak with Shin and ask him things, and asks to meet. The message is active and deliberate. She is not waiting for Takumi to manage the encounter.

Nana, meanwhile, is already translating Hachi's life through her own emotional problem. With Ren, she admits that her complicated feelings toward Reira will probably remain until Nana herself succeeds as a singer. Her rivalry is inseparable from artistic self-worth. Ren can still pull her emotionally, but he cannot solve the Reira wound for her.

### 3.2 The strawberry glasses are gone; the relationship is now competing with public narrative

The strawberry-glass motif from Volume 9 receives a hard transition. The paired domestic symbol has been broken. Ren reports that Takumi referred to Nana as Ren's woman and told Ren to **“discipline”** her, which enrages Nana. The vocabulary of possession and discipline hits exactly where Nana is most resistant.

Nana then tries to reach Hachi directly.

Rain increases her urgency. She reaches the secure building, leaves a note with the concierge, and plans to wait at Shirokanedai Station until seven. But the concierge tells her Room 302 is currently vacant. Nana correctly suspects a security tactic: staff are denying occupancy to repel fans or outsiders.

Her emotional interpretation outruns the facts. She imagines Hachi living in an apartment the world officially treats as empty, like a ghost who does not realize she is dead—**a woman existing only for Takumi and the child**. Nana asks internally whether Hachi is really okay with such a life.

This is an important **character belief**, not objective narration.

Volume 10 immediately gives Hachi her own answer.

### 3.3 Hachi tells Shin why she is keeping the child

Shin recognizes the pregnancy from Takumi's proposal and asks the question almost everyone else has avoided: is the child definitely Takumi's?

Hachi says she thinks probably, but cannot know with certainty.

Shin's answer is blunt: abort, tell Takumi, do not confuse marriage with happiness. He asks whether Takumi knows the uncertainty. Hachi responds with a revealing theory of intimacy: marriage is possible because people can know such things and still **受け入れ合える**—accept one another.

She then explains why she needed to tell Shin. She does not want to hide the truth from someone simply because she expects opposition.

Her identification with Shin's dead mother becomes decisive. Hachi imagines that Shin's mother must have suffered and nevertheless wanted to give birth to him. She asks why Shin came to believe he should not have been born and says she never wants her own child to think that.

Then she articulates the choice that Volume 9 had not yet fully given her:

> even if paternity is uncertain, she wants the child;  
> she wants to believe that with effort a happy family might still be made;  
> she wants to have **hope**.

Shin does not give her a romantic endorsement. He tells her that people and circumstances differ and that she has to proceed through her own judgment. His most painful response is personal:

> **「僕もハチの子供に生まれたかったな」**

The volume also establishes explicitly that Shin's mother died by suicide.

This scene performs two tasks at once.

First, it gives Hachi reproductive and marital authorship.

Second, it shows that her hope is partly shaped by a moral horror: she cannot bear the possibility that her child might someday interpret its own birth as a mistake.

Her choice is therefore neither simple romantic fantasy nor detached calculation. It is **hope under uncertainty**, entangled with empathy, fear, and a desire to create the kind of family story Shin never received.

### 3.4 The media system immediately converts Shin's private trauma into commodity

The page transition is vicious. After Hachi learns the truth of Shin's mother, the tabloid side treats the same facts as usable dirt: maternal suicide, uncertain paternity, family scandal.

The juxtaposition makes the media ethics of Volume 10 unmistakable.

A fact can be:

- a confession;
- a wound;
- a reason for compassion;
- or a headline.

The difference lies not in factual content but in the **relation between knower and known**.

### 3.5 Nana and Hachi miss each other because their information networks no longer overlap

Nana waits until evening. Hachi does not come.

Nana assumes Hachi is still working and plans around an obsolete picture of Hachi's routine. Hachi, meanwhile, tells Shin that her life has narrowed dramatically. Shin tells her not to shut herself away thinking only about the child and urges contact with the outside world.

Hachi asks the question that immediately disproves Nobu's later belief that he has disappeared from her story:

> **「ノブは元気…？」**

Shin says Nobu keeps playing guitar so much that it is difficult to sleep.

The next sequence cuts among Hachi, Nana, and Nobu: Nobu plays until his hands hurt; Hachi longs for dawn; performance and longing temporarily occupy the same page architecture even though the characters are physically separated.

### 3.6 The camera becomes a trigger

The celebrity explosion arrives through one of the volume's strongest visual metaphors.

A paparazzo has photographed Nana leaving Ren. The manga then aligns a camera lens with the barrel of a gun in Trapnest imagery carrying the English word **TRIGGER**. The narration states that the trigger capable of derailing a person's fate can be pulled in an instant.

This is not subtle—and it should not be made subtle in analysis.

> **The camera is being framed as a weapon whose projectile is public narrative.**

Morning television announces Ren's romance with Nana, identifies her as the singer of the rising BLACK STONES, and begins reconstructing their shared past for a mass audience.

Future-style narration reframes the moment:

> once the thick clouds disappeared and they were abruptly placed in a spotlight, they discovered they were not standing on a stage but in a **ring**.

The applause and abuse both remain in Nana's ears.

Celebrity is therefore not simply visibility.

It is **compulsory combat before an audience**.

### 3.7 Chapter 34: the same story produces different moral reactions depending on who controls the frame

Trapnest's side immediately shows the instability of “fact.” Reira objects when television presents fabricated or distorted details about Nana and Ren's past. Takumi answers that whether every detail is factually true is not the operative issue.

That difference matters.

Reira is concerned with whether the public story corresponds to lived history. Takumi is concerned with how the story functions inside the professional environment.

Their conversation then returns to Hachi. Reira challenges Takumi's marriage logic: if family form does not guarantee a good home, why force marriage? Takumi responds from his own biography. An abnormal father can damage a child; simply having parents is not enough, and he considers himself evidence.

He also allows for a future in which Hachi cannot tolerate the marriage: she could return to her family, and he would provide support.

This complicates any formulation that makes Takumi's entire motive “the child needs a conventional two-parent household.” His thinking is more pragmatic and more cynical than that. He wants to assume responsibility, but he does not idealize the family institution itself.

### 3.8 Hachi finally says the sentence nobody else can say for her: she loves Takumi

Junko reaches Hachi and forces the marriage question into direct language.

Hachi first defensively insists she is not marrying Takumi only for the child. Then she says:

> **「あたし ほんとは…… タクミの事 好きなの…」**

This is one of the most important retrospective corrections to Volume 9.

Hachi's relationship to Takumi cannot be modeled only as:

- money;
- pregnancy;
- security;
- fear;
- convenience;
- or coerced administration.

She is attracted to him and loves him.

Junko does not respond by romanticizing the situation. She recognizes that Nobu was the man who gave Hachi the greatest sense of confidence in herself, then tells her that genuinely loving Takumi is at least more psychologically honest than pretending the marriage is only about money or the child.

Hachi's reply is self-accusatory. Why does she fall for selfish, unreliable men like Takumi or Asano? Why can she not value men such as Shoji or Nobu, who valued her? She imagines that if Shoji had never grown tired of her, a peaceful life might have been best, and fears that with her own desire structured this way she may never become happy.

This is not a moral verdict from the text.

It is Hachi's own anxious theory of herself.

It should be read as **CB**, not as an omniscient diagnosis of female desire.

### 3.9 The friends stop Hachi from going to Nana for reasons that are materially rational and emotionally painful

Hachi immediately wants to go to Nana after seeing the scandal.

Kyosuke tells her not to.

His reasoning is not trivial: if reporters identify Hachi through Nana, the Takumi relationship can become public, which may damage Takumi professionally and expose the pregnancy and future child. He reassures her that Nana has bandmates and is strong; Hachi is the person he worries about more in the current circumstances.

This produces a devastating reciprocal blockade:

- Nana tries to reach Hachi and is blocked by security;
- Hachi tries to reach Nana and is blocked by publicity risk and friends trying to protect her.

Neither absence proves indifference.

Information scarcity again masquerades as emotional evidence.

### 3.10 Room 707 becomes a public coordinate

Reporters arrive at 707 and knock on the door, asking about Ren. Television crews broadcast from the building.

The apartment that functioned as private emotional geography is now geographically legible to strangers.

This is a major home-ledger transition:

> **707 does not merely become empty; it becomes searchable.**

The old home has lost not only Hachi's body but part of its privacy.

### 3.11 Someone wants maximum attention; the motive remains unresolved

BLAST's staff move members to a hotel. Shin reports an anonymous event involving Takumi's phone and the instruction to watch `Morning Seven`. The group becomes suspicious that attention is being deliberately concentrated.

Shin supplies the crucial caution: whoever is driving the attention may not be trying to promote BLAST. The intention could be to crush them.

This must remain **UA** at the Volume 10 endpoint.

Later evidence establishes Gaia's opportunistic participation in promotion, but it does not establish the original leaker or prove that Gaia initiated the scandal.

### 3.12 “Hachikō, watch me”: Nana uses television as a relationship channel

Reporters accuse Nana of using Ren for publicity. Her oldest wound is being defined as “Ren's woman” rather than as a singer. She understands that anything she says can become more content.

Then she speaks anyway.

Into the camera she says:

> **「ハチ公 見てなよ」**  
> **「あんたの望みは 絶対叶えてやるから」**

Hachi's letter is visually recalled: she had asked Nana to appear on television and keep showing Hachi her singing.

The public statement is therefore simultaneously:

- a media event;
- a declaration of artistic ambition;
- and a private message transmitted through mass media.

Hachi sees it and cries.

Future Hachi narration says that whenever she was knocked down afterward, she could rise again because Nana was always watching her.

The word **hero** is being operationalized: Nana becomes Hachi's hero by remaining visible enough to be emotionally reachable even when private contact fails.

### 3.13 Ren chooses present institutional obligation over nostalgic ownership

Ren's side of the scandal could have been written as a romantic rescue fantasy. It is not.

He recognizes Nana's pride and knows a public defense from him could make the problem worse. More importantly, he names Trapnest as an institutional reality beyond Takumi:

> Trapnest now involves production companies, a record company, and many businesses; Ren cannot simply do whatever he wants.

Then he gives the stronger formulation:

> **「バンドってのはさ 言ってみりゃ運命共同体だ」**

A band is, so to speak, a community of shared fate.

He says the people he must protect now are not simply the companions he left behind.

This is not proof that Nana no longer matters.

Volume 10 will later state the opposite: his feelings have not changed.

The change is that love no longer exhausts his obligations.

### 3.14 Chapter 35: Gaia's involvement is complicity through leverage, not proven authorship of the scandal

The chapter opens over a city aerial with media-strategy narration: a peaceful morning, aggressive live footage, and scandal involving a heavily made-up vocalist offer enough impact to create a winning broadcast package.

Inside Gaia, Kawano resists the rush to exploit the moment. Selling BLAST through this uproar would be mere **祭り上げ**—raising them through hype. He worries that the next magazine issue will intensify hatred toward Nana and recalls artists crushed by heartless slander and companies that privilege profit over people.

Matsuo points out the institutional reality: if Kawano openly resists, he may lose responsibility for the band. The fact that the broadcast decision bypassed him is itself evidence of higher-level maneuvering. Industry professionals can infer Gaia's involvement, and the media can ride the story knowingly.

Yasu later reconstructs the likely mechanism more precisely. The station that aired `Morning Seven` also controls music programs and has tie-ins with Gaia. If BLAST were introduced by company name and footage were used, the station likely sought permission in advance. The broadcast's promotional design was probably a condition Gaia attached to granting permission.

The evidentiary distinction is important:

> **Gaia appears complicit in converting the scandal into promotion. The volume does not prove Gaia created the original leak.**

### 3.15 Nana discards one kind of pride and adopts strategic ambition

Nana reacts to public humiliation by deciding that “clean” emergence is no longer available.

She says she has thrown away her stupid pride. If the world insists on tying her to Ren, she will use that connection. She will rise by whatever dirty means are necessary until she becomes too large for people to dismiss.

This is rhetoric of escalation, not evidence that she has actually committed unethical promotional acts.

The important psychological movement is:

> **Nana converts humiliation into ambition rather than retreat.**

That resilience is formidable, but the volume will also show its danger when she turns career success into a plan for Hachi's life.

### 3.16 Nobu learns the difference between moral disgust and professional strategy

Nobu is appalled by Gaia's behavior. Nana is clearly hurt; why debut through a company willing to capitalize on that?

Yasu's answer is the volume's most explicit professional ethic.

Any large company will contain opaque and compromised structures. Gaia can fund production and promotion at a scale BLAST cannot create alone. Music has to reach people before it can be judged.

Then Yasu states:

> **「世の中 必ずしも『正義』が勝つようには出来てねえんだよ」**  
> **「負けたくねえんなら したたかになれ」**  
> **「もうちょっと ずる賢くなれよ」**

If Nobu rejects that world entirely, Yasu tells him to go home to Terashima Ryokan.

This is not nihilism.

Yasu is forcing Nobu to decide whether he wants to preserve innocence or make music under imperfect conditions.

### 3.17 Nobu thinks he has disappeared from Hachi's story; the reader knows otherwise

Nobu and Nana begin talking about their own lives as stories.

Nobu jokes about **「伸夫物語」** and says Hachi will not disappear from his. Then he assumes the inverse:

> Hachi has probably already erased him from hers.

The dramatic irony is immediate.

Hachi has asked about him. She later remembers the full-moon period with him as probably the happiest, most cloudless happiness she has known.

The text therefore warns the reader against equating **lack of current access** with **lack of narrative significance**.

### 3.18 Hachi's happiest remembered moment and her current marriage choice coexist

Hachi tells Junko that the full-moon night was probably the happiest moment of her life: it felt as if everything she wanted had come into her hands; she was filled with dreams and hope; the future shone.

Then she says she doubts she will ever again be filled with such completely cloudless happiness.

This could be misread as proof that she “really” belongs with Nobu and is falsely choosing Takumi.

That is too simple.

In the same volume Hachi:

- says she loves Takumi;
- chooses to have the child;
- chooses marriage;
- wants her own household;
- and later enjoys Takumi's return, ring, gifts, and domesticity.

The stronger reading is:

> **Hachi can choose one future without rewriting another relationship out of the truth of her past.**

Memory is not a referendum.

### 3.19 Nana makes the “take Hachi back” fantasy explicit—and turns BLAST into a narrative intervention

Nana tells Nobu not to give up and jokingly recruits him into:

> **「ハチ公を取り戻せ大作戦」**

Her actual method is more revealing than the joke.

She says that if BLAST keeps appearing in Hachi's story in an irresistibly cool form, Hachi will eventually return. Takumi represents harsh reality; Nana will give Hachi a sweet dream.

At the Gaia contract meeting, this condenses into a sentence:

> **「『ハチ物語』のヒーローは あたしだ」**

This is love, ambition, possessiveness, and artistic motivation at once.

It is also ethically paternalistic.

Nana does not yet know the degree to which Hachi has actively chosen the Takumi household. She is deciding what kind of life will make Hachi happy without hearing Hachi's current account.

### 3.20 The hero fantasy is corrected almost immediately by future Nana

The manga does not wait until a later volume to undermine present Nana's confidence.

Hachi encounters public graffiti reading:

> **「ナナ死ね」**

and transforms the hateful inscription into **`ナナ LOVE♡`**.

Across this sequence future Nana addresses Hachi:

> **「あたしはもう あんたの物語のヒーローにはなれないけど」**

Then comes the deeper reversal:

> **「今もあたしの物語のヒロインの名前は奈々」**  
> **「とびきりかわいい あんただよ」**

This is among the strongest Nana/Hachi passages in the first ten volumes.

The relationship is not defined by Nana successfully reclaiming Hachi.

It is defined by Nana accepting that Hachi has her own story while still placing Hachi at the center of hers.

### 3.21 The flaw in Nana's strategy is named before Chapter 36 begins

An airplane takes off under future narration:

> in days so hectic there was hardly time to sleep, Nana did not notice the flaw in the strategy she had devised.

Chapter 36 tells us what that flaw is.

Nana was concentrating on showing Hachi her singing.

She was not asking how Hachi was doing.

She did not want to hear about the child or Takumi.

She admits it was jealousy.

The entire “hero” project therefore fails its own opening ethical standard. Nana reveals herself spectacularly but does not yet make room for Hachi's truth.

### 3.22 Chapter 36: Hachi's family begins incorporating Takumi into ordinary family judgment

At Hachi's family home, the marriage stops being only a celebrity abstraction.

Her mother and sister discuss where Hachi and Takumi should stay and deliberately give the couple privacy because they have been apart. Hachi says she is looking forward to seeing him. A family member reports that Hachi's father, previously prejudiced against entertainment work as disreputable, found Takumi unexpectedly courteous after speaking with him by international phone.

This matters because the Takumi household begins to acquire **social recognition beyond money and pregnancy**.

Hachi is not merely disappearing into an isolated celebrity apartment. Her natal family is beginning, cautiously, to incorporate Takumi into its evaluative world.

### 3.23 Hachi cannot reach Nana—and tells herself Nana is fine

Hachi explains that Nana's mobile phone is apparently broken. Hachi cannot call or email her, and Nana has not contacted her.

Other friends say Nana is doing very well, so Hachi reassures herself that Nana is strong.

The page then cuts to Nana's future confession:

> she was so intent on showing Hachi herself singing that she never even tried to ask how Hachi was doing; she did not want to hear about the child or Takumi; it was simply jealousy.

This sequence is the volume's clearest answer to the opening narration.

Mutual love is not enough.

Information filtered through third parties is not enough.

Heroic performance is not enough.

### 3.24 Reira's empathy is explicitly identified as projection

In London, Reira worries about Stella, a woman who had been involved with Takumi before Hachi's marriage. Reira imagines that Stella might secretly have hoped to marry Takumi and calls the situation cruel.

Mari praises Reira's ability to see another person's perspective and links it to the emotional reach of her singing.

Reira rejects the compliment.

She says it is only a difference in which person's position one can emotionally inhabit. She herself is selfish and thinks mostly about herself; she does not actually know what Stella thinks.

This is an unusually sophisticated self-correction:

> **empathy can be morally serious without being epistemically reliable.**

Reira recognizes that what looks like insight into Stella may actually be self-projection.

### 3.25 Shin turns Reira's money into communication rather than simple consumption

Shin's email chain to Reira becomes one of the volume's quiet relational structures.

He tells her about the BLAST camp, the enforced healthy schedule, the sky, and the stars. Sparkling things make him think of Reira, though he cannot explain why.

Later he says that if Reira is lonely, he will fly to London immediately—anywhere—if it would warm her heart and body even a little. He warns her not to mistake this for pure altruism. He simply has nothing he currently considers more important, and he cannot promise what tomorrow will make him.

Finally he explains why he bought the laptop. He wanted to use up the money Reira had given him, but did not want to spend it only for himself. The acoustic guitar came from the same impulse. He is not romantic enough to sing where the song cannot reach, so he will use the laptop to send messages and asks her to reply whenever she feels like it.

The text is turning money into a conflicted relational medium.

The established age and power asymmetry does not disappear. If anything, the explicit monetary history keeps it analytically active. But Shin is trying to alter the meaning of the money: from payment received into tools for music and communication.

### 3.26 BLAST's retreat into camp creates a temporary alternative household

While media stories escalate outside, BLAST enters a recording/training camp in the mountains.

The environment is deliberately mundane:

- early running;
- cleaning;
- meals;
- miso soup;
- practice;
- meetings;
- sleep schedules;
- stars at night.

The band is professionalizing through domestic discipline.

This is a meaningful counter-image to the glamorous celebrity story being manufactured outside. Publicly, they are scandal characters. Privately, they are young musicians eating oversalted soup and being forced into routines.

### 3.27 The industry manufactures audiences as well as stories

Gaia plans guerrilla lives at multiple crowded Tokyo locations and proposes hiring actor-extras as **サクラ**—seed spectators whose visible interest will encourage passersby to stop.

Nana objects: she does not need fake fans.

The promoter explains the social mechanism. Japanese crowds are often reluctant to be first; a new restaurant can attract customers by making the first line visible.

This adds another layer to Volume 10's public-truth problem.

The audience response can be genuine even when the first social cue is manufactured.

The text does not settle whether this is acceptable or deceptive. It asks us to distinguish:

- fabricated applause;
- engineered attention;
- and authentic interest that arises after engineered attention creates an opportunity to listen.

### 3.28 Media turns Yasu into a character assembled from selective facts

The tabloid story expands to Yasu. His upbringing, appearance, legal background, and defense of Nana are assembled into the figure of a violent **ヤミ弁護士 / dark or underground lawyer**.

The most important feature is not that every component is invented.

Some pieces are real.

Mari explains the judicial-training system and infers that Yasu may not have completed the post-exam apprenticeship required for ordinary lawyer practice, perhaps because band life made the schedule incompatible. She speculates that his employer recognized his ability and used him for office/legal work without the formal title.

This explanation is **character inference**, not omniscient confirmation.

What the media does is more basic: it takes partial facts and forces them into a character legible at a glance.

That is exactly what the volume has been doing with Nana.

### 3.29 Reira discovers that her memory of Yasu's breakup explanation may be incomplete

Reira had understood Yasu's path through a particular story: law was the real dream; the band could not be his future; their trajectories therefore diverged.

Learning that he may have sacrificed or deferred legal qualification for BLAST destabilizes that memory. She asks whether the explanation he once gave was simply an excuse to break up with her.

The volume does not answer.

This belongs in the unresolved relationship-history ledger.

### 3.30 Ren distinguishes reduced self-sacrifice from reduced love

Ren's response to Yasu is among the volume's most adult relationship passages.

He says Yasu did not throw himself into danger merely for the band. He did it for Nana.

Then Ren admits:

> he can no longer become that reckless for Nana;  
> he does not know when he became such a cold person;  
> and yet his feelings of love have not changed.

A remembered Nana asks Ren whether, if she died, he would die with her.

Reira rejects Ren's self-condemnation. Growing older means accumulating heavier baggage. People become less able to move exactly as desire commands. That is why they need someone who can carry those burdens with them.

She asks whether Ren and Nana cannot become that kind of pair.

This is a major revision of the youthful fusion fantasy.

> **Mature love is being proposed not as dying together, but as carrying weight together.**

### 3.31 Takumi returns with gifts, a ring, and an explicit hierarchy of value

Takumi's return gives the Hachi relationship one of its most straightforwardly affectionate domestic sequences in the series so far.

Hachi greets him, they kiss, and he brings numerous gifts. She jokingly demands the ring she requested. He has already hidden it in her clothing, then places it on her hand.

The material symbolism is obvious: the abstract marriage plan now has a visible object Hachi actively wanted.

Takumi then gives a remarkably frank self-description:

> **「一番は仕事」** — work is number one.  
> It occupies more than ninety percent of his brain.  
> Everything else gets neglected.  
> Women are perhaps second because without them he cannot relax.  
> He cannot give Hachi much attention, but he promises she will not suffer materially.

Hachi answers that being number one within second place would be enough.

Takumi jokes that she is actually number two even within that category, then reveals the joke's target: the unborn child—imagined as a daughter—gets the “number one woman” gift. The baby goods are presented affectionately, and the couple jokes about the name Sachiko.

This scene should be read in full tension.

Takumi is:

- materially generous;
- affectionate;
- funny;
- invested in the child;
- honest about his work-centered life;
- and still using a hierarchy in which women partly function as relief from work.

The domestic tenderness is real.

The instrumental language is also real.

Neither cancels the other.

### 3.32 Nana says Hachi's bond has become stronger than Ren's

Near the end, Nana's future narration supplies one of the first ten volumes' strongest statements about relational centrality:

> after leaving Ren in their hometown, during the following two and a half years, Nana feels that the bond with Hachi became stronger than the bond with Ren;  
> even when she is with Ren she feels lonely somewhere inside;  
> they can no longer become one the way they once did;  
> and yet her love for Ren remains unchanged.

This passage is not saying Nana “stopped loving Ren and loves Hachi instead.”

It is saying something more structurally interesting:

> **romantic love and relational centrality are not identical variables.**

Ren remains the beloved man whose image still defines glitter for Nana.

Hachi has become the stronger bond.

Volume 10 therefore makes the Nana/Hachi relation even harder to reduce to ordinary friendship without forcing it into a conventional romance label.

### 3.33 The volume ends by echoing Shin's glitter language

Shin said that sparkling things make him think of Reira.

The closing future narration has Nana tell Hachi that star-dust blurs and the night sky seems unusually bright, then says:

> **「あたしは今も キラキラしたものを見ると 蓮の事を思い出すから」**

A rose lies in water.

The parallel matters.

Both Shin and Nana use **キラキラ / glittering, sparkling** perception as involuntary relational memory.

The volume has spent hundreds of pages showing public “shine” being manufactured through television, scandal, promotion, makeup, cameras, and seeded crowds. Its final image gives us a different kind of shine:

> the private sensory trigger that survives after narrative control fails.

## 4. Nana Osaki — character-state update

Volume 10 strengthens Nana in one domain while exposing a major weakness in another.

She becomes **professionally more resilient and strategically adult**.

She also becomes **more openly possessive and narratively paternalistic toward Hachi**.

### 4.1 She can metabolize humiliation into ambition

The media attack strikes Nana exactly at the wound she has been trying to escape: she is publicly legible as Ren's woman before she is secure as Nana Osaki, vocalist.

Her response is not retreat.

She decides to use the connection, discard the pride that demanded a perfectly “clean” path to recognition, and become too successful to be dismissed.

This is a genuine increase in resilience.

It also marks her entry into professional strategy rather than adolescent purity.

### 4.2 Her idea of being Hachi's hero is loving and controlling at once

Hachi has explicitly asked to keep seeing Nana sing.

Nana honors that wish magnificently when she uses television to tell Hachikō to watch and promises to fulfill what Hachi asked.

But Nana then adds a second project that Hachi did **not** ask for: become so cool inside Hachi's story that Hachi will return.

That is the difference between:

- answering someone's expressed desire;
- and deciding what her life should mean.

Volume 10 makes this distinction central.

### 4.3 The future narration performs a retrospective correction on Nana's hero fantasy

Present Nana declares herself Hachi's hero.

Future Nana says she can no longer be Hachi's hero.

This is not merely sad foreshadowing. It is **ethical maturation in narrative form**.

She relinquishes jurisdiction over Hachi's story.

But she does not reduce the bond. Hachi remains the heroine of Nana's own story.

### 4.4 She finally admits the flaw: she was performing at Hachi, not listening to Hachi

Nana's confession in Chapter 36 is crucial.

She wanted to show Hachi her singing so intensely that she did not ask how Hachi was doing. She did not want to hear about Takumi or the child because of jealousy.

The opening narration's lesson now lands directly on her:

> expressing one's true feeling does not guarantee understanding.

Nana can sing her truth at national scale and still fail to ask Hachi a basic question.

### 4.5 Hachi is now a stronger bond than Ren without becoming a substitute romance

The future narration explicitly ranks bond-strength:

- Nana's bond with Hachi has grown stronger than the bond with Ren;
- Nana still loves Ren;
- she still involuntarily associates glitter with Ren;
- but the old fantasy of becoming one with him no longer works.

This is among the strongest evidence yet for Nana/Hachi as the series' relational center.

It is equally strong evidence against a simplistic “Nana is secretly sexually in love with Hachi” reduction. The text continues to allow different kinds of love to coexist without collapsing them into one category.

### 4.6 Singing becomes responsibility, not only self-completion

Volume 9 gave Nana's statement that the stage makes her complete.

Volume 10 adds a social burden to that artistic ontology. Looking at BLAST, she thinks Nobu keeps clinging to the guitar despite rejection and heartbreak, Shin is headed toward destruction if neglected, and therefore:

> **if Nana does not sing, they have no future.**

That is a dangerous amount of responsibility to place on herself.

But it also marks a shift from “the stage completes me” toward “my singing is part of a collective livelihood.”

## 5. Komatsu Nana / Hachi — character-state update

Volume 10 is one of the most important Hachi-authorship volumes so far.

Its central correction is simple:

> **Hachi is not merely being carried into marriage by Takumi's competence. She chooses the project.**

### 5.1 She chooses the child under acknowledged uncertainty

Hachi does not claim certainty about paternity.

She tells Shin that uncertainty exists.

She nevertheless wants the child and wants to believe a happy family can be built through effort.

The language of **希望 / hope** matters. Hachi is not saying she has proof that the family will be happy. She is choosing to act without proof.

### 5.2 Her reproductive choice is relationally informed but still her own

Shin's mother's suicide becomes part of Hachi's moral imagination. She cannot tolerate the idea that her child might someday think it should never have been born.

That does not make the choice irrational.

It shows that reproductive decision-making is embedded in stories about other lives.

### 5.3 She admits love for Takumi

This point should be treated as a cumulative correction, not an optional nuance.

Hachi says she actually loves him.

Any later analysis of Hachi/Takumi that omits that fact will underdescribe her agency.

Any analysis that uses the fact to erase coercion will underdescribe the ethics.

Both must remain in the same model.

### 5.4 She wants a household, not merely a wedding

Hachi's decisive phrase is not “I want to be Takumi's wife.”

It is:

> **「あたしは自分の家庭を持ちたいの！」**

She wants to possess/create a household of her own.

The aspiration is domestic authorship.

This helps explain why money, housing, ring, child, family recognition, and everyday routines matter so strongly to her. They are not superficial substitutes for emotion. They are the material grammar of the life she wants to build.

### 5.5 Nobu remains true inside memory

Calling the full-moon night probably the happiest moment of her life prevents a triumphalist reading of the marriage choice.

Nobu mattered.

The happiness was real.

The future changed.

Those propositions coexist.

### 5.6 Hachi is less isolated than Nana imagines, but her social world has narrowed

Nana imagines Hachi as a ghost existing only for Takumi and the child.

That is too absolute.

Hachi still has:

- Junko and Kyosuke;
- Shin;
- her natal family;
- concern for Nana;
- memory of Nobu;
- and active communication about the marriage.

Yet Shin is right to notice narrowing. Hachi's daily movement has contracted substantially, and publicity concerns make spontaneous access to friends harder.

The correct formulation is therefore not “social death.”

It is:

> **a shrinking and reorganization of social life around pregnancy, security, and the new household.**

### 5.7 Hachi protects Nana's public image in a tiny private act

The graffiti sequence is one of the volume's best Hachi images.

She encounters **「ナナ死ね」** and turns it into **`ナナ LOVE♡`**.

The act is materially small and publicly anonymous.

It mirrors Nana's televised message in reverse:

- Nana sends Hachi a private message through mass media;
- Hachi answers public hatred through a private act of care in public space.

## 6. Other materially affected characters

### 6.1 Takumi Ichinose — chosen partner, hierarchical realist, and unresolved sexual ethics

Volume 10 complicates Takumi without rehabilitating him.

New evidence:

- Hachi loves him;
- Hachi chooses the household with him;
- her family begins accepting him;
- he calls from abroad and behaves courteously with her father;
- he returns with gifts and a ring Hachi requested;
- he is affectionate and invested in the unborn child;
- he explicitly promises Hachi will not suffer materially.

He also states his hierarchy with unusual honesty:

- work is first;
- it occupies more than ninety percent of his mental life;
- women are second partly because they allow him to release pressure;
- Hachi should not expect much attention.

This is not romantic idealization.

It is a contract-like self-description of the life he is offering.

Most importantly, Volume 10 contains **no reckoning with the Volume 9 rape/sexual assault**. The absence must not be converted into exoneration. There is no apology, no recognition of violated consent, and no textual reclassification of the encounter.

### 6.2 Nobu Terashima — grief converted into practice, idealism confronted by industry

Nobu plays guitar until his hands hurt.

He experiences Hachi's loss as personal insufficiency, but the volume increasingly converts that grief into work.

His industry arc is equally important. He is disgusted by Gaia's opportunism. Yasu refuses to let him preserve innocence through abstraction: either learn to operate inside compromised structures or go home.

Nobu's growth question becomes:

> **can he become strategically adult without becoming cynical?**

### 6.3 Ren Honjo — love after fusion

Ren's major development is not falling out of love with Nana.

It is accepting that love no longer authorizes total recklessness.

He recognizes current institutional obligations to Trapnest and admits he cannot throw himself away for Nana the way Yasu can. Reira offers a model of partnership based on carrying growing burdens together.

The old ideal was **one body / one fate / die together**.

The emerging adult question is **can we remain two people and carry weight together?**

### 6.4 Yasu Takagi — care becomes costly enough to threaten a second life-path

Yasu protects Nana physically, punches a reporter, and accepts the possibility that professional consequences may force him to leave his office work.

The media then converts his mixed legal/band biography into a sensational character.

Mari's discussion suggests a real structural conflict between judicial training and band commitment, but her explanation remains inference. What is textually secure is that Yasu's care for BLAST is no longer costless support from the sidelines.

He has put his own career path at risk.

### 6.5 Reira Serizawa — projection, burden-sharing, and unstable memory of Yasu

Reira continues to function as one of the series' sharpest relational mirrors.

She knows her compassion for Stella may be projection.

She tells Ren adulthood increases burdens and that partnership means carrying them together.

She becomes unsettled when new information suggests the story she understood about Yasu's law/band choice may have been incomplete.

Her interpretive problem is therefore the same as the volume's:

> **how much of another person's motive are you actually perceiving, and how much are you supplying?**

### 6.6 Shin Okazaki — attachment without permanence, money converted into contact

Shin's most important line is that Reira is currently what he values most, but he does not know what tomorrow will bring.

That statement is emotionally intense and anti-romantic at once.

He also tries to change the function of money received from Reira by buying tools that create communication and music rather than simply consuming it for himself.

The relationship remains ethically unstable because age, money, celebrity, and prior asymmetry remain active. Volume 10 does not solve that problem.

### 6.7 Junko and Kyosuke — protective realism without omniscience

Junko gives Hachi space to admit love for Takumi while still challenging her patterns.

Kyosuke stops Hachi from rushing to Nana because he understands the publicity cascade that could expose Hachi's pregnancy and Takumi relationship.

Both act from care.

Both also contribute to the temporary separation between Hachi and Nana.

This is another recurring *NANA* pattern:

> **reasonable protection can still produce painful relational distance.**

## 7. Relationship-state changes

### 7.1 Nana / Hachi — from hero fantasy to heroine recognition

This is the volume's central relationship.

**Strengthened:** Hachi still watches Nana and draws strength from her.  
**Strengthened:** Nana still organizes artistic ambition around Hachi.  
**Strengthened:** future Nana explicitly says the Hachi bond became stronger than the Ren bond.  
**Complicated:** Nana's jealousy prevents listening.  
**Revised:** Nana's present hero claim is retrospectively corrected.  
**Strengthened:** Hachi remains the heroine of Nana's story.

The strongest formulation through Volume 10 is now:

> **Nana/Hachi is a mutually constitutive love bond whose centrality exceeds ordinary friendship categories, but whose very intensity tempts both women—especially Nana—to turn care into narrative role-assignment.**

### 7.2 Hachi / Takumi — agency strengthens; ethical contradiction remains

Volume 10 materially revises the relationship state.

Hachi:

- chooses the child;
- chooses marriage;
- says she loves Takumi;
- wants a household;
- asks for the ring;
- welcomes him affectionately;
- and imagines shared family construction.

Takumi:

- provides access, money, housing, gifts, and family contact;
- presents himself honestly as work-dominated;
- is affectionate toward the child;
- and makes no attempt to process the prior assault.

The relationship is therefore neither “pure coercion” nor “ordinary romance.”

It is a relationship containing genuine attraction, chosen commitment, material provision, hierarchy, entitlement, and already-established sexual violence.

### 7.3 Hachi / Nobu — no current couple, no emotional erasure

Nobu believes he has vanished from Hachi's story.

He has not.

Hachi asks about him and preserves the full-moon memory as extraordinary happiness.

What has ended is not the truth of the bond but its present claim on her future.

### 7.4 Nana / Ren — love persists after the fantasy of being one

The relationship becomes more adult and more estranged at the same time.

Ren's institutional obligations grow.

Nana's bond with Hachi overtakes the Ren bond in felt strength.

Both still love each other.

The volume's question is no longer “will love survive?”

It is:

> **what form can love take after fusion is no longer possible?**

### 7.5 Nana / Yasu — exposed devotion, still bounded

Nana calls Yasu obviously besotted. Yasu effectively admits that he can no longer pretend otherwise.

Yet his sexual joke is immediately withdrawn, and his directive remains: do not worry about me; sing.

The relationship remains characterized by love-as-care rather than possession, but Volume 10 sharply increases the cost Yasu is willing to pay.

### 7.6 Reira / Yasu — past narrative destabilized

Reira's concern and the legal-career discussion reopen the breakup story.

No new fact proves what Yasu's true motive was.

The relationship-history ledger should therefore move from “known incompatibility” toward **unresolved motive**.

### 7.7 Shin / Reira — communication deepens while structural concern remains

Shin's emails create emotional continuity across distance.

The laptop and guitar turn financial exchange into communicative infrastructure.

But none of this dissolves the age/power asymmetry already established. Greater tenderness is not the same as greater ethical equality.

### 7.8 BLAST / Gaia — from aspirant-label relationship to compromised mutual dependence

BLAST gains:

- contract;
- recording infrastructure;
- promotion;
- debut date;
- guerrilla-live planning;
- institutional scale.

Gaia gains:

- a suddenly newsworthy band;
- promotional value from scandal;
- commercial leverage.

The relationship begins not in innocence but in **mutual need under asymmetrical power**.

## 8. Japanese voice, address, and translation-sensitive language

Volume 10 is unusually sensitive to the difference between **what a person says, who gets to interpret it, and who turns it into a public narrative**. Several Japanese formulations therefore carry more analytical weight than a plot summary suggests.

### 8.1 「人は分かり合えるものでもない」 — disclosure is not understanding

The opening narration rejects a recurring romantic simplification: that two people can solve conflict merely by saying their “true feelings.” The key distinction is between **本音をさらけ出す** and **分かり合う**. Exposing the private self does not mechanically produce mutual comprehension.

That becomes the governing linguistic problem of the volume. Everyone is surrounded by speech, messages, interviews, press stories, emails, phone calls, and public statements; they remain capable of misunderstanding one another.

The positive ethical imperative is correspondingly modest:

> one may not be able to avoid being hurt, but one should make an effort not to hurt people around oneself.

This is not a promise of perfect mutuality. It is an ethics of restraint under epistemic limitation.

### 8.2 「希望を持ちたい」 — Hachi's choice is hope, not certainty

Hachi does not tell Shin that marriage will definitely make a good family. She says she wants to **hold hope** that effort can make one.

The grammar matters because it prevents a falsely triumphant reading of her decision. Hachi is not declaring that reality has finally conformed to her domestic ideal. She knows:

- the child's paternity is uncertain;
- Takumi is difficult;
- family structures can fail;
- Shin himself is evidence that being born into a family does not guarantee security.

Her decision is therefore better described as **chosen hope under acknowledged uncertainty**.

### 8.3 「自分の家庭を持ちたい」 — household authorship

Later Hachi is even more explicit:

> **「あたしは自分の家庭を持ちたいの！」**

The phrase is stronger than “I want to marry.” **自分の家庭** emphasizes a household of her own: a lived domestic unit rather than marriage as status alone.

This is one of the most important retrospective corrections to Volume 9. Takumi had previously announced marriage as a nearly settled joint fact. Volume 10 gives Hachi a first-person statement of the future she herself wants.

The same page still leaves Takumi's greater material and logistical power intact. Hachi's agency is real without becoming structurally equal power.

### 8.4 「好き」 and the problem of Hachi's desire

Hachi tells Junko:

> **「あたし ほんとは……タクミの事 好きなの」**

This matters because she has repeatedly tried to narrate the marriage through pregnancy, practicality, money, or family necessity. The confession does not say that those things are irrelevant. It says they are not the whole explanation.

Her subsequent self-accusation—why does she fall for selfish, bad men while failing to value men who treat her carefully?—should remain classified as **Hachi's self-theory**, not objective diagnosis. She has recognized a pattern in her desire. She has not proven a universal law about herself.

### 8.5 「部外者」 — intimacy erased by institutional boundary

During the Trapnest/BLAST scandal, Hachi calls herself a **部外者**, an outsider.

The term is painful precisely because it is both true and false.

Institutionally, she is outside:

- not band staff;
- not a musician;
- not press-facing;
- not publicly recognized as Takumi's fiancée;
- not someone who can safely enter the media crisis.

Relationally, she remains central to several of the people involved.

Volume 10 repeatedly produces this split: the person who matters privately may have no legitimate public role.

### 8.6 「物語」 — story as relational jurisdiction

Nobu and Nana speak almost jokingly about **「伸夫物語」** and whether Hachi has disappeared from his story. Nana then develops her plan in explicitly narratological terms:

> keep BLAST appearing magnificently inside Hachi's story.

The vocabulary is not decorative. **物語** becomes a way characters conceptualize the right to remain meaningful in another person's life.

The danger is that story-language also tempts the speaker into authorship. Nana can decide how *she* appears; she cannot decide what role Hachi must assign her.

### 8.7 「ヒーロー」 and 「ヒロイン」 — the decisive role reversal

Volume 9 made Nana Hachi's singular **ヒーロー**.

Volume 10 first intensifies that motif. Nana announces to the camera that she will grant Hachi's wish and internally decides:

> **「『ハチ物語』のヒーローは あたしだ」**

The future narration then corrects the claim:

> **「あたしはもう あんたの物語のヒーローにはなれないけど」**

But this is immediately paired with a much more revealing sentence:

> **「今もあたしの物語のヒロインの名前は奈々」**

The kanji **奈々** identifies Hachi, Komatsu Nana. Future Osaki Nana no longer claims the heroic right to rescue or direct Hachi, but she still places Hachi in the central feminine narrative role of her own life.

This is one of the strongest Nana/Hachi formulations in the manga so far because it changes the structure from:

> **I am the hero of your life**

into:

> **you remain the heroine of mine.**

The second formulation contains less jurisdiction and more confession.

### 8.8 「取り戻す」 — reclaim language remains ethically unstable

Nana names a **「ハチ公を取り戻せ大作戦」**.

The verb remains continuous with Volume 9's acquisition language. It expresses genuine grief and attachment, but it also treats Hachi as someone who can be retrieved from another relational system.

Volume 10 makes this more ethically unstable because Hachi has now explicitly chosen to build a household with Takumi.

Nana's plan is therefore not merely “help my friend escape.” It is a loving attempt to change the desire of a person whose present choice Nana does not want to hear about.

### 8.9 「したたか」 and 「ずる賢い」 — professional cunning without moral innocence

Yasu tells Nobu that the world is not structured so that “justice” automatically wins and that, if he does not want to lose, he must become **したたか** and a little more **ずる賢い**.

Neither term should be flattened into “be evil.”

The point is strategic adulthood:

- understand institutions;
- understand incentives;
- recognize that purity can be exploited;
- retain enough agency to survive compromised systems.

The moral question is not whether BLAST can remain perfectly clean. It is what compromises preserve the music and people without turning them into the thing they oppose.

### 8.10 「運命共同体」 — band as shared fate

Ren calls a band a kind of **運命共同体**, a community bound by fate.

This is stronger than “team.” It names a unit whose individual actions expose others to consequences and whose members inherit obligations from collective success.

The phrase explains why celebrity maturity changes Ren's relation to Nana. He no longer has only a lover and old friends. He belongs to an institution whose staff, companies, audiences, and members depend on his conduct.

### 8.11 「荷物」 — adulthood as accumulating burden

Reira's formulation to Ren is one of the volume's clearest adult-love statements:

> as people keep living, the luggage they carry becomes heavier; they need someone who can carry it together.

**荷物** converts adulthood from abstract maturity into weight. Love is no longer imagined as dying together, dissolving into one person, or proving intensity through self-destruction.

It becomes **co-bearing**.

### 8.12 「キラキラ」 — glitter as involuntary attachment memory

Shin says glittering things make him think of Reira. At the end Nana says glittering things still make her think of Ren.

The repeated **キラキラ** links two relationships that are otherwise ethically and structurally different.

Its function is not “these loves are equivalent.” It is to show how attachment becomes a perceptual reflex: a person changes what the world calls to mind.

## 9. Narration, time, memory, and information distribution

### 9.1 Volume 10 is a war among narrators

The volume contains an unusually high density of people telling stories about other people:

- tabloids narrate Nana and Ren;
- television narrates BLAST;
- fans and strangers narrate Nana from scandal fragments;
- Nana narrates Hachi as a ghost trapped for Takumi and a child;
- Hachi narrates her own marriage as chosen hope;
- Nobu narrates himself as a man who failed to act;
- Reira warns that empathy may only mean projecting oneself onto another person;
- future Nana corrects present Nana's hero fantasy.

The volume's formal question is therefore not simply “what is true?”

It is:

> **who has enough evidence to tell whose story, and what happens when a partial story becomes socially operative?**

### 9.2 Present Nana repeatedly mistakes information absence for personal truth

Nana does not know where Hachi is in a deep sense.

She has:

- an address;
- failed access to the apartment;
- old assumptions about Hachi's work routine;
- jealousy;
- knowledge of Takumi;
- her own memory of Hachi's desires.

From this she constructs the image of Hachi as a ghost existing only for Takumi and the baby.

Volume 10 later gives Hachi a more complicated interior account. The ghost image remains emotionally accurate to Nana's fear while becoming epistemically insufficient.

### 9.3 Reciprocal failed access is structurally important

Nana tries to reach Hachi and is blocked by security.

Hachi tries to go to Nana after the scandal and is discouraged because reporters could connect her to Takumi and the pregnancy.

Neither woman simply chooses not to care.

Institutional structures now mediate access:

- security;
- publicity;
- pregnancy secrecy;
- band management;
- press surveillance.

The old relationship once organized through “come home to 707” is becoming difficult to enact through physical proximity.

### 9.4 The future narrator is a correction mechanism, not an omniscient oracle

Future Nana gives several decisive reinterpretations:

- present Nana's hero position will not remain available;
- Hachi remains the heroine of Nana's own story;
- Nana later recognizes that she was so focused on showing Hachi her singing that she did not ask how Hachi was doing;
- Nana later recognizes jealousy as part of why she did not want to hear about Takumi or the child;
- Nana says her bond with Hachi became stronger than her bond with Ren.

These are powerful retrospective statements because they are first-person self-assessments from a later temporal position.

They still do not tell us every future event. The method should not use them to fill causal gaps that the text has not yet supplied.

### 9.5 Nobu is wrong about disappearing from Hachi's story

Nobu assumes that Hachi has likely erased him from her narrative.

The reader has almost immediate counterevidence:

- she asks Shin how Nobu is;
- she remembers the full-moon period as perhaps the happiest, most cloudless happiness of her life.

This is a classic *NANA* information-asymmetry wound. Nobu's self-blame is psychologically credible because he does not possess the evidence that the reader has.

### 9.6 Reira states the epistemic problem explicitly

Reira's reflection on Stella is a methodological statement for reading the entire work:

> emotional identification may tell us more about the interpreter than the interpreted person.

She says she does not actually know what Stella thinks.

This should retroactively caution the reader against treating Nana's Hachi-ghost theory, Nobu's self-condemnation, or even sympathetic readings of Takumi as transparent access to another mind.

### 9.7 Private communication survives through new media

Shin's emails to Reira form a quiet counter-system to the tabloid and television machine.

Both are mediated communication.

Their ethical structures differ:

- tabloid media extracts private facts without consent and sells narratives to strangers;
- Shin's emails are addressed, reciprocal in possibility, and allow the recipient to answer when inclined.

The volume is therefore not anti-media in a simple technological sense. It distinguishes **communication** from **appropriation**.

## 10. Visual/formal analysis

### 10.1 Camera becomes gun

The most explicit visual thesis arrives at numbered page 22 / spine 23.

A paparazzo photographs Nana. The page then cuts to an image of a firearm in a Trapnest `TRIGGER` visual while narration states that the trigger capable of deranging another person's fate can be pulled in an instant.

The transition visually fuses:

- camera shutter;
- gun trigger;
- celebrity image;
- sudden irreversible consequence.

This is not metaphorical decoration placed on top of the scandal plot. The manga makes the camera a weapon because an image can reorganize the social conditions under which a person lives.

### 10.2 The empty apartment is a negative image of occupancy

When the concierge claims unit 302 is empty, the apartment becomes paradoxical space:

- materially inhabited by Hachi;
- administratively presented as vacant;
- socially inaccessible to Nana.

Nana's ghost metaphor emerges from this architecture. Hachi is present and absent at once.

The building's security succeeds at its stated task while producing emotional horror from Nana's perspective.

### 10.3 Stage becomes ring

After the scandal, narration states that once the spotlight arrived, the place they stood was not a stage but a **ring**.

The visual/public-performance space has changed function.

A stage presumes:

- performance;
- spectatorship;
- artistic control.

A ring presumes:

- combat;
- opponents;
- winning and losing;
- blows one cannot fully choreograph.

BLAST's professional debut is thus visually born under conditions of contest before it is born under conditions of music.

### 10.4 Television creates impossible proximity

Nana cannot physically reach Hachi.

Hachi cannot safely go to Nana.

Yet Nana can look into a broadcast camera and tell Hachi to watch.

Hachi receives the message and cries.

The scene turns mass media—otherwise the volume's machinery of violation—into an imperfect medium of intimacy. The same apparatus that objectifies Nana allows her to reach the one person she most wants to reach.

The manga therefore refuses a clean “media bad / private good” binary.

### 10.5 Graffiti is rewritten rather than erased

Pages 145–146 provide one of the volume's most economical visual sequences.

Hachi encounters hostile public writing: **「ナナ死ね」**. She does not erase the wall back into neutrality. She alters the hostile mark into **「ナナ LOVE♡」**.

This act is small, private, and materially powerless compared with television or magazines. It nevertheless literalizes a major *NANA* procedure:

> **received text can be rewritten.**

The public writes Nana as hated object. Hachi performs a counter-authorship.

The vast blank field around the finished `LOVE` gives the intervention disproportionate visual clarity. Hachi cannot control celebrity discourse, but she can refuse to leave one sentence in its hostile form.

### 10.6 The hero/heroine correction is staged across bodies and text

Page 145 overlays future narration naming Hachi the heroine of Nana's story while Hachi is physically shown editing the wall. The formal effect is reciprocal:

- Nana's future voice names Hachi;
- Hachi's present body protects Nana's name.

Neither is physically beside the other.

Their bond is represented through acts of inscription at a distance.

### 10.7 Gaia's high-rise architecture versus improvised BLAST domesticity

The Gaia contract and promotion scenes place BLAST inside conference rooms, tall buildings, schedules, and professional machinery.

The training-camp scenes then cut to:

- cooking;
- cleaning;
- running;
- shared meals;
- cramped ordinary practice life.

Professionalization therefore expands outward while the band reproduces an inward household.

The tension resembles 707 on a new scale: intimacy must survive institutional enclosure.

### 10.8 Gifts, ring, and baby object — luxury translated into household comedy

Takumi returns with a spectacular sequence of gifts whose abundance visually registers his wealth and distance-travel capacity.

The diamond ring appears as the expected marriage object, but the scene immediately destabilizes prestige by turning into joking around who the “number one woman” is and revealing a baby gift.

The visual rhythm moves:

> luxury commodities → engagement symbol → teasing hierarchy → imagined child.

Takumi's material language is therefore not only ostentation. It is one of the ways he tries to make the future tangible.

### 10.9 Star field, rose, and glitter

Page 189 places Nana's future narration about Ren and Hachi over a star-filled field: she says the Hachi bond became stronger, that she still feels lonely beside Ren, and that her love for Ren remains unchanged.

The volume closes on page 191 with a rose in water and the statement that glittering things still make Nana think of Ren.

The ending does not resolve this as a romantic substitution equation.

Hachi can become Nana's strongest bond while Ren remains the involuntary object of romantic memory.

The visual language permits relational plurality instead of requiring one attachment to cancel another.

## 11. Home and domestic-space ledger

### Apartment 707 — lost coordinate, continuing emotional home

707 is no longer functioning as the shared household it once was.

Its significance increases rather than disappears.

By Volume 10 it serves as:

- the lost ordinary life Nana imagines recovering;
- a publicly discoverable celebrity location vulnerable to reporters;
- a memory-space against which the protected new apartment is measured;
- proof that a home can cease to be safe because the people inside become public objects.

The intrusion of reporters is the decisive material correction to nostalgia. 707 is emotionally home, but it is no longer a reliably protectable private environment.

### Hachi's protected apartment — from “ghost room” to chosen household project

Nana initially reads the new apartment as anti-home:

- officially vacant;
- secure to the point of invisibility;
- socially isolating;
- organized around Takumi and the pregnancy.

Hachi's own later statements complicate that reading.

She wants:

- a family;
- Takumi;
- the child;
- a household she can call her own.

The apartment therefore cannot be classified simply as a prison. It is a **chosen domestic project inside a highly asymmetrical relationship and security regime**.

### Hachi's natal family — durable fallback without dramatic idealization

Her family provides a different kind of home:

- ordinary teasing;
- pragmatic acceptance;
- a father willing to speak to Takumi;
- a mother and sister who do not require ritual distance;
- material and emotional fallback.

They neither sanctify Takumi nor reject Hachi's decision. Their stance is conditional: if he intends to make a household and care for Hachi, then work at it.

### BLAST's training camp — band becomes temporary household

The camp is important because BLAST's professional future is being built through ordinary communal labor.

Members:

- wake;
- run;
- eat;
- clean;
- practice;
- meet;
- communicate with people outside.

The band is not only a stage unit. It increasingly functions as a domestic micro-society.

### The volume's home thesis

Volume 10 weakens any simple equation:

> **707 = true home / luxury apartment = false home.**

A stronger model is:

> **Home is a relational practice, but every home also depends on material conditions of safety, access, labor, money, and recognition.**

707 had intimacy without durable security.

The new apartment has security and material capacity but reduced social permeability.

Hachi wants to make the latter into a real home rather than merely inhabit a protected property.

## 12. Material and economic adulthood

Volume 10 is one of the clearest transitions from youthful relational drama into institutional adulthood.

### 12.1 Takumi's wealth is both power and care infrastructure

His economic capacity produces:

- protected housing;
- international travel;
- gifts;
- engagement jewelry;
- family provision;
- the promise that Hachi will not suffer materially.

This capacity undeniably increases his power over the structure of the relationship.

It also solves real problems.

A critique that treats every material provision as fake would ignore the text's concrete concern with pregnancy, paparazzi, absences, and future childcare.

### 12.2 Hachi wants domestic dependence without conceptualizing herself as merely purchased

Hachi is not pursuing an egalitarian dual-career household. Her domestic ideal remains strongly gendered and materially dependent on Takumi's income.

But she frames the project as something she wants to **build**, not merely consume.

Her domestic labor, emotional work, pregnancy, family-making, and desire for household continuity are part of what she understands herself to contribute.

Whether the arrangement can give her enough autonomy remains an open question.

### 12.3 Nobu confronts the cost of professional purity

Nobu's disgust at Gaia's possible exploitation of scandal is ethically intelligible.

Yasu's response is materially adult:

- a record company is not friendship;
- promotion requires capital;
- large institutions contain actors one cannot fully know;
- refusing compromised structures may also mean refusing the resources needed for the music to reach anyone.

The choice is not purity versus corruption in abstraction. It is **which compromises one can survive without surrendering the work's purpose**.

### 12.4 Yasu makes professional sacrifice materially real

Punching the reporter matters not only as masculine protection. It risks the non-band professional path that had remained available to him.

His later willingness to treat that loss as permission to “gamble” on BLAST converts care into opportunity cost.

### 12.5 Celebrity means entire economic ecologies depend on a band member

Ren's explanation to Nana is crucial. Trapnest is not simply Takumi's personal kingdom or four musicians playing for themselves.

It supports:

- production staff;
- record-company interests;
- broadcasters;
- employees;
- promotional partners;
- other dependent livelihoods.

This is why adult responsibility constrains romantic spontaneity. Success creates constituencies.

## 13. Music, celebrity, industry, and fashion

### 13.1 BLAST's debut begins under scandal rather than artistic self-definition

Nana's original fear has effectively happened: the band's public visibility is accelerated through association with Ren and celebrity scandal.

Her response is not retreat.

She decides to use the situation.

This is a major professional turn from:

> I want to succeed only on clean artistic terms

toward:

> I will not surrender authorship simply because the conditions of visibility are dirty.

The danger is that using the scandal can also make the scandal part of the band's permanent public identity.

### 13.2 Nana's 「汚い手」 rhetoric should not be over-literalized

She vows to use any dirty means necessary and to use Ren's name if that is what the world has handed her.

At this point the statement is a declaration of strategic hardness, not evidence that she has already embraced every unethical tactic.

Her immediate actions remain bounded by:

- performing;
- accepting promotion;
- using visibility;
- consenting to professional machinery she distrusts.

The distinction matters for future tracking.

### 13.3 Gaia is implicated in exploitation but not proven to have originated the leak

Yasu reconstructs a plausible institutional chain showing that Gaia and a television station could cooperate to turn the scandal into promotion. Kawano and Matsuo's discussion supports deliberate commercial exploitation of attention.

What Volume 10 does **not** establish is who initially exposed Nana and Ren.

Therefore:

- **scandal exploitation:** strongly established;
- **Gaia promotional complicity:** strongly established;
- **Gaia as original leak source:** unresolved.

### 13.4 The planted crowd is a miniature model of manufactured popularity

The promoter proposes approximately twenty **サクラ**—paid/placed people—to generate an initial crowd around guerrilla performances.

Nana instinctively rejects it.

The justification is sociological rather than purely fraudulent: passersby may not stop unless someone else has stopped first.

The scene asks an excellent celebrity question:

> If attention needs an artificial seed before genuine attention can accumulate, when does promotion become deception?

The text does not yet settle the line.

### 13.5 Public image is assembled from fragments rather than invented ex nihilo

The media stories contain lies, simplifications, selective facts, and sensational roles.

That is more dangerous than pure fabrication because some pieces are true.

The press can transform:

- Ren's past;
- Nana's relationship;
- Yasu's legal background;
- Shin's family history;

into narratively coherent “characters” that the subjects themselves would not recognize.

Celebrity is therefore not merely exposure. It is **loss of interpretive monopoly over one's own facts**.

### 13.6 Fashion and luxury objects remain forms of identity and hierarchy

Takumi's gifts perform several functions at once:

- affection;
- wealth display;
- compensation for absence;
- aesthetic selection;
- material incorporation of Hachi into his world.

The engagement ring differs from the travel gifts because Takumi insists it is not a souvenir; it is already hers. The distinction symbolically separates **consumption** from **commitment**, even though both are enabled by the same wealth.

## 14. Gender, sexuality, and social scripts

### 14.1 Hachi chooses a conventionally gendered family ideal

Her desired future remains recognizably traditional:

- husband as high-earning professional;
- Hachi as domestic center;
- child as household core;
- marriage as the social frame around pregnancy and family.

The analysis should neither romanticize nor dismiss this preference.

Hachi's desire for domesticity is one of her most persistent self-authored aspirations. The fact that it fits a gender norm does not make it unreal.

The relevant question is whether the chosen structure permits her enough safety, voice, mobility, and recognition.

### 14.2 Hachi's attraction to Takumi is not reducible to patriarchy, but it is shaped inside it

Hachi explicitly loves Takumi while also naming his selfishness.

Her desire appears attracted to:

- decisiveness;
- adult competence;
- status;
- sexuality;
- confidence;
- the sense that he can impose order on uncertainty.

Those traits also produce hierarchy.

The manga's interest lies in the same qualities becoming attractive and dangerous depending on context.

### 14.3 Takumi's ranking of work, women, and family is bluntly gendered

Takumi says work occupies more than ninety percent of his mind and women are second because they provide release. This is instrumental language.

The scene then complicates it with:

- the engagement ring;
- his promise of material security;
- interest in the unborn child;
- playful imagining of a daughter.

His affection does not remove the sexism of the hierarchy. The sexism does not prove the affection is fake.

### 14.4 The “number one woman” joke briefly weaponizes jealousy, then converts it into fatherhood fantasy

Takumi tells Hachi she is not even number one among women, only to reveal that the privileged “woman” is the imagined daughter.

The joke depends on his known sexual reputation and Hachi's insecurity, then reroutes that anxiety into a child-oriented domestic fantasy.

It is both teasing and reassurance.

### 14.5 Nana/Hachi remains structurally romance-like without sexual recoding

Volume 10 strengthens the relationship's centrality dramatically:

- hero/heroine language;
- jealousy;
- rescue fantasy;
- future confession that the Hachi bond became stronger than the Ren bond;
- Hachi's public defense of Nana's name;
- mutually defining narrative roles.

But no new evidence overturns Volume 8's explicit distinction between Nana's feelings and sexual desire.

Best current classification:

> **love-coded, romance-structured, possessive, narratively central, and categorically unresolved rather than textually established as a conventional sexual romance.**

### 14.6 Ren/Nana moves from fusion fantasy toward adult differentiation

The old fantasy of dying together is explicitly recalled.

Ren now says he cannot throw himself away for Nana in the same way Yasu can, despite still loving her.

Reira gives the mature alternative: not fusion, but carrying accumulated burdens together.

The manga is not saying weaker self-sacrifice means weaker love. It is testing whether love can survive becoming less absolute.

### 14.7 Shin/Reira remains ethically unresolved

Their email intimacy becomes warmer, and Shin's use of money becomes less purely transactional.

None of that cancels:

- Shin's age;
- Reira's adulthood;
- prior sexual/economic structure;
- asymmetry in resources and social power.

Tenderness is analytically relevant. It is not an ethical solvent.

## 15. Ethics, consent, coercion, care, and power

### 15.1 Volume 9's sexual-assault finding remains unchanged

Nothing in Volume 10 retrospectively converts the Chapter 29 encounter into consensual sex.

This needs explicit preservation because Volume 10 gives substantial evidence of:

- Hachi's love for Takumi;
- her voluntary marriage choice;
- affectionate reunion;
- domestic joking;
- willingness to build a family with him.

Later love is not retroactive consent.

The ethical ledger therefore must hold simultaneously:

> **Hachi loves Takumi and chooses him.**

and

> **Takumi raped Hachi in Volume 9.**

Those propositions are not logically incompatible.

### 15.2 Hachi's marriage choice is genuine but bounded

A coercion-only reading becomes much harder after Volume 10.

Hachi independently articulates:

- desire for the child;
- acceptance of uncertain paternity;
- love for Takumi;
- desire for her own family;
- intention to build happiness with Takumi and the child.

But choice occurs inside conditions Takumi strongly shapes:

- housing;
- money;
- public secrecy;
- travel schedules;
- marriage logistics;
- sexual history;
- major information flows.

The correct ethical category is **real agency under asymmetrical power**, not either total freedom or total coercion.

### 15.3 Nana's rescue fantasy risks disrespecting Hachi's agency

Nana's desire to “take Hachi back” is easy to sympathize with because the reader knows Takumi's harms and because Nana's love is intense.

Yet Volume 10 refuses to let that sympathy settle the question.

Nana herself later admits she did not want to hear about:

- Takumi;
- the child;
- Hachi's actual life.

Her rescue project is therefore partially built on avoiding the person she wants to rescue.

This is one of the manga's clearest critiques of protective love becoming paternalistic.

### 15.4 Hachi's small acts model care without jurisdiction

Hachi cannot fix Nana's scandal.

She cannot control the press.

She cannot safely join the band crisis.

She can:

- watch Nana;
- be strengthened by Nana's message;
- rewrite hostile graffiti;
- keep caring.

These acts do not author Nana's life. They accompany it.

That makes Hachi's care an important countermodel to Nana's hero fantasy.

### 15.5 Yasu's violence is protective but still violence with external cost

Yasu punches and threatens a reporter after the reporter crosses a line with Nana.

The scene invites satisfaction because the reporter is predatory and insulting.

The analysis should still register:

- assault is an escalation;
- Yasu exposes himself professionally;
- he may worsen public narratives around the band;
- Nana herself is angry that he has risked his future.

Protective motive does not erase consequence.

### 15.6 Media coercion operates without direct physical force

Reporters do not need to imprison Nana to constrain her.

They can alter:

- where she goes;
- what she can say;
- who can safely visit;
- how audiences interpret her;
- what companies do with her image.

Volume 10 expands the project's power analysis beyond interpersonal domination. Institutions can narrow autonomy through **attention**.

### 15.7 Reira's projection critique is an ethics of epistemic humility

To care responsibly for another person, one must recognize the possibility of being wrong about what that person feels.

This bears directly on:

- Nana reading Hachi;
- Nobu reading Hachi;
- Yasu historically reading Reira;
- the audience reading celebrity subjects;
- the critic reading the manga.

The volume's opening demand not to hurt others therefore includes a cognitive obligation: **do not confuse your interpretation with their interiority.**

## 16. Motif and lexical tracking

### 物語 / story

The dominant new motif.

Private relationships are understood as stories containing heroes, heroines, departures, returns, and roles. Public media simultaneously creates counterfeit or partial stories around the same people.

### ヒーロー / hero

Moves from Hachi's idealizing category for Nana to Nana's self-appointed rescue role, then is retrospectively relinquished by future Nana.

### ヒロイン / heroine

Future Nana's stronger and less controlling formulation: Hachi remains the heroine of Nana's own story.

### 取り戻す / take back

Continues Nana's possessive vocabulary around Hachi and becomes more problematic once Hachi's independent marriage choice is explicit.

### 幽霊 / ghost

Nana's image for Hachi inside an apartment officially described as empty. Strong emotional perception; unreliable objective characterization.

### 引き金 / trigger

Links paparazzi camera to firearm and celebrity exposure to irreversible fate change.

### ステージ / stage and リング / ring

Artistic platform becomes combat arena once public celebrity discourse arrives.

### 希望 / hope

Hachi's reproductive and domestic choice is framed through hope rather than certainty.

### 家庭 / household-family

Stronger than generic marriage vocabulary in defining what Hachi actually wants.

### 正義 / justice, したたか / tough-canny, ずる賢い / shrewd

Yasu's vocabulary of professional survival. Moral correctness is insufficient without strategic capacity.

### 運命共同体 / community of shared fate

Ren's definition of a professional band as obligation extending beyond private friendship.

### 荷物 / burden-luggage

Reira's mature-love metaphor: adulthood accumulates weight; partnership means carrying it together.

### キラキラ / glitter

Shin→Reira and Nana→Ren attachment-memory trigger. Affection reorganizes perception.

### LOVE / rewriting

Hachi's graffiti intervention converts public hostility into private counterstatement. Text itself becomes reparable even when social damage is not.

### サクラ / planted audience

Commercial attention seeded artificially in order to create conditions for real attention. A useful future motif for authenticity and promotion.

### number one / number two

Takumi's explicit ranking language makes hierarchy legible even inside affectionate domestic scenes.

## 17. Counterreadings, limitations, and hypothesis stress-test

### “Hachi is only marrying Takumi because he decided for her.”

**Substantially overturned by Volume 10.**

Hachi independently tells Shin she wants the child and hopes effort can make a family. She tells Junko she really loves Takumi. She later insists that she wants her own household and has decided to build happiness with Takumi and the child.

Qualification: Takumi still controls far more of the material process and the relationship remains asymmetrical.

### “Because Hachi chooses Takumi, Nobu was only a fantasy or mistake.”

**Rejected.**

Hachi asks about Nobu and remembers the full-moon period as perhaps the happiest, most cloudless happiness of her life.

Choosing a future does not rewrite the emotional truth of a past relationship.

### “Nana's plan to take Hachi back is straightforwardly heroic because Takumi is harmful.”

**Too simple.**

Nana's love is real and Takumi has seriously harmed Hachi. But Hachi currently chooses Takumi, while Nana later admits that jealousy made her avoid hearing what Hachi actually wanted to say about him and the child.

A rescue that refuses the subject's voice risks becoming another authorship imposed on Hachi.

### “Future Nana saying she cannot be Hachi's hero means their bond eventually becomes less important.”

**Directly contradicted by the same sequence.**

Future Nana says Hachi remains the heroine of her story. Later she says the Hachi bond became stronger than the Ren bond.

The hero role weakens; the bond does not.

### “Nana/Hachi is now textually confirmed as a sexual/romantic couple.”

**Still too strong.**

Volume 10 greatly strengthens romance-structural centrality and love-coding. It does not overturn the explicit earlier denial of sexual desire or establish a conventional mutual sexual romance.

The text is more interesting than either categorical simplification.

### “Gaia leaked the Nana/Ren story to manufacture BLAST's debut.”

**Not established.**

The company and television ecosystem clearly exploit the scandal and appear to coordinate promotion around it. The original leak source remains unresolved.

### “Takumi's tenderness in Volume 10 proves the Volume 9 sex was misunderstood.”

**Rejected.**

Consent is local to the encounter. Affection before or after does not change the explicit objections and continuation established in Volume 9.

### “Takumi is purely calculating and does not care about Hachi or the child.”

**Too strong.**

Counterevidence includes:

- ring;
- material planning;
- affectionate reunion;
- baby gift;
- child-oriented joking;
- family contact;
- explicit promise of provision.

But his own hierarchy remains starkly instrumental and his prior sexual violation remains decisive ethical evidence.

Best current reading: **care, desire, paternal investment, hierarchy, selfishness, and entitlement coexist.**

### “Ren is becoming cold because he no longer loves Nana enough.”

**Rejected by Ren's own statement.**

He says his feelings have not changed. What has changed is his capacity to sacrifice without regard for other obligations.

Reira's burden metaphor offers the volume's preferred alternative explanation: adulthood makes people heavier with responsibilities.

### “Yasu is definitively an illegal/unlicensed ‘dark lawyer.’”

**Too strong.**

The press uses sensational language. Mari explains the training system and offers an inference about Yasu's legal work and abandoned career path. His precise professional status should remain carefully classified until directly established.

### “Shin/Reira's emails show that the relationship has become ethically healthy.”

**Not established.**

The communication becomes more emotionally reciprocal and less purely transactional. The age, sexual history, money, and adult/minor power asymmetry remain.

### “The planted crowd proves BLAST's audience is fake.”

**Rejected.**

The proposal concerns seeding initial attention. It does not establish that later listeners, fans, or reactions are fabricated.

The more interesting question is how authentic public interest may be catalyzed by artificial visibility.

### “The media story is false, therefore the real private story is simple.”

**Rejected.**

The media often distorts through selection, simplification, and framing rather than inventing everything. The private relationships are themselves ambiguous, contradictory, and partially misunderstood by participants.

There is no pristine simple story waiting behind the tabloid one.

### “Hachi's domestic choice means Nana was entirely wrong to perceive isolation.”

**Too strong.**

Shin independently tells Hachi not to shut herself in and notes how little she goes out. Security blocks Nana. Hachi's old social access has narrowed.

Nana is wrong to reduce Hachi to a ghost existing only for Takumi and the child. She is not wrong that the new domestic structure carries isolating risk.

### “Nana's bond with Hachi replacing Ren means Nana no longer loves Ren.”

**Directly contradicted.**

The final pages deliberately hold both propositions together:

- Hachi bond stronger;
- Ren still loved;
- glitter still recalls Ren.

*NANA* continues to reject a one-slot model of intimacy.

## 20. Cumulative delta and retrospective-correction status

Volume 10 does more than advance events. It **revises the confidence level of several Volume 9 interpretations** while preserving others unchanged.

### Revised — Hachi/Takumi marriage authorship

**Volume 9 state:** Takumi announces marriage as though it is jointly decided, while Hachi's own language to her mother remains less finalized. The analysis therefore had to distinguish Takumi's declaration from Hachi's independent consent to the marriage plan.

**Volume 10 correction:** Hachi now supplies direct first-person authorship.

She says:

- she wants the child despite paternity uncertainty;
- she wants to hope a good family can be built through effort;
- she really loves Takumi;
- she wants a household of her own;
- she has decided to build happiness with Takumi and the child.

**Status:** `revised / strengthened`.

The best current model is no longer “Takumi may be converting his preferred solution into joint reality before Hachi has chosen it.” It is:

> **Takumi initially controlled the process and announcement, but Hachi subsequently and independently chooses the underlying marriage/family project.**

### Strengthened — Hachi has agency inside asymmetry rather than no agency

Volume 10 strongly resists a model of Hachi as merely carried by more powerful people.

She:

- confronts Shin's objections;
- reasons from her own reproductive ethics;
- confesses her own desire;
- insists on her household goal;
- asks for a ring;
- interprets Nana's message as a reason to act rather than collapse.

The asymmetry with Takumi remains substantial.

**Status:** `strengthened`.

### Preserved without revision — Volume 9 sexual-assault classification

Hachi's love, marriage choice, affection, and domestic hope do not retroactively alter consent in Chapter 29.

**Status:** `unchanged / high confidence`.

This is an important archival point because later relationship complexity must not silently rewrite earlier local evidence.

### Strengthened — Hachi/Nobu was emotionally real even though Hachi chooses Takumi

Hachi asks about Nobu and calls the full-moon period perhaps the happiest, most unclouded happiness of her life.

This directly weakens any interpretation in which Takumi's selection proves Nobu was a shallow detour.

**Status:** `strengthened`.

### Strengthened and revised — Nana/Hachi structural centrality

Volume 9 established:

- Hachi's singular-hero language;
- Nana's possessive longing;
- the love-letter classification;
- intense structural centrality without a clean sexual category.

Volume 10 adds:

- Nana's explicit self-appointment as hero of Hachi's story;
- future relinquishment of that hero role;
- future declaration that Hachi remains the heroine of Nana's story;
- Nana's statement that the Hachi bond became stronger than the Ren bond;
- Nana's admission that jealousy made her avoid hearing about Hachi's chosen life.

**Status:** `strongly strengthened, ethically complicated`.

The relationship becomes more central while the text becomes more critical of Nana's desire to direct it.

### Revised — the hero motif

**Earlier:** hero could be read mainly as Hachi's idealization of Nana and Nana's aspiration to appear for Hachi in an ideal form.

**Now:** hero becomes a contested relational office.

Present Nana wants to rescue and retrieve.

Future Nana gives up the right to define herself as Hachi's hero while retaining Hachi as her heroine.

**Status:** `revised`.

A likely long-term distinction is emerging:

> **heroism = acting upon/for the other; heroine recognition = admitting how central the other is to one's own story.**

That distinction should remain provisional until later volumes test it.

### Weakened — “Nana has learned how to love Hachi nonpossessively”

Volume 10 makes the weakness explicit.

Nana wants Hachi back, assumes Hachi's life is intolerable, and constructs a professional strategy partly to pull Hachi toward BLAST. Future Nana admits that jealousy prevented her from asking what Hachi actually felt.

**Status:** `weakened`.

### Strengthened — Nana can recognize her own interpretive failure retrospectively

The future narration does not merely mourn. It critiques present Nana:

- performing instead of listening;
- refusing topics she does not want to hear;
- failing to ask how Hachi is doing.

**Status:** `strengthened`.

This increases the evidentiary value of future Nana as a self-corrective narrator while still not making her omniscient.

### Revised — Nana/Ren: love does not require relational primacy

Volume 9 already showed Nana resisting the idea that Ren can “complete” her.

Volume 10 goes further:

- Nana says the Hachi bond feels stronger;
- she can feel lonely with Ren;
- she still loves Ren;
- glitter still recalls him;
- Ren says his own love remains unchanged despite reduced willingness for self-sacrifice.

**Status:** `revised / strengthened`.

The romance is moving from fusion fantasy toward differentiated attachment.

### Strengthened — adult love as burden-sharing rather than self-annihilation

Reira's `荷物` formulation gives the series a new explicit vocabulary for partnership.

This stands in deliberate tension with Nana's old question about dying together.

**Status:** `new strong thematic node`.

Takumi's Volume 10 material adds genuine evidence of:

Volume 10 adds an important correction: reporters can invade 707 precisely because its openness and known location make it vulnerable. The secure apartment is not security theater; it solves a real celebrity problem.

A home can become too closed to sustain community or too open to protect intimacy.

Volume 10 raises the cost of Yasu's protectiveness and reopens his legal/professional history. Reira's questions also keep the breakup motive unsettled.

**Status:** `revised / still partially unresolved`.

### Strengthened — Reira is becoming a voice of epistemic self-critique

Her Stella reflection is a striking corrective to her own tendency toward emotional projection.

**Status:** `strengthened`.

This may become important beyond her romantic history because it gives Reira a theory of why sincere empathy can still be wrong.

## 21. Volume thesis and questions carried forward

Volume 10 is the point where *NANA*'s private relationships become **competing narratives under public pressure**.

The volume opens by saying that exposing true feelings does not guarantee understanding. It then spends four chapters demonstrating the proposition at every scale.

Nana thinks she understands Hachi and imagines her as a ghost trapped inside Takumi's domestic system.

Hachi, meanwhile, tells Shin and Junko something Nana does not know: she wants the child, loves Takumi, and chooses to build a household with him despite recognizing uncertainty.

Nobu thinks he has disappeared from Hachi's story.

The reader knows Hachi still asks about him and remembers their full-moon happiness as perhaps the happiest period of her life.

The press tells stories about Nana, Ren, Yasu, and Shin from fragments that are partly true and profoundly deforming.

Gaia and television can then make those stories economically useful regardless of who first created them.

Nana answers by deciding to create a stronger counterstory.

She will appear in Hachi's life as BLAST's brilliant hero until Hachi returns.

That plan is loving, ambitious, artistically generative—and flawed.

The later Nana already knows why.

She was so determined to **show** Hachi something that she stopped **asking** Hachi something.

That is Volume 10's deepest relational correction:

> **Love does not grant authorship over the beloved.**

The hero wants to save, retrieve, perform, and fulfill.

The listener has to tolerate learning that the other person may want something painful, compromised, disappointing, or simply different.

Future Nana's role reversal makes this explicit.

She can no longer claim to be the hero of Hachi's story.

But Hachi remains the heroine of hers.

The loss of the hero role is therefore not the loss of love. It may be the beginning of a more honest form of it.

The volume applies the same correction to romance.

Hachi's decision to marry Takumi does not make Nobu unreal.

Nana's bond with Hachi becoming stronger than her bond with Ren does not make her stop loving Ren.

Ren's inability to die or destroy himself for Nana as readily as before does not mean his love has diminished.

Reira's burden metaphor supplies the emerging adult alternative:

> **people accumulate weight; intimacy may have to become the practice of carrying weight together rather than proving love through fusion or self-destruction.**

The music plot undergoes a parallel maturation.

BLAST enters professional life through exactly the kind of compromised visibility Nana feared. The camera becomes a gun. The stage becomes a ring. The company becomes both resource and possible exploiter. A promoter proposes manufacturing the first twenty spectators so that genuine spectators may gather.

Yasu's lesson to Nobu is severe:

> being right is not the same thing as being capable of winning.

Yet the manga does not celebrate cynical opportunism. Kawano's resistance reminds the reader that institutions really can destroy talent for profit. Nana's task is not to become morally empty enough to survive the industry. It is to become strategically capable without surrendering the reasons she wanted the stage.

Hachi's graffiti scene gives the volume's smallest and perhaps clearest answer.

She cannot control the magazine.

She cannot control television.

She cannot control Gaia.

She cannot safely run to Nana's apartment.

She finds **「ナナ死ね」** and rewrites it as **「ナナ LOVE♡」**.

That is not victory over the public story.

It is the refusal to leave someone else's story uncontested.

### Compact Volume 10 thesis

> **Volume 10 is about the struggle over who gets to tell a person's story. Hachi claims authorship of her own imperfect domestic future; Nana tries to author herself as Hachi's rescuer; the media authors celebrities from stolen fragments; industry learns to monetize those stories; and future Nana finally distinguishes loving Hachi from having the right to be Hachi's hero.**

### Questions carried into Volume 11

1. **What, precisely, is the “flaw” in Nana's plan?**
   - Future Nana identifies a defect before the volume ends.
   - Jealousy and failure to listen are already part of it, but the full consequence remains unknown.

2. **Can Nana remain central to Hachi without trying to retrieve or redirect her?**
   - The hero role is already retrospectively unstable.
   - The heroine formulation suggests another relational model may be possible.

3. **How will Hachi's chosen family project operate in ordinary daily life?**
   - The intention is now clear.
   - Its actual distribution of time, labor, mobility, affection, and authority remains to be tested.

4. **How will Takumi's explicit priority system affect Hachi?**
   - Work occupies almost all of him.
   - Material provision is promised in place of abundant attention.
   - Hachi currently accepts that bargain.

5. **Will Takumi's paternal investment alter his behavior or merely add another protected priority?**
   - The baby gift and “Sachiko” joking are genuine evidence of imaginative attachment.
   - Their long-term significance is unknown.

6. **What happens when Hachi and Nobu next occupy the same social space?**
   - Hachi has not emotionally erased him.
   - Nobu falsely believes he has largely disappeared from her story.

7. **Will Nobu's self-blame become professional growth, relational withdrawal, or both?**
   - Guitar practice converts grief into labor.
   - His account of “doing nothing” remains harsher than the evidence warrants.

8. **Can Nana and Ren construct the burden-sharing relationship Reira describes?**
   - Love remains.
   - Fusion has weakened.
   - Both now carry institutions and other people with them.

9. **Why did Yasu and Reira actually separate?**
   - Volume 10 destabilizes the simple career explanation again.
   - Motive remains unresolved.

10. **What exactly is Yasu's legal/professional status?**
    - Media labels are sensational.
    - Mari supplies a plausible account, not final documentary proof.

11. **Who originated the Nana/Ren exposure?**
    - Gaia's exploitation is evident.
    - Origin remains unproven.

12. **How far will Gaia and media partners manufacture BLAST's visibility?**
    - The proposed planted crowd creates a concrete authenticity problem.
    - Nana rejects it, but the larger promotional machine is already active.

13. **Can BLAST distinguish strategic compromise from surrender of authorship?**
    - Yasu pushes cunning.
    - Nobu resists dirtiness.
    - Nana embraces aggressive use of the situation.

14. **What does celebrity do to 707 as a possible home?**
    - Reporters have already made its openness unsafe.
    - Emotional home and viable residence may diverge permanently.

15. **Will Hachi's social narrowing increase or reverse?**
    - Shin notices isolation.
    - Her family remains available.
    - Nana's group is increasingly difficult to access directly.

16. **How will the pregnancy become publicly managed?**
    - Kyosuke's warning shows that Hachi's privacy is now tied directly to Trapnest's publicity risk.

17. **Will Nana's health/memory irregularities return?**
    - Volume 9's memory-loss concern and unexplained cheek wound remain unresolved evidence nodes.

18. **How will Shin/Reira's relationship change as correspondence becomes less transactional?**
    - Emotional specificity is increasing.
    - The age/power problem remains unchanged in principle.

19. **Will Reira's new epistemic humility alter how she treats other people's desires?**
    - Her Stella reflection shows genuine self-awareness about projection.

20. **Does the public hero/heroine vocabulary remain central as BLAST becomes famous?**
    - Nana's public image and Hachi's private image of her are now on a collision course.

21. **Can a person remain the heroine of someone else's story without becoming responsible for that person's happiness?**
    - This is the most important relational question produced by the volume's ending.
