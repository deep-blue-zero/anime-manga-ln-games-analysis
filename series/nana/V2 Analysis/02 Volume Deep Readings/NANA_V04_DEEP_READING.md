# 『NANA』Volume 4 Deep Reading
## Losing the boyfriend, discovering the central relationship: betrayal, jealousy, home, and the return of Ren

**Canonical artifact:** `NANA_V04_DEEP_READING.md`  
**Cumulative scope:** Volumes 1–4 only.  
**Spoiler boundary:** No material from Volume 5 onward, *NANA 7.8*, the anime, later chapters, or remembered later developments is used to settle the meaning of Volume 4.  
**Methodological status:** First volume analyzed natively under `NANA_ANALYTICAL_METHOD_V2`, with evidence IDs and verified EPUB spine-page locators created during the primary-source reading.

---

## 1. Central thesis and volume role

Volume 4 is the first volume in which *NANA* makes a major relationship collapse and then asks where the emotional energy of that collapse goes.

Hachi loses Shoji.

But the volume does not structurally leave her alone.

Instead, the need that had been invested in the boyfriend category is redistributed across a relationship that the manga has already made difficult to classify: Hachi and Nana.

That movement is not subtle by the end of the volume.

Hachi:

- refuses Nana's instruction to **fight** Sachiko and **take Shoji back**;
- calls the breakup her own **loss** even while refusing the possessive contest;
- recognizes that she has a habit of becoming too absorbed in romance;
- declares that having Nana should make a boyfriend unnecessary;
- becomes jealous when Misato receives Nana's affection;
- imagines Nana and Misato kissing and then panics into the self-correction **「彼氏作らなきゃ…」**;
- says explicitly that Nana is **more important than a boyfriend**;
- demands a voice in who may stay inside Apartment 707 because it is **her home too**;
- asks Nana to disclose more of herself;
- engineers the circumstances in which Nana will see Ren again;
- and, in the final retrospective image, remembers unknowingly holding Nana's hand and says that she really wanted to keep holding it **forever**.

Volume 3 had already made a purely non-romantic account of Hachi's feelings toward Nana difficult to sustain by giving future Hachi the formulations **「かなり恋に近い」** and **「初恋みたい」**. Volume 4 does something more analytically useful: it **stress-tests that coding in ordinary relational behavior**.

Admiration becomes jealousy.

Domestic comfort becomes a dispute over shared-space boundaries.

Abstract intimacy becomes a request for reciprocal disclosure.

A declaration that women are safer because they do not provoke jealousy is immediately falsified by Hachi's jealousy toward another woman.

And the person Hachi claims should replace romantic need becomes the person she places above the boyfriend category itself.

At the same time, the volume refuses to make this a simple queer-awakening story. Hachi continues to desire men. She constructs an explicitly masculine fantasy of the **ナイト / knight** who will protect her. She tells herself she needs **「あたしを可愛がってくれる優しい男」**. When her jealousy of Misato becomes too difficult to categorize, she restores the heterosexual script instead of abandoning it.

The result is more interesting than categorical resolution:

> **Volume 4 shows that Hachi's relationship with Nana is becoming emotionally central faster than Hachi's available social categories can explain it.**

Nana undergoes an equally important movement in the opposite direction.

The woman who has presented herself as autonomous, rough, sexually confident, and difficult to embarrass admits that:

- she has never really had a close female friend;
- she does not know how such a friendship is supposed to work;
- she is poor at voluntarily narrating herself;
- she was raised without a father by her maternal grandmother;
- her grandmother died when she was fifteen;
- and she envies the noisy, warm household atmosphere Hachi takes for granted.

The strongest new information about Nana is therefore not simply that she still has unresolved feelings toward Ren.

It is that **her independence increasingly becomes legible as something built around deprivation**.

The volume does not invalidate her autonomy. Nana really did choose music, Tokyo, BLAST, and a life not subordinated to Ren. But her strength can no longer be read as evidence that she does not need forms of attachment she has rarely possessed.

This makes the final convergence especially powerful.

The Trapnest concert appears to set up Nana/Ren as the obvious emotional climax. Ren is physically onstage, publicly transformed into a celebrity version of the man Nana once knew. Nana cries. Hachi understands that the question **「今でもレンを好き？」** is too small for the accumulated history between them.

Yet Yazawa ends the volume not on Ren's face, not on the stage, and not on a reunion.

She ends on **Nana and Hachi's joined hands**.

Future Hachi says:

> **「あの時 知らぬまに つないでいた その手を」**  
> **「本当は ずっと 握りしめていたかった」**  
> **「いつまでもずっと」**

The volume therefore performs a striking structural double movement:

> Ren returns as Nana's unresolved romantic past at exactly the moment the manga confirms how much Hachi wants Nana in her own future.

That is Volume 4's central achievement.

## 3. Narrative architecture and causal close read

### Movement I — The affair ceases to be dramatic irony and becomes Hachi's reality

Volume 3 ended with a brutal information asymmetry.

The reader knew about Shoji and Sachiko.

Junko and Kyosuke knew enough to understand the danger.

Hachi did not.

Volume 4 destroys that asymmetry almost immediately.

The chapter opens with Hachi again registering Tokyo through disappointed expectation. Even in May, the night wind feels cold; Tokyo is not the warmer place she imagined when she moved from her mountain hometown (NANA_V04_E001).

The meteorological observation is simple, but it fits the emotional architecture precisely.

Tokyo was supposed to be:

- Shoji;
- adulthood;
- proximity;
- warmth;
- the place where desire would finally become ordinary life.

Instead Hachi literally feels cold.

Soon afterward she sees Shoji and Sachiko together with her own eyes.

This matters because the betrayal is no longer:

- Junko's warning;
- reader knowledge;
- Shoji's private guilt;
- or Sachiko's abstract fear of hurting a girlfriend she has never met.

Hachi becomes the perceiving subject.

Sachiko also ceases to be only the kind young coworker whom Hachi once helped after an injury. The woman Hachi compassionately recognized in Volume 3 and the woman sleeping with Shoji become the same person in Hachi's field of vision.

The recognition is ethically cruel because the rival was humanized **before** Hachi learned she was a rival.

### Sachiko refuses the seductress alibi

Sachiko's response to exposure is important.

She says:

> **「好きになったのは あたしの方だもん」**

and:

> **「彼女がいるって 分かってても どうしても 止められなかったんだもん」**

Then she tells Nana to hit her if that will settle things (NANA_V04_E004).

Sachiko is not claiming innocence.

That does not absolve her.

It does prevent a reading in which she becomes only:

> the passive girl Shoji happened to fall for.

She names her own desire and her own failure to stop acting under conditions she knew were ethically compromised.

Shoji, meanwhile, physically shields Sachiko.

Whatever residual affection he has toward Hachi, his body has now made a choice before he has provided Hachi an ethically clean ending.

### Nana converts betrayal into combat

Nana's reaction is protective and deeply revealing.

She wants Hachi to fight.

Her language is blunt:

> **「戦わなきゃ負けだぞ！」**  
> **「てめぇの男だろ！」**  
> **「取り返せ！」**

(NANA_V04_E005)

The moral intuition beneath Nana's anger is understandable:

> Hachi has been betrayed and should not simply disappear while the people who betrayed her determine the outcome.

But Nana expresses that intuition through a vocabulary of:

- victory;
- defeat;
- ownership;
- reclamation.

Shoji becomes **「てめぇの男」**—your man—and the answer to betrayal becomes **取り返す**, taking him back.

That is one of the first places where the project's distinction between **care** and **possession** becomes concretely useful.

Nana is caring for Hachi.

Nana's model of how to care is also possessive.

### Hachi's refusal is stronger than her self-interpretation

Hachi's answer is only:

> **「いらない……」**

(NANA_V04_E006)

The minimalism matters.

She does not make a counterargument.

She does not demand punishment.

She does not try to prove she loves Shoji more.

She does not accept Nana's premise that the correct response is competitive reclamation.

She refuses the object.

Yet almost immediately her retrospective language accepts the **competition metaphor** she behaviorally rejected.

She says she was:

- not adult enough to forgive betrayal;
- not single-minded enough to cling despite being hurt;
- and therefore:

> **「あたしの 負けだよ」**

(NANA_V04_E007)

This contradiction should be preserved.

Hachi's action says:

> I do not want a relationship I must win back through possession.

Her self-narration says:

> I lost.

Volume 4 is already showing that characters can act more wisely than the stories they tell about their own actions.

### Junko distinguishes causal contribution from betrayal responsibility

The post-breakup conversation with Junko is ethically difficult and should not be flattened into either:

> Junko cruelly victim-blames Hachi

or:

> Junko delivers the author's final judgment.

Junko explicitly says that Shoji was wrong to drift toward another woman without first ending the relationship:

> **「けじめもつけずに 他の女になびいた 章司が悪いよ」**

But she also tells Hachi that the relationship had problems before the affair and criticizes Hachi's tendency to cast herself too quickly as victim (NANA_V04_E008).

Her more useful formulation follows:

> **「恋愛だって 人間同士の 関わり合いなんだから」**  
> **「相手を 思いやれなきゃ 上手く 行きっこ ないよ」**

(NANA_V04_E009)

The distinction is important:

- Hachi can have contributed to a bad relationship dynamic;
- Shoji can still be responsible for choosing deception and infidelity;
- those propositions do not cancel each other.

Junko's **「被害者ぶる」** wording remains harsher than necessary and deserves resistance. A betrayed person really has been wronged. Hachi's failures in attentiveness do not make the betrayal symmetrical.

But Junko's larger principle—that romance is a form of reciprocal human relationship rather than a status that maintains itself automatically—becomes productive because Hachi immediately applies it elsewhere.

### The lesson moves from romance into friendship

When Nana returns to 707 with food, Hachi realizes she has been thinking almost entirely about her own pain and not about Nana's needs.

The scene is domestic rather than dramatic.

Nana comes home.

Hachi is there.

Food and tea become the medium of repair.

Hachi effectively imports Junko's principle into friendship:

> **「友情だって 人間同士の関わり合いなのよ」**

(NANA_V04_E010)

This is one of the volume's quietest but most important developmental steps.

Hachi does not learn:

> stop depending on people.

She learns something closer to:

> **relationships require reciprocal attention, including the relationship that currently feels safest.**

The breakup therefore becomes causally productive in the Nana/Hachi dyad.

### The ticket converts contingency into destiny

The two front-row Trapnest tickets arrive exactly when Hachi has been emotionally destabilized.

Future Hachi interprets them as more than luck:

> **「あれは神様がナナの為に 用意してくれたチケットだったね」**

(NANA_V04_E011)

This should not be promoted to cosmological fact.

It is evidence about Hachi.

She narrativizes coincidence.

When ordinary causality produces an emotionally meaningful event, she assigns it a story-shaped purpose.

The ticket therefore becomes the mechanism through which Hachi's next project emerges:

> if her own romance has failed, perhaps she can help Nana's unfinished romance become whole.

That is loving.

It is also the beginning of another form of overinvolvement.

### Movement II — Hachi replaces the boyfriend fantasy with a supposedly safer Nana fantasy

Chapter 10 opens with one of future Hachi's clearest self-indictments:

> **「あの頃 あたしは 誰かを上手に愛する事も出来ないのに 誰かに愛されたくて仕方なかった」**

(NANA_V04_E012)

The syntax matters.

The problem is not:

> I loved too much.

It is an asymmetry between:

- wanting to receive love;
- and not yet knowing how to love another person well.

That idea is consistent with the Shoji aftermath, but the volume immediately refuses to let Hachi solve it by simply abandoning attachment.

### Nana becomes the imagined solution to heterosexual instability

Hachi tells herself:

> **「あたしにはナナがいるし♡」**

Then:

> **「ナナがいれば寂しくないし」**  
> **「しかも女同士だから変な気も使わなくて済むし」**  
> **「妙なヤキモチとかもやかなくて済むし」**  
> **「安心してずっと一緒にいられるもん」**

(NANA_V04_E013)

This is almost a theory of same-sex friendship as a safe domestic institution.

Its components are revealing:

- no loneliness;
- no awkward gendered romantic performance;
- no jealousy;
- security;
- indefinite coexistence.

Hachi is not merely saying:

> Nana is a good friend.

She is assigning Nana the functions that romantic partnership failed to provide.

Then she explicitly contrasts romance:

> love is tiring;
> love hurts;
> love is consumptive.

The irony is immediate.

The thing Hachi thinks friendship with Nana will eliminate—**jealousy**—appears within the same chapter.

### The knight fantasy shows that Hachi has not ceased wanting heterosexual care

Hachi also says:

> **「あたしが欲しいのは彼氏じゃなくて」**  
> **「ただ誠実にあたしを守ってくれるナイトなの」**

(NANA_V04_E014)

This is not a rejection of men.

It is a refinement of her romantic fantasy.

The desired man is no longer simply:

- attractive;
- exciting;
- a boyfriend;
- proof that she is loved.

He is supposed to provide:

- fidelity;
- protection;
- gentleness;
- security.

This matters because later material should not be allowed to caricature Hachi's desire for domestic protection as simple shallowness. Volume 4 already shows the desire becoming more specific in response to betrayal.

It also remains vulnerable to paternalism:

> what happens when the man who can protect her also believes protection gives him authority?

That question is not answered here, but the linguistic groundwork is now present.

### Movement III — BLAST becomes public, and Nana becomes an object Hachi can lose to an audience

The Tokyo BLAST performance is more than a band milestone.

It changes the Nana/Hachi relationship because Nana becomes **publicly legible as an artist**.

Volume 2 let Hachi hear Nana sing in 707.

Volume 3 rebuilt BLAST.

Volume 4 places Nana on a stage in front of strangers.

### Hachi hears Nana's abandonment inside the songs

Hachi's internal response is unusually direct:

> **「両足で踏んばって」**  
> **「立っているのが精一杯で」**  
> **「独り置いてきぼりになる」**  
> **「ナナはそんな歌ばかり歌ってた」**

Then:

> **「それは あたしの気持ちだよ」**  
> **「遠くに行かないで ナナ」**

(NANA_V04_E015)

Two things happen simultaneously.

First, Hachi begins hearing Nana's artistic persona as an archive of Nana's own abandonment experience.

Second, she uses Nana's song to articulate **her own separation anxiety toward Nana**.

The fear is not:

> I hope BLAST succeeds.

It is:

> **do not go somewhere I cannot follow.**

This is the dark underside of Hachi's enthusiastic support for Nana's career.

She wants Nana to become extraordinary.

She also wants Nana to remain available as the person who makes her own life feel safe.

Those desires may eventually conflict.

### Misato introduces fandom as relational competition

Misato's presence intensifies this conflict.

She is not only a fan.

She is a person who:

- knows BLAST history Hachi does not know;
- admires Nana intensely;
- cries at the performance;
- receives Nana's physical affection;
- enters the 707 social world.

Hachi reacts:

> **「あの子 本気でナナの事好きなのかな」**  
> **「女同士なのに変なの！」**  
> **「ヤキモチなんか やくはずないと思ってたのに」**  
> **「変なのは あたしの方かも」**

(NANA_V04_E016)

This directly disproves Hachi's earlier claim that being two women guarantees freedom from jealousy.

More importantly, Hachi recognizes the contradiction herself.

She has a category:

> women friends do not produce romantic jealousy.

She has an affect:

> I am jealous.

The affect survives the category.

### The erotic fantasy is comic—and still evidence

Hachi then imagines Nana and Misato kissing.

Her thought process explicitly asks:

> **「でも 女同士って どーなるんだろう」**

After the fantasy appears, she panics:

> **「ヤバー！」**  
> **「もう少しで妄想するところだった！」**  
> **「彼氏作らなきゃ…」**

(NANA_V04_E017)

The comedy should not be discarded as analytically meaningless.

The joke works because Hachi's mind crosses a boundary she has just declared conceptually strange.

Then she repairs the breach by reasserting heterosexual normality:

> get a boyfriend.

The strongest careful interpretation is not:

> Hachi has now discovered a stable lesbian identity.

The text does not establish that.

It is:

> **Hachi's emotional attachment to Nana generates jealousy and erotic imagination that her existing heterosexual self-concept cannot comfortably absorb, so she manages the dissonance by returning to the boyfriend script.**

That is a much more precise claim.

### Hachi is more self-aware than her comic presentation suggests

The next page prevents us from treating her as merely confused.

She says:

> **「だいたい あたしは 恋に のめり込み過ぎるんだ」**

and recognizes that she loses perspective, spins herself up, and repeatedly damages relationships (NANA_V04_E018).

She also says she feels both emotionally and physically frustrated and still wants a gentle man who will cherish her.

So Volume 4 does not give us a Hachi who has simply transferred orientation from men to Nana.

It gives us a woman who is beginning to perceive that **her style of attachment** is itself a recurring problem.

That is far more important for the long-term character study.

### Movement IV — Apartment 707 becomes a home precisely because it develops boundaries

Misato's stay produces the first serious domestic sovereignty conflict between Nana and Hachi.

Hachi says:

> **「ここはあたしの家でもあるんだから」**  
> **「勝手な真似ばっかしないでよ ナナ！」**

(NANA_V04_E019)

This is a major advance in the home ledger.

In Volume 2, 707 became home through coincidence, shared rent, food, routine, and mutual choice.

In Volume 3, future Hachi endowed its table and chairs with archival meaning.

Volume 4 adds something essential:

> **home requires jurisdiction.**

Who may enter?

Who may stay overnight?

Who gets to decide?

What must be mutually agreed because the dwelling belongs to both people?

Hachi's protest is therefore not reducible to jealousy.

Jealousy energizes it.

The substantive claim is also legitimate:

> shared home means shared authority.

Nana leaves with Misato and does not return until morning (NANA_V04_E021).

The home can be exited.

Its continuity is not guaranteed.

That is exactly why the next retrospective passage hurts.

### Future Hachi names the weakness present Hachi could not see

Against a nearly empty white page, future Hachi says:

> **「思い返す度 胸が痛む」**  
> **「あの頃 あたしが もう少し大人で」**  
> **「ナナの弱さに気づいてあげていたら」**  
> **「今とは違う未来があったの？」**

(NANA_V04_E020)

This is one of the most important temporal statements in the series so far.

It establishes several things and leaves others open.

It establishes:

- future Hachi believes Nana possessed a weakness present Hachi failed to recognize;
- Hachi associates her own immaturity with that failure;
- the memory still causes pain;
- she imagines a counterfactual future in which better recognition might have mattered.

It does **not** establish:

- what later event Hachi is referring to;
- that Hachi could actually have prevented it;
- that her counterfactual is objectively correct;
- or that Nana's future is Hachi's responsibility.

This distinction is exactly why the v2 prospective/retrospective split matters.

Hachi's regret is fact.

The causal efficacy of her imagined alternative is not.

### Repair produces the clearest relational hierarchy yet

When Nana comes back, both women apologize.

Hachi explains how worried she was because Nana had no mobile phone and could not easily be located.

Nana jokingly asks:

> **「あたしは あんたの彼氏か！」**

Hachi answers:

> **「彼氏より ずっと大事だよ ナナは！」**

(NANA_V04_E022)

The importance of this line should not be minimized by saying:

> close friends can obviously be more important than boyfriends.

Of course they can.

The analytical point is that *NANA* itself is explicitly ranking relational categories after a volume in which:

- the boyfriend relationship has collapsed;
- Hachi has called Nana the safe alternative to romance;
- Hachi has become jealous of another woman's intimacy with Nana;
- Hachi has sexually imagined that intimacy;
- and Hachi has asserted joint rights to their home.

The line does not prove that Nana and Hachi are a canonical couple.

It does prove that the manga is deliberately asking the reader not to assume that the socially named romantic relationship is automatically the most important one.

### Movement V — Ren becomes public information, while Nana remains private information

Chapter 11 reverses an important asymmetry.

Hachi has lived closely with Nana.

She knows:

- how Nana eats;
- how she sleeps;
- how she performs;
- how she teases;
- how she occupies 707.

But she does not know one of the largest facts organizing Nana's emotional history:

> the Ren who used to play in BLAST is the same Ren who is now famous in Trapnest.

Hachi learns this indirectly through:

- Misato's fan knowledge;
- a music magazine;
- Nobu;
- public celebrity discourse.

The irony is sharp.

The person closest to Nana domestically must use **public media** to discover Nana's private past.

This becomes a central problem of Volume 4:

> proximity is not the same thing as knowledge.

### Nobu explains the wound as artistic as well as romantic

Nobu's explanation prevents Ren's departure from being reduced to Nana's broken heart.

He describes feeling betrayed when Ren left BLAST for Trapnest, while also separating that hurt from his respect for Ren's talent (NANA_V04_E024).

This gives the break multiple levels:

- romantic: Nana/Ren;
- communal: Ren/BLAST;
- artistic: local band versus professional opportunity;
- masculine friendship: Nobu/Ren;
- aspirational: what it means to be left behind by someone talented enough to enter the professional world first.

Nana's later drive to make BLAST succeed can therefore plausibly contain several motives simultaneously:

- self-authorship;
- artistic ambition;
- pride;
- refusal to be the abandoned person;
- desire to stand on equal ground with Ren.

No one of those should be promoted into a total explanation yet.

### Hachi becomes a benevolent manipulator

Once Hachi knows, she begins planning the Trapnest concert around Nana.

She imagines Ren seeing Nana in the front row.

She imagines him jumping from the stage to embrace her.

She decides to pretend she does not know the truth so Nana will not become embarrassed and refuse to attend.

The intention is loving.

The method is revealing.

Hachi wants to create the right emotional outcome by arranging other people's circumstances.

That resembles her broader romantic imagination:

> if the correct scene can be produced, the correct future may follow.

This is not sinister.

But it is worth tracking because Hachi's care can become **authorship of another person's story**.

The volume itself partially resists that impulse by keeping Nana's answer opaque.

### Movement VI — Hachi's inherited home reveals the deprivation beneath Nana's autonomy

Chapter 12 takes Nana into Hachi's family world before the concert.

This is structurally important because it gives the manga two different models of home in direct succession:

- **707** — chosen, urban, peer-built, fragile, negotiated;
- **Hachi's family home** — inherited, intergenerational, noisy, already furnished with kinship roles.

Nana moves into the second space as guest.

### Nana's register change matters

With Hachi's mother, Nana uses ordinary polite social Japanese:

> **「初めまして 大崎ナナです」**  
> **「お世話になります」**

(NANA_V04_E026)

This is useful linguistic evidence because Nana's roughness is not inability.

She can switch registers.

Her rough speech with Hachi, Nobu, Yasu, Shoji, and the BLAST circle is therefore relational and identity-bearing, not evidence that she lacks conventional social competence.

### Nana praises Hachi in language Hachi rarely hears directly

When speaking to Hachi's family, Nana characterizes Hachi as bright, straightforward, kind, and encouraging to be around.

Hachi's reaction matters because Nana does not usually narrate their relationship in sentimental language to Hachi's face.

The family setting externalizes what the pair often communicates indirectly.

### Nana finally explains the absence behind 「故郷などない」

Volume 3 gave Nana the absolute line:

> **「あたしには故郷などない」**

Volume 4 gives the biographical structure underneath it.

Nana tells Hachi:

> **「親父も最初からいないし」**  
> **「母方のおばあちゃんとこに ずっと預けられてて」**  
> **「でも おばあちゃんも死んで…15の時に」**

Then:

> **「だから ちょっと憧れる」**  
> **「こーゆー にぎやかな 家庭の雰囲気とか」**  
> **「縁がなかっただけに」**  
> **「楽しいよ」**

(NANA_V04_E027)

This substantially revises the cumulative home model.

Nana's rejection of origin-home is not equivalent to indifference toward domestic warmth.

She says the opposite.

She **憧れる / akogareru**—longs for, admires, aspires toward—the kind of family atmosphere Hachi treats as ordinary.

This creates a crucial parallel:

- Hachi is trying to build the stable home she imagines adulthood should contain;
- Nana has scarcely experienced inherited home as a stable category at all.

The shared 707 household may therefore satisfy different needs for each woman.

For Hachi, it is an adult domestic future she unexpectedly creates before marriage.

For Nana, it may be a form of family/home she did not inherit.

That asymmetry deserves long-term tracking.

### Nana admits she does not know how to have a close female friend

Hachi cries because Nana has finally told her something personal.

She says she had felt lonely because Nana did not talk about herself.

Nana answers:

> **「あたし今まで 親しい女友達って いなかったから」**  
> **「どーつき合えば いーか いまいち よく分かんなくて」**

and explains that talking voluntarily about herself is difficult, so she lets things go unsaid (NANA_V04_E028).

This is one of the volume's most important Nana lines.

It changes the meaning of her opacity.

Some of what Hachi experienced as:

- coolness;
- secrecy;
- strength;
- independence;

may also be:

- lack of practice;
- fear of exposure;
- difficulty initiating intimacy;
- an attachment style developed without ordinary peer or family security.

We should not medicalize this into diagnosis.

The manga gives us a simpler and stronger description:

> **Nana does not know how to do this kind of closeness because she has not had it before.**

That makes Hachi's importance to her easier to see without needing to decide what category the relationship ultimately belongs to.

---

### Movement VII — 「今でもレンを好き？」 is too simple a question

Hachi finally asks:

> **「今でもレンを好き？」**

Nana does not answer yes or no.

Later she says:

> **「知ってて あたしを連れて来てくれたんだね」**  
> **「ありがとう」**

(NANA_V04_E029)

This is not an accidental evasion.

The entire volume has been teaching Hachi that relational categories can be too simple for lived attachment.

Shoji can have loved Hachi and fallen seriously for Sachiko.

Hachi can want men and become jealous of Misato over Nana.

Nana can refuse to follow Ren and still be organized by the history of loving him.

So future/present Hachi concludes:

> **「好きだなんて言葉 軽々しく口に出来る程 単純な想いなら とっくに会いに行ってたよね」**

(NANA_V04_E030)

This is Hachi's interpretation, not an omniscient statement.

But it is an increasingly mature one.

She is beginning to understand that:

> **the intensity of a relationship cannot always be measured by whether someone can say “I still like/love you.”**

Nana's refusal to go after Ren does not prove absence of love.

It may prove that the attachment is entangled with:

- pride;
- betrayal;
- autonomy;
- career;
- grief;
- identity;
- the fear of becoming subordinate to someone else's path.

The manga has not yet resolved which mixture governs.

---

### Movement VIII — Public spectacle makes private history visible

The Trapnest concert is the volume's obvious dramatic climax.

But it is formally more complex than:

> Nana sees her ex-boyfriend.

Ren has become public property.

His face appears in magazines, promotional posters, tickets, merchandise, and the stage apparatus of a major band.

Nana's private history with him is now embedded inside a celebrity identity thousands of strangers consume.

Hachi sits at the front row as the person who knows Nana intimately **now**, watching the man who knew Nana intimately **before**.

That position is almost geometrically perfect for the series' relational architecture.

### Nana and Hachi cry for different but connected reasons

Hachi is overwhelmed by the performance and by the magnitude of the moment.

Nana cries while watching Ren.

Yazawa cuts between:

- Ren onstage;
- Reira under stage light;
- Hachi's tears;
- Nana's tears;
- the crowd;
- the private reactions inside public spectacle.

The sequence makes visible a theme that will likely matter increasingly as the bands become more public:

> **fame creates situations in which thousands of people can look at the same body while only a few understand why that body matters personally.**

### Future Hachi's prayer creates anticipatory dread without establishing the future

Over the performance, retrospective narration says she repeatedly prayed that night for Nana to be happy (NANA_V04_E032).

The wording carries ominous force.

But methodologically it must remain foreshadowing, not retrospective fact inflation.

The page tells us:

- future Hachi remembers praying;
- the memory has enough emotional weight to be narrated from later;
- happiness is not presented as uncomplicated.

It does not yet tell us what occurs later.

### The volume's true last image belongs to Nana/Hachi

The final page shows joined hands.

Future Hachi says:

> **「あの時 知らぬまに つないでいた その手を」**  
> **「本当は ずっと 握りしめていたかった」**  
> **「いつまでもずっと」**

(NANA_V04_E033)

This ending transforms the concert's apparent romantic geometry.

Ren is the person Nana is looking toward.

Hachi is the person physically holding Nana.

Hachi's later memory is not:

> I wish I could have made Nana reunite with Ren.

It is:

> **I wanted to keep holding your hand.**

That does not erase Ren.

It tells us that the manga is building two different forms of relational permanence at the same time.

## 4. Nana Osaki — character-state update

### What Nana wants

By the end of Volume 4, Nana's wants are more legible but less simple.

She wants:

- BLAST to succeed;
- artistic standing that belongs to her rather than to Ren;
- Hachi to be protected from humiliation;
- 707 to remain livable;
- intimacy with Hachi without possessing an established script for female friendship;
- contact with the warmth of Hachi's family world;
- and, very strongly implied, some form of contact or reckoning with Ren that she cannot reduce to the simple verb **好き**.

### What she fears

The volume gives increasing evidence for fears around:

- abandonment;
- humiliation;
- losing relational control;
- being made dependent;
- revealing need;
- and being forced into a subordinate position inside another person's future.

None of those is stated as a diagnostic list by Nana.

They emerge from behavior.

### Protection and possession become intertwined

Her command to Hachi—**fight, reclaim your man**—shows that Nana interprets relationship loss through a contest logic she herself has reason to understand.

This does not mean Nana literally believes people are property.

It means that when attachment is threatened, her instinctive vocabulary is competitive and possessive.

That should be tracked longitudinally.

### Her stage identity reveals vulnerability she does not narrate conversationally

Hachi hears BLAST's songs as being about:

- barely standing;
- being left behind;
- enduring alone.

Whether every lyric should be read autobiographically is unresolved.

But the juxtaposition is important:

> **Nana can sing abandonment publicly more easily than she can explain private vulnerability to Hachi.**

Art may therefore function as emotional disclosure through indirection.

### Her female-friendship admission is a major revision

The line **「親しい女友達っていなかった」** changes how we should read much of Nana's social style.

Hachi is not merely one more friend inside a mature relational repertoire.

She may be the first close female friendship Nana has had to learn how to sustain.

This makes:

- jealousy;
- domestic negotiation;
- apology;
- disclosure;
- and fear of separation

more consequential.

### Home deprivation is now explicit

Volume 3's **「故郷などない」** sounded almost ideological.

Volume 4 supplies the deprivation behind it:

- absent father;
- mother absent from her upbringing;
- grandmother as caregiver;
- grandmother dead when Nana was fifteen.

Then Nana says she envies Hachi's lively family atmosphere.

The home thesis must therefore be revised:

> **Nana does not reject home because she transcends domestic need. She has lacked inherited home and therefore may have fewer practiced ways of recognizing or safely asking for it.**

### Ren remains unresolved—not erased

Nana's tears at the concert make continued attachment strongly supported.

But we should preserve the exact prospective state:

- she has not told Hachi simply that she still loves Ren;
- she has not yet spoken to Ren within this volume's endpoint;
- the future of the relationship is unknown;
- Hachi's matchmaking fantasy is not equivalent to Nana's decision.

That distinction matters.

## 5. Komatsu Nana / Hachi — character-state update

Volume 4 may be Hachi's most important volume so far because it substantially increases her **self-awareness** without making her emotionally stable.

### She knows she performs innocence

Hachi says:

> **「もう純粋じゃなかった」**  
> **「でも純粋なフリをした」**  
> **「ナナに嫌われたくなかったから」**

(NANA_V04_E002)

This is a decisive correction to any reading that treats Hachi as simply naïve.

She can recognize the difference between:

- what she feels;
- what she knows;
- what she presents;
- what she believes another person wants to see.

Her innocence can be partly sincere and partly performed.

### Her deepest breakup fear is not only losing Shoji

The Shoji rupture hurts.

But Hachi's emotional system rapidly searches for another stable relational structure.

Nana becomes that structure.

This is why the line **「彼氏よりずっと大事」** matters so much.

### She refuses possession more clearly than Nana does

When instructed to reclaim Shoji, Hachi says **いらない**.

This is ethically significant.

She is devastated.

She still chooses not to make love into a contest for ownership.

Her later **「負け」** narration shows she has not fully escaped that metaphor internally, but the behavior matters.

### She is capable of morally serious self-criticism

Hachi recognizes:

- she wanted to be loved without knowing how to love well;
- she over-immerses herself in romance;
- she loses perspective;
- she can neglect another person's needs while absorbed in her own emotions.

This complicates the common caricature of Hachi as someone who simply never learns.

She is learning.

The harder problem is whether insight can become stable practice.

### She transfers the lesson of reciprocity to Nana

After Junko tells her romance is a human relationship requiring **思いやり**, Hachi begins thinking about friendship the same way.

This is a genuine developmental advance.

She does not merely replace Shoji with Nana as a passive provider of comfort.

She begins asking:

> what does Nana need from me?

That is precisely the capacity future Hachi later regrets not having developed enough.

### Her relationship categories are under strain

Hachi tells herself:

- Nana eliminates jealousy because they are both women;
- then she becomes jealous;
- then she imagines female-female kissing;
- then she tells herself to get a boyfriend;
- then she says Nana matters more than a boyfriend;
- then she ends the volume wanting to hold Nana's hand forever.

The text is not asking for an immediate sexuality label.

It is showing **category failure**.

### Hachi becomes a curator of other people's futures

She takes the Trapnest ticket and turns it into a project:

> Nana and Ren should see one another.

This is compassionate because she perceives Nana's unfinished attachment.

It is also continuous with Hachi's tendency to think in scenarios:

- correct partner;
- correct domestic future;
- correct meeting;
- correct romantic resolution.

Her empathy is real.

Her projection is real too.

Both should remain visible.

## 6. Other materially affected characters

### Shoji — culpability becomes choice rather than temptation

Volume 3 established that Shoji knew what he was doing was wrong before the affair became fully explicit.

Volume 4 completes the moral movement.

The key facts are now:

- Hachi sees the betrayal directly;
- Shoji does not end the relationship before being exposed;
- he protects Sachiko during the confrontation;
- the relationship with Hachi ends.

The manga does not require us to believe Shoji never loved Hachi.

Its harder proposition is:

> **love that is no longer accompanied by honest choice cannot maintain an ethical relationship by inertia.**

### Sachiko — agency without villain reduction

Sachiko's confession matters because she refuses to make Shoji solely responsible for the emotional beginning.

Yet Shoji remains responsible for his own choices.

The volume therefore preserves two-agent responsibility.

Sachiko is neither:

- an innocent object;
- nor a one-dimensional homewrecker.

### Junko — moral clarity and moral harshness coexist

Junko correctly says Shoji was wrong to betray Hachi without ending things.

She also correctly identifies relational failures that existed before the affair.

Her **「被害者ぶる」** language, however, is unnecessarily accusatory toward the person who was deceived.

Junko should therefore remain a **counter-narrator**, not an infallible moral chorus.

### Nobu — loyalty learns differentiation

Nobu explains Ren's departure with more complexity than simple resentment.

He can simultaneously hold:

- hurt at being left;
- professional respect for Ren;
- belief in BLAST;
- loyalty to Nana.

This is an early example of one of *NANA*'s mature relational capacities:

> **disappointment does not require total devaluation of the person who disappointed you.**

His growing ease with Hachi also deserves continued tracking, but Volume 4 does not yet justify forcing that ease into a new romantic conclusion.

### Yasu — bridge between worlds

Yasu remains unusually capable of moving between:

- ordinary professional adulthood;
- BLAST;
- Nana's private distress;
- Ren's history.

The late-night contact around the concert reinforces his role as relational infrastructure.

He is not merely Nana's protector.

He is one of the few people who can understand several incompatible sides of the system at once.

### Misato — fandom enters the household

Misato's importance in this volume is less biographical than structural.

She represents:

- Nana's pre-Tokyo fan history;
- public attachment to Nana;
- knowledge Hachi lacks;
- the possibility that Nana belongs emotionally to people outside 707;
- the first serious trigger for Hachi's jealousy.

Her appearance makes celebrity/fandom an interpersonal force before BLAST is famous on a national scale.

### Ren — private lover becomes public image

For Hachi, Ren changes ontological category in Volume 4.

He was:

> the man in Nana's old story.

He becomes:

> a famous musician whose image exists in magazines, posters, tickets, and stage light.

The public image does not erase the private person.

It makes access to the private person more difficult.

That tension should become increasingly important if BLAST and Trapnest move deeper into the industry.

## 7. Relationship-state changes

### Nana / Hachi

**Entering state:** domestic partnership/found-family intimacy; explicitly romance-adjacent in future Hachi's retrospective language; Hachi highly admiring; Nana protective; shared 707 home.

**Volume 4 movement:**

> safety fantasy → jealousy → domestic conflict → separation anxiety → apology → explicit hierarchy → reciprocal disclosure → family integration → joined-hands permanence

The relationship becomes more mutual.

Hachi learns that Nana:

- can be hurt;
- lacks relational experience;
- envies family warmth;
- has difficulty self-disclosing.

Nana learns that Hachi:

- wants reciprocal disclosure;
- worries intensely when Nana disappears;
- will assert domestic boundaries;
- still wants Nana even after conflict.

**End state:** categorically unresolved but structurally more central than ever.

### Hachi / Shoji

**Entering state:** official couple under severe strain; Shoji already emotionally/sexually involved with Sachiko; Hachi unaware.

**Movement:** revelation → confrontation → refusal to compete → breakup → self-critique.

**End state:** romance ended within the current prospective scope.

The ethical lesson is not:

> Hachi was wrong to be hurt.

It is:

> relationship failure and betrayal responsibility are separate analytical questions.

### Shoji / Sachiko

**Entering state:** hidden affair with guilt and attempted retreat.

**Movement:** exposure → Sachiko names desire/culpability → Shoji protects Sachiko → relationship becomes public to the harmed partner.

The relationship's future remains outside this volume's concern, but the secret has ended.

### Nana / Ren

**Entering state:** old lovers separated for years; Ren now in Trapnest; Nana building BLAST without him.

**Movement:** Hachi discovers the identity link → learns breakup history → arranges concert exposure → Nana admits neither yes nor no to continued love → Nana cries seeing Ren.

**End state:** unresolved attachment strongly established; actual reunion not yet shown.

### Nana / Yasu

Yasu continues to function as a person Nana can approach when dysregulated or displaced from 707.

This is secure in a way Nana's more volatile attachments are not.

The exact category should remain open.

### Nana / BLAST

BLAST transitions from reconstructed private project to public performance in Tokyo.

That changes Nana's identity from:

> woman trying to become a singer

into:

> singer already capable of generating fans, crowd response, and relational consequences through performance.

## 8. Japanese voice, address, and translation-sensitive language

### Hachi: retrospective self-critique becomes sharper

Volume 4 gives some of the clearest evidence yet that present-event Hachi and retrospective Hachi should be tracked separately.

Present Hachi is:

- immediate;
- comic;
- hyperbolic;
- romantically categorical;
- quick to construct scenarios.

Retrospective Hachi can say:

> **「もう純粋じゃなかった」**  
> **「純粋なフリをした」**

and:

> **「誰かを上手に愛する事も出来ないのに 誰かに愛されたくて仕方なかった」**

This is not simply older Hachi mocking younger Hachi.

It is older Hachi assigning moral structure to experience.

### 「負け」 and 「取り返す」: the language of possession

Nana says:

- **負け** — lose;
- **取り返せ** — take him back.

Hachi later says:

- **あたしの負け** — my loss.

The shared vocabulary indicates that both women can conceptualize romance through competitive possession even when Hachi's behavior refuses the logic.

This lexical cluster should be tracked if it recurs.

### 「思いやり」: reciprocity rather than romance as magic

Junko's use of **思いやり** shifts the ethical vocabulary away from feeling and toward relational practice.

A strong English rendering must capture more than generic “kindness.” In this context it means active consideration of the other person's needs and circumstances.

That is why Hachi can immediately transfer the principle from romance to friendship.

### 「女友達」 becomes a marked category

Nana's:

> **「親しい女友達っていなかった」**

should not be flattened into “I didn't really have girlfriends.”

The category is specifically a **close female friend**.

That matters because the volume has just spent many pages showing Hachi struggling with what female-female intimacy is allowed to mean.

Both women therefore lack an easy script, but for different reasons:

- Hachi has a script and her feelings exceed it;
- Nana says she scarcely has experience with the relationship category at all.

### Hachi's 「彼氏よりずっと大事」 is structurally emphatic

The line does not merely say Nana is important.

It establishes comparison:

> **more important than a boyfriend.**

The boyfriend category is the standard against which Nana is being ranked and exceeded.

### Nana's formal register with Hachi's mother

The shift to:

> **「初めまして 大崎ナナです」**  
> **「お世話になります」**

shows that Nana's usual roughness is chosen/relational rather than incompetence.

Future voice analysis should therefore ask:

> with whom does Nana permit herself roughness, and when does she switch into socially conventional femininity/politeness?

### 「憧れる」: Nana and home

Nana uses **憧れる** for Hachi's family atmosphere.

This is stronger than saying she merely “likes” it.

It carries aspiration/longing toward something one does not possess.

That makes it a high-value lexical anchor for Nana's relation to domestic life.

### 「好き」 becomes inadequate

Hachi's eventual thought that Nana/Ren's attachment may be too complex for **好き** to capture is itself a linguistic maturation.

Earlier Hachi often treats relational vocabulary as if naming settles meaning.

Here she begins realizing that the word can be too simple for the structure underneath it.

## 9. Narration, time, memory, and information distribution

Volume 4 has unusually dense retrospective architecture.

### Retrospective node A — Tokyo is colder than expected

Spine p.6 uses weather to place present disappointment against earlier expectation.

The narrator already knows that migration did not deliver the imagined warmth.

### Retrospective node B — performed innocence

Spine p.19 gives future Hachi explicit knowledge that younger Hachi was managing Nana's perception of her.

This is evidence that retrospective Hachi does not merely know later events; she can reinterpret her earlier self-presentation.

### Retrospective node C — the ticket becomes providence

Spine p.53 converts lottery luck into a purposeful object:

> God prepared the ticket for Nana.

This is narratorial meaning-making, not verified supernatural intervention.

### Retrospective node D — inability to love well

Spine p.55 frames the Shoji failure as a mismatch between receiving and giving love.

This is a moral reclassification of younger Hachi.

### Retrospective node E — counterfactual Nana

Spine p.98 is the most important temporal node:

> if I had been more adult;
> if I had noticed Nana's weakness;
> would there have been another future?

The conditional grammar must remain conditional.

It establishes regret, not causal certainty.

### Retrospective node F — happiness prayer

Spine pp.182–183 produces anticipatory dread without telling us why the prayer matters later.

### Retrospective node G — the hand

Spine p.186 compresses the whole volume's relational architecture into physical memory.

The future narrator does not remember only what the concert sounded like.

She remembers touch.

### Information-distribution map

At the beginning of Volume 4:

- reader: knows Shoji/Sachiko;
- Hachi: does not;
- Junko/Kyosuke: know substantially more than Hachi;
- Nana: learns through confrontation;
- Hachi: still does not know Ren's current public identity in relation to Nana.

By Chapter 11:

- Hachi learns Ren's identity through public/fan channels;
- Nana still does not know how much Hachi knows until Hachi reveals it;
- Hachi deliberately withholds the extent of her knowledge while trying to produce a reunion opportunity.

The volume therefore repeatedly turns **knowledge asymmetry** into relational pressure.

## 10. Visual/formal analysis

### The breakup moves from crowded confrontation to isolated refusal

The Shoji/Sachiko confrontation uses:

- close facial panels;
- bodies interposed protectively;
- Nana's forward physical aggression;
- Hachi increasingly withdrawing from the center.

When Hachi says **「いらない」**, the panel gives her grief more visual space than argumentative speech.

The sequence then opens into night and distance.

Her refusal is visually a departure from the competition.

### Empty 707 after betrayal

The post-breakup pages repeatedly show:

- bed;
- darkness;
- empty table/chairs;
- Hachi's small body inside larger domestic space.

707 remains physically home.

It cannot automatically protect her from emotional collapse.

This is important because the manga does not fetishize chosen home as magical cure.

### BLAST performance changes scale

The concert pages enlarge:

- boots;
- instruments;
- microphone;
- stage light;
- crowd noise;
- Nana's body as performance object.

Hachi's gaze is repeatedly inserted among the performance panels.

The reader therefore experiences Nana as simultaneously:

- artist;
- public object;
- Hachi's intimate person.

### Misato jealousy is visualized through tonal rupture

Yazawa shifts abruptly from serious, beautiful panels into comic fantasy when Hachi imagines Nana and Misato kissing.

The tonal change does not erase the content.

It makes the content psychologically tolerable to Hachi and reader.

Humor becomes a way to show what Hachi cannot yet state without embarrassment.

### Doors become domestic jurisdiction

The Misato conflict is staged around entry/exit.

Hachi's assertion that 707 is her home too is followed by Nana physically leaving the apartment with Misato.

The door is therefore not neutral background.

It marks who has the power to:

- admit;
- exclude;
- leave;
- return.

### The family house is visually bright and populated

Compared with the black night-city and concert pages, Hachi's family home is open, bright, and crowded with bodies.

That visual warmth gives concrete form to Nana's **憧れる**.

### Trapnest turns intimacy into spectacle

The final concert sequence is built around distance:

- Nana in the audience;
- Ren onstage;
- front-row proximity that is still separated by performance architecture;
- thousands of other spectators sharing the visual object.

Private history becomes publicly visible without becoming publicly intelligible.

### The last page removes faces

Spine p.186 shows only hands.

That formal choice is crucial.

The volume ends by stripping away:

- celebrity;
- gender performance;
- facial self-presentation;
- spoken categories.

What remains is contact.

Future Hachi then supplies the meaning:

> she wanted to keep holding on.

## 11. Home and domestic-space ledger

### Apartment 707

**Ownership/lease:** shared tenancy remains the operative structure.  
**Who returns:** Nana and Hachi both still treat 707 as a default place of return.  
**Food/care:** Nana brings food when Hachi is depressed; Hachi begins recognizing that she should reciprocally consider Nana's needs.  
**Guest authority:** first major conflict. Hachi asserts equal say over overnight guests.  
**Exit:** Nana leaves with Misato after the dispute and stays away until morning.  
**Repair:** mutual apology restores coexistence.  
**Meaning:** home has matured from shared comfort into a negotiated relationship with boundaries.

The most important new proposition is:

> **707 becomes more genuinely a home when the occupants begin fighting about what shared home obligates them to do.**

Domestic conflict is not proof that the home was false.

It is proof that both people now claim it.

### Hachi's family home

The family house adds a new category:

> inherited home.

Hachi experiences its warmth as ordinary enough that Nana's longing makes her cry.

Nana experiences it as something she has lacked.

This asymmetry suggests that the two women may use 707 differently:

- Hachi recreates familiar domestic warmth in adult form;
- Nana discovers a form of home she has little inherited experience recognizing.

### Home and knowledge

A further complication appears:

Hachi shares a home with Nana but does not know Nana's past.

So *NANA* is separating:

> **living together**

from

> **being known.**

The volume's repair requires movement toward both.

## 12. Material and economic adulthood

### Hachi's job does not solve purposelessness

After the breakup, Hachi confronts unstructured time.

She wishes she could work because busyness would distract her, but her schedule leaves empty hours.

She fills some of that emptiness through shopping.

This matters after Volume 3's emphasis on obtaining respectable employment.

The job is real progress.

It does not automatically provide:

- purpose;
- intimacy;
- identity;
- emotional regulation.

Adulthood is not solved by employment any more than romance is solved by obtaining a boyfriend.

### Junko separates independence from relational competence

Junko's criticism also makes an important material point.

Hachi having found work and becoming more self-supporting is not, by itself, proof that she behaved well as a partner.

Economic adulthood and relational adulthood are distinct achievements.

### BLAST's public performance changes artistic labor

BLAST now has:

- a public venue;
- fans;
- travel/rehearsal logistics;
- performance preparation;
- reputational consequences.

The band is still far from Trapnest's industrial scale, but music is becoming labor rather than only aspiration.

### Trapnest demonstrates the next scale

Trapnest's world includes:

- magazine coverage;
- tour branding;
- assigned seating;
- promotional photographs;
- large venues;
- backstage organization;
- a public audience that knows the musicians as images.

Nana's artistic ambition therefore now has a visible industrial comparison point.

## 13. Music, celebrity, industry, and fashion

### BLAST's first major Tokyo public proof

Hachi's reaction to BLAST shows that Nana's musical power cannot be reduced to Hachi's crush/admiration.

Other spectators respond.

Misato already has intense fan attachment.

Junko recognizes the band's professional potential.

The volume therefore gives BLAST external validation.

### Nana's songs as indirect autobiography

Hachi hears abandonment and endurance in the songs.

This should be carried forward as a hypothesis:

> Nana may disclose through performance what she resists disclosing conversationally.

The songs are evidence for artistic identity, but Hachi's reading remains a character interpretation rather than direct authorial confirmation of every lyric's autobiographical source.

### Fandom competes with private intimacy

Misato is the first major sign that success will create people who feel emotionally attached to Nana without belonging to her private household.

Hachi's jealousy tells us celebrity will not merely create career opportunities.

It will alter relationship boundaries.

### Ren as branded image

Hachi learns about Ren through the machinery of celebrity.

His face appears as product and publicity.

This creates a sharp contrast:

> Hachi knows Nana's breakfast table but not her history with the man on the magazine page.

### Fashion remains relational language

The volume repeatedly contrasts:

- Hachi's feminine dresses, pearls, shopping, and domestic presentation;
- Nana's punk stage silhouette, choker, leather, boots, and controlled black palette;
- Misato's highly stylized fan/subcultural presentation;
- Trapnest's polished celebrity styling.

These are not simply outfits.

They mark different ways bodies enter public space:

- Hachi as consumer/ordinary young woman;
- Nana as authored punk performer;
- Misato as fan who turns subculture into identity;
- Trapnest as professional image system.

## 14. Gender, sexuality, and social scripts

Volume 4 is the strongest gender/sexuality volume so far because it lets the characters themselves produce conflicting classifications.

### Hachi's heterosexual self-concept remains intact

She wants:

- a knight;
- a gentle man;
- someone who will protect and cherish her.

She is not narrating herself as someone who has stopped desiring men.

### Nana exceeds the friendship script

At the same time Hachi says:

- Nana prevents loneliness;
- women should not cause jealousy;
- she is jealous of Misato;
- she imagines two women kissing;
- Nana matters more than a boyfriend;
- she wants to hold Nana's hand forever.

The strongest prospective reading is therefore **multivalence**, not hidden-label certainty.

### The text is interested in the inadequacy of categories

Hachi's own language produces the problem:

> **女同士なのに変なの！**

The feeling is real enough that she must explain why it is “weird.”

This is qualitatively different from a reader importing queer coding from outside.

The character herself experiences a mismatch between gender category and affect.

### The final hand image resists categorical closure

Holding hands in itself is not proof of romance.

Future Hachi's wish to keep holding forever gives the gesture unusually high emotional intensity.

The volume therefore continues the pattern from Volume 3:

> the manga does not force Nana/Hachi into a conventional romantic confession, but repeatedly gives their bond the language, visual emphasis, jealousy, domesticity, and retrospective longing normally associated with central love relationships.

That structural observation is now strongly supported.

## 15. Ethics, consent, coercion, care, and power

### Shoji's betrayal

The volume makes the ethical sequence clear enough that responsibility should not be blurred.

Relationship difficulties existed.

Shoji still had the option to end the relationship before entering another one.

He did not.

Hachi's flaws explain context.

They do not authorize deception.

### Sachiko's responsibility

Sachiko knew Shoji had a girlfriend.

She says she could not stop her feelings/action.

That explains her subjective experience.

It does not make the choice morally neutral.

### Nana's protective aggression

Nana's anger is morally intelligible but not automatically legitimate.

Her solution—fight, reclaim—would reduce Hachi's agency if imposed.

Hachi's **「いらない」** is therefore an important assertion of autonomy against even benevolent pressure.

### Junko's care can become moral overreach

Junko is trying to prevent Hachi from learning the wrong lesson:

> I was perfect; betrayal happened to me from nowhere.

But the phrase **「被害者ぶる」** risks minimizing the real injury.

A better ethical synthesis is:

> Hachi should examine how she participated in the deterioration of the relationship **without accepting responsibility for Shoji's decision to betray rather than communicate/end it.**

### Hachi's care for Nana becomes more sophisticated—and more interventionist

Hachi begins practicing reciprocity.

She also begins arranging Nana/Ren contact without fully asking Nana what she wants.

This gives us an early distinction:

- noticing another person's need is care;
- deciding the correct outcome for them can become control.

Volume 4 does not condemn Hachi's matchmaking.

It gives enough evidence to keep the question open.

## 16. Motif and lexical tracking

### **思いやり** — reciprocal regard

New high-value term. Introduced explicitly in Junko's relationship analysis and immediately generalized by Hachi beyond romance.

### **負け / 取り返す** — loss / reclaim

Competitive-possession vocabulary around romance. Nana uses it prescriptively; Hachi internalizes it retrospectively.

### **彼氏** — boyfriend as category

The volume repeatedly uses the boyfriend category as a benchmark that Nana exceeds or that Hachi invokes to restore normative order.

### **女友達** — female friend

Becomes unusually marked because both women struggle with what the category can contain.

### **ヤキモチ** — jealousy

Crucial contradiction term. Hachi says women should make jealousy unnecessary, then experiences it.

### **大事** — important/precious

Hachi's **「彼氏よりずっと大事」** elevates Nana within her relational hierarchy.

### **憧れる** — longing/admiration

Previously central to Hachi's Nana-oriented feeling; now Nana uses it for Hachi's family atmosphere. The same semantic field of longing therefore connects person and home.

### **好き** — like/love

Ends the volume as explicitly insufficient for Nana/Ren's accumulated history.

### **愛する / 愛される** — loving / being loved

Future Hachi contrasts competence in loving with hunger to be loved.

### Hands

The final joined hands should enter the visual-motif ledger.

At this point the hand means:

- involuntary/instinctive contact under emotional pressure;
- mutual physical anchoring;
- future Hachi's desire for permanence.

Do not yet assume every later hand recurrence has the same meaning.

### Black Stone cigarettes

The cigarette brand circulates through Yasu, Shin, and the concert preparation pages and becomes visually tied to anxiety, adult coolness, and interpersonal continuity. Its exact symbolic weight remains unresolved; recurrence is established, interpretation should remain provisional.

### Tickets / media objects

The Trapnest ticket and magazine mediate private relationships.

Objects produced by the entertainment industry become tools through which Hachi accesses Nana's emotional history.

## 17. Counterreadings, limitations, and hypothesis stress-test

### Hypothesis A — Hachi is fundamentally “dependent”

**Status: complicated.**

Supporting evidence:

- she wants a protective knight;
- she fears loneliness;
- she rapidly reorganizes emotional security around Nana;
- she admits over-immersing in romance.

Counterevidence:

- she refuses to fight to possess Shoji;
- she is working and maintaining her own household responsibilities;
- she can recognize her own relational failures;
- she tries to make friendship reciprocal rather than merely extract comfort.

Best current formulation:

> Hachi has strong attachment/security needs, but “dependence” alone is too blunt because she is also developing a coherent ethic of domestic reciprocity.

### Hypothesis B — Nana is truly independent rather than attachment-dependent

**Status: weakened in simple form; strengthened in complex form.**

Nana's autonomy is real.

But Volume 4 shows:

- little history of close female friendship;
- family deprivation;
- longing for domestic warmth;
- strong unresolved Ren attachment;
- vulnerability around Hachi's anger;
- songs Hachi hears as abandonment-centered.

Best current formulation:

> Nana's independence is both genuine self-authorship **and** defensive architecture around unmet attachment needs.

### Hypothesis C — Nana/Hachi structurally occupies the position of the central love relationship

**Status: strongly strengthened.**

Evidence added this volume:

- Nana as alternative to boyfriend/loneliness;
- Hachi jealousy over Misato;
- female-female kiss fantasy;
- explicit **「彼氏よりずっと大事」**;
- shared-home conflict and repair;
- request for reciprocal disclosure;
- Nana's admission that Hachi is an unprecedented close female friend;
- final “hold your hand forever” narration.

Counterevidence/qualification:

- Hachi continues to desire men;
- Nana/Ren remains a major romantic axis;
- neither woman labels Nana/Hachi a romantic partnership;
- some same-sex intimacy is played comedically.

Best current formulation:

> The structural-centrality hypothesis is becoming much stronger; the categorical-romance hypothesis remains open.

### Hypothesis D — 707 is already a stable found-family home

**Status: strengthened and complicated.**

The guest dispute proves that the home matters enough to create jurisdictional conflict.

Its stability is not automatic.

It must be maintained through:

- mutual consideration;
- apology;
- shared authority;
- return.

### Hypothesis E — Hachi's matchmaking of Nana/Ren is pure empathy

**Status: complicated.**

It is empathetic.

It also reflects Hachi's tendency to turn relationships into scripts and to believe a meaningful coincidence should culminate in a meaningful romantic resolution.

Future volumes should test whether Hachi can support Nana without authoring Nana's decision.

### Hypothesis F — Future Hachi's counterfactual proves she could have prevented later harm

**Status: rejected as overclaim.**

The text proves future Hachi **believes/wonders** this.

It does not prove the counterfactual causal claim.

This is a critical epistemic boundary for the unfinished series.

### Hypothesis G — Junko is the manga's moral authority

**Status: qualified.**

She often sees relational dynamics clearly.

Her harshness toward Hachi shows that her judgments should be treated as character judgments, not automatic authorial verdicts.

- domestically aspirational;

## 21. Volume thesis and questions carried forward

### Volume thesis

Volume 4 is fundamentally about **what remains when a socially legible romance fails**.

Hachi's answer is not solitude.

It is a more difficult recognition:

> the person who feels safest, most important, most like home, and most frightening to lose may not fit neatly inside the relationship category Hachi expected to organize adulthood.

Shoji's betrayal destroys one fantasy:

> obtain boyfriend → move to Tokyo → love becomes stable home.

But the volume does not mock Hachi for wanting home.

It relocates the question.

707 becomes a home that requires boundaries.

Nana becomes a person Hachi must learn to care for rather than merely admire.

Nana's own self-sufficiency is revealed to coexist with profound deprivation of family and close female friendship.

Ren returns not as an easy answer but as the embodiment of a history too complicated for the word **好き**.

And the volume's final formal decision is decisive:

> the public spectacle is Ren;
> the private gesture is Nana and Hachi holding hands;
> the remembered desire for permanence belongs to Hachi.

The strongest current formulation is therefore:

> **Volume 4 converts the Nana/Hachi relationship from an extraordinary friendship with romance-adjacent coding into the story's clearest competing structure of permanence: a bond that now contains home, jealousy, bodily intimacy, mutual care, fear of loss, and future longing even while both women's heterosexual attachments remain fully real.**

### Questions carried forward

1. Will Nana and Ren actually speak, and if so, what does each believe the old relationship now promises?
2. Can Nana integrate Ren into a life that now includes 707, Hachi, and the rebuilt BLAST without reproducing the old dependency structure?
3. How will Hachi respond if Nana's romantic past becomes a present relationship rather than a story she can curate from outside?
4. Does Hachi's jealousy toward Misato remain situational, or does it expose a recurring possessive structure in her attachment to Nana?
5. Can Hachi practice **思いやり** without converting care into authorship of other people's choices?
6. Will Nana become more capable of voluntary self-disclosure after explicitly admitting she does not know how close female friendship works?
7. Does 707 remain the pair's shared home if careers and romantic relationships begin pulling them into different spaces?
8. How will BLAST's growing public life change Hachi's access to Nana and Nana's ability to maintain private identity?
9. Will the recurring vocabulary of **負け / 取り返す / ヤキモチ / 大事 / 憧れる / 好き** continue to distinguish love, possession, attachment, and home?
10. What later event gives future Hachi's p.98 counterfactual—**「違う未来があったの？」**—its full meaning, and how much causal authority should that regret actually receive?
11. What later context explains why future Hachi remembers the hand on p.186 with such intense permanence language?
