# 13 — *NANA 7.8*: Paratext, Publication Context, and Retrospective Revision
## What the official 2003 fan book can clarify—and what it cannot decide

## 1. Governing conclusion

*NANA 7.8 — ナナ&ハチ Premium fan book!* is valuable precisely because it is **not** a hidden answer key to the mature manga.

It is an official object published at a very specific point in the work's development: its first printing is dated **19 March 2003**, and its final teaser advertises **Volume 8 for 15 May 2003**. [NANA78-DF-001; NANA78-MT-006] It therefore freezes an official self-presentation of *NANA* after the first seven tankōbon had established the two Nanas, Room 707, BLAST, Trapnest, Ren, Takumi, Nobu, Shoji, Sachiko, and the pregnancy crisis, but before the much longer later architecture of marriage, professionalization, celebrity enclosure, Ren's death, the future separation, and Chapters 81–84 had fully unfolded in collected form.

That position gives the fan book two simultaneous values.

First, it supplies unusually strong evidence about **what the work, its author, its editors, and a small sample of its readers understood themselves to be engaging with in early 2003**. Direct interviews with Yazawa discuss the original dual-protagonist concept, fate, the retrospective form, the difficulty of predicting how characters will respond to planned events, the music/fashion environment, and her resistance to one-to-one real-person models. The book's editorial architecture foregrounds two women, parallel lives, relationships, fashion, interiors, places, telephones, email, punk culture, Vivienne Westwood, clubs, bands, and reader identification. [NANA78-YA-002–006; NANA78-ED-001–005]

Second, it demonstrates how rapidly *NANA* had become more than a serialized romance narrative. The official publication turns its world into a **lifestyle, music, fashion, urban-geography, and fandom object**. Readers are invited to tour locations, reproduce Nana's makeup, cook like Hachi, learn punk through Nobu, consult Yasu, evaluate relationships, read mock music journalism, compare favorite characters, and reread the manga until they become what Yazawa jokingly calls a proper *NANA* maniac. [NANA78-YA-001; NANA78-CR-001–004; NANA78-MT-001–005]

The mature interpretive rule is therefore:

> **Use *NANA 7.8* to triangulate the manga's early architecture, publication culture, and self-understanding. Do not use it to overrule what the later manga actually demonstrates.**

The fan book materially strengthens several conclusions already reached in Documents 01–12. It also places useful limits on deterministic readings. It does **not** settle the Nana/Hachi relationship into a conventional romantic category; it does not retroactively justify or condemn later relationship conduct; it does not explain Nana's eventual disappearance; it does not decide Hachi's final marriage; and it cannot tell us what Yazawa would have written after Chapter 84.

Its value is historical and interpretive, not oracular.

# 2. Source audit and publication position

## 2.1 Documentary facts

The inspected PDF is a 204-page scan of the Japanese *NANA 7.8 — ナナ&ハチ Premium fan book!* published by Shueisha. Its colophon records:

- first printing: **2003-03-19**;
- second printing: **2003-07-08**;
- author: **矢沢あい / Ai Yazawa**;
- publisher: **集英社 / Shueisha**. [NANA78-DF-001]

The book's final illustrated page states that *NANA* Volume 8 would go on sale on **2003-05-15**. [NANA78-MT-006]

This fixes the correct interpretive time index:

> **Every authorial and editorial statement in *NANA 7.8* is an early-2003 statement.**

It is evidence about the work as Yazawa and Shueisha presented it at that point, not evidence that every later development had already been decided in detail.

## 2.2 Production credits and why attribution matters

The contents/credits pages explicitly distinguish Yazawa from the fan book's editorial and production staff. The book credits art direction/design, design, an **Editor & Writer** team, photographers, a stylist, and members of the *Cookie* editorial department. [NANA78-DF-002]

That matters methodologically. The fan book contains at least five different kinds of discourse:

1. **direct Yazawa commentary** — the signed greeting and interview;
2. **documentary information** — dates, credits, bibliographic data, real places and products;
3. **editorial characterization** — profiles, A-to-Z entries, summaries, rankings, labels;
4. **reader reception** — the curated `Voices to NANA` roundtable and reader contributions;
5. **playful metatext** — mock magazines, character-room gimmicks, `淳子の部屋スペシャル`, awards, quizzes, and promotional material.

A sentence printed in the fan book is therefore **not automatically a sentence by Yazawa**.

The controlling evidence hierarchy for this document is:

> **manga demonstration > direct Yazawa commentary > documentary fact > official editorial framing > reader-reception material > playful metatext.**

The PDF was inspected render-first. All 204 pages were visually surveyed through rendered page sequences/contact sheets, with higher-resolution inspection of the contents, colophon, Yazawa interview, reader-reception section, story chronology, mock music journalism, and closing material. # 3. What kind of object is *NANA 7.8*?

The contents reveal that *7.8* is not organized as a conventional guidebook that simply explains plot facts.

Its principal sections include:

- `NANA's Gallery — Memories & Regrets`;
- a character relationship chart and character profiles;
- `A to Z of NANA`;
- `NANA's world TOUR GUIDE`;
- `Interview to YAZAWA AI`;
- `Voices to NANA`;
- `NANA's Story`;
- `Character Room` features such as Nana's makeup lesson, Hachi's cooking, Yasu's telephone advice, Nobu's `I LOVE PUNKS`, Shin's `Sweet Dreams`, and Junko's advice features;
- `ROCK ON JAPON`;
- `NANA Carte`;
- recurring `美里レポート` pages;
- `淳子の部屋スペシャル`;
- and promotional/reader material. [NANA78-DF-002; NANA78-ED-001]

This organization is itself evidence.

The official publication assumes that readers want to know not only **what happened**, but also:

- what the characters wear;
- what they listen to;
- how they decorate space;
- where they go in Tokyo;
- what brands and objects define their world;
- how to imitate their aesthetic practices;
- how other readers feel about them;
- and how BLAST and Trapnest might look if consumed through actual music journalism.

The fan book consequently treats *NANA* as an **inhabitable cultural environment**.

That strongly supports, without creating, the conclusions of Documents 08, 11, and 12: fashion, music, phones, rooms, consumer objects, magazines, urban geography, work, and celebrity are not ornamental decoration around a relationship plot. They are part of the form through which the relationships become socially legible.

# 4. Yazawa's greeting: the fan book as a rereading machine

The signed `ごあいさつ` is one of the clearest guides to how the publication understands its own function. Yazawa jokes that a reader confused by the designation “7.8” should reread the manga, then reread the fan book, then perhaps find herself rereading the manga again until she has become a full-fledged `NANAマニアック`. She ends by thanking the reader for becoming hooked. [NANA78-YA-001]

This is playful, but analytically useful.

The fan book presents itself not as a replacement for the manga but as a **loop back into the manga**. Its ideal reader moves:

> manga → fan book → rereading → fan book again.

That is exactly the right use for this Phase-6 source.

The supplemental material should send us back to the primary text with sharper questions. It should not become a shortcut that allows us to stop reading the manga.

This also helps explain why *7.8* is full of taxonomies, maps, profiles, props, favorite-scene prompts, and mock cultural journalism. Its purpose is to make previously read material newly noticeable and newly collectible.

# 5. Direct Yazawa commentary: what it genuinely adds

## 5.1 The dual-protagonist concept is early, deliberate, and fate-conscious

The author interview is headlined around the idea of **two people tossed about by fate** and begins by discussing the circumstances from which *NANA* developed. Yazawa describes the two same-named young women as the originating conceptual structure rather than one woman being a late-added foil for the other. [NANA78-YA-002]

This strongly supports Document 01's treatment of the series as two parallel biographies that become one shared clock.

It also corrects a simplistic developmental history in which either Nana or Hachi should be treated as the “real” protagonist while the other is merely a secondary heroine. The fan book's entire design reinforces duality:

- the title calls itself a `ナナ&ハチ Premium fan book`;
- the early relationship diagrams place both women at the center of intersecting networks;
- the story section gives them paired biographical/chronological treatment;
- and the author interview discusses the work through their paired construction. [NANA78-ED-002; NANA78-ED-004]

This does **not** mean both women receive identical narrative functions. Hachi carries much of the retrospective first-person framing; Nana becomes the absent/desired center of the future architecture. But the duality is foundational rather than accidental.

## 5.2 Fate is part of the design—but not a license for determinism

The interview repeatedly associates the initial concept with `運命`, while the 2003 fan book packages the two women as lives whose encounter feels fated. [NANA78-YA-002]

This strengthens the conclusion that fate language in *NANA* should not be dismissed as merely Hachi's naïve romantic vocabulary. It belongs to the work's official early self-description as well.

But the same interview also places an important limit on deterministic interpretation.

When asked about abandoned or alternative plot developments, Yazawa explains that the story has a **future-looking-back structure**—`物語が未来から振り返ってる形式`—so some future conditions constrain what the narrative must eventually pass through. Yet she also says that how characters respond to those events can depart from what she expected; the characters' reactions matter to the route by which the story arrives there. [NANA78-YA-005]

This is one of the most important supplemental findings in the entire book.

It supports three propositions simultaneously:

1. the future-retrospective architecture was **not** invented late in the serialization;
2. the existence of planned future constraints does **not** imply that every intermediate relationship outcome was fixed in detail;
3. “fate” in *NANA* is best understood as pressure, contingency, and retrospective structure—not as proof that the cast are puppets moving through an immutable romance chart.

This materially strengthens Document 09 while qualifying any interpretation that treats future narration as a fully predetermined blueprint.

## 5.3 Yazawa explicitly values multiple viewpoints

Another interview spread is headlined `いろんな視点から『NANA』を見て欲しい`—a desire for *NANA* to be seen from many viewpoints. [NANA78-YA-004]

The surrounding interview discusses readers identifying with different characters and the manga's unusually broad field of recognizable positions.

This is congruent with what the sequential reading independently found: *NANA* repeatedly withholds a single moral observer who can make every relationship simple. Hachi, Nana, Junko, Yasu, Nobu, Takumi, Ren, Shin, Reira, Yuri/Asami, and others each possess information, needs, and blind spots that reorganize the same event.

The paratext therefore strengthens the methodological choice to reconstruct **relational constellations** rather than reduce the cast to heroes, villains, and romantic competitors.

It does not mean “all viewpoints are equally correct.” The manga still gives us enough evidence to classify coercion, deception, assault, exploitation, or misinformation. Multi-perspectival narration is an epistemic structure, not moral relativism.

## 5.4 No one-to-one character “models”

The interview contains a section explicitly titled `モデルはいません!` (“There are no models!”). Yazawa addresses the tendency to ask whether particular characters correspond directly to real musicians, acquaintances, or celebrities and rejects a simple one-person-to-one-character mapping. [NANA78-YA-003]

This is useful because *NANA* is saturated with recognizable cultural material:

- punk history;
- Sex Pistols/Sid Vicious imagery;
- Vivienne Westwood;
- rock-club aesthetics;
- Japanese celebrity media;
- fashion editorial culture;
- Tokyo neighborhoods and venues.

The correct interpretive move is therefore not to search for a secret real-world person who “is” Nana, Ren, Reira, or Takumi. Cultural references help construct the characters' worlds, but the characters remain fictional syntheses with their own textual psychology.

## 5.5 Timing, contingency, and human behavior

The final interview spread is headlined `人生ってすべてタイミングだと思う`—“I think life is all timing.” Yazawa discusses how small turns in circumstances and feeling can alter human behavior and how manga drama emerges from those changes rather than from perfectly clean moral or romantic design. [NANA78-YA-006]

This does not authorize the critic to call every development arbitrary. The manga still possesses strong causal architecture.

What it does support is the mature distinction between:

> **trajectory** and **destination**,  
> **structure** and **mechanical predestination**,  
> **character pattern** and **character inevitability**.

That distinction is particularly important because the work remains unfinished.

# 6. Editorial character profiles: useful snapshots, dangerous authorities

The character-profile section assigns the cast short English-language branding phrases alongside ages, measurements, key words, drawings, and compressed descriptions. Examples include:

- Nana Osaki: `WITH AMBITION`;
- Komatsu Nana/Hachi: `THE EARNEST GIRL STRUCK IN LOVE`;
- Yasu: `THE GREATEST SUPPORTER FOR ALL`;
- Nobu: `THE FRANK NATURE`;
- Shin: `THE REALISTIC SUGAR BOY`;
- Reira: `THE MISTY SONGSTRESS`;
- Misato: `THE Black Stones FANATIC`;
- Junko: `THE CONGENIAL FRIEND`;
- Sachiko: `THE TENDER & BRAVE GIRL`;
- Shoji: `THE SWEET EX-BOYFRIEND`. [NANA78-ED-002]

These tags are revealing—but primarily about **editorial legibility**.

They compress complicated subjects into instantly marketable character functions. Hachi becomes romance, Nana ambition, Yasu support, Junko friendship, Misato fandom. None is completely false. None is sufficient.

The mature corpus can therefore use these profiles for two limited purposes:

1. to identify the traits the official publication foregrounded in 2003;
2. to measure how much later narrative development exceeds those early categories.

They should **not** be used as if Yazawa had supplied definitive psychological diagnoses.

This is especially important for Hachi. `THE EARNEST GIRL STRUCK IN LOVE` accurately captures how easily early readers and marketing can organize her around romance. The full manga demonstrates that she becomes much more: worker, domestic subject, pregnant woman, mother, wife, mediator, caregiver, narrator, ritual keeper, and architect of social home.

Likewise, calling Yasu “supporter for all” describes his ensemble function but says nothing by itself about the ethical distinction between helping, information control, paternalism, and returning agency developed in Documents 05 and 07.

Official characterization is therefore evidence of **framing**, not a veto on close reading.

# 7. `A to Z of NANA`: the world becomes a database

The longest early section of the fan book alphabetizes the series into people, places, objects, habits, brands, technologies, music references, and social practices. Its entries range across categories such as art school, beer, fashion, girlfriends, kissing, live performance, punk, Ren, Sid Vicious, Tokyo, Vivienne Westwood, email, mobile phones, interiors, tobacco, offices, and other recurring elements. [NANA78-ED-003]

The section is important because it reveals the **granularity at which the official publication expects readers to care**.

The manga's world is not reduced to:

> Nana / Hachi / which boyfriend wins?

It is instead broken into:

> rooms, clothes, brands, phones, bars, schools, railway stations, beer, cigarettes, bands, letters, lovers, jobs, furniture, musical references, and city spaces.

That strongly reinforces Document 11's conclusion that Yazawa gives relationships material form. The fan book then converts those material forms into a reference database.

There is also a productive tension here.

The manga often shows that objects acquire meaning through changing relationships. The guidebook necessarily freezes them into entries. Thus `A to Z` is extremely useful for **retrieval and cultural orientation**, but less useful for interpreting the changing meaning of an object across time.

A ring in a glossary is a ring. A ring in the manga can be promise, publicity, marriage, jealousy, continuity, performance, or memory depending on who wears it, when, and why.

# 8. The real-world guide: fiction becomes geographically inhabitable

`NANA's world TOUR GUIDE` maps the fiction onto real urban and commercial geography. It includes restaurants/bars, interiors and furniture shops, railway and neighborhood points, and music-related locations presented as places through which a reader can experience something resembling the characters' world. [NANA78-CR-001]

This is cultural-reference material, not mainline canon.

Its importance lies elsewhere: the official fan book treats *NANA* as a world that can be **visited**.

The fictional social system is anchored in recognizable Tokyo consumption and mobility:

- travel by rail;
- meeting in bars and restaurants;
- shopping for interiors and fashion;
- going to live houses;
- navigating neighborhoods;
- reproducing urban youth culture through actual places.

This strengthens the historical reading developed in Document 12. *NANA* belongs to a pre-smartphone but highly mobile, consumer-literate, magazine-mediated urban youth culture. The guide turns that cultural specificity into fan pilgrimage.

# 9. `Character Room`: identity as something readers can perform

The `Character Room` features are among the clearest examples of paratext becoming lifestyle instruction. The contents include:

- Nana's makeup lesson;
- Hachi's cooking;
- Yasu's telephone consultation room;
- Nobu's `I LOVE PUNKS`;
- Shin's `Sweet Dreams`;
- Junko's advice material. [NANA78-CR-002]

These pages are playful magazine features rather than hidden narrative episodes.

Yet formally they do something fascinating: they translate character into **practice**.

Nana can be approached through cosmetics and styling.  
Hachi through cooking and domestic competence.  
Nobu through punk knowledge.  
Yasu through counsel.  
Junko through social judgment and advice.

The fan is invited not only to admire the character but to **perform a small part of the character's social function**.

This is especially useful when placed beside Document 11's claim that fashion and objects are forms of self-authorship. The official publication recognized that readers did not experience Nana's clothes or Hachi's food as neutral decoration. They were part of how those women became legible and imitable.

At the same time, the section shows the danger of paratextual simplification. Nana is not reducible to makeup; Hachi is not reducible to cooking; Yasu is not merely an advice machine. The feature format deliberately isolates recognizable traits because recognizability is what makes a magazine page work.

# 10. `Voices to NANA`: an early reception snapshot, not a population survey

The reader section is useful only if its evidentiary limits remain explicit.

The central roundtable brings together **five female readers**: three current high-school students and two women in their twenties. [NANA78-RC-001] They discuss favorite characters, favorite scenes and lines, the manga's influence, friendships, fashion, romance, and the ways they compare people in their own lives to *NANA* characters.

This is not representative survey research.

It is:

- a tiny sample;
- selected by an official fan publication;
- shaped by editorial prompts;
- and composed entirely of women within a relatively narrow age range.

It cannot establish “what Japanese readers thought.”

But it does establish something more modest and still useful:

> **By early 2003, official fandom discourse already treated *NANA* as a language for thinking about real relationships, style, identity, and social types—not merely as a story to consume passively.**

The participants debate favorite men and women, identify with different characters, discuss scenes that feel realistic, and describe the manga's influence on taste and conversation. Reader-contribution boxes similarly show fan art, partisan preference between male characters, and readers adopting the cast as a vocabulary for ordinary life. [NANA78-RC-002–004]

This early reception has two implications.

First, it helps explain why the manga's relational complexity became culturally powerful: readers could recognize pieces of themselves and their acquaintances without requiring a single morally perfect heroine.

Second, it warns against retroactively reducing historical reception to today's shipping categories. The 2003 material is already interested in romance, attraction, gendered coolness, friendship, lifestyle, and identification all at once.

# 11. `ROCK ON JAPON`: the fan book performs the celebrity machine

One of *7.8*'s most revealing metatextual inventions is its fake rock-journalism layer.

`ROCK ON JAPON` presents Trapnest and BLAST through layouts resembling professional music magazines: artist interviews, live reports, rankings, awards, and editorial commentary. The section includes a Trapnest feature/interview and a BLAST live report, followed by playful `NANA` music-award material. [NANA78-MT-001–003]

This material should not be treated as straightforward diegetic fact unless a specific statement is independently supported by the manga.

Its stronger value is formal.

The fan book asks the reader to experience fictional musicians through the very media apparatus that the later manga increasingly makes consequential:

> artist → photograph → interview → magazine layout → fan interpretation → public image.

In 2003 this is largely playful wish-fulfillment. The reader gets to consume Trapnest and BLAST as if they were real bands.

From the perspective of the completed extant manga, however, the section becomes retrospectively interesting. The later narrative will show that magazine framing, photographs, interviews, publicity strategy, and public appetite can enclose private life. *NANA 7.8* itself participates in that conversion while the story is still early enough for it to feel mostly glamorous.

A reasonable retrospective interpretation is therefore:

> **The fan book unintentionally demonstrates the attraction of the celebrity apparatus that the later manga will subject to much harsher scrutiny.**

This is a metatextual inference, not a claim that Yazawa designed the fan book as a critique of its own marketing.

# 12. `NANA's Story`: retrospective summary as parallel-life architecture

The story-summary section visually reconstructs the two women's lives through paired biographies and chronologies. Nana's page foregrounds an unknown father, a vanished mother, her grandmother, music, Ren, and BLAST; Hachi's page foregrounds provincial upbringing, school, romantic history, Junko, Shoji, and the path toward Tokyo. [NANA78-ED-004]

The value of the section is architectural rather than interpretively final.

It makes explicit what the mature synthesis independently found:

> **The story begins with two separate life histories that converge into one shared urban chronology.**

But summary necessarily removes ambiguity. A timeline turns lived scenes into fixed entries. It is therefore best used to check documentary sequencing and official early emphasis, not to settle motivation.

The summary section also reminds us how much of the later manga did not yet exist for this book's readers. The devastating meanings later attached to marriage, fame, Ren's addiction and death, future Nana, preserved 707, children, photographs, and reunion have not yet accumulated.

The paratext is a **snapshot of an unfinished work inside an even earlier unfinished state**.

# 13. `淳子の部屋スペシャル` and the Volume-8 boundary

The late `淳子の部屋スペシャル` material belongs to the franchise's explicitly metatextual/comedic mode rather than ordinary mainline realism. [NANA78-MT-004]

Its presence in the same volume as serious biographies, author interview, lifestyle material, and mock journalism shows how comfortable the publication is with shifting ontological registers. Characters can be:

- fictional persons inside the story;
- subjects of profiles;
- magazine celebrities;
- hosts or guests in gag metatext;
- visual fashion models;
- and objects of reader debate.

The closing illustrated teaser then returns directly to the serialized narrative threshold. Hachi appears with the line `約束を果たせなくてごめんね`, followed by the advertisement `NANA 8 2003.5.15 on sale!` [NANA78-MT-006]. The line later belongs to material analyzed in Volume 8 and therefore is **not unique supplemental canon**.

The important point is publication mechanics:

> *7.8* literally positions itself as the bridge between already-collected memory and the next installment.

That is why its best interpretive use is transitional rather than final.

# 14. Retrospective revision ledger

The mature corpus can now state precisely what *NANA 7.8* changes.

## 14.1 Strengthened: the dual-protagonist architecture

The paired-Nana concept is directly discussed by Yazawa and built into the fan book's entire organization. This strengthens Document 01's conclusion that the series is architecturally founded on two lives rather than one protagonist plus satellite heroine. [NANA78-YA-002; NANA78-ED-002; NANA78-ED-004]

**Revision status:** strengthened, not overturned.

## 14.2 Strengthened: future retrospection was an early formal commitment

Yazawa explicitly describes the story as one that looks back from the future. [NANA78-YA-005]

This is unusually strong paratextual confirmation for Document 09's temporal analysis.

**Revision status:** materially strengthened.

## 14.3 Qualified: future structure does not equal total predestination

The same interview distinguishes constrained future conditions from characters' responses, which may not proceed exactly as the author initially predicts. [NANA78-YA-005]

**Revision status:** deterministic readings weakened.

## 14.4 Strengthened: multi-perspectival reading belongs to the work's early self-understanding

The interview explicitly encourages multiple viewpoints, while the reader section demonstrates identification distributed across the cast. [NANA78-YA-004; NANA78-RC-001]

**Revision status:** ensemble/constellation method strengthened.

## 14.5 Strengthened: fashion, music, space, objects, and technology are part of the work's core cultural appeal

The sheer proportion of the fan book devoted to A-to-Z material, fashion, makeup, cooking, locations, interiors, phones, music history, bands, live houses, and mock journalism strongly supports Documents 08, 11, and 12. [NANA78-ED-003; NANA78-CR-001–003; NANA78-MT-001]

**Revision status:** strengthened.

## 14.6 Qualified: editorial archetypes are useful but shallow

The profile labels show how the cast was marketed in compact form, but the later manga repeatedly exceeds those tags. [NANA78-ED-002]

**Revision status:** no character thesis should be simplified to its 2003 profile label.

## 14.7 Unchanged: Nana/Hachi remains structurally central without categorical settlement

The fan book's title and design strongly center `ナナ&ハチ`. It repeatedly pairs them visually and narratively. That supports their exceptional structural status.

But the fan book does not provide a decisive authorial statement that converts their relationship into a settled mutual conventional romance or sexual partnership.

**Revision status:** Document 04 unchanged: structurally romantic, queer-coded, domestically/familially real, categorically unfinished.

## 14.8 Unchanged: later ethical judgments remain governed by later manga evidence

Because *7.8* predates much of the later Hachi/Takumi material, Ren's death, the mature Shin/Reira developments, and the future separation, it cannot be used to soften or intensify judgments whose decisive evidence appears later.

**Revision status:** Document 07 unchanged.

## 14.9 Unchanged: the hiatus questions remain open

Nothing in this 2003 supplement establishes:

- why future Nana leaves;
- how long the separation lasts;
- whether the central group reunites;
- the final state of Hachi's marriage;
- the final category of Nana/Hachi;
- or the completed destination of the series.

**Revision status:** trajectory remains distinct from destination.

# 15. What *NANA 7.8* adds to the full-series interpretation

After the complete mainline reading, *NANA 7.8* is most valuable as a record of **early self-consciousness**.

The mature manga feels extraordinarily architected because later losses reframe early happiness. The supplement confirms that at least some of that architecture was already consciously formalized by 2003: the two Nanas, fate, future retrospection, parallel lives, music and fashion as social systems, and the desire for readers to move among multiple viewpoints were not inventions of retrospective criticism.

At the same time, the fan book reveals how little an official guide can contain the future work.

Its profiles need clean labels.  
Its A-to-Z needs discrete entries.  
Its music magazine needs marketable stars.  
Its reader section needs favorite-character questions.  
Its lifestyle features need traits that can be imitated.  
Its chronology needs events that fit on lines.

The manga's great achievement is that those categories repeatedly become insufficient.

Hachi stops being merely “the girl struck in love.”  
Yasu's support becomes ethically complicated.  
Celebrity ceases to be merely glamorous.  
Fate becomes grief and contingency rather than romantic guarantee.  
Room 707 becomes an archive rather than just a cute shared apartment.  
The bands become institutions.  
The future becomes absence.  
The women become people no profile can summarize.

That contrast is not a defect in the fan book. It is exactly why the fan book is such useful evidence.

> **The paratext shows us the forms through which *NANA* was made culturally legible in 2003; the later manga shows us how lived persons exceed every legible form.**

This is the mature Phase-6 conclusion.

# 17. Frozen Phase-6 propositions

1. *NANA 7.8* is an early-2003 paratext positioned between Volumes 7 and 8, not a commentary on the mature extant corpus.
2. Direct Yazawa commentary must be separated from the fan book's credited editorial writing.
3. The paired-Nana / dual-protagonist architecture is early and deliberate.
4. Fate belongs to the work's early self-description, but does not prove complete plot predestination.
5. Yazawa explicitly identifies the narrative as looking back from the future by 2003.
6. The same interview indicates that character responses can develop in ways not fully predicted in advance.
7. Multi-perspectival reading belongs to the work's early authorial/editorial self-understanding.
8. Yazawa rejects simple one-to-one real-person models for the cast.
9. The character-profile layer is evidence of editorial marketing emphasis, not definitive psychology.
10. Official 2003 framing already treats *NANA* as a music/fashion/urban-material culture object as well as a relationship drama.
11. `A to Z of NANA` demonstrates the franchise's early database-like consumption of places, objects, brands, technologies and relationships.
12. `NANA's world TOUR GUIDE` converts fictional geography into real-world fan pilgrimage and should be classified as cultural-reference material.
13. Character Room features convert character identity into reproducible lifestyle practice; they are metatext, not hidden narrative canon.
14. `Voices to NANA` is useful as a curated early-reception snapshot but cannot establish population-level reader opinion.
15. Early reader reception already uses *NANA* as a vocabulary for real friendship, romance, style, identity and social comparison.
16. `ROCK ON JAPON` formally simulates the celebrity/media apparatus later made ethically consequential in the manga.
17. The fan book's own marketing demonstrates the attraction of turning artists and characters into public consumable images.
18. `NANA's Story` strongly reinforces the parallel-life architecture while remaining an editorial retrospective summary.
19. The Volume-8 teaser confirms the supplement's transitional publication function rather than adding an independent final answer.
20. Nothing in *NANA 7.8* resolves the final Nana/Hachi category, Nana's future absence, Hachi's final marriage state, or the manga's unfinished destination.
21. The mature corpus's strongest existing conclusions are therefore **refined and historically grounded**, not overturned, by the supplement.
22. The governing Phase-6 rule remains: **the manga establishes; Yazawa comments; the paratext frames; interpretation connects—none should be silently substituted for another.**
