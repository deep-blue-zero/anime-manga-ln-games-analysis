## Analytical priority

Hiro's AV pass should test five textual propositions:

1. whether `神格` has a reproducible visual/vocal signature distinct from ordinary charisma;
2. whether bodily fragility/exhaustion is aestheticized, hidden, or transformed in performance;
3. whether `かわいい` desire creates a distinct performative mode from the goddess/mystical image;
4. whether crisis pressure produces audible/visible performance-state changes;
5. whether `光景 → コントラスト → サンフェーデッド` forms a developmental musical/visual sequence comparable in importance to Temari's or Saki's core song sequences.

# E. AV hypotheses to test

## HIRO-AV-H01 — difficulty-seeking voice vs fear-of-loss voice
Does Dear 005/008 playful interest in difficulty sound materially different from Dear 028–30 fear that the shared life could end?

## HIRO-AV-H02 — `神格` as performed stillness/distance
Does stage direction construct divinity through stillness, camera distance, breathiness, restraint, fragility, lighting, or another device?

## HIRO-AV-H03 — cute desire is musically audible
Does Hiro's singing/acting become more playful, bright, frontal, or bodily relaxed in cute/comic repertoire, or is “cute Hiro” largely lyrical/visual rather than vocal?

## HIRO-AV-H04 — crisis improves performance
Can the text's claim be observed through projection, attack, dynamics, phrasing, body, eye line, or staging, rather than accepted only because characters say so?

## HIRO-AV-H05 — bodily fragility and spectacle
Does choreography accommodate physical limitation, aestheticize it, conceal it, or transform it into an unusual performance grammar?

## HIRO-AV-H06 — `光景 → コントラスト → サンフェーデッド`
Does the three-principal-song sequence move from solitary/abstract impossibility toward relational stakes and self-owned dream, or does the musical evidence demand another developmental model?

## HIRO-AV-H07 — post-victory miracle vs ordinary Hiro
Compare H.I.F.-era stage/public delivery with Dear 037 ordinary speech. The text insists peak state is momentary; AV should test whether the performance language likewise marks a maximum state.
