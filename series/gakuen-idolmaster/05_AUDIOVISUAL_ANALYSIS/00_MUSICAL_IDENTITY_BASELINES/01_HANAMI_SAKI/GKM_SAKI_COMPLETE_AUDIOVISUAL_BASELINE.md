# HANAMI SAKI — COMPLETE AUDIOVISUAL BASELINE

## 0. Executive finding

The audiovisual corpus confirms the textual reading of Hanami Saki as a **constructed all-rounder**, but it changes where the center of that construction lies.

Saki does not possess one fixed sonic genre, one emotionally uniform speaking voice, or one stage persona that remains unchanged across songs. Her most stable audiovisual identity is a **conversion procedure**:

> exposure becomes propulsion;  
> bodily effort becomes spectacle;  
> private contradiction becomes direct address;  
> inherited or common material becomes a challenge to comparison;  
> dependence becomes renewed forward motion rather than passive surrender.

That procedure is audible, visible, and acted.

In dialogue, Saki can move among at least four sharply differentiated performed states:

- a low, measured intimate register used when she is trying to hold herself together or make space for another person;
- a high, volatile affective register used for panic, frustration, laughter, embarrassment, and immediate competitive reaction;
- a consciously reassembled confidence register in which she names herself, raises her posture and vocal projection, and converts uncertainty into a public task;
- a formal stage register that begins with disciplined gratitude and can pivot into commanding rivalry.

These are not a true self and several masks. They are context-sensitive ways of being Saki. The voice acting makes the textual thesis precise: **confidence is not the absence of uncertainty; it is a performed technology for deciding what uncertainty will do next.**

The musical corpus shows a similar structure. Saki’s principal individual songs are not randomly scattered across genres. `Fighting My Way`, `Boom Boom Pow`, `EGO`, and `Wildest Flower` share a broadly modern, bass-forward electronic/pop production environment in the supplied masters. Yet the wider repertoire—`Campus mode!!`, `がむしゃらに行こう！`, `ガラクタロード`, `GO MY WAY!!`, `初`, `ENDLESS DANCE`, and `雨上がりのアイリス`—moves across bright school pop, comic effort-song, prescribed competition material, inherited franchise repertoire, theatrical abstraction, and unit performance.

What remains Saki-like across those changes is less a single arrangement vocabulary than a performed insistence on **forwardness**:

- phrases are treated as actions rather than contemplative states;
- gestures frequently extend outward, point ahead, strike, gather, or summon;
- direct eye contact and frontal staging turn the audience into a participant in her declared future;
- contrasts—cute/strong, embarrassed/commanding, private/public, solitary/relational—are not hidden but driven into the performance.

The foundational before/after pair is `Fighting My Way` and `Wildest Flower`.

`Fighting My Way` presents Saki as fragmented self-construction. Its official MV surrounds her with red-black-white typography, media panels, repeated portraits, slogans, interface imagery, and aggressive editorial motion. Vulnerability appears, but it is immediately turned into branded kinetic force. The self is something she must declare and keep making under conditions of scrutiny.

`Wildest Flower` does not replace this with softness. Its official MV is heavier, more bodily, and more historical: running copies of Saki, exhaustion, a fall, warning-label collage, tears, impact, sketch-like memories, other figures, and renewed movement. The self is no longer maintained by pretending that no break occurred. It survives by carrying breakage, prior selves, and other people forward.

The most accurate audiovisual formulation is therefore:

> **`Fighting My Way` is Saki producing an invincible image out of exposed weakness. `Wildest Flower` is Saki producing indomitability out of accumulated breakage.**

The dialogue performances provide the missing proof that this is not merely an MV-symbolism claim. In Dear Idol 023, Saki can move from controlled older-sister composure to a raw cry of frustration, then immediately order another race—without having emotionally recovered. In Dear Idol 027, she returns joking, asks to cry against the Producer, speaks at length in a lowered and uncertain register, and only then rises into the declaration of the `不屈のトップアイドル`. In the `ガラクタロード` communication, she reverses the direction of care and invites the Producer to disclose weakness. In Dear Idol 035, she actively rejects the idea that his exclusive dream is merely a burden and names it `期待`—expectation that makes an idol shine. In Dear Idol 036, she begins her Prima Stella address in formal gratitude and then transforms the ceremony into an open challenge.

This does not settle every ethical problem. The audiovisual evidence strengthens Saki’s agency in the Producer relationship: she hears, interprets, teases, rejects his framing, and consciously accepts the bond. It does not erase the asymmetry of a Producer whose dream has become singularly organized around one idol. Likewise, the victory speech is generous because it invites pursuit and shared challenge, but remains hierarchical: Saki still places herself at the front and commands everyone to follow.

The complete baseline therefore **confirms, strengthens, and narrows** the Phase-3 textual thesis:

> **Hanami Saki is the idol of constructed indomitability. Her defining audiovisual act is not simply to sound strong or look energetic. It is to make weakness, embarrassment, plurality, dependence, inherited material, and even collapse perform as forward motion without denying that they occurred.**

## 1.3 Evidence classes used here

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `AV-A` | Directly observed audio/video evidence in the supplied file |
| `AV-B` | Directly observed work plus exact script/asset/timing correlation |
| `AV-C` | Script-visible cue or staging fact whose rendered quality is not independently isolated |
| `SIG` | Derived signal measurement; useful comparatively, not equivalent to a musical score or isolated-stem analysis |
| `INF` | Interpretation supported by observed form but not directly stated by the source |

Most major claims in this document are `AV-A/AV-B`. Numerical tempo, spectral, loudness, onset, and prosodic summaries are `SIG` and are treated conservatively.

## 1.4 Analytical limitations

The baseline is complete relative to the agreed sample, but not unlimited.

- No isolated vocal stems were supplied. Exact tessitura, formant structure, breath noise, vocal compression, and singer-only dynamics cannot be separated cleanly from the full mix.
- The supplied masters differ in resolution, frame rate, codec, silence padding, interface lead-in, and loudness. Raw LUFS values are not treated as mastering-quality comparisons.
- Automated pitch tracking in dialogue can be contaminated by BGM and should be read as a relative pattern across phases, not as a publication-grade transcription of every fundamental-frequency contour.
- The prescribed and inherited songs have not yet been compared against every other idol’s rendition. Saki’s version can be analyzed internally, but claims of unique divergence must wait for a multi-idol comparison.
- `雨上がりのアイリス` belongs to the `U1` ensemble track. It is evidence of Saki’s unit-performance capacity, not a scene that can be pasted into every solo-route biography.

These limits narrow claims; they do not invalidate the baseline.

# 2. The audiovisual thesis: Saki as a conversion system

## 2.1 Why “energetic ace” is inadequate

Saki is frequently staged with forward motion, large gestures, bright or saturated color, rapid cuts, and forceful declarations. It would be easy to summarize her audiovisual profile as “energetic ace.” That description captures frequency but not function.

Her energy is rarely neutral exuberance. It does several different jobs:

- it converts exposure into action in `Fighting My Way`;
- it converts physical confidence into public pleasure in `Boom Boom Pow`;
- it converts multiple image variants into authored selfhood in `EGO`;
- it converts institutional belonging into public encouragement in `Campus mode!!`;
- it converts collapse into historical continuity in `Wildest Flower`;
- it converts embarrassment and failed polish into comic effort in `がむしゃらに行こう！`;
- it converts prescribed common material into a demand for individual comparison in `ガラクタロード`;
- it converts inherited repertoire into renewed challenge in `GO MY WAY!!` and `ENDLESS DANCE`;
- it converts unit belonging into a field of rotating mutual attention in `雨上がりのアイリス`.

The stable element is not “fast” or “loud” in isolation. It is the decision to make a condition **do something**.

## 2.2 Direct address

Across the 3DMVs, Saki’s performance persona repeatedly orients toward a visible or implied interlocutor:

- direct camera looks;
- frontal pointing and open-arm summoning gestures;
- gestures that move from the body outward;
- commands embedded in lyrics or stage speech;
- use of the crowd as a force field rather than decorative background.

This corresponds to her textual habit of naming the next future aloud. A Saki performance commonly feels less like private expression accidentally overheard and more like a challenge issued to viewers, rivals, the Producer, Ume, or herself.

The important correction is that direct address does not always mean dominance. In `Campus mode!!`, it becomes guidance and permission. In `雨上がりのアイリス`, directness is distributed among three members. In `Wildest Flower`, the gaze emerges from a visual narrative of falling and continuation rather than uncomplicated superiority.

## 2.3 Body as instrument, record, and proof

Saki’s body carries several audiovisual meanings:

- athletic instrument;
- object of production and costume;
- source of kinetic command;
- site of exhaustion and failure;
- archive of prior training and damage;
- proof that continuing is not abstract.

`Fighting My Way` often converts the body into graphic material: portrait, silhouette, panel, poster, interface object, slogan-bearing image. `Wildest Flower` returns weight to it: running, falling, punching, crying, rising. `Boom Boom Pow` treats physicality as pleasure and ease. `がむしゃらに行こう！` permits the body to be awkward, embarrassed, and funny. `雨上がりのアイリス` places it inside handholds, shared lines, and rotating formations.

That progression reinforces the textual body thesis. Saki does not cease instrumentalizing herself, but the body becomes more than a solitary optimization project. It becomes a place where relationship, memory, failure, and pleasure remain visible.

## 2.4 Multiplicity without incoherence

The official MVs frequently multiply Saki:

- repeated images and panels in `Fighting My Way`;
- costume and persona catalogues in `EGO`;
- running copies, sketch memories, and accumulated figures in `Wildest Flower`.

The meanings differ.

In `Fighting My Way`, multiplication resembles media saturation and contested self-definition. The image must keep asserting itself against fragmentation.

In `EGO`, multiplication becomes curation. Outfits, chibi schematics, filmstrips, and production imagery present selfhood as authored display. “Ego” is not only selfishness; it is the right to decide which constructed self enters the frame.

In `Wildest Flower`, multiplication becomes continuity. Prior Sakis are not discarded as failed prototypes. They are carried forward as the path on which the present self runs.

This is one of the strongest formal supports for the Phase-3 claim that mature Saki gathers prior selves rather than purifying herself into one finally authentic version.

# 3. Speaking voice and acted personhood

## 3.1 Baseline qualities

Across the six scenes, Saki’s performed speech is unusually elastic. Stable tendencies include:

- clear articulation even at high emotional intensity;
- rapid shifts between teasing, command, self-disclosure, and formal address;
- a high and mobile comic register for panic or embarrassment;
- an ability to lower and slow the voice substantially in intimate scenes;
- frequent re-expansion into projected certainty after a pause, BGM reset, or relational cue;
- laughter as both genuine pleasure and a way of converting vulnerability back into movement.

Her public “Saki voice” is therefore not simply her natural resting voice. It is an expertly repeatable mode she can enter, lose, rebuild, and deploy.

## 3.2 Public and private do not form a simple true/false opposition

The six scenes disprove a crude model in which private Saki is weak and public Saki is fake.

Private Saki can be:

- composed and solicitous in the `ガラクタロード` phone call;
- raw and loud in Dear Idol 023;
- teasing and observant in Dear Idol 035;
- openly needy in Dear Idol 027.

Public Saki can be:

- formally grateful;
- competitively theatrical;
- emotionally exposed through a song;
- genuinely joyful;
- strategically self-naming.

The distinction is better understood as **audience-shaped regulation**. Saki decides which emotional force is useful in the current relationship and arena. Growth does not abolish this regulation. It makes the transitions more voluntary and less dependent on denying the preceding state.

## 3.3 Confidence as production technology

The `Fighting My Way` commu is the clearest small-scale demonstration.

Saki begins in a high, restless state because Ume is present and the song will expose her complicated feelings. She already knows that asking the Producer for an “ignition line” is a routine. The Producer names her as weak, vain, and prone to bluffing, then calls that version admirable. Saki melts into a delighted, almost childlike response, catches herself, exaggerates her own worth, and ends by declaring that she will sing indomitability and victory as she feels them.

The confidence is produced in real time through:

1. exposure;
2. accurate recognition;
3. praise that includes rather than erases the weakness;
4. comic pleasure;
5. self-naming;
6. a forward task.

The personal BGM cue enters after a moment of acoustic clearing, formalizing the transition. Confidence is not a lie laid over fear. It is the final stage of a relational production process.

## 3.4 The voice can remain broken without becoming inert

Dear Idol 023 and 027 together provide the strongest acting proof for the `無敵` → `不屈` distinction.

In 023, Saki’s first-loss admission is quiet and controlled. The cry of frustration then erupts at a substantially higher and more energetic register. Immediately afterward she orders both of them to raise their faces and run again. The voice has resumed command, but the scene does not claim emotional restoration. Later speech becomes quieter again as she asks the Producer to stand beside her and allow her to lean without pretending.

In 027, the sequence is even clearer. Her return is playful; crying against the Producer lowers the performance; self-doubt occupies an extended subdued middle; the final indomitable declaration rises sharply only after the Producer gives a new definition of strength and Saki receives it.

The performance therefore rejects two simplifications:

- loudness is not proof that pain has ended;
- quietness is not proof that agency has disappeared.

Indomitability is the capacity to move between those states without declaring one illegitimate.

## 3.5 Reciprocal witness

The `ガラクタロード` communication materially revises the earlier textual model of Saki’s dependency.

The nighttime scene begins with regulation: stretching, warm drink, sleep preparation, hinoki aroma. Saki speaks in a softened domestic register. She then notices that she usually does all the talking and asks the Producer to speak. More specifically, because she has shown him so much weakness, she wants to know his.

When he describes the day she lost to Ume and his failure to reach out, Saki does not seize the moment to restore the heroic Producer image. She answers that he is now beside her, rejects his claim of helplessness, and identifies his abandonment of the original ideal as the choice that gave her courage to reach back.

Her final teasing withdrawal prevents the scene from becoming solemn therapeutic closure. Yet the relational direction has changed:

> the Producer can be weak before Saki;  
> Saki can interpret his weakness without ceasing to trust him;  
> their shared story is not only “he witnesses her collapse.”

The relationship remains intensive and potentially enclosing, but it is no longer adequately described as one-way emotional dependence.

## 3.6 Formal stage speech and competitive command

Dear Idol 036 demonstrates Saki’s public range within one address.

She first speaks to Ume privately as victorious older sister and rival. Their exchange is loud, laughing, frustrated, tender, and mutually recognizing. Ume calls Saki her Prima Stella; Saki answers by calling Ume `わたしのアイドル`—my idol.

The public speech then begins in a disciplined formal register. Saki thanks the audience and names teachers, companions, rivals, training, practice, and shared movement. The structure distributes credit before naming the fulfilled dream.

Then comes `だけどっ!`

The music clears before the pivot. Saki abandons ceremonial closure and addresses the other idols as competitors who should not simply congratulate her. Her pitch and projection rise across the progression from private sisterhood, to public gratitude, to challenge. The personal theme returns as she imagines their teeth grinding and commands all of them to follow while she leads the charge.

The result is neither modest collectivism nor solitary triumph. It is **generative hierarchy**:

- she occupies the front;
- she turns the title into an invitation to attack it;
- she makes her victory a shared next problem.

That is the enacted form of public older-sisterhood.

# 4. Singing identity and musical profile

## 4.1 A qualified sonic family

The full-mix signal analysis indicates that Saki’s principal individual songs cluster more than the earlier text-only analysis could establish.

`Fighting My Way`, `Boom Boom Pow`, `EGO`, and `Wildest Flower` are all comparatively low-frequency-weighted within the supplied set. Their median spectral centroids are lower than the bright school/prescribed material, and their mixes place substantial energy below roughly 160 Hz. These are broad mix-level facts, not isolated-vocal properties.

By contrast:

- `Campus mode!!` and `ガラクタロード` are the brightest/mid-forward supplied objects by spectral-centroid estimate;
- `初` is bright and dynamically spacious;
- `GO MY WAY!!` and `ENDLESS DANCE` occupy inherited/theatrical spaces;
- `雨上がりのアイリス` has a wider dynamic profile and ensemble distribution.

The earlier hypothesis that Saki is entirely genre-fluid should therefore be narrowed:

> **Her individual image-song core has a recognizable modern, bass-forward production family. Her procedural adaptability becomes most visible when she inhabits common, inherited, prescribed, or unit material outside that family.**

## 4.2 Vocal conclusions that the corpus does support

Without stems, the analysis avoids publication-grade claims about exact range or isolated timbre. The supplied mixes do support several conservative observations:

- Saki’s lead remains highly legible against dense arrangements.
- Her performed identity survives both low-frequency-heavy personal songs and brighter ensemble/common material.
- The stage persona repeatedly privileges attack, forward articulation, and phrase-level momentum over a floating or purely atmospheric delivery.
- Emotional contrast is not achieved only through becoming quieter; it is often achieved by changing the relation between attack and surrounding arrangement.
- Shared-song individuation depends heavily on phrasing, gesture, visual embodiment, and role commitment rather than on one unchanging vocal color.

A future stem-level pass could refine breath, onset, resonance, and register. It is not required to establish the current character argument.

## 4.3 `Fighting My Way`: exposed weakness as self-launch

The song’s commu explicitly defines it as dangerous because it exposes the complicated relation beneath Saki’s older-sister image. The official MV responds by refusing intimacy in a soft visual language. Instead, exposure is processed through typography, interface panels, repeated portraits, slogan fragments, red-black-white contrast, and high editorial density.

The signal profile reinforces that restlessness: among the supplied songs, `Fighting My Way` has the highest estimated onset rate. The result feels less like a confession that pauses for sympathy than a confession engineered into attack.

The 3DMV returns that graphic argument to the body. Saki occupies a saturated solo stage, frequently meets the camera directly, and turns the crowd into an arena for self-declaration. The vulnerable content is not visually hidden, but it is staged as something she can command.

Character function:

> the “invincible” Saki is already made from weakness; the early mistake is believing the production succeeds only if the seams disappear.

## 4.4 `Boom Boom Pow`: physical confidence as pleasure

`Boom Boom Pow` provides necessary counterevidence to any reading that treats Saki’s energy as always defensive. Its 3DMV uses an outdoor sunset/festival environment, warmer color, sporty styling, expansive space, and a less claustrophobic relation to the audience.

Here physicality is celebratory. Movement does not primarily prove that she can survive exposure. It expresses the pleasure of occupying space, rhythm, and attention. This matters because mature Saki cannot be reduced to trauma processing. She enjoys force, performance, and being looked at.

The song remains part of the low-frequency-weighted personal family, but its audiovisual function is lighter: **confidence can be sensuous, playful, and physically easy, not only laboriously reconstructed.**

## 4.5 `EGO`: persona as authored multiplicity

The official `EGO` MV foregrounds production itself: filmstrip imagery, outfit catalogues, chibi diagrams, a clapperboard, repeated selves, styling, and instructions to look.

Its strongest contribution is to reject the idea that costume or persona necessarily falsifies Saki. The MV’s question is who authors the image. Saki’s “ego” is the right to curate multiple visible forms without being reduced to any one of them.

The visual movement from enumerated outfits and constructed images toward performance does not reveal a naked self behind the costumes. It shows that construction is part of selfhood.

## 4.6 `Campus mode!!`: institutional aspiration personalized as guidance

The brightly colored campus stage, balloons, star motifs, school costume, and open gestures place Saki inside Hatsuboshi’s collective aspirational language. The visible lyric framing emphasizes thought, failure, and the presence of allies.

Saki’s version does not make the school song purely individual. It makes her public-older-sister mode legible: institutional optimism becomes something she can direct toward people who are not yet certain of themselves.

This is one place where direct address becomes inclusion rather than challenge.

## 4.7 `Wildest Flower`: the weight of accumulated continuation

`Wildest Flower` is the core developmental work.

The official MV begins with stadium lighting and multiplication, but the running copies do not function like the media panels of `Fighting My Way`. They form a history of effort. The MV passes through exhaustion, a fall, label-like visual noise, punching, tears, sketch memories, other presences, and renewed movement.

The supplied mix is the darkest/heaviest of the core individual songs by median spectral-centroid estimate and is strongly low-frequency-weighted. It also shows a broader RMS span than `Fighting My Way` in the analyzed files. These are relative signal facts, not a complete arrangement analysis, but they align with the visual argument: the later song carries more weight and dynamic expansion.

The 3DMV adds black-clad shadow/backup figures and a stadium environment. Saki remains the focal performer, but the stage image no longer pretends that motion is solitary. Others become visible as shadow, support, memory, or relational force.

The difference from `Fighting My Way` is not:

> dark song → happy song  
> weak Saki → strong Saki  
> solitary song → group song

It is:

> fragmented self-production under scrutiny  
> → accumulated self-continuity after collapse.

`Wildest Flower` is indomitable because it preserves the fallen body inside the victorious image.

## 4.8 `がむしゃらに行こう！`: making unpolished effort entertaining

The classroom-as-stage environment, recording/interface motifs, sporty school styling, and comic expressions let Saki perform a kind of imperfection not organized as tragedy.

The song’s significance is that “messiness” can become entertainment. Saki can be frustrated, embarrassed, noisy, and still delightful. This widens the audiovisual meaning of exposure: not every weakness needs solemn recognition or heroic reconstruction.

## 4.9 `ガラクタロード`: prescribed material and procedural individuation

The 3DMV is maximalist: multicolored toybox staging, bright crowd fields, rapid role changes, and a pink-purple performance look capable of moving among cute, aggressive, absurd, and triumphant poses.

This is the strongest single work for the “procedural individuality” hypothesis. The song is prescribed common material. Saki makes it legible as hers not by changing its underlying identity, but by committing completely to each shift and turning variety itself into competitive force.

A crucial limit remains: without other idols’ versions in the same comparative frame, this document does not claim that every specific choice is uniquely Saki’s. It establishes that her rendition is internally coherent with her broader performance grammar.

## 4.10 `GO MY WAY!!`, `初`, and `ENDLESS DANCE`: inheritance without museumification

These works test whether Saki can inhabit repertoire whose identity precedes the current personal arc.

`GO MY WAY!!` uses classic bright directional imagery—roads, arrows, stars, and a conventional red idol costume. Saki’s forwardness fits the composition naturally. The performance treats inherited optimism as a present challenge rather than nostalgic preservation.

`初` supplies an orthodox institutional baseline: cream-white dress, broad audience, softer closeups, and foundational idol sincerity. It proves that Saki’s appeal is not dependent on graphic aggression or bass-heavy personal singles. She can inhabit conventional brightness without disappearing.

`ENDLESS DANCE` shifts into dark surreal theatre: green-purple space, stairs, doors, shadow, carnival/doll-like motifs, and symbolic abstraction. Saki does not overwrite that world with her usual athletic red vocabulary. She enters it and remains identifiable through bodily commitment and forward presence.

Together, these works show that adaptation is not surrender. Her signature can survive inherited visual grammar.

## 4.11 `雨上がりのアイリス`: distributed center and relational embodiment

The Re;IRIS 3DMV is the strongest visual counterexample to any claim that Saki must monopolize attention.

The stage uses lavender rain/light imagery, handholding, mutual gazes, shared pathways, rotating focal positions, and formation changes that allow Temari and Kotone to carry attention without presenting Saki’s temporary recession as defeat.

Saki remains recognizable when she re-enters the foreground, but the group’s meaning depends on her ability to support another person’s line and inhabit a collective image.

Within `U1`, this is evidence that mature Saki can experience distributed center as a stronger competitive ecology rather than dilution of self.

# 5. `Fighting My Way` and `Wildest Flower` as a before/after pair

## 5.1 Shared authorship, different self-models

Both works are unusually suitable for comparison because official metadata assigns the same lyricist/composer-arranger pairing. That does not prove a deliberately planned diptych by itself, but the audiovisual contrast is substantial enough to analyze as a developmental pair.

| Dimension | `Fighting My Way` | `Wildest Flower` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self-image | fragmented, repeated, branded, contested | multiple, historical, accumulated |
| Motion | editorial attack and graphic propulsion | running, falling, impact, renewed movement |
| Vulnerability | immediately weaponized as assertion | retained as weight within continuation |
| Others | primarily audience/media field around a solo image | traces, shadows, memories, and relational accompaniment |
| Sonic signal | restless onset density; bass-forward | darker/heavier centroid; bass-forward; broader level span |
| Character state | confidence constructed to survive exposure | indomitability constructed after collapse |

## 5.2 What changes

The later work permits time and weight.

The first song’s self-construction feels urgent because exposure threatens to split public image from private feeling. The later song does not need to prove that the split never existed. It makes the split, fall, and multiplicity part of what the audience is asked to receive.

The difference is ethical as well as psychological. Early Saki wants to remain the object Ume can chase without seeing the cost. Later Saki can be seen as someone who cried, leaned on another person, and continued.

## 5.3 What does not change

Saki does not become contemplative in the sense of relinquishing competitive force.

Both works move forward. Both convert inward material into public address. Both use a dense modern production environment. Both insist on active self-authorship.

The later song is not a rejection of the earlier one. It is an accumulation of it.

# 6. Relationship revisions produced by audiovisual evidence

## 6.1 Saki and the Producer

The acting evidence strengthens Saki’s agency.

She is not merely the recipient of a Producer who defines her:

- she detects his stiffness and trembling retrospectively;
- she solicits his weakness;
- she interprets his failed ideal;
- she rejects his claim that his devotion is only a burden;
- she decides to call it expectation;
- she chooses to accept responsibility for the dream.

The relationship is therefore co-authored more substantially than the text-only pass could fully prove.

The enclosure question remains. In Dear Idol 035, the Producer describes her as the only idol of his life, and the scene does not reject exclusivity. The performance makes that exclusivity emotionally mutual and consciously accepted; it does not make the institutional asymmetry disappear.

Provisional judgment:

> **AV strengthens reciprocity and consent within the scene, while leaving structural dependency unresolved.**

## 6.2 Saki and Ume

Dear Idol 036 offers the strongest performed evidence of mutual recognition currently available from Saki’s side.

The sisters can laugh, rage, promise another race, call each other idol and Prima Stella, and share the view from the summit. The scene is no longer organized around Saki hiding pain to protect a vertical older-sister image.

Yet hierarchy has not vanished. Saki has retaken the title and speaks from the front. A fully horizontal interpretation requires Ume’s own core pass and support/event evidence.

## 6.3 Saki and the audience

The audiovisual corpus confirms that the audience is not a passive receiver.

Crowds are staged as:

- pressure;
- energy field;
- witness;
- challenger;
- institutional public;
- group that Saki thanks;
- group she commands;
- source of force she incorporates.

Her stage identity depends on making the audience’s presence actionable. This is the performed version of the `外付けの筋肉` metaphor, though the MVs also show that the relationship can become invitation and shared aspiration rather than pure instrumentality.

## 6.4 Saki and the group

`雨上がりのアイリス` provides direct audiovisual proof that Saki’s visibility can coexist with distributed attention. The earlier unit reading is therefore strengthened: she does not mature by becoming less distinct, but by entering a structure where other people’s focal moments increase rather than threaten the total field.

# 8. Revision to the Phase-3 character thesis

The text-only thesis remains valid but can now be made more exact.

## 8.1 Revised compact thesis

> **Hanami Saki is the idol of constructed indomitability. Her defining audiovisual practice is to convert what could arrest her—weakness, embarrassment, bodily limit, failure, dependence, multiplicity, inherited material—into a forward-facing performance without requiring the audience to forget that the arrest occurred.**

## 8.2 Revised account of confidence

Confidence is neither an inner essence nor merely a protective lie. It is:

- a vocal register;
- a posture;
- a relational response to accurate recognition;
- an editorial and visual style;
- a bodily rhythm;
- a self-naming speech act;
- a way of turning the next action into something publicly legible.

Its ethical maturity depends on whether it permits the preceding weakness to remain real.

## 8.3 Revised account of musical identity

Saki’s musical identity has two layers:

1. **a core personal-song family**—dense, contemporary, bass-forward, image-conscious, and highly compatible with direct assertion;
2. **a procedural performance signature**—forward attack, bodily commitment, direct address, rapid role-switching, and conversion of the source material into a comparative challenge.

The second layer explains why she remains recognizable in unlike shared songs. The first prevents the analysis from pretending that composition and arrangement are irrelevant.

## 8.4 Revised account of relationship

Saki’s post-defeat bond with the Producer is not only dependency. It is an increasingly reciprocal system of witnessing and re-authorship.

The unresolved question is no longer whether Saki possesses agency in the bond. She plainly does. The question is whether the pair can preserve that agency under exclusivity, institutional power, separation, or incompatible goals.

# 9. Remaining limits and future tests

The baseline resolves the initial AV queue. It also defines what later phases must test.

## 9.1 Multi-idol prescribed-song comparison

Saki’s `ガラクタロード` is internally coherent and highly individuated. A later H.I.F. musical study should compare:

- phrasing and vocal attack;
- choreographic role choices;
- costume/stage relation;
- camera behavior;
- emotional interpretation;
- how each idol handles the same prescribed material.

Only then can “uniquely Saki-like” claims be promoted from internal inference to comparative evidence.

## 9.2 Ume’s audiovisual perspective

The Saki-side victory scene supports mutual recognition. Ume’s own songs, MVs, and voice acting must test:

- whether Saki’s restored lead feels liberating or reimposes verticality;
- how Ume performs admiration and competitive rage;
- whether “my Prima Stella” and “my idol” carry symmetrical emotional force.

## 9.3 Stem-level vocal analysis

A later technical module could use official instrumental/vocal stems if available to refine:

- tessitura;
- onset and consonant behavior;
- breath management;
- register transitions;
- sustained-note profile;
- vibrato and dynamic shaping;
- speaking/singing continuity.

No current character conclusion depends on pretending that work has already been done.

## 9.4 Events and support cards

The audiovisual baseline does not substitute for Phase 4–5. Those sources must test whether the performed maturity visible in major hinge scenes generalizes to ordinary friendship, refusal, leisure, jealousy, material life, and situations where competition is not the appropriate organizing value.

# 10. Final baseline statement

The complete AV corpus does not reveal a hidden “real Saki” beneath the textual character. It reveals the labor by which her many real states remain connected.

The high comic panic before `Fighting My Way`, the restrained nighttime care in `ガラクタロード`, the scream after losing to Ume, the lowered voice while asking to cry against the Producer, the formal gratitude of the Prima Stella, the laughter that challenges Ume, the graphic fragmentation of the first official MV, the weighted accumulation of `Wildest Flower`, the maximal role-switching of `ガラクタロード`, and the distributed focality of Re;IRIS all belong to the same person.

Their unity is not sameness.

Their unity is the recurring act:

> **Saki takes whatever state she is in, makes it answerable to someone, and turns it toward the next race.**

That is the audiovisual form of constructed indomitability.
