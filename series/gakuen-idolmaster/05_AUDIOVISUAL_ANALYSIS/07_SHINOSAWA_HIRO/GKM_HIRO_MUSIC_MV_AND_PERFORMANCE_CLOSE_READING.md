# SHINOSAWA HIRO — MUSIC, MV, AND PERFORMANCE CLOSE READING

## 0. Method

The pass keeps four source forms separate:

1. **song commu** — story authorship and intended character meaning;
2. **3DMV/rendered performance** — body, choreography, expression, and virtual camera;
3. **authored MV/lyric video** — symbolic/editorial world not necessarily literal story continuity;
4. **derivative montage** — reception argument, never primary proof.

The audio metrics are full-mix. The “experimental proxy” used in the technical appendix is a within-packet descriptive composite of beat irregularity, chroma/tonnetz change, spectral flux/flatness, structural novelty, and dynamics. It is not an objective genre classifier.

## 1. Musical thesis — curated experimentalism, portable resistance

Hiro's catalog is unusually willing to place character music near the boundary where pop form becomes unstable, conceptual, or abrasive. That is not accidental flavor. It mirrors her narrative function.

But two layers must be distinguished:

- **curated experimentalism:** the unusual compositional/editorial environments repeatedly selected for Hiro-specific works;
- **portable resistance:** the quieter traits that remain when she enters common repertoire—soft precision, visible effort, asymmetrical weighting, and refusal to counterfeit ease.

The first belongs strongly to repertoire design. The second belongs more plausibly to Hiro as performer.

## 2. `光景`

### Song-commu function

The earliest song commu converts Hiro from a private difficult project into someone who must imagine being known. It does not simply ask whether she can complete a live. It asks what an audience can perceive in a performer who initially struggles to transmit herself.

### Authored MV function

The authored form gives Hiro an environment in which musical irregularity is not failure. Dense or discontinuous change becomes the world she inhabits. The vocal need not dominate by force because the arrangement is designed around negotiation and surprise.

### 3DMV function

The rendered performance returns the abstract work to a body whose technique can be compared across states. This is where “Hiro improves” must be tested rather than asserted.

### Finding

> **`光景` is not a conventional debut song about believing in oneself. It is a formal proposition that an unsuitable body and an unsuitable musical language can become mutually legible.**

## 3. `コントラスト`

`コントラスト` shifts the organizing idea from singular oddity to relation among differences. Its value lies in refusing a clean binary between weak/strong, strange/normal, distant/cute, or hobby/dream.

The full/static presentation provides a useful audio control against the 3DMV. Differences attributable to camera and choreography can be separated from properties of the recording itself.

> **The song makes contradiction productive by arranging it rather than solving it.**

## 4. `サンフェーデッド`

The late principal work matters because it returns to a highly authored experimental language after Hiro has acquired responsibility. If the series wished to show maturity by normalization, this would be the place to do so. Instead, it preserves difficulty while allowing attachment, fear, and future desire to enter.

The authored MV and rendered performance should therefore be read together but not collapsed. One constructs symbolic/editorial instability; the other asks what Hiro's developed body can do inside a public performance grammar.

> **`サンフェーデッド` is experimentalism after consequences.**

## 5. `メクルメ`

`メクルメ` is a key control because it comes from a different creative context and is not merely a duplicate of the main trilogy's logic. Its lyric-video and full/static forms allow direct attention to sonic and graphic construction without choreography supplying character meaning.

Its presence strengthens the claim that Hiro-specific music is curated for formal volatility. It also warns against treating “Hiro music” as one signature sound. The commonality is not one genre; it is tolerance for instability and concept.

## 6. `コンテンポラリのダンス`

This work makes the body/form relation explicit. “Contemporary dance” is not merely a title or costume: it invites the audience to treat movement as inquiry rather than standardized idol display.

For Hiro, that is especially apt. Her body cannot be assumed to disappear into fluent technique. Movement remains visible as a decision, limit, and experiment.

## 7. `ガラクタロード`

The commu establishes the work as a retrospective identity hinge. Hiro's prepared answer—choosing the least suitable profession—remains true. The deeper answer—wanting to become cute—reframes the entire origin.

The 3DMV lets the public idol carry a secret that was initially difficult to say aloud. The work therefore joins three layers:

- difficulty as chosen resistance;
- cuteness as ordinary desire;
- Producer's dream as future-bearing relation.

## 8. `Campus mode!!`

This is the strongest institutional control. The song's inherited brightness does not automatically match Hiro's body or self-image. Its value lies in exposing adaptation.

Hiro does not need to transform the composition into experimental art to make the performance meaningful. The fact that she can inhabit ordinary Hatsuboshi repertoire without erasing visible effort is itself development.

## 9. `初`

`初` places beginning inside a system that already knows Hiro has improved. It can therefore be read at two levels:

- firstness as the memory of genuine incapacity;
- firstness as the willingness to keep entering unfamiliar form.

The lower-resolution source limits microvisual claims, but it remains adequate for broad movement, timing, and source comparison.

## 10. `みちなるひろがる`

The duet's form is analytically rich because Hiro and China embody different routes through weakness. China begins with privilege, affection, and technical immaturity; Hiro begins with genius, bodily unsuitability, and chosen resistance.

The 3DMV and authored MV show shared focality rather than a solo character using a supporting partner. The missing commu prevents strong claims about how the characters themselves narrate the duet.

> **Current finding: reciprocal contrast is visible; exact relational authorship remains open.**

## 11. `標`

The China/Hiro version supplies an additional duet control and helps distinguish a unit relation from the sonic world of `みちなるひろがる`. It belongs primarily to later China↔Hiro synthesis rather than the center of Hiro's solo model.

## 12. `ハッピーミルフィーユ`

This source is a necessary cute/social counterweight. Hiro can enter sweetness, Valentine framing, gift exchange, and peer comedy without the performance becoming a parody of her serious identity.

The commu is particularly useful because it gives the cute mode a social context. Cuteness is something done with and for others, not merely an image imposed on her.

## 13. `仮装狂騒曲`

Costume and theatrical premise make artificiality overt. Hiro's ease with strange frameworks suggests that role-play can be a route to expression rather than concealment.

The work is also an excellent comedy control: her deadpan does not disappear when the surrounding source becomes flamboyant. The contrast intensifies it.

## 14. `ミラクルナナウ(ﾟ∀ﾟ)！`

This is the strongest novelty/comic range test. It asks whether Hiro can participate in high-energy digital absurdity without being reduced to “the weird girl placed in the weird song.”

Her continuity lies in precision and underreaction, not in making the song more solemn.

## 15. `Howling over the World`

The aggressive common repertoire tests attack and external force. It is useful because Hiro's ordinary identity is often associated with softness and stillness.

The source supports range, but it should not be used to claim that aggression is her natural center. Common arrangement and choreography carry much of the demand.

## 16. `ENDLESS DANCE`

The nocturnal kinetic source tests whether Hiro can survive a performance grammar organized around sustained movement and fashionable force. Its analytical value lies in comparison with the more custom experimental works: energy can be conventionalized without becoming easy.

## 17. `がむしゃらに行こう！`

The effort rhetoric is almost provocatively suited to Hiro because she rejects ordinary equations between hard work, moral virtue, and appropriate aptitude. The performance can therefore function sincerely and ironically at once.

## 18. Reception source — `どんどん上手くなる光景`

This derivative montage argues that viewers can see Hiro becoming better. It is valuable because it identifies what part of the game's formal system fans find emotionally rewarding.

It cannot establish chronology, causality, or animator intent by itself. The correct workflow is:

1. identify the montage's claimed progression;
2. locate the underlying renderings;
3. separate body control from camera/costume/source-state differences;
4. retain only claims that survive the primary-source comparison.

The reception claim itself is still significant: **Hiro's appeal includes improvement, not only static incongruity.**

## 19. Formal-development model

The current musical sequence is best represented as:

> **`光景` — mismatch becomes premise**
>
> **`コントラスト` — mismatch becomes relation**
>
> **`サンフェーデッド` — mismatch carries attachment and future**

`メクルメ` and `コンテンポラリのダンス` prove the language is broader than one trilogy. `ガラクタロード` reveals the ordinary desire beneath the difficult answer. Common and seasonal works test whether Hiro can live outside custom experimental protection.

## 20. Final musical formulation

> **Hiro's music does not merely sound experimental because the franchise wanted a clever sonic gimmick for a genius character. Its formal nonconformity does narrative work. It creates a pop space where interruption, imbalance, conceptual density, and unstable fit can be productive rather than embarrassing. Yet the common-song controls matter just as much: they show that her career cannot depend forever on custom environments that translate every limitation into art. Mature Hiro must carry a distinctive low-force, effort-visible identity into shared professional grammar. Her catalog's achievement is that it allows both—strange form as home, and ordinary repertoire as accountable test.**
