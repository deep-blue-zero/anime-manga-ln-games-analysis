# GKM RELATIONSHIP SYSTEMS II - RIVALRY, FRIENDSHIP, CLASS, AND SUCCESSION

## 0. Purpose, authority, and principal thesis

This is the second canonical Phase-6 relationship-system synthesis for the Gakuen Idolmaster V2 corpus. Architecture v2.4 gives it a deliberately networked scope:

1. Hanami Saki / Tsukimura Temari / Fujita Kotone;
2. Kuramoto China / Hanami Ume / Shinosawa Hiro;
3. Juo Sena / Amaya Tsubame;
4. class networks;
5. senior/junior structures;
6. succession and inheritance;
7. Producer as relational node.

`GKM_RELATIONSHIP_SYSTEMS_I_HANAMI_REVERSI_AND_SYNGUP.md` remains the canonical home for Saki/Ume, Lilja/Sumika/REVERSI, and Temari/Misuzu/Rinha/SyngUp. Those systems appear here only when they establish a boundary condition for the wider network. The dedicated Kaya Rinha reconstruction also remains separate and mandatory.

### 0.1 Principal thesis - developmental authority is distributed

The completed source pass does not support a model in which each idol has one defining relationship that explains all meaningful growth. Even the most central bonds are surrounded by classmates, rivals, seniors, juniors, teachers, trainers, family, institutional officers, fans, and producers who possess different kinds of standing.

The strongest Phase-6 formulation is:

> **Gakumas develops people by distributing developmental authority across a network. A mature relationship system knows who may challenge, teach, stop, comfort, recruit, criticize, represent, inherit, or refuse in a particular domain without assuming that one person's importance grants jurisdiction over every other domain.**

This is why the three focal systems are useful together.

- Saki, Temari, and Kotone become close without becoming temperamentally similar. Their friendship works because authority moves by situation: Saki may lead conditioning or decisive opportunity capture, Temari may govern vocal questions, Kotone may govern customer reality, money, labor, social reading, or practical logistics. Friction remains audible because no member has to become the universal caretaker.
- China, Ume, and Hiro begin from a shared low-status joke, but the friendship matures through unequal competence rather than equality of ability. Ume can carry physical tasks, Hiro can carry academic or systems problems, China can mobilize hospitality, social resources, and recipient-aware coordination. Each is weak in one domain and unusually strong in another.
- Sena and Tsubame are both high-status and historically entangled. Their mature rivalry works only when No.1 and No.2 stop functioning as total identities. Tsubame can admire Sena and still police her institutional behavior; Sena can remain the star Tsubame loves while needing Tsubame's criticism, history, and independent ambition.

The same principle scales upward.

A class is useful because it contains more relationships than a unit. Seniority is useful when it grants obligation and access without claiming omniscience. Succession works when a junior inherits a function and re-authors it rather than becoming a copy. A Producer is most valuable as a high-centrality coordinator who can see across domains, but that very centrality becomes dangerous if it replaces the peer, family, rival, or institutional relationships that make refusal and correction possible.

### 0.2 Systems II extends, rather than replaces, the Phase-5 model

Phase 5 established an eight-layer relationship progression:

1. default care language;
2. behavioral intimacy;
3. care as technique;
4. interpretive literacy;
5. social infrastructure;
6. continuity without stasis;
7. translation and brokerage;
8. internalized relational priors.

Systems II adds a network-level question:

> **Who has legitimate relational authority over which problem?**

Closeness alone does not answer it. A person may be emotionally intimate but technically unqualified, institutionally senior but locally dependent, professionally responsible but ethically overreaching, or low-status yet uniquely competent in the problem at hand.

The downstream reconstruction rule is therefore:

> **Character prior + current state + domain competence + recipient model + relationship history + access permission + network position + institutional role + internalized others + current authority boundary.**

The last term matters. A plausible character simulation should not ask only who the character trusts. It should ask why that person has standing to intervene here, and what the character would do if that standing were exceeded.

## 1.2 Continuity rule - do not turn relationship recurrence into one universal calendar

Systems II is especially vulnerable to false concatenation because the same relationship grammar appears in Produce routes, Dear Idol tracks, Unit Story, numbered events, support stories, and conditional H.I.F. states.

The analysis therefore preserves several distinctions:

- U1 evidence proves that Saki, Temari, and Kotone can form a functioning unit in that ensemble track. It does not mean every later modular friendship scene is chronologically inside the same Re;IRIS biography.
- China, Ume, and Hiro have repeated friendship evidence across events/support/character tracks, but route-specific title results are not merged into one shared postgame history.
- Sena/Tsubame childhood history and repeated institutional rivalry are longitudinally strong, but summer/winter H.I.F. champion states remain route-sensitive.
- Producer relationships are especially branch-bounded. The Producer who develops Saki in one route, Temari in another, Kotone in another, or Asari in Event 029 cannot be treated as one simultaneous multi-idol manager unless the source explicitly creates that shared state.

The synthesis promotes repeated relational mechanisms, not unsupported chronology.

## 1.3 Working definitions

### Friendship

Friendship is not defined here by one use of the word `friend`. It is supported by repeatable access: voluntary time, ordinary presence, tolerated correction, private-space access, practical dependence, embarrassment survival, repair after friction, and expectation that the relation persists beyond immediate utility.

### Rivalry

Rivalry is not simple hostility or rank competition. In mature form it is a relationship permission structure: each person grants the other standing to demand growth, take victory seriously, and remain meaningful after either outcome.

### Class network

A class network is a medium-strength social structure. It is larger than a dyad or unit and less total than family. It creates repeated exposure, norms, comparison, collective stakes, weak-tie brokerage, and opportunities for skills or reputations to become socially legible.

### Succession

Succession is not identity replacement. It is transfer of responsibility, memory, access, repertoire, or institutional function across time. Healthy succession preserves usefulness while permitting redesign.

### Producer node

The Producer is treated as a role-bearing relation node: selector, diagnostician, coordinator, confidence scaffold, resource router, and sometimes crisis engineer. Producer centrality does not automatically authorize exclusivity or erase other relationships.

# 2. Comparative overview - three focal systems and three network technologies

| system | social technology | primary strength | principal danger | mature correction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Saki / Temari / Kotone | high-friction reciprocal specialization | strong correction and nonredundant competence | mistaking closeness for unit compatibility; one person's strength becoming universal standard | operational friendship with moving domain authority |
| China / Ume / Hiro | affiliative mutual aid among unequal beginners | low-status shame becomes shared future and reciprocal usefulness | romanticizing equality; hiding resource/body/skill asymmetries | secure friendship that makes asymmetry shareable |
| Sena / Tsubame | adversarial intimacy inside governance and fandom | high-trust challenge across public/private states | fixed No.1/No.2 identity; fan ownership; rivalry used to avoid vulnerability | renewable rivalry between independently authorized actors |
| class networks | repeated medium-strength contact | norm creation, brokerage, collective identity, unexpected learning | treating classmates as background or assuming class unity | plural social ecology with bounded collective stakes |
| senior/junior structure | vertical access plus obligation | knowledge transfer, safety, institutional memory | seniority mistaken for universal epistemic authority | porous rank with reciprocal teaching |
| succession | temporal transfer | continuity beyond one person | replacement, imitation, burdening juniors with unfinished identity | re-authored function and answerable inheritance |
| Producer node | high-centrality coordination | individualized development across domains | enclosure, outcome paternalism, monopolized interpretation | orchestration without social replacement |

These systems do not converge toward low-conflict harmony. They converge toward **distributed legitimacy**.

A mature ensemble repeatedly answers questions such as:

- Who is best positioned to notice this problem?
- Who knows how to solve it?
- Who is allowed to say it directly?
- Who can carry the message if direct access is blocked?
- Who can stop a person whose own ambition has made self-limitation unreliable?
- Who can inherit the practice later?
- Which person should *not* decide, even if their judgment is accurate?

That is the network logic Systems II formalizes.

# 3. Saki, Temari, and Kotone - friendship through reciprocal specialization

## 3.1 The trio begins as a competitive federation, not an affinity group

The Saki/Temari/Kotone system is easy to misread because its surface remains abrasive long after the relationship becomes strong.

U1 gives the clearest early laboratory. Saki initially treats a unit as a problem of dead weight and useful competition. Kotone defeats her in dance. Temari forces Saki to revise her model of idol strength. Temari initially rejects the shared Producer and the unit because dependence and center hierarchy threaten old wounds. Kotone's practical intelligence repeatedly keeps conflict from becoming pure ego contest.

The trio becomes possible because none of them is reducible to one scalar ranking.

Saki may be the all-round optimizer and opportunity-capturer. Temari may be the strongest vocal specialist. Kotone may appear weaker under narrow prestige metrics yet become indispensable in audience reading, labor realism, dance/fanservice, practical leadership, money, customer behavior, and social interpretation.

The group therefore teaches a foundational Systems-II rule:

> **Horizontal relation does not require equal ability. It requires that ability be plural enough that no one ranking can settle every question.**

## 3.2 Friendship labels lag behind behavioral access

Phase 4 and Phase 5 repeatedly show Saki naming the friendship more easily than Temari or Kotone.

Saki calls Temari and Kotone her friends and can exaggerate that language for comic effect. Temari often denies or resists the label while continuing to eat, travel, work, practice, argue, sleep near, and accept help from them. Kotone can mock overperformed closeness and still be visibly affected by Saki's simple affection.

The correct reading is not that Saki is deluded and the others are not friends.

It is that the label negotiates expectation.

For Temari, `friend` can threaten to imply softness, obligation, or a level of declared intimacy she is uncomfortable naming. For Kotone, closeness can trigger debt/accounting instincts or embarrassment. Saki is comparatively willing to convert operational access into an explicit social category.

Behavior is therefore the stronger evidence surface.

## 3.3 Domain authority moves among the three

The trio becomes mature when leadership is situational.

### Saki

Saki contributes:

- conditioning and recovery knowledge;
- fast situational diagnosis;
- aggressive opportunity capture;
- meal/health attention;
- competitive framing;
- willingness to take public responsibility.

Her danger is universalization. Support stories repeatedly show that Ume-calibrated bodily assumptions cannot simply be exported to China, Kotone, or Temari.

### Temari

Temari contributes:

- vocal authority;
- high professional standards;
- ruthless technical sincerity;
- ability to identify when a performance claim is musically unserious;
- competitive pressure that can prevent care from becoming patronizing.

Her danger is score-conditioned worth and the tendency to treat intensity as proof of seriousness.

### Kotone

Kotone contributes:

- social reading;
- commercial/customer literacy;
- practical logistics;
- money and labor realism;
- audience hospitality;
- dance/fanservice expertise;
- ability to recognize when a technically accurate plan is socially or ethically unusable.

Her danger is converting every received benefit into debt and treating time as a resource that must always produce income or measurable return.

Together they become a **reciprocal specialization network**.

## 3.4 Conflict is part of the access grammar

The trio's warmth often arrives through forms that would sound hostile without established permission.

They interrupt speeches, ambush one another in play, complain about food, call out reckless decisions, mock sleep behavior, argue about calories, challenge professional judgment, and continue interacting afterward without requiring ceremonial repair.

This does not mean all harshness is harmless. The relationship is strong precisely because the corpus shows different outcomes when boundaries are actually crossed elsewhere.

The trio instead demonstrates **friction tolerance**:

> the members know enough about one another to distinguish ordinary abrasive address from withdrawal of the relationship.

For reconstruction, smoothing the dialogue into constant supportiveness would make the friendship less accurate, not more.

## 3.5 Event 019 is the clearest operational proof

The emergency Nagoya live converts friendship into professional trust.

Saki accepts an emergency opportunity and names Temari and Kotone as the friends she will bring. Temari criticizes the recklessness. Kotone mocks the situation. Both nevertheless reverse immediately into participation.

During the trip:

- roles distribute by competence;
- Saki performs producer-like load balancing without being their formal Producer;
- Temari and Kotone tolerate unusual sleeping proximity and later weaponize the resulting embarrassment as jokes;
- Saki can rest because she trusts the others to remain functional;
- disagreement continues without threatening the project.

The event's strongest conclusion remains:

> **They do not become temperamentally compatible. They become legible enough to coordinate.**

## 3.6 Socioeconomic asymmetry is part of the friendship model

Kotone prevents the trio from becoming an abstract meritocratic friendship.

Saki's athletic upbringing, family resources, and self-management infrastructure give her far more margin for failure than Kotone's work-heavy life. Temari's specialized training prestige does not map cleanly onto ordinary practical competence. Kotone may admire both while living under constraints they cannot simply optimize away.

Events and support stories repeatedly correct psychologizing interpretations of Kotone's exhaustion. Work hours, younger-sibling responsibility, sleep, school, retests, and institutional hardship support all matter.

A credible Saki/Temari/Kotone model therefore asks:

> **Can the more resourced friends adapt their care to the cost structure of the less resourced friend rather than treating different capacity as lower commitment?**

The later evidence increasingly says yes, but not automatically.

## 3.7 Friendship does not imply unit compatibility

One of the most useful Phase-5 corrections is explicit: all three can be real friends and still reject the idea that they should form a unit.

This matters because fan analysis often treats closeness as latent unit destiny.

The trio says otherwise.

They can:

- trust one another;
- exchange expertise;
- travel together;
- represent the school together;
- enjoy each other's company;
- act as rivals;
- and remain professionally incompatible as a permanent unit.

> **Friendship is not a weaker form of unit formation. It is a different relational institution.**

## 3.8 Peer production is useful precisely because it lacks full Producer authority

Saki sometimes performs producer-like operations: capability assessment, load balancing, meal planning, set-list thinking, motivational challenge, contingency support.

Kotone often performs manager-like operations through logistics and social reading. Temari teaches technique with unusual precision.

Yet none possesses the formal Producer Course jurisdiction over the others.

That limit is analytically valuable. Peer intervention remains easier to refuse, mock, reverse, or counterchallenge.

The trio therefore demonstrates that developmental work need not be monopolized by the Producer role.

## 3.9 Saki/Temari/Kotone claim transitions

- **STRENGTHEN:** the trio is an explicit friendship system, not merely U1 coworkers or recurring event partners.
- **STRENGTHEN:** friction and care are simultaneous; banter does not imply weak attachment.
- **PROMOTE:** reciprocal specialization is the central operational mechanism.
- **REJECT:** strongest technical idol = natural leader in every domain.
- **REJECT:** friendship implies permanent unit compatibility.
- **STRENGTHEN:** socioeconomic difference changes what equal effort and equal access mean.
- **OPEN:** how stable the friendship remains when major title outcomes place one member far above the others in a route-specific state.

## 3.10 Reconstruction rules

When simulating the trio in a new situation:

1. assign the problem to a domain before assigning leadership;
2. preserve Temari's label resistance even when behavior is friendly;
3. preserve Kotone's money/time/material realism;
4. preserve Saki's instinct to act quickly and optimize;
5. allow tactical alliances to shift inside one conversation;
6. let criticism occur without mandatory rupture;
7. do not make all three want the same professional structure;
8. check whether class/resources make the same proposed solution cost different amounts to each person.

# 4. China, Ume, and Hiro - secure friendship through asymmetric competence

## 4.1 The Worst Three convert rank shame into a shared beginning

The trio originates in one of the cleanest social transformations in the event corpus.

China, Ume, and Hiro discover that their entrance positions are poor. Rather than allowing the ranking to isolate them, Ume proposes the self-authored `Worst Three` identity and a shared future in which their weak start becomes part of the story they will someday tell as idols.

The important point is not positive thinking.

The same low-rank fact produces three different affects:

- China experiences weakness as a call to legitimate effort and proper idolhood;
- Ume converts it into propulsion and group challenge;
- Hiro finds the low starting point intrinsically attractive because difficulty makes the project interesting.

The group works because it does not require one emotional interpretation of failure.

## 4.2 Equal rank does not mean equal weakness

The Worst Three label could easily flatten the trio into three incompetent beginners. The full corpus rejects that.

### Ume

Ume is physically extraordinary. She may be institutionally or technically underformed, but her body, rebound capacity, sensory awareness, and direct social energy are unusual strengths.

### Hiro

Hiro is physically near the floor while intellectually exceptional. Academics, systems reasoning, software/reward-loop evaluation, plant knowledge, and abstract problem solving can place her far above peers in other domains.

### China

China begins with genuine performance weakness and sheltered ordinary-life experience while possessing cultural knowledge, wealth, hospitality infrastructure, social magnetism, and an unusual capacity to organize dependence into legitimate cooperation.

Their friendship is therefore an anti-scalar system.

> **The shared category is not equal deficiency. It is willingness to let visible weakness coexist with locally superior competence.**

## 4.3 Friendship gives Ume a world not organized by Saki

For Ume, this trio is a major individuation mechanism.

Her deepest early relational grammar is pursuit of Saki. With China and Hiro, Ume does not need to be younger sister, chaser, or the person being trained by an older-sister system.

She can be:

- friend;
- host and guest;
- morale engine;
- technical teacher;
- novice in someone else's domain;
- hiking companion;
- study partner;
- vacation companion;
- and someone whose ordinary enthusiasm is wanted for itself.

This horizontal social field makes Ume larger without making Saki smaller.

## 4.4 Friendship gives Hiro shared stakes

Early Hiro chooses difficulty largely for private stimulation.

China and Ume change the moral structure of difficulty.

If a friend succeeds, Hiro can feel pride or jealousy. If Hiro wins, someone she values loses. If China or Ume is worried, Hiro can choose a task because another person's state matters, not only because the task is interesting.

Rivalry therefore creates something early Hiro lacks:

> **shared stakes.**

Her development toward professional responsibility becomes more intelligible once weakness is no longer a private experiment.

## 4.5 China learns that hospitality must model the recipient

China's inherited resources are real and unequal.

The mature friendship does not pretend otherwise.

At the family property, China can offer a training room, pool, equipment, meals, and travel conditions that Ume and Hiro could not reproduce symmetrically. Later, however, her hospitality becomes more recipient-aware: she persuades Ume by mentioning a new training machine, understands what makes Hiro enjoy a vacation, and stops treating generosity as one generic good.

The correct model is not "wealth does not matter because they are friends."

It is:

> **resource asymmetry becomes less enclosing when the resource-holder learns to use abundance to enlarge shared agency rather than purchase deference.**

China's later willingness to use Kuramoto resources to strengthen Hiro as a rival follows the same principle.

## 4.6 Competence exchange makes dependence nonshaming

The trio repeatedly reverses who is teaching whom.

- Ume carries physical confidence and practical body knowledge.
- Hiro tutors academics but discovers that understanding a subject is not the same as understanding why another person does not understand it.
- China supplies cultural/social knowledge and material access while also needing help with ordinary tasks or physical training.
- Saki can appear indirectly as Ume's nutrition infrastructure, showing how one relationship network can feed another without joining it.

No exchange needs to balance transaction by transaction.

This is one of the clearest examples of the Phase-5 reciprocity rule:

> help is sustainable when roles are expected to reverse somewhere, not necessarily in the same currency or the same scene.

## 4.7 Friendship firsts are analytically important

The support pass repeatedly gives the trio experiences that are ordinary for other students and therefore especially revealing here:

- first extended friend vacation;
- private room visits;
- China's first home friend visit;
- hiking;
- studying together;
- homemade lunches;
- ordinary evening time;
- repeated invitations to visit family homes;
- shared leisure that is neither training nor formal event work.

For China and Hiro in particular, the novelty is explicit.

These scenes should not be dismissed as slice-of-life filler. They are evidence that the characters have acquired a social category they did not previously possess.

## 4.8 The friendship becomes internally portable

`story_0088` provides the deepest Systems-II evidence.

Hiro describes an internal model of China that asks what China would do and pushes Hiro toward proper idol behavior. China answers that she has an internal Hiro whose imagined enjoyment of difficult training helps her reinterpret pain. They then joke about wanting an internal Ume as well.

This is more than affection.

The friend has become part of action selection.

> **The trio no longer needs physical co-presence for the relationship to influence behavior.**

That gives the friendship unusual reconstruction value. In a novel situation, a believable Hiro can reason with a China-like objection; a believable China can stabilize effort through a Hiro-like reframing; Ume's directness can plausibly become an internal action prompt for both.

## 4.9 Future distance does not revoke friendship

The later support pass explicitly moves Hiro beyond repeated uncertainty about whether the trio qualifies as friends.

She reaches a simpler mature rule: people she likes being with are friends, and future careers or physical distance would not erase the affection.

This is important because Gakumas often links intimacy to shared projects. The trio shows that a bond can survive even when the shared project no longer guarantees daily contact.

Friendship becomes **durable without constant proof**.

## 4.10 The trio also becomes institutional material

The friendship is not isolated from Hatsuboshi governance.

Ume and China are student-council members. Hiro is considered for the next council. China becomes the named next president. Their competencies are increasingly legible as parts of a successor cohort rather than only private quirks.

But institutionalization should not be allowed to redefine the friendship as a future work team.

The private relation predates and exceeds office assignment.

## 4.11 China/Ume/Hiro claim transitions

- **PROMOTE:** the Worst Three is a self-authored social identity, not fandom shorthand.
- **STRENGTHEN:** the trio is a secure friendship cluster with private-life access.
- **STRENGTHEN:** friendship and rivalry coexist without forcing hierarchy.
- **PROMOTE:** asymmetric competence exchange is the central mechanism of reciprocity.
- **STRENGTHEN:** China's wealth matters causally and does not make the friendship reducible to patronage.
- **PROMOTE:** internalized friend-models are direct evidence of relational incorporation.
- **STRENGTHEN:** future distance does not automatically terminate the bond.
- **REJECT:** shared low status means similar abilities or similar attitudes toward difficulty.

## 4.12 Reconstruction rules

When simulating the trio:

1. preserve three distinct responses to difficulty;
2. let the locally strongest person teach without making her globally superior;
3. model China's hospitality as increasingly recipient-specific;
4. model Ume's enthusiasm as socially perceptive more often than "oblivious athlete" shorthand suggests;
5. model Hiro as capable of initiating social contact, hosting, and caring, not only being studied;
6. let class/resource asymmetry remain visible without turning every scene into class conflict;
7. use internalized-other reasoning when the friends are absent;
8. do not require explicit friendship-label reassurance once the mature state is established.

# 5. Sena and Tsubame - adversarial intimacy, institutional conscience, and renewable rivalry

## 5.1 The relationship predates Sena's current supremacy

Sena/Tsubame cannot be modeled as "champion and challenger" who meet only after Sena becomes the academy summit.

Their history includes childhood proximity, ordinary family/home access, fandom, school governance, competition, and a period in which Tsubame could stand above Sena in relevant ability. This matters because later hierarchy is historically contingent rather than natural.

Tsubame's despair is powerful precisely because she remembers another relation to Sena.

## 5.2 Tsubame is simultaneously fan, rival, witness, and officeholder

Tsubame's relation to Sena contains categories that would be mutually exclusive in a simpler rivalry story.

She is:

- a fan of Sena's perfected idol image;
- a childhood friend with private knowledge;
- vice president beside Sena's presidency;
- an institutional critic;
- a rival who wants to defeat her;
- a person who fears losing her;
- and someone willing to build a professional future around continued contest.

None of these cancels the others.

The analytical mistake would be to choose the "real" relation and call the others masks.

## 5.3 Fandom gives Tsubame a legitimate stake but not ownership

Sena's public perfection mattered to Tsubame. When Sena changes, fails, or exposes private weakness, Tsubame is not irrational for feeling that something valuable has been altered.

Sena's core reading explicitly treats Tsubame as recipient-side pressure on self-reinvention: the idol form did real work in another person's life.

But fan investment does not confer ownership of the person.

Systems II therefore preserves both propositions:

> **An idol owes serious attention to the effects of a public form. A fan does not gain veto authority over the idol's future self.**

Tsubame matures when admiration can survive a Sena who is no longer safely perfect.

## 5.4 Rivalry fails when the rival becomes unreachable

The worst version of the Sena/Tsubame relation is static No.1/No.2.

If Sena is complete and Tsubame is merely the permanent second, then competition no longer generates development. Tsubame can even become comfortable inside the No.2 identity because it protects her from the terror of a genuinely open result.

Later evidence explicitly destabilizes this.

Tsubame recognizes the problem, rejects comfort in No.2, and wants a Sena who keeps growing because defeating a stagnant Sena would be meaningless.

Sena, meanwhile, can privately envy Tsubame's "wings."

Comparison runs both directions.

> **A viable rival must be reachable enough to challenge and different enough to remain informative.**

## 5.5 Private knowledge does not expose public perfection as fake

Support and event material gives Tsubame unusually intimate access to Sena's ordinary states: childhood memory, leisure, food awkwardness, private anxiety, old home access, and moments in which the public crown is not operating.

This does not make the stage persona fraudulent.

It gives Tsubame evidence of how much work is required to create it.

This is a broader corpus principle: role permeability is not mask exposure.

## 5.6 Tsubame functions as institutional conscience

Tsubame is one of the strongest controls on Sena's tendency to convert developmental vision into jurisdiction.

When Sena proposes the student-council pseudo-agency, Tsubame immediately identifies the contradiction: will Sena spend herself producing juniors instead of remaining a worthy rival?

When succession or mentorship becomes a substitute for Sena's own development, Tsubame objects.

When private information about Kotone arose from a disciplinary consultation, Tsubame does not gossip it upward to Sena merely because Sena is president.

This makes Tsubame more than harsh enforcer.

She represents a principle of **role-bounded authority**.

## 5.7 Their governance styles are complementary and conflictual

Sena's student-council style is visionary, developmental, recruitment-heavy, and willing to redesign systems around latent potential.

Tsubame's style is sharper:

- identify the standard;
- identify the failure;
- demand correction;
- protect institutional function;
- and, when necessary, stop the person whose effort has become unsafe.

The pair works partly because neither style is sufficient alone.

Tsubame can detect Sena's overreach. Sena can create opportunities Tsubame's stricter logic might not generate. Rinami and other officers are still needed because even the pair together miss affective information.

This is important: Sena/Tsubame are not a complete governing dyad.

## 5.8 Tsubame's strictness is condition-sensitive care

Support evidence significantly revises any "always push harder" model.

With Hiro, Tsubame begins severe running supervision, observes actual physical danger, and immediately shifts to rest, hydration, and safety. With Kotone, she checks sleep/health and ends training early based on observed fatigue even when Kotone says she is fine.

Her care can be directive and unwelcome while still being recipient-sensitive.

The relevant distinction is:

> **discipline versus preventable collapse.**

Tsubame believes a senior may sometimes have professional standing to stop a junior. Systems II must preserve both the effectiveness and the possibility of overreach.

## 5.9 The winter wager turns rivalry into future architecture

Tsubame's late route provides one of the clearest mature rivalry structures in the corpus.

If she wins the winter H.I.F., Sena should continue as an idol after graduation and keep fighting her as a same-agency rival. Sena accepts.

The wager matters because victory does not terminate the relationship.

It creates the next contest.

That is **renewable rivalry** at a vocational scale.

## 5.10 Succession must not turn Tsubame into "the next Sena"

Tsubame can inherit institutional responsibilities, songs, training obligations, public standards, and winner's responsibility without becoming Sena's replacement personality.

Her value lies partly in being different:

- more armored;
- more declarative;
- more correction-oriented;
- differently vulnerable;
- and historically shaped by looking upward rather than occupying the summit continuously.

The institution loses information if it treats succession as cloning.

## 5.11 Sena/Tsubame claim transitions

- **PROMOTE:** major Phase-6 dyad combining childhood friendship, fandom, rivalry, governance, and private witnessing.
- **STRENGTHEN:** Tsubame's fandom and rivalry are simultaneous, not sequential replacements.
- **STRENGTHEN:** Tsubame's harshness is condition-sensitive rather than intensity-maximizing.
- **STRENGTHEN:** Sena needs Tsubame's criticism even when Sena holds higher public status.
- **REJECT:** No.1/No.2 is the final ontology of the relation.
- **REJECT:** private imperfection proves Sena's idol form fraudulent.
- **PROMOTE:** renewable rivalry is a chosen future architecture.
- **OPEN:** how much of the bond later monographs should describe using romance-coded language beyond the textually secure language of love, jealousy, and lifelong contest.

## 5.12 Reconstruction rules

When simulating Sena and Tsubame:

1. preserve Tsubame's right to challenge Sena directly;
2. preserve Sena's expectation that Tsubame can answer back;
3. keep fandom alive inside rivalry;
4. distinguish private familiarity from public-office behavior;
5. let Tsubame enforce safety and standards without making her infallible;
6. let Sena create opportunities without making her talent eye omniscient;
7. remember that either can need the other while still remaining independently motivated;
8. do not resolve an argument merely by having the higher-ranked idol win the interpretive dispute.

# 6. Class networks - medium-strength ties as developmental infrastructure

## 6.1 The class is not background scenery

Phase 2 and Phase 4 establish class as a real production and social cell.

Classes support:

- collective activities;
- inter-class competition;
- shared reputational stakes;
- peer learning;
- ordinary repeated contact;
- social norm formation;
- and access to people who would not necessarily choose one another as intimate friends.

This is analytically distinct from units, families, or Producer relationships.

## 6.2 Class 1-1 behaves like a competitive federation

Class 1-1 repeatedly turns strength into a social expectation.

Saki, Temari, Kotone, Lilja, Sumika, and others coexist in a culture where comparison, visible competence, challenge, teasing, and opportunity capture are normal. The class can be harsh, but later events show it becoming capable of practical care and collective adaptation.

Its best social technology is not harmony.

It is **competitive legibility**: people know what others are good at, know who can challenge whom, and increasingly know when competition should give way to support.

## 6.3 Class 1-2 develops affiliative resilience

Class 1-2's strongest early symbol is the Worst Three.

Rather than organizing low status as shame, the class develops a more affiliative culture in which unusual weaknesses can become shared identity. The sports-festival event makes this class-wide: Class 1-2 loses but metabolizes the loss collectively, and China can become a moral center despite weak physical rank.

This does not make Class 1-2 less ambitious.

It means ambition is more often routed through **mutual aid and shared story**.

## 6.4 Inter-class rivalry is serious and bounded

The sports festival provides the clearest control.

Class 1-1 and Class 1-2 want to win. Weakness has collective consequences. Yet Saki can respect China as the "boss" of Class 1-2, Temari can recognize Misuzu as a threat, and competition does not require dehumanization.

Class rivalry therefore functions as a meso-level version of individual rivalry:

> **collective identity becomes developmental when defeat remains socially survivable.**

## 6.5 Joint lessons prevent social siloing

Support stories directly establish ordinary joint instruction between Class 1-1 and Class 1-2.

This matters because event-only contact could be dismissed as special crossover logic. Joint lessons mean the school routinely exposes different class cultures and competence profiles to one another.

The institution therefore creates horizontal permeability even while preserving class identity.

## 6.6 Medium-strength ties provide brokerage

Not every useful relation needs deep intimacy.

Classmates can:

- transmit information;
- identify a problem;
- recommend another helper;
- normalize unfamiliar behavior;
- create work opportunities;
- notice fatigue;
- introduce a hobby;
- share institutional knowledge;
- or provide a comparison set that breaks an overly narrow self-model.

These medium-strength ties are especially important for character reconstruction because they reduce the temptation to route every problem through a signature best friend or Producer.

## 6.7 Class is not unit, family, or permanent identity

Class membership is a powerful social container and should not be totalized.

Students form cross-class friendships, dorm relations, council relations, family ties, Producer ties, external work contacts, and rivalries that exceed class borders.

Class is best modeled as **repeated opportunity structure**, not final identity.

## 6.8 Ordinary-course contact expands the social world further

Mashiro Yu and the ordinary-course game/broadcast contacts show that idol-course students remain embedded in a school larger than idol production.

Ordinary-course peers can:

- observe idol-course culture from outside;
- mediate publicity;
- collaborate technically;
- provide hobby or club contacts;
- and preserve school memory without sharing idol-course norms.

This keeps Hatsuboshi from becoming a socially closed idol terrarium.

# 7. Senior/junior structures - real hierarchy, porous expertise

## 7.1 Seniority is real

The corpus does not support pretending that year/status hierarchy is irrelevant.

Seniors possess:

- institutional memory;
- procedural access;
- social legitimacy;
- safety responsibility;
- familiarity with events and public expectations;
- and often the right to correct or instruct juniors.

Tsubame can assign standards. Rinami can orient newcomers. Mao can govern dorm work. Sena can recruit and create opportunities.

The hierarchy has practical consequences.

## 7.2 Seniority does not create universal expertise

Phase 5 repeatedly makes rank porous.

A junior can know more about:

- a body or movement problem;
- a hobby;
- technology;
- a social audience;
- a family need;
- a specific training method;
- or a cultural practice.

Examples include Ume teaching or supplying embodied evidence upward, juniors correcting Sena's assumptions, and domain experts being taken seriously despite lower formal status.

The key rule is:

> **Rank assigns responsibility more reliably than it assigns truth.**

## 7.3 Senior care styles are not interchangeable

Later monographs should preserve distinct senior grammars rather than collapsing them into generic mentorship.

- Rinami tends to offer, hold, reopen choice, and temporarily reverse caretaker/cared-for roles.
- Tsubame tends to assess, decide, sharpen, and justify intervention through professional obligation.
- Mao tends to protect through practical service, dorm monitoring, craft, and role-sensitive encouragement.
- Sena tends to create developmental environments, opportunities, and rival structures, with a recurring risk of deciding before consent catches up.

The same junior can respond differently to each style.

## 7.4 Good seniority includes being teachable

The strongest senior/junior scenes are reciprocal.

Tsubame studies Ume's movement because Ume exceeds her in one physical domain. Sena lets Ume teach her an ordinary summer ritual. Mao and Sumika can solve outreach problems by provoking authentic rather than formally correct behavior. Senior status survives because the senior can learn without experiencing learning as status collapse.

## 7.5 Stopping can be legitimate care

Tsubame stopping Hiro or Kotone is important because Gakumas often celebrates determination.

The corpus nevertheless repeatedly distinguishes admirable effort from preventable collapse.

A senior may have legitimate standing to stop someone when:

- the senior has relevant responsibility;
- the recipient's condition is observable;
- the intervention is tied to safety or professional sustainability;
- and the limit is not simply the senior's preferred pace.

This is not blanket authorization for paternalism. It is a bounded care function.

## 7.6 Mentorship becomes capture when succession or care erases first-person desire

Sena's early successor model, Misuzu's old Temari body jurisdiction, and several Producer relations all demonstrate the same structural danger.

A more experienced person may be correct about talent and still be wrong about ownership.

The healthy senior/junior relation therefore asks:

> **Can the junior use what the senior knows without becoming the answer to the senior's unfinished life?**

# 8. Succession and inheritance - function transfer without identity replacement

## 8.1 Replacement is the recurring failed model

Succession repeatedly begins as a tempting simplification.

A senior imagines:

- I will leave;
- you will inherit;
- continuity requires you to carry what I carried;
- therefore your development can be understood through resemblance to me.

The corpus repeatedly rejects this as incomplete.

Kotone refuses Sena's successor category and demands rivalry. Ume corrects Sena's one-way legacy logic. Tsubame refuses to let Sena replace her own competitive life with mentoring. China inherits the presidency while explicitly lacking Sena's individual governing style.

## 8.2 Healthy succession transfers function

The better model is:

> **identify the useful function, transfer the knowledge or responsibility, and allow the recipient to redesign the form.**

This is why China can become student-council president without becoming Sena.

China's own leadership theory is dependence-explicit: gather capable people, recognize limits, make roles visible, and remain answerable for the whole. That is a legitimate succession precisely because it differs from Sena's concentrated supremacy.

## 8.3 The student-council handoff is distributed

Phase 4 and Phase 5 make the handoff procedural rather than symbolic.

- China is explicitly named the next president.
- Ume and Misuzu are established junior council members.
- Hiro is a next-council candidate.
- Sena, Tsubame, and Rinami divide handoff work.
- Tsubame explicitly asks Misuzu to support China and Ume after the seniors leave.
- juniors propose new scheduling infrastructure rather than only reproducing old workflow.

The council survives because responsibility becomes **distributed and generative**.

## 8.4 Cultural inheritance works the same way

`Campus mode!!` is one of the clearest examples.

The song is:

- Hatsuboshi identity;
- a scarce performance opportunity;
- a generational comparison surface;
- taught laterally and downward;
- and explicitly understood by Mao/Rinami as a baton received from seniors and passed forward.

The successor is not asked to imitate one canonical performance personality.

The successor is asked to answer the inherited material in a new body.

## 8.5 Mundane rituals are also institutional memory

Institutional inheritance is not only titles and songs.

It includes:

- a festival viewing place passed from an older council member to Rinami to China;
- dorm cooking/fire traditions;
- Mao becoming dorm leader because earlier seniors sustained her;
- ordinary event-management knowledge;
- fan-response habits;
- safety procedures;
- and the expectation that someone who was once helped will recognize a future person who needs the same function.

This is one of the strongest Phase-5 conclusions:

> **Institutional memory is often a person remembering how someone treated them, then reproducing the useful function for a later recipient.**

## 8.6 Rivalry can be a better succession relation than inheritance

Sena's mature route repeatedly converts succession into rivalry.

Kotone, Saki, Tsubame, China, Ume, and others matter most when they can answer back rather than merely preserve Sena's legacy.

A rival relation keeps the predecessor inside the risk structure.

The senior does not get to retire into interpretive sovereignty while the junior bears the future.

This is why Sena's project matures toward circulatory legacy rather than self-erasure.

## 8.7 Graduation does not require relational disappearance

Support material allows seniors to imagine returning as alumni, maintaining professional rivalry, continuing community participation, and remaining available without occupying the same office.

This is a crucial temporal correction.

Succession does not require the predecessor to die socially so the successor can be real.

## 8.8 Succession claim transitions

- **REJECT:** succession = identity replacement.
- **PROMOTE:** succession = transfer of useful function plus recipient re-authorship.
- **STRENGTHEN:** China presidency is legitimate because it does not depend on Sena imitation.
- **STRENGTHEN:** cultural repertoire and mundane care rituals are institutional inheritance.
- **PROMOTE:** rivalry can be a superior successor relation when it keeps the predecessor answerable.
- **STRENGTHEN:** graduation changes access/office but need not terminate relationship.

# 9. Producer as relational node - coordination, coauthorship, and enclosure risk

## 9.1 The Producer occupies structured liminality

The institutional corpus establishes that GakumasP is simultaneously:

- a first-year Producer Course student;
- a Hatsuboshi student;
- a credentialed producer with powers/resources unavailable to ordinary Idol Course students;
- and an object of continuing evaluation and education.

This liminality is central to the relationship design. The Producer is physically and socially near enough to participate in everyday development, but institutionally different enough to control schedules, opportunities, resources, training decisions, and professional interpretation.

The role is therefore neither ordinary classmate nor ordinary adult manager.

## 9.2 There is no single universal Producer relationship

The corpus strongly resists flattening all character routes into one model.

Across route-bounded states, Producer can function as:

- Saki's external diagnostician and witness to collapse;
- Temari's chosen partner and containment-transfer point;
- Kotone's material rescuer, manager, debt target, and intimate witness;
- China's teacher/strategist and confidence scaffold;
- Hiro's harsh-evaluation partner and coauthor of difficult situations;
- Ume's accelerator and world-expander;
- Tsubame's provocative developmental partner;
- Sena's senior/producer counterpart in a route where producing becomes part of Sena's second vocation.

These functions rhyme, but they are not one simultaneous biography.

Any Phase-7 reconstruction must preserve route-specific authority and continuity.

## 9.3 Producer value begins where self-management stops being enough

The Producer is most useful when a character's existing strengths are insufficient to diagnose their own next problem.

Saki already works hard; telling her to work harder is low-value. The Producer sees where self-optimization has plateaued.

Temari already knows technical singing; the Producer helps convert effort, image, body, and ambition into a broader developmental plan.

Kotone already possesses extreme practical endurance; the Producer's early success is partly creating an ordinary enough life that talent can become visible at all.

China already possesses support; the Producer helps her interpret support as legitimate without making it an excuse for nonresponsibility.

Hiro already understands difficulty; the Producer helps convert private attraction to difficulty into public idol craft and shared obligation.

Tsubame already possesses standards; the Producer destabilizes standards that have become identity defense.

This yields a high-confidence functional definition:

> **The Producer's distinctive value is cross-domain perspective: seeing a developmental bottleneck that the idol's own competence, history, or defensive theory cannot currently see.**

## 9.4 Material coordination is part of intimacy because bodies and money are real

Producer work in Gakumas is not only emotional support or artistic advice.

It includes:

- schedules;
- rest;
- nutrition;
- injury and fatigue detection;
- work substitution;
- budget/debt navigation;
- event access;
- song/image coordination;
- public opportunity;
- external professionals;
- and sometimes family or institutional liaison.

This is why Producer relationships can become unusually intimate quickly. The role has access to high-consequence parts of a young performer's life.

That access is useful and ethically dangerous for the same reason.

## 9.5 The central risk is not simple control; it is monopoly of interpretation

The strongest producer relationships often contain accurate insight.

The danger is that accuracy can become self-authorizing.

Examples differ by route:

- Saki may accept the Producer as singular witness to the self beneath constructed invincibility.
- Temari may transfer dependency from older caretakers into a Producer who validates dangerous intensity.
- Kotone may experience material rescue as debt so large that career becomes repayment.
- China may find idol activity without the Producer nearly unimaginable.
- Hiro may reward outcome-paternalism because difficult pressure itself is attractive.
- Tsubame may depend on Producer belief to imagine a future while still contesting his methods.

The common structural risk is:

> **one person becomes the privileged interpreter of talent, need, permission, and future.**

The relationship may feel reciprocal while still concentrating institutional power.

## 9.6 A healthy Producer node does not replace the social graph

Phase 4 and Phase 5 significantly correct any Producer-centric model.

Characters develop through:

- rivals who can say what the Producer cannot;
- friends who provide ordinary life;
- seniors who carry institutional knowledge;
- juniors who expose obsolete assumptions;
- family who provides history and material infrastructure;
- trainers who possess technical authority;
- classmates who make strengths socially legible;
- brokers who can carry care across blocked edges;
- and fans who create obligations and possibilities the Producer does not own.

The healthiest structural formulation is therefore:

> **Producer as orchestrator, not social replacement.**

A producer should enlarge the number of relationships through which the idol can act, learn, refuse, and recover.

## 9.7 Producer Course itself is a network

Support Series 3 explicitly shows Asari encouraging Producer Course peer/professional networking because other producers' experience can someday benefit assigned idols.

The Producer attends a senior networking event, learns from practitioners, and participates in ordinary peer talk as well as professional exchange.

This matters because it prevents the Producer role from being imagined as lone-genius management.

The institution expects producers to depend on other producers.

That is the same distributed-authority principle the idol side increasingly learns.

## 9.8 The "reliable magician" is a professional performance, not omniscience

Asari describes modern producers as generalists and specialists who should appear to idols as reliable problem-solving magicians, even when visible certainty includes performed professional composure.

This is not license to falsify evidence.

It means the Producer also performs a role.

Systems II therefore applies the same role-permeability rule used for idols:

> professional confidence can be constructed and ethically useful without making every confident judgment true.

The Producer needs peers, teachers, trainers, and idols partly because the reassuring role can otherwise hide uncertainty from the person performing it.

## 9.9 Producer-like behavior exists outside Producer Course

Sena's pseudo-agency is the clearest institutional example. Saki sometimes performs producer-like coordination among peers. Seniors teach, recruit, protect, and create development environments.

But the institution explicitly distinguishes producer-like function from Producer Course credential and authority.

This distinction is important for ethics.

A friend may diagnose accurately without having scheduling power. A senior may mentor without contract/resource priority. Sena may scout without receiving the full Producer Course jurisdiction.

Authority is therefore layered rather than binary.

## 9.10 Event 029 is an adversarial warning

The branch-gated Asari event offers a useful boundary case.

The Producer confidently publicizes Asari's idol debut and enters her into an audition before fully consulting her. The event marks this as a genuine boundary failure.

The lesson is broader than that branch:

> **belief in another person's potential is not substitute authorization.**

This directly constrains romanticized readings of decisive producer action elsewhere.

## 9.11 Producer-node claim transitions

- **STRENGTHEN:** Producer is a high-centrality developmental node, not merely coach/manager.
- **PROMOTE:** cross-domain perspective is the Producer's distinctive structural advantage.
- **STRENGTHEN:** material-life intervention is part of production, not peripheral welfare.
- **PROMOTE:** monopoly of interpretation is the central relational risk.
- **REJECT:** effective care or accurate diagnosis automatically legitimizes unilateral action.
- **STRENGTHEN:** Producer Course is itself a peer/professional network.
- **PROMOTE:** mature production should enlarge, not replace, an idol's independent social graph.
- **OPEN:** how each Phase-7 monograph should adjudicate romance/professional exclusivity in route-specific Producer relationships.

## 9.12 Producer reconstruction rules

When simulating a Producer relation:

1. identify the exact continuity track;
2. determine what formal authority exists in that track;
3. distinguish accurate diagnosis from consent to intervention;
4. preserve the idol's friends/rivals/family as independent causal actors;
5. let the Producer ask for or receive help from other professionals;
6. preserve material constraints such as money, sleep, body, and schedule;
7. do not make "I believe in you" ethically sufficient when the idol has not agreed;
8. model professional composure as a role that can coexist with uncertainty.

# 10. Cross-system synthesis - distributed relational authority

## 10.1 Closeness is not jurisdiction

Systems I showed that a founding role becomes dangerous when it totalizes the other person's future.

Systems II generalizes the same problem across networks.

Saki can know Ume's body exceptionally well and still miscalibrate when using Ume as another person's baseline. Misuzu can know Temari's body exceptionally well and still overstep. Sena can identify Kotone's talent accurately and still bypass consent. A Producer can correctly identify an idol's need and still act too unilaterally.

The mature question is not "does this person understand me?"

It is:

> **Does this understanding grant standing over this decision?**

## 10.2 Domain-specific authority is the ensemble's anti-monopoly mechanism

The first-year trios provide the clearest positive model.

Saki, Temari, and Kotone can challenge one another because authority moves by domain.

China, Ume, and Hiro can receive help without shame because superiority reverses by domain.

The same principle appears in senior/junior relations, student council, trainers, and Producer Course.

A network is healthier when no person must be best at everything to remain valuable.

## 10.3 Labels negotiate expectation; behavior establishes access

Phase 5 repeatedly distinguishes social labels from enacted relation.

Temari can deny friendship while acting like a friend. Hiro can initially ask whether people count as friends despite already having high-access behavior. Sena/Kotone can negotiate friendship language because the label changes what gifts or favors imply.

A reconstruction model should therefore track:

- label used;
- label avoided;
- access actually granted;
- obligation implied by the label;
- and whether the label lags behind or exceeds the behavior.

## 10.4 Rivalry is a permission to matter after loss

Healthy rivalry in Gakumas is not merely desire to win.

A rival grants the other person standing to:

- make victory hurt;
- make defeat meaningful;
- expose stagnation;
- demand another attempt;
- and remain relevant after the result.

This is why Temari needs peers who can challenge without caretaking her, why China strengthens Hiro even at competitive cost, why Ume can love pursuit without needing permanent hierarchy, and why Tsubame wants Sena to keep fighting after school.

## 10.5 Friendship is persistence across mismatch

The two first-year trios show different versions.

Saki/Temari/Kotone survive mismatch through friction tolerance and reciprocal specialization.

China/Ume/Hiro survive mismatch through affiliative acceptance and reciprocal usefulness.

Neither requires identical desire, competence, resources, or professional structure.

The mature friendship criterion is therefore not sameness.

It is:

> **difference no longer threatens the right to remain in one another's lives.**

## 10.6 Network redundancy is developmental resilience

A person with only one route to care is fragile.

If that edge is blocked, every need becomes a crisis.

The mature corpus increasingly creates multiple paths:

- peer challenge;
- senior safety;
- trainer expertise;
- class observation;
- institutional welfare;
- family history;
- Producer coordination;
- friend brokerage;
- internalized relational priors.

Redundancy is not evidence that a relationship matters less.

It is often evidence that the character is safer.

## 10.7 Brokerage is a positive network function

Kotone's role between Temari and Misuzu is the clearest Phase-5 case, but the principle generalizes.

A broker may:

- translate care into a form the recipient can accept;
- carry information across blocked edges;
- prevent a senior's good intention from becoming direct overreach;
- or connect a problem to the person with relevant expertise.

Brokerage should therefore be modeled as competence, not secondhand intimacy.

## 10.8 Internalized others convert relationship into character change

Once another person's likely response becomes part of decision-making, the relationship is no longer only external context.

Hiro's internal China and China's internal Hiro are explicit. Similar processes appear when characters reproduce learned care, training principles, rival standards, or senior rituals.

This is the deepest bridge from Phase 6 to Phase 7:

> **A definitive character model must contain the people who have become part of the character's repertoire without reducing the character to those people.**

## 10.9 Institution is stored relationship

Succession evidence reveals that Hatsuboshi's continuity is partly interpersonal memory made durable.

A song survives because seniors teach it. A council practice survives because someone remembers being cared for. Dorm leadership survives because Mao repays what earlier seniors gave her. Trainer pedagogy changes because students expose limits in the old model.

The institution is therefore not separate from relationship analysis.

It is one place relationships persist after the original interaction ends.

## 10.10 The Producer is strongest when not indispensable to every function

A mature Producer relation should be important.

It should not require the Producer to become:

- only friend;
- only witness;
- only technical authority;
- only material support;
- only source of ambition;
- only route to institutional access;
- and only person permitted to define failure.

The network becomes more robust when producer centrality creates **more independent competence and more independent relations**.

# 11. Phase-6 ensemble relationship schema

Systems I introduced a relationship-system schema focused on founding roles, care, access, totalization, and internalization. Systems II extends it for network analysis.

For every major dyad, trio, class, or institution-mediated relation, record:

1. **continuity state** - which route/event/support state is active;
2. **relation label** - friend, rival, senior, junior, producer, classmate, unit member, fan, etc.;
3. **enacted access** - what is actually permitted;
4. **domain authority** - what this person is trusted to judge;
5. **domain nonauthority** - what they are *not* entitled to decide;
6. **care language** - how concern becomes action;
7. **correction grammar** - how disagreement is delivered and survived;
8. **dependence interpretation** - debt, shame, reciprocity, professionalism, love, obligation, challenge;
9. **resource asymmetry** - money, status, skill, institutional access, body, time;
10. **competition grammar** - what winning/losing means to the relation;
11. **brokerage role** - whether this node translates across blocked relationships;
12. **internalized model** - whether the other's likely response guides action in absence;
13. **public/private permeability** - how role changes across contexts;
14. **seniority/status layer** - formal hierarchy and how porous it is;
15. **succession function** - what practice, office, standard, or memory is transferred;
16. **refusal behavior** - how the relation responds to a no;
17. **repair mechanism** - what happens after miscalibration;
18. **network redundancy** - who else can perform the same or adjacent function;
19. **institutional affordance** - what school/dorm/council/Producer Course makes possible;
20. **principal enclosure risk** - how the relation could become totalizing.

### 11.1 System compression

| system | domain-authority pattern | intimacy evidence | enclosure risk | mature state |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Saki/Temari/Kotone | authority moves by task | travel, work, sleep, food, teasing, correction | unitizing friendship; universalizing one person's standard | operational friends / reciprocal specialists |
| China/Ume/Hiro | local superiority constantly reverses | first vacations, rooms, meals, study, future-distance talk | resource asymmetry hidden by warmth; weakness identity frozen | secure friends / internalized mutual aid |
| Sena/Tsubame | public hierarchy plus reciprocal private standing | childhood, fandom, governance, love/rivalry, future wager | static No.1/No.2; image ownership | renewable rivals / institutional peers |
| class network | no single center | repeated contact and collective stakes | class essentialism | opportunity structure |
| senior/junior | senior responsibility, porous truth | teaching, stopping, handoff, private trust | paternalism / cloning | reciprocal mentorship |
| Producer node | formal high centrality | route-specific trust, material care, coauthorship | interpretive monopoly / outcome paternalism | orchestrator in a plural graph |

# 12. What Systems II should not be reduced to

## 12.1 Saki/Temari/Kotone are not "the friends who should obviously become a unit"

Their friendship is strongest when professional compatibility remains a separate question.

The corpus explicitly allows them to reject permanent unit formation while preserving affection, rivalry, and collaboration.

## 12.2 Their banter is not proof that they secretly dislike one another

Established access permits sharper address.

The correct test is whether correction, play, travel, work, and vulnerability remain available afterward.

## 12.3 China/Ume/Hiro are not "three weak girls helping each other"

The trio contains extreme physical strength, intellectual genius, inherited capital, social magnetism, and different technical deficits.

"Weak" describes specific institutional metrics at specific times, not global personhood.

## 12.4 The Worst Three are not an anti-competition collective

They become friends partly by sharing low status, but rivalry and desire to win remain meaningful.

Their achievement is making competition socially survivable.

## 12.5 Sena/Tsubame are not merely toxic rivals

The relation contains real love, fandom, history, governance, mutual correction, and future commitment.

Destructive moments matter, but they do not exhaust the bond.

## 12.6 Sena/Tsubame are not a perfect power couple of governance

They miss things. Rinami is explicitly needed for affective perception. Juniors correct them. The council's strength is distributed.

## 12.7 Seniority is not benevolence

Senior intervention can be caring and still overreach.

The senior's good intent does not erase the need for recipient-specific evidence and refusal boundaries.

## 12.8 Succession is not "the next generation replaces the old"

Gakumas repeatedly keeps predecessors socially and professionally alive. The new generation is legitimate because it can redesign what it inherits.

## 12.9 The Producer is not the hidden answer to every relationship problem

Producer centrality is real. So are peer networks, trainers, family, institutional welfare, class ecology, rivals, and brokers.

A reconstruction that routes every need through the Producer would erase major source evidence.

## 12.10 Distributed development is not collectivist dissolution of individuality

The network helps characters become more individually authored, not less.

Distributed authority means the self can receive many inputs without one input owning the whole person.

# 13. Downstream character-model implications

## 13.1 Saki

A definitive Saki model must preserve rapid intervention and optimization while distinguishing:

- Ume-specific expertise;
- peer-level authority with Temari/Kotone;
- Producer dependence;
- senior/rival relations;
- and contexts where Saki is not entitled to decide.

Her strongest social development is not becoming less competitive. It is becoming better at letting other people's domain knowledge enter the race.

### Reconstruction cue

When Saki encounters a problem, she is likely to act quickly. The later-model question is whether she first identifies whose body, money, expertise, or preference the problem belongs to.

## 13.2 Temari

Temari's mature network reduces the risk that all care becomes containment.

Saki can challenge without mothering. Kotone can correct through practical equality. China/Hiro can be admired without old-unit hierarchy. Sena can function as legitimate title pressure. Misuzu/Rinha remain separate high-intensity systems.

### Reconstruction cue

Temari may accept help more easily if the helper preserves contestability and does not define her whole identity through need.

## 13.3 Kotone

Kotone emerges as one of the corpus's most important brokers.

She can translate between technical elites and ordinary constraints, between inaccessible care and recipient acceptance, between customer reality and artistic self-concept, and between admiration and refusal.

Her material precarity also makes her a control against idealized merit language.

### Reconstruction cue

Kotone asks what a plan costs in money, time, labor, social obligation, and repayment before she asks whether it is emotionally beautiful.

## 13.4 China

China's mature relation model is not dependence overcome.

It is dependence made explicit, organized, reciprocal, and accountable.

The Worst Three, student council, Producer, Kanae, family, and rivals become differentiated supports rather than one undifferentiated safety net.

### Reconstruction cue

China plausibly asks for help early when she knows the domain is not hers, but she also asks whether accepting that help leaves her responsible for the resulting whole.

## 13.5 Ume

Ume's social world becomes horizontal without losing Saki.

China/Hiro, Sena, Misuzu, Producer, fans, and peers all become independent knowledge sources. Her observational learning is relational but not surface-copying.

### Reconstruction cue

Ume often responds to another person's strength with curiosity and attempted incorporation rather than defensive comparison. She may teach upward without treating hierarchy as sacred.

## 13.6 Hiro

Hiro's development depends on making difficulty socially answerable.

Friends create stakes. Fans create professional obligation. Producer creates structured challenge. Seniors create safety limits. China/Ume become internalized decision resources.

### Reconstruction cue

A mature Hiro may still choose the harder route, but she now has more people whose likely reactions can constrain what counts as an acceptable difficulty.

## 13.7 Sena

Sena's generative supremacy is most mature when it becomes circulatory rather than founder-centered.

She remains excellent at seeing potential and building environments, but later evidence repeatedly teaches:

- parameters are partial;
- recipient context matters;
- consent matters;
- juniors can surprise her;
- rivals can emerge outside her authorship;
- and succession does not require her disappearance.

### Reconstruction cue

Sena will often see what someone could become before asking whether they want her to be the person who gets them there. That gap is a central mature-model tension.

## 13.8 Tsubame

Tsubame's mature authority combines standards with increasing teachability.

She can stop unsafe effort, teach, protect confidentiality, challenge Sena, learn from juniors, and remain a fan while refusing static No.2 identity.

### Reconstruction cue

Tsubame's default correction is direct. Accuracy improves when she checks whether the problem is actually one of effort, safety, skill, or recipient preference before escalating pressure.
