# TSUKIMURA TEMARI — DIALOGUE AND VOICE-ACTING CLOSE READING

## 0. Central conclusion

Temari's speaking performance does not divide neatly into a false cool voice and a true childish voice. It presents a **stateful person** whose pitch, pacing, projection, posture, and diction reorganize around threat, trust, rivalry, pain, appetite, and public duty.

The development across Dear Idol 1–37 is not a reduction in volatility. It is increasing capacity to:

- know which state she is in;
- let another person witness it;
- request the kind of recognition she wants;
- move from a vulnerable state into action without pretending the vulnerable state never happened;
- allow several states to belong to one name.

The decisive line is not a vocal trick but a relational achievement: before the winter final she asks the Producer to call her simply `手毬`. The person entering the stage is not an abstract cool idol or the managed child called `まりちゃん`. She chooses the intimate name and the performance it will launch.

# 1. Method and limits

The analysis uses four complete Dear Idol compilations, seven complete song commus, exact Japanese ADV extracts, rendered body/facial performance, BGM and scene transitions, and conservative 40-second full-mix prosodic proxies.

The numerical proxies may include BGM and other voices. They are used only when they agree with visible performance and exact dialogue. No claim depends on an automated pitch contour alone.

# 2. Scene 1 — early overwork
## Permission to burn as recognition

### 2.1 Dramatic problem

Temari does not trust care that slows her. Misuzu's long management history made bodily protection feel like proof that she was weak and permanently dependent.

### 2.2 Performed opening

The scene begins with compact control. Temari appears upright and comparatively contained. As strain and frustration emerge, the body flushes, gaze lowers, and hands tighten.

### 2.3 The relief of authorization

When the Producer authorizes a harder course, Temari's energy rises. The visual transition toward determined posture is important. She does not receive the decision as exploitation from outside; she experiences it as accurate recognition.

### 2.4 Ethical consequence

This is why the Producer can become both liberating and dangerous. He understands that Temari wants help that increases agency, not help that defines her limit. But granting every desired escalation can become a way of loving the self-destructive demand.

### 2.5 Verdict

The scene supports `T-V0 → T-V3 → reassembled control`. Temari's bond begins partly through permission to expend herself.

# 3. Scene 2 — Dear Idol 010
## Artificial wings, selection, and future command

### 3.1 Disclosure

Temari explains that she likes the self who strains toward an admired form and fears reverting whenever she relaxes. She asks whether the Producer really chooses her over Misuzu.

### 3.2 Voice state

The scene uses a more searching, lowered performance than her routine abrasive banter. The question is not rhetorical swagger. She is requesting selection while expecting disconfirmation.

### 3.3 `造り物`

`私の翼は造り物で、本物じゃない` carries shame because construction is equated with inferiority. Her ability depends on effort, imitation, and support rather than effortless genius.

### 3.4 Reassembly

After the Producer chooses her, Temari returns to dry humor and command: follow me to the highest place. The command does not invalidate the preceding vulnerability. It converts selection into a task.

### 3.5 Verdict

Ambition functions as a vocal and psychological reassembly mechanism.

# 4. Scene 3 — Dear Idol 016
## Rinha, sobbing, and filial imitation

### 4.1 Rupture

This is the most visibly uncontained scene in the selected corpus. Temari bends, cries, clenches and lifts her hands, and produces prolonged pleading. The full-mix window also has the highest selected pitch proxy and extreme dynamic spread, though visual and textual evidence govern.

### 4.2 The childish register

`ヤダヤダヤダヤダ` could be treated as gap comedy in another context. Here it is the grammar of a younger person confronting an origin figure's disappearance.

### 4.3 The confession

Temari states that Rinha shaped singing, dancing, and cool `キャラ作り`. The statement is emotionally difficult because it risks making her proudest self derivative.

### 4.4 Rinha's response

Rinha's shock and tears show that the influence was not transparent even to the model. Temari's imitation is simultaneously appropriation, devotion, inheritance, and an unspoken bond.

### 4.5 Verdict

The supposedly least cool voice communicates one of the most important truths about the cool idol. Childlike performance and authored persona are causally connected, not mutually exclusive.

# 5. Scene 4 — Dear Idol 019–020
## Misuzu becomes a rival

### 5.1 Old grammar

Misuzu historically read Temari's body, anticipated need, and restrained danger. Temari interpreted that accuracy as possession and superiority.

### 5.2 New speech

The rivalry scenes let Temari demand that Misuzu stop yielding. The performance alternates familiarity, irritation, and direct challenge rather than maintaining a cool diplomatic distance.

### 5.3 Why this matters

Conflict becomes preventive rather than terminal. The pair can say the difference before it accumulates into another SyngUp! collapse.

### 5.4 Verdict

The healthier relation is not less intense; it is more speakable.

# 6. Scene 5 — Dear Idol 021–023
## Becoming Misuzu's fan and losing to Sena

### 6.1 Misuzu's independent world

Temari's response to Misuzu's performance combines admiration and threat. The lower rendered proxy in the selected window should not be overread because the segment includes performance and other speakers. Visually and narratively, the important point is stunned attention: Temari can become an audience member inside the relationship.

### 6.2 `負けは負けです`

After Sena wins, Temari uses compact judgment. She refuses to turn causal influence, moral contribution, or closeness of result into victory.

### 6.3 Distributed affective labor

The Producer becomes the person who is openly furious and partial. Temari recognizes his anger as belief in her. She does not need to perform every feeling herself in order for the relationship to carry it.

### 6.4 Verdict

Controlled speech can represent integration rather than denial when another person is allowed to hold the overflow.

# 7. Scene 6 — Dear Idol 027
## Airplane, rocket, and competitive tenderness

### 7.1 Misuzu's correction

Misuzu reframes artificial wings through technology: an airplane can fly higher than a bird.

### 7.2 Temari's uptake

Temari does not merely accept consolation. She escalates the metaphor into a rocket and immediately turns it back into rivalry.

### 7.3 Voice and relation

Warmth and challenge alternate without requiring a rupture. The pair can validate and provoke one another in the same exchange.

### 7.4 Verdict

Temari has begun to receive help without allowing help to finalize her identity as the weaker person.

# 8. Scene 7 — `Luna say maybe` commu
## Denial, humming, and song as indirect speech

### 8.1 Disavowal

Temari insists the song's girl is too different from her. The denial is not maintained by avoidance; she keeps returning to the song.

### 8.2 Voice-state contrast

Ordinary speech carries embarrassment and defensiveness. The pre-live state has heightened energy and anxiety. Song then organizes what prose cannot settle.

### 8.3 Verdict

The performance supports a basic Temari rule: she may be able to sing a truth before she can state it as autobiography.

# 9. Scene 8 — `アイヴイ` commu
## The many Temaris and a trusted observer

### 9.1 Comedy as ontology

The commu cycles through crying, spoiled, angry, severe, and cool Temaris. The comedy is doing analytical work: every reduction is recognizable and inadequate.

### 9.2 `わかってるね`

After viewing the final PV, Temari's response shifts from evaluation to delighted recognition. Her inner speech reveals pleasure at appearing cool in the Producer's eyes.

### 9.3 Stage selection

The Producer's formulation—that a girl has many sides and the stage carries especially compelling ones because she is an idol—gives Temari a model of selective truth.

### 9.4 Verdict

The Producer's gaze is productive because it returns a version Temari can desire without requiring denial of the others. This also gives him unusually high authorship power.

### 10.2 Unit fantasy

### 10.3 Verdict

# 11. Scene 10 — `一体いつから` commu
## Learning emotional techniques

### 11.1 Dependence named without humiliation

Temari can say that she relies on Misuzu in daily life. That admission would once have threatened the entire self-concept.

### 11.2 Multiple methods

China's gentle expression and Misuzu's audience-oriented control become legitimate techniques rather than weaker substitutes for Temari's full-force feeling.

### 11.3 Verdict

The acting and story support a shift from authenticity-as-total-force to authenticity-as-intentional communication.

# 12. Scene 11 — `ガラクタロード` commu
## Blue flame and making a category real

### 12.1 Self-recognition crisis

Temari openly states that her interior is not conventionally cool. She nevertheless wants the category because Kei and Rinha defined her ideal.

### 12.2 The Producer's phrase

`超高温で完全燃焼する青い炎` produces immediate recognition. Temari hears her desired form verbalized accurately.

### 12.3 Rendered cost

After rehearsal she is panting. The Producer fears what such expenditure would mean in the actual performance.

### 12.4 Public audacity

Temari decides not to request admission to an existing category. She will make her version the reference point.

### 12.5 Verdict

The scene is the clearest fusion of voice, body, concept, and public ambition in the baseline.

# 13. Scene 12 — Dear Idol 035
## First-name sendoff

### 13.1 Request

Temari asks for one action before the final: do not call her `月村さん`; call her `手毬` and send her out.

### 13.2 Relational precision

This is not a generalized request for informality. It is a timed ritual of selection immediately before performance.

### 13.3 Response

The resulting call—go become the top idol—and her bright answer convert intimacy directly into stage action.

### 13.4 Verdict

Temari now requests dependence in a form she authors.

# 14. Scene 13 — Dear Idol 036–037
## Public title, private role reversal, and present-tense love

### 14.1 Public declaration

Temari accepts Prima Stella while refusing to become a guardian of a static summit. Her public speech remains directional: attack higher, sing above the sun, create new dreams.

### 14.2 Emotional lag

Privately, she admits that feeling has not caught up with the fact of victory. This prevents the title from functioning as magical cure.

### 14.3 Role reversal

The Producer cries; Temari lends the handkerchief, reads the tears, and becomes the stabilizing person. Reciprocity is enacted rather than merely declared.

### 14.4 Present-tense love

`今日の私` and `いまのあなた` are precise. The statement does not define timeless essence. It values the current selves produced by the route.

### 14.5 Immediate future

She asks for the next goal and agrees to a meeting immediately after H.I.F., interrupted only by ramen. Ambition, appetite, intimacy, and comedy coexist.

### 14.6 Verdict

The climax integrates the voice states rather than crowning only the formal idol voice.

# 15. Cross-scene performance grammar

## 15.1 High pitch

High pitch may signal:

- pain and panic;
- childish pleading;
- anger;
- embarrassment;
- sudden pleasure;
- deliberate novelty/cute performance.

It is not a reliable synonym for immaturity.

## 15.2 Lower pitch and reduced projection

A lower or quieter state may signal:

- professional control;
- defeat containment;
- shame;
- intimate trust;
- reflection;
- suppression.

It is not a reliable synonym for coolness or health.

## 15.3 Elongation

Elongated vowels frequently mark the collapse of compact control. They can be comic, manipulative, genuinely pained, or all three. Context determines whether the elongation is a demand, plea, or release.

## 15.4 Clear articulation under affect

One stable performance trait is that words remain comparatively intelligible during strong affect. Temari's combustion is rarely formless noise. Even the rupture often communicates a specific demand.

## 15.5 Reassembly through future language

After vulnerable disclosure, Temari often regains form by naming a future:

- highest place;
- next rivalry;
- rocket;
- top idol;
- next meeting.

Future tense is one of her principal vocal regulation tools.

# 16. Voice-state model

| code | state | relational risk | mature potential |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `T-V0` | compact professional control | suppression mistaken for health | clear judgment and public reliability |
| `T-V1` | abrasive/direct | cruelty or defensive isolation | early conflict, honest boundary |
| `T-V2` | childlike need/panic | manipulation, shame, dependency | direct expression of attachment and pain |
| `T-V3` | combustive protest | escalation, self-harm, social rupture | forceful refusal and competitive ignition |
| `T-V4` | lowered intimacy | enclosure around one witness | trust, self-disclosure, chosen reliance |
| `T-V5` | formal stage voice | persona becomes obligation | public promise, professional authorship |
| `T-V6` | playful/cute performance | image trivialization | persona elasticity and shared pleasure |

Temari's growth is the ability to route among these states without requiring one to invalidate the others.

# 17. Relationship implications

## 17.1 Producer reciprocity

The late corpus confirms genuine reciprocity: Temari asks, listens, interprets, cares, and selects. She is not merely produced from outside.

## 17.2 Producer power

The same person supplies image, training conditions, conceptual vocabulary, ritual recognition, and future direction. Accurate understanding magnifies authority. Mutuality is therefore not the end of the ethical analysis.

## 17.3 Misuzu

The relation improves when performance, rivalry, and explicit disagreement supplement care. Misuzu's own core pass remains necessary before bilateral conclusions.

## 17.4 Rinha

The rupture proves that imitation is affective lineage. Temari's future rivalry depends on being able to acknowledge that source.

# 18. Final voice-acting thesis

Temari's voice acting makes construction audible.

The cool idol is not a separate speaker who replaces the needy child. She is one way the same person organizes pressure for a particular audience and task. The route's achievement is not smooth consistency. It is increasing ownership over inconsistency.

> **The voice becomes truthful not when it stops changing, but when Temari can let the changes belong to one self and choose what each state will do next.**
