# Juo Ryuusei

This portrait covers the named roles and continuities at Source Lock 1.2. The locator table defines the scope of spoken, performed and documentary evidence.

Juo Ryuusei is the representative director of 100 Pro, the father of Juo Sena and the son of the Hatsuboshi headmaster, Juo Kunio. He enters a room asking what it would take to make a decision commercially, professionally or publicly defensible. He can regard the same student as an attractive prospect, a reputational risk and an artist who ought to be allowed to set terms for herself if she can support them. Much of his power consists in determining which uncertainty his organization will carry. His preferred answer can feel like a verdict to somebody close to him, especially when he speaks with the economy of an executive about a question the other person experiences as intimate.

## A recruiter who can be made to change his terms

Ryuusei gives his title and name when he recruits Mao after her N.I.A. final. He has seen enough to be surprised by her defeat of Shirakusa Gekka, and wants to reach her before other agencies do. He names acting history as publicity value and distinguishes the school’s ability to produce from dealing with agencies and doing adult professional work. He can see that her existing skills have value in their own right even though the immediate offer presents them through the market. The pressure the title carries is visible in the encounter. Mao asks whether she is going to be expelled. Her Producer offers to take the responsibility. Ryuusei momentarily considers the phrase of expulsion, then tells them not to leap to conclusions and states the purpose of his visit. The dialogue supplies the alarming implication without settling whether he intended a joke or a deliberate reminder of rank. The scene does not demonstrate institutional authority to expel her. He then distinguishes voluntary withdrawal for an earlier professional debut from provisional Class 3 affiliation through graduation, and gives her time to discuss the decision with her Producer and parents. (R1)

When he discusses the offer with Sena, the same appraising eye focuses on a different kind of danger. He sees clear value in Mao and rates Sena above her at the present time. An unsuccessful H.I.F. in front of the media could attach to Mao before a professional debut, so her entering gives him a reason for concern. Sena dislikes assessing the choice that way and credits Mao’s relationship with the Producer as a real condition of her change. Mao later asks if that Producer will be removed. Ryuusei says yes, acknowledging ability while pointing to his student status and to different production methods. The planned investment still gives Mao access to professional idols, equipment and training, and he offers a path in which a career in acting can complement her stage work. His account of acting for her wishes is partial. He initially expects her to accept his staffing decision and evaluates exposure by the agency’s interests. He had indicated that this Producer could join after graduation, which does not make him Mao’s immediate producer under the original offer. (R2)

The pressure is specific enough to renegotiate. Mao and the Producer enter together and ask what result would establish him as the right person to produce her. Mao offers to win H.I.F. Ryuusei calls it a result others would recognize while assessing the two as incapable of defeating Sena then. He acknowledges that a proven idol and proven Producer together would have value to his company, so a conditional exception is viable. He initially assumes they mean the summer contest. Mao names winter and the Producer concedes that the summer opposition exceeds them presently. They return Ryuusei’s own objection to unsupported grand claims. He credits the Producer for pursuing a dream while looking for a workable means. In Mao’s Dear winter outcome, he congratulates her, explicitly confines the defeat of Sena to that day, admits the partnership has demonstrated its claim, and keeps the promise to accept them together. He marks the win as the start of more work. This change shows that his evaluation and staffing decisions can be challenged. A later Mao communication gives a smaller test. He has papers ready and wants to announce the team's affiliation at the high point of public attention. When the Producer asks to review the terms because they concern Mao's life, he grants him until the next day. Ryuusei explicitly dislikes wasted time. The Producer suspects he was being tested, and his own decision to grant the time is the direct fact. It gives no basis to infer that he would often yield on a less concrete test or that his preferred risks are the same as Mao’s. (R3)

## Which testimony he will use

A different offer arises when Rinami’s Producer wants 100 Pro’s help with a student-organized project. Ryuusei accepts it for company consideration, cites the risk of a student organizer, and weighs a chance to discover talent or create a favor with a promising Producer against a limited direct institutional benefit. He makes no immediate promise of partnership in the scene. In Rinami’s Dear 037 the Producer reports a separate subsequent approach. He suspects earlier N.I.A. interest and anticipates a firmer 100 Pro offer after H.I.F.; those are his report and expectations. The scene does not record Rinami agreeing to join. He can respect visible commitment while keeping the decision conditional and using a possible later gain to understand present costs. The Producer finds him difficult in inner speech, which supplies an observer’s impression. It does not establish a hidden plan. (R4)

In Event 028 he accepts directions from Rinami at a performance venue, thanks her, and asks whether she will appear as an idol. When she asks what separates professional idols from others, he says the question sounds like a freshman’s. He then gives a personal answer. Defining talent strikes him as fruitless. Dance, looks, singing and even the capacity to make people like you do not tell him its essence. He says that the top idols he has seen loved idols, whatever form the love took. The line leaves the balance between love for the people, the work and the calling open. This moment distinguishes the person who assigns potential to a recruit from the person who can say why a list of selection traits cannot define a vocation. It also shows that a dry or demoting opening can lead to a seriously offered reply. It is habitual judgment at one scene, not a commitment of corporate procedure. Rinami recognizes him from a brochure and thinks he rarely appears publicly. That is her access to him, not a census of his appearances. (R5)

Ryuusei previously agrees to arrange the unofficial contest after Misuzu's Producer proposes it and he identifies a benefit for 100 Pro. The live in Misuzu Dear 031 gives another division of authority. Ryuusei calls a professional’s objection about exceptionally strong students entering as trainees useful testimony and proposes that festival rules be corrected. He declines to provide a formal verdict about the live itself. He can also read the self-correction of an idol he works with as development and see how a close contest can generate publicity for an event that had looked like a foregone conclusion. He can count a criticism as useful even when the criticized trainees make the school and the prospective festival more compelling. This is a sourced opinion and evaluation. The lines establish neither a rule change nor that he controls the decisions of Kunio’s school. (R6)

## A father who paves a path and communicates sparsely

In a separate Sena communication, Ryuusei describes childhood as the period when choices with long consequences arrive before a child knows how to make them. The best he can do is put her on a route he thinks promising and leave the later decision to her. He reports a check from his wife, who warned him about loading his life onto a child's. He connects it with his acknowledged failure to make his first idol flourish as a young Producer. He thinks she saw him trying to replay that failure through Sena. In his account, she invited him to devote that unfinished attention to producing her again. He says she is still pursuing a life as an actor. He attributes some family misunderstanding to infrequent meetings and proposes that the family eat together on the occasion of another H.I.F. visit. Their later meal and his wife's reasoning beyond his account are open. His account is evidence that this danger occurs to him, and that he can accept a warning from a person who has both family and professional standing with him. It leaves persuasive paternal expectations as a live counterreading. (R7)

His treatment of the production plan in Sena Dear 028 tests whether leaving her a choice is only a principle easy to state afterward. He reads the plan, judges the Producer capable and invites him to continue with Sena after graduation. That shows a different answer to the staffing question from his original Mao terms. He then dismisses Sena's own producing as make-believe and tells her to stop when she joins 100 Pro. Sena refuses, explicitly disputes his present jurisdiction because she is not yet an agency idol, and proposes to prove her combination of producing and being an idol before graduation. He ends the audience but tells her Producer he looks forward to seeing it. After she earns the two series of audition distinctions, he admits he was wrong, apologizes and retracts that description. He endorses her combined future and says he is pleased she exceeded his forecast. She had hoped he would be chagrined. Leaving a later choice open, applying pressure as an agency leader and revising an actual paternal judgment can coexist. The Producer's imagined dismissal in Dear 029 is a counterfactual voiced within another account. It supplies no later act of firing by Ryuusei. (R12)

Sena gives both a contrary and a supporting witness. She says the family's relations are good and that she often finds little to say after long periods apart. In describing the President professionally she calls him capable, cunning, imposing and tenacious about favored idols. The Producer silently notices a similarity to Sena, while Sena teases the Producer by comparing him to her father. The comparisons concern how a person appears to others. After the preceding summer's Prima Stella title, she remembers Ryuusei saying she would not succeed at the professional level. She took it as a confirmation of where her skill stood. Misuzu calls them both hasty. The remembered wording is sparse enough to leave Ryuusei’s actual evaluative criterion or his longer intention open. Sena also says elsewhere that she herself finds happiness in pursuing the path laid out for her. Her account of choice and experience survives even where his control over opportunities is evident. (R8)

A very different family act is visible when the Producer arranges a surprise audience. Sena treats the possibility as unlikely. The family comes, and she sees her father in her merchandise. She learns from the Producer that he had asked to wear a different version, the `Campus mode!!` one, while looking as forbidding as ever to this witness. To Sena the combination changes her image of him and makes the family’s presence a distinct pleasure and source of encouragement. The T-shirt request and attendance qualify a picture of complete unavailability. Her anger at the surprise survives alongside its effect. One occasion of wearing merchandise does not establish an ordinary hobby or the thought behind the version he selected. (R9)
