# Hatsuboshi's dance trainer

The dance trainer deals candidly with what another person can do today. When Ume's athletic ability fails to produce good dancing, that discrepancy fascinates her. When Mao's fatigue threatens an injury, she recognizes the stakes for a third-year student and still tells Mao to stop. She assigns exacting work and can take genuine pleasure in a pupil's effort. Her certainty about a movement often exceeds her certainty that a person can accept the advice. She can ask coworkers for reassurance, acknowledge that a student has exposed a teaching problem, and work to carry a professional standard farther than the timetable allows.

The academy calls her ダンストレーナー. Her role includes dance instruction and work on competition preparation, and she explains competition rules and calls the runners to the starting line in Event 014 (`adv_event_014_main-05` L158). ## The lesson in front of her

During Kotone's practice the trainer counts transitions, notices a late turn, credits the rhythm, and asks for a movement that reads larger. She sets a ten-minute break. Kotone calls her a demon in her absence and acknowledges that she appreciates the strict teaching. The trainer comes back with a sports drink. She says she seldom buys one for a pupil and wanted to recognize Kotone's effort. Kotone explains why she wants to make each hour count. The trainer approves and resumes the lesson. Praise here is explicit, tied to what one student has done, and followed by more work. The trainer's own claim to be kind and Kotone's private disbelief both belong in the account. (Support `adv_csprt-2-0038_01` L8–175.)

On another day she diagnoses Kotone's mistake as tension and tells her to relax. The direction is clearer than its implementation, and Kotone privately resents it. Pressed for something practical, the trainer makes two suggestions. First, repeat the dance until the body can do it without occupying all of the dancer's attention. Second, picture the spectators while rehearsing, since repeating movements with no picture of the eventual performance can diminish the quality of the work. Kotone agrees to try. The trainer asks whether the advice helped and admits that advising outside technique is difficult. When Kotone praises her, she claims it is what a trainer should do and turns back to the lesson. Student questioning gets a more usable explanation out of her. (Support `adv_csprt-2-0038_02` L13–145.)

She makes a related distinction in a separate winter H.I.F. teaching communication. Depending on what the student Producer names, she asks how much technical development fits the time, what the assigned idol can make of her particular strengths, or how the choreography invites a response from the audience. These are alternative answers to one teaching prompt. They show the range of what she can consider when deciding what a performance needs. They give no universal scoring formula. ## How she speaks and what to expect

Her ordinary written instruction is compact. She uses `私`, often addresses a pupil by surname or as `おまえ`, and puts the correction early in the sentence. `遅れたぞ` in Kotone's lesson marks a particular late turn. `不満か` in Hiro's individualized lesson invites an answer without taking the edge off a demand. She uses startled wording, declares firm limits, and sometimes tries to provoke a response by comparison. In a private conversation with the two trainers she opens with a request for advice and admits she is unsure. With a pupil who praises her, she may hesitate and redirect the conversation to practice. In a separate lesson for the Producer she offers to teach what the student wants to learn. One answer prompts instruction in recognizing an idol's movement signs of strain. A separate answer initially startles her, then she agrees to teach the Producer to dance because doing it can deepen understanding of production. (Produce school communication `adv_pevent_003_cmmn_school_2-003` L4–17, L41–53 and L57–69, alternate responses.) The written shifts are evidence of register and interaction. No isolated voice, typical acoustic contour, singing sample, or musical repertoire is attributed to her here.

- When a pupil is working hard and making a diagnosable mistake, she is likely to name the physical issue and propose repeated work at a reachable scale. A purpose such as what a spectator can see or respond to can change her advice.
- If ordinary instruction repeatedly overwhelms a pupil, she can change the task and enlist a Producer. If accumulated fatigue endangers the very performance the pupil is pursuing, she can advise ending the session. Her low estimate of Hiro is a judgment at that point in Hiro's route.
- When athletic promise and actual dance learning diverge, she investigates and takes the teaching problem personally. She may invite comparison in hopes of provoking anger. Ume's encouragement in response shows how differently the student can read the challenge. With another pupil, such a comparison could discourage them. The sources do not establish a general effect.
- When puzzled by how a student sees her, she is capable of asking colleagues for a check. Her colleagues can interpret a group's request to try again as engagement. An individual pupil's separate account can expose the limits of a class-wide judgment. Outside work she can share embarrassment and show admiration.
