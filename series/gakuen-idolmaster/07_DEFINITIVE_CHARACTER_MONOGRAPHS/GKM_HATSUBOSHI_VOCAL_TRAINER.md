# Hatsuboshi’s vocal trainer

The vocal trainer takes a student's present singing seriously enough to tell her what still needs work. She can ask Hatsuboshi's most eager singer to yield her turn, challenge a confident performer with breathing drills, and defend a beginner against an assessment she thinks has come too early. She jokes when she teaches and can take pleasure in her own demonstration. The joke can go wrong. A student who takes it literally may need someone else to point out the mismatch.

Hatsuboshi identifies her by her job. She teaches singing to idols and teaches student Producers enough about voice to help them care for their performers. The support stories, separate Produce alternatives and Dear continuities show distinct situations in which she can be compared. They establish a consistent written role with a recurring `trvo` cue assignment. Her name, age, training, household and life before the job remain unknown. ## How she works with Producers and colleagues

In different selectable answers, she covers taking over a lesson when she is away, looking after an idol's throat, and learning to sing in order to understand it from the inside. In the last answer she jokes that the Producer wants to become an idol and then offers to teach from the beginning and to be strict. ## Written voice and ordinary conduct

She often addresses a pupil by surname with さん and uses polite sentence endings, elongations and occasional musical-note marks in the printed dialogue. Her opening instructions can be friendly and precise at once. A written stumble sometimes follows surprise, self-praise being challenged or a joke taken literally. In the staff setting she can open by making herself available to hear a concern. In the misjudged lesson she challenges a Producer directly. The work does not give those written patterns a typical sound, tempo or timbre.

On a holiday she says that time to talk with her colleagues feels new and enjoyable. Their subject remains the generational distance they notice at work. She tells them she selected what she thought was a current exercise song and Temari called it retro and said she liked it. Her recognition that students also use words the staff do not know turns the incident into a mutual distance to navigate. Her attempt at a contemporary choice gives evidence about her teaching intention. In a separate Christmas-party scene she asks her colleagues to show more restraint after they call for presents. The ordinary glimpses give her a role among peers apart from teaching. They leave her usual leisure and personal taste in music unknown. (Support `adv_csprt-3-0100_03` L8–34 and L81–178. Ume song story `adv_cidol-hume-3-005_03` L186.)

- When a confident pupil challenges a routine, expect her to explain what the apparently basic exercise can still do and give the pupil another try. She can insist on work after praise.
- When a beginner overestimates praise or a Producer overstates an early verdict, she tends to preserve a route to improvement. That instinct can both protect a pupil and make the technical limit less clear. Compare the chosen relationship before extrapolating.
- When a pupil appears to be straining, she can support rest even as she wants to train commitment. Her humor can invite rapport. Lilja shows that a literal listener can turn it into a different task.
- When a colleague doubts a severe approach, she can endorse its intent and remain uneasy about its use. She enjoys talking with coworkers away from work, but one outing gives little basis for her life away from them.
