# Hiwatari Kanae

Hiwatari Kanae knows how to stand where somebody else needs her. She watches for what Kuramoto China may have missed, learns what useful service requires, judges those who claim to be able to help, and resents being made a spectator to that work. She applies this formidable capacity to the wrong question when she assumes that China must want someone who suppresses every ambition of her own. What changes in the admitted story is Kanae’s model of what loyalty permits. She learns that judging, helping and competing alongside China can belong to the same life. The attachment stays particular. Her new stage gives her a stake that she can ask other people to recognize as hers.

This portrait includes spoilers through the Japanese Dear 010 and Produce Series 001 material admitted under Source Lock 1.2. Kanae serves China as a live-in attendant and enters the Idol Department after a selective transfer. China holds the Prima Stella title and is about to become student-council president when Kanae’s Dear story begins. The exact earlier China Produce route is open. ## Work she chose and work she was taught to choose

China tells the Producer that a young Kanae appealed directly to her grandfather. What matters about the report is that Kanae acted on a perception of what China needed despite rank and household hierarchy. On a later run Kanae remembers choosing to go out on days off to see China smile, with no instruction from China. She also treats the young China’s call to run an idol road together as an injunction that gave her a purpose. These do not make her choice illusory. They show how she organizes choice around a person she took seriously before she could name a career.

Kanae initially chooses an affiliated school to learn how to serve. Lessons, domestic work and attention to China’s career then deepen her specialized knowledge. In the support story `adv_csprt-1-0013_02.txt`, she sees a tuft of China’s hair China cannot see, puts it right and takes satisfaction in the reply that it helps China feel prepared. She also checks supplies and makes breakfast before attending to her own school day. This is material labor that consumes attention and time. The household, her own education and her attachments are all implicated. The sources do not establish her pay, disposable income, generalized acceptance of hierarchy or financial independence from the household.

China in turn notices things Kanae cannot see about herself. When China wins H.I.F., Kanae is proud and fears being left farther away. China privately wants Kanae to have a dream of her own. China hears Kanae ask for public lessons or a way to learn enough to help better, then brings the transfer idea to the Producer. She worries that an overt request would make it another attendant’s duty. She admits wanting the Producer’s attention to herself and makes Kanae the exception. She agrees that Kanae must choose. The admitted story through Dear 010 shows the Producer disclosing China’s wish, while it contains no scene in which he discloses that China proposed the transfer. Earlier, the post-election appeal to dream and aim for the top is an address Kanae admires without recognizing herself as its addressee. Each woman thinks hard about what the other needs, and each can withhold the information the other needs to answer.

## Difficult trust and the claim to stand beside China

Kanae does not simply accept the Producer’s legitimate authority over China or over herself. Within earlier China material he remembers Kanae judging his competence and maintaining narrowly businesslike interactions. She can later help him resist the grandfather’s oversized plan and treat him as a rival for proximity to China. These witnesses are distinct from Kanae’s own Dear framing. The new production contract starts another test. She agrees for a limited period in order to gain his knowledge and threatens to report him if working with her leads him to neglect China. He accepts the condition. The antagonism contains a promise of work, a means of appeal and a source of later embarrassment.

After the assessment she names a jealousy-like response to his place alongside China. She may thank him in ways that resemble service and insists that they are her own acts. She also asks him to take over part of China’s support while she trains and performs. Sharing a task she once used to protect a unique place supplies stronger evidence of trust than gratitude by itself. Her assurance that she has not sulked, insistence that she will retain the best seat beside China, and claim that becoming a top idol will secure it make clear that the rivalry continues. In Dear 010 she hopes to see the same view and improve what she can offer from there. She states the ambition in the first person and with China still in mind.

A branch-specific failure ending makes the limit clear. Kanae does not fully trust the Producer even while saying he is the only person she can ask if she continues. He says he will earn trust through results. Kanae agrees to answer through results too. In the True ending she recognizes a wish to be publicly valued in her own right, tells him that helping China continues to matter and entrusts her life as an idol to him. Those two responses describe different achieved situations. The words supply no license to infer a permanent private relationship outcome or the authority to direct her life outside idol work. Her jealousy-like admission identifies a real attachment condition without assigning it a genre or exclusive romantic meaning.
