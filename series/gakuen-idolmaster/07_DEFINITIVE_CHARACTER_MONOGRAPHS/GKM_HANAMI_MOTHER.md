# The Hanami sisters’ mother

Amaya Tsubame has enough respect for the mother of her rival, Hanami Saki, to suffer through one of her massages. She also has a practical reason to go back. It hurts terribly, and it works, according to Tsubame. Saki has offered more than once to take over. When the young idol finally manages to tell her trainer how honored she feels, the older woman cuts through the formality with two words, `恐縮禁止`. No standing on ceremony. She calls herself a beginning idol trainer and says that her job is to train them both with everything she has. In another route, the academy’s own trainers flatter her. This time it is she who asks, in elaborate professional language, for their continuing guidance. The change of register has a point. She can refuse a young athlete’s deference while offering it to a colleague whose experience she values. (Saki Dear 032 L187–250, L300–369. Ume Dear 034 L310–358.)

## What her daughters remember

The Producer knows of both Hanami parents as famous athletes who continued to work after retiring. Saki gives a less public account of what that meant. The adults were so busy they seldom came home. Her grandparents looked after the children, and Saki and Ume often spent their time together. Saki can be proud of a family whose gifts and photographs she remembers and still say that most of the company she had was her sister’s. The matching Halloween outfits she recalls elsewhere came from her grandparents. The scenes establish who was present as the girls remember it. Their mother’s own feelings about being away remain unknown. (Saki Dear 010 L281–306. Support 3-0112 part 02 L7–37.)

Motherhood becomes visible again in a piece of backstage work easy to overlook. Just before Ume performs, the Producer points out the people in the audience who helped while Saki was away. Ume names her mother and the staff. Food and physical care made a difference, she says. She has a complaint about the massage, which she attributes to her mother specifically. When the Producer remarks on their resemblance, Ume protests that she is hardly that rough. Their different accounts of the same help can stand together. The Producer admires a capable trainer. Her daughter remembers the force of her hands. The audience includes more than a proud parent arriving to watch the result. It includes people whose work made the performance possible. (Ume Dear 021 L336–367.)

Ume says her mother told her, with some pride, that her daughters’ decision to become idols sent her back to studying. She says she obtained every qualification then available to her, received staff introductions from the headmaster, and put together a facility for idol training. The Producer adds that he had a hand in it. In Saki’s corresponding conversation about the facility, Saki credits her parents and the Producer. She mentions hiring staff and bringing in high-level trainers through the academy. The Producer expects the place to offer outside expertise, equipment for video analysis and possibilities for joint training. Ume gives her mother particular initiative in a project with multiple contributors. A boast repeated by a daughter is good evidence for the kind of accomplishment the mother wanted her to hear about. It supplies neither a list of credentials nor a precise distribution of ownership. (Ume Dear 021 L367–396. Saki Dear 010-01 L6–70.)

Both parents took part in another decision. When the Producer took the training plan that he and a trainer had prepared to them, he recalls a fierce argument about their daughter’s development. Ume can picture her parents quarreling, Saki calls them doting, and the Producer says the exchange improved the plan. None of those speakers assigns a particular side to the mother. The parents are individually identifiable elsewhere. At the family training center it is the father, the company president, who introduces himself, offers his books and debates who should be its public representative. Saki says it was her father who asked her for a filmed assessment of the academy’s machinery. When Ume later asks whether she has disappointed both parents by becoming an idol, it is again her father who distinguishes his hopes for himself from his support of his daughters. The story gives us work Ume describes and training her mother directly undertakes. How she would answer the question put to her husband remains open. (Ume Dear 003 L94–133, 025 L328–415, 026 L722–747. Support 3-0023 part 02 L78–104.)

## The moment a training problem becomes an artistic one

On Saki’s route, Saki and Tsubame have just competed against one another. Tsubame praises the stages in which she heard Saki find something of her own before the recent performance of *Wildest Flower*. She tells Saki to take her past with her. She fully intends to beat her next time. Going back to the older trainer does not dissolve that rivalry. Tsubame wants the lessons with Saki to continue because Saki makes her stand a little straighter. She says the mother’s care and training have already changed what she can do physically and that continued work could improve her dancing. The mother's quick response, “That’s it,” picks up an idea the pupil has just articulated. (Saki Dear 032 L31–94, L124–187, L372–431.)

The mother reminds them that Tsubame can already hold her own vocally against a top idol. The Producer adds that Saki has developed *Wildest Flower*. Then the mother names what they should work on next. She asks them to master an intermission dance more impressive than the “Ume Challenge” so that they can compete against Ume and Sena. There is a difference between aiming for a stronger stage and asking two young performers to reproduce a stronger athlete’s body. Tsubame thinks the disparity makes the assignment impossible. The mother turns to Saki, who has found ways to beat her sister despite that very disparity. She asks whether either of them is incapable of the acrobatic movements themselves. Tsubame thinks she could do something similar. She doubts she would look as good as Ume in a direct comparison. That second objection is more specific and makes another solution possible. (Saki Dear 032 L423–501.)

The solution has several authors. The mother selects the problem and asks what the two performers can already do. The Producer suggests that a staff can design an entire sequence and staging that surpass an improvised feat of raw power. Saki begins to wonder how the audience might react and whether the surprise will lift them. Tsubame calls the task outrageous and joins her in taking it on. There is no performance by the mother here and no completed result to attribute to her. What she does for their stage is worth describing in its own right. She hears one pupil make a diagnosis, draws on the other pupil’s competitive experience, and creates a problem to which the Producer, the staff and the performers can each contribute something different. Ume’s exceptional abilities are a benchmark in this scene. Neither the dialogue nor the proposed performance establishes her as an opponent in a feud with her own mother. (Saki Dear 032 L506–554, L563–595.)

The gym exchange also puts a limit on how uncomplicated her encouragement can look. Tsubame estimates that the pain ranks among the worst she has known. Saki asks her mother to go easier. Tsubame tells them she is fine, and the mother sends the answer back to Saki with a cheerful musical note in the text. Saki repeats the phrase in protest. Earlier, Tsubame explained that it would be difficult to leave while a younger colleague was enduring the treatment, that it genuinely helped, and that it could serve as a reminder of a defeat. Those are Tsubame’s reasons to stay, including her sense of obligation. Saki's objection preserves another response to what the same trainer is doing. A spirited exchange among competitors does not establish that either girl would welcome the same treatment in every situation. (Saki Dear 032 L187–259, L275–358.)

## A senior athlete who knows when she is the junior

The returning authority figures in Ume’s rehearsal room provide an answer to the question of what the mother meant by a “beginning” trainer. The academy’s visual trainer counts it a piece of good fortune to have worked with her. The dance trainer had been a fan and calls her a national hero. The vocal trainer says her reputation as a trainer in a different sport is well deserved and says she learned from observing her. The source leaves that sport undesignated here. These are judgments by three distinct people on an admired colleague. Ume herself has just watched those trainers take responsibility for the delayed completion of her song. They had continued to see room for improvement and found it hard to call the work finished. It is a staff with expertise and opinions of its own. (Ume Dear 034 L251–310, L322–331.)

The mother plainly enjoys being praised by this group. Her first printed response ends in a musical note. She goes on to say the honor was hers and to ask for their instruction in a much more formal register. She qualifies her claim to junior status with `アイドルトレーナーとしては`, “as an idol trainer.” Age, national recognition and earlier professional success settle little about who has the most to teach in this room. Nor does junior status make her passive. In Saki’s other route she can formulate a demanding assignment and hand the meeting over to the Producer to work out a plan. Here she can accept a compliment, acknowledge what the trainers know and leave the floor to Ume. Ume thanks her mother, the trainers and all of the staff together. Their achievement is that she feels ready to put herself before an audience. (Ume Dear 034 L342–377. Saki Dear 032 L354–369, L423–518.)

## How the recordings identify her

The named direct speaker is `花海姉妹の母`, the Hanami sisters’ mother. Her dialogue is carried in printed boxes while the camera often shows the people to whom she is speaking. At 27:24 of the registered Saki Dear 028–037 recording, the mother's name appears over an image of Tsubame as the text declines her deference. At 28:41 that name is still attached to the mother’s proposal while Tsubame is pictured. At 28:53 and 29:06 the picture shows Saki as the mother turns the question about physical ability toward the two performers. The difference between the person named in the text and the person in the center of the picture matters. Tsubame’s response to bodily care and Saki’s response to an artistic challenge are part of how the scene lets us know the older woman.

In Ume’s recording the mother’s name appears in turn over the vocal trainer at 33:28, the visual trainer at 33:31, the dance trainer at 33:34 and Ume at 33:38. The prior two complete-file observations describe these as unvoiced printed remarks. None of her thirteen named text intervals in Saki Dear 032 or four in Ume Dear 034 overlaps an individual voice command in the locked scripts. Her change from familiarity to professional courtesy is written and addressed. The sampled gestures, facial expressions and individual voices of the characters who surround her belong to those characters. The two recorded scenes give ample material for understanding how a trainer enters an idol performance from the rehearsal room. They offer no basis for assigning her a personal singing repertoire, a vocal manner or a distinct on-screen body in these shots.
