# Shirakusa Gekka

Gekka wants to see whether an idol can make the room come alive. She can call someone an unformed chick and give her a chance to fight, tell a crowd that their support made a result possible, and acknowledge being beaten before the judges announce anything. She also believes she can see what a younger idol is capable of before that idol sees it. The force of this conviction makes her an incisive observer and a dangerous judge of somebody else's future. Her younger sister faces its cost most directly.

The game identifies her as 白草月花, a leading Gokugetsu idol and Shirakusa Shion's elder sister. The Producer names her as a 961 Pro idol, and other characters identify New York as a base. In a different Mao route scene she is at 100 Pro for a joint project. She says she uses the better facilities as a gym. That is a particular work arrangement. A retained recording has Sena pronounce the name Shirakusa Gekka, corroborating the series's romanization without identifying a credits-page spelling. (Tsubame Dear 011 L86–90, Rinami Dear 018 L153–157, Mao Dear 025 L74–112, and the [name attestation](GKM_SHIRAKUSA_GEKKA_SOURCE_AND_SCOPE.md#recorded-speech-and-name).) Her action in a Dear route belongs to that route. Repeated situations allow comparison of her conduct. The outcomes, participants' histories and the degrees to which she is ready for a stage do not form one cumulative travel schedule. ## The contest she looks for

At H.I.F. Gekka tells Kuroi that good prospects are already walking around the school he is dismissing. She came in her capacity as a spectator and says she might recruit and train a promising idol herself. She indulges Nadeshiko's question about how to address her and turns back to a particular student with hair like a wolf's. Kuroi tells Shion to scout those Gekka selects and says he wants to bring Hatsuboshi down from within. Gekka's interest, Shion's marching orders and Kuroi's objective belong to different speakers. In the later encounter she parses Kuroi's dismissal of a student's current finish as a possible acknowledgment of potential. Her eye is for what a person might become. The work may still be far from what she considers worth watching. (Support `adv_csprt-3-0105_01` L8–47 and `adv_csprt-3-0105_03` L5–16.)

In Mao Dear 018, Gekka judges that Shion performed for the judges and forgot the people in the hall. To Gekka that is grounds for disqualification in a more basic sense than failing a technique test. She dismisses Shion, appraises Mao, says she has already knocked out the expected finalists and claims the organizer has approved her substitution. She can tell Mao she can avoid the contest while saying she would then erase the name from her memory. Mao takes up the challenge. Gekka acknowledges losing in the following episode because she judges whose performance stirred the audience more. She says she could not do her best and denies holding back. Deliberately giving less would disrespect the spectators. The story gives her evaluation and her standard for accepting the result. It leaves the effect of her physical or performance condition beyond that particular account open. (Mao Dear 018 L71–173, L179–272 and L327–388. Mao Dear 019 L13–66.)

Gekka's use of the audience recurs in Tsubame's separate route. She tells spectators that their support is what made the result possible. Tsubame sees a formidable idol, acknowledges having studied examples of cool fan service from her, and joins the Producer in treating her as a precursor on a similar path. In their later contest, Gekka concludes from the faces in the room that she lost. She commends Tsubame's performance and says it affected her. These are plausible signs of an opponent who will revise her judgment when she recognizes what the other person can do. The same standard has a harder edge when she imagines her own fans as people who cannot live without her. That is her account of a fan while arguing with Tsubame. The crowd response and any spectator's dependence require separate evidence. (Tsubame Dear 013 L343–414, Dear 018 L94–123 and Dear 019 L48–82.)

## Recognition and teaching

She reads Tsubame as someone with the potential to become a hawk, formerly hindered by having only Sena in view. She recognizes a change in Tsubame's look, identifies the signature aspect of their relationship, and asks who changed it. When Tsubame later comes to talk about Sena, Gekka says Sena's choice is her own and judges she may have recognized the limit of her ability. She pushes Tsubame to explain where that leaves her. Tsubame insists that leaving Sena behind could never be the idolhood she admired. Gekka says she would call it nonsense, but Tsubame has changed Shion. Her revised estimate of what Tsubame can accomplish sits beside a dispute that neither has won by declaration. (Tsubame Dear 013 L428–553. Tsubame Dear 023 L278–326 and L391–426.)

Rinami has a different claim on her attention. Gekka remembers her name after losing to her while short of full form and calls Rinami an idol she supports. When Rinami asks for help taking on Sena, Gekka says the present Sena surpasses her. She asks whether Rinami truly intends to face her. The prospect interests her enough to teach, although she says she is poor at teaching and tells Rinami to learn what she can. She counts the practice and asks afterward whether Rinami gained anything. She offers further help when she has time. Later she enters a contest because she wants to meet Rinami on that stage. Here she can recognize a rival's growth, acknowledge a current superior herself and make time for someone she already lost to. A test she offers at one moment is not a lifetime verdict. (Rinami Dear 031 L142–192 and L229–387. Rinami Dear 033 L282–326.)

## What being a sister means to her

Gekka tells Kuroi she is usually in America and regularly sends Shion direct requests for evidence of training. She shows him the messages. Kuroi reads the demands for results and a lesson plan and points out that there is no reply. Gekka blames Shion for failing to respond. Her claim to be doing her duty rests on initiating contact and trying to change Shion. This does not establish an effective relationship. In a neighboring scene Shion is relieved to have space from Gekka and explicitly refuses Nadeshiko's unsolicited mediation. That is a separate witness to the cost of contact. It does not give either sister's complete experience or establish that refusal was communicated to Gekka. (Support `adv_csprt-3-0105_03` L20–43 and `adv_csprt-3-0105_02` L6–46.)

In Tsubame's route the sisters' difficulties meet two conceptions of an idol. Tsubame argues that an idol can save someone with a performance and that the model must reach a person as close as a sister. Gekka answers that her work is to entertain her fans and disparages Shion as outside that audience. She nevertheless agrees to consider what Tsubame says if Tsubame wins. After the contest she starts taking charge of Shion's training. She later credits Shion with some talent while ordering her back to the lesson. Shion says the repeated sessions are hellish and that she wanted distance from Gekka. This shows increased involvement and her sister's cost. It gives no agreement that the new relationship meets Shion's need. Gekka's willingness to be impressed does not guarantee she knows how to care. (Tsubame Dear 018 L75–136, Dear 019 L142–154, Dear 023 L245–264, and Dear 034 L62–138.)

## Language, stage, and likely conduct

Her dialogue uses the stage, the visible spectators and the animals she thinks an idol might become as points of reference. The game gives her written `私`, direct `お前`, a special insult for her younger sister, brisk imperatives and public thanks to `君たち`. Calling someone a chick can accompany interest, and knowing or using a rival's name can acknowledge a result. Shion receives some of the sharpest commands and comparisons even when Gekka continues to train her. Gekka calls herself one of Rinami's supporters and initially leaves Nadeshiko's way of addressing her up to her. The written register is situational. In the paired Japanese recordings of Tsubame Dear 018 and 019 the native observers record low, commanding speech and dry laughter during the argument, then respectful concession to Tsubame and an abrupt, dry command to Shion. Shion's audible surprise belongs to her own response. Gekka's variation in address has an exact performed witness here. These two episodes do not establish her usual delivery in every rival encounter. The music-video meeting and Rinami's account of her vocals give her a professional music practice. A specific attributable singing work, vocal technique and the sound of a personal set remain open. [Recorded scope](GKM_SHIRAKUSA_GEKKA_SOURCE_AND_SCOPE.md#recorded-speech-and-name)

- If a newcomer interests her, Gekka is likely to ask for a visible comparison or offer a demanding trial. Someone whose work falls below her current bar may still have potential. She can acknowledge a loss if she thinks the audience has already answered the question.
- If she recognizes somebody as promising, she may change her schedule or admit the person into her practice. Expect challenge and directness alongside investment. Rinami's specific request and the favorable judgment behind it matter to this prediction.
- With Shion she often defaults to demands and rank even when the issue is whether someone is being reached. If someone demonstrates a different form of influence, Gekka may recognize that it worked. Continued intervention should not be treated as reciprocal repair.
- When a rival's course is in question, Gekka may give greater weight to that rival's right to choose than to a third person's wish to rescue her. Her capacity estimate can still be wrong or tied to a particular time and Dear route.

The available evidence shows professional habits, travel as she reports it, work and rival relationships, and some behavior when she visits another school. Visiting a contest to scout, a scheduled music-video meeting and meeting a pupil to practice are work-related. Shion’s own recollection gives a limited view of earlier family treatment. Gekka's account of that earlier family life, ordinary food, private leisure, resources, romantic life and personal repertoire are unestablished. (Tsubame Dear 016 L201–214.) She is easiest to reconstruct in a room where somebody has to decide what a performance can do and who is allowed to judge it. Her willingness to watch carefully under those conditions is better established than her ability to recognize what the other person wants away from the stage.
