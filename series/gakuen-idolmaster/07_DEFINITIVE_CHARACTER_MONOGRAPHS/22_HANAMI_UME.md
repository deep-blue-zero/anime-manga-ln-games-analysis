## A person who gives herself something to reach

Hanami Ume (花海佑芽) is an athlete who asks unfamiliar people to come along. She brings an athlete’s body and little idol technique to Hatsuboshi. On meeting her Producer for the first time, she decides he is the person she wants. She can be the loudest person in a room and privately worry for a long time that somebody she respects regrets having chosen her. She builds commitments around a specific person, tests them in action and likes a result she can share or contest. Once she finds the pattern of an unfamiliar skill, the terms of a match can change quickly. The first impression of thoughtless confidence misses both the learning and the fear.

The first object of pursuit is Saki. Being her sister makes Ume capable of trusting an exacting standard, learning from a loss and thinking about the next contest before a celebration is over. It also gives her an old reason to fear that a person she loves can leave the activity before she catches up. Ume's first route grows by making that attachment answerable to a choice of her own. Each sister says she would continue as an idol if the other left. For Ume, fans, the Producer and love of performing have become reasons to continue. Saki asks Ume for another match. D-UME Dear 001 L191–228, 003 L194–228, 004, 006, 009 L56–106 and 019 L92–262.

There is a harder side to this ethic. Ume can approve of somebody burning through herself for a worthy ending, feel threatened by being replaced in somebody's attention or turn an ordinary request into a race. The mature case depends on learning that competition can give back the heat it consumes, and that the opponent has to stay free to do something unforeseen. Whether she can accept a loved person's decision to stop competing altogether remains a separate question.

The sisters’ recollection in support 3-0052 part 01 gives Ume a way to describe what has changed. She recalls that entering the same school let her follow Saki. Her earlier excitement about attending the same school was real and did not yet identify a specific wish to be an idol. She says the present question matters enough that she wants to work out her own answer. She says this just after affirming that she trusts her sister. The account gives no date for her first independent aspiration. It makes a way of receiving reassurance intelligible without transferring to Saki the right to determine what Ume wants. ## Reconstructing her in an unfamiliar situation

- Give her a reason to take a first step, such as a rival, a friend’s ordinary wish or a task she wants to try. She can make a fast commitment from observation and intuition. Where the stakes merit it, let her gather information, revise her oral explanation and make her own career decision. A bad beginning is compatible with real skill acquisition after visible practice.
- Assign the source of fear. Losing another round has a different meaning from being left behind, fearing that the Producer might prefer Saki, or not knowing what remains after winning. She may first joke or propose a rematch. Give her room to ask the pointed question she has been avoiding, particularly with Saki, the Producer or her parents.
- On a day away from idol work, an informal visit, manga or a shared practical task can engage her. She can be an insistent friend while a companion values time or effort differently. Sports and progress talk are available without assuming that she has no quiet time.
- In a fight or rehearsal distinguish helping, copying and taking charge. She studies what an opponent did, can put it bodily into practice, and responds to people who can ask for a serious performance. When a peer slows her down or a senior worries about safety, her admiration alone does not establish compliance.
- Maintain personal roles. Saki can be a dear rival and practical carer. Hiro and China give reciprocal friendship and distinct techniques. Rinha has an audience and a future she can decide herself. Sena alternates between challenger, scout or actual producer according to track. The Dear Producer has a concrete place in her fears and professional decisions. “I love you” cannot be lifted out of who heard it, with which choice and under which accepted expectations.

Ume's claim to be independent is strongest where she defines a future she would choose even if an important companion took another path. It is still conditional. A worthy rival still gives her a reason to return to practice and a person with whom to share the result. Available evidence does not yet tell us whether she will find a satisfying rhythm when neither recovery nor a next rival makes the immediate direction obvious.
