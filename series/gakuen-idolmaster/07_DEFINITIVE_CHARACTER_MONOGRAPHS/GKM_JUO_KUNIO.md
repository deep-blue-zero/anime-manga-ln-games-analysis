# Juo Kunio

Juo Kunio is the headmaster of Hatsuboshi Academy and Sena Juo’s grandfather. He makes institutional work feel personal. He greets a new Producer student with a laugh and a challenge, asks a struggling candidate to aim at the top, and can turn a request from another grandfather into an order with a deadline. Those acts come from a man confident in what he sees in people and willing to use his authority. When he tells a would-be Producer that he believes in them, the belief is often attached to something very difficult he has just asked them to do. The trust inside that gesture is genuine enough for him to risk opportunities on people. The resulting work and risk generally fall on those people. [K1, K3–K8]

This portrait covers the game’s admitted tutorial, selected Dear routes, song communications, support and event stories, Produce branches and Unit Story. It contains spoilers for their competition outcomes. Each story keeps its own outcome. The largest absences are prior biography and private life, detailed performance evidence for his own voice, and knowledge of what he does away from the work and family relationships shown here. The available sources let a reader distinguish his judgment and interpersonal conduct from those of a generic benevolent headmaster. They support no comprehensive account of his inner life.

## An educator with an industry past

Kunio introduces himself as 十王邦夫, with the reading じゅうおうくにお, and as Hatsuboshi’s 学園長. He describes two simultaneous obligations for a Producer Course student. The student is still being educated and is already responsible for finding and developing an idol. He presents the course as an initiative the school promotes and leaves the practical orientation to the class teacher, Asari Neo. She greets the new student by name. The school mission Kunio voices and the personal teaching she supplies are different kinds of work. His own industry past enters the admitted record through Asari’s report that his son inherited 100 Pro from the headmaster and continued to grow the company. That gives Kunio a prior position in the agency and leaves the details of how he gained, exercised or transferred the position unknown. [K1–K2]

Kunio names education as a way to make a great idol, and he expects professional work to put education under pressure. In the China Dear route, he says Sena had lessons from age three and calls her the finest idol education might produce. He can distinguish individual raw capacity from accumulated preparation even while making an unhelpful comparison between the two girls’ underlying talent. In the cover-live event he commends Saki, Ume and Kotone to a professional opportunity alongside Sena’s recommendation. The event staff asked for them, and Kunio’s explanation stresses respect for 765 Pro, ability to make the show succeed and reliability on assignment. Enjoying competitive novelty and entrusting paid or professional work have their own criteria. He conveys the choice of song with enthusiasm, but the scene does not give him personal authorship of the choice. [K6, K12]

## What he sees in an unlikely student

At Hiro Shinosawa’s interview Kunio says she has prospects and invites her to aim to become a top idol. In Hiro’s Dear 001, the prospective Producer says her practical score was zero. Hiro asks if full written marks explain her admission. The Producer says that even so, the usual result would have been rejection. The interview is recalled by Hiro and staged as a headmaster line in the script. It strongly supports his backing an exception. It does not establish how many people signed the admission or exactly which rule he invoked. The Producer cannot see an idol career for her, and Hiro thinks he is right in a meaningful respect. Kunio does not supply a technical refutation of that assessment. [K3]

In a separate card communication, Hiro seeks his judgment precisely because he gave her the strongest assessment at interview. He answers that her attractiveness lies in her capacity to surprise. One wants to see what she does next, even after the qualifications that once explained why people watched her no longer organize her life. That is a discriminatingly personal judgment. It still says little about her physical limits, how she will practice or the Producer’s responsibilities for her health. His curiosity grants permission to develop an unconventional talent and is a poor substitute for the more exacting work of teaching it. In the Halloween communication Hiro startles Kunio as a prank. A school announcement summons her Producer. Later he brings Hiro a cake from Kunio and conveys Kunio’s promise that he will be harder to surprise next time. The scene is a small test of what Kunio means by liking unpredictability. He can be at the receiving end of it, involve Hiro’s student Producer and prolong the joke through a gift. The cake is evidence of this gesture, with no finding about Kunio’s own tastes. [K4–K5]

## Old friends, family pride and the boundary of a favor

When Kuramoto China enters the Idol Course, Kunio entrusts her to the Producer after a request from her grandfather, whom he calls an old friend. His D-CHINA report on the first deadline is unusually candid. He praises the Producer’s long preparation plan, yet passes on a demand for China to hold the academy’s Prima Stella title that very summer. The two grandfathers had quarreled over whose granddaughter was cuter after Kunio had provoked his friend with praise of Sena. Kunio calls Sena his pride and says how dear she is to him. His success at getting a reaction from his friend has a cost. The Producer and China are asked to make a competitive outcome out of a dispute they did not start. The Producer privately points out the absurdity of comparing two supposed talents when one has received years of specialist lessons. Kunio’s promise of belief carries both confidence and the means to avoid doing the impossible assignment himself. [K6]

The support story with Sena as China’s would-be Producer tests this behavior against the opposite teaching plan. When Sena proposes work at once while continuing fundamentals, Kunio asks whether that is too soon. He then relays the same summer demand. Sena later calls the other grandfather’s request unreasonable, while China calls her out for giving an extremely unready student immediate work. Kunio has enough practical judgment to question haste and still passes on the pressure that encourages it. In the shared event where he asks Sena to take responsibility for China, he gives her some discretion over what that will mean. The authorization for Sena to produce is reported by her in a separate scene. If her activity conflicts with the Producer Course, she must yield. She cannot bind an idol by contract, lacks the course’s privileges, and faces limits on facilities. Kunio converts family trust into an opportunity for Sena, but she herself describes it as subordinate authority. Tsubame thinks that boundary appropriate and Rinami thinks it may still be generous for this particular person. [K7, K9]

The dispute changes form in D-CHINA. After China challenges her grandfather, Kunio returns with a request for the next H.I.F. and says the family wants no penalty attached. The Producer calls the accompanying signed message heavy. Kunio estimates it at about fifty grams. The joke describes the space between formal removal of the penalty and pressure the recipient still feels. Later Kunio reports that he refused the grandfather’s demand for a judge’s seat and succeeded in talking him out of it. He suspects the Producer encouraged the request. The Producer denies it in language that makes the possibility of retaliation intelligible, but the source supplies no admission. Kunio simultaneously treats expensive, family-funded songs as legitimate weapons China can bring to the contest. He also distinguishes China’s strength as a celebrity from whether she can beat Sena in this competition. He calls the Producer’s trust in China a mark of excellent production and says the H.I.F. contains no flukes or miracles. His practical line permits resources an idol actually deploys and draws a boundary at a family member acquiring official power over the verdict. The distinction preserves the possibility of legitimate achievement while leaving the inequality of access plainly visible. [K8]

## N.I.A. and the pleasure of being underestimated

In Sena’s Dear 012 the Producer asks about entering Sena in N.I.A., NEXT IDOL AUDITION. Kunio allows it. He says Kuroi and his own son planned the competition without him. He portrays the omission as a slight he has held onto and an attractive event he resents being kept out of. When the situation around Gekka Shirakusa gives him an opening, he advises announcing Sena’s entry before Kuroi can evade it. Kunio laughs at the picture of Kuroi’s furious reaction. The inserted Kuroi line belongs to the imagined response and cannot show how the other man actually received the news. Kunio then says four people will take the criticism. The Producer supplies Kuroi, Kunio and President Juo. Kunio names the student Producer as the fourth. It is protective in one sense. He says he will stand in public with the student. It also drafts the student into responsibility for a quarrel between adults with far greater power. [K10]

Sena’s communication identifies President Juo as her father, Kunio as her grandfather, and treats the reported disagreement as an ordinary parent–child quarrel. In Mao’s Dear 021 the executive introduces himself as Juo Ryuusei, representative director of 100 Pro. Those anchors establish the family and business identities. Ryuusei is also the strongly supported referent of “my son” in Dear 012 because of the immediate exchange about the four people and the later account. Kunio gives the planning allegation rather than an independently observed account of deliberations. The records do not inventory his children, confine all his interests to Hatsuboshi, or settle the actual ownership and governance of the event. [K2, K10–K11]

Having urged the announcement, Kunio still says N.I.A. must remain a contest for new idols. He expects criticism if the two established stars enter. The Producer predicts a rule adjustment and suggests a separate duel for the established idols. Sena understands that the newcomer competition would still have a winner of its own. Kunio assigns the dual task of winning her own match and developing a junior who can win the newcomer competition. The exchange establishes his intention and challenge, while whether and how the format changes belongs to a later outcome. Office, grudges and a real educational purpose all operate in the same decision. The evidence supports reading him as someone who likes the game of influence, can see when part of its cost needs absorbing, and can use authority to keep a professional developmental distinction intact. It leaves open whether he saw the newcomers’ interests before the opportunity to embarrass Kuroi presented itself. [K10]

## Ceremony and evaluation

Across divergent winter Dear outcomes, Kunio praises the selected competitors, acknowledges the audience and speaks for the institution as the prize is transferred. In Sena’s route he names Sena. In Hiro’s and China’s routes he names the respective heroine and identifies Sena as the incumbent. In Saki’s he names Saki and identifies Ume as incumbent. A Produce failure branch for Sena has him name Kotone. These utterances preserve the headmaster’s public function across outcomes. They do not join the results together or make him the unilateral chooser. His pride in his granddaughter therefore cannot explain every decision he announces. [K13]

The Unit Story adds a harder measure of judgment. In the H.I.F. unit result he lists the members’ singing, dance and expression and judges Begrazia to be stronger overall. He says the evaluators were divided because Re;IRIS moved them despite that difference. The decision he voices after deliberation values who made the others, including rivals, shine more brightly and awards Re;IRIS the win. He gives the criterion and the dissent a hearing as part of the ceremony. No route establishes whether he was the deciding vote or whether he consistently held that criterion before deliberation. Read with Hiro’s admission, the scene shows why unusually good outcomes may matter to him even when a conventional table of skill favors somebody else. Read beside the China deadlines, it also shows why his judgments cannot be called reliably fair simply because he can articulate a defensible result. [K14]

## His speech in the room

Kunio’s written register supplies `わし` for himself, `おぬし` for a familiar addressee below or across his authority, `じゃ` and related forms, and emphatic commands such as `せい` or `みせい`. He uses surname-and-given-name plus `君` for students in formal acknowledgment, bare `星南` for his granddaughter in direct counsel, `若きプロデューサー候補生` or `プロデューサー` to confer a professional role, and `倉本爺` for his old friend. `黒井の小僧` is his own dismissive way of referring to Kuroi in the account of a rivalry. The laugh opens the tutorial even before his title appears in the nameplate. He breaks off while asking about the student’s name and moves straight to an acknowledgment. The script supplies no intervening answer and he never speaks the name. Pleasantry can be a short preface to an imperative, while formal ceremony uses a shared `我々` when he speaks as a representative. His interruptions, dollops of praise and switches to orders are matters of rhetoric. One should hear the account of the renamed deadlines and the dry fifty-gram joke before reducing him to a constant roar. [K1, K4, K6, K8, K10, K12–K14]

## Limits and expected conduct

The narrative establishes an older man with a long working relationship to Hatsuboshi and 100 Pro, a proud grandparent, a man capable of accepting a joke at his own expense, and someone who readily invites young people into high-stakes work. His family proximity and his former position tell us something about influence. They do not quantify his assets, physical health, daily home routine, dietary preferences or past marriage. The materials give no basis for reconstructing his romantic and intimate life. Inviting the unlikely student or enjoying the rival’s discomfiture shows an appetite for surprise inside his work, with no implied pattern of private hobbies. The sources show an accumulation of tests of a working disposition. They do not securely depict a personality change from the initial welcome through the late awards. [K1–K14]

- **A surprising candidate with poor measured skill.** He may notice a reason people will keep watching and authorize a promising start. His practical criteria are firmer when a defined job needs reliable completion. Expect neither a universal exception nor a lesson plan from his enthusiasm alone.
- **An established figure asks for help.** He is disposed to route the request through the Producer or student he trusts, introduce it with familiarity, and then expect results. Relative privilege and the recipient’s capacity to refuse matter. Distinguish favors involving resources from favors involving the decision itself.
- **Another institution tries to outmaneuver him.** He may remember the slight, seize a public opening and enjoy the likely reaction. He can share exposure and put explicit boundaries around the new opportunity. He is not thereby able to command every organizer.
- **A family member loses a public competition.** Known alternate outcomes support a formal, affirmative announcement of a different winner. Private consolation, lobbying habits outside these cases and how he handles his own long-term loss remain unknown.
- **A familiar person catches him off guard.** Playful recovery is plausible in a low-stakes situation like Hiro’s prank. The answer need not rhyme with a high-stakes institutional response. The evidence for private wit does not extend to all intimacy.

The supplementary qualification rests on unusually well-differentiated conduct toward a new student, an unorthodox candidate, a peer sponsor, a granddaughter, an adult rival and a public audience. A reviewer should test whether the portrait still works without the diagnostic examples from one route. New source material could materially change the account of his earlier agency career, how the discretion over admissions is shared, his home life, his actual delivery in named scenes, or how the school song was written and used in his prior career.
