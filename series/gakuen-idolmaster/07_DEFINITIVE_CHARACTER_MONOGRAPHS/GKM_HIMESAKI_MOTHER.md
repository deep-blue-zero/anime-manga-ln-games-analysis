# Himesaki Rinami’s mother

Just before a performance, Himesaki Rinami answers a call from her father’s phone and discovers it is her mother. Her mother says she wanted to hear Rinami’s voice, then offers to call another time. Rinami suspects that something has happened to her father. Twice she presses for an answer. When Rinami says she can tell from her mother’s voice that something is wrong, her mother stops putting off the news. She asks Rinami to listen calmly and tells her that her father is in the hospital. Here, as at other moments in her daughter’s career, she cares about what Rinami is about to take on and has to decide how much to say about it. [The hospital call](GKM_HIMESAKI_MOTHER_SOURCE_AND_SCOPE.md#hospital-and-household)

## The request to go to Hatsuboshi

In the scene that opens the final part of the school-festival event, Rinami remembers telling both parents that she wants to attend Hatsuboshi Academy. Her mother tells her to settle down and asks whether she is serious. Rinami says she is and volunteers the judgments she has received about her singing. Her father separately asks if she has thought it through and tells her she can always come home if things get hard. Her mother asks the first question, then the scene moves to the exchange with her father. Her answer to the proposed enrollment is outside the scene. [Asking to go](GKM_HIMESAKI_MOTHER_SOURCE_AND_SCOPE.md#school-family-and-earlier-memories)

Rinami tells the story differently depending on what she is explaining. To her friend Arimura Mao she says she prevailed on both parents to let her attend. In a separate conversation, she says that her father had expected her to take over work at the family brewery. When she finally told him she wanted to become an idol, he encouraged her to do what she loved most and persuaded her mother as well. The daughter remembers needing both parents’ agreement. Her mother’s question shows that she takes the decision seriously. Her reason for needing persuasion and the words with which she eventually agreed remain unknown. The accounts do not fix when those discussions took place in relation to the scene at the dining-room table. [Recollections of the decision](GKM_HIMESAKI_MOTHER_SOURCE_AND_SCOPE.md#school-family-and-earlier-memories)

## Caring from home

Rinami’s younger sister Shiori asks whether she is eating the honeyed pickled plums their mother sent. Shiori says they help with fatigue and are good for the throat. Rinami says she is gratefully eating them. The gift gives her mother a practical way to look after a daughter living elsewhere. In that same conversation Shiori describes their father watching live streams, making fan goods for the neighbors and using Rinami’s recorded voice as an alarm. Shiori tells her school friends. The plums give their mother a distinct place in the family response. The reason for choosing them and whether she made them herself are left unstated. Rinami also remembers being told off by her mother after eating a different, red-tongue-staining plum sweet when she was young. That small recollection gives an instance of ordinary correction alongside the later gift. [The plums and the household](GKM_HIMESAKI_MOTHER_SOURCE_AND_SCOPE.md#school-family-and-earlier-memories)

Rinami remembers her parents often being away at work when she was young, and watching anime in the evenings with Shiori. In another conversation she remembers crying around the time Shiori was born because she felt that neither parent was paying attention to her anymore. That is how Rinami remembers the change. The stories give no account from her mother of how she divided her time, or of what infant care looked like in the household. What work she did and who took responsibility for the brewery beyond Rinami’s account of her father’s plans are also unknown. [The childhood accounts](GKM_HIMESAKI_MOTHER_SOURCE_AND_SCOPE.md#school-family-and-earlier-memories)

## What she tells Rinami before a performance

When her mother first tries to defer the call, she checks whether the examination is important. Rinami’s direct request changes the conversation. Her mother explains that the accident happened that morning, that it involved a bicycle, and that her father is now in surgery. She reports the doctor’s assessment that his life is not in danger. She gives a reason for reassurance by saying she is there with him, then apologizes for the timing. Her initial hesitation and the specifics that follow are compatible with wanting to protect her daughter. They also show that she can give her daughter information she might rather postpone when Rinami insists on hearing it. The call does not explain how long she had planned to wait or whether she intended to tell her without being asked. Rinami later gives Sena her own judgment about the loss. She says the accident mattered and that her insecurity would probably have kept her from beating Sena anyway. [Before and after the live](GKM_HIMESAKI_MOTHER_SOURCE_AND_SCOPE.md#hospital-and-household)

Later, Rinami’s mother reports a week of prescribed rest for observation and says she will be going to the hospital for a while. Rinami answers that she will handle the house and look after Shiori. Her mother establishes what she herself can take on, and Rinami volunteers a way of making that possible. The moment shows how care can move in either direction in this family. It does not establish that the mother ordered Rinami to step in, that the arrangement lasted, or that she expected Rinami to give up idol work to keep the house going. [Hospital and household](GKM_HIMESAKI_MOTHER_SOURCE_AND_SCOPE.md#hospital-and-household)

## Watching the girl who chose the work

Before the main H.I.F. contest, Rinami tells her Producer that she is going to become Prima Stella. Shiori lets her know that her family can see her. The Producer says he has connected the camera. Her mother addresses Rinami by name and says she does not know much about idols, but that she is rooting for her. Her father follows with his own reflection about the child reluctant to ask for things and asks Rinami to pursue her choice without regrets. Her mother’s words put a limit on what she claims to understand. She is still willing to see and encourage Rinami at the moment that choice matters. The scene shows the family witnessing a conversation that Rinami initially thought was private. It gives no indication that her mother set up the connection or decided to keep it from Rinami. [The family camera](GKM_HIMESAKI_MOTHER_SOURCE_AND_SCOPE.md#support-without-expertise)

A portrait covering only the admission question could make her seem disposed to stop a risky choice. A portrait covering only the camera call could make her seem willing to go along with any decision. Across the admitted scenes her way of taking care is more specific. She checks whether Rinami means what she is saying, sometimes tries to manage when difficult information reaches her, and can answer frankly when Rinami asks plainly. Once the choice is Rinami’s, there is direct evidence that she is willing to support her daughter in work she does not know well. In a comparable family emergency, asking about timing and conveying concrete information once pressed are better grounded expectations than either habitual secrecy or immediate disclosure. What she does with Shiori privately, what she says to her husband away from the children and how she conducts herself at work are outside the portrait’s evidence.

The written role uses Rinami’s given name and sometimes refers to herself as “Mum.” In the hospital call she also says “I” when stating that she is there, and quotes the doctor with a respectful verb. The changing pauses and degree of directness are written into those exchanges. Four scripts give her thirteen dialogue boxes and no attached character-voice commands. Paired observers also found the school-request line presented as unvoiced text in the specific Event 028 recording. Rinami has already identified her mother by the time she says she can tell from her voice that something is wrong. That is Rinami’s statement within the scene. The admitted evidence does not give this portrait her personal name, an actor credit, a heard vocal register or a sung part.
