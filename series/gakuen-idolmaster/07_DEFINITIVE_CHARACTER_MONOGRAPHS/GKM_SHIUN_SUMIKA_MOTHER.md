# Shiun Sumika’s mother

The scripts identify the woman as Sumika’s mother. They give her no personal name or separately assigned voice, and little account of her life away from her daughter. She calls Sumika after a long stretch in which they have barely spoken. It comes late, when Sumika and Katsuragi Lilja are together in their dorm room. Sumika says she has barely spoken to her mother since coming to school. Lilja urges her to answer. Her mother starts with a question anyone in the family might ask over the phone, “Sumika?” Then she congratulates her on becoming Prima Stella. Sumika’s first response is to ask how she knows. Her mother had objected to this career. She has nevertheless kept up with what her daughter has done. In this Sumika-winning story the title gives her something definite to say when she calls. It is the performance she has seen that she will credit with changing her understanding. [The call and its setting](GKM_SHIUN_SUMIKA_MOTHER_SOURCE_AND_SCOPE.md#the-call-after-the-win)

## Before the entrance examination

A support story puts her in the company of both girls while Hatsuboshi is still the school they hope will admit them. Lilja arrives in Japan and greets both Sumika and her mother. The two students have kept in touch and can help each other with different subjects. Lilja asks for help with Japanese writing. Sumika asks Lilja to help her with mathematics, and Lilja agrees to a last review together. When Sumika admits she is anxious about maths, Lilja reassures her that there will also be a practical test. Sumika’s mother interrupts. She addresses Lilja affectionately as リーリヤちゃん and tells her not to spoil Sumika, who, she says, is quick to slack off. Sumika tells her mother to stay out of their conversation. [The entrance-exam support](GKM_SHIUN_SUMIKA_MOTHER_SOURCE_AND_SCOPE.md#before-enrollment)

She intervenes while the girls are preparing for an examination they have already decided to take. She hears reassurance and steps in with a warning. The two girls have already identified their weaknesses and arranged to help one another. Her daughter has grounds to resent the interruption, quite apart from whether the mother has ever seen her put off studying. The warning gives us the mother’s view of Sumika on this occasion. The other practical detail is her presence when Lilja arrives. Sumika’s mother can turn up for the girls while remaining a difficult person to have in the conversation.

## What her daughter remembers

There is a more sustained history behind that irritation. Sumika tells her Producer that she began ballet at a parent’s prompting and came to enjoy it. She leaves open whether one parent or both prompted her. Recalling her childhood in Hokkaido, she says that on the occasions she travelled to Tokyo for a competition, her mother went with her. Later she lived with an uncle in Sweden and found she had more freedom than at home. The Producer asks if home felt confining. Sumika gives him silence. These accounts place her mother beside her during some of the work. They leave open which parent first proposed ballet, what the mother gave up to make the trips, and whether she imagined a particular career for herself or her daughter. [Sumika’s accounts](GKM_SHIUN_SUMIKA_MOTHER_SOURCE_AND_SCOPE.md#what-sumika-reports)

After the injury in Sweden, Sumika returned to Japan. In Event 020 she tells Lilja that she started dressing like a gyaru partly in rebellion against her mother’s nagging. Friends and magazines taught her more, until the style felt natural to her. In her Dear story Sumika remembers that telling her parents she wanted to become an idol was frightening. Those memories establish how a daughter experienced her family’s expectations. The later call adds the mother’s own admission about one specific dispute. It also gives Sumika an opportunity to reconsider what she brought to their quarrels. [Style, ballet and the decision to tell her parents](GKM_SHIUN_SUMIKA_MOTHER_SOURCE_AND_SCOPE.md#what-sumika-reports)

## Asking a different kind of permission

When Sumika reminds her that she opposed the idol plan, her mother agrees. She says she gave permission reluctantly once Sumika’s father backed his daughter. That is the mother’s account of the disagreement, including the father’s position. He has no speaking turn in the scripted call. The mother goes on to say what changed for her. “I saw your live.” Sumika privately identifies it as the live where she disclosed what she had kept secret. Her mother does not recite the disclosure back to her. What she says she has finally understood is the feeling with which Sumika first said she wanted to be an idol. She apologizes for the harsh things she said. [Apology and reply](GKM_SHIUN_SUMIKA_MOTHER_SOURCE_AND_SCOPE.md#the-call-after-the-win)

Sumika apologizes in turn. Unsure how to face ballet, she says, she took her uncertainty out on her mother. Then she makes a decision clear. She loved ballet, its lessons still matter to her, and she will pursue her dream as an idol. Her mother asks whether she, too, may support her. The sequence gives the exchange its force. The woman who describes herself as having once granted reluctant permission now asks a question her daughter can answer. Sumika tells her she needs no permission and asks her to keep watching. The apology makes room for a continued relationship in which Sumika can name what she will do. It settles neither the entire history of their disagreements nor every argument they might have again.

The mother’s next request is ordinary. However busy Sumika is, she ought to come home to Hokkaido occasionally. Sumika offers to bring Lilja next time. Her mother also passes on the news that Sumika’s father is downhearted because he thinks his daughter dislikes him. Sumika remembers the chat message she sent him and realizes she forgot to follow it up. We see the invitation and Sumika’s intention to visit. The story ends before that visit or the repair with her father. What the mother has accomplished on this particular night is to reopen contact directly and make herself answerable to her daughter for her own words.
