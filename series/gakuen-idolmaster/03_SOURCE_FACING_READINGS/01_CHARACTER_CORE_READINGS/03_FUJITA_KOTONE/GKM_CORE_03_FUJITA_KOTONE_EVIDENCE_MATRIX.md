# FUJITA KOTONE — EVIDENCE AND FALSIFICATION MATRIX

## 0. Use of this document

This matrix prevents the Phase-3 reading from hardening into an unfalsifiable character essay. Each claim is provisional and may later be strengthened, narrowed, revised, or rejected by numbered events, support-card stories, Re;IRIS synthesis, other character cores, or direct audiovisual evidence.

Evidence classes used here:

- **A1** — raw Japanese ADV directly inspected;
- **A2/A3** — dialogue extract / complete analysis bundle;
- **U1** — separate Unit Story track, comparative only unless corroborated;
- **AV-PENDING** — textual hypothesis awaiting audio/video inspection.

Continuity warning: `P1[KOTONE]`, `P2[KOTONE]`, `D-KOTONE`, `C-KOTONE`, `PE-KOTONE`, and `U1` are not automatically additive.

# 3. Producer relationship claims

## 3.1 Strongest evidence for reciprocity

- Kotone repeatedly initiates date/flirt/exclusivity language.
- She asks the Producer not to obtain another partner until her retirement.
- She seeks more intimate naming.
- In the Re;IRIS dream comparison, she explicitly prefers the present Producer.
- She asks him to remain after H.I.F. loss when professional reframing fails.
- The Producer builds plans around her own stated money/top-idol desires rather than replacing them with a purer motive.

## 3.2 Strongest evidence for asymmetry

- He sets rest/work limits.
- He controls or strongly shapes schedule and production opportunities.
- He accesses household financial information and arranges refinancing support.
- He defines target conditions and competitive milestones.
- His praise becomes a temporary substitute for Kotone's own confidence.
- His final repayment request can be read as claiming the whole idol career as reciprocal obligation.

## 3.3 Falsification priority

Later sources should be searched specifically for:

1. Kotone saying **no** to a Producer plan and being respected;
2. Producer error that Kotone diagnoses better than he does;
3. gifts/care Kotone receives without repayment framing;
4. non-Producer relationships capable of carrying her through failure;
5. whether romantic exclusivity ever interferes with professional or peer freedom.

# 5. Performance and audience claims

## 5.1 Textually strongest anchors

- Dear 016: fan ownership becomes irrelevant during performance.
- Dear 018: audience enjoyment + being praised as cute + good work + fee.
- Dear 024: expensive ticket creates obligation that everyone leave happy.
- `Yellow Big Bang！`: technically excellent work is insufficient if Kotone's own enjoyment has vanished.
- `GO MY WAY!!`: anyone entering the venue becomes “my fan” in the sense that Kotone owes them value.

## 5.2 Counter-reading to preserve

A skeptical reading could argue that this is **customer-service capitalism**, not a transcendent performance ethic: the paying customer must receive satisfaction.

That reading is not entirely wrong. Kotone's ethic is materially service-oriented. The reason “hospitality” is presently the better term is that the text also emphasizes her own enjoyment, audience energy returning to her, and dissolution of preexisting fan ownership. It is reciprocal rather than one-way customer appeasement.

The AV pass should test which side dominates in performance staging.

# 6. Cuteness and persona claims

## Working proposition

> Kotone's cute image is a practiced interface that increasingly includes rather than excludes her greed, weakness, vulgarity, and ambition.

Strongest textual supports:

- `世界一可愛い私` explicitly allows weak/greedy traits into the song;
- fanservice training treats intimacy as scalable craft;
- she alternates sugary orthography with rough colloquial speech without narrative “reveal” that one invalidates the other;
- `Yellow Big Bang！` requires authentic visible enjoyment inside polished performance.

Primary counterevidence to seek:

- scenes where public cuteness causes alienation or dissociation;
- explicit statements that she hates the cute brand;
- conflict between profitable cute work and a desired non-cute artistic mode;
- voice acting that marks cute mode as acoustically strained/exhausting.

# 7. Japanese voice and translation cautions

| Japanese feature | current function | translation risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `おカネ` | stylized recurrence of financial worldview | flattening to generic “money” loses orthographic persona texture |
| `成り上がる` | upward social/economic ascent with self-made/vulgar energy | “be successful” is too neutral; “get rich” may omit status ascent |
| `普通` repetition | ordinary process as achieved luxury | stylistic smoothing would erase the point |
| elongated cute forms (`～っ`, `～～`, hearts) | performative cuteness, teasing, fanservice, affection | normalizing to plain English loses register switching |
| rough colloquial forms | blunt peer/private texture | over-translating as delinquent speech could exaggerate class coding |
| repayment/payment metaphors | intimacy made morally legible through exchange | translating all as generic gratitude erases material logic |
| `スター性` | nonquantified social/star effect | “charisma” is useful but may prematurely close meaning |
| `ライバル` vs `後継者` | reciprocity versus inherited teleology | both as “rival/successor” are semantically easy but structurally crucial |
