# GKM CORE 04 — ARIMURA MAO / 有村麻央

## 0. Executive thesis

Arimura Mao can be summarized too easily as the princely girl who must learn that she is cute. The corpus itself repeatedly invites that reading and then makes it inadequate.

The strongest Phase-3 formulation is:

> **Arimura Mao is the idol of reclaimed aspiration. She learns not to stop performing the prince, but to stop using the prince to disappear.**

Mao's childhood dream is not a superficial preference for masculine clothing. She sees a musical-theatre prince under the spotlight, decides that she too will become a prince, enters child acting, and for a time experiences a near-perfect correspondence between body, assigned role, admired image, and desired self. She later describes that period as one in which she was `無敵` and could remain the ideal Mao.

Puberty breaks that correspondence. Her body becomes more visibly female, her chest develops, she does not grow as tall as she hoped, and the industry stops casting her in the boy/prince roles through which the dream had once been materially possible. What follows is not simply gender nonconformity and not simply adolescent body dissatisfaction. It is a **role-category injury**: Mao mistakes the historically convenient form in which her aspiration had been recognized for the substance of the aspiration itself.

She therefore tries to preserve the lost category by fixing herself inside it. Boyish clothes, black coffee, rejection of sweets and plush toys, rigid cool poses, resistance to cute styling, avoidance of the transformed actress she once admired, and the refusal to form units all participate in the same strategy: if she can keep acting the prince hard enough, perhaps change itself can be denied.

The Producer's early intervention is deliberately paradoxical. He does not tell her that the prince dream is childish and should be abandoned. Nor does he simply validate every element of the defensive persona. He tells her that her actual behavior—giving an umbrella to a junior, carrying an injured student, protecting staff, helping people before thinking—was already cool before any stage pose made it so. At the same time, he insists that she is objectively cute and must stop treating cuteness as evidence of defeat.

The route's first correction can therefore be stated as:

> **coolness is not the male-coded surface of the prince role; it is a repeatable ethical and performative relation to other people.**

That correction is only the first half of the arc. If the story ended with Mao accepting a cute “true self” underneath a false prince, it would merely replace one compulsory category with another. Dear 004 explicitly rejects that possibility. If Mao simply “acts cute,” the Producer says, then `王子様を演じていたこれまでと、本質的にはなにも変わりません`. She is already cute; she need not perform generic cuteness. But neither is the prince discarded. Dear 005 makes the balancing proposition explicit: the `カッコイイ王子様` Mao is also a true Mao.

Dear 015 then completes the reversal. Mao can finally call the old prince ideal a `呪い` and `呪縛` because it once bound her to a fixed image. Yet she immediately refuses the inference that the ideal was therefore false. The will that kept aspiring to the prince was `ちゃんと本物`. Mature Mao chooses to wear, love, and continue acting the role until she becomes what she calls a “real prince.”

The endpoint is thus not:

> fake prince → authentic cute girl.

It is:

> **fixed aspirational self-erasure → embodied self-acceptance → voluntary performance → relational, transformable princehood.**

This is why `Fluorite` is so important even before audiovisual inspection. Its text gives Mao a language for identity that can flow, absorb, refract, and change without becoming false. In the song communication she reaches one of the clearest self-theories in the corpus:

> `ボクらしさって、ボクだけが決めるものじゃない。`
>
> `ボクと、みんなとの関係の中で、ボクらしさが生まれる`

“Mao-ness” is neither a sealed inner essence nor an externally imposed brand. It emerges among Mao, the Producer, fans, juniors, friends, rivals, inherited songs, and the roles she chooses to continue inhabiting.

That relational model also explains the evolution of the prince ethic. Mao begins by wanting to stand alone at the center of the spotlight. She later becomes dorm leader, protector, rival, teacher, bearer of inherited Hatsuboshi tradition, and finally Prima Stella. Her mature `一番星` speech refuses to treat the first star as private property: everyone who enters Hatsuboshi once possessed a light they could see, walls and setbacks can obscure it, and the role of the visible star is partly to keep that sky illuminated for others.

This does not make Mao purely selfless. She is repeatedly called greedy, stubborn, competitive, and sometimes selfish. She wants *all of it*: cute and cool, the top-idol dream, the Producer, professional legitimacy, senior responsibility, rivalry, the prince, and the freedom to keep changing. Her route increasingly treats that appetite as generative rather than shameful.

The final image in Dear 037 therefore matters more than any simple “accept yourself” slogan. Mao explains the theatre's center position, `0番`, and wants to stand there with the Producer while still insisting that the ideal prince remains far ahead. Her idol mode is not arrival but motion:

> `憧れに手を伸ばして、変わり続けていく`

The prince survives precisely because Mao no longer requires herself to remain unchanged.

# 2. Story-state overview

## 2.1 Childhood: when aspiration and category aligned

Mao's origin is unusually literal. Her mother takes her to musical theatre. Mao sees the prince-role top star, experiences the figure as overwhelming beauty/coolness, and declares that she too will become a prince.

Child acting initially rewards this aspiration. Her appearance allows her to receive boy and prince roles. She researches roles seriously, including figures such as Robin Hood and Minamoto no Yoshitsune. Acting is therefore never only disguise. It is craft, study, physical memory, staging literacy, improvisation, and a way to inhabit aspiration.

The important retrospective phrase is not merely that she was happy. It is that the old Mao could be `無敵` and remain an ideal Mao. The dream appeared to possess a stable body and a stable professional category.

## 2.2 Puberty: category loss becomes self-loss

Dear 003 names the rupture directly:

> `体はどんどん女の子っぽくなって、`
>
> `体は変わっても、心は変わらない。`

The second statement is Mao's attempted defense against the first. Her body changes in ways the old casting system no longer reads as compatible with her prince/boy niche. She also does not achieve the height she associates with the role. She is no longer selected for the same work and eventually leaves the child-actor path.

This should not be flattened into “Mao dislikes being a girl.” The text supports a more bounded proposition:

- she experiences feminizing puberty as the loss of a professional/aesthetic category she treasured;
- she begins treating signs coded as cute/girlish as proof that she is moving away from the prince;
- she then organizes presentation around preventing that interpretation.

The corpus does **not**, at this stage, warrant assigning Mao a modern gender-identity label. It does warrant sustained analysis of gendered embodiment, stage role, presentation, and the social meaning attached to puberty.

## 2.3 Defensive princehood: keeping the form to deny the change

The defensive strategy spreads beyond clothes.

Mao suppresses or discards:

- plush toys she genuinely liked;
- sweet foods;
- more feminine styling;
- spontaneous “cute” reactions;
- fear of horror;
- vulnerable need;
- some forms of peer collaboration.

She drinks black coffee despite disliking bitterness because it seems cool. She treats “cute” as an evaluative threat. She avoids the later acting career of the former prince she idolized because the woman's transformation feels like betrayal of the fixed image Mao needs to preserve.

This is why the prince functions for a period as both aspiration and `呪縛`.

## 2.4 Producer intervention: separate ethical coolness from visual category

The Producer chooses Mao after seeing her give her umbrella to a junior and run through the rain. Before any contract, he has already read the “prince” in conduct rather than appearance.

That reading becomes the conceptual lever of Dear 003–004:

- Mao was not cool *because* she had once been cast as a prince;
- the instinct to protect, help, persist, and face the stage already contains the desired quality;
- the changed body need not disqualify that quality;
- but denying obvious cuteness makes the idol's self-presentation rigid and legible as strain.

The provocative instruction to “become very cute first” should therefore not be read as a destination. It is exposure therapy against a category she has made synonymous with loss.

## 2.5 P1 / STEP1: from opposition to combinability

Series 1 repeatedly tests whether Mao can hold two public qualities at once.

The route experiments with:

- cuter clothes and hair;
- old cool vocal strength;
- prince-style posture without forcing every expression into a prince mask;
- ordinary reactions rather than constant role maintenance;
- stage choices that allow “cute” and “cool” to reinforce rather than cancel one another.

P1 is also where failure reveals that Mao's growth is not a clean self-help montage. She can become disappointed, want to quit, skip practice, or temporarily conclude that the experiment is not working. The strongest failure-state correction is that “cool” cannot mean never appearing pathetic. Repeatedly rising again becomes part of the new definition.

## 2.6 Dear 009 / `Fluorite`: accepted change becomes a performable theory

Dear 009 marks the first major integration. Mao can say she previously hated both the self who could not be the prince and the self who forcibly performed the prince.

The new stage image combines:

- changed current body;
- cuteness she no longer treats as defeat;
- the cool voice and theatrical history she retains;
- the prince image she still wants;
- the Producer's recognition of what was already cool.

The associated `Fluorite` communication deepens the point. The concept of `流れる / 変化する` initially sounds to Mao like indecision. She comes to understand change as a mode of survival and brilliance. More radically, she rejects a purely private model of identity:

> `ボクらしさって、ボクだけが決めるものじゃない。`
>
> `ボクと、みんなとの関係の中で、ボクらしさが生まれる`

This is the core reason the mature Mao cannot be described by an authenticity-versus-performance binary.

## 2.7 N.I.A.: performance becomes mobile rather than fixed

Dear 011 makes the new flexibility tactical. The Producer plans to alternate and cross:

- cool Mao;
- cute Mao;
- cute styling with cool material;
- hard styling with cute material.

The “prince” is no longer one costume that must never be contradicted. It becomes a repertoire.

N.I.A. also brings Shion and Gokugetsu into the arc. Shion calls Mao a fake prince and targets the child actor `有村あきら`. This works precisely because she attacks the old wound: perhaps Mao's prince is merely failed childhood casting.

Dear 015 is Mao's mature answer. She grants the accusation's historical truth—her old dream became a curse—without conceding that the dream was false:

> `この服は、ボクを呪縛していた、過去の夢と憧れ。`
>
> `王子様に憧れ続けたボクの意思は、ちゃんと本物`
>
> `王子様の役を演じ続けてやるんだ。`
>
> `いつか、本物の王子様になる、その日まで`

The same action—acting the prince—has changed ethical status because Mao's relation to it has changed. What was once compulsory preservation becomes deliberate authorship.

## 2.8 Professional transition and H.I.F.: the question moves from self-acceptance to coauthorship

Dear 021–026 shifts the conflict outward. Juo Production / 100Pro can offer Mao professional infrastructure and a path commensurate with her new market value. The central problem is no longer “can Mao accept cute?” It is **who gets to author the next Mao**.

Ryuusei Juo offers professional experience, network, and a coherent prince-image strategy. His proposal is not obviously malicious. In many ordinary career models it is the rational choice.

Mao refuses the replacement of her student Producer because the relationship itself has become part of the desired work:

> `だからこそ。ボクには、<あの人=プロデューサー>が必要`
>
> `ボクを大好きな、一番のファンに、プロデュースして欲しい`

She acknowledges that this may not be the wisest choice:

> `そうかもしれません。でも――ボクらしい選択です。`

The route therefore turns “Mao-ness” into a decision about production relations as well as image.

## 2.9 Summer loss: visible recommitment does not equal emotional recovery

Dear 027 is a crucial counterexample to simplistic resilience. Mao can frame the loss publicly as a step and still break down privately:

> `負けたあああああああああっ！`

This is methodologically important across Gakumas:

> **behavioral recommitment and emotional recovery are separate variables.**

The Producer also reveals that he never treated the summer H.I.F. as the realistic final proof. He distinguishes boldness from gambling with Mao's life/career. Ryuusei calls him a “dream-showing realist.”

## 2.10 Winter H.I.F.: aspiration becomes generative leadership

Dear 028–037 changes scale again. Mao's task is no longer merely to optimize herself. She trains with and observes the rest of the cast, learning the forms of light she cannot reproduce by imitation. Her theatrical `観察と憑依` become tools for recognizing difference rather than swallowing others into one model.

She also reframes seniority as repayment. As dorm leader, she cares for juniors because seniors once made room for a stubborn younger Mao. Sena's own institutional legacy becomes legible through the same lens: a senior can repay the system by raising the horizon of those behind her.

Mao's photo-book concept makes another correction to princehood. The prince can be afraid. Facing fear can itself become princely. Vulnerability no longer automatically disqualifies the image.

When Mao defeats Sena and becomes Prima Stella, her speech names fans, workers, trainers, parents, rivals, comrades, and Producer. The first star becomes a visible orientation point in a sky populated by many lights rather than proof that only one person's light is real.

Dear 037 then refuses closure. The ideal remains ahead. Mao's final mode is:

> `憧れに手を伸ばして、変わり続けていく`

# 3. The prince problem: role, aspiration, and self-erasure

## 3.1 The prince begins as desire, not deception

It is analytically important not to retroactively call childhood princehood a false self. The child Mao genuinely loves the role, works for it, researches it, and experiences recognition through it. The later problem is not that she “was acting.” Actors can sincerely inhabit roles.

The problem is that Mao eventually turns one successful role-form into an ontological rule:

> if I am not legible as that prince, I am no longer the person who could become what I admired.

That is why body change becomes catastrophic.

## 3.2 Performance becomes harmful when it becomes compulsory ontology

Dear 004 supplies the clean test. If Mao simply replaces “act prince” with “act cute,” nothing fundamental changes. The object has changed; the compulsory logic has not.

This gives the route a more sophisticated performance theory than “be yourself.” It distinguishes:

- **performance as imposed correction** — adults tell Mao what marketable girlhood should look like;
- **performance as defensive fixation** — Mao uses princehood to deny bodily and professional change;
- **performance as craft** — acting, posing, voice, choreography, staging, styling;
- **performance as chosen aspiration** — the role one deliberately continues because it expresses a valued direction;
- **performance as relation** — a form made with fans, peers, rivals, Producer, songs, and inherited institution.

Mature Mao does not become less theatrical. She becomes more capable of deciding *why* she is theatrical.

## 3.3 `可愛い` is first a threat category, later a resource

The early Mao does not merely dislike a style. “Cute” carries three painful implications:

1. her body has changed away from the child-prince casting category;
2. adults may again overwrite her stated desire with a marketable feminine image;
3. accepting cute may feel like admitting the old dream was impossible.

This is why forced skirts and cute songs were experienced as violations despite the later Mao enjoying skirts, hair styling, sweets, and cute reactions.

The change is not that those objects acquire new intrinsic properties. Mao's relation to them changes because they no longer function as proof that the prince has died.

## 3.4 `カッコイイ` moves from surface correspondence to ethical persistence

The strongest evidence for a deeper coolness model is outside costume.

Mao is repeatedly cool when she:

- gives up her umbrella for a junior;
- carries an injured person;
- intervenes against harassment;
- protects rivals from contempt;
- refuses to abandon Rinami's hope because of one defeat;
- admits a loss and returns;
- crawls through an ugly struggle rather than preserving appearance;
- keeps the promise to stand beside/against Sena;
- makes space for juniors to inherit what she inherited.

The P3 failure material makes the transformation explicit. Mao can call herself `カッコ悪く` struggling, thrashing, and crawling through mud and still make that struggle part of the prince.

A prince who must never look uncool is fragile. A prince who can survive visible uncoolness is durable.

# 4. Body, puberty, and gendered presentation

## 4.1 What the text directly supports

The source directly connects Mao's crisis to:

- feminizing puberty;
- chest development;
- insufficient height relative to her imagined prince form;
- loss of boy/prince child roles;
- adult pressure toward cute feminine idol presentation;
- her own use of masculine-coded clothing and behavior as resistance.

These are not incidental costume notes. The body is where the old dream becomes difficult.

## 4.2 What the text does not settle

The corpus does not establish that Mao understands herself through a contemporary trans/nonbinary identity category. It also does not establish that her eventual enjoyment of feminine-coded clothing “proves” a cisgender resolution.

The safer formulation is:

> Mao's narrative is about **gendered legibility and role possibility**: how a changing body affects which social/stage categories are made available to her, and how she learns to prevent those categories from monopolizing the meaning of the body.

## 4.3 Reclaiming the changed body without treating it as destiny

Mao eventually can enjoy:

- skirts;
- different hair;
- cute photographs;
- sweets;
- visibly frightened reactions;
- feminine/cute image work.

But the text does not make those the “truth” that defeats the prince. Instead she recombines them with:

- cool voice;
- prince clothing;
- martial physicality;
- stage center aspiration;
- theatrical gesture;
- protective conduct.

The body becomes a larger performance instrument because it no longer needs to certify one fixed identity.

## 4.4 Material body discipline and overextension

Mao is physically capable. Martial arts, running, climbing, choreography, athletic games, and general stamina recur. This can obscure risk.

The Producer repeatedly has to distinguish capacity from unlimited capacity:

- he detects hidden fatigue when she feels good enough to keep training;
- school lessons emphasize injury disclosure and long-term health;
- Mao's dorm-leader work adds invisible load;
- paid work can fatigue arms or body;
- she can treat “I can endure it” as evidence that endurance is wise.

This creates a small but important parallel with her old prince psychology: **competence itself can become another image she feels compelled to maintain.**

# 5. Talent and craft: Mao is not merely an image character

## 5.1 Acting is real technical capital

The child-actor history gives Mao concrete strengths:

- memorization;
- role research;
- improvisation;
- stage blocking and sightlines;
- expressive repertoire;
- observing other performers;
- interpreting lyrics through dramatic situation;
- communicating staging ideas to staff;
- teaching through demonstration once she learns to verbalize technique.

Her problem is not “too much acting.” It is **using acting to avoid self-revision**.

## 5.2 The limit of intuitive theatrical language

`Campus mode!!` gives a revealing teaching scene. Mao initially tells Kotone to move like a hummingbird and express the soul of Hatsuboshi—language vivid to Mao but too impressionistic to teach reliably. The Producer makes her translate felt image into concrete instruction: fingers, toe placement, posture, angle, timing.

This is a useful skill delta:

> expert embodiment → explicit transferable craft.

Mao becomes a better performer by having to make tacit stage knowledge legible to someone else.

## 5.3 Voice and appearance are textually framed as productive mismatch

The scripts repeatedly contrast:

- cute/small appearance;
- cool/strong singing voice.

This is a **textual** characterization claim. The actual timbre, range, breath behavior, attack, register, or mixing cannot be concluded before AV inspection.

The textual strategy nevertheless matters: Mao's public identity is designed around preserving difference rather than forcing all channels to match one category.

## 5.4 Observation becomes relational competence

Winter-H.I.F. preparation turns Mao's actorly observation outward. She trains with other idols not to steal one supreme technique but to learn that each person possesses a differently colored light.

The arc therefore converts `観察と憑依` from actorly imitation into comparative recognition.

# 6. Care, dorm leadership, and the prince as social vocation

## 6.1 Helping is not merely image strategy

Mao helps strangers and juniors before the current Producer relationship. This is precisely what attracts him.

The Produce Events reinforce that she does not primarily instrumentalize these acts. When asked whether she should advertise herself to someone she helps, she dislikes exploiting the person's vulnerability. She prefers the possibility that the person may later recognize her by chance.

This is strong counterevidence to a cynical reading of “prince” as branding.

## 6.2 Yet helping also helps Mao like herself

Mao is unusually self-aware about a secondary motive:

> helping people lets her feel closer to the person she wants to be.

She asks whether this makes the behavior impure. The corpus generally refuses that purity test. Good conduct can simultaneously help someone else and help Mao author herself.

## 6.3 Dorm leadership is inherited care

Mao explicitly says she is not as naturally effortless at care as Rinami. She makes mistakes, gives unwanted advice, and can become overbearing. She nevertheless watches juniors closely because seniors once watched over her.

This makes dorm leadership a form of repayment rather than proof of innate maternal perfection.

The U1 intervention during Saki and Temari's escalating food fight is representative. Mao enters as authority, listens to Kotone's judgment that the conflict is important, temporarily permits it, and sets herself as the escalation backstop. That is small evidence of **bounded authority rather than immediate command**.

## 6.4 Protector identity can suppress Mao's own needs

The same social role produces risk.

Mao is more comfortable being:

- the person consulted;
- the person who escorts;
- the person who protects;
- the person who watches over others;

than being the one who openly says “I need this.”

Examples include:

- hiding cute preferences;
- framing the dorm summer event as something for juniors before admitting she herself wants the memory;
- maintaining public composure after defeat before crying privately;
- embarrassment when juniors see cute styling;
- needing explicit permission to rest or receive care.

Mature princehood therefore requires not only broader self-expression but **permission to occupy the protected position as well as the protector position**.

# 7. Failure, rivalry, and the redefinition of coolness

## 7.1 P1 prevents a frictionless self-acceptance narrative

Mao's P1 branches include:

- audition failure;
- disappointment;
- self-blame;
- visible joy she initially tries to restrain;
- result grades that cannot coexist in one chronology;
- a branch where poor simulation contributes to skipping practice and wanting to give up.

The route repeatedly teaches that the correct response to failure is not a stable personality trait Mao already possesses. It is a skill she develops.

## 7.2 Shion is the hostile mirror

Shion is effective because she voices the brutal version of Mao's private old theory:

- failed child star;
- talentless adult-stage aspirant;
- fake prince;
- dream as evidence of inability to accept reality.

Mao can answer her only after conceding part of the accusation. The dream did become a curse. The old prince did function as avoidance. The victory is not disproving those facts; it is refusing Shion's conclusion that a compromised aspiration must be discarded.

This is one of the strongest examples in Gakumas of **revision without repudiation**.

## 7.3 Gekka is the skill-gap reality check

Mao's confrontation with Gekka prevents narrative self-esteem from being treated as equivalent to technical supremacy. Mao can win crowd response and still admit that Gekka could not fully exert herself and that the underlying skill gap is enormous.

Confidence is therefore not “I am secretly already the best.” It is permission to enter a field where others may still be substantially stronger.

## 7.4 Sena changes from unreachable summit to person to be rescued by rivalry

The late Mao/Sena relation turns the prince motif outward. Mao apologizes that she failed to become a real rival earlier and promises:

> `ボクが、キミを塔の上から、連れ出してあげる`

Sena answers with the role-name:

> `――王子様。`

The prince is now neither child casting nor self-protective costume. It is a relation of challenge that gives the summit another future.

## 7.5 Losing can be ugly without invalidating the prince

P3 failure language is unusually explicit:

> `カッコ悪くあがいて、もがいて、泥の中で這い上がる`

Mao also quotes theatrical texts in defeat, including *Henry V* and *The Tempest*. This is not decorative culture-reference alone. It shows how stage language has moved from prison to resilience technology. She can use roles and quotations to continue rather than to hide.

# 8. The Producer: reader, author, fan, and dependency risk

## 8.1 Why Mao accepts this Producer when she rejected others

Mao's first problem with previous adults is not that they saw cuteness. It is that they treated cuteness as authorization to overwrite her stated desire.

The new Producer distinguishes himself by prior research and by seeing conduct:

- he has watched her previous work;
- he understands the prince dream;
- he saw her help a junior without an audience;
- he does not ask her to discard the dream.

This creates unusually deep legitimacy for his later interventions.

## 8.2 His “cute” prescription is still an exercise of power

That legitimacy should not erase asymmetry.

The Producer still:

- defines an early diagnostic framework;
- tells Mao she is cute;
- selects image experiments;
- controls schedule and workload;
- manages opportunities;
- frames the final competitive proof;
- mediates professional transition;
- becomes the preferred interpreter of what Mao can be.

The route demonstrates that this works. Phase 3 should not silently convert effectiveness into ethical innocence.

## 8.3 The best version of the relationship refuses to define Mao completely

Dear 007 is especially important. Mao asks:

> `ボクらしさって、なんでしょう？`

The Producer does **not** supply a final answer. He understands that his answer would only become another person's imposed definition.

This refusal creates space for the later relational model of `ボクらしさ`.

## 8.4 Producer as first fan

By N.I.A. and the professional conflict, “Producer” and “fan” become deliberately entangled. Mao wants the person who produces her to be the person who loved the difficult, awkward, still-failing Mao enough to invest in her.

Ryuusei's objection is institutionally intelligible: professional work may need someone more experienced. Mao's response is not a proof that experience is irrelevant. It is a claim that production is partly **shared struggle and shared dream**, not just optimization.

## 8.5 Coauthorship can empower and enclose

The late pair achieve a strong form of coauthorship. They imagine becoming world-best top star and world-best Producer together.

That intimacy has two possible readings that must remain simultaneously available:

- **enabling:** Mao refuses a professionally rational replacement and chooses the collaborator who helped her regain agency;
- **enclosing:** the person who first made her feel correctly seen becomes difficult to imagine being without, potentially concentrating emotional and professional authority in one relationship.

The text currently favors the enabling interpretation but does not eliminate the second.

# 22. Phase-3 conclusion

Mao's route is unusually valuable because it refuses two easy moral stories at once.

It refuses:

> “Never change; remain true to the dream exactly as first imagined.”

The fixed prince nearly destroys her capacity to enjoy the stage.

It also refuses:

> “Mature by abandoning the childish ideal and accepting the ordinary self underneath.”

The ideal was not a childish error. The will behind it was real, and the mature Mao deliberately chooses the prince again.

What changes is the ontology of the role.

The child prince was a role Mao could occupy because body and casting happened to align.

The defensive prince was a role Mao used to deny that alignment had ended.

The mature prince is a role Mao can keep choosing while permitting the chooser to change.

That is why the best Phase-3 sentence remains:

> **Mao learns not to stop performing the prince, but to stop using the prince to disappear.**

And the final direction of movement is not toward a stable label but toward `0番` with the capacity to keep revising what standing there can mean.
