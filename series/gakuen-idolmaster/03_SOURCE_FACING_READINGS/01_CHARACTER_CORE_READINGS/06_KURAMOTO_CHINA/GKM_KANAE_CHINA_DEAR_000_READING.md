## Reciprocal care and the cost of success

Kanae's care is at once skilled labor, a formal household relationship and personal attachment. The Producer's sense of pressure is his perspective. Kanae's own inner speech ties her effort to China's happiness. Her affiliated school and live-in status give the labor a social setting. At the election, Kanae protects China's independent achievement by resisting family resources and by offering practical help behind the stage. The source shows her refusal in that exchange. It gives no complete account of the Kuramoto family's election involvement.

China appreciates the relationship without always making her wish for Kanae's own life understood. In part 03 she deliberately speaks about the right to dream even for a person without an idol or president's role. Kanae hears praise for China's stance. China's own internal reaction says her message did not reach Kanae (M049–055). Part 04 reunites the two backstage after Kanae feels left behind in the hall. China consoles Kanae, calls herself almost like an older sister in that moment, and still wishes for a dream of Kanae's own (M060–077). The reunion and the unease both belong in their relationship state.

In part 05, Misuzu's observation that people can share a stride and see a different view gives Kanae a language for the problem. Misuzu refers to a similar experience and mentions Temari on her way out. This is cross-track evidence of how Misuzu thinks about proximity and divergence. The exchange does not name an incident that can be assigned to the Temari–Misuzu chronology. Kanae's idea of `同じ高さ` and first-hand training remains motivated by support in her stated account. The Producer's surprised response supplies an open next development. The arc ends before a proposal is specified or any training or debut takes place.
