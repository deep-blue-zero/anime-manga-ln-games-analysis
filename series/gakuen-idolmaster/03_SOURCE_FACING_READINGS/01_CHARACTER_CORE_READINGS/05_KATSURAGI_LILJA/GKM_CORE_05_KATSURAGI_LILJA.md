# GKM CORE 05 — KATSURAGI LILJA / 葛城リーリヤ
## Phase 3 source-facing character deep reading

## 0. Status, purpose, and evidence boundary

This document is the canonical **Phase-3 source-facing textual reading** of 葛城リーリヤ / Katsuragi Lilja under GAKUMAS V2 Source Lock 1.0. It is not her Phase-7 definitive monograph. It is designed to establish a continuity-safe model of her character, identify claims that later events/support-card/AV evidence must test, and prevent the later REVERSI synthesis from treating Lilja only as an accessory to Shiun Sumika.

# 1. Executive thesis — courage made circulatory

The V1 formulation that Lilja is the cast's exemplary beginner survives, but V2 requires a more precise center than “a talentless girl who succeeds through hard work.”

> **Katsuragi Lilja is the idol of courage made circulatory: a girl who initially treats lack of skill as a fixed premise and compensates through self-erasing effort, then learns to convert courage received from Sumika, her Producer, family, rivals, teachers, and fans into an audience-facing performance that returns courage to other people.**

Her development is therefore not:

> no talent → enough effort → success.

It is closer to:

> **fearful desire → borrowed belief → compulsive effort → governed effort → technical competence → discovered/cultivated charisma → reciprocal support → differentiated partnership → public transmission.**

Three corrections are essential.

First, Lilja's early technical weakness is **real**. The story does not merely hide an elite singer/dancer behind low self-esteem. She lacks formal training, struggles with foundations, misjudges her own errors, becomes narrow under technical load, and requires unusually explicit instruction.

Second, “no talent” becomes **too broad** as a final description. Dear 024–025 identifies a distinct visual/charismatic aptitude — a capacity to draw people into `わたしが主人公の物語`, which Shion calls a kind of `カリスマ性` / `<r=ビジュアル>魔性</r>`. Yet the text also insists that this quality is intermittent and unusable without better foundations. Visual training, posture, gesture, styling, dramaturgy, and emotional conditions all help make it reproducible. Her eventual `葛城結界` is therefore neither pure natural gift nor pure grit; it is a cultivated performance system built from both.

Third, effort is **not automatically virtuous**. Lilja's defining danger is that the sentence “I have less talent, therefore I must work more” can become a rationale for treating her own body as expendable. Dear 014 makes the danger explicit:

> `それを、証明するためなら――わたしの体なんて、壊れたっていい`

Her mature courage must therefore acquire governance: rest, pacing, technique, trust, efficient pedagogy, reciprocal care, and the ability to rely on others.

The strongest mature formulation is not that Lilja becomes unafraid. She remains frightened repeatedly. It is that she learns to make fear **socially usable**:

> she receives courage, acts while afraid, makes that action visible, receives support in response, and then returns the accumulated courage through performance.

That circuit becomes both her personal psychology and her idol aesthetic.

# 2. The first idol act happens before the first lesson

Lilja's Dear route begins from spatial and cultural displacement. She is newly living in Japan, uncertain enough to get lost on campus, and trying to orient herself without Sumika beside her. Her formal idol skills are not what the Producer notices.

When he asks what an idol most needs, Lilja guesses the visible criteria: being cute, singing well, dancing well, speaking well. His answer is:

> `――強い意志を持っていること。`
>
> `勇気を持って、夢へと踏み出す才能です。`

The evidence he gives is biographical action rather than performance output. Lilja wanted Hatsuboshi badly enough to persuade her parents, study, pass the entrance process, leave her family, move from Sweden to Japan, and enter an unfamiliar environment for a dream she did not believe herself naturally qualified to pursue.

This makes her migration structurally important without reducing her to “the foreign girl.” She is Japanese-Swedish, Japanese-proficient, already connected to Japanese popular culture through family, anime, games, songs, and Sumika. Produce Event `school_004` explicitly says Japanese itself is not a major communication obstacle (`日本語は堪能なので問題ない`). What changes is the **living environment**, educational context, social environment, and physical distance from her family.

The first act of idolhood is therefore not a song.

It is the decision to **arrive**.

That is why fear cannot simply be removed from Lilja's model. Her courage is meaningful because the route repeatedly preserves the fear. She does not become a Hiro-like seeker of impossible difficulty; she would often prefer the task to be easier. Nor does she begin with Saki-like confidence. Lilja's characteristic motion is:

> I am frightened; I may be unsuited; I still want this; therefore I will take the next step.

The Producer's first intervention is to name that motion as a talent before Lilja can name it herself.

# 3. “Talentless” is first a premise, then a category under revision

One of Lilja's most important early lines is not a plea for reassurance:

> `わたしに、才能なんてない。`
>
> `それは前提だから。――諦める理由には、ならない。`

She treats technical inferiority as a premise. This is psychologically different from simple denial. She can accept that other students are better and still reject the inference that she should quit.

The early corpus strongly supports the technical component of this self-assessment. She is inexperienced, has weak dance foundations, poor performance bandwidth, difficulty showing expression, stage anxiety, inefficient self-training, and a tendency to confuse exhausting effort with correct practice. In Dear 024 Shion demonstrates that even after substantial growth Lilja is not yet at H.I.F.-main-stage technical minimum. In Dear 025 the criticism becomes precise:

> `あなたは目の前のパフォーマンスに精一杯すぎるんですよ。`
>
> `基礎が疎かだから、目の前に精一杯になってしまうんです。`

The problem is not merely “she needs more passion.” It is that insufficient automatization of basics consumes her entire attentional budget.

But the V1 statement that Lilja has “no hidden talent” cannot remain canonical without revision.

Shion eventually identifies a separate capacity:

> `あなたが持っているのは、一種のカリスマ性。`
>
> `人を自分の世界に惹きこむ、<r=ビジュアル>魔性</r>――`

and even calls part of its substrate `生まれ持った特別な才能`: her distinctive visual presence. Sumika independently struggles to verbalize the same effect:

> `声質も、雰囲気も、表情も、仕草も、その全部から、勇気をもらえる。`

The correction, however, must not swing too far in the opposite direction. The route does **not** reveal that Lilja was secretly an elite natural performer all along.

Shion immediately qualifies the discovery:

- Lilja cannot always produce the atmosphere.
- Her technical ability still lags badly.
- Her upset victory depended on a favorable emotional condition and intermittent activation.
- Visual training has already changed how her natural appearance is carried.
- without foundations she cannot consciously use the capacity.

The better talent taxonomy is:

1. **Technical skill:** initially weak, gradually trained.
2. **Persistence / `強い意志`:** behaviorally real but dangerous when ungoverned.
3. **Relational transmissiveness:** others want to support her and can receive courage from visible effort.
4. **Visual/charismatic world-making:** partly dispositional, partly cultivated, initially intermittent.
5. **Imaginative symbolic authorship:** her otaku/fantasy vocabulary helps convert abstract charisma into an ownable performance concept.

The word `天才` at Dear 035 is therefore intentionally ironic and transformative. When Lilja finally says:

> `わたしは、アイドルの天才なんだって！`

she has not discovered that all earlier deficits were fake. She has learned to accept a **different theory of what idol talent can consist of**.

# 4. Effort: first weapon, first danger

Lilja's identity initially organizes itself around asymmetry:

> everyone else can do more naturally; therefore I must spend more time.

P1 repeatedly shows this ethic. Even after visible improvement she says:

> `わたしには、頑張ることしかできないけど――それだけは、誰にも負けません！`

The admirable part is obvious. She can sustain repetition, accept correction, return after embarrassment, and treat yesterday's self as a reachable threshold.

The dangerous part arrives just as consistently.

She has difficulty treating rest as productive. She suppresses hobbies to train. She hides extra practice. She converts external insult into a demand for bodily proof. In N.I.A., Shion's contempt is especially destabilizing because it attacks the Producer's judgment as well as Lilja herself. Lilja has historically been able to absorb insults directed at her. What she cannot tolerate is the claim that the person who chose her was wrong.

That produces Dear 014's crisis. She trains against explicit instruction, injures herself, and internally accepts bodily destruction as an acceptable price for vindication.

The route's answer is not “believe in yourself harder.”

The Producer tells her there is no shortcut and that destroying herself will lengthen the path. Sumika recognizes the shape of the danger through her own injury history. Lilja is asked to do something harder for her than effort:

> `ひとりで無理するのは、やめる。`
>
> `わたしは、センパイを信じるから。`

That makes **trust a training skill**.

P1/P2 side material reinforces this. The Producer monitors hydration, training load, sleep/rest, food, and stress. Asari's school discussion explicitly frames support distance as a professional problem: Lilja will often keep going under stress, so the Producer must decide when to watch, when to provide recreation, and when to intervene.

Lilja also begins returning the same care. In Produce Event `activity_003`, she gives the overworked Producer relaxation items because he is the one who taught her that rest matters. In `activity_019` she worries that even his holidays are becoming work. Thus care becomes reciprocal rather than a one-way Producer service.

A mature Lilja thesis must therefore reject:

> **effort = virtue.**

The more defensible formulation is:

> **Effort is one of Lilja's central capacities only after it becomes governable.**

The capacity to stop, recover, accept efficient instruction, enjoy leisure, and delegate safety to trustworthy others is not external to her courage. It is part of what keeps courage from becoming panic wearing moral language.

# 5. Performance: from executing correctly to delivering something

Dear 003 and Dear 006 establish Lilja's core performance problem before the story has language for `魔性`.

She is initially consumed by execution. Looking happy, smiling naturally, being watched, and remembering the audience all compete with simply surviving the task. Her own standard is “do this correctly.” The Producer introduces a different standard:

> `大事なのは、届けようという意志。`
>
> `精一杯の自分を見せることです。`

This does not mean technique is irrelevant. Shion later makes the opposite correction: if Lilja must spend all cognition on rhythm and steps, she cannot freely address the audience at all. The two lessons are complementary:

- **technique is not the purpose of performance;**
- **technique creates the spare capacity through which purpose can become perceptible.**

Sumika supplies the first concrete addressee. Lilja asks that her friend attend because generic “audience” is too abstract. She can ask a more actionable question:

> Can what I am doing reach **this person**?

When the first live helps Sumika recover enough courage to return toward idolhood, Lilja receives empirical evidence that performance can act on another person's future.

That event becomes the template for her entire idol philosophy.

Her mature stage logic is therefore not “look how hard I work.” It is:

> **I am afraid and imperfect; I act anyway; if that action reaches you, it may help you act too.**

This is why technical improvement does not replace visible effort in her identity. Technique eventually allows effort to become less desperate and more intentional, but the act of reaching remains central.

# 6. Fans: from proof of existence to a circuit of courage

Lilja's N.I.A. fan philosophy is unusually explicit.

When rankings are poor, she resists reducing low numbers to failure:

> `ファンは、数字じゃないんですね。`
>
> `ひとりひとり、大切な存在です。`

This is not anti-metric naïveté. The Produce system still requires votes, visibility, audience acquisition, and strategy. The important distinction is ontological: a fan is not identical to the number through which an institution aggregates them.

Lilja's fan relation progresses through several stages:

1. **Audience as threat:** being seen exposes inadequacy.
2. **Audience as specific addressee:** she first performs toward Sumika.
3. **Audience as evidence:** smiles prove her current self can reach someone.
4. **Fans as individual supporters:** rankings cannot erase the people inside the number.
5. **Fans as safety signal:** seeing supporters reduces the terror of being judged.
6. **Fans as energy source:** support allows performance above her ordinary state.
7. **Performance as return:** Lilja wants to give courage back to those who gave it.

This becomes explicit in the winter-H.I.F. material. The Producer explains `葛城結界` not as mind control or mystique for its own sake, but as a social loop: people support Lilja; visible supported Lilja shines; the audience takes courage from her; she receives that response and continues.

Dear 033 makes the circulation biographical. Sumika first gave Lilja courage. Lilja's live returned courage to Sumika. Together they want to pass that courage to people who resemble their earlier frightened selves.

The mature Lilja stage is therefore best understood as a **circulation system**, not a one-directional inspirational lecture.

# 7. Shion: antagonist, technician, and corrective to the romance of grit

Shirakusa Shion is analytically valuable because she repeatedly says things that are cruel in tone but partly true in content.

Her early claim that Lilja has no talent is too broad. But her technical criticism is often excellent.

Dear 024–025 corrects a possible weakness in the route's own motivational language. If we stopped at “strong will” and “effort moves people,” the analysis could accidentally romanticize inefficient practice. Shion refuses that.

Her pedagogy is specific:

- repair basic steps;
- count errors accurately;
- stop using “I did it” as a beginner-standard ceiling;
- teach one variable at a time;
- use logic and reproducibility for a learner without intuitive sense;
- free attentional bandwidth before asking for charisma or expression.

Her insult `凡人なのだから論理的に考えなさい` is cruel, but the underlying advice becomes constructive. Lilja is not expected to imitate a naturally intuitive performer. She needs a technical system suited to the way she learns.

Lilja's response reveals another underappreciated strength: low defensive vanity.

She can be hurt. She dislikes Shion. But when someone identifies a real deficit, she tends to ask how to repair it. This helps explain why hostility repeatedly becomes usable training material. The virtue is not ego-lessness; by H.I.F. she explicitly wants Prima Stella. It is that identity defense does not consistently outrank learning.

The Shion relationship also prevents the later charisma discovery from becoming cheap vindication. Even after identifying Lilja's special appeal, Shion insists the technical gap remains.

Thus the route's mature talent theory becomes plural:

> grit cannot replace technique; technique does not exhaust charisma; charisma still needs technical support; all three can be trained into a transmissive performance.

# 8. `魔性`, `葛城結界`, and the coauthorship of “natural” charisma

Lilja's later visual identity is one of the clearest examples in Gakumas of an allegedly “natural” quality becoming meaningful only through production.

Shion points to a born visual substrate: Lilja can look unusually beautiful and fantastical. But she also explains why that was not socially legible before. Lilja shrank, looked down, and failed to occupy visual space.

The Producer adds the next layer: visual training changed posture and gesture. Then the H.I.F. route adds emotional conditions. Then `Atmosphere` adds styling and deliberate dramaturgy. Finally Lilja herself gives the whole system a symbolic language:

> `<r=ブレイブリー・エリア>葛城結界</r>`

Her “barrier idol” vocabulary is comedic chuuni play, but it is also analytically accurate. She imagines performance as a field in which people are drawn into a world and courage becomes amplified.

The crucial point is authorship.

In the `Atmosphere` communication, the Producer proposes changes such as exposing her forehead so the face becomes more readable, while Lilja asks questions, accepts or resists details, proposes fantasy props, and enthusiastically appropriates the concept. Earlier black-costume imagery is interpreted as inner strength; white/fairy/mystical styling becomes another facet. The result is not simply “the Producer discovers the real fairy inside Lilja.”

It is **coauthored branding/performance construction**:

- natural appearance supplies material;
- training changes carriage;
- emotion changes accessibility;
- styling makes a reading more likely;
- music and staging organize the atmosphere;
- Lilja's own fantasy vocabulary makes the system psychologically hers.

This is why the “Nordic snow fairy” image must not be allowed to consume the character. Her Swedishness can become production material, but her performance center is not exoticism. The material is valuable to the extent that Lilja recognizes, chooses, reshapes, and uses it.

# 9. Sweden, family, and culture: bridge rather than ornament

Lilja's Sweden background has several distinct functions.

## 9.1 Migration produces real material pressure

Living in Japan for the first time means environmental stress, different school content, family distance, and the need to construct an adult support network around herself. Produce Event `school_004` explicitly makes this a Producer-care question.

She is not linguistically helpless. The same event says her Japanese is proficient. Other scenes show more specific cultural unfamiliarity: Japanese history is less familiar; school phrasing can trip her; she compares Swedish and Japanese food, school customs, climate, and leisure.

## 9.2 The family is warm rather than an obstacle archetype

Her mother initially worries about the idol plan, but this is not framed as hostility. Lilja prepares materials to explain Hatsuboshi and Japanese idols. Her father — Japanese, and a source of anime/game exposure — is more immediately legible as a bridge to the culture. Birthday and Christmas material emphasizes attachment rather than liberation from family.

That matters because coming to Japan is costly precisely **because home is not portrayed as intolerable**.

## 9.3 Popular culture precedes professional idolhood

`ガラクタロード` revises any overly literal claim that Lilja entered Hatsuboshi with zero performance history. She had **no formal training**, but her mother praised her singing, she sang anime songs at home, later learned idol songs through Sumika, and tried some choreography for fun.

This is an important V2 correction. Her professional beginnerhood is real; her aesthetic life did not begin on enrollment day.

## 9.4 Otaku imagination becomes professional material

The robot-war fantasy communication, anime enthusiasm, game analogies, and magical terminology show an interior far more vivid than her quiet social surface initially suggests. The fantasy scene is not literal continuity evidence of combat, but it is useful symbolic evidence. Even in imagined heroism, Lilja's stated desire is:

> `誰にも、泣いてほしくない`
>
> `みんなに、笑っていてほしい`

The same imaginative system later helps her conceptualize an idol stage as a courage-producing field.

Her idolhood is therefore a bridge in several directions:

> Sweden ↔ Japan; family ↔ school; private fandom ↔ public profession; fantasy vocabulary ↔ technical stage design.

# 10. Sumika: from borrowed path to differentiated partnership

Lilja and Sumika are inseparable from each other's histories, but V2 must be careful with the word **inseparable**.

The old reading sometimes pushed REVERSI too close to fusion: two sides of one thing, neither complete without the other. The Lilja corpus itself eventually argues against that as a mature endpoint.

## 10.1 Sumika first gives Lilja a route into the world

From Lilja's perspective, Sumika is:

- beloved best friend;
- admired person;
- guide to idols;
- social translator;
- the person who brings her out of inward isolation;
- the person who proposed the original shared dream.

Lilja explicitly says Sumika `自分の世界に閉じこもっちゃうわたしを外に連れ出してくれる`.

This means the original dream is genuinely relational. Lilja does not have to prove that a dream is less hers because another person first made it imaginable.

## 10.2 Lilja's belief can become pressure

From the Sumika side — still provisional here until Sumika's own V2 core — admiration for the old prodigy can preserve an image Sumika cannot inhabit after injury. Lilja's own route begins to correct this when she learns that Sumika hides weakness and refuses help.

In the REVERSI communication she names Sumika's principal “flaw” not lack of strength but concealment:

> `清夏ちゃんは、嘘つきなんです！`
>
> `わたしを頼ってくれないんです……！`

The historical care direction reverses. Lilja tells the Producer how to recognize Sumika's false “I'm fine” and later says directly:

> `じゃあ、頼ってほしいな。わたしもいるし、センパイもいる。清夏ちゃんは、ひとりじゃないよ。`

## 10.3 Shared dream becomes rivalry rather than possession

The decisive differentiation occurs after REVERSI becomes competitive for H.I.F. Lilja initially suppresses desire for the singular Prima Stella because wanting an individual title feels like betrayal of the shared goal.

Sumika corrects her:

> `欲しいものは欲しいって、そう言っていいんだよ`

Lilja can then hold two propositions simultaneously:

- I want us to win together.
- I want to beat you for the individual title.

The phrase `仲間でライバル` is not a minor genre convention. It marks Lilja's growing ability to desire **inside** attachment without treating differentiation as abandonment.

## 10.4 Fulfillment eventually requires separate dreams

Dear 037 supplies the strongest anti-fusion evidence. After the shared dream is fulfilled, Lilja is sad because the old form of desire has ended. Sumika proposes that they now pursue different dreams while continuing to help each other:

> `もう同じ夢は見れなくても、助け合うことはできるでしょ？`

This is a much healthier mature formulation of REVERSI than “neither exists without the other.”

> **Their bond survives the loss of a shared telos.**

That is the important endpoint to test later from Sumika's own V2 perspective.

# 11. REVERSI: authentic relationship, public narrative, competitive technology

REVERSI is not only emotional payoff. It becomes an institutional strategy.

The Producer recognizes that Lilja and Sumika cannot straightforwardly beat a figure such as Sena through technical completion alone. He therefore makes their relationship publicly legible through rehearsal streaming and story circulation.

Dear 030 is unusually frank about how this works. Officially, popularity does not determine H.I.F. judging. But judges are humans with tastes, prior information, and interpretive frames. If viewers know the history behind `ときめきエモーション`, the performance can acquire additional emotional intelligibility.

This produces an ethically interesting Gakumas problem:

> **authentic intimacy becomes competition-relevant paratext.**

The route does not reduce that to exploitation. Lilja and Sumika consent to the stream, answer questions, tell their own history, and use the public response as encouragement. Their affection is not fabricated by the Producer.

But neither is publicity neutral. The Producer deliberately turns relationship history into a performance resource.

This should remain active for Phase 8/9:

- When does making a real bond legible become healthy authorship?
- When does it become instrumentalization?
- How much control do the girls retain over which parts of intimacy become public evidence?
- Does the “episode power” model reward performers for turning private vulnerability into marketable story?

Lilja's corpus does not supply a final universal answer. It demonstrates a case where the participants appear to experience the process as meaningful, while preserving the institutional mechanism.

# 12. The Producer: borrowed belief, coauthor, regulator, object of vindication

Lilja's Producer relationship begins before she has stable self-belief.

She does not immediately believe “I am talented.” She believes that **he may be right about her**.

P1 makes the scaffolding explicit. After a successful early route, she says her confidence grew because he believes in her more than she believes in herself. `ending-true-01` goes so far as to say that what feels ordinary to him feels miraculous to her.

This makes the Producer's interpretive authority unusually powerful.

## 12.1 The empowering side

He performs several real functions:

- names her first step as `強い意志` rather than evidence of foolishness;
- constructs training structure for a novice;
- regulates rest and bodily load;
- translates vague charisma into actionable production;
- produces opportunities, exposure, and strategy;
- gives her feedback calibrated to growth;
- refuses to let technical weakness become a moral verdict;
- later admits his own limitations and mistakes.

The relationship also becomes reciprocal. Lilja cares for his health, sees his overwork, learns he is a fallible first-year Producer-course student rather than a perfect adult authority, and eventually wants to support his dream as he supported hers.

## 12.2 The dangerous side

Lilja repeatedly ties her own legitimacy to proving his judgment correct.

After Shion attacks him, she risks her body partly to prove `センパイの目が、正しい`. In H.I.F. she wants Prima Stella partly so she can return and tell him that the idol he produced became a top idol, thereby proving that **he** is a great Producer.

This mutual-vindication structure is emotionally moving, but it can become circular:

> he believes in me → I must succeed to justify him → my success proves his belief → his belief becomes more central to my self-conception.

Idol Communication 010 explicitly complicates the phrase “strong Lilja.” She says:

> `本当は、センパイが思うほど、わたしは強い人間じゃない`

Her ability to stand with confidence remains partly relationally sustained.

Dear 037 pushes the relationship beyond career duration. Lilja asks:

> `ずっと一緒にいてくれますか？`
>
> `わたしの夢が終わっても……わたしと一緒にいてください。`

The Producer knowingly deflects the romance-adjacent implication into the language of continuing dreams and postpones a direct answer until she is no longer an idol.

The safe Phase-3 conclusion is therefore:

> **The Producer is not simply the source of Lilja's courage, nor has she outgrown him into pure independence. He becomes one node in a reciprocal support system whose enabling effects are clear but whose long-term emotional enclosure remains unresolved.**

Later phases should test whether Lilja can self-regulate, choose against his preferences, fail without experiencing his belief as debt, and sustain self-worth during meaningful separation.

# 13. School, work, and ordinary life: becoming more than the dream

The 83 Produce Events matter because a Lilja reading built only from Dear would risk turning her into a moral allegory about effort.

Ordinary scenes reveal several stabilizing domains.

## 13.1 She is a student, not a training machine

She worries about exams, unfamiliar Japanese-history content, dorm life, food, leisure, and school culture. Her Japanese is strong, but not all educational knowledge is equally portable from Sweden.

## 13.2 She has domestic competence and pleasure

She cooks and bakes, enjoys Sumika's pleasure in eating her food, shares tea and sweets, and maintains family-linked food traditions. Body care therefore cannot be reduced to calories or idol optimization.

## 13.3 She works commercially

Sales events expose her to real promotional labor: interviews, sampling, customer conversation, cultural talk, fan conversion, reception work. Her Swedish background can become an asset in commercial communication (`スウェーデンの話を活かしたトークで売上に貢献`).

This is useful for the institutional ledger because the same life history that produces vulnerability can also become labor-market differentiation.

## 13.4 Leisure remains morally difficult but increasingly available

She still tends to turn time into training, and even admired hobbies can be sacrificed when comparison intensifies. Yet other scenes show anime, collaborations, food, festivals, shopping, games, and time with Sumika or the Producer as genuine pleasures rather than mere recovery interventions.

The mature question is not whether leisure “improves performance.” It is whether Lilja can retain a life whose value is not completely justified by performance.

# 14. Failure: from “of course I lost” to grief without resignation

Lilja's changing relationship to failure is one of the best longitudinal indicators of growing self-regard.

Early defeat is easy to explain away:

> others are more talented; I am not; therefore losing is natural.

By P2, the same outcome hurts differently. The final-failure fragment says:

> `これまでは、不合格でも……心のどこかで、納得してました……。`
>
> `仕方ない、って……。わたしなんかじゃ、って……。`
>
> `でも、今回は……合格したかった。`

This is progress because disappointment requires a claim on the result.

The Series-3 selection-failure fragment goes further. When Sumika initially reaches for “it was unreasonable for first-years anyway,” Lilja refuses `仕方ない`. The shared promise gives failure weight. The route then allows emotional disorganization: the Producer recommends time to process rather than immediate optimization.

The Series-3 final-failure branch likewise shows a failed REVERSI result without relational collapse. The promise is not fulfilled, but Sumika and Lilja remain able to imagine starting again.

Dear 036 provides the opposite `D-LILJA` result: REVERSI wins and Lilja becomes Prima Stella. Both must remain available as different result states.

The longitudinal change is therefore:

> **failure moves from confirming inferiority to violating a legitimate desire.**

That makes Lilja more vulnerable to grief, but also more fully agentic.

# 15. Prima Stella: courage becomes public authority, not final proof of essence

Dear 035–036 deliberately mirrors the first meeting.

The same girl who once could not believe the phrase “you have idol talent” can now say:

> `わたしは、アイドルの天才なんだって！`

She enters H.I.F. wanting three things at once:

- fulfill the shared promise with Sumika;
- win together as REVERSI;
- defeat Sumika for the singular Prima Stella.

In `D-LILJA`, REVERSI wins and Lilja receives the title. Sena's explanation is crucial: both performers shone, but Lilja received votes because her performance delivered courage to people, including Sena herself.

This completes the route's central circulation:

1. Sumika gives courage to Lilja.
2. Producer names Lilja's courage as talent.
3. fans and rivals return support.
4. Lilja becomes capable of standing beside Sumika.
5. Lilja and Sumika return courage to one another.
6. Lilja makes the accumulated circuit publicly perceptible.
7. even the prior Prima Stella receives enough courage from Lilja to want to continue.

Her acceptance speech is notably anti-self-made. She credits Sumika, Producer, early audiences, N.I.A. supporters, H.I.F. helpers, and the repeated declaration `わたしはアイドルになります`.

That does not erase Lilja's agency. It identifies agency as **relationally enabled but personally enacted**.

The title also does not stabilize into essence. Dear 037 contains an important deflation:

> today she may briefly have been a top idol; tomorrow she may feel like an ordinary idol again.

The journey continues.

Thus Prima Stella is not “proof she was always secretly destined.” It is evidence that a long chain of received and returned support can produce a real high-level result in one coherent story state.

# 16. Wanting: gratitude develops an ego without losing gratitude

A subtle Lilja problem is that gratitude can become self-minimization.

Early Lilja is frequently satisfied simply to be included, chosen, supported, or allowed to try. She can interpret ambition as presumptuous.

The winter H.I.F. arc requires her to learn a different ethical grammar.

When she discovers that only one member of the winning unit can become Prima Stella, her first instinct is to renounce the title in order to protect the shared dream. Sumika says:

> `欲しいものは欲しいって、そう言っていいんだよ`

This is a character-development hinge because it teaches Lilja that **desire is not theft**.

She can want something scarce without negating love.

She can compete with the person she loves without wishing for Sumika's diminishment.

She can receive help without making eternal gratitude the only acceptable form of selfhood.

The route's growth from “I should be grateful to stand here” to “I want to win” is therefore as important as its growth from technical weakness to competence.

# 19. Counterevidence and falsification targets

The following are required brakes on the current model.

1. **Do not turn courage into an essence.** Lilja says she is not as strong as the Producer thinks. Courage is partly relationally scaffolded.
2. **Do not turn injury into a solved lesson.** Later scenes still show overwork impulses, pleasure sacrifice, and effort fixation.
3. **Do not erase technical hierarchy.** Shion remains dramatically more technically complete in the relevant scenes.
4. **Do not retroactively erase beginnerhood with `魔性`.** Charisma becomes usable only after substantial training and is intermittent.
5. **Do not exoticize Swedishness.** It is family/cultural history and sometimes commercial/visual material, not the explanatory center of her appeal.
6. **Do not universalize D-LILJA victory.** Dear 036 is track authority, not the only valid Series-3 result.
7. **Do not fuse Lilja and Sumika.** The route explicitly develops separate desire and separate dreams.
8. **Do not call Producer dependence resolved.** Mutual care exists, but self-regulation under separation remains untested.
9. **Do not treat public intimacy as ethically neutral.** REVERSI's relationship history is deliberately used as competitive framing.
10. **Do not claim the AV realizes `葛城結界` successfully until the actual music/MV/voice evidence is inspected.**

# 20. Phase-3 provisional character model

## Fear / wound

- basic belief that other people possess legitimate talent and she does not;
- fear that desire is presumptuous;
- fear of public exposure and disappointing people;
- fear that the Producer's belief may be wrong because of her;
- migration/family-distance vulnerability;
- fear that she cannot stand beside Sumika rather than merely behind her.

## Desire

- become an idol worthy of the live that first moved her;
- stand with Sumika and fulfill their promise;
- make the Producer's belief in her meaningful;
- give courage to people who feel afraid or inadequate;
- eventually want the summit personally rather than only relationally.

## Maladaptive strategy

- compensate for perceived lack through sheer hours;
- treat bodily limits as morally suspicious;
- convert insult into proof obligation;
- borrow self-worth almost entirely from trusted others;
- suppress individual desire to preserve relationship harmony.

## Adaptive capacities

- persistence under fear;
- low defensive vanity and high correction uptake;
- ability to form deep trust;
- increasingly specific audience address;
- ability to receive and return support;
- imaginative symbolic authorship;
- capacity to convert rivalry into learning;
- willingness to let love survive differentiation.

## Mature but incomplete state

Lilja can now say she wants Prima Stella, accept charisma as a real capacity, use technique rather than only effort, lead Sumika as well as rely on her, narrate her own history publicly, and conceive of her stage as a space that returns courage.

She has **not** demonstrated a final state of fully internalized self-worth independent of Producer/fan/Sumika support, nor a definitive resolution of overwork risk or career/romantic enclosure.

# 21. Phase-3 thesis to carry forward

> **Lilja does not become an idol by proving weakness was an illusion. She becomes an idol by learning how weakness, technique, courage, imagination, and relationship can be organized into something transmissible. Her defining power is not solitary indomitability but circulation: Sumika, family, Producer, rivals, teachers, and fans give her reasons and structures to move; she makes that movement visible; other people receive courage from it; their response returns strength to her. The final danger is that this loop can sustain her only if effort remains governable and relationship remains differentiated rather than compulsory.**

That formulation should govern later event/support-card reconciliation unless stronger evidence requires revision.
