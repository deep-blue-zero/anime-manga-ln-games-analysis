# 1. Executive thesis — self-authored expectation

The obvious surface reading of Sumika is "a lazy gyaru with enormous talent who learns to work hard." The complete source does not support that model.

> **Shiun Sumika is the idol of self-authored expectation: a former ballet prodigy whose injury taught her that being believed in can feel like a threat, who first survives by making herself unserious and later learns to choose which expectations she will answer, which ideals she will author, and which vulnerabilities she will make public. Her development does not replace a false gyaru with a serious 'true self.' It teaches her to make playfulness, fear, discipline, dependence, ambition, and deliberately performed strength coexist. At maturity, expectation stops being only a trap and becomes a public promise she can answer on her own terms: if someone lacks the courage to step forward, her dance will push their back.**

A useful trajectory is:

> **assigned prodigy future → injury and fracture → expectation as danger → strategic unseriousness → borrowed/relational courage → chosen strong-idol fantasy → social exposure and envy → dependence admitted → institutional belonging chosen → technical rebuilding → expectation relapse → selfishness/self-authorship → artistic coauthorship → public vulnerability → courage through dance → companion/rival differentiation → summit as public promise.**

Several propositions must remain simultaneously true:

1. Sumika's childhood ballet talent was real. Her route is not a hidden-beginner story.
2. Her knee injury was materially serious, and later physical imbalance remains real; however, the deepest persistent barrier becomes psychological anticipation of failure and disappointment.
3. Her bright gyaru sociality is genuine. The defensive mechanism is **strategic unseriousness**, not the falsity of the entire gyaru self.
4. Sumika uses performance deliberately. `強いあたしの仮面` is a chosen beautiful lie intended to become true, not a mask that must simply be removed.
5. Lilja's trust is first experienced as an intolerable obligation, then becomes mutual support, then must mature further into rivalry and differentiated futures.
6. The Producer often enables self-authorship by accepting weakness without defining Sumika by it, but his importance becomes intense enough that dependence/enclosure remains an open ethical question.
7. Sumika's mature courage is not fearlessness. Dear 034 explicitly makes fear the condition under which courage can exist.
8. Her mature performance ethic transforms autobiographical vulnerability into a **vocational promise** rather than raw confession for its own sake.

The central question is therefore not:

> **Will Sumika finally become serious?**

It is:

> **Can Sumika live under other people's hopes without either fleeing them or surrendering authorship of the person she is trying to become?**

# 2. Ballet prodigy: the lost future was real

Sumika begins from a structural position almost inverse to Lilja's.

Lilja starts with dramatically weak idol fundamentals and must accumulate competence. Sumika enters with a history of **recognized elite ability**. Dear 005 describes her as a notable ballet prodigy, someone whom others called `天才`, and the wider corpus later reconstructs serious childhood training, competition, Swedish study, and a life that had naturally begun organizing itself around ballet.

That matters because the injury does not merely remove an activity. It destroys a future that had already been socially written for her.

The right-knee injury is serious. The knee later heals, but Sumika continues experiencing fear when running, jumping, or committing fully to movement. Her body becomes a site of anticipation: even after recovery, forceful action carries the felt possibility that pain will return.

This produces a crucial distinction:

> **The injury is physical fact; the later barrier is not reducible to current tissue damage.**

Sumika herself eventually admits that Hatsuboshi and idolhood initially functioned partly as escape. Ballet was the domain in which expectation, talent, injury, and failure had become inseparable. Becoming an idol gave her a way to approach performance from somewhere else.

But the complete route does not shame that beginning. Dear 005 immediately distinguishes **origin motive** from **current commitment**. Sumika may have entered while avoiding ballet, yet idolhood has become a sincere object of desire.

The later ballet farewell in Dear 010 is therefore not a triumphant return to the "real" art she should always have pursued. She performs ballet one final time because she does not want to leave it as something she only escaped from. Then she chooses idolhood.

The arc is not:

> ballerina → failed ballerina → true ballerina restored.

It is:

> **ballerina → injured former prodigy → evasive adolescent → idol who can thank ballet, leave it behind as a career, and reuse its embodied knowledge without being owned by the abandoned future.**

# 3. The gyaru is real: armor is a function, not an essence claim

V1 was correct to notice armor in Sumika's brightness. V2 requires a more precise formulation.

Ordinary Produce Events repeatedly show that Sumika genuinely likes:

- amusement parks and arcades;
- shopping and styling;
- teasing the Producer;
- television drama and emotional melodrama;
- food and outings;
- hanging out with friends;
- fashion and cute/cool presentation;
- casual public conversation;
- talking to fans as though they were new friends;
- discovering entertaining things in ordinary life.

Her easy social fluency is also professionally useful. In retail, promotional, interview, family-facing, and handshake work, she often succeeds precisely because interaction does not initially feel ceremonious to her. She can approach strangers with low social friction.

This evidence falsifies a simplistic reading in which:

> bright gyaru Sumika = false persona produced only after trauma.

The better claim is:

> **Sumika's social brightness is authentic, but she learns to deploy casualness strategically when seriousness threatens exposure.**

The difference is vital.

When the stakes are low, joking is pleasure.

When the stakes are high, joking can become a pressure-release valve, a way to pre-devalue the result, or a way to deny other people the power to see that she cares.

This is why `サボり` cannot be treated as one stable psychological object. Sometimes Sumika genuinely wants rest, fun, or a less disciplined day. At other times, failing to train preserves an escape hatch:

> If I did not really try, then the result cannot fully judge me.

P1 already makes this visible. After a failure, she can say that because she skipped lessons, perhaps she does not have the right to be fully frustrated. The Producer's reply—then work enough next time that you **can** be frustrated—points toward the route's deeper logic.

Serious effort creates evaluability.

Evaluability is exactly what injured-prodigy Sumika learned to fear.

# 4. Expectation is the real trauma amplifier

Dear 008 gives the route its decisive reinterpretation of the Lilja promise.

Sumika initially frames the problem through injury. The dance game during an outing complicates that explanation: she can move playfully without triggering the same paralysis. The barrier is not simply "dance equals pain."

She eventually says the thing she had been avoiding:

> Lilja's trust felt **heavy**.

Lilja believed Sumika could recover. Lilja believed in the old promise. Lilja's confidence was loving—but to Sumika, love had become attached to the danger of disappointing someone.

The injury provides an objective tragedy. Failure after full recovery would feel different. It could imply:

> I tried completely, and still could not become the person someone believed I was.

This gives Sumika a powerful defensive incentive to remain partially unrealized.

Expectation can then be survived by keeping some distance between the evaluated performance and the deepest self.

This pattern appears at multiple scales:

- Lilja expects the strong Sumika she admires.
- The Producer expects latent greatness.
- classmates remember the prodigy.
- N.I.A. spectators rediscover the ballet genius.
- fans begin expecting elite dance.
- Hatsuboshi competition turns ability into public ranking.

Sumika's problem is therefore not simply low confidence.

It is the ability to distinguish:

> **expectation that supports a chosen project**

from

> **expectation that colonizes the project and writes the required self in advance.**

That is why `self-authored expectation` is the most useful current thesis label.

# 5. The beautiful lie: performance as chosen bridge

Dear 008 also provides one of Gakumas's clearest explicit theories of deliberate self-construction.

Sumika does not decide to stop performing.

She decides to perform **consciously**.

Her formulation is explicit:

> `強いあたしの仮面を被るんだ。理想の自分を演じるの。`

And then:

> `受け入れた上で、嘘をつくの。`
>
> `その嘘が本当になるまで、ね♪`

This is not a confession that the public idol is fraudulent.

The sequence is:

1. acknowledge the frightened self;
2. stop pretending that weakness is absent;
3. choose a stronger ideal anyway;
4. perform toward that ideal;
5. allow repetition, relationships, craft, and lived success to make the performance increasingly true.

The Producer's response matters:

> `ふたりで幻想を作りましょう`

The idol is explicitly a **coauthored fantasy**.

That places Sumika alongside Temari, Mao, and Rinami while preserving her distinctive route logic.

Temari's constructed coolness becomes authored truth.

Mao reclaims the prince after compulsory identification loosens.

Rinami learns that naturalness itself can be produced.

Sumika says: **I know this is a lie; I am choosing it because it names the person I want to practice becoming.**

This is why the endpoint cannot be "remove the mask."

A successful route lets the mask become less necessary as protection while preserving performance as artistry and aspiration.

# 6. P1: from strategic noninvestment to the right to care

The Series-1 route already contains the basic pattern in compressed form.

Early Sumika attempts to lower the stakes. She is casual, skips training, needles the Producer, and repeatedly behaves as though the project matters more to him than to her.

But branch outcomes reveal emotion leaking through that stance.

Failure hurts.

Improvement feels good.

She increasingly trains because she wants to show the Producer and Lilja something better. By the true ending, she gives the Producer handmade cookies specifically because she wants to attempt something herself and show him the result even though it is imperfect.

Her summary is unusually important:

> she had been running from ballet, idolhood, and her best friend—things she actually liked.

The Producer's contribution is not an anti-avoidance command. Sumika explicitly values that he **did not simply tell her not to run**. He stayed beside her and kept supporting the possibility that she could choose to face those things again.

The true ending finally reaches:

> `目指すよ、アイドルのトップ！`

The significance is not that Sumika has become uniformly industrious.

It is that she is willing to let idolhood become important enough that results can wound her.

# 7. Producer relationship: weakness permitted, dependence intensified

The Producer's strongest early intervention is interpretive restraint around weakness.

Dear 007 exposes Sumika's private rule:

> `Pっちには明るいあたしだけ見てほしい`

She believes the weak, crying, frightened version of herself is undesirable information—something no one benefits from seeing.

The Producer does not respond by saying the weak Sumika is the only authentic one. Instead he warns that hiding too much risks losing the actual person inside the protective performance and asks her to rely on him.

This is productive because it expands the range of states Sumika can survive relationally.

The relationship gradually becomes:

**evaluator → witness → regulator → coauthor → emotional refuge → person whose happiness Sumika wants to produce → near-exclusive partner.**

This makes the route ethically stronger and harder at the same time.

Later Sumika can say variations on:

- she wants **this** Producer, not another;
- she cannot imagine her happiness without him beside her;
- she wants him to make her happy into the future.

These lines are not merely romantic-comedy decoration. They indicate that the person who makes vulnerability survivable can become woven into the structure by which the self remains coherent.

At the same time, the Producer repeatedly acts to increase Sumika's authorship. CIDOL 012 / the `Love & Joy` development is especially important: once she asks to design choreography and presentation herself, he deliberately creates more space rather than filling it with his own answers. Sumika initially experiences that as distance because she has become accustomed to strong guidance; later she recognizes that he was trying to draw out her preferences.

This yields an important Phase-3 formulation:

> **The Producer can scaffold autonomy without being external to the dependency problem.**

Both propositions remain true.

# 8. Lilja: love becomes dangerous when belief becomes obligation

Lilja must be analyzed from Sumika's side before REVERSI synthesis.

At the beginning of Sumika's route, Lilja is:

- best friend;
- childhood connection from Sweden;
- person with whom idol aspiration was reactivated;
- object of Sumika's protectiveness;
- believer in Sumika's strength;
- witness to the former prodigy;
- and later an idol whose own seriousness can shame Sumika's avoidance.

The dangerous element is not that Lilja demands cruelly.

It is that **Sumika converts Lilja's faith into an obligation to remain the strong person Lilja believes in.**

Dear 014 brings this to crisis. Sumika tells Lilja that the person she believes in is not present-day Sumika but the old `神童`. That statement must not be frozen as objective relationship truth. It is Sumika's fear.

Lilja rejects the interpretation.

The independent Lilja Phase-3 pass strengthens that rejection: Lilja's mature attachment increasingly concerns Sumika as a changing current person, and the relationship itself becomes healthier through differentiation.

Sumika and Lilja's reconciliation is therefore not:

> Lilja finally stops expecting anything.

It is:

> **Sumika stops treating reliance as betrayal of the role she occupies in Lilja's imagination; Lilja stops accepting secrecy as a condition of closeness.**

The resulting promise is explicit: Sumika will rely on Lilja and the Producer fully rather than protect them through concealment.

This is one of the decisive corrections to CIDOL 007, where Sumika, after seeing how much her weakness hurts Lilja, defensively concludes she should never show weakness again. Dear 014 proves that strategy unsustainable.

# 9. N.I.A. makes expectation social, not just intimate

Dear 011–020 scale Sumika's private expectation problem into public culture.

N.I.A.'s popularity/fan-vote ecology fits Sumika extremely well. She is socially fluent, legible, attractive, and good at accumulating attention. Her following grows quickly.

Then the very thing success creates becomes a new pressure source.

Classmates begin treating her as fundamentally different:

- `天才` returns;
- old social ease becomes harder to maintain;
- friends interpret her changed priorities as distance;
- rapid success produces envy;
- malicious rumors suggest bought votes and connections;
- public visibility turns ordinary friendship into reputational vulnerability.

This is why Shion's attack works.

Shion does not merely challenge Sumika onstage. She engineers isolation and offers another institution as sanctuary.

Sumika's refusal matters because it becomes an institutional choice:

> `あたしは初星学園に残る`
>
> `あたしは"ここ"で、アイドルになる！`

Hatsuboshi is not automatically benevolent. Sumika has already encountered envy, ranking pressure, overwork risk, and a system capable of turning private relationships into performance resources.

But it becomes **chosen belonging** because the relationships she trusts and the idol self she is authoring exist there.

Shion also clarifies Sumika's ethics. Shion's instrumental formulation of fans as things from which money can be extracted is not merely rude; Sumika treats it as a genuine incompatibility of idol philosophy.

By N.I.A. victory, audience support has begun changing valence:

> expectation can lift rather than crush.

But Dear 024–026 prove the change is not permanent or automatic.

# 10. Tsubame loss: identity cannot be reduced to the thing she is best at

Dear 021 reopens the deepest competence wound.

Sumika loses to Amaya Tsubame in a dance-heavy summer selection and immediately asks:

> if she loses in dance, what remains?

That question reveals how much identity has migrated back into the very domain that once injured her.

The Producer correctly says idolhood exceeds dance.

Sumika nevertheless insists on rebuilding dance.

This is not a contradiction. The route refuses both extremes:

> dance is everything

and

> dance does not matter because she has personality.

It matters because it is the craft through which Sumika wants to communicate.

The body itself still contains history. Post-injury avoidance created right/left strength imbalance and movement compensation. Her trauma is therefore neither purely mental nor identical to active injury. It has left a **biomechanical afterlife**.

The response is unglamorous strength training.

This matters because it grounds later transcendence in ordinary physical work.

# 11. Teaching Lilja converts intuition into explicit craft

Dear 023 is valuable for both characters.

Sumika has substantial embodied expertise. But being able to perform something is not the same as being able to explain it.

Coaching Lilja forces her to verbalize:

- posture;
- balance;
- movement quality;
- technical correction;
- efficient training;
- and the difference between disciplined effort and destructive overwork.

This gives Sumika a new relation to the prodigy past.

She no longer has to choose between:

> ballet genius

and

> carefree idol.

She can **translate inherited bodily knowledge into care for another performer**.

The result is not merely Lilja receiving expertise. Teaching makes Sumika's own knowledge more explicit and owned.

It also reverses their old asymmetry. Sumika once experienced Lilja's effort and belief as pressure; later she can regulate Lilja's dangerous overwork without needing to remain invulnerable herself.

# 12. Fan expectation recreates the old trap

Dear 024–026 are the central late test of the route.

After physical reconstruction, Sumika's dancing becomes spectacular. Old ballet footage resurfaces. The public rediscovers the `神童`.

The result is exactly what childhood Sumika learned to fear:

> people now expect something from her again.

She responds by trying to become a technically perfect good girl for those expectations.

The performance is excellent.

It is also deadened.

Temari's diagnosis is concise: meeting fan expectation matters, but if Sumika kills herself to do it, she cannot shine.

This should not be generalized into anti-audience individualism.

The problem is not listening to fans.

The problem is allowing an external model of `紫雲清夏` to become so dominant that Sumika's own desire disappears from the performance.

This is why the Producer starts pushing `わがまま`.

The word is comic at first because Sumika tries to **perform selfishness as another assignment**. That failure is conceptually perfect.

Self-authorship cannot be installed by command.

Dear 026 finally names the problem:

> she is afraid of other people.

More precisely, she knows what betrayed expectations look like and fears becoming their cause.

After injury, being un-expected felt safe:

> **if nobody expects, nobody can be disappointed.**

But idolhood is intrinsically an occupation of expectation.

Sumika therefore needs a different answer.

# 13. The ideal Sumika must be authored from inside

Dear 026 supplies the mature correction to Dear 008.

The Producer summarizes Sumika's method accurately:

> hide weakness and perform the ideal idol.

But then adds the decisive constraint:

> **the ideal Shiun Sumika cannot be decided by other people.**

Everyone will project ideals onto an idol.

The task is not to eliminate projection.

It is to prevent projection from becoming the sole author.

The Producer even distinguishes a professional and personal reason.

Professionally: an idol who only matches prediction cannot exceed expectation.

Personally: he wants Sumika's idol life to be happy.

Sumika notices the boundary crossing.

This scene therefore joins three levels that later synthesis should keep distinct:

1. **artistic** — surprise and self-authorship produce stronger work;
2. **psychological** — Sumika needs wants that do not originate as obligations;
3. **relational/ethical** — the Producer is no longer only describing optimal performance; he is participating in the definition of Sumika's happiness.

The first clearly self-owned wish to emerge is not abstract independence.

It is relational:

> perform H.I.F. together with Lilja.

That is an important warning against equating self-authorship with solitude.

# 14. `カクシタワタシ` to `Love & Joy`: the authorship problem moves into music

Sumika's song communications form an unusually coherent textual sequence even before AV inspection.

## 14.1 `Tame-Lie-One-Step`

The foundational solo already links hesitation and movement.

Sumika likes the upbeat, danceable surface but recognizes lyrics about uncertainty and the next step. The Producer interprets the song as something capable of giving courage; Sumika chooses a delivery intended to combine brightness with gentleness rather than merely maximal energy.

This is the first musical form of:

> frightened person moves anyway.

## 14.2 `カクシタワタシ`

The second major song makes the hidden-self structure explicit.

The Producer wants a song grounded in:

- ballet;
- injury;
- Lilja;
- the frightened self;
- and Sumika's current feelings.

Sumika initially fears that such a song violates her public fantasy.

Yet the production rule is important: the song is designed so that **Sumika herself wants to sing it**.

She participates materially in the lyrics. Her interest is not exposure for exposure's sake. She wants a song that can stay beside someone like her former self.

At this intermediate stage, song becomes a protected channel through which weakness can be communicated while the larger public persona remains strong.

Later Dear material supersedes the idea that weakness must remain confined to song. Sumika eventually chooses public autobiographical disclosure. The earlier strategy was developmental, not final.

## 14.3 `Love & Joy`

CIDOL 012 corresponds to the STEP3 `Love & Joy` development. Its premise is no longer "show the hidden weak Sumika."

Sumika says the major earlier problems have changed:

- trauma is no longer governing her as before;
- she and Lilja can speak honestly;
- she now wants to become stronger.

The new theme is:

> `弱さを強さに変える曲`

Then the production method changes.

Sumika asks to design choreography herself because:

> `あたしが伝えたいことは、あたししかわからない`

She had previously feared that offering opinions to experts would be burdensome or illegitimate.

Now she wants to coauthor:

- lyrics;
- choreography;
- ballet-derived motion;
- styling;
- hair;
- fanservice;
- stage expression.

The Producer deliberately steps farther back to draw out those preferences.

This is the audiovisual object's central future test:

> **Does `Love & Joy` actually look and sound like an authored synthesis of gyaru brightness, ballet line, chosen strength, and weakness-transformed-into-motion?**

Textually, that is what the commu claims.

# 15. REVERSI: relationship history becomes production material

Sumika's first explicit selfish wish produces the REVERSI path.

Dear 027–030 establish a three-person project: Sumika, Lilja, and Producer.

The unit is not simply "best friends singing together."

One of its central production decisions is to make their **real history** legible to audiences and judges.

The Producer gathers childhood photos and constructs a profile/history presentation. Sumika recognizes the logic because she has experienced it as a fan: knowing the invisible work and history behind a performer changes how performance is emotionally received.

The line between truth and marketing therefore becomes intentionally porous.

The story being used is real.

The act of selecting, sequencing, framing, and presenting it is production.

This is a recurring Gakumas institutional problem:

> **authentic intimacy can become competitive/public capital without becoming fabricated.**

Consent is materially present here. That does not make the transformation analytically neutral.

The final REVERSI synthesis should compare this directly with Lilja's experience of the same process.

# 16. Public disclosure: vulnerability becomes vocation

Dear 031–032 provide the mature endpoint of the mask problem.

As Sumika approaches the "ideal" she had once been performing, the ideal becomes harder to define.

That is a success, not a failure.

The strong Sumika can no longer be reduced to:

> never show fear.

She recovers the reason dance matters.

She wants to show that someone who was broken by trauma can stand again.

The resulting vocational formulation is:

> **`ダンスで勇気づけられるアイドル`**

An idol who can give courage through dance.

The performance preparation then moves beyond protected song disclosure. Sumika proposes publicly telling the story she had previously hidden:

- ballet talent;
- Sweden;
- injury;
- depression;
- separation from Lilja;
- idol fandom;
- Hatsuboshi;
- the promise she avoided;
- fear of expectation;
- support;
- recovery.

The Producer warns that the fantasy may shake.

But he also says the earlier lie has gradually become true through the road Sumika has walked.

This is crucial.

Public disclosure does not expose the strong idol as fraudulent.

It changes the meaning of strength.

The strongest Sumika is now someone who can say:

> **I was afraid; I broke; I was supported; I stood again; if you break, watch me and I will try to give you the courage to move.**

That is not raw confession.

It is autobiographical material transformed into a public promise.

# 17. Courage requires fear

Dear 034 makes the route's mature courage concept explicit.

Before H.I.F., Sumika is frightened and cannot sleep.

The Producer does not say:

> you are not afraid.

He says the opposite.

Fear is what makes courage possible:

> if fear does not exist, courage cannot emerge.

And:

> a person capable of encouraging others is someone who knows fear.

This is the decisive philosophical movement away from the early `strong Sumika` mask.

The mature public promise does not rest on fearlessness.

It rests on **action while fear remains real**.

Sumika asks the Producer to hold her hand.

She receives courage.

Then she goes to give courage.

The circuit resembles Lilja's relational courage but is not identical.

Lilja's route foregrounds courage borrowed by a beginner and returned through persistence.

Sumika's route foregrounds **the re-authoring of expectation after a prodigy identity collapsed**.

For Sumika, being seen and believed in used to threaten judgment. At maturity, being seen becomes the condition under which she can promise something outward.

# 18. REVERSI must differentiate to mature

The unit's own name and some intermediate language invite fusion.

CIDOL 017 describes Sumika and Lilja as intimately connected, almost two sides that cannot be separated without changing the whole.

That is emotionally accurate at that story state.

It is **not** a safe final ontology.

Dear 033 makes the needed correction.

Only one performer can become Prima Stella.

Sumika initially thinks of Lilja as companion more readily than rival.

Lilja wants the title.

The Producer also asks Sumika, personally, to win.

The relationship then becomes:

> `仲間でライバル`

and:

> `手を取り合いながら、競い合う`

This is the same V2 correction already visible from Lilja's side.

REVERSI's healthy endpoint is not:

> two incomplete people finally become one unit.

It is:

> **two people whose histories are constitutive of each other become capable of wanting incompatible outcomes without interpreting difference as abandonment.**

Dear 037 continues this by allowing the old promise to end and separate future idol ideals to emerge.

The later Pro-level REVERSI synthesis should therefore treat **fusion language as a developmental state**, not a metaphysical truth.

# 19. Prima Stella: summit as public promise, not proof of fearless completion

Dear 036 belongs to `D-SUMIKA` only.

Within that track, REVERSI wins and Sumika becomes Prima Stella.

The victory does not establish that:

- Sumika is now free of fear;
- she is the permanent strongest dancer;
- all alternate P3 outcomes disappear;
- or her relationship dependencies have been solved.

What it establishes is that the route's authored performance ethic has reached public ratification.

Sena says Sumika's dance gave even her courage.

That matters because the route has spent so long distinguishing:

> being technically impressive

from

> making the technical act mean something to someone else.

Sumika's speech then returns to the language of becoming:

> `ねぇみんな！ あたし、強くなったよ！`

But strength is immediately redistributed outward:

> become the self you want and live happily;

and if you lack courage to take the next step:

> `キミの背中は、あたしが押してあげるからね！`

This is **summit-as-promise**.

The title gives Sumika a larger audience to whom she can answer the expectation she has finally chosen to accept.

# 20. Dear 037: the proof comes back from the Producer

The post-victory scene closes the relational circuit.

The original Sumika needed idols to give her courage after injury.

Later she wants to become the kind of idol who can do that for someone else.

In Dear 037, she asks the Producer—briefly not as her Producer—to tell her what he actually felt.

He admits insecurity and moments when he nearly gave up.

Then he tells her that **her lives gave him courage**.

This becomes the most direct validation of Sumika's mature goal:

> if her performance gave him courage, then she has become the person she wanted to become.

The proof is not a score.

It is relational effect.

The scene also preserves the dependency problem. Sumika wants continued support and asks him to keep making her happy into the future.

That should not be dismissed as merely romance-coded banter, nor automatically condemned as unhealthy.

It should remain an open structural question:

> **Can the person who helped Sumika stop being governed by other people's expectations remain central to her happiness without becoming another external author she cannot safely lose?**

# 21. Japanese register — textual observations only

Sumika's written register is one of the clearest character systems in the corpus even before voice acting is inspected.

Recurring features include:

- clipped casual contractions;
- `～じゃん`, `～っしょ`, `マ？`, `ウケる`, `ダルい`, `ハズい`;
- playful naming of the Producer as `Pっち`;
- teasing pseudo-romantic framing (`デート`);

The most important interpretive rule is:

> **casual register is not equivalent to low seriousness.**

Sumika can articulate the route's most difficult insights without abandoning gyaru speech.

That matters because a "become serious" reading might incorrectly expect linguistic purification.

Instead, mature Sumika gains the ability to say serious things **in her own established voice**.

Textual hypothesis for AV:

- defensive brightness;

- private disclosure;

- fan-directed encouragement;
- romantic/intimate Producer address;

may form audibly distinct performance states. This remains unverified until direct audio inspection.

Sumika contributes several important Phase-8 institutional questions.

- mental/trauma awareness;

## 22.4 Producer authority works partly through controlled distance

CIDOL 012 makes the Producer's developmental technique explicit: he sometimes supplies strong direction, and sometimes deliberately withdraws it to force Sumika to state preferences.

That is sophisticated pedagogy.

Because Sumika is emotionally dependent on his approval, it is also governance.

The later synthesis should preserve both descriptions.

# 26. Phase-3 bounded conclusion

The strongest current formulation is:

> **Sumika does not mature by discovering that the cheerful gyaru was false, nor by recovering the invulnerable ballet prodigy she used to be. She becomes capable of consciously authoring a public ideal while keeping the frightened person inside its history. Her old problem was not expectation itself but expectation experienced as a verdict written by someone else. Her mature idolhood changes the grammar: she chooses the promise, chooses the people whose belief she will answer, chooses what vulnerability becomes public, and increasingly helps author the performance through which the promise is delivered.**

The climax therefore returns to a paradox already latent in Dear 008.

The strong Sumika began as a lie.

But the route never asks whether the lie was metaphysically pure.

It asks what happens when a frightened person practices a chosen ideal long enough, with enough craft and enough relational support, that the performance accumulates a real history.

By Dear 037, the answer is not abstract.

The Producer says her performance gave **him** courage.

That means the project has crossed from self-protection into social efficacy.

Sumika has become capable of doing for another person what idols once did for her.

The remaining challenge is whether that authored expectation can stay hers when the audience becomes larger, the title becomes heavier, the Producer becomes more indispensable, and REVERSI's two members continue becoming people with dreams that no longer perfectly coincide.

That is the correct handoff from Phase 3 into AV verification, later Lilja/Sumika comparative synthesis, events/support-card ecology, and ultimately the Phase-7 monograph.
