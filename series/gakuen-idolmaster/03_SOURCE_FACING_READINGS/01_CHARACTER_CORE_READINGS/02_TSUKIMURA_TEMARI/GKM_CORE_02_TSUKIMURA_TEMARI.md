# GKM CORE 02 — TSUKIMURA TEMARI / 月村手毬

## 0. Executive thesis

Tsukimura Temari is best understood as the **idol of authored combustion**.

She is not naturally the serene, self-contained, emotionally cold “cool beauty” her first impression suggests. Nor is that public identity merely a fraudulent mask concealing one authentic, lazy, childish girl underneath. Temari constructs herself because she cannot tolerate the person she remembers having been: dependent, undisciplined, physically heavy, spoiled, unpleasant, without a strength she could respect, and unable to imagine that anyone—including herself—might admire her. Idolhood gives her a form toward which she can live.

The resulting character is built from a series of apparently impossible coexistences:

- she calls herself lazy while working with punitive intensity;
- she wants to be independent while requiring unusually intimate bodily and emotional support;
- she hates being restrained while repeatedly exceeding safe limits;
- she imitates admired idols in order to become herself;
- she claims coolness while singing through emotional overexposure;
- she resents care that makes her feel weak while longing to be accurately known;
- she wants first place partly to escape self-hatred, yet can only reach it through people who remember the self she wants to escape;
- she insists that loss is loss, but allows relationships to carry feelings she cannot yet hold alone;
- she loves food with almost childlike transparency while turning eating, weight, reward, discipline, and shame into a moral system.

The governing image is not ice. Temari eventually rejects the socially expected idea of `クール` as coldness, emotional thinness, or distance. The Producer describes her instead as a **blue flame burning at extremely high temperature**: not calm because little is happening, but visually controlled because everything is being consumed. Temari recognizes that description as the first accurate language for the idol she has been trying to make.

Her longitudinal movement is therefore not adequately described as:

> fake cool girl → honest needy girl

or even:

> self-hatred → self-acceptance.

It is closer to:

> hated, dependent prior self  
> → artificial wings built from imitation, discipline, and punitive ascent  
> → solo independence that mistakes care for containment  
> → renewed rivalry in which Misuzu and Kaya Rinha become people she can need without surrendering the race  
> → defeat and re-acceleration under Juo Sena’s title  
> → a winter-H.I.F. Temari able to say that **today’s self is the self she likes most**, while immediately naming another horizon.

That final self-love is real but not unconditional. Temari does not discover a stable inner essence that deserves love regardless of movement. She learns something narrower and, for her, more credible: that the self she has deliberately made—through failure, imitation, help, appetite, rivalry, song, and chosen performance—is not false merely because it is constructed. She can love the current Temari without declaring the work finished.

This distinction revises the strongest claim from the V1 analysis. V1 correctly identified her as a “lazy person who works harder than almost anyone,” and correctly treated her artificial wings as meaningful rather than counterfeit. But the phrase “fabricated cool identity” risks reproducing Temari’s own shame: as though the needy girl were true and the idol were only defensive theater. The V2 corpus instead supports:

> **Temari’s coolness is an authored truth.**

It is real because she chose it, practiced it, failed inside it, let other people contest it, and made it communicable through performance. The stage does not hide her authentic self. It is the medium in which feelings she cannot say directly become speakable.

Her central ethical problem remains unresolved. The same system that lets her fly can reward self-destruction. The Producer succeeds partly because he does not simply forbid full combustion; he redesigns her body, stamina, repertoire, food, rest, publicity, and competitive schedule so that the combustion can continue longer. Temari experiences this as propulsion rather than enclosure. Yet her consent is not always obtained in advance, the relationship becomes intensely exclusive, and her recovery from dependency may consist partly in transferring dependency from SyngUp! to one Producer who organizes nearly every condition of her life.

A more rigorous provisional thesis is therefore:

> **Temari is the idol of authored combustion: a girl who builds artificial wings because she cannot love the self that stands still, then learns that imitation, dependence, bodily need, and emotional exposure do not invalidate those wings. Her development lies not in becoming natural, effortless, or finally at rest, but in making the constructed self answerable to rivals, fans, friends, and a Producer—and in discovering that a chosen performance can be one of the most truthful things a person does.**

This Phase-3 document establishes that model from Temari’s core source families. It does not yet incorporate every numbered event or support card, does not settle the final ethics of Temari/Misuzu/Rinha or Temari/Producer dependency, and makes no direct sonic or acting claim until the requested audiovisual material is supplied.

# 2. Story-state overview

## 2.1 Initial state: former No. 1, present collapse

Temari enters Hatsuboshi carrying a prestigious public history and a humiliating private present. She had been called the top middle-school idol, but the current body cannot sustain the old performance. Weight gain, skipped meals, deteriorated grooming, emotional exhaustion, low stamina, and the aftermath of SyngUp!’s destruction have made her visibly worse than the previous year.

She responds to this not by requesting comfort but by controlling access. She tells the Producer that allowing him to produce her does not mean she will obey any instruction she finds unconvincing. Yet the quickest way through that defense is expert evaluation. She cannot resist information about her own performance. When the Producer offers a technically grounded diagnosis, her desire to know defeats her desire to remain aloof.

The early pattern is already complete:

> resist authority → demand evidence → react emotionally to the diagnosis → accept the plan if it advances the idol → deny that acceptance constitutes trust.

## 2.2 STEP1/P1: independence produced through intimate management

The first route does not simply train Temari harder. It reconstructs the conditions under which her dangerous strength can be used.

The Producer intervenes in:

- food quantity and composition;
- weight reduction without starvation;
- grooming and daily presentation;
- recovery;
- stamina;
- rehearsal pacing;
- emotional stress;
- the very meaning of full-power singing.

This is paradoxical. Temari’s declared goal is to stop depending on others, particularly the former unitmates whose support made her feel like dead weight. Yet her solo independence is created through one person assuming an unusually comprehensive caretaking and regulatory role.

The difference, from Temari’s point of view, is not the absence of dependence. It is that the Producer makes dependence feel like **training toward autonomy**, whereas SyngUp! care felt like evidence that autonomy was impossible.

## 2.3 STEP2/N.I.A.: the past becomes a public rival field

N.I.A. forces Temari to confront the two people whose care and excellence had become entangled with shame.

Kaya Rinha is not merely a former unitmate. She is:

- the living link to the H.I.F. performance that made Temari want to become an idol;
- the singer and “cool” figure Temari imitated;
- the person who absorbed public hatred after SyngUp!’s collapse;
- an idol attempting to turn her remaining career into an ending;
- a rival whose disappearance would make Temari’s own derivation feel orphaned.

Misuzu is not merely a caretaker. She is:

- the childhood companion who first shared Temari’s dream;
- the person who knew and managed her bodily needs;
- a singer Temari underestimated because Misuzu rarely exerted herself visibly;
- someone whose care became intolerable because it could make Temari feel permanently childlike;
- a rival who must stop yielding if the relationship is to become survivable.

Temari’s N.I.A. victories do not prove that she was right to reject them. They make a new relationship form possible: she can beat, admire, need, and be needed without returning to the exact structure that destroyed SyngUp!.

## 2.4 STEP3 and summer H.I.F.: defeat produces a new pursuit

Misuzu’s solo performance reveals a frightening possibility: the person Temari thought was standing behind her can generate a world in which Temari becomes a spectator. Temari does not merely fear losing. She experiences the pleasure and humiliation of becoming Misuzu’s fan.

Juo Sena ultimately wins the summer Prima Stella title. Temari refuses consolatory moral accounting. She acknowledges that her own presence may have changed the field and that the Producer’s work may have helped produce the winning conditions; nevertheless, `負けは負け`—a loss is a loss.

The key development is that Temari no longer interprets another person’s superiority only as evidence that she should disappear. She converts Misuzu’s awakening, Sena’s victory, and Rinha’s return into the next structure of pursuit.

## 2.5 Winter H.I.F.: present-tense self-love

By the winter H.I.F., Temari’s route no longer asks whether she can become independent of everyone who formed her. It asks whether she can stand at the summit while naming those formations as hers.

She beats Saki and treats that victory as a defining class rivalry rather than the destruction of Saki. She eats food prepared through intimate knowledge, accepts help with hated vegetables, lets Misuzu prepare her, confronts Rinha’s continued fan support, and asks the Producer to call her `手毬` without the distancing honorific.

When she becomes Prima Stella, she does not promise to preserve the title unchanged. She says she will make it greater, attack higher, and sing as both the titleholder and **Tsukimura Temari**. The final state is not rest. It is the ability to say:

> today’s Temari is the Temari I like most

without needing that sentence to mean:

> tomorrow must remain the same.

## 2.6 U1: unit belonging through abrasive equality

Unit Story provides a parallel proof that Temari can live in a group without reproducing SyngUp! exactly.

She initially rejects the shared Producer and the unit with open hostility. The arrangement threatens both exclusive Producer access and the unresolved memory of being the weak center supported by stronger members. Saki’s response is not soothing. Saki demands more effort, gives Temari the harsh phrase she secretly wants, and treats her as someone capable of catching up.

Temari eventually accepts because the dream remains. Within Re;IRIS she:

- claims center on singing ability and then accepts rotating centers;
- shares a room and vulnerable self-history with Kotone;
- argues with Saki over food and the treatment of people as machines;
- learns recipient-specific care rather than generic optimization;
- admits that Kotone can lead the unit;
- contributes her voice as repayment and as collective force;
- nearly exits when center order triggers insecurity;
- uses provocation to reactivate Saki after defeat;
- tells Misuzu to stop yielding and become a rival.

`U1` does not prove that all SyngUp! wounds are resolved. It proves that Temari can inhabit a unit when conflict, role distribution, and dependence remain speakable.

# 3. Source-family readings

## 3.1 Produce Story: conditional outcomes and the body that cannot be ignored

### 3.1.1 Temari’s problem is not lack of singing talent

The growth material and route dialogue consistently treat her vocal ability as exceptional. Her principal limitations are bodily sustainability, performance variance, emotional regulation, and the tendency to equate “real effort” with the edge of collapse.

This makes her unlike a straightforward beginner. A poor Producer could improve her averages by suppressing the dangerous upper range. The effective Producer instead attempts to preserve the extraordinary peak while building a body and schedule capable of surviving it.

That strategy is one reason Temari experiences him as different from Misuzu and Rinha. He does not say, “stop flying.” He says, in effect, “we will redesign the aircraft.” The ethical difficulty is that an aircraft metaphor can also reduce a person to a performance system.

### 3.1.2 The P1 result ladder distinguishes pride from delusion

The mutually exclusive branches reveal a stable standard without forming one biography.

- In failure, Temari initially claims she is not depressed, seeks technical explanation, and is genuinely hurt when the Producer names the performance as disastrous. The hurt becomes a renewed vow rather than proof that criticism was unwanted.
- In a near-top result, she recognizes objective quality but refuses to call non-victory victory.
- In middling states, she is angered by the gap between present standing and the top-idol objective.
- In a barely successful state, fear and self-disgust become explicit: she does not want another result that gives her reason to hate herself.
- In first-place/true-labeled states, success does not end the evaluation. She asks what comes next and expands “world’s best to the Producer” into a world-scale goal.

The invariant is not that Temari is impossible to satisfy. It is that evaluation matters because it answers an identity question: **is she moving toward or away from the person she can respect?**

### 3.1.3 Food is not comic decoration

Food in the Produce routes operates simultaneously as:

- nutritional necessity;
- feared weight gain;
- evidence of regression;
- pleasure;
- reward;
- care;
- surveillance;
- bargaining instrument;
- a state in which her emotional transparency becomes difficult to hide.

When the Producer says he has prepared meals that will restore her best condition, Temari’s resistance collapses almost instantly. The speed is funny, but the underlying structure is serious. She wants permission to eat without believing she has abandoned the idol. The Producer’s calculations temporarily make pleasure morally safe.

### 3.1.4 Rest is accepted most easily when renamed as work

Temari struggles to value rest as an end. She can accept it when it is framed as optimal training, future performance, or a controlled “reward day.” This is a practical solution and a psychological compromise.

The unresolved question is whether she can ever experience leisure that need not justify itself through later output.

### 3.1.5 P2 and P3 preserve the same failure language at larger scale

N.I.A. and H.I.F. expand the stakes but do not erase the old fear. Under decisive failure she returns to formulations such as “when it matters, I lose” and “I hate myself,” then asks the Producer to remain and begin again.

The significant change is not that the self-hating language disappears. It is that failure no longer expels every relationship. The request to start over is itself evidence that she can remain in contact while ashamed.

## 3.2 Dear Idol: the longitudinal argument of the character

### 3.2.1 The Producer selects a known problem, not an untouched elite

Temari knows that information about her old conflict and current decline exists. The Producer’s decision to approach after reviewing that material matters because selection is no longer based solely on the legendary middle-school reputation. He chooses a difficult present and a dangerous future.

This creates the basis for trust: not unconditional approval, but the belief that accurate negative knowledge does not automatically end selection.

### 3.2.2 STEP1 reconstructs the body before it reconstructs the biography

The earliest intervention is concrete:

- she has gained weight;
- she is skipping meals;
- her stamina is poor;
- grooming has deteriorated;
- mental exhaustion is reducing performance;
- SyngUp!’s collapse remains the causal wound.

The body is the first archive of the breakup. Before Temari can narrate what happened, it is already visible in sleep, hair, clothing, appetite, weight, and breath.

### 3.2.3 `恩は返します` is Temari’s first usable trust language

Temari refuses to say she trusts the Producer. Instead she says that she decides what counts as a debt and that debts will be repaid. This is not mere tsundere comedy. `恩` gives her a relationship form that preserves agency.

To say “I trust you” would expose dependence. To say “I will repay what you did” allows connection to be structured as chosen obligation.

Later, song becomes the most important repayment medium.

### 3.2.4 SyngUp! was destroyed by asymmetry made unspeakable

Temari’s memory of the old unit is organized by inferiority. She could occupy the apparent center because Rinha improvised around her and Misuzu watched, supported, and regulated her. The arrangement let Temari finish performances that her body could not sustain alone.

The other members did not necessarily experience this as carrying dead weight. Temari did. Their kindness therefore became evidence against her desired self.

The breakup was not simply:

> Temari selfishly rejected kind friends.

Nor was it:

> Misuzu and Rinha suppressed Temari’s rightful full power.

It was a failure to create a form in which three truths could coexist:

1. Temari’s peak was real and worth protecting.
2. the support was real and necessary;
3. necessity did not have to mean permanent hierarchy or humiliation.

Because those truths were not fought over honestly, resentment accumulated until the only available fight was catastrophic and public.

### 3.2.5 Misuzu’s care was both accurate and intolerable

Misuzu correctly perceives Temari’s self-destructive performance. She also manages food, sleep, grooming, pace, and emotional state so completely that Temari can experience the care as a return to childhood dependence.

Temari’s accusation is therefore not that Misuzu does not love her. The very precision of the love is the problem. Misuzu knows what Temari needs before Temari can claim authorship over needing it.

The mature relationship requires Misuzu to remain capable of care while ceasing to define herself as Temari’s wings.

### 3.2.6 Temari’s confession that she is lazy is a theory of dependence

When Temari says she cannot function in a unit because she is lazy, she does not mean she avoids effort in ordinary observable terms. She means:

> if someone will catch me, I will lean;
> if someone will regulate me, I may stop regulating myself;
> if someone is kind enough, I may become the person I hate.

This is why support initially looks like sabotage. The feared danger is not merely external control; it is her own desire to surrender control.

### 3.2.7 The childhood idol encounter creates both dream and curse

The H.I.F. performance Temari sees as a child appears goddess-like. The world becomes song, ordinary perception disappears, and Kaya Rinha’s words give the event a future tense: someday she too will sing that way.

Temari calls those words like a curse because they make a previously impossible desire imaginable. She is already disliked by herself; now she has an image of the self she might fail to become.

The same event produces Misuzu’s dream. SyngUp! therefore begins not from professional convenience but from a shared revelation whose meaning differs for each girl.

### 3.2.8 Artificial wings do not become natural

Temari’s route repeatedly uses wings as the image of aspiration, support, and constructed capacity. The mature correction is not that the wings become biologically hers.

The people around her offer different wing models:

- Misuzu risks becoming the wings on which Temari depends;
- Rinha supplies a model to imitate;
- the Producer engineers conditions of flight;
- fans supply lift and destination;
- rivalry gives altitude meaning;
- Temari ultimately says she will become a rocket rather than wait for natural wings.

The metaphor shifts from authenticity to function. The question becomes not “were these wings born from the true self?” but “who controls them, what do they cost, and can they carry her without erasing the people who built them?”

### 3.2.9 Rinha is origin, rival, protector, and wounded derivative relation

Temari’s attachment to Rinha is unusually layered.

She admires Rinha’s song, dancing, coolness, and proximity to Kaya Kei. She models her public character on Rinha. She also needs to defeat Rinha so imitation can become authorship.

Rinha, meanwhile, has absorbed hostility after the SyngUp! rupture and attempts to convert her remaining idol career into an ending that transfers fans elsewhere. Temari’s response is not dignified acceptance. She cries, demands that the admired idol return, reveals the extent of her imitation, and wounds Rinha with the discovery that she—not only her sister—was Temari’s actual model.

The relationship becomes healthier only when both can be origins and future rivals rather than original and copy.

### 3.2.10 Fans make the old unit socially real

A SyngUp! fan who remains happy that Temari continues singing forces a correction. Temari nearly treats the fan’s attachment as invalid because Misuzu and Rinha are absent. Saki and Kotone redirect her toward the fan’s actual statement.

The important principle is:

> the unit ended, but the performance reached someone;
> therefore the past cannot be declared meaningless by the people who made it.

Temari must learn that fans may preserve several idols and several eras without betraying any of them.

### 3.2.11 Plan A and Plan B expose the difference between mystique and attraction

The Producer initially attempts to create scarcity and mystery around Temari. Temari enjoys the grandness of the idea. The plan collapses through leaked conflict and her inability to sustain controlled distance.

The replacement strategy—abrasive, boastful, combustible ace—appears less dignified but proves more truthful to the public Temari actually generates. Her poor behavior and extraordinary performance become part of the attraction.

This does not mean all misconduct is authentic or valuable. It means the marketable persona succeeds when it gives form to a real contradiction rather than merely suppressing it.

### 3.2.12 Misuzu’s performance makes Temari a fan

Temari’s reaction to Misuzu’s summer-H.I.F. performance is one of the route’s most important reversals. She can no longer preserve the childhood model in which Misuzu is the sleepy caretaker behind her.

To become Misuzu’s fan is both admiration and humiliation. It reveals that Temari can be moved into the audience position by someone she had treated as relational infrastructure.

The mature rivalry requires Misuzu to become a person Temari must chase, not merely a person who keeps Temari alive.

### 3.2.13 Sena’s victory separates causal importance from victory

The Producer suggests that Temari’s pressure changed the field and may have helped produce the conditions under which Sena became Prima Stella. Temari refuses to substitute causal importance for the title.

This protects the seriousness of competition. It also shows that relational contribution and scoreboard loss can both be true.

Temari’s `負けは負け` is not nihilism. It prevents consolatory narratives from stealing the motivating clarity of defeat.

### 3.2.14 STEP3 turns reliance into declared method

In the later training material Temari can say, without collapse, that she has always relied on Misuzu and Rinha and that she relies on Misuzu every day. She can discard pride instrumentally if doing so helps her improve.

This is a major change from STEP1. Dependence is no longer automatically equivalent to regression because the relationship has acquired rivalry, refusal, and explicit negotiation.

The claim must remain provisional until Misuzu’s own pass tests whether the dependence is genuinely less totalizing.

### 3.2.15 Winter H.I.F. integrates rather than purifies

The late route gathers:

- the disliked child;
- the middle-school center;
- Rinha’s copy;
- Misuzu’s dependent friend;
- the Producer’s difficult assigned idol;
- Saki’s rival;
- China and Hiro’s admired senior/friend;
- a fan of other idols;
- someone who loves food and hates vegetables;
- a singer who can say what speech cannot;
- a titleholder who still wants the next contest.

Temari wins not by discarding those versions but by making their coexistence part of `月村手毬として`—as Tsukimura Temari.

## 3.3 Idol Communications: song as the truth medium of a constructed person

### 3.3.1 `Luna say maybe`: denial before recognition

Temari first claims that the song’s twisted, emotionally inarticulate girl is unlike her and that technical skill will be enough. The surrounding behavior immediately contradicts this pose: she is delighted by the song, sings it repeatedly, and eventually acknowledges the synchronization.

The song’s dramatic function is not to reveal a hidden “real Temari” that invalidates her coolness. It gives the cool idol a form in which inarticulacy itself can become performance.

### 3.3.2 `アイヴイ`: the Producer’s gaze becomes a public artifact

Temari entrusts the promotional video to the Producer and asks him to show how he sees her. The resulting concept includes both ordinary weakness and stage coolness.

The important assertion is:

> many Temaris exist; the idol is the person who brings the coolest one to the stage.

This is one of the strongest textual rejections of an authentic-self/mask binary. The stage self is selected and intensified, not therefore false.

### 3.3.3 `仮装狂騒曲`: pretending can become practice

The Halloween communication places Temari inside literal costume construction and a public making-of process. Her lack of craft skill threatens the image; the Producer makes the imperfection part of the project.

They discuss bad personalities and the possibility that pretending to be good for long enough may create real goodness. Temari extends that logic to performance: the idol is the best costume because it lets an intended self become repeatable.

This is not an argument that morality is whatever one performs. It is an argument that repeated chosen conduct can become constitutive.

### 3.3.4 `Campus mode!!`: tradition as comparative burden

Temari calls the song Hatsuboshi itself. Singing it requires resolve because every version is compared with seniors and inherited memory.

The song also returns her to Rinha’s instruction and the period in which the three girls imitated Kaya Kei. To perform it now is to prove independence without denying derivation.

### 3.3.5 Communication 009: roots refuse solemn purity

Temari narrates the H.I.F. encounter, SyngUp!’s formation, and the birth of the shared dream. She then abruptly detours into an intensely specific account of encountering `ちくわぶ`.

The comedy is not a failure of the character study. It prevents the origin myth from becoming a sanitized sacred biography. Food, embarrassment, bodily preference, and attraction to the Producer belong to the same person as the transcendent idol encounter.

When asked for roots, she eventually admits that episodes involving the Producer keep coming to mind. The relationship has become part of the story through which she explains herself.

### 3.3.6 `雨上がりのアイリス`: exclusive assignment and first fanservice

The unit dream reveals that Saki and Kotone have become imaginable partners. The Producer responds with jealousy toward his own dream counterpart, and Temari reverses care by asking him to say everything.

Her formulation—`あなたの担当アイドルは、夢と違って……私だけなんです。私で我慢してください`—mirrors the Producer’s earlier “settle for me” language. The pair uses mock concession to express singular choice.

The following meal, jealousy confession, and first blown kiss make the reciprocity unusually explicit. They also intensify the enclosure question: both define the relation partly through exclusion of other possible Producers/idols.

### 3.3.7 `がむしゃらに行こう！`: competition among friends

Temari says Kotone may be unbeatable “as a human,” describes Saki and Kotone with intimate precision, and then treats a shared-song performance as another contest.

The Producer calls them a friendly trio. Temari resists the wording while supplying the evidence. She can now experience friendship through continued comparison rather than seeing closeness as the end of rivalry.

### 3.3.8 Communication 016 / STEP3 song: emotional range becomes a technical objective

The SyngUp! training with Misuzu and China is not simply about singing harder. It expands the meanings of “putting feeling into a song.”

Temari wants:

- China’s ability to deliver gentleness;
- Misuzu’s ability to control audience feeling;
- her own full-body emotional force.

She asks others to teach her without treating the request as humiliation. Technical maturity here means acquiring multiple emotional vectors rather than making every song an act of self-immolation.

### 3.3.9 `ガラクタロード`: coolness is reauthored as blue flame

Temari worries that her self-recognition may be wrong because the song and the people around her expose heat, comedy, greed, and emotional excess.

The Producer distinguishes socially conventional coolness from their chosen coolness. He names her not as ice but as blue flame: complete combustion at extreme heat.

Temari understands that she had been dragged by the existing category. She can instead make her version so compelling that it becomes the category’s future reference point.

This is the clearest textual support for **authored coolness**.

## 3.4 Produce Events: ordinary life as the stress test of the ideal

### 3.4.1 Temari is least cool when she is trying hardest to preserve coolness

Ghost stories, romantic misunderstanding, jealousy, games, sweets, shared umbrellas, and the Producer’s attention repeatedly make her composure collapse.

These scenes should not be treated only as gap-moe. They show the maintenance cost of the image and the speed with which attachment bypasses it.

### 3.4.2 Leisure becomes compulsion

Temari can turn music games, karaoke, practice hacks, and relaxation plans into all-or-nothing training. Even sleep optimization can become another form of overwork.

This confirms that the problem is not the external schedule alone. She converts almost any activity into an evaluative system.

### 3.4.3 Public performance competence does not generalize automatically to service work

She can sing, narrate, prepare from a script, and project authority. Ordinary distribution, customer service, and socially smooth work are less natural. She is often honest where commercial politeness might prefer concealment.

The distinction is useful: stage legibility is a specialized achievement, not proof of generalized social ease.

### 3.4.4 Food remains the shortest route to unguarded response

Sweets, juice, ramen, curry, tonkatsu, and reward meals make her anticipation and pleasure immediately audible in the text. Weight anxiety follows almost as quickly.

Food is therefore where the idealized future and the feared prior body collide most routinely.

### 3.4.5 Temari can care through technical sincerity

She is eager to sing for children, prepares seriously for fan-facing work, gives direct advice, and chooses song as a gift when she does not know what object the Producer would want.

Her care is often more convincing when she can translate it into performance or concrete action than when she must state affection directly.

## 3.5 Live, growth, and startup material

The compact live lines preserve her basic public logic:

- give everything;
- go toward the Producer/audience;
- ask whether the song reached;
- interpret exhaustion as evidence of expenditure;
- treat failure as unacceptable information rather than an identity to accept.

Growth dialogue confirms the asymmetry between exceptional song and still-developing stamina. Birthday material reveals the rapid shift from childlike excitement over cake to an attempt to restore composure. For the Producer’s birthday, she offers a private song because song is the form of value she trusts most.

## 3.6 Unit Story: an alternate laboratory for dependence

`U1` exposes Temari’s exclusive and unit-averse tendencies more directly than the solo route.

She does not want the Producer divided. She does not want another unit after SyngUp!. She assumes vocal superiority should make her center. Yet she can revise after evidence:

- Saki’s competence and refusal to coddle;
- Kotone’s leadership and social intelligence;
- the need for different centers by song;
- the unit’s ability to create force beyond individual addition.

Her conflict with Saki over food is particularly important. Temari accuses Saki of treating people like machines. This is not simply a joke about vegetables. Temari—whose own body has been heavily managed—can recognize the ethical failure of generic optimization when directed at someone else.

Her later challenge to Misuzu also shows maturation. She does not ask Misuzu merely to return as caretaker. She demands that Misuzu stop yielding and become someone who can defeat her.

# 4. Authored coolness

## 4.1 `キャラ作り` is not proof of fraud

Temari openly identifies Rinha as a model for her cool character construction. That fact can be read as exposure: the icy singer was copied.

The stronger reading is developmental. Most artistic identities begin through imitation. The moral and aesthetic question is whether the imitation remains borrowed prestige or becomes a form capable of answering new experience.

Temari’s coolness becomes hers when:

- she understands what she was actually imitating;
- Rinha learns that Temari admired *her*, not only Kaya Kei;
- the persona survives the revelation of neediness and appetite;
- songs give the persona emotional content Rinha’s model did not supply;
- Temari defines coolness as blue-flame combustion rather than inherited coldness.

## 4.2 The private girl does not invalidate the public idol

Temari’s private behavior includes:

- crying;
- jealousy;
- food obsession;
- fear of ghosts and thunder;
- poor grooming;
- childish dependence;
- bad excuses;
- awkward fanservice;
- delight at praise;
- transparent need for the Producer.

The text repeatedly makes these qualities funny. It does not conclude that the stage is a lie.

The stage is where she can organize those unstable feelings into something controlled enough to reach another person.

## 4.3 Coolness as total combustion

The blue-flame metaphor matters because blue flame appears visually controlled while indicating extreme heat. It joins apparent opposites:

- elegance and violence;
- restraint and intensity;
- clarity and consumption;
- technical precision and emotional overexposure.

It also preserves danger. Complete combustion can illuminate, but it can also use all available fuel.

## 4.4 The category becomes aspirational rather than descriptive

Temari wants to become the reference point by which future people understand `クール`. This is not simply vanity. It converts a received genre category into an authored project.

The risk is that identity remains permanently dependent on public proof. If she must remake the category continuously, she may never be allowed an unperformed self.

# 5. Self-hatred, effort, and present-tense self-love

## 5.1 The hated child is a remembered construction too

Temari’s childhood account—fat, lazy, spoiled, unpleasant, talentless—should not be accepted as a neutral objective profile. It is the memory through which current discipline justifies itself.

Some features are directly supported: dependency, food pleasure, low self-regulation, social difficulty. But “no strengths” is contradicted by later evidence that her emotional intensity, ear, will, and capacity for song existed in latent form.

The prior self may be as narratively simplified as the public cool idol.

## 5.2 Effort is both liberation and punishment

Temari likes herself when she is approaching the ideal. This gives effort positive emotional meaning: action can create a self she respects.

It also means rest threatens identity. If self-love is generated through approach, stopping can feel like reversal.

This is why her work ethic cannot be praised without qualification. It is admirable because it is chosen and productive. It is dangerous because suffering can become moral evidence.

## 5.3 “Lazy” names desire, not observable behavior

Temari’s laziness is most visible as desire for comfort, food, sleep, and being taken care of—not as a stable lack of output. She works against that desire with unusual force.

Calling herself lazy allows her to preserve an adversary inside the self. There is always someone to defeat again tomorrow.

## 5.4 Self-acceptance remains dynamic

By the winter conclusion she can say that the current Temari is the one she likes most. This is a genuine change because love no longer requires the old self to be forgotten or support to be denied.

But she immediately resumes ascent. The route does not test whether she could retain self-regard during prolonged stagnation, injury, ordinary life, or a future in which the top becomes unreachable.

The appropriate formulation is **present-tense self-love**, not completed unconditional acceptance.

# 6. Talent and technical identity

## 6.1 Temari’s principal gift is not generic hard work

Her singing talent includes:

- unusually high emotional force;
- the ability to dominate attention;
- strong technical baseline and rapid recovery toward elite form;
- auditory/interpretive sensitivity to other singers;
- willingness to expose internal contradiction through song;
- the capacity to make dangerous effort legible as artistic intensity.

She is not an underdog in the simple sense.

## 6.2 High variance is the central technical problem

Temari can exceed her stable capacity in a single performance. The same trait produces unforgettable peaks and disastrous depletion.

This is why pacing, stamina, emotional regulation, and repertoire matter more than another generic increase in will.

## 6.3 Rival singers expand technique by expanding feeling

Rinha, Misuzu, China, Saki, Kotone, and Sena do not merely supply competitive motivation. They reveal different vocal or performative values:

- Rinha: origin model and technical cool;
- Misuzu: audience control and soft domination;
- China: gentleness that changes listeners without force;
- Saki: constructed competitiveness and public frontality;
- Kotone: human/social intelligence and disciplined professionalism;
- Sena: summit legitimacy and the ability to awaken challengers.

Temari’s late technique becomes plural because it learns to desire what other singers do rather than treating difference only as threat.

## 6.4 Song is where affect becomes skill

Temari can be inarticulate in ordinary conversation and extremely precise in song. This is not a magical bypass of character development. It is a specialized communicative competence.

The later task is to let what becomes sayable in song change what becomes possible in life.

# 7. Body, food, rest, and the politics of care

## 7.1 The body is biography

Temari’s body records:

- breakup stress;
- appetite and deprivation;
- weight gain;
- skipped meals;
- poor sleep;
- overtraining;
- stamina limits;
- the memory of being cared for by Misuzu;
- the Producer’s authority;
- the possibility of pleasure without collapse.

The body is not an obstacle external to the “real” singing self. It determines which artistic self can appear and for how long.

## 7.2 Food is both love and jurisdiction

Who feeds Temari matters.

Misuzu’s food carries childhood intimacy and possessive knowledge. The Producer’s food carries calculation, professional authority, and increasingly domestic exclusivity. Friends’ food creates broader belonging. Temari’s ability to eat certain vegetables depends on relational framing.

Feeding can therefore mean care, control, competition, seduction, reward, or permission.

## 7.3 The Producer’s nutritional competence is effective and intrusive

He correctly identifies starvation as performance damage and prepares a sustainable plan. This materially improves her health.

He also assumes extensive authority over food, weight, and reward. Temari often consents eagerly because the plan lets her eat without moral panic. The final ethics cannot be inferred from effectiveness alone.

## 7.4 Misuzu’s care cannot be reduced to pathology

It would be easy to contrast a bad possessive Misuzu with a good autonomy-producing Producer. The corpus does not support so simple a split.

Misuzu often knows Temari’s body more accurately and sees dangers the Producer may be willing to operationalize. Her possessiveness is real; so is the fact that she has kept Temari alive and functioning.

The final synthesis must compare not only intention but consent, adaptability, refusal, and whose desired future governs the care.

## 7.5 Rest remains underdeveloped as a human good

Temari can accept rest as training and reward. The sources do not yet establish that she can accept rest that produces nothing.

Support-card and event passes should search specifically for unproductive pleasure, cancelled goals, and someone refusing to let her convert recovery into another metric.

# 8. Failure and rivalry

## 8.1 Temari refuses therapeutic reinterpretation of losing

She can acknowledge growth, influence, and relational contribution while maintaining that a lost competition was lost.

This protects the seriousness of her ambition. It can also make self-worth dangerously score-dependent.

## 8.2 Immediate recommitment may conceal delayed affect

Like Saki, Temari can identify the next task rapidly. Unlike Saki, her failure language often returns directly to self-disgust.

The AV pass must test whether the spoken performance distinguishes strategic restart from actual emotional stabilization.

## 8.3 Rivalry makes support tolerable

Temari can accept help more easily when the helper remains someone she can fight. Rivalry prevents care from freezing the relationship into caretaker and dependent.

This explains why she needs Misuzu and Rinha not merely to forgive her but to contest her.

## 8.4 Saki and Kotone provide a post-SyngUp! peer form

Saki gives direct challenge without maternal containment. Kotone provides practical social judgment and leadership. Both can tease, oppose, and help Temari without making her entire identity their project.

This peer ecology may be one reason old relationships can later be revisited more safely.

## 8.5 Sena converts title hierarchy into productive debt

Sena’s victory hurts because it is legitimate. Temari’s later desire is not to expose Sena as undeserving but to inherit and enlarge the title.

This is succession as rivalry rather than mere replacement.

# 9. SyngUp!: a failed form that must not return unchanged

## 9.1 Three necessary forces

| member | necessary force | distortion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Temari | voice, ambition, full combustion | self-destruction; care experienced as humiliation |
| Misuzu | care, atmosphere, bodily knowledge | possession; indispensability; containment |
| Kaya Rinha | model, rivalry, external horizon, public memory | disappearance; origin hierarchy; self-erasing protection |

SyngUp! failed not because these forces were false, but because they could not be coordinated without consuming one another.

## 9.2 The breakup was a failure of form

The members cared. They also avoided, maneuvered, overprotected, overperformed, and interpreted for one another instead of fighting clearly enough.

The public leak and canceled final live turned private dysfunction into fan injury and reputation.

The later “disbandment live” or final reckoning matters because closure must become public enough to answer the public trace without pretending the old unit can simply resume.

## 9.3 Reconciliation is not reunion

Temari’s route repeatedly rejects the idea that repair requires returning to the old unit structure. They can train, sing, compete, and remember together without making the prior form permanent again.

This is one of Gakumas’s strongest relational claims:

> a relationship can become real again without becoming what it was.

## 9.4 The triangle cannot be reduced to Temari/Misuzu

Rinha is not a third wheel in a two-person romance. Temari’s idol origin, cool persona, fandom, public scandal, and rivalry all pass through Rinha. During fear, Temari calls for both Misuzu and Rinha.

The old unit was a triangle of emotional gravity; any synthesis that treats Rinha only as external plot pressure will remain incomplete.

## 9.5 Yuri coding and category discipline

Temari/Misuzu includes unmistakably intimate coding: bodily care, jealousy, pet-name language, quasi-date framing, fear-based regression, rivalry, domestic familiarity, and possessive statements. Temari/Rinha also contains admiration, grief, imitation, fan devotion, and emotionally violent reunion.

The safest current classification is:

- explicit former-unit/friend/rival structure: high confidence;
- intense dependency and quasi-familial intimacy: high confidence;
- yuri-readable/romantic-adjacent coding: strong;
- explicit canonical romance: not established.

The distinctive subject is not simply whom Temari loves romantically. It is how love becomes unsafe when care, identity, and artistic survival are concentrated inside too few people.

# 10. Temari and the Producer

## 10.1 Why this Producer works for her

He offers several things SyngUp! could not offer in the same form:

- external technical diagnosis;
- a plan directed toward Temari’s stated ambition;
- willingness to criticize directly;
- protection that does not present itself as asking her to become smaller;
- exclusive attention;
- delight in her difficulty;
- language for feelings she cannot organize;
- institutional access and strategy.

He makes care legible as propulsion.

## 10.2 He is also highly controlling

The relationship includes authority over:

- meals;
- weight trajectory;
- rest;
- training load;
- public persona;
- media strategy;
- competitive scheduling;
- high-risk institutional wagers;
- who may enter the production relationship.

At least one later strategy involving 961/Gokugetsu stakes is arranged without Temari’s advance consent. Success does not retroactively erase that issue.

## 10.3 Reciprocal interpretation is real

Temari is not merely managed. She listens to the Producer’s fear, invites disclosure, offers a singular assigned-idol reassurance, reinterprets his emotions, gives song and fanservice as return, and increasingly stabilizes him when he carries the affect of her losses and victories.

The final relationship model must therefore hold both:

- asymmetric institutional authority;
- meaningful reciprocal care and co-authorship.

## 10.4 Exclusive language is central

The pair repeatedly defines itself through singularity:

- she is his only assigned idol;
- he has not wanted to produce another;
- he does not want even a dream self to intrude;
- she asks him to settle for her;
- he frames the work as worth a life;
- she asks for first-name address.

This can be read as devotion, professional vocation, romance coding, mutual possession, or all simultaneously. The text does not force one category.

## 10.5 The relationship may transfer rather than solve dependency

A critical counter-reading is that Temari escapes Misuzu’s total care by entering an even more institutionally comprehensive one-person system.

The difference may be that the Producer points in the direction Temari wants. That matters enormously; it is not the same as full autonomy.

Later phases must test:

- refusal;
- disagreement;
- time apart;
- alternative collaborators;
- mistakes by the Producer;
- whether Temari can continue when his exclusive witnessing is unavailable.

## 10.6 Their strongest achievement

The strongest achievement is not that he makes her confident. It is that he can tell her an unpleasant truth without making selection disappear, and she can eventually tell him a humiliating truth without believing the production relationship will end.

That is a form of trust she could not sustain inside SyngUp! before the breakup.

# 11. Fans, visibility, and public identity

## 11.1 Fans complicate singular attachment

Temari initially experiences fandom through exclusivity and succession: fans belonged to SyngUp!, Rinha, or a vanished era. She gradually learns that a fan can retain love for Rinha, remember SyngUp!, and support solo Temari simultaneously.

This plural attachment is an important corrective to her own possessive instincts.

## 11.2 Rinha’s fan transfer is both care and troubling asset logic

Rinha’s attempt to redirect fans protects their future and Temari’s. It also treats fandom strategically as something that can be transferred.

The final music/fan synthesis should distinguish:

- fans as people with memory and choice;
- fan counts as competitive resources;
- inherited audiences as ethical responsibility.

## 11.3 Public abrasiveness becomes an attraction

Temari’s inability to maintain mystique produces a more viable public image: difficult, proud, emotionally excessive, technically overwhelming, and visibly invested.

This does not mean audiences reward pure authenticity. The abrasiveness is still edited, narrated, and strategically framed.

## 11.4 Stage sincerity is specialized

Temari says she can speak honestly onstage. The stage is therefore not opposite to sincerity. It is the institution that gives sincerity form, audience, timing, and musical structure.

The risk is that ordinary life may remain dependent on performance for truth to become sayable.

The scripts establish what songs mean to Temari and how she talks about them. They do not establish the actual sound of the composition, vocal production, arrangement, mix, choreography, or MV.

## 13.3 Provisional sonic hypothesis

The source text suggests a musical identity organized around **emotional force disciplined into legibility**, not around cold elegance.

No cross-idol superiority claim should be made before direct comparison.

# 14. Japanese textual voice and register

## 14.1 Baseline register

Temari’s speech alternates among:

- clipped directness;
- formal-polite distance with the Producer early on;
- rapid emotional escalation;
- childlike elongation and protest;
- low, intimate confession;
- aggressive second-person challenge;
- unexpectedly careful gratitude;
- stage-formal declaration.

The contrast is central to characterization.

## 14.2 Politeness is a boundary tool

Early `です/ます` language with the Producer can sound formally controlled while carrying hostility. As intimacy grows, plain forms, teasing, command, and first-name requests reduce distance.

The shift should be tracked relationally rather than described as a general decline in politeness.

## 14.3 `私で我慢してください`

Literally, Temari asks the Producer to “put up with” or “settle for” her. The wording performs self-deprecation and exclusivity simultaneously.

It does not mean she believes she is an inferior assignment in a stable sense. It allows a strong singular claim to be expressed through mock concession.

## 14.4 `負けは負け`

This compressed repetition refuses narratives that convert loss into symbolic victory. Its severity can sound emotionally blunt in English if translated only as “a loss is a loss,” but the repetition is also a method of restoring clarity.

## 14.5 `まりちゃん`

Misuzu’s pet-name use carries childhood continuity, intimacy, and sometimes ownership. Temari’s reactions to it change by context: irritation, regression, comfort, embarrassment, and acceptance.

Do not translate every instance into a neutral “Temari” if relational texture matters.

## 14.6 Rinha’s abrasive intimacy

Rinha’s insults and imperatives can coexist with protection, admiration, and tears. Literal harshness does not exhaust the relationship function.

The AV pass should test how much delivery converts contempt into familiar aggression.

## 14.7 Ruby and title language

`<r=プリマステラ>一番星</r>` joins a Japanese “first star” image with the franchise title `Prima Stella`. Temari’s later language toward the sun, sky, wings, aircraft, and rocket develops around this title system.

## 14.8 Coolness vocabulary

The distinction among:

- `冷たい` — cold;
- `感情が薄い` — emotionally thin;
- `クール` — cool as image/category;
- `青い炎` — blue flame;
- `完全燃焼` — complete combustion;

is analytically decisive. English “cool” can obscure the extent to which Temari and the Producer are redefining the category from within.

# 15. Institutional fit and friction

## 15.1 Hatsuboshi gives constructed identity legitimacy

The school’s songs, auditions, Producers, trainers, ranks, units, and inherited titles provide public structures in which a self-made persona can become real through repeated social recognition.

Temari’s artificiality is therefore not a defect the institution cures. It is precisely the kind of project the institution can formalize.

## 15.2 The Producer curriculum enables total intervention

Hatsuboshi’s broad conception of production lets the Producer address food, body, psychology, publicity, song, competition, and relationships as one developmental system.

This makes him effective. It also makes the institution capable of legitimizing unusually intrusive authority.

## 15.3 Rankings can intensify self-hatred

For Temari, external rank and internal worth are already dangerously close. N.I.A. and H.I.F. give aspiration clear form but can make regression feel publicly objective.

## 15.4 H.I.F. converts biography into title legitimacy

The final winter title is not based only on technical ability. Temari’s history of collapse, reconstruction, rivalry, and public force becomes part of how the victory is made meaningful.

The final comparative synthesis must still ask whether similar narrative authorization is applied consistently across other idols.

## 15.5 SyngUp! exposes the limits of unit formation

The institution can provide new competitions and new units, but it cannot repair a relationship merely by reassigning people together. Kotone’s critique of Misuzu is institutionally important: winning someone into a unit is not reconciliation.

Failure, near-first, middle, barely passing, first-place, normal endings, and true-labeled endings are mutually exclusive C3 outcomes.

## 16.7 Communication placement

Multipart episodes are internally ordered. Cross-episode chronology is C2/C4 unless song release, story references, or explicit internal statements anchor it.

# 17. Adversarial reading and counterevidence

## 17.1 Counterargument: the story romanticizes self-hatred as productive fuel

Temari’s punitive relation to herself generates spectacular work. The narrative repeatedly rewards it with growth, rivals, fan attention, and titles.

The Producer regulates the damage but rarely rejects the premise that Temari should keep turning dissatisfaction into acceleration.

## 17.2 Counterargument: “blue flame” aestheticizes self-destruction

Redefining coolness as total combustion validates her emotional truth. It may also convert a dangerous inability to stop into an appealing brand.

The music/AV pass must distinguish controlled intensity from glorified depletion.

## 17.3 Counterargument: the Producer does not solve containment; he monopolizes it

Temari experiences his care as enabling because it serves her chosen ambition. But food, rest, publicity, competition, and emotional interpretation become concentrated in one relationship.

Mutual devotion can coexist with structural enclosure.

## 17.4 Counterargument: self-love remains victory-conditioned

Her strongest positive self-statements occur after major achievement and before/after first place. We still lack a long stable state of non-winning self-regard.

## 17.5 Counterargument: Rinha’s suffering becomes Temari’s developmental resource

Rinha’s reputational sacrifice, attempted retirement, and return are essential to Temari’s arc. A Temari-centered narrative can easily turn that cost into useful backstory without giving Rinha equal authorship.

## 17.6 Counterargument: Misuzu’s concern may be more ethically serious than the route allows Temari to admit

If Temari’s full-force singing repeatedly resembles life being cut away, restraint may not be mere possessiveness. The final evaluation needs Misuzu’s evidence and actual performance inspection.

## 17.7 Counterargument: fans are treated as transferable force

Rinha and the Producer can speak strategically about fans moving between idols. The text also depicts fans as persons with memory. The two models remain in tension.

## 17.8 Counterargument: constructed identity can become an endless obligation

The claim that a performed self becomes real is liberating. It can also make Temari responsible for reproducing “Temari” indefinitely, even when tired or changed.

## 17.9 Counterargument: ordinary life is still subordinated to idol utility

Food, leisure, games, relationships, and hobbies frequently become training, reward, publicity, or comparative data. The corpus has not yet shown whether Temari can preserve a life not consumed by the project of becoming lovable.

# 18. Stable observations

1. Temari’s singing ability is exceptional; her central early limitations are stamina, bodily condition, emotional variance, and pacing.
2. The SyngUp! breakup and its aftermath are embodied in food, sleep, grooming, weight, and performance decline.
3. She wants criticism when it is specific and actionable, even when it hurts.
4. She frequently denies trust while behaving through debt, compliance, disclosure, and return.
5. Her self-description as lazy does not describe low output; it describes desire for comfort and fear of dependence.
6. She associates effort with the self she can like and rest with potential regression.
7. Misuzu’s care is both genuinely protective and experienced as potentially engulfing.
8. Kaya Rinha is a direct model for Temari’s cool persona and idol aspiration.
9. Temari’s imitation is not erased; it is reauthored through rivalry.
10. She refuses to turn competitive loss into symbolic victory.
11. After N.I.A., she can accept help from former unitmates while continuing to compete with them.
12. She is emotionally transparent around food despite attempts at coolness.
13. Song functions as a more reliable medium of direct truth than ordinary speech.
14. Her public image becomes more effective when it incorporates abrasiveness and contradiction rather than pure mystique.
15. She can listen to and interpret the Producer’s weakness, not only receive his care.
16. The Producer relationship is mutually singular in language but institutionally asymmetric.
17. She develops meaningful peer ties with Saki, Kotone, China, and Hiro outside SyngUp!.
18. U1 proves unit capacity under distributed roles and open conflict.
19. Winter-H.I.F. victory lets her name the current self positively without ending ambition.

# 19. Working hypotheses

## H-TEMARI-01 — Authored combustion

Temari’s stable identity is the conversion of self-disgust, appetite, rivalry, imitation, and attachment into disciplined public intensity.

**Falsifier:** later sources show the apparent intensity to be externally assigned or largely absent outside a few climaxes.

## H-TEMARI-02 — Constructed coolness is chosen truth

The stage persona is not less authentic because it is modeled and practiced. It is one of the primary ways Temari makes otherwise inarticulate feeling real.

**Falsifier:** performance consistently contradicts her own values or is experienced primarily as coercive alienation.

## H-TEMARI-03 — Self-love is present-tense and movement-dependent

Temari can love the self she has made today, but the sources do not yet establish self-regard independent of progress.

**Falsifier:** later ordinary-life material shows stable affection for herself during inactivity, failure, or inability to improve.

## H-TEMARI-04 — Rivalry makes care tolerable

Temari can receive care when the caregiver remains someone she can oppose, surpass, and disappoint openly.

**Falsifier:** support scenes demonstrate comfortable care without rivalry or show rivalry intensifying the same dependence.

## H-TEMARI-05 — Artificial wings become plural infrastructure

Mature Temari no longer needs one person to be her wings. Producer, rivals, friends, fans, songs, and institutions distribute the conditions of flight.

**Falsifier:** later evidence shows practical dependence remaining concentrated in one relationship.

## H-TEMARI-06 — Food is the primary trust-and-control interface

Food scenes expose who has authority, who knows her body, when pleasure is permitted, and how affection becomes negotiable.

**Falsifier:** event/support evidence shows food mostly functioning as disconnected comedy.

## H-TEMARI-07 — Song expands life only when its truths return to speech

Performance lets Temari say what ordinary conversation cannot. Maturation depends on those disclosures changing non-stage relationships.

**Falsifier:** song remains compartmentalized and produces no relational change.

## H-TEMARI-08 — Her musical development should move from singular full-force confession toward controlled emotional plurality

The STEP3 training explicitly seeks gentleness, audience control, and greater variants of feeling without losing full combustion.

**Falsifier:** direct AV shows no meaningful development in vocal/arrangement/performance grammar.

## H-TEMARI-09 — Post-SyngUp! health depends on relationship distribution

Saki, Kotone, China, Hiro, Sena, and the Producer reduce the pressure on Misuzu/Rinha to contain all of Temari’s identity.

**Falsifier:** later evidence shows the broader network remaining superficial while the old triangle still governs nearly everything.

- She rejects being held back but needs others to regulate unsustainable performance.
- She calls herself lazy while producing extraordinary effort.
- She treats food as danger and as the clearest form of pleasure and intimacy.

# 27. Provisional conclusion

Temari begins from a severe belief:

> the person who stops striving becomes the person she hates.

Idolhood initially appears to offer escape. If she can sing hard enough, become cool enough, defeat the people above her, and reach the highest title, then perhaps the old self will no longer have jurisdiction.

The corpus refuses a simple cure. The old self remains in appetite, fear, dependence, bad grooming, jealousy, awkward speech, and the pleasure of being cared for. What changes is not their disappearance. Temari gradually stops treating their existence as proof that the idol is counterfeit.

Misuzu and Rinha remain dangerous because they know too much and matter too much. The Producer remains dangerous because he can turn almost every part of her life into one coherent plan. Fans remain dangerous because their belief becomes rank, expectation, and inherited memory. The institution remains dangerous because it publicly rewards the very combustion that may consume her.

Yet these relationships also make authorship possible. Temari’s self is not independent of others; it is answerable to them without being reducible to them. She can say that Rinha was her model, Misuzu her indispensable care and rival, the Producer her singular co-author, Saki and Kotone her peers, China and Hiro people she admires, Sena the titleholder she must surpass, and still stand onstage as `月村手毬として`.

The provisional V2 conclusion is therefore:

> **Temari does not become authentic by taking off the mask. She becomes authentic by learning which masks she chose, which were imposed, which relationships may hold them, and what truth each performance can carry. Her artificial wings matter because they are built—and because, once built, they permit a girl who could not love herself at rest to create a self capable of singing above the sun.**

That achievement is real. Whether it can become a life rather than an endlessly accelerating performance remains one of the central questions the later event, support-card, Misuzu, Rinha, and audiovisual passes must answer.
