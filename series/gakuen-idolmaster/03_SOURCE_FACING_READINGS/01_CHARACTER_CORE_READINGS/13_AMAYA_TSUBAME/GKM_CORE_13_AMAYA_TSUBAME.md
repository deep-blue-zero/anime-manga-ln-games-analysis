# GKM CORE 13 — AMAYA TSUBAME / 雨夜燕
## Phase 3 source-facing character deep reading

## 0. Status, purpose, and evidence boundary

This is the canonical **Phase-3 source-facing textual core** for Amaya Tsubame. It establishes a falsifiable provisional character model from Tsubame-owned source families before the numbered-event pass, support-card pass, relationship synthesis, audiovisual integration, and definitive Phase-7 monograph.

The audited bundle contains **173 unique source objects / 4,407 dialogue messages**:

- Produce Story — **42 / 804**;
- Produce Events — **81 / 1,104**;
- Idol Communications — **9 / 365**;
- Dear Idol — **28 / 2,093**;
- Live — **8 / 17**;
- Growth — **3 / 12**;
- Startup/seasonal — **2 / 12**.

Produce Story contains **31 Series-1** and **11 Series-2 / N.I.A.** objects. No Series-3 Produce Story source is present in the frozen Tsubame bundle. This document therefore does not manufacture a `P3-C[TSUBAME]` biography. Dear 000–027 is treated as the coherent `D-TSUBAME` route; song communications remain `M-TSUBAME` unless continuity evidence places a scene more narrowly.

Eight high-load Dear scenes—002, 006, 010, 018, 019, 023, 024, and 027—were checked against A1 raw ADV scripts. The dialogue bundle is the reading layer; raw ADV governs exact-text and staging disputes.

No claim here about Yuna Ogata's voice acting, Tsubame's singing timbre, choreography, camera language, animation, facial acting, MV dramaturgy, or rendered live staging is inferred from text. The song communications establish textual performance intent only. A separate AV request register specifies what must be acquired and tested.

Legacy V1 discussion is used only as a claim inventory. It is not evidence.

# 1. Executive thesis — heroic rivalry

The strongest Phase-3 formulation is:

> **Amaya Tsubame is the idol of heroic rivalry. She begins with “Hatsuboshi No.2” as a defensive identity: she claims to pursue Juo Sena while copying Sena's training, looking downward for reassurance, and preserving the comforting possibility that the summit is simply inaccessible. Producer and Misuzu break that shelter. Tsubame then builds an idol vocation that no longer depends on Sena: a dark-hero ideal in which performance should ignite courage, refuse abandonment, and make the winner answer for the feelings of fans and defeated opponents. Only after that independent ideal exists can she choose Sena again honestly. Sena becomes neither god nor condition of Tsubame's identity, but a beloved rival whom Tsubame can live without, save without owning, challenge without idealizing, and invite into an indefinitely renewable contest.**

A compact label is **heroic rivalry**.

The corresponding development is:

> **defensive No.2 → hierarchy shattered → authored dark-hero identity → outward rivalry and public obligation → independent heroism → chosen renewable rivalry → open star-quality development.**

This formulation preserves the strongest V1 insight—that “No.2” is simultaneously ambition and refuge—but the expanded Dear 021–027 material changes the endpoint substantially. Tsubame does not merely become a “real rival.” She acquires an idol ideal that survives a hypothetical world without Sena. That independence is what makes her final return to Sena mature rather than compulsory.

---

# 2. `学園No.2`: a title that protects the wound it names

Tsubame is introduced as student-council vice president, one of Hatsuboshi's most capable idols, and the person who publicly occupies the unofficial position immediately beneath Sena. She has the ability to justify the reputation:

- excellent academic performance;
- disciplined training;
- high administrative competence;
- strong physical and performance fundamentals;
- confidence under ordinary pressure;
- and an ethic of immediate correction after failure.

The problem is not fraudulent pride. It is that `学園No.2` gradually becomes a completed identity.

The phrase lets Tsubame say two incompatible things at once:

1. **I am the only one worthy of challenging Sena.**
2. **Sena is the one exception I am allowed to lose to.**

The first proposition sounds like rivalry. The second stabilizes hierarchy.

Producer recognizes the contradiction at the moment of recruitment. His provocation works because Tsubame has spent years receiving confirmation that she is excellent. Praise cannot move her. What can move her is the accusation that her proud self-description contains surrender.

The route therefore treats the No.2 label as a psychological defense with real social rewards. Tsubame can dominate nearly everyone beneath Sena and preserve the fantasy of upward pursuit without ever needing to build a positive image of herself after victory.

This is why the early question is not merely:

> Can she beat Sena?

It is:

> **Can she survive becoming a person for whom “Sena's pursuer” is no longer sufficient self-description?**

---

# 3. Effort can be sincere and still function as avoidance

Dear 002 is the route's foundational training diagnosis.

Tsubame uses essentially Sena's training menu and increases the volume. That behavior is not fake. She works hard. Her body and technique are genuinely disciplined. But Producer identifies a second function underneath the effort: doing “more than Sena” preserves a ready explanation if the result remains unchanged.

The hidden syllogism is:

> I trained harder than Sena → I still lost → therefore the difference must be fundamental → therefore the hierarchy is not my responsibility.

The raw ADV verifies Producer's explicit formulation that she can tell herself she worked harder and that losing after that is therefore unavoidable. When he asks Tsubame to imagine “the Tsubame who surpasses Juo Sena,” she cannot immediately do so.

This is not a critique of hard work. Later P2 and Dear material repeatedly validates difficult training, increased load, technical discipline, and deliberate practice. The correction is narrower:

> **effort is developmental only when it remains answerable to the goal rather than serving as moral proof that the worker deserves success.**

Tsubame's early overtraining is psychologically safe because it is quantitative. Becoming *different* from Sena is harder. It removes imitation as alibi.

The mature Tsubame therefore does not stop training brutally. She learns to ask what training is for.

---

# 4. Misuzu's victory destroys the one-loss exception

Dear 005–007 provides the route's first irreversible rupture.

Tsubame initially regards the first-years as beneath serious rivalry. Producer deliberately engineers a direct confrontation with Misuzu and stakes a student-council condition on the result. Misuzu wins narrowly.

The loss is devastating because it destroys the exceptional structure that made No.2 stable. Tsubame can no longer say:

> I lose only to Sena.

Her raw-ADV self-diagnosis is unusually severe. She realizes that she has been:

- chasing Sena's back;
- recognizing a brilliance she believed she lacked;
- pretending to pursue while already conceding the outcome;
- looking downward at others for reassurance;
- and using superiority over the field to avoid the vulnerability of genuine upward contest.

This is one of the most important V2 controls against a flattering reading of Tsubame. Her discipline and strictness do not make her immune to status anxiety. In fact, they can serve it.

The episode also prevents a simplistic “Producer fixes her” account. Producer does something ethically aggressive: he engineers a humiliating encounter because he believes her pride must be broken. Tsubame later accepts the result and rebuilds, but the route does not eliminate the asymmetry of that intervention.

Her response is not to abandon rivalry. It is to reclaim the word:

> she will become Sena's rival **in the true sense**.

That distinction becomes the spine of the route.

---

# 5. Sena before idolhood: Tsubame once stood above her

Dear 008–010 radically complicates the apparently vertical relationship.

Tsubame and Sena come from families close enough that they knew each other as children. Before idol skill reorganized the hierarchy, young Sena was clumsy, emotional, and often someone Tsubame looked after. Tsubame remembers herself as better at ordinary tasks.

This matters because Tsubame did not originally meet Sena as a distant sovereign. She knew the child before the institution made Sena into an ideal.

Sena's early idol confidence therefore irritated Tsubame. Sena could be inept in ordinary life yet become uncannily complete when idolhood entered the frame. Tsubame begins idol activity partly from competitive disbelief: if Sena can do this, surely she can too.

Then the asymmetry reverses.

Tsubame discovers that idol Sena possesses something she cannot easily reproduce. The person she once regarded as a younger/clumsier equal becomes an object of admiration. Over time, admiration hardens into a wall.

The route therefore describes a particularly painful status reversal:

> **Tsubame did not begin beneath Sena. She participated in creating the mental category in which Sena became above her.**

This helps explain why simple exhortations to “believe in yourself” are insufficient. The hierarchy is biographical, not merely competitive.

---

# 6. Tsubame's first wish was to free Sena

Dear 010 supplies one of the route's deepest retrospective revelations.

Tsubame's original desire to defeat Sena was not only ego. She wanted to **free** her.

Young Tsubame perceived Sena as someone “born to become an idol” and “raised to become an idol.” The contrast frightened her: ordinary Sena could be a clumsy child, but idol Sena was always perfect. Tsubame wanted victory to prove that Sena was not identical to the role imposed upon her—to remind Sena that she was an ordinary child as well as an idol.

The irony is central:

1. Tsubame begins idolhood partly to liberate Sena from idol totalization.
2. Tsubame becomes captivated by Sena-as-idol.
3. Tsubame organizes her own identity around pursuing that image.
4. Years later she must again free Sena—but this time from Sena's belief that she must choose between idolhood and another vocation.

The mature route therefore returns to the childhood motive at a higher level.

Tsubame never truly wants Sena imprisoned by idolhood. What she wants is a Sena who **chooses** idolhood strongly enough to fight her.

That is why the late retirement conflict cannot be reduced to possessive refusal. Tsubame eventually states that if retirement is genuinely Sena's desire, she will accept it. Her intervention is justified, in her own ethical logic, only because she believes Sena is using resignation to misdescribe her own wish.

---

# 7. First victory: being named `ライバル`

Dear 009 gives Tsubame her first direct win over Sena.

Several details matter.

First, Tsubame does not frame the result as autonomous self-sufficiency. She explicitly recognizes Producer's role and treats the victory as something achieved through their partnership.

Second, the most important recognition is not “you are No.1.” It is Sena calling her **rival**.

Third, Tsubame immediately rejects any attempt by Sena to turn defeat into graceful withdrawal. If Sena wants to declare herself satisfied with idolhood, Tsubame insists she should do so only after beating Tsubame again.

This exposes what Tsubame has really wanted from victory:

> not a dead summit, but a summit that answers.

A single win does not complete the route because her true object is not the score. It is **contestability**.

Tsubame wants a relation in which either party can wound the other's certainty and force further development.

That is why her eventual ideal is renewable rivalry rather than succession.

---

# 8. Producer: provocation becomes reciprocal trust

Tsubame and Producer have one of the most abrasive production relationships in the cast.

He initially works through:

- insult;
- challenge;
- strategic disrespect;
- engineered confrontation;
- and declarations of confidence more aggressive than comforting.

She responds with:

- `貴様`;
- anger;
- threats;
- theatrical contempt;
- and increasingly explicit reliance.

The comic register can obscure the seriousness of the bond. Producer repeatedly imagines a future Tsubame she cannot yet picture. Tsubame eventually treats that imagination as a resource she can borrow.

Series-1 Produce Story makes this explicit. Her perfectionism has allowed her to preserve a respectable, successful life while treating Sena as the exception she need not overcome. Producer says he wanted to become the catalyst that broke that shell. Tsubame's answer is not that any sufficiently competent Producer would have done. Her endpoint is personally specific: she says that **without him she cannot shine** and asks him not to abandon her.

Dear later makes the relation more reciprocal.

By the summer H.I.F. loss, Tsubame does not merely demand that Producer believe in her. She tells him that she trusts him and therefore requires him to trust her. After defeat, she is distressed by his old melodramatic promise to take ultimate responsibility and explicitly rejects a future in which he leaves her side.

The relationship thus develops from:

> you are the provocateur who can move me

to:

> **we are mutually obligated participants in the future I am choosing.**

The authority asymmetry remains real. He engineers encounters and withholds strategic information. Later synthesis should therefore distinguish emotionally chosen dependence from institutional equality.

---

# 9. `理論武装して`: rebellion becomes performance form

The `理論武装して` communication establishes Tsubame's first strong solo performance grammar.

Producer initially markets her as a `ヒール`—an antagonist or heel—without obtaining clean consent. The choice is structurally apt because Tsubame's existing persona is:

- confrontational;
- sharp;
- proud;
- severe;
- theatrical;
- and positioned against the Sena-centered order.

The song permits those traits to become style rather than defects.

But Tsubame immediately imposes an ethical limit on Producer's branding. If he asks her to sing only from the desire to defeat Sena, she worries that the performance will cease to face fans. Her own formulation is clear: **an idol sings for fans**.

This is one of the earliest places where Tsubame's rivalry becomes subordinate to vocation.

After defeating Sena, she can still use the rebellious emotional engine, but the live belongs to the people who support her. In other words:

> rivalry supplies heat; audience obligation determines where the heat goes.

That distinction becomes foundational for the dark-hero ideal.

---

# 10. `Campus mode!!`: inheritance without submission

The later `Campus mode!!` communication asks Tsubame to perform Hatsuboshi's traditional repertoire while representing the academy in external competition.

The problem is aesthetically exact. The song carries bright inherited conventions that do not automatically fit Tsubame's sharpened cool persona. She has also internalized years of performance habits around the repertoire.

The solution is neither rejection nor dutiful imitation.

She must combine:

- institutional pride;
- fighting spirit;
- the inherited song;
- and her own increasingly dark-heroic style.

That makes `Campus mode!!` a textual miniature of her broader institutional role.

Tsubame is not a rebel who wants Hatsuboshi destroyed. Her rebellion is **for the institution's future**. She wants a Hatsuboshi tradition strong enough to survive reinterpretation and pass onward to juniors.

This anticipates her N.I.A. role. She increasingly fights not only *against Sena* but *for a field of people whose expectations she represents*.

---

# 11. The winner's burden: victory creates obligations

One of Tsubame's largest moral developments appears after external competition.

Early Tsubame thinks vertically:

- who is first;
- who is second;
- who deserves to stand above whom;
- who can beat Sena.

Later Tsubame says that after defeating a powerful opponent, the live is not simply a celebration of her correctness. She must satisfy even spectators who wanted the loser to be standing there.

This is **winner's responsibility**.

The principle changes what victory means:

> **to win is to inherit the disappointed expectations displaced by your victory.**

This prevents Tsubame's combative ethos from collapsing into pure domination. The stronger the claim she makes through victory, the larger the audience she owes an answer.

The same logic appears in ordinary fan material. Tsubame values rare direct contact with supporters, recognizes that idolhood depends on fan support, and can accept even Producer's absurd fan merchandise when he frames himself as her first fan.

Her route therefore does not oppose rivalry and fan service. It subordinates private rivalry to a public account of what the performance must give back.

---

# 12. `アイドルは、相手が誰であろうと、見捨てはしない`

The N.I.A. / Gokugetsu sequence broadens Tsubame's ideal beyond Sena.

Shirakusa Tsukika functions as an external horizon: a performer strong enough that Tsubame must fight as Hatsuboshi's representative rather than merely as Sena's pursuer. Shion, meanwhile, exposes another version of the comparison wound—a person damaged by proximity to a seemingly unsurpassable older figure.

Tsubame recognizes enough of herself in Shion to intervene.

Her key proposition is that **an idol does not abandon someone, whoever that person is**.

This is not gentle universalism. Tsubame's care remains confrontational. She challenges, scolds, evaluates, and sometimes inflicts painful truth. But the moral difference from a purely eliminative hierarchy is substantial:

> weakness is a reason to confront someone, not proof that the person should be discarded.

That is why Tsubame can be severe toward juniors while remaining meaningfully caring. Her ideal is not “everyone is already enough.” It is closer to:

> **I refuse to make your present weakness the final definition of you.**

This becomes the relational basis of her heroism.

---

# 13. The hero ideal predates the Sena rivalry as vocation

Dear 019 adds an essential origin layer.

A childhood flashback reveals Tsubame watching heroic animation and explicitly wanting to become a hero. She asks whether an idol can be that kind of figure.

This matters because it gives her a positive ideal that is not generated by adult rivalry with Sena.

The hero desire is older and more general:

- stand before danger;
- refuse abandonment;
- give others courage;
- make a road visible;
- and become someone whose presence changes what another person believes possible.

The later “dark hero” branding therefore succeeds because it does not invent a marketable personality from nothing. It translates a private childhood aspiration into professional form.

Tsubame's hidden enthusiasm for robot/hero media in Produce Events reinforces the continuity. She loses sleep rewatching dramatic power-up scenes, gets visibly excited about robot films, gives objects extravagant names, and responds happily to theatrical dark-hero language.

These quirks are not disposable comic garnish. They reveal that the severe vice president's self-authored aesthetic is deeply imaginative.

---

# 14. Saving people through performance: arrogance acknowledged, not erased

Tsubame's mature idol ideal is explicitly salvific.

She wants to become an idol capable of saving people through performance. She also knows this formulation is arrogant. The route does not resolve the problem by declaring her ideal objectively correct.

Dear 018 stages the disagreement through Tsukika.

Tsubame criticizes an idol ideal that can ignore a suffering person nearby. Tsukika counters with a narrower professional account: performance is for fans; one non-fan's personal response cannot define an idol's worth.

Producer does not simply rule that Tsubame is right. Instead, he acknowledges plural idol forms and tells Tsubame that if she wants to press her ideal on others, she must do so through a performance powerful enough to persuade.

This is a crucial ethical limit.

Tsubame's mature heroism is not:

> I know what is good for everyone and therefore may command them.

It is:

> **I will stake my ideal publicly, make it answerable to the stage, and accept that another person can remain unconvinced.**

The distinction matters especially in the late Sena arc, where Tsubame must separate saving Sena from controlling Sena.

---

# 15. Tsukika: the second summit that frees Tsubame from a one-person world

Tsukika's narrative function is larger than “stronger external rival.”

She demonstrates that the sky continues beyond Sena.

Earlier Tsubame's competitive map is almost closed:

> Sena above → Tsubame below → everyone else beneath.

Misuzu breaks the lower boundary. Tsukika breaks the upper one.

Now Tsubame must operate in a field with multiple horizons:

- Sena;
- Tsukika;
- Misuzu;
- juniors;
- the external institution;
- fans;
- and eventually the professional world.

Tsukika also names the Sena-only gaze as a shackle. Tsubame refuses to discard Sena, but that refusal changes meaning once she proves capable of contest outside the dyad.

Defeating Tsukika therefore matters less as evidence that Tsubame is universally stronger than as evidence that **her agency has become portable**.

She can now become herself against someone who is not Sena.

---

# 16. N.I.A. teaches reciprocity: the strict senior can also learn

Tsubame's student-council identity initially encourages a top-down form of care.

She is competent, knows rules, corrects mistakes, tutors juniors, and expects compliance. Much of this is genuinely useful. Produce Events show excellent administrative language, careful vendor/business interaction, self-study in secretarial skills, and an almost compulsive willingness to revisit errors.

But the N.I.A. structure increasingly places her in reciprocal relationships.

Misuzu is especially important. Tsubame first underestimates her, loses to her, apologizes, then participates in an environment where they can teach and challenge one another. Tsubame's authority is not erased, but it ceases to guarantee epistemic superiority.

This is a recurring Phase-3 correction:

> **strictness is one of Tsubame's care languages, not proof that she always knows what another person needs.**

Later event/support phases should test how consistently she allows juniors to answer back.

---

# 17. The ordinary self is stranger than the public disciplinarian

Produce Events reveal a substantial private specificity:

- robot and hero anime enthusiasm;
- repeated viewing of dramatic power-up sequences;
- calligraphy;
- Japanese food preferences;
- dorayaki/anko interest;
- bamboo-sword practice;
- elaborate chūnibyō naming for pens and objects;
- good academics;
- secretarial/business-language study;
- fear of bugs despite denial;
- traditional-cultural curiosity;
- and a tendency to become flustered when ordinary intimacy is made visible.

One scene gives a fountain pen an extravagant Japanese name and then happily accepts an equally theatrical German-style gloss. Another has her caught writing an anime-title phrase in calligraphy. Another has her lose sleep over a robot show's power-up episode.

These details matter because the early No.2 identity is universalizing. It reduces Tsubame to:

> discipline + hierarchy + Sena + victory.

Her actual personhood is much more idiosyncratic.

The success of the heel/dark-hero route depends on making that specificity professionally legible rather than suppressing it.

Tsubame's self-authorship therefore occurs not only at the level of rivalry. It occurs aesthetically: she becomes increasingly willing to let the idol contain the weird person.

---

# 18. Formality and `貴様`: register as controlled combat

Tsubame's written speech has unusually clear register separation.

With teachers, vendors, and institutional partners she can use competent polite language. Produce Events involving contracts, business language, school staff, and secretarial study establish that she is not socially incapable of formality.

With Producer, Sena, and close rivals, her characteristic language becomes much rougher and more theatrical:

- `貴様`;
- imperatives;
- challenge rhetoric;
- exaggerated insults;
- declarations of combat;
- and proud first-person `私` framed through martial imagery.

This should not be reduced to “tsundere speech.” It functions as a relational technology. Combat language allows her to express:

- trust without softness;
- affection without surrender;
- dependence without passivity;
- and care without abandoning hierarchy or challenge.

The same mechanism helps explain `クライアイ`: Tsubame can make love legible through a declaration of war because war-language is already one of her intimate registers.

Actual vocal-performance claims remain pending AV inspection.

---

# 19. `クライアイ`: rival-love becomes authored song

The STEP3 `クライアイ` communication is one of Tsubame's most important musical texts.

The song begins as a targeted intervention for Sena. Tsubame helps write lyrics and repeatedly fails in revealing ways: over-chūnibyō imagery, generic romance, excessively literal aggression, and stylized adult language all appear before the emotional core becomes clear.

Producer asks what actually lies beneath the desire to compete with Sena forever.

The answer is **love**.

The mature emotional grammar is therefore:

> strike me → wake me → keep me burning → do not become finished → let me do the same to you.

This is not conventional romance, and Phase 3 should not formalize a romantic couple status absent explicit textual authority. But it is unmistakably intimate. Tsubame and Sena make care intelligible through mutual contest.

The communication also expands Tsubame's intended performance register beyond rebellion. Producer asks for composure, adult allure, and beckoning authority rather than only upward rage. Textually, the goal is an equal who invites another equal into battle.

Later, Tsubame refuses to leave the song permanently private. She recognizes that its language can also encourage fans and deliberately turns the performance outward.

Thus `クライアイ` undergoes the same movement as Tsubame herself:

> private Sena fixation → self-authored meaning → public idol function.

---

# 20. Sena's second retirement crisis tests whether Tsubame is actually independent

Dear 021–024 is the decisive V2 addition.

After N.I.A., Tsubame's measurable parameters can exceed Sena's. Sena responds not by intensifying rivalry, but by attempting to retire early. She concludes that her own growth has ended and that Tsubame can carry the future.

Tsubame initially experiences this as collapse.

This creates the route's hardest falsification test:

> If Sena disappears, does Tsubame's idol self disappear too?

If the answer were yes, then the entire N.I.A. expansion would be cosmetic. The hero ideal would still be subordinate to the childhood attachment.

Tsubame eventually answers **no**.

The raw ADV of Dear 024 is explicit:

> `ヒーローみたいなアイドルになる！ その想いに、貴様の有無は関係ない！`

and immediately after:

> `私はアイドルで在り続けるぞ！`

She also says that if Sena genuinely wants to quit, she will not deny that choice.

This is the route's clearest individuation statement.

Tsubame no longer needs Sena to justify idolhood.

Only now can her attempt to save Sena become ethically intelligible as a choice rather than identity panic.

---

# 21. `貴様がいない世界で生きる覚悟`: separation makes reunion possible

Dear 024 goes even further.

Tsubame tells Sena that she has made herself ready to live **in a world without her**.

This sentence is not rejection. It is the condition for non-coercive attachment.

The logic is:

1. I will remain an idol without you.
2. Therefore I do not need to force you to remain for my survival.
3. I still believe you want to remain.
4. I will risk confronting you with that belief.
5. If you freely choose rivalry again, our bond becomes chosen rather than structurally necessary.

Sena then admits that retirement was not the whole truth. She wants production and idolhood. She is afraid of being left behind and has used Tsubame's growth as a reason to abandon her own desire.

The raw ADV explicitly has Sena say she does not truly want to give up and later recognize that she wants to remain Tsubame's rival.

This produces one of GKM's strongest formulations of relational independence:

> **individuation does not require detachment; it requires the ability to stay after proving one could leave.**

Tsubame and Sena do not resolve their relationship by becoming less important to each other. They become less compulsory.

---

# 22. Liberation revisited: the mature Tsubame frees choice, not outcome

The late Sena crisis completes the childhood “free Sena” motif.

Child Tsubame wanted to defeat Sena to prove she was more than the idol role imposed upon her.

Late Tsubame faces a different false totalization: Sena now believes that because her measured idol growth has stalled and her producer desire has emerged, she should cease being an idol.

Again, Tsubame rejects the forced identity equation.

But the mature intervention is more sophisticated. She does **not** insist:

> You must be an idol because I want you to be.

She tells Sena:

> if quitting is truly your will, I will accept it.

Her claim is instead epistemic and relational:

> I know you well enough to believe your stated resignation is incomplete.

This remains risky. “I know your real desire better than you do” can become paternalistic. The route mitigates the danger by making Tsubame's claim answerable to performance, conversation, Sena's explicit confession, and Sena's subsequent choice.

Thus her heroism becomes less about determining outcomes and more about **creating the confrontation in which another person can recover a silenced desire**.

---

# 23. Kotone and the plurality of rescue

The late arc prevents Tsubame from becoming Sena's sole savior.

Kotone arrives furious about Sena's retirement and insists that the admired Sena still wants idolhood. Tsubame and Kotone then compete over who can most effectively reignite her.

Tsubame wins the live contest, but the narrative distributes the rescue.

Kotone's performance gives Tsubame further evidence of a kind of force that conventional parameters do not capture. Even in defeat, Kotone receives courage from Tsubame. Later Sena describes the roles in complementary terms: Tsubame reveals the road; Kotone helps draw her onto it.

This is important ethically and thematically.

Tsubame's mature heroism is not solitary omnipotence. It can **carry other people's belief**.

The figure who once looked downward to feel safe now becomes capable of taking a junior/fellow idol seriously enough to let that person's love alter the intervention.

---

# 24. `スター性`: the route finally exceeds technique without abandoning technique

The late Dear route gives Tsubame a second major epistemic correction.

Her early development problem was “more training is not automatically better training.” The later problem is “more technical score is not automatically more idol power.”

Before summer H.I.F., Tsubame can possess superior conventional parameters. Sena nevertheless wins by developing a form of nonquantified `スター性`—charisma/attractive force that changes what the performance can do socially.

Tsubame does not reject the outcome as unfair.

Dear 027 explicitly has her conclude:

> `技術だけがアイドルの魅力ではない。`

She chooses to cultivate her own star quality.

This is a major maturation because it avoids both extremes:

- **technocratic reduction:** only measurable skill is real;
- **anti-skill romanticism:** technique does not matter because “heart” wins.

Producer's strategy remains conditional. In Dear 025 he still prefers conventional dance development when Tsubame has measurable room to grow. Only after Sena overturns that gap does the winter strategy deliberately target the unmeasured dimension.

The mature proposition is therefore:

> **technical excellence is necessary to Tsubame's identity but insufficient to explain idol effect.**

---

# 25. Heroic `スター性`: attraction may activate through targeted care

The route offers a working hypothesis that Tsubame's nonquantified attraction intensifies when she is trying to save someone.

Evidence includes:

- her impact on Shion;
- Kotone receiving courage despite losing;
- the Sena-only live functioning as an intervention;
- Sena explicitly crediting the live with showing hope;
- and Tsubame's long-standing hero ideal.

This should remain a **working hypothesis**, not a metaphysical law.

The textual corpus does not establish a stable numeric or supernatural mechanism. It suggests a repeatable social-performance pattern:

> Tsubame becomes especially compelling when conflict is attached to responsibility for another person's future.

This aligns with her dark-hero grammar. Her strongest “saving” mode is not soothing care. It is a confrontation that says:

> I refuse to let this diminished version of you become final.

Later AV analysis should test whether the voice, movement, camera, and stage language actually differentiate this mode from ordinary rivalry performance.

---

# 26. Summer H.I.F. defeat: one of her healthiest losses

Sena wins summer H.I.F. and retains/claims the Prima Stella horizon through newly expanded star quality.

Tsubame's response is psychologically important because it differs from the Misuzu loss.

The Misuzu loss shatters defensive pride.

The Sena summer loss occurs after Tsubame has already become more independent. She can therefore hold two feelings simultaneously:

- joy at Sena's renewed brilliance;
- rage at her own insufficiency.

Neither cancels the other.

She does not need to devalue Sena to preserve herself. She does not need to reinterpret the result as statistical anomaly. She can say, effectively:

> Sena was stronger; I am thrilled she is strong; I will become stronger anyway.

That is actual rivalry.

The defeat also opens a future rather than closing one. Winter H.I.F. becomes the next contest.

---

# 27. The winter wager: rivalry as chosen future architecture

Dear 027 ends not with final supremacy but a contract for continuation.

Tsubame proposes that if she wins the winter H.I.F., Sena should become a 100pro idol after graduation and continue fighting her as a same-agency rival for as long as their lives/careers allow. Sena accepts the wager; the route notes their direct record as 1–1 and makes winter the genuine deciding encounter for the current arc.

The surface is characteristically absurd and grandiose.

The deeper structure matters:

- Tsubame can now imagine professional life beyond school;
- Sena's future is no longer assumed to be retirement;
- rivalry is no longer a temporary ladder to No.1;
- both women retain separate agency;
- and the relationship becomes a machine for keeping the future open.

This is **renewable rivalry**.

Victory does not end the relationship. It renegotiates the next challenge.

That makes Tsubame a useful comparison to Ume. Both ultimately reject the idea that defeating the beloved rival should finish the bond. But Tsubame's route is less bodily/familial and more institutional, aesthetic, and explicitly vocational.

---

# 28. Tsubame's strictness: care through sharpening

Tsubame's student-council behavior is easy to misread in two opposite directions.

It would be wrong to reduce it to harsh comedy. She genuinely cares about standards, juniors, school functioning, and the consequences of sloppy work.

It would also be wrong to romanticize every correction as benevolent mentorship. Tsubame can enjoy authority, assume her own interpretation is correct, and move quickly from observation to command.

Her strongest form of care is **sharpening**:

- identify the weakness;
- state it plainly;
- provide a standard;
- demand correction;
- remain present for the next attempt.

This differs from Rinami's holding care, Misuzu's enveloping care, Mao's protective care, or Lilja's courage-sharing.

The N.I.A. and late Dear routes improve the ethic by teaching Tsubame that the person she sharpens can also teach her.

Later events/support cards should test the limits of this reciprocity, especially with China, Misuzu, Ume, Lilja, Saki, Hiro, and other juniors.

---

# 29. Perfectionism: correction is productive until it becomes identity defense

Series-1 Produce Story makes Tsubame's perfectionism unusually visible.

After failure she wants:

- exact cause;
- video review;
- immediate diagnosis;
- targeted practice;
- and responsibility assigned accurately rather than emotionally blurred.

This is often admirable. She can recover from disappointment by turning affect into analysis.

But the true-ending material also shows the danger. Tsubame's competence allowed her to preserve a stable life in which almost everything could be corrected—except the one hierarchy she had decided was special.

Thus her perfectionism has two forms:

### Productive correction

A mistake becomes information and produces changed practice.

### Defensive perfectionism

The person works ferociously inside a framework that protects the identity structure she is afraid to revise.

Producer's value is not teaching Tsubame to care about excellence. She already does. It is forcing the framework itself to become revisable.

---

# 30. Rest is not betrayal of discipline

P2 material provides a useful anti-caricature.

Tsubame can understand rest instrumentally and intelligently. She does not believe that ceasing visible effort for a moment is moral failure. She can accept recovery when it is necessary to sustain full-power contest.

This matters because the early “more training than Sena” problem could otherwise be misread as a global pathology of compulsive exertion.

The better formulation is:

> **Tsubame's danger is not that she can never rest; it is that she can use effort as evidence that the hierarchy is not her fault.**

Once the goal and method are aligned, extreme effort itself is not presented as alien to her.

---

# 31. Fan relationship: support is not an abstraction

Produce Events and song communications give Tsubame a more developed fan ethic than a pure rivalry reading would predict.

She recognizes:

- direct fan meetings as valuable because such contact is rare;
- support as a condition of continuing idolhood;
- merchandise and audience performance as part of the shared live ecology;
- Producer as, in his own ridiculous way, her “number-one fan”;
- and the live as something owed to supporters rather than an extension of private conflict.

Her reaction to fan-performance ideas can be embarrassed or comic, but she does not dismiss them as beneath the art.

This makes her hero ideal reciprocal. Tsubame imagines herself as someone who gives courage, but she is also sustained by the people who choose to watch and support her.

The route has not yet developed this reciprocity as extensively as Kotone's or Lilja's, so later event/support material should test whether Tsubame can conceptualize fans as more than the public to whom she owes an excellent result.

---

# 32. Institution: vice president becomes representative rather than subordinate

Tsubame's relationship to Hatsuboshi is structurally important.

She begins as vice president beneath Sena—the political analogue of No.2. Her school role and idol role therefore mirror one another.

Yet the route does not simply promote her into Sena's place.

Instead Tsubame increasingly becomes capable of representing Hatsuboshi in her own style:

- she fights external rivals;
- teaches juniors;
- carries traditional repertoire;
- performs administrative work;
- protects standards;
- and treats victory as responsibility for the institution's public meaning.

This is a healthier form of institutional inheritance than copying Sena.

She can remain vice president or rival without making “secondness” her ontology.

The distinction is crucial:

> **a role can be subordinate without the person becoming psychologically subordinate.**

---

# 33. Tsubame and Sena: love does not require romance to be structurally central

The textual corpus uses unusually intimate language around Tsubame and Sena:

- childhood closeness;
- admiration;
- jealousy;
- desire for lifelong contest;
- explicit love as the emotional core of `クライアイ`;
- fear of losing the other;
- and a willingness to organize future career choices around continued rivalry.

These facts justify describing the bond as **love**, because the text itself does so at the level of emotion and song concept.

They do **not**, on Phase-3 evidence alone, authorize formalizing a canonical romantic partnership.

The analytical gain is larger if the bond is not prematurely narrowed.

Tsubame and Sena combine:

- friendship;
- rivalry;
- fandom;
- childhood care;
- institutional partnership;
- competitive eros in the broad literary sense;
- and a chosen future orientation.

The route's distinctive claim is that combat can be a form of intimacy when both participants remain free to answer.

---

# 34. Tsubame and Producer: dependence without erasure

Tsubame repeatedly uses strong dependence language toward Producer:

- he is the person whose belief makes her future imaginable;
- she says she cannot shine without him;
- she asks him not to abandon her;
- and she eventually frames future victory partly as something she wants to bring back to him.

This can look contradictory beside her individuation from Sena.

The distinction is that the two dependencies perform different functions.

Sena initially supplies **identity structure**: Tsubame knows who she is by being the one below/chasing Sena.

Producer supplies **developmental partnership**: he helps Tsubame create forms of herself she did not previously possess.

The latter can still become unhealthy if his judgment becomes irreplaceable authority. Phase 3 does not prove that risk fully resolved. But Tsubame increasingly contests him, demands reciprocal trust, and retains aesthetic/ethical vetoes—especially around fan orientation.

Thus the current finding is:

> **the Producer bond is intense and chosen, but still institutionally asymmetric; later synthesis must not mistake emotional reciprocity for equal formal power.**

---

# 35. The “dark hero” is not merely branding

By the end of the textual route, `ダークヒーロー` functions at three levels.

### 35.1 Market/aesthetic identity

It gives a coherent public frame for Tsubame's severity, rock energy, coolness, theatricality, and chūnibyō taste.

### 35.2 Ethical identity

It names her desire to help through confrontation rather than soothing.

### 35.3 Developmental identity

It allows her to become an idol whose positive ideal does not require Sena as its sole reference point.

That third function is the most important.

Tsubame does not become independent by ceasing to care about Sena. She becomes independent by acquiring a reason to be an idol that **belongs to Tsubame**.

The dark hero is therefore a bridge between differentiation and continued attachment.

---

# 36. The swallow and the sky: flight after the shell

The route repeatedly uses flight, shell, star, and sky imagery, and Tsubame's name `燕` (“swallow”) makes the pattern especially resonant.

Live snippets include the command to break her shell. Dear repeatedly imagines rivals as stars and ascent as an opening sky.

The most productive reading is not a simple name pun.

The **shell** is the closed hierarchy:

> Sena / Tsubame / everyone else.

The **sky** is the plural competitive field discovered after that hierarchy breaks.

Tsubame's flight is therefore not merely movement from No.2 to No.1. It is movement from a one-dimensional ladder into an open field where:

- Misuzu can beat her;
- Tsukika can challenge her;
- Kotone can surprise her;
- fans can alter meaning;
- Sena can fall and rise;
- technical rank can be overturned by star quality;
- and winter can remain undecided.

The open sky is narratively more important than the crown.

# 48. Phase-3 conclusion

Tsubame's story is not finally the story of a No.2 learning to become No.1.

It is the story of a person who mistakes a hierarchy for a vocation and then has to build the vocation underneath it.

She begins with a paradox:

> **Sena gives her something to chase, but that chase prevents her from imagining herself after arrival.**

Misuzu breaks the protected hierarchy. Producer forces her to replace volume with direction. Tsukika expands the sky. Fans turn victory into obligation. Shion exposes the ethics of non-abandonment. Robot-hero fantasies become a dark-hero performance ideal. `クライアイ` turns rival-love into authored art. Sena's second retirement crisis finally forces the decisive question: does Tsubame exist as an idol if Sena does not?

Her answer is yes.

That answer is why she can choose Sena again.

The mature Tsubame does not need rivalry to prove that she exists. She **uses rivalry to keep existence unfinished**—hers, Sena's, the institution's, and potentially the audience's. The hero does not merely defeat the wall. She keeps opening roads through it.

That is the bounded Phase-3 meaning of **heroic rivalry**.
