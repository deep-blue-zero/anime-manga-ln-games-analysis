# GKM SUPPORT SERIES 03 PART 001–025 DEEP READING

## 0. Scope, method, and principal finding

This is the fifth canonical Phase-5 support-card reading for the Gakuen Idolmaster V2 corpus and the first reading of Support Series 3.

The architecture-defined source is `support_series_3_part_001-025.dialogue.txt`. As with the first Series-2 bundle, the filename omits a real `story_0000`: the actual byte boundary is **26 support stories, `story_0000` through `story_0025` inclusive**, with **three scripts per story**, for **78 unique scripts / 1,534 messages**. The artifact retains the architecture-defined `PART_001_025` name for stable routing while all inventory, locator, and coverage arithmetic follows the bytes.

Support-story numbering is not treated as global chronology. Three parts grouped under the same story ID are related evidence surfaces, not automatically adjacent moments. Stronger continuity placement is used only where the dialogue itself supplies an explicit school-year, seasonal, institutional, or remembered-history anchor.

The A2 dialogue extraction is the reading surface. A1 raw paths remain the wording authority. No card illustration, facial animation, camera state, costume rendering, or vocal performance is inferred from text alone. Performance-sensitive ambiguities are routed to the audiovisual queue.

### Principal tranche thesis — care becomes social infrastructure

The Phase-5 support pass has now established four increasingly precise layers:

1. **Support Series 1:** ordinary scenes expose default care languages and default miscalibrations.
2. **Series 2 Part 001–025:** intimacy often exists behaviorally before people agree on the label.
3. **Series 2 Part 026–050:** care becomes durable when translated into technique.
4. **Series 2 Part 051–074:** relationships become reconstructively stable when people learn how to interpret one another without simplifying them.

Series 3 Part 001–025 adds a fifth:

> **Care becomes social infrastructure when support can circulate through groups, roles, routines, and institutions instead of remaining a one-to-one obligation.**

The stories repeatedly show characters building or entering systems that make ordinary vulnerability survivable:

- Saki cooks for Kotone and Temari, tutors Kotone, monitors Ume's nutrition, enforces Ume's rest, and expects reciprocal help only when it is someday needed rather than demanding immediate repayment.
- Temari is checked by Saki and Kotone, checks Kotone's injury and work behavior, and continues mutual behavioral regulation with Misuzu even while the two insist they are estranged.
- Ume, China, and Hiro become a three-person friendship system in which physical strength, academic ability, cultural knowledge, and social enthusiasm circulate unevenly but productively.
- Mao and Rinami operate as different kinds of seniors: Mao through visible intervention and prince-coded protection, Rinami through emotional containment, institutional guidance, and permission to rely on someone else.
- Sumika and Lilja turn shared domestic life into a distributed maintenance system: communication, shopping, food, photography, late-return monitoring, and small rewards.
- Sena explicitly redesigns the student council into a quasi-production environment, while the ordinary council work simultaneously gives Misuzu a non-romanticized route back toward Temari and gives China/Ume/Misuzu structured responsibility.
- Asari treats Producer Course peer networks, teacher support, professional persona, cooking, and practice exercises as infrastructure for producing better producers.
- Hatsuboshi itself supplies dorm labor, hardship aid, trainers, class remediation, and cross-year senior support that redistribute problems before they become individualized moral failures.

The key modeling consequence is:

> **A plausible Gakumas character should not solve every problem through her most famous dyad. She should route needs through the social system she actually inhabits.**

Kotone can receive tutoring from Saki, socioeconomic help from Rinami, physical-care intervention from Temari, and professional pressure from Sena without any one relationship replacing the others. Ume can be physically managed by Saki, academically helped by Hiro, socially grounded by China, and institutionally developed by Sena. Temari can be irritated by Saki, cared for by Kotone, behaviorally regulated by Misuzu, and still instinctively call for Rinha when frightened.

This tranche therefore makes **network position** a first-class character-modeling variable.

### Secondary thesis — labels negotiate expectations; they do not create the bond

Series 3 also repeatedly distinguishes relational labels from behavioral facts.

Saki calls Temari and Kotone friends while they deny it. Hiro asks whether Ume and China are friends because she has never had one before. Sena adopts “friend” language because Kotone says friendship would justify accepting a live ticket. Ume, China, and Hiro explicitly affirm friendship only after already behaving as a durable group. Mao performs “prince,” but the role matters because it organizes real acts of protection rather than because it conceals a supposedly truer self. Student-council membership can simultaneously be civic work and Sena's production apparatus.

> **In Gakumas, a label is often a negotiation over what obligations and access a relationship permits. Behavior supplies the underlying evidence.**

This matters especially for simulation: `友達`, `先輩`, `お姉さま`, `プロデューサー`, `ライバル`, and `王子様` should not be treated as static database tags. Each label changes what a character believes she may ask, refuse, criticize, protect, or receive.

# 2. `story_0000` — friendship without professional fusion

Saki's attempt to feed Kotone and Temari is simultaneously nutritional care and social declaration. Kotone accepts the food; Temari distrusts the appearance; Saki converts the meal into repeated public emphasis on `友達` because Ume has apparently worried that her older sister has too few friends.

The comedy is useful precisely because the friendship label is contested. Temari says they are not friends. Kotone calls them merely classmates. Saki responds that if **she** considers them friends, then friends they are.

Yet the third part refuses the easy conclusion that social intimacy should become an idol unit. Saki rejects forming with Ume because doing so would remove the possibility of competing against her. Temari rejects reconstructing her old unit. Kotone shuts down discussion of Sena's recruitment. When Sumika proposes that the three first-years form a unit because they are so close, all three reject it instantly.

### Claim transitions

- **Saki ↔ Temari ↔ Kotone — PROMOTE:** an ordinary friendship cluster is behaviorally real even when the noun is disputed.
- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** she can desire friendship explicitly and somewhat awkwardly; competitive confidence does not eliminate social need.
- **Temari — STRENGTHEN:** `友達じゃない` remains a poor literal measure of actual relational access.
- **Kotone — STRENGTHEN:** social belonging and professional alignment are separate decisions.

### Phase-6 rule

> **Friendship does not imply unit compatibility, shared producer, or surrendered rivalry.**

This is an important guardrail against turning social closeness into an idol-organization claim.

# 3. `story_0001` — Temari and Misuzu: estrangement still points toward the same summit

The first part gives Temari as observer. She hides reflexively from Misuzu, scolds herself for doing so, then watches Ume and China pull Misuzu into ordinary class life. Her response is mixed: relief that Misuzu is functioning socially, irritation, and a faintly jealous `うまく、やってるんだ` followed by `ばかじゃないの`.

The second part reverses the vantage point. Misuzu hides from Temari for the same reason—she expects contact to make Temari uncomfortable—then sees Kotone greet Temari, exchange lunch and money, and banter with her. Misuzu is relieved that Temari has made a class friend, but her repeated attempts to overhear and final `むぅっ` make the emotional cost visible.

The third part is the tranche's first major Phase-6 promotion. Temari directly tells the estranged Misuzu that she will climb in her own way and wants Misuzu to **watch**. Misuzu answers that she will climb in hers. `道は違えど……目指す場所は、いまも同じ。` They close together on `夢の場所で` before Misuzu restores her characteristic joke about waiting there while napping.

### Claim transitions

- **Temari ↔ Misuzu — PROMOTE/STRENGTHEN:** the estrangement is not a severing of orientation; each remains an audience for the other's becoming.
- **Temari — STRENGTHEN:** proving herself can function as communication when direct emotional explanation feels impossible.
- **Misuzu — STRENGTHEN:** respect for Temari's path survives disagreement about method.

### Phase-6 formulation

> **The pair's pre-repair bond is competitive witnesshood under conditions of broken access.**

Neither needs to agree on training philosophy before the other's gaze matters again.

# 4. `story_0002` — Saki, Temari, and Kotone: care can be right in purpose and wrong in consent

Part 1 is an unusually clean miscalibration case. Saki has been making Temari diet lunches that work; Temari discovers that one ingredient was snake, which she intensely dislikes. Saki's defense—Temari ate it safely and should focus on the nutritional result—reveals the limitation of an optimization-first care model. The meal may be physiologically successful while violating a recipient preference Saki failed to ask about.

Part 2 turns the trio into a miniature argument over Temari herself. Saki and Kotone are fighting over whether Temari's habit of denying her own concern should be corrected. Kotone thinks she should learn to say what she means. Saki argues that the existing contradiction is simply Temari-like. Temari's response is not gratitude but outrage at being treated as their child.

Part 3 shows why both interpretations contain evidence. Temari catches injured Kotone trying to work, calls the choice self-defeating, and gets her to rest. Then she secretly checks Kotone's workplace to ensure Kotone has not gone anyway. When exposed, she denies concern badly enough that Kotone treats the denial as comic proof of affection.

### Claim transitions

- **Saki — REVISE:** successful physical optimization does not substitute for recipient preference/consent; care can misfire through excessive outcome focus.
- **Temari ↔ Kotone — STRENGTHEN:** concern can include jurisdictional intervention into work behavior, followed by covert verification.
- **Saki ↔ Kotone — STRENGTHEN:** both have enough access to Temari to debate her communicative habits in front of her.
- **Temari — STRENGTHEN:** verbal denial is a stable defensive register, not reliable evidence of absent concern.

# 5. `story_0003` — Saki and Ume: older-sister authority meets local dependence

Saki uses a day off to test Ume's makeup ability, explicitly linking the lesson to an earlier pre-enrollment training session. Her rule is consistent: Ume must eventually possess the skill herself, even if Saki is willing to do the work for important performances.

A city outing then reverses expertise. Saki rarely leaves the school grounds and initially treats the trip as geographical reconnaissance. Ume knows the surrounding city better and becomes guide. The humor culminates when Saki, normally equipment-focused, asks Ume to choose cute clothes for her.

The third part introduces China. China praises Saki extravagantly after Saki rescues her from Ume's dangerous dodgeball and calls her `咲季お姉さま`. Saki loves the sound of it. Ume becomes immediately jealous and demands another match.

### Claim transitions

- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** older-sister care aims at recipient self-sufficiency, not permanent dependency.
- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** competence is domain-specific; Ume can guide her socially/geographically outside the school.
- **Ume — STRENGTHEN:** sister-category jealousy is real and playful rather than reducible to rivalry.
- **Saki ↔ China — OPEN/PROMOTE:** China can rapidly map admired senior competence into `お姉さま` language, while Saki positively receives that role.

### Modeling note

Saki should not be reconstructed as uniformly worldly or socially dominant. Put her outside the domains she optimizes and she can become the person who asks for guidance.

# 6. `story_0004` — the Hanami rivalry invades rest itself

Two dream vignettes establish how completely competition structures the sisters' imaginative life. Ume dreams of returning to their age-eight sports-day race and immediately accepts a rematch. Saki dreams of a televised athletic contest with Ume, dreads the body-favoring format, competes anyway, wins, and wakes exhausted on what was supposed to be a rest day.

The literal value of the dreams is limited, but their remembered grammar is not. Both sisters imagine closeness through contests.

Part 3 returns to waking behavior. Ume tries to train during a mandatory complete-rest day because unused energy makes her restless. Saki catches her, refuses the rationalization, then channels the need into stretching and mutual massage. When Ume says friends find her massage painful, Saki gives the important correction: technique must be calibrated to the recipient's body.

### Claim transitions

- **Saki ↔ Ume — STRENGTHEN:** rivalry is not only an event structure; it is an imaginative default.
- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** recovery is treated as training infrastructure and can justify direct intervention.
- **Ume — STRENGTHEN:** inactivity is genuinely difficult for her; overactivity on rest days is not merely bravado.
- **Ume — STRENGTHEN:** physical help needs recipient calibration; force appropriate for Saki may not transfer to ordinary peers.

# 7. `story_0005` — Mao and Rinami: same-year origins, different public callings

This story supplies a particularly useful first-year historical correction. Rinami remembers becoming lost while looking for the dorm and encountering Mao, whom she assumes is a cool senior. Mao is in fact another new student. The error matters because Mao's prince-coded social performance was already functioning at first contact: he sees someone confused, adopts a composed helping role, and escorts her.

The second part shows their mature third-year seniority. Rinami finds a crying newcomer who cannot keep up with group dance. She does not offer false reassurance. She discloses her own unit failure and being removed, tells the girl it is acceptable to cry, and reframes grief as compatible with later strength. Mao adds that the capacity to mourn one's own inadequacy is a first step and that Hatsuboshi will keep supporting the student so long as the student does not give up.

Part 3 reveals a durable difference in self-conception. Rinami asks Mao to help student council. Mao declines: he respects Rinami's service but admits that he wants the spotlight and audience. Rinami, by contrast, says she likes being an idol **and** likes supporting people who want to become idols.

### Claim transitions

- **Mao ↔ Rinami — PROMOTE:** their relationship begins at entry and is founded partly on Rinami reading Mao's already-active prince behavior as senior-like competence.
- **Mao — STRENGTHEN:** desire for the spotlight is consciously owned rather than a shameful residue to be transcended.
- **Rinami — STRENGTHEN:** support of other aspirants is a positive vocation alongside her own performance ambition.
- **Hatsuboshi institution — STRENGTHEN:** senior testimony and peer normalization are presented as part of the school's developmental ecology.

# 8. `story_0006` — China and Rinami: admiration flows toward care, not only supremacy

Rinami helps China with council work and casually identifies her as a plausible next president because Sena has been giving her important tasks. China is shocked. The scene is not the first explicit future-presidency evidence in the corpus, but it strengthens the idea that the succession was legible to seniors before China had fully internalized it.

Part 2 anchors their first meeting during student-council photography. China says she was terrified by powerful seniors and fearless peers; Rinami was the one person who felt immediately gentle. China therefore adopted `莉波お姉さま` not merely as an honorific flourish but as a relational reading: Rinami made a frightening institution inhabitable.

Part 3 is even more important for Rinami's idol model. China says she wants to become an idol like Rinami and ranks Rinami's appeal alongside Sena's. Rinami tries to redirect the admiration toward “greater” idols. China refuses the downgrade and argues that anyone who says otherwise simply does not know Rinami well enough. Rinami finally accepts that admiration as something she should answer through effort.

### Claim transitions

- **China ↔ Rinami — PROMOTE:** Rinami is an explicit idol-role model for China, not only a kind senior.
- **Rinami — STRENGTHEN:** she can underestimate the idol value of relational gentleness until a recipient names it back to her.
- **China — STRENGTHEN:** admiration is evaluative; she can reject the admired person's own self-downgrade.

### Phase-7 implication

Rinami's idol charisma should not be modeled solely through maternal/older-sister care. China explicitly experiences that care as part of what makes Rinami a desirable idol.

# 9. `story_0007` — Lilja and Mao: princehood as orientation technology

At a dorm welcome gathering, Lilja is overwhelmed by the crowd and without Sumika. Mao notices the isolated newcomer, offers a drink, introduces himself as dorm leader, infers her Nordic origin correctly, and gives her a stable conversational frame. Lilja leaves thinking he is a prince-like senior.

Part 2 repeats the mechanism spatially. Lilja gets lost, Mao locates the problem, corrects her mistaken building, offers to carry her if necessary, and finally escorts her. Princehood here is not merely image—it is a **procedure for reducing another person's uncertainty**.

Part 3 makes the asymmetry more interesting. Lilja is overtraining because she believes she has less talent than everyone else. Mao tells her she is a first-year student who should still be exploring possibility. When Lilja asks whether Mao also worries, he admits he does, then retreats into humor about height. Lilja reassures him that he is a perfect prince; Mao answers that at least in front of juniors, he should remain the perfect prince he is supposed to be.

### Claim transitions

- **Mao ↔ Lilja — PROMOTE:** Mao is one of Lilja's earliest senior anchors at Hatsuboshi.
- **Mao — STRENGTHEN:** “prince” behavior is a service protocol that provides orientation, safety, and composure to juniors.
- **Mao — STRENGTHEN:** senior composure also contains a burden—junior admiration can make him feel responsible for maintaining the role while he remains anxious himself.
- **Lilja — STRENGTHEN:** self-assessed talent deficit, not lack of desire, drives dangerous overwork.

# 10. `story_0008` — Sumika and Lilja: care without confiscating ambition

Part 1 shows a long photographic history extending to Lilja's pre-enrollment visit to Japan. Sumika keeps candid images because she finds Lilja cute; Lilja is embarrassed and invokes idol image-consciousness. Sumika's immediate response is not to stop photographing her but to offer to make even casual photos look better.

Part 2 is the more important care scene. Sumika waits because Lilja is late from training, warns that the gate is closing and the night route is dangerous, but explicitly says she is **not** asking Lilja to stop working hard. The demand is narrower: communicate when you will be late.

Part 3 turns the care reciprocal. Sumika prepares crepes as a reward because Lilja usually cooks for her and has been training hard. The execution is imperfect—Sumika mistakes salt—but the relational logic is clear.

### Claim transitions

- **Sumika ↔ Lilja — STRENGTHEN:** pre-/early-enrollment shared history includes shopping, photographs, domestic food exchange, and safety monitoring.
- **Sumika — STRENGTHEN:** good care often means reducing risk around another person's chosen effort rather than confiscating the effort.
- **Lilja — STRENGTHEN:** she accepts communication/safety boundaries more easily than attempts to devalue her work.

### Modeling rule

A plausible Sumika intervention should often preserve the other person's fun or agency while making participation safer/easier.

# 11. `story_0009` — Ume, China, and Hiro: bedrooms make friendship material

The trio's remedial cooking session is comic but socially important. They are not merely three “weak students.” Their failures differ, and they treat repeated failure as a shared adventure. Temari's refusal to taste their food gives the scene an external norm check.

Parts 2 and 3 move into private rooms. Hiro's room is warm, cluttered, and organized around “places to put things” rather than conventional desk/floor use. Ume and China read the space as character information rather than evidence that Hiro is failing morally. Hiro says plainly that their presence makes the day fun.

Ume's room is comparatively tidy. China assumes a maid must explain it; Hiro has to explain that dorms do not include maids. Ume says she and Saki routinely help clean each other's rooms while also cleaning individually.

### Claim transitions

- **Ume ↔ China ↔ Hiro — STRENGTHEN:** friendship is entering private-space familiarity, not only shared class/training activity.
- **Hiro — STRENGTHEN:** domestic disorder is compatible with hospitality and social pleasure; it is not evidence of global incapacity.
- **Ume — STRENGTHEN:** regular room maintenance exists and is partly embedded in sibling reciprocity.
- **China — STRENGTHEN:** wealthy-household assumptions remain a source of genuine but corrigible misunderstanding.

# 12. `story_0010` — the trio explicitly becomes “friends”

China invites Ume and Hiro home and says this is the first time she has brought school friends into the house. Hiro then asks whether Ume and China are actually her friends because she has never had friends before and does not know how to classify the relationship.

Ume answers immediately. China goes further, listing things she likes about Hiro until Hiro becomes embarrassed and asks Ume for rescue. The scene is important because the friendship label is not applied by narration from above; it is negotiated by participants who have unequal experience with friendship itself.

Part 2 shows the trio's ordinary lunch rhythm and bodily inequality. Ume wants dodgeball; China refuses because she expects bodily destruction; Hiro is curious precisely because it sounds dangerous. They still eat together.

Part 3 closes with shared post-training exhaustion. China suddenly calls after the others and declares that although they are still “dropouts,” they will all become idols. The line transforms friendship into a shared future without requiring equal pace.

### Claim transitions

- **Hiro — PROMOTE/STRENGTHEN:** lack of prior friendship is explicit in this support boundary and should be treated as a real social-history claim.
- **Ume ↔ China ↔ Hiro — PROMOTE:** explicit friendship cluster with shared aspiration.
- **China — STRENGTHEN:** she verbalizes affiliation readily and can articulate reasons for liking another person.
- **Hiro — STRENGTHEN:** direct praise from friends produces embarrassment rather than detached curiosity.

# 13. `story_0011` — Sena's production system contains both overreach and restraint

Part 1 uses student-council work to create a legitimate route between estranged people. Ume cannot collect input from absent students. Misuzu volunteers to take the classes containing people important to her, openly acknowledging that work gives her a non-awkward reason to speak to Temari. This is a strong example of institution as relational infrastructure.

Part 2 is one of the best constraints on the “Sena always overrides consent” model. Sena asks Misuzu why she accepted recruitment. Misuzu says the answer is too troublesome to explain. Sena does **not** force disclosure. Instead she says she will improve as producer until Misuzu wants to tell her voluntarily. Misuzu thanks Sena for valuing her current self.

Part 3 anchors Sena's recruitment of Ume. Ume admits she did not know who Sena was or what exactly Sena would do; she accepted through intuition that Sena would make her stronger. Sena says her own choice was not intuition: after watching many idols, she perceives current ability and potential almost numerically and recruits promising newcomers on that basis.

### Claim transitions

- **Sena — REVISE/STRENGTHEN:** her production style includes genuine consent-sensitive restraint in some domains; overreach is situational rather than total.
- **Sena — STRENGTHEN:** self-described talent perception is highly quantified/visualized (`数値で視える`). Treat this as Sena's phenomenological claim, not literal supernatural fact unless independently established.
- **Misuzu ↔ Temari — STRENGTHEN:** institutional duty can provide an acceptable bridge across relational estrangement.
- **Sena ↔ Ume — STRENGTHEN:** Ume's trust began before complete information, through intuitive confidence in developmental benefit.

# 14. `story_0012` — estranged care becomes mutual self-regulation

Misuzu sees Temari overtraining and thinks she no longer has the right to stop her. Instead she abruptly asks Ume to join a moderate dance session in the room Temari is using. The intervention is indirect: Misuzu cannot command Temari to rest, but she can place an alternative model of “slowly, moderately” inside Temari's field of view.

Part 2 reverses the mechanism. Temari sees Misuzu napping and remembers that she used to drag Misuzu back to work. Saki misreads Temari's distraction as a toilet problem, which finally pushes Temari into action. Temari wakes Misuzu through loudly performed vocal practice while insisting Misuzu has no jurisdiction over where she trains.

The elite first-year lesson in part 3 makes their shared interpretation explicit. Without speaking, each knows what the other wants to say: Misuzu should not waste her ability by slacking; Temari should not destroy herself by overdoing it. Each makes a tiny adjustment—Misuzu will work a little harder, Temari will hold back a little.

### Claim transitions

- **Temari ↔ Misuzu — MAJOR PROMOTION:** even during estrangement, each remains part of the other's self-regulation system.
- **Misuzu — STRENGTHEN:** when direct authority is unavailable, she can intervene through environmental modeling rather than confrontation.
- **Temari — STRENGTHEN:** care can emerge as criticism of wasted ability; her desired Misuzu is not a compliant Misuzu but an engaged one.

### Phase-6 formulation

> **Their conflict is partly a disagreement over the acceptable operating range of the other's body and ambition.**

Repair cannot be reduced to “accepting differences” because both genuinely believe the other's default is dangerous.

# 15. `story_0013` — Sena and Kotone: relation labels as access strategies

Sena waits outside Kotone's job, badly pretends the encounter is accidental, then offers a ride. Kotone experiences the insistence as physically/socially threatening and leaves. Sena's private conclusion is simple: producing people is difficult.

Part 2 supplies a major recruitment-history statement. Sena tells Kotone that although she has approached several promising people, Kotone is special: **the first person Sena ever wanted to raise as a producer**. # 28. Cross-tranche synthesis — five ordinary systems now visible

## 28.1 Friendship as non-transactional credit

Kotone repeatedly worries about whether she can repay help. Saki's answer is not charity language but generalized reciprocity: help now because people pursuing the same dream will help when roles reverse. The Ume/China/Hiro trio exhibits the same structure without stating it philosophically. Ume contributes physical capability; Hiro contributes academics; China contributes cultural/social resources; nobody requires equivalence at every exchange.

> **Healthy friendship in this tranche is not equality of contribution at each moment; it is confidence that contribution can circulate over time.**

## 28.2 Siblinghood as persistent monitoring infrastructure

Saki/Ume support now includes food design, meal logging, app-mediated feedback, room cleaning, makeup, fashion, umbrellas, equipment analysis, massage, rest enforcement, and competition. This is too broad to model as “Saki worries about Ume.” It is an operating system with multiple channels.

At the same time, recipient calibration remains a persistent failure point. Ume's massage can hurt ordinary friends; Saki can put snake into Temari's food because the nutritional optimization blinded her to preference. The support pass therefore continues to separate **care intensity from care accuracy**.

## 28.3 Dormitory life as developmental institution

The dorm is not merely housing. It creates cross-year exposure, ordinary labor, sleep/fatigue observation, food exchange, welcome rituals, safety intervention, and opportunities for seniors to see juniors outside performance. Mao/Lilja, Mao/Kotone, Rinami/China, and Sumika/Lilja all rely on this environment.

Dorm proximity should therefore be treated as a causal variable in later relationship synthesis.

## 28.4 Student council as dual civic and production infrastructure

Series 3 gives the cleanest source for the current council's dual function. Sena intentionally treats it as a quasi-agency for selected juniors, yet the civic work is not fake: scheduling, class input, photography, recordkeeping, and future succession all matter independently.

The dual structure creates both opportunity and ethical risk. It gives Misuzu a legitimate bridge to Temari and gives juniors access to seniors; it also lets Sena combine institutional authority with production ambitions. Later institutional synthesis should preserve both functions rather than choosing one.

## 28.5 Producer Course as professional peer network

Asari's scenes substantially widen the Producer model. Producers need peer networks, inherited knowledge, broad skill, specialized skill, cooking, scouting, and a consciously authored reassuring persona. The main Producer can be socially unusual while still participating productively in professional networking.

The professional “wizard” role is explicitly a **performed responsibility**, not proof that the producer never experiences uncertainty.
