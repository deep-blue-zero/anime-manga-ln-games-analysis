# GKM SUPPORT SERIES 01 DEEP READING

## 0. Scope, method, and principal finding

This is the first canonical Phase-5 support-card reading for the Gakuen Idolmaster V2 corpus. Phase 4 established how the cast behaves when event stories place several characters inside explicit projects, conflicts, jobs, public performances, holidays, and succession problems. Phase 5 asks a different question:

> **What survives when the stakes become too small to justify a grand performance?**

The source boundary is the complete `support_series_1.dialogue.txt` analysis bundle: **26 unique support-card scripts / 470 messages**, organized as thirteen numbered support stories (`story_0000` through `story_0012`), each with two parts.

These are small vignettes rather than one chronological narrative. The two parts grouped under one support-story number often share a focal character without describing adjacent moments. They must therefore be read as **paired evidence surfaces**, not automatically as Part 1 → Part 2 biography.

The raw support scripts do not expose an explicit human-readable card-title/owner field in this analysis bundle. The “focal character” labels below are therefore **analytical assignments based on sustained scene centrality**, not claims about hidden UI metadata. The mapping is nevertheless unusually regular: the thirteen story IDs supply one two-part focal pair for each playable idol.

The A2 dialogue extraction is the reading surface. A1 raw-script paths are retained for every support part. No uninspected facial animation, vocal nuance, camera state, card illustration, or rendered visual composition is promoted into textual fact. Performance-sensitive questions are routed to the audiovisual queue.

### Principal tranche thesis

Support Series 1 provides unusually clean evidence that Gakumas character identity is carried by **micro-habits of relation**.

The large routes tell us what characters want. These support stories show what they *automatically do* around other people:

- Saki converts concern into optimization and immediately risks using the wrong person's baseline.
- Temari volunteers before anyone else can hesitate and notices the first peer willing to contest her for the floor.
- Kotone uses performative sweetness, strategic evasion, and rapid social reading to protect time, labor, and emotional space.
- Mao polices rules while privately maintaining martial-arts practice and still finds cute-performance work embarrassing enough to threaten her chosen image.
- Rinami performs care more naturally when she stops trying to imitate an assigned “little-sister” persona; her actual biological-sister care is affectionate and firm rather than soft submission.
- Hiro treats discomfort, criticism, physical observation, and difficulty as things worth approaching, but her curiosity is socially specific rather than a generic desire to suffer.
- Lilja's uncertainty is attached to particular language/culture tasks, not a lack of intelligence or general Japanese competence.
- Sumika repeatedly turns pleasure—karaoke, food, casual reward, shared fun—into a form of social regulation.
- China combines real physical weakness with persistence and an almost reverent desire for ordinary peer life such as dorm common rooms.
- Ume's body is genuinely exceptional enough that “normal training” for her can look absurd from another person's perspective, while her social role remains morale-building rather than domineering.
- Misuzu's care persists after relational rupture, but she can deliberately change the *route* by which care reaches someone.
- Sena converts perceived personal completion into scouting and institutional succession work.
- Tsubame's severity is paired with precise teaching, disciplined study, and affectionate peer memory.

The first Phase-5 formulation is therefore:

> **Low-stakes behavior exposes each character's default care language, default avoidance strategy, and default model of what another person needs.**

This matters for later behavioral reconstruction. A plausible simulation cannot be built only from crisis decisions or climax speeches. It must know what Saki does when a friend says “I'm tired,” what Kotone says when she wants to escape a lecture, how Misuzu helps someone she is deliberately not approaching directly, what Hiro notices about another person's posture, and how Tsubame gives a technical correction when no rivalry speech is required.

## 1.2 Support-story continuity rule

Support stories are modular `M-*` evidence unless an embedded memory or explicit state anchor earns stronger classification.

Three distinctions are essential:

1. **Support story ID is not chronology.** `story_0000` does not precede `story_0001` in-world merely because its number is lower.
2. **Two parts under one story number are not automatically sequential.** They frequently share a focal character while switching social partner, setting, and implied date.
3. **Embedded history can be stronger than the containing support object's placement.** Temari's middle-school vocal class, for example, is direct historical evidence even though Support Series 1 itself is not one calendar track.

## 1.3 Focality is analytical, not metadata-derived

The bundle's support objects carry blank derived-character metadata. The regular one-idol-per-story pattern is reconstructed from scene centrality:

## 1.4 Producer absence is itself a tranche property

No Producer dialogue appears in these 26 scripts.

Phase 5 specifically asks us to inspect low-stakes Producer behavior where it exists. Support Series 1 instead presents a **peer-, trainer-, dorm-, sibling-, and student-council-centered ecology**. That means this tranche is especially valuable for removing Producer-route bias from character models: the cast behaves without needing the player character to organize every interaction.

# 2. Support `story_0000` — Hanami Saki: optimization as care, competition as reflex

**Sources:**
- `transcripts_raw/04_support_card_stories/support_series_1/story_0000/part_01.txt` — 16 messages
- `transcripts_raw/04_support_card_stories/support_series_1/story_0000/part_02.txt` — 20 messages

**Participants:** Saki, China; Saki, Hiro  
**Primary functions:** training care, calibration failure, academic rivalry, sibling-identity reflex

## 2.1 China says “I cannot,” and Saki hears “the method needs improvement”

China finishes a lesson exhausted and says the training was hard for her. Saki immediately diagnoses a foundational weakness and offers additional training because the lesson ended early.

The care is genuine. So is the failure of calibration.

Saki's argument is revealing: time is finite, no second should be wasted, and **Ume normally tolerates this level of extra work**. China has to say explicitly that she is not Hanami Ume and cannot be treated as if she were.

Saki responds by promising a program optimized for China, but the scene ends with China still pleading to be heard.

This is one of the cleanest low-stakes examples of a Phase-4 pattern: Saki does not neglect weaker people; she can care so aggressively through optimization that she temporarily treats a person's stated limit as a problem to route around.

The Ume reference is especially important. Saki's normal comparison class is distorted by years of living with an extraordinarily athletic younger sister. Her error is not “she thinks everyone is lazy.” It is more specific:

> **Saki can mistake an exceptional household baseline for a reasonable starting point until another person forces recalibration.**

### Claim transitions

- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** default care language = identify deficit → construct intervention → begin immediately.
- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** optimization urgency is sincere affection/professionalism, not merely dominance.
- **Saki — REVISE:** her individualization skill is real but not automatic. She can initially import Ume's body as an inappropriate reference standard.
- **China — STRENGTHEN:** politeness does not prevent direct boundary assertion. She explicitly rejects being equated with Ume.

## 2.2 Hiro turns a test result into immediate rivalry

The second vignette places Saki preparing for school tests. She sees Hiro wandering after training and challenges the fact that Hiro apparently does not need to study.

Hiro recognizes her not by name but as “the person who got second place.” Saki responds by fully naming the defeat: she is Hanami Saki, second to Hiro on the last test.

Then Hiro calls Saki “Ume's little sister.”

Saki's correction—**I am the older sister**—is nearly instantaneous. The sister identity is not an occasional theme she invokes only around Ume. It is a high-salience self-definition that can be triggered by a stranger getting the direction wrong.

Saki then vows to beat Hiro next time. Hiro, meanwhile, collapses from post-training exhaustion while still casually implying that tests are easy.

### Claim transitions

- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** competitive recognition generalizes beyond idol performance. Academic ranking is enough to activate a concrete rematch goal.
- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** older-sister identity is a fast reflex, not merely a reflective value.
- **Hiro — STRENGTHEN:** intellectual ease and bodily fragility remain sharply dissociated.

### Relationship delta

**Saki ↔ Hiro:** early relation can begin from rank awareness rather than intimacy. Saki makes the relation competitive immediately; Hiro's recognition is flatter and more observational.

### Japanese-register note

Saki's rapid sequence `違うわっ！ わたしがお姉ちゃんよっ！` is useful modeling evidence. Embarrassment or surprise does not make her syntax tentative; it increases declarative force.

# 3. Support `story_0001` — Tsukimura Temari: initiative before friendship

**Sources:**
- `.../support_series_1/story_0001/part_01.txt` — 19 messages
- `.../support_series_1/story_0001/part_02.txt` — 20 messages

**Participants:** Temari, vocal trainer, middle-school classmates; Temari, Saki, vocal trainer  
**Primary functions:** pre-high-school professional norm, initiative, social contempt, first competitive recognition

## 3.1 Middle-school Temari fills every available vacuum

Near the end of middle school, the vocal trainer repeatedly asks for volunteers. Temari volunteers for diaphragmatic breathing, long tones, and the full-song demonstration.

The trainer eventually asks if anyone else will try. Nobody does.

Temari's internal response is severe:

> if they have no motivation, why did they enroll?

This does not require us to accept her judgment as fair. It tells us the norm she is already using before the high-school cast fully forms. For Temari, willingness to expose oneself to evaluation is part of what it means to deserve the training environment.

She is not waiting for external competition to make her ambitious. She already treats unused opportunity as almost morally offensive.

### Claim transitions

- **Temari — STRENGTHEN:** high professional standards clearly predate the Class 1-1 conflicts.
- **Temari — STRENGTHEN:** initiative itself is a moral/professional metric for her; technical ability is not the only thing she judges.
- **Temari — OPEN:** the scene is Temari's perception of classmates' motivation, not objective evidence that the classmates lack ambition.

## 3.2 Saki is the first person in the vignette who contests the floor

After high-school entry, Temari again volunteers first. This time Saki also raises her hand.

Temari initially yields because Saki is a new external entrant. Then they both volunteer for the next opportunity and argue over who gets it.

Saki says she wants to be first. Temari calls that selfishness. Saki offers a counter-definition: call it motivation.

Temari's private reaction is not simple dislike. She notices Saki's nerve and thinks of her as strange precisely because Saki **bothers to compete directly**.

This is small but historically valuable. Event 001 later shows their rivalry functioning as class social technology; Support 0001 shows the earlier perceptual trigger:

> Saki is interesting to Temari because she refuses the passivity Temari despises.

### Claim transitions

- **Temari — STRENGTHEN:** she responds positively, if irritably, to demonstrated initiative even before affection exists.
- **Saki ↔ Temari — STRENGTHEN:** rivalry begins as competition for opportunity itself, not only comparison after performance.
- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** she rhetorically reclassifies self-assertion as professional motivation rather than apologizing for it.

### Continuity promotion

- Part 1: **C1 historical anchor — middle-school Temari before high-school progression.**
- Part 2: **C1-compatible early high-school encounter** with external entrant Saki, plausibly very near the start of Class 1-1 formation. Do not force an exact day without an explicit bridge.

### Japanese-register note

Temari's internal language is harsher than her spoken classroom behavior. She can think `やる気がないなら、なんで入学したんだろう` while externally remaining concise and procedurally compliant. Behavioral modeling should preserve that separation between internal judgment and social output.

# 4. Support `story_0002` — Fujita Kotone: performative affect as boundary management

**Sources:**
- `.../story_0002/part_01.txt` — 20 messages
- `.../story_0002/part_02.txt` — 19 messages

**Participants:** Kotone, Saki, dance trainer; Kotone, Hiro, Sena  
**Primary functions:** overwork, playful manipulation, talent denial, tactical concealment

## 4.1 Kotone can use exaggerated affection strategically without making all affection fake

After dance training, Kotone is exhausted. The trainer again tells her to build stamina. Saki immediately returns to the issue of Kotone's jobs and proposes a custom SSD/fitness intervention.

Kotone does not directly debate the plan. She tells Saki that the concern alone has made her feel much better, deploys exaggerated `だいしゅきぃ♪`, and then volunteers for another round of training.

Once Saki accepts the emotional signal and backs off, Kotone privately concludes that Saki was easy to handle.

This is excellent register evidence because it demonstrates **instrumental cuteness** in a low-stakes peer interaction.

The wrong inference would be “Kotone's affectionate language is fake.” The better model is:

> Kotone can sincerely appreciate concern while deliberately exaggerating sweetness to shape another person's behavior.

Her social intelligence includes recognizing which affective display will end an unwanted intervention faster than argument.

### Claim transitions

- **Kotone — STRENGTHEN:** cutesy register can be strategic social labor.
- **Kotone — STRENGTHEN:** boundary defense often works through redirection rather than confrontation.
- **Kotone — STRENGTHEN:** continued employment and stamina problems remain materially linked; “quitting” is a process constrained by circumstance, not one clean decision.
- **Saki ↔ Kotone — STRENGTHEN:** Kotone understands Saki's care algorithm well enough to manipulate its stopping condition.

## 4.2 Hiro helps Kotone hide from Sena—and then punctures Kotone's talent denial

Kotone asks Hiro to misdirect Sena, who is pursuing her to discuss talent. Hiro does so with almost no hesitation.

After Sena leaves, Kotone jokes that Sena must have confused her grades with someone else's. Hiro says the mistake is unlikely because Sena also knew Hiro's name.

Then Hiro offers a compact alternative:

> maybe Kotone simply has not noticed it herself.

Kotone's reply is pragmatic rather than romantic: she hopes talent is real because then she can make a lot of money. But she immediately dismisses the possibility.

This is a useful support-story correction to any model of Kotone as secretly craving abstract artistic validation. Her self-belief problem is tied to material stakes and long experience, while another low-status peer can see the blind spot without turning it into a speech.

### Claim transitions

- **Kotone — STRENGTHEN:** talent denial is durable enough to survive elite external recognition.
- **Kotone — STRENGTHEN:** income remains a legitimate motivational language; she does not need to purify ambition into “art for art's sake.”
- **Hiro — STRENGTHEN:** she can offer accurate social/cognitive observations in extremely compressed language.
- **Hiro ↔ Kotone — STRENGTHEN:** casual trust is already sufficient for concealment and a small piece of personal feedback.

### Japanese-register note

Kotone's `だいしゅきぃ♪`, `サンキュ～`, elongated vowels, and rapid shifts between exaggerated sweetness and internal bluntness should be preserved in simulation. She does not become formally vulnerable simply because the topic is serious.

# 5. Support `story_0003` — Arimura Mao: image strategy and ordinary responsibility

**Sources:**
- `.../story_0003/part_01.txt` — 20 messages
- `.../story_0003/part_02.txt` — 20 messages

**Participants:** Mao, visual trainer; Mao, Kotone  
**Primary functions:** cute-role embarrassment, deliberate image strategy, dojo practice, dorm governance

## 5.1 “Cute” is difficult because Mao understands performance, not because she cannot perform

The visual trainer praises Mao's physical control, then asks her to pose with maximal deliberate cuteness.

Mao resists. Importantly, she does not say she cannot understand acting. The trainer explicitly reminds her that idol work requires range and that Mao knows this. Mao agrees and says she practices; what she wants is **more time before showing that mode to other people**.

Then she names the concern directly: too much visible cute practice may interfere with her image strategy.

This sharply supports the Phase-3 refractive-role model.

The embarrassment is real. So is the strategic layer. Mao is not a person whose “true feminine/cute self” is trapped under a false prince shell. She is an actor-author managing when different registers become public.

### Claim transitions

- **Mao — STRENGTHEN:** prince presentation is consciously managed image authorship.
- **Mao — STRENGTHEN:** cute register is practiced and available but socially exposing.
- **Mao — REJECT:** inability to perform cute behavior. The problem is readiness/image control, not basic incompetence.

## 5.2 Dojo practice and curfew enforcement belong to the same Mao

Kotone spots Mao returning late and imagines a scandalous night out. Mao catches the tail immediately.

The truth is mundane: Mao is returning from the dojo and has properly filed a curfew exception. She then turns the investigation back on Kotone and discovers Kotone's own permissions are probably informal/noncompliant.

Mao ends the scene by taking Kotone home for another lecture.

This gives the senior/prince model excellent ordinary-life grounding:

- Mao maintains martial-arts training outside standard lessons;
- Mao knows and follows dorm administrative procedures;
- Mao enforces those procedures on juniors;
- her correction can coexist with theatrical `キミ` senior speech rather than becoming bureaucratic coldness.

### Claim transitions

- **Mao — STRENGTHEN:** senior authority includes routine rule enforcement and administrative competence.
- **Mao — STRENGTHEN:** physical discipline is not limited to idol lessons; dojo practice is ongoing ordinary life.
- **Mao ↔ Kotone — STRENGTHEN:** their relationship comfortably supports junior mischief, senior interception, and repeated lecturing.

### Institution delta

Hatsuboshi dorm life includes a curfew/late-return permission mechanism. Mao follows it; Kotone appears to rely on informal peer notification instead. This is useful evidence that school discipline has formal processes even when enforcement is relational.

# 6. Support `story_0004` — Himesaki Rinami: care works best when it stops imitating a role

**Sources:**
- `.../story_0004/part_01.txt` — 20 messages
- `.../story_0004/part_02.txt` — 19 messages

**Participants:** Rinami, visual trainer; Rinami, Ume, Rinami's younger sister by phone  
**Primary functions:** acting, assigned little-sister persona, relational imagination, biological-sister boundary setting

## 6.1 The trainer improves Rinami by removing the “little-sister” template

Rinami rehearses a grateful acting scene. The visual trainer says it is slightly stiff and identifies the likely problem: Rinami is trying too hard to perform **“little-sister-ness.”**

The trainer knows Rinami's circumstances but asks her to temporarily remove them from the task and imagine someone close to her instead.

Rinami repeats the same lines. The trainer immediately says the result is much better and that Rinami's own strengths are visible.

This is a compact but very important role-authorship scene.

A preexisting “little sister” story about Rinami is not automatically the route to authentic performance. She becomes more convincing when she stops chasing a category and draws on an actual relational feeling.

### Claim transitions

- **Rinami — STRENGTHEN:** authentic-feeling performance emerges through relational memory rather than fixed archetype imitation.
- **Rinami — REVISE:** “little-sister identity” is not sufficient as an explanatory master key. It can itself become an artificial performance constraint.
- **Rinami — STRENGTHEN:** she can transform private relational feeling into professional acting without the relation being literally reenacted.

## 6.2 Rinami as actual older sister is stricter than the soft stereotype

Ume hears that Rinami plans to go home because her younger sister has a fever. The sister calls and says Rinami should stay at school.

Rinami interprets the request skeptically: the sister probably wants to enjoy skipping school without interference and may try to go out despite the fever. Rinami tells her to sleep properly and remains ready to intervene.

Ume calls this “strong” and says Rinami feels like an older sister.

Rinami laughs and compares herself unfavorably to Saki's sister intensity, then offers to visit Ume if Ume gets sick.

This is useful because biological care is not presented as passive indulgence. Rinami's older-sister mode includes suspicion, rule enforcement, affection, and readiness to show up physically.

### Claim transitions

- **Rinami — STRENGTHEN:** care can be firm, skeptical, and behaviorally directive.
- **Rinami — STRENGTHEN:** older-sister behavior is an actual family practice, but it does not map neatly onto her performed “little sister” exercises.
- **Rinami ↔ Ume — STRENGTHEN:** Ume readily recognizes and receives Rinami's sisterly care language.

### Japanese-register note

Rinami's friendliness is not equivalent to uncertainty. Phone language such as `ちゃんと寝るのよ？` carries gentle authority. Later synthesis should distinguish hedging in social entry from firmness once a care obligation is accepted.

# 7. Support `story_0005` — Shinosawa Hiro: curiosity pushes against ordinary social boundaries

**Sources:**
- `.../story_0005/part_01.txt` — 18 messages
- `.../story_0005/part_02.txt` — 17 messages

**Participants:** Hiro, Temari; Hiro, China  
**Primary functions:** criticism-seeking, difficulty preference, bodily observation, boundary comedy

## 7.1 Hiro asks Temari for harsher criticism because normal discouragement is not discouraging

Hiro has watched Temari give China blunt advice and asks to receive the same treatment—preferably harsher.

Temari is alarmed because she already worries that her usual comments are severe enough to make people depressed. Hiro reassures her that harsh words will not make her sad; they will make her happy.

Temari's private conclusion is basically: *this person is scary*.

The scene strengthens the resistant-form model, but it also gives it a social constraint.

Hiro is not merely attracted to abstract failure states. She actively seeks an interlocutor who can provide **strong evaluative resistance**. Criticism is interesting because it creates a difficult surface to work against.

The scene is comedy partly because Hiro does not automatically account for the emotional burden this request puts on Temari. Her preference can be entirely sincere while remaining socially disorienting.

### Claim transitions

- **Hiro — STRENGTHEN:** resistance-seeking extends to interpersonal evaluation, not only physical/technical hardship.
- **Hiro — STRENGTHEN:** she can clearly state unusual preferences rather than hiding them.
- **Hiro — REVISE:** difficulty-seeking is not synonymous with isolation. She may actively recruit other people to create useful difficulty.
- **Temari — STRENGTHEN:** she is self-aware enough to know her criticism can wound; harshness is not wholly unreflective.

## 7.2 China is interesting to Hiro as a body to study

Hiro stares at China all morning. China eventually asks why.

Hiro says China's posture is beautiful and she wants to observe it. China happily offers to cooperate—until Hiro asks to watch **all day, until China goes to sleep, from every angle**.

China refuses.

This is small but valuable for behavioral modeling. Hiro's curiosity is embodied, literal, and capable of overshooting ordinary social scale. She notices posture as something technically beautiful and wants sustained observation as a learning object.

The joke works because Hiro's request is not framed as predatory intention. It is a sincere research-like extension that ignores the point at which normal consent becomes unreasonable.

### Claim transitions

- **Hiro — STRENGTHEN:** visual/physical curiosity can attach to other people's embodied technique.
- **Hiro — STRENGTHEN:** unusual requests often arise from literal extension of a real interest rather than a desire to provoke.
- **China — STRENGTHEN:** generosity has boundaries; she happily offers some help and directly refuses excessive access.

### Simulation caution

Do not generalize these scenes into “Hiro always wants abuse” or sexualize the material. The evidence supports **difficulty-seeking, criticism-seeking, bodily observation, and socially unusual curiosity**. It does not by itself establish a universal erotic motive.

# 8. Support `story_0006` — Katsuragi Lilja: specific language vulnerability and safe peer scaffolding

**Sources:**
- `.../story_0006/part_01.txt` — 14 messages
- `.../story_0006/part_02.txt` — 12 messages

**Participants:** Lilja, Kotone; Lilja, Sumika  
**Primary functions:** Japanese tongue-twister practice, peer encouragement, luck ritual, shared reward

## 8.1 Kotone supports without turning Lilja's difficulty into fragility

Lilja worries about a vocal-training tongue-twister exercise. Kotone says the material is basic and reassures her that Kotone is there.

Lilja repeatedly mangles the tongue twister. Kotone encourages another try, celebrates partial improvement, and then laughs when the attempt stops resembling the original phrase at all.

The tone is important. Kotone does not treat Lilja as too delicate to tease. The support works because failure can become funny without becoming humiliating.

This provides a useful low-stakes distinction for Lilja's language profile:

> **Specific phonetic/cultural tasks can still be difficult even when ordinary Japanese conversation is fully functional.**

Do not use tongue-twister failure as evidence of broad Japanese incomprehension.

### Claim transitions

- **Lilja — STRENGTHEN:** culture/language uncertainty remains task-specific and embodied.
- **Kotone — STRENGTHEN:** she can provide playful scaffolding that alternates encouragement and teasing without destabilizing the recipient.
- **Kotone ↔ Lilja — STRENGTHEN:** trust is sufficient for corrective humor.

## 8.2 Sumika treats Lilja's “luck” as a familiar shared trait

At a crowded drawing/lottery, Sumika asks Lilja to try because she believes Lilja is unusually strong in this kind of situation. Lilja draws the top prize—ice-cream tickets—and immediately says they should eat together.

This does not establish supernatural luck. It establishes a **relationship belief**: Sumika has accumulated enough experience to expect Lilja to succeed at chance-based moments and can call on that shared story casually.

The reward also matters. Lilja's first response is not possession but sharing.

### Claim transitions

- **Lilja — STRENGTHEN:** received good fortune is quickly converted into shared pleasure.
- **Sumika ↔ Lilja — STRENGTHEN:** their ordinary relationship contains accumulated micro-myths (“Lilja is strong at this kind of thing”) that need not be literally true to be socially real.

# 9. Support `story_0007` — Shiun Sumika: pleasure as relational regulation

**Sources:**
- `.../story_0007/part_01.txt` — 17 messages
- `.../story_0007/part_02.txt` — 16 messages

**Participants:** Sumika, Lilja, vocal trainer; Sumika, Kotone  
**Primary functions:** singing preference, karaoke, casual reward, labor motivation

## 9.1 Sumika likes singing most when it remains pleasurable

Lilja tells Sumika that her singing was beautiful. Sumika says she likes singing too—but likes singing at karaoke **even more than singing in lesson**.

Lilja recognizes the reason from observation: Sumika always looks happy at karaoke, and Lilja becomes happy watching her.

Sumika immediately turns that recognition into a plan: go together after school.

This strengthens the Phase-4 professional-pleasure thesis at a smaller scale. Sumika's preference for leisure context is not evidence that she secretly dislikes singing or lacks professional seriousness. It tells us what conditions make the activity emotionally renewable.

### Claim transitions

- **Sumika — STRENGTHEN:** pleasure is not merely reward after work; it is part of how she keeps artistic activity alive.
- **Lilja — STRENGTHEN:** she tracks other people's affect and can name the contagion of Sumika's enjoyment.
- **Lilja ↔ Sumika — STRENGTHEN:** shared fun is an explicit developmental resource, not background fluff.

## 9.2 A crepe promise is enough to change Kotone's work affect

Kotone is rushing to a job after homeroom runs late. Sumika praises her effort and promises to buy her a crepe later.

Kotone immediately shifts from frustration to exuberance and says she can work at double strength.

Sumika explicitly asks whether the reward restored her motivation.

The scene is almost a miniature behavioral experiment. Sumika knows that Kotone's material life cannot be solved by telling her to “work less,” but she can alter the affective cost of the day through a concrete small pleasure.

### Claim transitions

- **Sumika — STRENGTHEN:** default care often uses accessible pleasure rather than abstract reassurance.
- **Kotone — STRENGTHEN:** food/reward language is behaviorally salient and does not need to be psychologized away.
- **Sumika ↔ Kotone — STRENGTHEN:** easy peer affection can coexist with very different relationships to labor.

### Japanese-register note

Sumika's `～っしょ`, clipped casual forms, and reward-oriented joking are not merely “gyaru flavor.” They are functional tools for lowering interpersonal pressure.

# 10. Support `story_0008` — Kuramoto China: weakness without shame and longing for ordinary peer infrastructure

**Sources:**
- `.../story_0008/part_01.txt` — 20 messages
- `.../story_0008/part_02.txt` — 20 messages

**Participants:** China, Ume, Hiro, dance trainer  
**Primary functions:** physical weakness, persistence, Worst Three support, dorm aspiration

## 10.1 China's poor physical baseline is explicit and socially survivable

China struggles badly in dance lesson and says her physical-education grades were also poor. The trainer says this is obvious.

Ume immediately supplies counterevidence: China is still practicing and has been working on a pull-up/turnover skill. Hiro adds a surprising metric—China can hold a handstand longer than Hiro.

The trainer does not romanticize any of this. Those efforts do not automatically produce dance results. But the trainer praises the friendship and the importance of mutual improvement.

China asks to try again immediately and is told no because the lesson has to rotate.

This is an excellent micro-version of her Phase-3 model:

> receive an accurate negative evaluation → register it without denial → mobilize social support → answer with renewed effort.

### Claim transitions

- **China — STRENGTHEN:** she can openly name poor prior performance without collapsing into shame avoidance.
- **China — STRENGTHEN:** her persistence is not dependent on pretending effort already equals competence.
- **Ume/Hiro ↔ China — STRENGTHEN:** Worst Three support includes concrete observation of each other's training, not just verbal friendship.

## 10.2 The dorm is exciting because ordinary shared life is exciting

China asks Ume for a dorm experience tour and reacts with extreme enthusiasm to the entrance, common space, shared meals, and studying together.

Nothing objectively luxurious is being offered. The attraction is precisely ordinary peer infrastructure.

This is socioeconomically important. China's privileged household gives her access to resources many peers do not have, but it does not give her the experience she wants here: **routine horizontal cohabitation with classmates**.

Her fascination should not be used to claim dorm life is universally better than her home. It does show that material privilege and social deprivation/novelty can coexist along different axes.

### Claim transitions

- **China — STRENGTHEN:** ordinary peer life carries aspirational value independent of material comfort.
- **China — STRENGTHEN:** privilege should not be modeled as social saturation; she can be inexperienced with highly ordinary communal routines.
- **Ume — STRENGTHEN:** she readily becomes guide/host without making China's excitement embarrassing.

### Socioeconomic note

Support material is particularly useful here because the relevant contrast is not “rich versus poor” in abstract ideology. It is **which forms of everyday life money does and does not automatically provide**.

# 11. Support `story_0009` — Hanami Ume: exceptional body, ordinary group morale

Ume carries Saki's individual-training notes because they largely match the trainer's own plan. Hiro notices a target of fifty laps around the field and asks whether it is a mistake.

# 12. Support `story_0010` — Hataya Misuzu: care after rupture changes route, not existence

**Sources:**
- `.../story_0010/part_01.txt` — 16 messages
- `.../story_0010/part_02.txt` — 18 messages

**Participants:** Misuzu, Temari, dance trainer; Misuzu, Ume  
**Primary functions:** SyngUp aftermath, indirect care, baking, generalized nurturance

## 12.1 Misuzu stops handing Temari the drink; she does not stop preparing it

After a joint Class 1-1/Class 1-2 dance lesson, Temari stays for extra practice. Misuzu automatically inventories the support she has prepared: extra drink and towel.

Then she catches herself.

She says she **no longer needs to prepare those things**. After a pause, she changes the rule: she can leave them near Temari's belongings without giving them directly.

This is exceptionally dense relational evidence.

A rupture has occurred strongly enough that Misuzu is trying to respect a changed boundary. But embodied care habit persists before conscious decision. Her solution is not to erase the care; it is to **reroute it through lower-contact delivery**.

This should become a major input to Phase-6 SyngUp synthesis.

### Claim transitions

- **Misuzu — STRENGTHEN:** care can be habitual/automatic before it becomes deliberate.
- **Misuzu — STRENGTHEN:** she is capable of modifying contact behavior after relational rupture rather than insisting that good intentions entitle her to closeness.
- **Misuzu ↔ Temari — PROMOTE:** post-rupture care residue is direct evidence; separation does not equal emotional extinction.
- **Temari — OPEN:** Temari does not know about the indirect delivery in this scene; do not infer her reaction.

## 12.2 Baking is a generalized care practice, not Temari-exclusive behavior

Misuzu decides to make cupcakes during free time. Ume is delighted and says she loves Misuzu's sweets, comparing them with Saki's food as things that make her feel able to train more.

Misuzu accepts the comparison warmly. Ume offers to help as repayment for all the sweets; Misuzu accepts and gives her a role suited to her strength.

This matters because it prevents overfitting Misuzu's domesticity to Temari alone. She genuinely likes making food and can let another person help.

### Claim transitions

- **Misuzu — STRENGTHEN:** food-making is a broad care/pleasure practice, not only a possession mechanism toward Temari.
- **Misuzu — STRENGTHEN:** she can receive reciprocation and delegate a task instead of needing to provide unilaterally.
- **Misuzu ↔ Ume — STRENGTHEN:** warm low-pressure rapport exists outside the central Temari bond.

### Japanese-register note

Misuzu maintains polished `です/ます` speech even in domestic casual scenes, while `まりちゃん` remains an intimate lexical marker. Formal softness should not be treated as emotional distance by default.

# 14. Support `story_0012` — Amaya Tsubame: exacting mentorship and ordinary academic dominance

Tsubame leads a first-year lesson event and tells students not to slack because stamina is foundational.

- **Tsubame — STRENGTHEN:** she evaluates juniors on both technical uptake and social conduct.
- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** fast motor learning is visible to senior observers even before later development.

- **Sena ↔ Tsubame — STRENGTHEN:** rivalry comfortably spans low-stakes school performance and childhood teasing.

# 16. Ordinary social ecology

## 16.1 Food as relation rather than simple preference

Series 1 already gives several functions for food:

- Sumika promises Kotone a crepe as motivational care.
- Lilja shares won ice-cream tickets with Sumika.
- Misuzu bakes cupcakes and accepts Ume's help.
- Ume describes Saki's cooking as something that restores training energy.

Later synthesis should distinguish:

1. food preference;
2. food as labor/reward;
3. food as care delivery;
4. food as reciprocity;
5. food as social invitation.

These are related but not interchangeable.

## 16.2 Bodies are radically non-comparable

The tranche produces a striking bodily range:

- China is exhausted by training peers find ordinary and has poor prior PE performance.
- Ume treats extreme running volume as plausible warm-up because trainer and sister independently calibrate her that way.
- Hiro can ace academic work while collapsing from training and envying physical loads that would overwhelm her.
- Mao's athletic background supports exact posture control.
- Tsubame treats stamina as foundational and provides precise biomechanical correction.

This reinforces a key anti-flattening rule:

> **“hard-working” cannot be inferred from workload size without body-specific context.**

## 16.3 Socioeconomic context appears through ordinary logistics

Kotone's ongoing part-time work affects stamina, schedule, and rule compliance. China, despite wealth, is enchanted by mundane dorm communal life. Mao's dojo and dorm permission processes show access to structured extracurricular life. These support stories make class position visible through **time, housing, labor, and routine**, not merely explicit money dialogue.

## 16.4 Dorm life is a major informal social technology

The dorm provides:

- common eating/studying space;
- curfew rules;
- permission exceptions;
- opportunities for unplanned peer observation;
- a form of everyday horizontal life China explicitly desires.

This should feed Phase-6 ensemble analysis because relationships formed through repeated co-presence may not need narratively dramatic “bonding scenes.”

# 18. Japanese voice and register findings

These are textual-register findings only. Prosody remains AV-gated.

## 18.1 Saki

Strong declaratives and imperative momentum persist even in care. `今から色々教えてあげるわっ！` and `一分一秒も無駄にしちゃダメよ` show why warmth should not be simulated by softening her syntax.

## 18.2 Temari

Spoken classroom language remains controlled while inner narration can be contemptuous. When Saki competes openly, Temari's irritation becomes more animate and peer-directed rather than distant.

## 18.3 Kotone

Elongated vowels, cutesy intensification, hearts, and playful self-performance can be deliberately instrumental. The model must support rapid shifts among exhaustion, exaggerated affection, internal bluntness, and casual peer slang.

## 18.4 Mao

`ボク`, `キミ`, polished senior framing, and rule-enforcement language coexist with high embarrassment under cute-role instruction. Do not convert embarrassment into a different base voice.

## 18.5 Rinami

Gentle surface language can deliver firm sibling directives. Her acting becomes less stiff when relationally grounded, suggesting that voice/performance may change through *who she imagines addressing* rather than through abandoning her normal register.

## 18.6 Hiro

Extremely compressed literal utterances remain central: `あっち`, `ほんと？`, `隅から隅まで`, `とつげき～`. She can produce socially startling content without increasing verbal complexity.

## 18.7 Lilja

Hesitation markers increase under phonetic unfamiliarity but do not vanish after success. Improvement should not be simulated by deleting her pauses or cautious entry style globally.

## 18.8 Sumika

Casual `～っしょ`, invitations, praise, and food/reward language create low-pressure solidarity. Her register is socially active, not merely decorative slang.

## 18.9 China

`ですわ` register remains stable through exhaustion, embarrassment, dorm euphoria, and absurd jokes. Emotional intensity changes without dissolving identity-bearing speech.

## 18.10 Ume

Martial/cheer framing (`今日の戦場`, `突撃`, `えいえいおー`) becomes group morale play rather than literal aggression. Her high energy is inclusive.

## 18.11 Misuzu

Polite syntax remains natural in domestic interaction. Intimacy is often carried by content and naming (`まりちゃん`) rather than by dropping politeness.

## 18.12 Sena

Polished institutional speech remains stable even when the content is private plateau anxiety. Her “perfect” presentation therefore cannot be identified merely by formality level.

## 18.13 Tsubame

Command/declarative speech (`手を抜くな`, `私を頼れ`) is functional mentorship as well as rivalry style. The childhood tease `泣き虫星南ちゃん` shows a separate intimacy channel inside the same forceful voice.
