## Principal findings

1. Hatsuboshi is best described as a hybrid developmental institution: school, production incubator, competitive sorting system, labor intermediary, public venue, and industry pipeline.
2. Its macro-curriculum expands from stage access (`初`), to fan-mediated visibility (N.I.A.), to comparative artistic identity and evaluation of the complete Producer–idol collaboration (reformed winter H.I.F.).
3. Producer education includes scouting, diagnosis, scheduling, technical literacy, strategy, material support, body/condition care, and accountability for outcomes.
4. The institution recognizes plural forms of idol excellence, including technique, accuracy, expression, individuality, audience connection, adaptability, durability, and charisma that exceeds numerical measurement.
5. Cooperation and unit formation are genuine developmental goods, but the Prima Stella system repeatedly reintroduces singular scarcity.
6. Paid work, tuition, public records, and professional opportunity make material inequality part of the institutional story rather than an external concern.
7. Event 005 establishes the student council pseudo-agency as an exceptional succession mechanism and clarifies the authority boundary around credentialed P-course Producers.
8. 100 Pro and external attention place winter H.I.F. inside a widening school–industry network, while the exact terms of that involvement remain unspecified.
9. The strongest provisional institutional thesis is that Hatsuboshi is both enabling and disciplinary: the same systems that develop students also sort, expose, regulate, and unequalize them.
10. The V1 claim that Hatsuboshi is “protagonist-like” is strengthened only to the level of causal institutional agency; personhood, moral unity, and final thematic primacy remain unresolved.
