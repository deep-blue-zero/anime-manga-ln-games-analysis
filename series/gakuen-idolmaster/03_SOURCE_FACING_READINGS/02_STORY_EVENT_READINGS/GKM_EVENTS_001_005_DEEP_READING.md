# GKM EVENTS 001–005 DEEP READING

## 0. Scope, method, and principal finding

This is the first canonical Phase-4 numbered-event reading for the Gakuen Idolmaster V2 corpus. Its purpose is not to summarize five event plots. Phase 3 produced thirteen provisional character models from character-core material; Phase 4 tests those models inside multi-character social configurations where no single idol or Producer relationship controls the scene.

The source boundary is the dedicated `event_001-005.dialogue.txt` analysis bundle: **27 unique source scripts / 1,670 messages**. Twenty-five scripts are the five five-part numbered events. Two additional scripts, `adv_event_highscore_introduction-01/02.txt`, are event-system/institutional framing and are analyzed separately rather than misidentified as portions of Event 001. The older shared `03_story_events_001-005` bundle is a strict 25-script subset and is therefore an alias view, not additional evidence.

Exact wording is controlled by Source Lock 1.0. This pass reads the A2 dialogue layer while retaining A1 raw-script locators for every source. No uninspected facial expression, camera movement, vocal delivery, or rendered staging is promoted into a claim. Places where performance evidence could materially discriminate between interpretations are routed to the audiovisual queue.

### Principal tranche thesis

Events 001–005 reveal **peer relation itself as a production technology**.

The five stories repeatedly move idol development out of the isolated Producer–idol dyad and into social systems that characters construct for one another:

- Class 1-1 becomes a **competitive federation**: members do not need to become temperamentally harmonious or even agree that they are friends. They learn to make rivalry, bluntness, teasing, care, and reciprocal usefulness coexist.
- Class 1-2 becomes an **affiliative mutual-aid group**: explicit friendship, acceptance of eccentricity, and shared low status create a different route into collective resilience.
- The third-year summer story treats **care work, audience pleasure, and senior responsibility** as part of professional idol formation rather than distractions from it.
- The summer-festival return to Class 1-1 shows that competition can become **pedagogy, play, and intimacy**: conflict is not abolished; it is domesticated into a relationship form.
- The student-council story turns generational succession into a literal production project and exposes the institution's own safeguards against concentrated authority.

The broader implication is that Hatsuboshi does not merely train individual performers. It creates overlapping environments in which idols **produce one another**—through rivalry, teaching, friendship, audience work, criticism, institutional office, and sometimes badly calibrated care.

This substantially strengthens the Phase-2 institutional reading while adding a corrective: Hatsuboshi's developmental ecology is not harmonious by design. Much of its generative power comes from allowing incompatible motives and styles to collide without requiring them to become identical.

# 2. Event-system introductions — competition as dual training

**Sources:**

- `adv_event_highscore_introduction-01.txt`
- `adv_event_highscore_introduction-02.txt`

The school announces `アイドル強化月間`, addressed jointly to `プロデューサー科` and `アイドル科` students. The stated challenge is not simply which idol performs best. The director asks who can make an idol shine most brightly and frames the month as a period for both **idol growth and improvement of the producer's own skill**.

The follow-up introduces individualized special programs after the school has observed the participants' ability.

This is a small source, but it reinforces a central institutional proposition: Hatsuboshi treats production as a trainable practice evaluated alongside idol development. The Producer is not an invisible neutral facilitator. Production competence itself is an object of competition, pedagogy, and institutional testing.

### Institutional delta

**STRENGTHEN:** Hatsuboshi's model is co-developmental. It trains the person being produced and the person doing the producing.

**STRENGTHEN:** individualized interventions are institutionally normal; special treatment need not imply a break from the school's pedagogy.

**OPEN:** the exact relation between `アイドル強化月間` and the numbered events is not established by this bundle. Do not use physical bundle adjacency as chronology.

# 3. Event 001 — Class 1-1: cooperation without harmony

**Sources:** `adv_event_001_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Lilja, Sumika, Temari, Saki, Kotone; Class 1-1 background collective  
**Primary setting:** first-year Class 1-1, beginning with first-day introductions  
**Conflict:** a class composed of ambitious, socially incompatible students must become capable of collective action  
**Institutional function:** establishes that class membership has agency-like and competitive consequences beyond ordinary homeroom administration

## 3.1 The class begins as an anti-team

The first scene is unusually valuable because it catches several characters before later relationships can make their behavior look inevitable.

Temari introduces herself as an internal-progression student in her fourth idol year and immediately warns inexperienced entrants not to drag the class down. Saki answers by advertising her entrance-exam first-place status and promising to silence the internal students through ability. Kotone tries to lower the temperature. Sumika privately diagnoses the atmosphere as awful. Lilja, while visibly intimidated, identifies a different problem: **she does not want to lose**.

That last point matters. Lilja does not ask Sumika to protect her from the competitive environment. She is worried that her own weakness may compromise a class whose success matters to her dream. This is one of the clearest ensemble confirmations of the Phase-3 claim that timidity and ambition are not opposites in Lilja.

Sumika's response is equally important. She does not solve Lilja's fear by telling her not to care about competition. She translates the fear into a social intervention: if class fragmentation creates a competitive weakness, build class cohesion.

The event therefore begins with a chain:

> **Lilja's private competitive fear → Sumika's social diagnosis → collective intervention.**

That is already an example of courage becoming circulatory. Lilja supplies the need; Sumika gives it social form.

## 3.2 Lilja versus Temari: fear does not cancel jurisdiction

The Event 001 confrontation with Temari is one of the strongest Phase-4 tests of Lilja.

Lilja explicitly admits that she may be the weakest member of the class. Temari's initial answer—if so, quit being an idol—is severe and entirely consistent with early Temari's professional absolutism. Lilja does not answer with counteraggression. She agrees that she must work harder, but refuses Temari's implied authority to decide whether her aspiration is legitimate.

Her actual argument is institutional and future-oriented:

- the class will need to cooperate;
- classes may compete;
- her own dream is harmed if her class loses;
- therefore even the strongest member has obligations to collective effectiveness.

Sumika jokingly supplies the blunt sentence Lilja cannot quite finish: do not drag *us* down.

The joke works because the logic is genuinely Lilja's even though the aggressive delivery is not. Temari recognizes the argument. She agrees to cooperate because class cohesion serves her own top-idol goal.

This is not friendship-through-kindness. It is **reciprocal jurisdiction negotiated through shared stakes**. Lilja accepts Temari's demand that weak members improve; Temari accepts Lilja's counter-demand that strong members contribute to the collective.

### Claim transitions

- **Lilja — STRENGTHEN:** strong will can appear while fear, hesitation, apology, and low technical confidence remain fully present.
- **Lilja — STRENGTHEN:** her ambition is not merely Producer-borrowed belief; very early ensemble material gives her an independent competitive stake.
- **Temari — STRENGTHEN:** harshness is often organized around performance standards rather than simple dislike of people.
- **Temari — REVISE:** “socially cold” is too simple. Once persuaded that relation has performance value, she changes behavior quickly.

## 3.3 Saki: competition as epistemology

Sumika persuades Saki to participate by speaking Saki's language: class cohesion might matter because classes compete. Saki immediately tries to claim leadership on the theory that the best idol should lead. Lilja's simple objection—she does not yet know Saki well enough to know whether Saki should lead—punctures status as self-certifying legitimacy.

Sumika then engineers a direct Saki–Temari singing contest.

Saki loses.

Her response is decisive for the Phase-3 model. She does not retreat from Temari, minimize the loss, or reinterpret the contest as meaningless. She immediately says that she likes people with ability, declares Temari a friend, and demands a future dance rematch.

For Saki, contest is a way of **acquiring information about another person**. Once the other person's strength becomes real, rivalry creates relational value. This is why her later sentence that she will compete with the entire class “to deepen mutual understanding” is funny but not empty. The logic is genuinely hers.

Event 001 therefore strengthens an important distinction:

> Saki's competitiveness is not only a desire to rank above other people. It is one of her principal methods for making other people *real* to her.

That can become intrusive, but it also makes her highly receptive to evidence. A person who beats her is not expelled from the social field; the person becomes more interesting.

## 3.4 Kotone's exhaustion makes class conflict material

Kotone initially refuses the after-school gathering because she has work. Later Saki notices that Kotone looks persistently unwell and learns that she is working many shifts. Temari condemns the situation as incompatible with serious idolhood. Kotone counters with a socially sharp version of her usual defense: high-consciousness people can dislike her low-consciousness way of living, but she has circumstances they do not know.

Saki does something different. She does not demand disclosure. She massages Kotone, gives her food and hydration, calls the intervention only first aid, and explicitly says that she does not know Kotone's circumstances and does not intend to intrude.

This is significant because Saki's care does not require interpretive ownership. She recognizes a bodily problem and acts within the part she can see.

Kotone calls her “older-sister-like.” Saki enthusiastically accepts the role.

### Claim transitions

- **Kotone — STRENGTHEN:** early “greed” and work orientation sit on top of a visibly exhausting material schedule. The event does not reveal the family debt, but it shows why “she just cares about money” is already inadequate.
- **Kotone — STRENGTHEN:** she can defend her boundary aggressively without becoming socially withdrawn.
- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** older-sister identity generalizes beyond Ume. It is a preferred mode of useful competence, not only a biological role.
- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** she can help without first obtaining the full private explanation.
- **Temari — OPEN:** her criticism is substantively correct about Kotone's health risk but socially injurious because she treats unknown constraint as weak commitment. Later event/support evidence should test whether she learns to separate standards from causal judgment.

## 3.5 Temari's nickname proposal: intimacy miscalibrated, not absent

At the classroom party Temari apologizes for the earlier way she spoke. Her proposed repair is comically overcorrected: everyone should immediately use nicknames. She suggests `りーぴゃん` for Lilja, then admits that she does not dislike being called by a nickname herself.

The important reading is not “Temari is secretly cute.” It is that **Temari has poor calibration of interpersonal distance**. She can move from defensive hostility to an unexpectedly high-intimacy proposal without possessing the ordinary social gradations that would make the move feel natural.

Kotone and Sumika normalize the situation toward first-name address instead.

This gives Phase 6 a concrete mechanism for Temari's social difficulty: not lack of desire for relation, but an unstable translation between desire and acceptable social form.

## 3.6 Class 1-1's social contract

The ending refuses a conventional harmony resolution.

The members acknowledge that their dreams differ. Temari says they probably cannot become friends. Sumika asks whether that really matters. What they can agree to is bounded cooperation:

- do not impede Temari;
- contribute to Saki's class victory;
- help when useful;
- continue for the year together.

The class becomes viable without becoming ideologically unified.

### Event 001 thesis

> **Class 1-1 creates solidarity by converting incompatibility into usable friction.**

Its members do not solve difference. They discover procedures—competition, teasing, negotiation, care, names, games—through which difference stops being fatal to cooperation.

### AV requests

**P0:** `adv_event_001_main-02` — Lilja/Temari confrontation and Sumika's ventriloquized blunt line. Voice ownership, timing, and Temari's freeze/recovery would materially sharpen the social reading.

**P1:** `adv_event_001_main-05` — Temari's apology and `りーぴゃん` proposal. Delivery could discriminate between earnest overcorrection, deadpan experiment, and more self-conscious embarrassment.

# 4. Event 002 — Class 1-2: the Worst Three and affiliative resilience

**Sources:** `adv_event_002_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** China, Hiro, Ume, Misuzu; Class 1-2 background collective  
**Primary setting:** first-year Class 1-2, beginning with first-day introductions  
**Conflict:** low-ranked newcomers and an infamous former elite must build a class relation while anticipating competition with Class 1-1  
**Institutional function:** contrasts another class culture and demonstrates that class identity can become a developmental resource

## 4.1 “Worst Three” as voluntary identity

China worries that her last-place entrance score will make her socially isolated. Hiro reassures her by expecting to rank even lower, only to discover she is second-to-last. Ume then reveals that she entered from the supplementary list and jokes that she did not actually pass the regular exam.

The sequence produces the self-named `ワーストスリー`.

What could have become a shame hierarchy becomes an affiliative identity. Ume proposes that the three climb from the bottom together. Hiro explicitly likes starting from the very bottom. China, who does not naturally eroticize difficulty the way Hiro does, decides that she will keep up because she came to become a proper idol.

This scene is useful because the three characters **assign different meanings to the same low-status fact**:

- China experiences it as evidence requiring accountable work.
- Hiro experiences it as valuable resistance.
- Ume experiences it as an exciting starting line in a race upward.

Their friendship does not require psychological sameness.

### Claim transitions

- **China — STRENGTHEN:** her weakness is accurately perceived rather than coyly denied; aspiration begins from accepting the evidence.
- **Hiro — STRENGTHEN:** difficulty is intrinsically attractive to her before later stakes complicate it.
- **Ume — STRENGTHEN:** losing position is not humiliating if it creates a race she can enter.
- **Worst Three relation — PROMOTE:** shared low status is not merely a fandom label or later retrospective grouping. It is self-authored early social infrastructure.

## 4.2 Ume's body is socially legible before idol technique is

Ume's search for Misuzu is comic exaggeration, but it also supplies a clean early distinction between athletic capacity and idol skill. She races through the school, identifies Misuzu at extreme distance, and treats physical action as her default solution.

That matters later in Event 005, when Sena cannot read Ume's idol talent but is astonished by the physical demonstrations that won the headmaster's supplementary-admission approval. Across the tranche, Ume's body is an obvious resource whose translation into “idol talent” remains uncertain.

This supports the Phase-3 distinction between **developmental potential** and conventional starting metrics.

## 4.3 Misuzu: reputation and behavior do not align cleanly

Misuzu has left the class schedule because she went to observe a friend in another class with whom she is fighting, then became distracted by warm weather and fell asleep outside.

Her explanation is a compact version of the Phase-3 Misuzu problem. Care, avoidance, laziness, possessiveness, and self-authored pace are not separable into clean moral boxes. She really is concerned about the friend. She also really does choose a nap over schedule compliance.

China responds not by moral panic but by inviting the self-described `不良` to be friends while also insisting that promises and time must be respected.

The rest of the class is initially intimidated because Misuzu's middle-school unit has a notorious reputation. Ume and Hiro puncture that social distance through curiosity. The conversation turns rumors into stories, and the class physically closes the gap.

### Claim transitions

- **Misuzu — STRENGTHEN:** `lazy genius` remains inadequate. She violates schedule, but the action originates in a relational concern and then drifts into chosen comfort.
- **Misuzu — STRENGTHEN:** public reputation can make her socially frightening even while her direct interpersonal behavior is gentle.
- **China — STRENGTHEN:** receptive authority is visible at a small scale. She welcomes Misuzu without surrendering the norm that commitments matter.
- **Ume/Hiro — STRENGTHEN:** unconventional behavior is often met with curiosity rather than policing, making them unusually effective at reducing stigma.

## 4.4 Hiro's adversity ethic receives an early moral boundary

When the class learns that it may compete with Class 1-1, Hiro initially likes the prospect of difficulty. She then distinguishes between two things:

> struggling herself is enjoyable; becoming the reason hardworking friends lose is not.

This is a high-value Phase-4 confirmation of the integrated AV revision from “Hiro loves failure” toward **Hiro loves resistance**.

A selfish pain-seeking model predicts indifference to burdening the group. Event 002 directly contradicts it. Hiro wants difficulty whose consequences she can own. Friendship creates a reason to improve even when easy success remains unattractive.

**STRENGTHEN:** `chosen impossibility becoming accountable attachment`.

## 4.5 China turns anxiety into practice

China's response to the threatened class competition is one of the tranche's cleanest statements of her ethic. She says that fear and anxiety created by idol activity can only be dispelled through steady practice.

This is not confidence rhetoric. She remains anxious. The solution is not to imagine herself already excellent but to give anxiety a governed behavioral answer.

That maps closely onto the later `receive → register → organize → answer` AV formulation: she receives the threatening information, registers the fear, converts it into an actionable norm, and tries to organize the group around the next task.

## 4.6 Misuzu's jealousy and the unnamed friend

The final scene contains a high-value relationship locator. Misuzu is upset that her friend in Class 1-1 has apparently made close friends while remaining in conflict with Misuzu. She responds childishly and honestly: if that person is going to make friends, Misuzu will make many friends in Class 1-2 too.

Given the established Phase-3 Misuzu/Temari history, Temari's Class 1-1 membership, and the active quarrel state, **Temari is the leading identification of this unnamed friend**. However, Event 002 itself withholds the name. The identification should therefore remain an explicit analytical inference rather than be laundered into textual fact until cross-source confirmation is cited.

Misuzu and Hiro decide to organize a class party partly to prove useful to China and Ume after causing trouble. China and Ume overhear, refuse exclusion, and turn the plan into the class's first collective task.

### Event 002 thesis

> **Class 1-2 creates solidarity by making weakness, eccentricity, and social trouble survivable inside explicit friendship.**

This is not simply “the nice class” opposite the “competitive class.” Ume is intensely competitive, Hiro actively wants hardship, Misuzu is possessive and noncompliant, and China enforces norms. The difference is that their default relational claim is inclusion rather than conditional cooperation.

### AV requests

**P0:** `adv_event_002_main-05` — Misuzu's `悔しい` / “that girl made close friends” passage. Tone and pacing would help distinguish wounded possessiveness, comic sulking, and deeper abandonment anxiety.

**P1:** `adv_event_002_main-01` — Worst Three naming sequence. Useful for modeling how China, Hiro, and Ume assign sharply different affects to the same low-rank information.

# 5. Event 003 — last summer: professionalism recovered through pleasure and care work

**Sources:** `adv_event_003_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Mao, Rinami, Sumika; Kotone in opening; trainers, staff, customers, Azzassy  
**Primary setting:** third-year voluntary summer school and associated beach live  
**Conflict:** Mao's last-summer result pressure narrows her judgment until Rinami and Sumika restore the audience and social ecology to view  
**Institutional function:** expands idol labor beyond formal training and frames public service, festival pleasure, and audience relation as professional formation

## 5.1 Mao's time scarcity

Mao repeatedly names the event as one of the final major chances of her third year. That time pressure explains behavior that would look merely preachy in isolation.

She first admonishes Kotone for working too much. The concern is substantively grounded: Event 001 has already shown Kotone's exhaustion. But Mao assumes that the solution is obvious because her own horizon is dominated by the brevity of a three-year idol education.

The same pattern intensifies with Sumika. Seeing Sumika work at a beach restaurant while other first-years may be training, Mao warns that three years disappear quickly and that Sumika may fall far behind Saki, Temari, and Lilja.

Sumika refuses the implied jurisdiction: summer vacation is hers to spend.

The scene is valuable because **both characters are partly right**.

Mao is correctly identifying opportunity cost. Sumika is correctly identifying a boundary: without knowing why she is working, Mao cannot convert professional advice into authority over her summer.

## 5.2 Mao can diagnose her own overreach

Rinami later asks whether Mao is thinking about the encounter. Mao immediately admits that she went too far, explicitly stating that Sumika's school-life choices are Sumika's own and that Mao did not have standing to say so much.

This is a strong confirmation of Mao's Phase-3 model because it shows role revision occurring without external punishment. Her `先輩` / dorm-leader / prince mode creates an intervention impulse; evidence and reflection allow her to rewrite the performance.

**STRENGTHEN:** reclaimed aspiration through refractive role authorship.

**STRENGTHEN:** Mao's protector/senior role is real and useful, but not automatically legitimate in every context.

**REVISE:** senior care should not be modeled as uniformly well-calibrated. Under anxiety, Mao can overextend from concern into jurisdiction.

## 5.3 Rinami's care is not reassurance; it is reframing

Rinami notices Mao's tension before Mao names it. She does not answer by saying that results do not matter. Instead she changes the performance frame.

The summer live is a festival. The audience is coming to get wet, shout, laugh, and participate. The performers need to enjoy the festival if they want to give the audience that experience.

This is a compact early expression of **reversible hospitality**:

1. Rinami receives Mao's fear.
2. She holds it without denying the stakes.
3. She offers a different relation to the event.
4. Mao answers by changing her behavior.
5. The changed behavior later enlarges both Mao's and the audience's experience.

Rinami is not maternally soothing Mao back into passivity. She redirects professionalism toward the people performance is for.

**STRENGTHEN:** care and competition/professional ambition are additive for Rinami.

**STRENGTHEN:** hospitality is produced through attention and framing, not merely softness.

## 5.4 Sumika's “unseriousness” contains real obligation

When Mao returns to the beach restaurant, she learns that Sumika is spending the summer there because the shop belongs to a relative whose health has failed. The restaurant has long supported the school's summer live. Closing it would damage an event the family and community care about, so Sumika is filling the labor gap.

This materially revises any simple equation of “not training” with “avoidance.”

Sumika still chose not to explain herself when challenged. That is consistent with her resistance to being governed through other people's expectations. But the hidden content of the choice is not indifference. It is family and community responsibility.

She also says that she treats customer-facing speech and posture as practice: service work can be performed as if she is already under an audience's gaze.

**STRENGTHEN:** self-authored expectation / kinetic self-authorship.

**REVISE:** strategic unseriousness is a defense against judgment, not a reliable indicator of low investment or low responsibility.

**STRENGTHEN:** Sumika can convert an externally imposed practical duty into an internally useful performance laboratory.

## 5.5 Repair becomes collaboration

Mao apologizes once she knows the facts. Sumika immediately recognizes that the original criticism came from concern and admits that she pushed back too hard.

The repair is notable for its symmetry. Neither person is required to become wholly wrong.

Later, when the restaurant becomes dangerously understaffed, Mao and Rinami spend their free time helping. Mao moves between taking orders, public-facing idol promotion, and physically intervening when another worker is being harassed. Sumika's verdict—Mao really is a prince—is earned through action rather than image.

This makes the event a particularly strong Mao text. Princehood becomes **portable social conduct**: taking responsibility, stepping into disorder, preserving another person's room to work, and then returning the scene to collective pleasure.

It also prevents a moralizing reading of “real idol work” versus ordinary labor. The restaurant and the live become one festival ecology. Customers at the restaurant become live spectators; the live drives food sales; the sick grandmother watches the live remotely and is emotionally restored; Sumika's service labor makes the festival possible.

## 5.6 Sena's absence and competitive mercy

Mao says she is relieved Sena did not attend the summer school. Sena's stated reason is that she wants to watch idols rather than perform; Mao interprets this as a polite excuse because Sena knows her participation would take opportunities away from others.

The interpretation is **Mao's inference**, not direct Sena confession, but it fits the later Event 005 picture of Sena as someone who thinks institutionally about how her own supremacy changes the developmental field.

This should be retained as a moderate-confidence precursor to generative supremacy, not promoted to uncontested fact.

## 5.7 The final-summer wound

After the successful live Mao privately realizes that her last summer at Hatsuboshi is ending. The important emotional movement is not triumph over fear. It is acceptance of finitude without regret: the summer ends, but she gave herself to it fully.

That ending helps explain why “enjoy the festival” is not a lightweight lesson. For Mao, pleasure becomes an answer to time scarcity that performance results alone cannot solve.

### Event 003 thesis

> **Professional seriousness fails when it forgets what the profession is for.**

Mao begins by interpreting limited time as a reason to optimize harder. Rinami and Sumika show her a larger professional ecology: audience pleasure, family obligation, service labor, community memory, and the performer's own joy are not interruptions to idol work. They are part of the thing being produced.

### AV requests

**P0:** `adv_event_003_main-03` — Mao's self-critique, Sumika's explanation, apology, and reconciliation. Delivery is important for distinguishing pride wound from reflective responsibility.

**P0:** `adv_event_003_main-05` — Mao's internal “last summer” passage. This is a major low-action affective state relevant to reconstruction of Mao outside competitive dialogue.

**P1:** `adv_event_003_main-02` — Mao's first confrontation with Sumika, to compare senior/prince register under stress against the repaired state.

# 6. Event 004 — rivalry becomes pedagogy, play, and friendship

**Sources:** `adv_event_004_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Saki, Temari, Kotone, Sumika, Lilja  
**Primary setting:** Class 1-1 summer outing at a Japanese festival  
**Conflict:** the class is more functional after Event 001 but still relationally rough; Lilja's first festival becomes a laboratory for cross-friendship and competitive teaching  
**Institutional function:** low-stakes texture with unusually high reconstruction value

## 6.1 Event 001 did not “fix” Class 1-1

The opening immediately rejects a simplistic progress narrative. Sumika says the atmosphere is still terrible even after the bonding party. Saki and Temari still fight. Both initially reject the summer outing for training.

This is good continuity. The event does not interpret group development as conflict removal. Event 001 established procedures for living with friction; Event 004 tests whether those procedures can generate pleasure and care.

## 6.2 Rationality is one of Saki's self-descriptions, not her whole causal structure

Kotone persuades Saki by arguing that a festival is a rational opportunity for a would-be class leader to build cohesion. Saki agrees with the logic, then asks whether Lilja will attend. Once told yes, she changes her decision.

The scene should not be overread as secret romance or hidden favoritism. Its analytical value is simpler: Saki's “rational” self-understanding does not exhaust her behavior. Personal competitive interest in Lilja matters.

**REVISE:** Saki's rationality is an authentic cognitive style but can also become retrospective language for choices with relational motives.

## 6.3 Sumika deliberately loosens exclusivity

When the group becomes separated in the crowd, Sumika and Temari eventually find the others but remain at a distance. Sumika explains that Lilja is becoming friends with Saki and Kotone without her, so she wants to watch rather than interrupt—even though she is a little jealous.

This is a crucial refinement to the Lilja–Sumika relationship.

Sumika's closeness is not ownership. She experiences the threat of relational expansion, names jealousy, and nevertheless chooses the behavior that gives Lilja room to build other ties.

**STRENGTHEN:** synchronized differentiation is not only a late REVERSI performance solution. The underlying relational capacity exists in ordinary social life.

**STRENGTHEN:** Sumika's social intelligence includes knowing when **not** to enter the scene.

## 6.4 Saki teaches in order to make the rivalry real

Saki initially crushes Lilja at festival games. Kotone criticizes the unfairness of taking pride in beating a complete beginner. Saki's solution is revealing: she does not stop competing. She starts teaching.

She corrects Lilja's posture, support, gaze, relaxation, and repeat attempts. The goal is not paternalistic accommodation. Saki wants Lilja competent enough that victory over her means something.

The developmental mechanism is therefore:

> **recognize another person's seriousness → transfer competence → remove excuses → demand a real contest.**

This pattern closely resembles Saki's broader generative rivalry, but here it appears in the smallest possible setting: a festival shooting game.

Her care is also embodied and procedural. She checks whether Lilja rested and hydrated. She keeps teaching until Lilja can beat her.

That is why Lilja experiences the contest as care. She tells Kotone that she is happy because Saki keeps teaching her even though she repeatedly fails.

### Claim transitions

- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** competition can function as pedagogy and investment in another person's growth.
- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** she prefers opponents who can answer her; domination without developmental reciprocity is unsatisfying.
- **Lilja — STRENGTHEN:** she is intensely competitive under the timid surface. Kotone directly notices that Lilja wears the same serious “I will beat you” face as Saki.
- **Lilja — STRENGTHEN:** received instruction is not passive rescue. She converts it into repeated self-directed attempts.

## 6.5 Cross-cultural texture is character, not trivia

Lilja has never attended a Japanese festival. She does not know how to open ramune or what `射的` is. The event lets that unfamiliarity exist without making her socially incompetent or exoticized.

Kotone explains objects; Saki teaches games; Lilja asks that her new friends teach her more about Japan.

This is useful for reconstruction because it locates one kind of uncertainty precisely. Lilja's Japanese is highly proficient. Her vulnerability is not generic “foreigner confusion”; it appears around specific embodied cultural practices and unfamiliar leisure scripts.

## 6.6 Kotone as regulator, then participant

Kotone spends much of the event preventing Saki's appetite from consuming Lilja's preferences. She negotiates a condition that if Lilja wins, they will go find Sumika and Temari. She tells Saki that beating a beginner is embarrassing. She notices when a public crowd is forming.

Yet the same social regulator eventually gets pulled into the game and becomes loudly competitive herself.

That duality matters. Kotone's social intelligence is not detached managerial composure. She can read the room, police excess, and then willingly become part of the chaos once the stakes feel safe.

**STRENGTHEN:** her competence is relational participation, not external control.

## 6.7 Temari understands more than she admits

From the edge of the scene Temari tells Sumika that Lilja has enough nerve that she does not need protecting. Sumika teases her for having learned Lilja well.

Later Temari again yields to Lilja's invitation while verbally denying that Lilja has special leverage over her.

The Event 001 relation has become a stable grammar: Lilja's quiet earnestness reaches Temari because it cannot easily be dismissed as status play. Temari may be hostile to soft social pressure, but she respects conviction.

## 6.8 “Friend” versus “comrade”

At the end Lilja uses her earned wish to ask Saki and Kotone to be friends. Saki refuses the premise: there is no need to spend a wish on something already settled—then immediately denies having called them friends and instead calls Lilja a `仲間`.

The label dispute should not obscure the functional relation. Event 004 demonstrates:

- voluntary leisure together;
- teaching and learning;
- concern for safety;
- teasing;
- future plans;
- willingness to rearrange training;
- shared cultural experience;
- a desire to keep competing.

Class 1-1's relational language remains combative even as its behavior becomes increasingly affiliative.

### Event 004 thesis

> **Class 1-1 does not mature by becoming less competitive. It matures by making competition safe enough to carry teaching, play, affection, and mutual development.**

### AV requests

**P0:** `adv_event_004_main-04` — Saki/Lilja repeated contest and teaching sequence, including Sumika/Temari observation. Facial and vocal transitions are central to distinguishing coercive pressure from mutually escalating play.

**P1:** `adv_event_004_main-05` — Lilja's friendship wish, Saki's `仲間` answer, Sumika's jealousy admission, and Temari's reluctant participation.

# 7. Event 005 — Sena's prototype agency and the politics of succession

**Sources:** `adv_event_005_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Sena, Tsubame, Rinami; substantive interactions with China, Misuzu, Saki, Ume; Kotone discussed; Class 1-1 cameo  
**Primary setting:** student-council freshman recruitment  
**Conflict:** Sena attempts to turn the student council into a quasi-idol agency that will produce her successor, while peers and candidates expose the limits of her judgment and authority  
**Institutional function:** major evidence for student-council powers, Producer Course priority, succession, talent recognition, and the golden-generation cohort

## 7.1 The student council as a bounded pseudo-agency

Sena proposes that the current student council operate as a `疑似的なアイドル事務所`. She will recruit promising freshmen and personally produce them as possible successors.

The institution permits the experiment—but under explicit constraints:

- an idol-course student acting as producer cannot contractually bind assigned idols;
- she does not receive the full benefits available to Producer Course students;
- facility use is restricted;
- when her activity conflicts with Producer Course activity, she must yield;
- her authority is explicitly smaller than that of a P-course student.

This is one of the tranche's most important institutional findings.

It demonstrates that Producer Course privilege is not merely cultural prestige. It includes **formal powers and resource priority**. It also demonstrates that Hatsuboshi recognizes a conflict-of-role problem when an elite idol and student-council president attempts to accumulate production authority.

The safeguards are imperfect—Tsubame and Rinami both regard the whole arrangement as questionably permissive—but they are real.

### Institutional transitions

- **STRENGTHEN:** the Producer Course is a distinct professional jurisdiction rather than just a label for “students who give advice.”
- **PROMOTE:** contracts, institutional benefits, and facility access are concrete components of producer authority.
- **STRENGTHEN:** Hatsuboshi can tolerate experimental cross-role production while imposing conflict-of-interest limits.
- **OPEN:** how consistently these safeguards are enforced outside Sena's exceptional case.

## 7.2 Sena names the project: successor before rival

Sena explicitly describes the desired recruit as:

- a top-idol egg;
- a possible savior who can guide Hatsuboshi;
- the successor to the `一番星`.

This is direct Phase-4 support for **generative supremacy**, but it also preserves the early problem inside that formulation. Sena is trying to make the future through other people while still occupying the position from which their future is defined.

Tsubame immediately attacks the practical contradiction: if Sena spends time managing others, does she still expect to beat Tsubame herself?

The scene therefore contains the mature Phase-3 problem in embryo:

> Can Sena produce competitors without converting them into extensions of her own crown?

## 7.3 Tsubame: bluntness with a privacy ethic

When Sena names Kotone as a candidate, Tsubame reveals that Kotone once consulted her about how a middle-school student could work legally. Tsubame had initially intervened because Kotone was attempting unauthorized work.

Sena is surprised this was never reported to her. Tsubame's answer is quietly important: the consultation arose from a private disciplinary matter, so it was not information to gossip about.

This expands Tsubame beyond “strict rule enforcer.” Her severity contains **role-bounded discretion**. She can enforce a rule and still regard the subordinate person's private circumstances as protected.

**STRENGTHEN:** heroic rivalry includes institutional ethics, not only performance ideals.

**STRENGTHEN:** Tsubame's care often appears as correct boundaries rather than soft reassurance.

## 7.4 Kotone: talent is obvious to Sena before status catches up

Sena calls Kotone's idol talent `比類なき`, despite Tsubame remembering poor school performance. Sena wants to bring Kotone close and refine the talent personally, but Kotone has not accepted.

This is an important external perspective on Kotone:

- low grades and overwork are not valid proxies for low ceiling;
- Sena's possessive recognition (`私のモノ`) does not automatically secure consent;
- Kotone's later refusal of successor scripting is foreshadowed by the fact that even early recruitment is not easy to impose on her.

**STRENGTHEN:** material precarity obscures capability without defining it.

**STRENGTHEN:** accurate recognition does not equal jurisdiction—a principle that later becomes central to Sena's development.

## 7.5 China: inherited endorsement without automatic legitimacy

The headmaster asks Sena to take responsibility for China and says China's idol talent is roughly comparable to Sena's. The statement is authoritative evidence of **the headmaster's evaluation**, not a neutral scientific fact about total idol value.

China accepts the student-council invitation instantly because Sena is an admired senior. Her enthusiasm makes the asymmetry visible: this is exactly the kind of inherited endorsement that later China must learn to govern rather than merely receive.

The scene therefore fits the Phase-3 thesis unusually well without requiring retroactive maturity.

**STRENGTHEN:** China begins inside networks of affection, institutional sponsorship, and inherited legitimacy.

**STRENGTHEN:** the ethical problem is not whether those resources exist, but whether she can make their use answerable.

## 7.6 Misuzu: ability, reputation, and hidden motive

Sena chooses Misuzu from the former SyngUp! members because she believes Misuzu combines extraordinary skill with large latent capacity. Her joking third reason is that Misuzu is the only former member who appears well behaved.

The scene adds an elite external evaluation to Misuzu's Phase-3 portrait. Misuzu's low-energy manner should not be mistaken for low ceiling.

Misuzu initially finds the student council troublesome. She changes her answer only after asking how many additional members the quasi-agency can accept.

That sequence strongly suggests that her decision is relationally strategic, but **the event does not state the intended beneficiary**. Do not fill the gap automatically. Carry it forward as an OPEN relationship hypothesis.

**STRENGTHEN:** high capability and self-authored low-tempo living coexist.

**OPEN:** why the remaining-capacity question changes Misuzu's decision.

## 7.7 Sena versus Saki: mirror recognition through antagonism

Sena's encounter with Saki is one of the most important scenes in the tranche.

Sena observes that Saki has grown significantly since the entrance ceremony, then predicts that Saki will soon encounter a wall she cannot cross. When Saki asks what Sena herself would do at such a limit, Sena answers with an absolutist claim: an idol who has reached her growth limit has no future; a star that cannot reach the dream simply disappears.

Saki does not accept the senior's authority. She says that Sena's older public words once embodied the pride and ambition she admired in Hatsuboshi. The present Sena is therefore a disappointment. Saki then announces that she will destroy the supposedly unbreakable wall and become an example **for Sena**, replacing the example supplied by Sena's earlier self.

After Saki leaves, Sena insults her talent and calls her reckless—while visibly, according to Rinami's dialogue, becoming more animated. Tsubame and Rinami both say Saki resembles Sena. Sena refuses to recruit her.

Her reason is decisive:

> Saki is not a successor. She is an enemy/rival.

This scene directly exposes the difference between two relational categories in Sena's early production project:

- **successor** — someone Sena can cultivate from above;
- **rival/enemy** — someone whose claim can answer Sena from the same competitive plane.

Saki becomes valuable precisely because she refuses the first category.

### Claim transitions

- **Sena — STRENGTHEN:** generative supremacy is rooted in the tension between successor-making and rival-making.
- **Sena — STRENGTHEN:** she is attracted to resistance against her own doctrine.
- **Sena — STRENGTHEN:** possessive recognition and genuine desire for others' growth coexist.
- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** she responds to deterministic ceiling claims by converting them into a challenge rather than requesting reassurance.
- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** her “false genius” self-understanding is compatible with elite observers seeing real limits. Her development claim is not “I secretly have infinite natural talent” but “a limit is not entitled to final jurisdiction.”
- **Saki/Sena — PROMOTE:** mirror rivalry is a major Phase-6 relationship edge.

## 7.8 Ume defeats Sena's talent-reading eye by being unreadable

Sena looks for one more freshman who might develop fast enough for the summer H.I.F. She sees Ume running and immediately chases her.

The crucial discovery is not that Sena sees enormous talent. It is that **Sena cannot read Ume at all**.

Ume is a supplementary admit. Her demonstrated qualification was extreme athletic capacity: inverted fingertip push-ups and acrobatics. Sena is astonished that someone with that body entered from the bottom of the selection system. Yet the thing that excites Sena is precisely that her usual eye fails.

This is a small but powerful epistemic wound in the early Sena model.

If her talent-recognition skill were perfect, Ume would be only another high-potential asset. Instead Ume becomes a source of uncertainty—and uncertainty becomes exciting.

**STRENGTHEN:** Ume's development cannot be reduced to conventional idol parameters.

**STRENGTHEN:** Sena's later recognition that visible metrics are only part of idol power has an early ensemble precursor.

**REJECT:** Sena's eye should be modeled as infallible talent detection.

## 7.9 Rinami as soft institutional resistance

Rinami spends much of Event 005 mediating between Sena and Tsubame, but she is not merely smoothing conflict. She asks whether an Idol Course student can legitimately act as producer. After hearing the formal limits, she remarks that Sena may still be getting off lightly.

Her politeness does not weaken the judgment.

This gives a useful low-stakes reconstruction rule for Rinami: **soft delivery can contain firm institutional evaluation**.

**STRENGTHEN:** hospitality is compatible with boundary judgment and criticism.

## 7.10 Event 005 thesis

> **Sena tries to reproduce the future vertically and discovers the most interesting people are those who resist being legible as successors.**

China readily enters the inherited structure. Misuzu enters for reasons that remain partly opaque. Kotone resists possession. Saki rejects the successor relation so completely that Sena reclassifies her as a rival. Ume defeats the talent-recognition apparatus by being unreadable.

The story therefore does more than introduce a student council roster. It dramatizes the limit of succession as a model of development.

### AV requests

**P0:** `adv_event_005_main-04` — Sena/Saki wall confrontation and Sena's post-conversation reaction. The difference between anger, delight, recognition, and competitive arousal is performance-sensitive.

**P0:** `adv_event_005_main-05` — Sena's reaction to being unable to read Ume's talent and the transition into excitement.

**P1:** `adv_event_005_main-01` — Tsubame/Rinami responses to Sena's pseudo-agency plan; useful for institutional-role voice modeling.

# 10. Institutional findings

## 10.1 Classes function as production-social units

Sumika explicitly compares an Idol Course class to an idol agency. That should not be converted into formal legal equivalence, but the analogy is supported by practice:

- class activities require collective competence;
- classes compete;
- members' individual weaknesses can become collective liabilities;
- class culture shapes training motivation and social support;
- classes can build internal methods for regulating conflict.

Class assignment is therefore part of idol ecology, not neutral school scenery.

## 10.2 Two class models coexist

Class 1-1 and Class 1-2 produce different social technologies.

**1-1:** competition → recognition → bounded cooperation.  
**1-2:** inclusion → shared identity → mutual aid → competition.

The institution does not force one temperamentally ideal mode of teamwork.

## 10.3 Summer school

Event 003 establishes a third-year voluntary two-night/three-day summer program with training on the first two days and a beach live on the final day, plus bounded leisure. For third-years it is emotionally framed as a scarce late-school opportunity.

This is relevant to later senior-character chronology and to the institutional theme of accelerated developmental time.

## 10.4 Public ecosystem around school performance

The beach live depends on local businesses, temporary labor, spectators, families, and public festivities. Audience relation is not confined to the stage. The event makes the idol economy and surrounding service work mutually reinforcing.

## 10.5 Student-council pseudo-agency

Event 005 provides direct evidence that the school can permit a student-council president from the Idol Course to engage in restricted production.

Key governance facts:

- Producer Course authority has formal priority;
- contracts are a meaningful component of producer power;
- institutional benefits and facility access are producer resources;
- cross-role experiments can be authorized but bounded;
- student-council recruitment has a recurring freshman cycle—Sena, Tsubame, and Rinami compare the current recruitment to their own two years earlier.

## 10.6 Succession as institutional ideology

Sena uses `後継者` not merely as personal flattery but as an answer to Hatsuboshi's future. Prima Stella is implicitly linked to institutional reproduction: the current summit is expected to create conditions for another summit.

Event 005 also begins to criticize that ideology from within. The most generative candidates are not necessarily those most willing to inherit a defined role.

# 11. Thematic findings

## 11.1 Competition is a relationship language

Across four of the five numbered events, competition generates relation rather than merely rank.

- Saki learns Temari through losing.
- Saki teaches Lilja because an unprepared opponent is not satisfying.
- Ume wants a stronger Class 1-1 because a weak rival would make victory less meaningful.
- Hiro wants difficulty but not at her friends' expense.
- Sena distinguishes successors from rivals and becomes most excited when challenged or surprised.

A useful Phase-8 hypothesis is therefore:

> **Gakumas treats a worthy rival as a person whose independent agency increases the value of one's own future.**

That is not universally benign—rivalry can still become coercive—but it explains why many characters experience another person's resistance as a form of recognition.

## 11.2 Care without ownership is a recurring achievement

Several scenes distinguish helping from possessing.

- Saki aids exhausted Kotone without demanding her private story.
- Sumika gives Lilja room to form new friendships despite jealousy.
- Rinami reframes Mao without deciding Mao's ambition for her.
- Tsubame protects Kotone's confidential history.
- Mao's error with Sumika occurs precisely when concern becomes presumed jurisdiction; repair requires restoring Sumika's authorship.

This creates a strong cross-series ethical axis for later synthesis:

> **When does accurate perception create responsibility to help, and when does it become a false claim of jurisdiction?**

Sena's production project is the large institutional version of the same problem.

## 11.3 Weakness becomes social infrastructure

The events repeatedly refuse to make weakness merely private deficit.

- Lilja's fear motivates Class 1-1 cohesion.
- Kotone's exhaustion elicits forms of care and conflict that reveal class differences.
- the Worst Three build identity out of low rank;
- Hiro's weakness becomes ethically serious when friends depend on her;
- Ume's supplementary admission destabilizes elite talent perception.

Weakness can generate relation, but the stories do not romanticize it as automatically good. It creates obligations and risks.

## 11.4 Pleasure is not the opposite of professionalism

Event 003 and Event 004 are especially clear. Mao becomes better prepared for the summer live when she recovers the festival's pleasure. Lilja improves at games because Saki makes serious practice playful. Sumika turns service work into stage-relevant practice.

This strengthens the broader project caution against treating visible suffering as the only evidence of seriousness.

## 11.5 Institutions reproduce themselves through people

The class, summer school, student council, Producer Course, local festival economy, and H.I.F. are not background containers. They are mechanisms that create character possibilities.

At the same time, characters modify those mechanisms:

- Sumika invents a bonding intervention.
- Class 1-2 copies and transforms it.
- Sena redesigns the student council into a pseudo-agency.
- Saki refuses Sena's succession model and forces a rival model into view.

Institution and character are mutually productive.

# 12. Japanese voice/register findings

This section records textual register evidence only. Performed vocal claims remain AV-gated.

## 12.1 Temari: distance can collapse too quickly

Early Temari uses surnames and blunt imperative/evaluative language. Once she decides the class should be closer, she jumps toward nickname use rather than moving smoothly through ordinary acquaintance stages. Her register behavior supports the social-distance calibration reading.

High-value tokens:

- `葛城` / `紫雲` after initial nonrecognition;
- immediate shift to `咲季` after Saki demands first-name address;
- proposed `りーぴゃん`;
- willingness to be called by a nickname herself.

## 12.2 Saki: declarative challenge plus care imperative

Saki's speech alternates between self-advertising challenge and practical instruction. Her care frequently sounds like command:

- rest;
- hydrate;
- adjust posture;
- look at the target;
- keep practicing.

This is useful for reconstruction: warmth should not automatically soften her syntax.

## 12.3 Kotone: register mobility as social tool

Kotone can move among:

- loose peer slang;
- deferential senior address;
- ironic politeness toward Temari;
- teasing manipulation of Saki;
- direct protective criticism when Lilja is being steamrolled.

The shifts are functional rather than evidence of unstable identity.

## 12.4 Lilja: hesitation remains while claims become firm

Lilja retains pauses, apologies, and uncertainty markers even when making nonnegotiable claims. The Event 001 Temari confrontation is especially useful because syntactic softness coexists with refusal to abandon the top-idol dream.

Do not reconstruct confident Lilja by deleting hesitation.

## 12.5 China: `わたくし / ですわ` survives panic and comedy

Even in low-status embarrassment, frantic chasing, and social anxiety, China's identity-bearing formal/feminine register remains durable. The content and tempo destabilize more than the register skeleton.

This directly supports the integrated AV formulation that `わたくし／ですわ` is not a brittle mask.

## 12.6 Hiro: deadpan compression

Hiro frequently gives short, literal, emotionally incongruous evaluations: ranking disappointment, “lower than low,” trouble as desirable, Misuzu as “heavy,” or absurd historical comparisons. Humor often comes from low-marking delivery at the textual level rather than elaborate phrasing.

AV is still needed for prosodic calibration.

## 12.7 Misuzu: formal softness can carry possessiveness

## 12.9 Rinami: soft register contains judgment

## 12.10 Sena and Tsubame: intimacy through adversarial address

# 17. Phase-4 tranche conclusion

Events 001–005 validate the architecture's decision to postpone definitive character monographs until after ensemble reading.

The Phase-3 models survive, but they become materially better.

The biggest corrections are not new secret backstories. They are **mechanisms**:

- Saki's rivalry is a way to know and build people.
- Temari's social difficulty includes overrapid intimacy, not only distance.
- Kotone's social intelligence is participatory and embodied in material precarity.
- Mao can mistake care for jurisdiction when time anxiety rises, then repair without abandoning the senior role.
- Lilja's ambition is already active inside her fear.
- China answers anxiety through governed practice and social responsibility.
- Hiro's appetite for difficulty stops where it would make friends pay for her pleasure.
- Rinami can criticize institutions without abandoning warmth.
- Sumika can feel jealousy without enclosing Lilja and can choose obligation without advertising it as seriousness.
- Ume turns low rank into propulsion while remaining difficult for elite measurement to interpret.
- Misuzu's softness comfortably contains truancy, possessiveness, competence, and care.
- Sena's successor project becomes most generative when people refuse to remain successors.
- Tsubame's severity contains confidentiality and role ethics.

The tranche-level conceptual result is therefore:

> **Gakumas treats development as socially coauthored but not socially homogenized. The strongest relations in these events do not erase difference; they create forms in which difference can keep answering back.**

That proposition should remain provisional until Events 006–021+ and the support-card pass test whether the pattern generalizes beyond this unusually foundational first tranche.
