# GKM EVENTS 021+ DEEP READING

## 0. Scope, method, and principal finding

This is the fourth and final canonical Phase-4 numbered-event reading for the Gakuen Idolmaster V2 corpus. It follows `GKM_EVENTS_001_005_DEEP_READING.md`, `GKM_EVENTS_006_012_DEEP_READING.md`, and `GKM_EVENTS_013_020_DEEP_READING.md` and uses the same source-facing discipline: the dedicated event bundle is read as a complete tranche; claims are routed through continuity rather than event number; Phase-3 character models are stress-tested against ensemble behavior; and performance-sensitive questions are queued rather than guessed from dialogue alone.

The locked source boundary is `event_021-plus.dialogue.txt`: **34 unique source scripts / 1,858 messages**. The numbering is not contiguous:

- Events 021–026 contain five scripts each;
- Events 027–028 are **absent from Source Lock 1.0**;
- Event 029 contains four scripts.

This artifact therefore closes Phase 4 against the **frozen corpus actually present**, not against an imagined complete numeric sequence. The absence of 027–028 is a snapshot-coverage fact. It is not evidence that the events never existed elsewhere, were deleted in-world, or should be reconstructed from context.

Event 029 is also structurally and continuity-wise different from the preceding six events. It places Asari-sensei in the role of idol and the Producer Course student in the role of her producer. The event text supports analysis of that role reversal, but it does not establish that the premise should be silently merged into Asari's ordinary institutional biography. It is therefore treated as **branch/novelty-gated modular evidence** unless a later continuity authority explicitly promotes it.

### Principal tranche thesis

The previous Phase-4 tranches established three increasingly complex social functions:

1. Events 001–005: **peer relation as production technology**;
2. Events 006–012: **other people as error-correction systems for aspiration**;
3. Events 013–020: **peers and loved ones as continuity-bearing witnesses**.

Events 021+ add the mechanism that lets all three persist beyond a single developmental episode:

> **Inheritance becomes durable when it circulates rather than merely transfers.**

A role, song, institution, ideal, friendship, or aspiration remains alive not when the successor reproduces it exactly, and not when the originator disappears after handing it over, but when later participants can answer it, alter it, return it, and make it available to still more people.

Across the tranche:

- Tsubame's childhood admiration of Sena becomes adult rivalry, but intimate knowledge of Sena's ordinary weakness does not cancel the public ideal. It makes the ideal more meaningful because Tsubame knows the work that produced it.
- Kotone repays Mao's first-day kindness through competent care, then helps Mao imagine a fandom that can love the prince and the ordinary person together. Mao in turn uses Kotone's friendship to face Sena's stage rather than continue avoiding it.
- Temari repairs a missed year of ritual with Misuzu by turning shared memory into a new artifact: a one-copy birthday/lullaby CD. Hiro does not replace either friend; she mediates until Temari can author the gift herself.
- Sena tries to treat `ENDLESS DANCE` as a legacy object to be handed down. Ume and Misuzu reject the funeral logic, insist Sena sing with them, and thereby return the song to circulation without dispossessing Sena of it.
- China, Ume, and Misuzu move from juniors managed by seniors into a next student-council generation that invents its own work systems and care rituals. Their legitimacy comes from doing the work differently while remaining answerable to the institution they inherit.
- Saki, Ume, and Kotone approach a 765 Production cover not as reproduction but as lineage. Their stated problem is how to make **their** `GO MY WAY!!`, and success is defined partly by creating an opportunity for the next Hatsuboshi group to follow.
- Event 029, within its gated premise, makes institutional pedagogy reversible: the teacher teaches the Producer, then becomes the Producer's idol; Saki and Temari can still challenge her as a rival while Kotone maintains ordinary role etiquette.

The tranche therefore clarifies a larger Gakumas principle:

> **Succession is not replacement. A healthy legacy creates more authors.**

This applies equally to rivalry, friendship, student government, songs, idol history, and character identity.

### Event 021 and the “one year” anchor

Event 021 supplies one of the cleanest local chronological markers in the event corpus. Tsubame discovers a photograph from the H.I.F. at which Sena first became Prima Stella and reflects that **one year has passed**. The event also contains the earlier H.I.F. memory itself: Sena wins, Tsubame places second, and Tsubame temporarily concludes that Sena has become unreachable.

This historical material can be promoted as C1 within the Sena–Tsubame shared history even though the containing event remains modular.

### Events 024–025 as a bounded succession sequence

Event 024 culminates Sena's year-long work producing first-year student-council idols and explicitly frames graduation as near. Event 025 then names China as **next student-council president** and has the junior trio design their own work system while the seniors test whether they can let go.

The safest bounded relation is:

`Sena's freshman production project -> Event 024 culmination -> late-year student-council succession state in Event 025`.

No stronger merge with every character route is required.

### Event 029 continuity gate

The internal order of Event 029 is simple and strong:

`Producer agreement -> first audition success -> second audition success/live access -> live and continuation decision`.

What is **not** established is whether Asari's idol debut belongs in the ordinary institutional history shared by the rest of the corpus. Until that is explicitly bridged, use Event 029 for:

- Asari possibility-space;
- teacher/Producer relational inversion;
- branch-specific speech and behavior;
- institutional comedy and producer pedagogy;

but not for an unqualified statement such as “Asari became a working idol during the main first-year chronology.”

# 2. Event 021 — Sena and Tsubame: the star becomes reachable again

**Sources:** `adv_event_021_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Sena, Tsubame, Rinami; Mao in H.I.F. memory  
**Primary settings:** student-council room, café, Tsubame's home, H.I.F. memory, observation deck  
**Conflict:** overwork exposes the senior leaders' inability to receive care; private childhood history and an old defeat expose why Tsubame's rivalry with Sena once collapsed and why it can now resume  
**Institutional function:** senior leadership health, delegation, and the relational cost of being Prima Stella

## 2.1 Rinami's care becomes a hard institutional boundary

Event 021 opens with Tsubame asleep in the student-council room and Sena visibly tired. Each immediately tries to make the other rest while refusing the same permission for herself.

The logic is symmetrical:

- Sena: Tsubame is busy and should rest; Sena's own council work is a president's duty.
- Tsubame: supporting the president is a vice-president's duty; Sena looks worse and should rest.
- both: idol work and council work are obligations that cannot simply be displaced onto others.

Rinami ends the deadlock by forbidding both student-council work. When Tsubame tries to replace council work with training, Rinami has already arranged for the trainer to forbid that too.

This is one of the clearest examples in the corpus of Rinami's hospitality becoming **coercive enough to protect the recipient from her own refusal**. Her normal preference for reversible care is not an absolute prohibition on command. When two high-status peers are locked into reciprocal self-sacrifice, Rinami uses formal access and social authority to remove their option to continue.

**STRENGTHEN — Rinami:** care includes knowing when consent is distorted by role obligation and exhaustion. She can impose a temporary boundary in order to restore later agency.

**STRENGTHEN — Sena/Tsubame:** both possess strong duty reflexes that can become mutually reinforcing overwork rather than healthy professionalism.

## 2.2 Forced leisure exposes a friendship older than the idol hierarchy

Once work and training are removed, Sena takes Tsubame to a café. What follows is not “Prima Stella versus rival.” It is two childhood friends reopening a long private archive.

They remember:

- Sena trying black coffee as a child and becoming tearful;
- Tsubame beating Sena in a 50-meter race and on a test;
- Sena becoming lost and crying when Tsubame found her;
- Tsubame imitating a childhood “Sena-chan dance” and proudly showing others.

The scene matters because neither woman treats these memories as compromising material that must be hidden to preserve status. They are weapons for teasing, proof of intimacy, and evidence that both have always been more ridiculous and vulnerable than their public images suggest.

This strengthens a pattern established in Events 017 and 020: **ordinary history does not sit outside idol identity; it supplies the relational context that makes public identity intelligible.**

### Japanese-register note

Private Sena/Tsubame conversation permits unusually rapid movement between present senior composure and childhood-coded speech. Sena's `ねえねえ` enthusiasm, Tsubame's teasing `泣き虫星南ちゃん`, and their mutual first-name address create a register that should not be simulated as either purely formal senior speech or pure hostile rivalry.

## 2.3 Tsubame is a fan because she knows Sena is imperfect

At Tsubame's home they watch a recording of a Hatsuboshi alumni joint live. The room itself contains a concealed archive of Sena merchandise. Sena asks the central question directly: Tsubame knows better than almost anyone that the public “perfect Sena” is incomplete, so why does she remain Sena's strongest fan?

Tsubame first offers the easy professional distinction: idols naturally have public/private differences. Then she rejects that explanation as insufficient.

She has watched Sena absorb severe training, keep practicing, and gradually become the person who shines on stage. She knows Sena cries, acts without thinking, and is terrible at scouting. Precisely **because** she knows the ordinary Sena, she values the idol Sena more.

This is a major refinement of the project's role-authenticity model.

> Public excellence is not authentic because private weakness is irrelevant. It can become more meaningful because a witness knows the labor connecting weakness to excellence.

**STRENGTHEN — Tsubame:** her fandom, rivalry, and childhood attachment are not separable compartments. They inform one another without collapsing into one emotion.

**STRENGTHEN — Sena:** the “perfect” idol persona is understood by intimate peers as an achievement, not an ontological claim that Sena literally lacks flaws.

## 2.4 The old H.I.F. defeat: “completed Sena” as a crisis of imagination

The H.I.F. memory shows the cost of Sena's first Prima Stella victory from both sides.

Sena wins and immediately experiences the summit as pressure. Her hands shake. She realizes that previous Prima Stella idols had to continue shining while being chased.

Tsubame, meanwhile, has delivered what she considers her best possible performance, places second above older students, and experiences the result as valueless because only first place matters to her. Mao tries to tell her that second place is objectively extraordinary. Tsubame refuses the consolation.

More importantly, she concludes that the idol called Juo Sena has become **complete**. She can no longer imagine a route to victory.

The crisis is not laziness. It is a collapse of counterfactual imagination: Tsubame cannot picture an action that changes the outcome, so effort loses meaning.

This makes her present recovery in Event 021 more significant. The later Tsubame is not simply “more confident.” She has recovered the ability to imagine Sena as a reachable future rather than a completed object.

**REVISE — Tsubame:** heroic rivalry is not an uninterrupted trait. It has a historical failure state in which admiration becomes immobilizing because the opponent appears complete.

**STRENGTHEN — Sena:** becoming Prima Stella can isolate the winner and demoralize the nearest rival simultaneously; supremacy produces relational externalities in both directions.

## 2.5 The observation deck: reachability replaces childhood impossibility

The event's opening childhood memory has young Sena point to the evening's first star and suggest that tall Tsubame might reach it. Tsubame says it is impossible.

At the observation deck years later, Sena reminds her of the story. Tsubame says she has changed. She formally renews the vow to surpass Sena while they are still enrolled.

The language is structurally the opposite of her H.I.F. collapse:

- then: Sena is complete; no winning image exists;
- now: Tsubame is formidable; of course she can do it.

Sena answers with teasing challenge, but her inner monologue is revealing: **she envies Tsubame**. Tsubame has wings Sena does not have; what remains is for Tsubame to trust them and step forward.

This prevents a simplistic hierarchy in which Sena is the complete mentor and Tsubame merely chases. Sena sees in Tsubame a form of freedom or forward movement she herself lacks.

### Event 021 thesis

> **Rivalry survives intimacy when the rival remains a person rather than becoming a monument.**

Tsubame can chase Sena again because she knows the crying child, the exhausted president, the imperfect scout, and the brilliant Prima Stella are one continuous person. Intimate knowledge makes the star more reachable without making her less bright.

### Relationship promotions

- **Sena ↔ Tsubame — PROMOTE:** childhood-friend / fan / rival / governance-partner relation. None of these labels is sufficient alone.
- **Rinami ↔ Sena/Tsubame — STRENGTHEN:** Rinami can exercise protective authority over the two highest-status student leaders when their duty reflexes become self-destructive.
- **Mao ↔ Tsubame — STRENGTHEN:** Mao is an early witness to Tsubame's deepest competitive collapse and responds with respect rather than humiliation.

### AV requests

- **P0:** `adv_event_021_main-03` — Tsubame explaining why knowledge of imperfect Sena strengthens rather than weakens her idol evaluation.
- **P0:** `adv_event_021_main-04` — Tsubame's post-H.I.F. breakdown and `完成した` judgment.
- **P0:** `adv_event_021_main-05` — renewed vow, Sena's response, and Sena's inner “wings” comparison.
- **P1:** `adv_event_021_main-02` — childhood teasing and private-friend register reversion.
- **P1:** `adv_event_021_main-01` — Rinami's transition from concern to prohibition.

# 3. Event 022 — Mao and Kotone: care makes self-confrontation possible

**Sources:** `adv_event_022_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Mao, Kotone, Rinami; Sena through notes/live; dorm students through care messages  
**Primary settings:** dormitory during Mao's illness; first-day flashback; recovery outing and Sena live  
**Conflict:** Mao treats the final senior-year H.I.F. as existential proof of whether she can be an idol and drives herself into illness; receiving care requires relinquishing the senior/prince role long enough to become dependent  
**Institutional function:** dormitory mutual aid, third-year endgame pressure, cross-year friendship

## 3.1 “If I cannot become an idol, I have nothing”: Mao's stakes become explicit

Mao overtrains for the summer H.I.F. despite the dance trainer ordering her to stop. Her internal monologue gives the pressure unusual clarity: for a third-year student this is the final chance, and if she cannot become an idol she believes she has **nothing**.

This is stronger than ordinary competitive anxiety. It explains why Mao's old role problem carried existential force. Idolhood is not one possible career among interchangeable alternatives in this state; it is the remaining structure through which Mao can imagine a valuable future.

The resulting illness is therefore not a generic “hard worker gets sick” story. It is a failure of self-preservation caused by identity foreclosure.

**STRENGTHEN — Mao:** late-stage aspiration includes catastrophic identity stakes. The risk is not only losing H.I.F.; it is interpreting loss as proof that no valuable self remains.

## 3.2 Kotone's competence reverses the senior/junior relation without humiliating Mao

Kotone finds Mao feverish, ignores Mao's attempt to minimize the problem, takes her back to her room, and begins care with the practical expertise of an older sibling.

Her competence is mundane and highly specific:

- hydration;
- room humidity;
- changing towels;
- medicine;
- food;
- sleep;
- checking whether Mao can walk;
- refusing symbolic objections such as “a senior should not cause trouble.”

Mao's seniority gives her no jurisdiction over the biological fact that she is ill. Kotone's ordinary family labor becomes the more relevant expertise.

This is another example of Gakumas distributing authority by **domain** rather than permanent rank.

Kotone does not need to perform reverence while providing care, and Mao does not lose dignity because the first-year student knows more about nursing sick people.

**STRENGTHEN — Kotone:** sibling labor has produced transferable care competence, not merely financial burden and maturity rhetoric.

**STRENGTHEN — Mao/Kotone:** their relation can reverse direction without becoming unstable.

## 3.3 Fever removes Mao's role control but does not reveal a “truer” Mao

Half-asleep, Mao calls for her mother, says she wants to become an idol, requests specific foods, sings an old Kansai local commercial song, and becomes what Kotone later calls a `わがまま王子様`.

The scene is useful precisely because it should **not** be interpreted as “the prince was fake; childish Mao is real.”

Illness reduces Mao's capacity to curate presentation. What surfaces is dependency, regional food memory, childhood performance history, and appetite. All of these are genuine. So is the prince role she chooses when healthy.

The correct revision is additive:

> Mao's role-authored self has more private material available than she normally lets the role display.

This continues the “refractive role authorship” model rather than overturning it.

## 3.4 The first-day wallet memory shows why Kotone's care is reciprocal history

Kotone remembers meeting Mao on entrance day after losing her wallet. Mao was strange-looking to her—ill-fitting sleeves and deliberate prince styling—but immediately helped search despite being a stranger.

Mao's explanation is characteristic: she is a prince, and she does not want a new student's first day to become a bad memory.

The wallet is eventually returned through the office, but Mao's effort matters relationally even though it did not directly solve the material problem.

Event 022 therefore turns the illness episode into **reciprocity across time**. Kotone is not arbitrarily nursing a nice senior. She is returning a kind of care that helped define Mao for her on the first day of school.

**STRENGTHEN — Mao:** “princehood” is practical ethics enacted toward strangers, not merely aesthetic self-branding.

**STRENGTHEN — Kotone/Mao:** present care sits inside a memory-bearing relationship rather than a one-off dorm obligation.

## 3.5 The dorm likes Mao enough to let her fail at being exemplary

Mao apologizes repeatedly for failing at health management and burdening the dorm. Kotone rejects the premise that the other students experience the event primarily as inconvenience.

Messages arrive from Lilja, Sumika, Misuzu, Temari, Hiro, Saki, and Ume offering food, errands, sleep support, research, replacement work, and—in Saki's case—the comically named “special SSD.”

The point is not that Mao's overwork was harmless. It is that **care debt is not automatically moral debt**. A person can fail at self-management, receive help, and remain loved without having to convert every act of care into a future repayment ledger.

This is especially important for Mao because her senior/prince identity predisposes her to interpret usefulness as obligation.

## 3.6 Kotone names Mao as an older friend

On their recovery outing, Kotone explains that she has long experience caring for younger siblings and peers but has never really had an **older friend**. Mao feels different from Sena, Tsubame, or Rinami: still respected as a senior, but accessible in a way that resembles friendship.

Mao is pleased rather than correcting the category back to hierarchy.

This should be promoted for Phase 6. Cross-year relation in Gakumas is not exhausted by mentor/junior language. Mao/Kotone is a clear case where asymmetrical age and institutional seniority coexist with horizontal friendship.

## 3.7 Kotone argues for a wider princehood; Mao uses Kotone to face Sena

Mao admits that she likes sweets but hides the preference because she worries it conflicts with the cool prince image her fans expect.

Kotone's answer is not “stop being a prince.” She says a cool prince who likes sweets is entirely viable, and that fans might like Mao more if they knew more of her ordinary self.

This is an important outside confirmation of the Phase-3 Mao thesis. The person who first found Mao's prince presentation odd now sees no contradiction between that role and additional visible traits.

Later Mao admits the deeper reason she brought Kotone to Sena's live: she was afraid to watch alone. She had repeatedly avoided Sena's third-year performances by claiming she needed to train. Watching Sena shine forced Mao to confront both the rival and the self she feared was inadequate.

Kotone does not solve that fear. Her presence makes the confrontation tolerable enough for Mao to choose it.

Mao's concluding resolution is precise: she will stop running and look directly at **Sena and herself** so that she can remain a cool version of Mao by choice rather than avoidance.

### Event 022 thesis

> **Receiving care can be an act of authorship when it makes self-confrontation possible.**

Kotone does not rescue Mao from the H.I.F. problem. She gives Mao enough embodied and relational safety to face it herself.

### Relationship promotions

- **Mao ↔ Kotone — PROMOTE:** cross-year friendship with reciprocal care history; Kotone explicitly experiences Mao as an older friend.
- **Mao ↔ Sena — STRENGTHEN:** Mao's avoidance of Sena's live demonstrates that rivalry can become painful enough to produce evasion, while Sena's ticket functions as an invitation back into relation.
- **Mao ↔ dorm ensemble — STRENGTHEN:** Mao is not merely dorm administrator/caretaker; the dorm can collectively care for her.

### Japanese-register evidence

- Kotone's rough peer-like imperatives remain compatible with respectful care toward a senior. Politeness level tracks function, not a single stable “junior voice.”
- Mao's illness speech becomes simpler, more childlike, food-specific, and regionally textured without deleting her `ボク` identity.
- Mao can call the outing an “escort” and still accept Kotone directing clothing choices; prince syntax and receptive behavior coexist.

### AV requests

- **P0:** `adv_event_022_main-01` — Mao's exhaustion, collapse, and Kotone's first caregiving intervention.
- **P0:** `adv_event_022_main-02` — fevered `ママ` / idol wish and the first-day wallet memory.
- **P0:** `adv_event_022_main-05` — Mao admits she was afraid to watch Sena alone and resolves to face Sena and herself.
- **P1:** `adv_event_022_main-03` — “selfish prince” illness requests and dorm-wide care messages.
- **P1:** `adv_event_022_main-04` — Kotone naming Mao as an older friend.

# 4. Event 023 — Temari, Hiro, and Misuzu: repair through authored attention

**Sources:** `adv_event_023_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Temari, Hiro, Misuzu; Ume and China in birthday scene  
**Primary settings:** classroom, shopping mall, Class 1-2, birthday gathering  
**Conflict:** after a birthday missed during the SyngUp! rupture, Temari wants a gift adequate to reconciliation but cannot translate long shared history into one object  
**Relational function:** Hiro mediates between two people she understands without appropriating the relationship

## 4.1 Temari wants ritual repair, not merely a successful present

Temari begins in February worrying about Misuzu's birthday. She and Misuzu exchanged gifts every year from childhood, but the previous year SyngUp!'s dissolution made celebration impossible. Their relationship has since improved, so Temari wants this year's gift to function as a **redo**.

This is why ordinary consumer advice fails. The problem is not “what does Misuzu like?” It is “what artifact can carry the fact that a relational ritual was broken and is now available again?”

Temari considers asking Rinha but predicts she will be told to think for herself. She instead notices Hiro deliberately advertising that she knows Misuzu well and is also close to Temari.

Temari verbally denies the friendship—`別に仲良しじゃない`—and immediately asks Hiro for intimate help.

The contradiction is useful modeling evidence: Temari's speech can understate relation even while behavior depends on it.

## 4.2 Hiro's mediation style is playful, intrusive, and highly accurate

Hiro accompanies Temari shopping, narrates their outing as a new story chapter, lures her into games and a photo booth, and gradually changes the problem from merchandise selection into relationship reconstruction.

She asks the most important diagnostic question: **when is Misuzu happy?**

Temari's memories all point toward relation:

- Misuzu is happy Temari watches her live;
- happy to go out together;
- happy Temari cleaned;
- happy Temari ate a disliked vegetable.

Hiro jokingly proposes that the present is therefore **Temari herself**. Temari rejects the literal version, but Hiro has identified the structure correctly.

Misuzu does not primarily want an expensive object. She wants evidence that the person she values is occupied by her presence.

## 4.3 Misuzu articulates possessive care unusually plainly

Hiro's hilariously poor “agent” interrogation is transparent enough that Misuzu quickly understands someone else is probably asking through her. Misuzu nonetheless gives the answer.

She says she prefers giving to receiving. She wants to fill important people with herself; happiness is seeing someone she loves become **full of Misuzu**.

This is one of the cleanest non-crisis formulations of Misuzu's centripetal attachment.

It should not be softened into generic generosity. The giving is possessive in structure: the gift's pleasure comes from occupying the recipient's attention and environment.

Yet it is also not reducible to coercion. Misuzu gives Hiro a usable clue and later cooperates as a “double spy” so Temari can create something authored rather than dictated.

**STRENGTHEN — Misuzu:** care and possession remain structurally intertwined even in benign gift exchange.

## 4.4 Shared history becomes design material

Hiro asks Temari to recount prior birthday gifts. Temari can do so at enormous length—Hiro jokes that it became a twelve-episode epic. The important finding is that Temari remembers the history in fine detail.

Hiro notices that Temari has never simply asked Misuzu what she wants. Temari refuses to begin now because she wants **this gift to be something she and Hiro think through together**.

That answer itself marks relational growth. Temari is not trying to reproduce the childhood dyad exactly. She allows a newer friend to help author the repair.

A remembered request finally supplies the form: Misuzu once asked Temari to sing her a lullaby every night so she could nap comfortably. Temari dismissed it at the time as another excuse not to train. Hiro believes Misuzu was serious.

Temari's solution is therefore a one-copy CD containing a birthday song and lullaby recorded specifically for Misuzu.

The gift works because it converts four layers into one artifact:

1. childhood ritual;
2. a previously ignored request;
3. Temari's professional strength—song;
4. current willingness to expend sustained attention on Misuzu.

## 4.5 Hiro is a mediator, not a replacement intimacy

The final reveal that Hiro and Misuzu were effectively running a double-agent operation is important. Hiro solved both people's problem:

- Temari needed enough information to author a meaningful gift;
- Misuzu wanted Temari to spend time thinking about her rather than simply supplying a shopping list.

# 5. Event 024 — Sena's producer year: legacy returns to circulation

**Sources:** `adv_event_024_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Sena, Ume, Misuzu, China; Rinami/Tsubame in council texture  
**Primary settings:** student-council training, culture-festival planning, interview assignment, Shibuya Sound Fest  
**Conflict:** Sena's year-long producer project reaches the point where the juniors can act independently; Sena then tries to convert one of her signature songs into a one-way inheritance object, and her own idols reject the premise  
**Institutional function:** culmination of Sena's quasi-producer/student-council successor project

## 5.1 The opening deliberately juxtaposes “finished product” and messy beginning

Event 024 begins at Shibuya Sound Fest with Sena addressing Ume and Misuzu as idols she has spent a year cultivating. ## 5.2 Culture-festival planning shows juniors producing without Sena

Sena explicitly changes her usual behavior during culture-festival planning. Earlier Sena would have taken charge. Instead she assigns Ume and Misuzu to develop the student-council project themselves.

Misuzu's response is a notable state marker. She says the student-council members are now **important people** to her. Helping them is therefore something she wants to do, even while she apologizes for still lacking spontaneous initiative.

Ume and Misuzu initially drown in collected suggestions because they have solicited too many incompatible preferences. Their solution is not to ask Sena to rescue them. They identify a higher-order criterion—an experience that produces a one-day-only special memory—then search old culture-festival records for reusable structure.

They find an old immersive mystery-play concept and adapt it. When the possibility of recruiting someone more technically skilled arises, they explicitly choose to finish the project together.

This is a clean progression from Phase-4 scaffolded authorship toward **independent co-authorship**.

**STRENGTHEN — Ume:** energetic creativity is now paired with archival learning and concept selection; she does not require every problem to be solved through immediate action.

**STRENGTHEN — Misuzu:** when relationally invested, she can perform tedious source review and design labor without ceasing to describe effort as effort.

## 5.3 The interview assignment shows belonging through critique

An interview request about the student council produces three different junior self-presentations:

- Ume writes heavily about Saki;
- China centers people she is grateful to;
- Misuzu writes about the senior council members so candidly that Ume and China fear Tsubame will become furious.

Tsubame does indeed lecture them, but Sena judges the answers as heartfelt and recognizably individual. She thanks Tsubame for performing the disciplinary role Sena knows she often softens.

The scene clarifies the senior governance pair:

- Sena protects growth and accepts expressive individuality;
- Tsubame polices standards and consequences;
- both know that the other function is necessary.

Sena then says Ume, Misuzu, and China have each shown excellent growth in different forms and calls them the school's hope. Her willingness to graduate is grounded not in finding miniature Senas but in observing **differentiated competence**.

## 5.4 `ENDLESS DANCE`: Sena initially imagines succession as relinquishment

The most important scene is the Shibuya Sound Fest setlist discussion.

Sena assigns `ENDLESS DANCE`—the song associated with her Prima Stella victory—to Ume and Misuzu and says she will not sing it herself.

Her reasoning is institutional:

- the song was written for Hatsuboshi idols, not Sena alone;
- after Sena's victory, others stopped using it;
- Sena's prestige effectively monopolized a school asset;
- if she graduates with that monopoly intact, the song risks becoming a monument rather than a living part of Hatsuboshi performance culture.

She therefore wants Ume and Misuzu to **take it back from her**.

This is generous, but the juniors immediately identify the hidden assumption: Sena believes inheritance requires her withdrawal.

They refuse.

Misuzu says asking the two of them to overwrite Sena underestimates her own idols. Ume says restraint is unlike Sena and insists she sing too. Their challenge is not to replace Sena's `ENDLESS DANCE` but to meet it while she is still present.

The succession model becomes:

`originator performs + juniors answer -> plural ownership`.

That is more generative than:

`originator withdraws -> juniors inherit empty slot`.

## 5.5 Ume and Misuzu de-monopolize the song in different ways

After the live, Sena finds many new performance videos of `ENDLESS DANCE`.

She attributes two different effects to her juniors:

- Ume's visibly joyful performance broke the “curse” that made other people afraid to touch the song;
- Misuzu's singing showed high-level performers a new legitimate way to approach it.

The distinction matters. Cultural transmission needs both **permission** and **possibility**.

Ume makes participation feel allowed. Misuzu expands the technical/aesthetic option space.

Temari then contacts Sena wanting to include the song in a future set, demonstrating direct circulation beyond the event trio.

This is one of the strongest pieces of evidence in the corpus for a mature institutional understanding of idol tradition: a song becomes more valuable as more people can inhabit it without erasing prior versions.

## 5.6 Sena learns that letting go is not the same as making room

Sena initially calls the event a succession ritual. After seeing the song flourish, she admits that she no longer wants to let it go.

Misuzu and Ume are unsurprised. Ume says an idol cannot endure refusing to sing a song she loves.

Sena then says something crucial about the producer project: she thought she was guiding them, but has learned far more from them than she expected.

This is not false modesty. Across Events 005, 013, 024, and 025, producer Sena repeatedly encounters limits to one-way authority:

- people resist being legible as successors;
- juniors produce outcomes she could not calculate;
- the act of teaching reveals her own immaturity;
- legacy survives better through co-presence than self-removal.

**STRENGTHEN — Sena:** generative supremacy matures from successor-selection toward **circulatory legacy design**.

### Event 024 thesis

> **A legacy becomes common property when the originator remains available to be answered, not when the originator disappears.**

`ENDLESS DANCE` returns to Hatsuboshi because Ume and Misuzu perform with Sena, against Sena, and beyond Sena at the same time.

- Tsubame sees a system that depends heavily on senior correction.

The event does not pretend that “trust the next generation” means ignoring poor execution.

This is a major institutional state promotion and should be routed as such.

Her leadership style is already recognizable from the Phase-3 model. She does not claim to have a finished plan. She states responsibility publicly, then admits to Ume and Misuzu that she has no concrete idea and asks them to think with her.

This is not failed authority. It is **receptive authority** in institutional form:

The important point is that the junior council does not answer a process problem with promises to “work harder.” It changes the process.

This directly echoes the Phase-4 correction theme:

**STRENGTHEN — Ume:** Ume's practical intelligence includes transferring self-management tools into organizational design.

China proposes a party funded with money the juniors earned as idols and centered on food they prepare themselves.

When Tsubame asks whether Misuzu will actually help other people's work, Misuzu answers that supporting a president she trusts does not feel like work; it is closer to a hobby.

This does not mean she has acquired a general work ethic. It provides a sharper causal rule:

That is consistent with her friendship labor in Event 014 and gift logic in Event 023. It should not be generalized to all duties.

The key maturation is not “we no longer need the seniors.” It is:

The criteria are explicit:

Professionalization is not disenchantment.

The implication is strong:

This makes the cover event structurally parallel to Event 024. In both cases, an older song becomes alive when successors stop treating the predecessor's version as a closed answer.

**STRENGTHEN — Ume:** reciprocal propulsion includes an increasingly autonomous self-address loop.

The live succeeds. Temari tells them it was good and, after Ume starts imagining more cover songs with more members, says `私たちの番` may indeed come.

That line completes the event's lineage model.

The trio's success is not only that they performed the song well. It is that the first assignment made a **next assignment imaginable for others**.

This is the same circulatory legacy grammar seen in `ENDLESS DANCE`:

`receive -> interpret -> perform -> widen access`.

# 8. Event 029 — Asari-sensei as idol: role reversal under a continuity gate

**Sources:** `adv_event_029_main-01` through `main-04`  
**Participants:** Asari-sensei, Producer; Saki, Temari, Kotone; unnamed Hatsuboshi teacher  
**Primary setting:** branch-specific production agreement, auditions, and live

## 8.1 The branch reverses the normal pedagogy but preserves its ethics

The Producer is delighted to finally produce Asari. Asari repeatedly argues that the assignment makes no institutional sense:

- Hatsuboshi contains many appealing idol candidates;
- the Producer's grades depend on the success of the assigned idol;
- her normal job is to teach him how to produce idol students.

The Producer answers not with abstract novelty but with confidence that **Asari specifically can become a good idol**.

The role reversal is comic, yet the underlying pedagogical relationship remains intact. Asari evaluates his producer behavior even while becoming its target. When he declares he will make her an idol, she says the stance is good producer mentality.

This is an institutional Möbius strip:

`teacher evaluates student as producer -> student produces teacher as idol -> teacher continues evaluating student's production`.

The branch therefore explores whether teaching and being produced are mutually exclusive roles. Its answer is no.

## 8.2 The Producer's enthusiasm crosses consultation boundaries

The Producer distributes a special announcement publicizing Asari's idol debut and enters her into an audition before fully consulting her.

Asari is correctly angry about the unilateral action.

This should not be romanticized as merely decisive production. The scene explicitly marks a boundary failure: the Producer's confidence and speed outrun the idol's informed participation.

Asari nonetheless continues because she believes the surrounding interest is real and because she does not want to approach the task half-heartedly.

**Branch-specific REVISE — Producer:** enthusiasm/initiative can become overreach when the producer treats belief in the idol as substitute authorization.

## 8.3 Asari's professionalism transfers immediately from teaching into idol labor

After passing the first audition, Asari says that once she participates she must do it seriously. Anything less would be disrespectful to idol-course students who work hard.

This is the central non-comedic character evidence in Event 029.

Her professional ethic is role-transferrable:

- she may think becoming an idol is absurd;
- she may be embarrassed;
- she may not have sought the opportunity;
- but once she occupies the role, she judges herself by the standards she teaches.

Saki and Temari immediately accept the role transition in their own way: if Asari is an idol in the audition, then she is a rival. Teacher respect no longer entitles her to competitive leniency.

Kotone restores ordinary politeness by dragging them away and apologizing.

This trio is revealing:

- Saki: challenge formalizes recognition;
- Temari: harsh equalization through professional standards;
- Kotone: social boundary repair.

## 8.4 Stage embarrassment reveals role visibility, not refusal of idolhood

After the second audition, Asari realizes how embarrassing it will be to perform as an idol in front of students and colleagues who know her primarily as a teacher.

The embarrassment resembles the role-authenticity issues seen with Mao and Sena, but with a different source. Asari is not afraid a private flaw will undermine an idol persona. She is afraid two institutionally separate role audiences will **collide**.

The Producer encourages her to offer fan service anyway and says both fans and producer are waiting for her.

Asari finally says she will sing for her fans and for him.

This is the point at which the branch moves from reluctant compliance toward chosen performance.

## 8.5 The ending preserves both roles

After the live, Saki and Temari immediately renew their competitive challenge; Kotone simply praises the performance; a colleague says he will support Asari both as fellow teacher and as fan.

Asari herself says:

- the process was sudden and absurd;
- being supported made her happy;
- she actually had substantial fun;
- the production was good;
- she may be willing to continue a little longer.

She ends by addressing the student as `わたしの――プロデューサーくん`.

The branch therefore does not conclude with “teacher becomes idol instead.” It preserves dual role identity.

### Event 029 thesis

> **Role reversal can expand identity without invalidating the role that made the reversal possible.**

Within this branch, Asari remains teacher precisely while learning what it feels like to be the idol produced by her own student.

- **Asari — STRENGTHEN within Event-029 possibility-space:** professionalism transfers across roles; embarrassment does not prevent serious commitment once she accepts a task.

# 9. Cross-event synthesis

## 9.1 Circulatory inheritance as the fourth Phase-4 social mechanism

The final tranche completes the social architecture that has emerged across all numbered events.

### Mechanism 1 — relation creates productive conditions

Events 001–005 showed that development is socially coauthored. Class 1-1's competitive federation, Class 1-2's affiliative mutual aid, Saki/Lilja pedagogy, and Sena's successor project all established that characters do not develop as isolated optimization problems.

### Mechanism 2 — relation corrects error

Events 006–012 showed that effort can be sincere and still badly aimed. Rinami's recipient-centered planning, Temari/China task specialization, Saki's rejection of inefficient suffering, Hiro's acceptance of collaborative help, and Mao's role negotiation all made other people into error-correction systems.

### Mechanism 3 — relation preserves continuity through change

Events 013–020 showed that identity can change without discontinuity because other people remember earlier versions, private versions, and contradictory versions of the same person. The witness prevents growth from requiring self-erasure.

### Mechanism 4 — relation makes legacy transmissible

Events 021+ show what happens once development has already occurred: the result must somehow survive graduation, changing roles, new performers, and new generations.

The answer is **circulation**.

- Tsubame carries childhood Sena into adult rivalry.
- Kotone carries first-day Mao into reciprocal present care.
- Hiro helps Temari carry childhood ritual into repaired friendship.
- Ume and Misuzu carry `ENDLESS DANCE` beyond Sena without removing Sena from it.
- China/Ume/Misuzu carry senior governance forward through different work systems.
- Saki/Ume/Kotone carry predecessor idol culture forward by making a new cover rather than copying an old one.
- Event 029 imagines pedagogy circulating back from student to teacher.

The governing Phase-4 formulation can therefore be stated as:

> **Gakumas models development as socially coauthored, socially corrected, relationally remembered, and institutionally recirculated.**

## 9.2 Succession and replacement must remain analytically separate

The word “successor” is dangerous if it implies that one person vacates a slot and another becomes the new copy.

This tranche repeatedly rejects that structure.

### Sena and Tsubame

Tsubame does not become “the next Sena.” Her adult answer to Sena is valuable precisely because she has a different developmental problem: she must recover belief that the star is reachable. Sena privately envies Tsubame's “wings,” establishing that comparison runs in both directions.

### Sena and the student-council juniors

Sena's success as producer is not that Ume, Misuzu, or China become miniatures of her. Their different competencies are what allow them to correct her.

### `ENDLESS DANCE`

Sena's original handoff plan treats succession as replacement: she stops singing; juniors inherit. The juniors reject it. The song returns to the institution when Sena remains one legitimate performer among an expanding set.

### Student council

China becomes next president, but Event 025 immediately distinguishes her leadership grammar from Sena's. China admits uncertainty, gathers input, and organizes a shared answer. Continuity lies in responsibility to Hatsuboshi, not imitation of Sena's personality.

### 765 cover lineage

Saki, Ume, and Kotone explicitly decide that respect requires finding their own `GO MY WAY!!`. The successful successor is not the one who disappears into predecessor style. It is the one who can make the inherited work speak again.

This distinction should govern later specialist synthesis:

> **Succession = continuity of responsibility or possibility. Replacement = substitution of identity. Gakumas strongly favors the former and repeatedly resists the latter.**

## 9.3 Public/private integration reaches a mature formulation

Events 017, 021, and 022 together make a particularly strong role-authenticity cluster.

### Sena

Tsubame knows Sena is not literally perfect. That knowledge does not expose the public idol as fraudulent. It gives Tsubame privileged evidence of the discipline required to create that performance.

### Mao

Kotone sees fevered dependency, old CM history, sweetness preferences, and fear of Sena. None of these invalidates princehood. They enlarge the range of material Mao can potentially allow princehood to refract.

### Asari, branch-gated

Event 029 literalizes audience collision: colleagues and students know Asari as teacher while watching her perform as idol. The result is embarrassment, not identity collapse. A colleague can support “teacher Asari” and “idol Asari” simultaneously.

The combined rule is now strong:

> **Authenticity in Gakumas is usually continuity of agency across roles, not the absence of roles.**

A performed identity becomes suspect when it prevents the character from acting, receiving care, or acknowledging desire—not merely because it differs from private behavior.

## 9.4 Care becomes durable when it can reverse direction

The final tranche contains multiple reversals of care:

- Rinami forces Sena/Tsubame to rest after years of serving under their leadership.
- Kotone cares for Mao after Mao once helped her on entrance day.
- Hiro helps Temari repair Misuzu, while Misuzu quietly helps Hiro help Temari.
- Sena's produced juniors teach Sena how legacy can work.
- juniors who were repeatedly corrected by seniors build a party to care for the seniors and a system meant to reduce future correction burden.
- the student Producer in Event 029 applies lessons learned from Asari back onto Asari herself.

The reversal is not bookkeeping. The original care is not repaid like debt. Instead, the relation becomes capable of **changing direction without changing identity**.

That should be treated as a maturity marker in later reconstruction models. A character who can only give care but cannot receive it remains less flexible than a character whose relational role can reverse without shame or collapse.

## 9.5 Institutional memory is carried by people, artifacts, and procedures

Phase 4 has now exposed three forms of institutional memory.

### People

Sena, Tsubame, Rinami, Mao, and the headmaster carry knowledge of older Hatsuboshi norms and idol examples. Juniors learn through proximity to those witnesses.

### Artifacts

- old culture-festival records in Event 024;
- Sena's live recordings and photographs in Event 021;
- old performance discs in Event 026;
- songs such as `ENDLESS DANCE` and `GO MY WAY!!`;
- Temari's one-copy birthday CD as micro-scale relational archive.

### Procedures

- student-council handoff;
- schedule-management systems;
- delegation and disciplinary roles;
- production methods;
- representative selection and cover-live preparation.

This matters because a mature institution cannot depend solely on charismatic memory. Event 025 especially shows the juniors converting tacit senior oversight into explicit workflow tooling.

## 9.6 Phase 4 complicates “self-reliance” as a developmental ideal

Across all four tranches, characters become more autonomous while also becoming more relationally entangled.

Sena can produce juniors and still need Tsubame/Rinami. Tsubame can renew rivalry precisely because childhood knowledge remains available. Mao can face Sena because Kotone accompanies her. Temari can author a gift because Hiro helps her think. The junior council becomes independent by preserving senior knowledge in new systems. Ume's self-address in Event 026 grows out of a song, a sister, peers, and predecessor idols.

The mature endpoint is therefore not independence in the sense of **needing no one**.

It is:

> **the ability to choose dependence, receive correction, preserve agency, reciprocate care, and carry useful relation forward.**

This formulation should constrain later character-simulation work. “More mature” versions of these characters should not automatically be written as less emotionally dependent, less relationally specific, or more uniformly self-contained.
