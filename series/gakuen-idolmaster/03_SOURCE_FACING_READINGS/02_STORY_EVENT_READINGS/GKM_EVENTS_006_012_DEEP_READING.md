# GKM EVENTS 006–012 DEEP READING

## 0. Scope, method, and principal finding

This is the second canonical Phase-4 numbered-event reading for the Gakuen Idolmaster V2 corpus. It follows `GKM_EVENTS_001_005_DEEP_READING.md` and preserves the same source-facing purpose: **stress-test the thirteen Phase-3 character models inside multi-character situations where friendship, work, clients, fans, school authority, and peer pedagogy can force behavior that character-centered routes do not necessarily expose.**

The source boundary is the dedicated `event_006-012.dialogue.txt` bundle: **35 unique source scripts / 2,409 messages**, exactly five scripts each for Events 006 through 012. Unlike the previous tranche, there are no extra event-system introduction scripts embedded in this bundle.

The A2 dialogue layer is the primary reading surface, while every finding retains its A1 raw-script locator. No uninspected facial expression, vocal performance, animation, camera movement, or rendered staging is promoted into a fact. Performance-sensitive discriminations are routed to the audiovisual queue.

### Principal tranche thesis

Events 006–012 repeatedly ask a deceptively simple question:

> **What happens when wanting something badly and working hard are not enough?**

The answer is not that effort is worthless. The answer is that **effort must acquire direction, recipient, technique, and social correction**.

Across the tranche:

- Ume and China possess abundant enthusiasm and imagination, but Rinami teaches them to orient an event toward an audience rather than toward their own brainstorm.
- Temari possesses elite song ability and professional seriousness, but China teaches her to deploy the strength she actually has instead of forcing herself through a social task she performs badly.
- Kotone possesses service-work competence and social intelligence, but Saki and Temari must stop her from converting competence into unilateral overwork.
- Lilja possesses extraordinary persistence, but Saki explicitly rejects effort as an `免罪符` when the training itself is inefficient.
- Hiro loves the painful process of making chocolate from cacao beans, but eventually decides that a difficult failure is inadequate when the purpose is to delight friends; she asks Rinami for help.
- Mao can satisfy a client by taking the cute-role assignment, but Temari and China help create a form in which professional responsiveness does not require suppressing Mao's own desire to play the prince.
- Sumika can hide fear behind brightness and avoidance, but Saki forces the hidden problem into a form that can be worked on rather than lied around.

The tranche therefore strengthens the institutional thesis that Hatsuboshi is a developmental ecology, but adds a sharper formulation:

> **Gakumas repeatedly treats other people as error-correction systems for aspiration.**

Peers do not merely encourage one another. They identify bad methods, translate strengths across contexts, supply missing skills, constrain dangerous excess, disclose hidden needs, and sometimes force another person to distinguish *trying hard* from *moving effectively*.

This is not a celebration of friction for its own sake. The best interventions preserve the other person's authorship. Rinami does not replace Ume's ideas; Kotone does not speak for Lilja once Lilja can speak herself; Saki does not become Sumika or Lilja's goal; Mao does not erase Temari and China's inexperience. The developmental ideal is **scaffolded self-authorship** rather than rescue or solitary grit.

# 2. Event 006 — student-council event design: creativity requires a recipient

**Sources:** `adv_event_006_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Ume, China, Rinami, Hiro; Sena as assigning authority  
**Primary setting:** student-council planning project using a hot spring on the Kuramoto family's property  
**Conflict:** Ume and China can generate exuberant ideas but initially lack a usable method for deciding which ideas serve other people  
**Institutional function:** student council as experiential production/event-design laboratory

## 2.1 Sena delegates rather than solves

Sena opens by assigning Ume responsibility for proposing a student-involving council event. China is placed on the team and Rinami is assigned as adviser. Sena explicitly removes their ordinary work so they can concentrate on the project.

This is useful institutional evidence for two reasons.

First, the student council is not merely ceremonial governance. It is allowed to **design programs that shape student experience**. Second, Sena's developmental style here is assignment-based: she creates a bounded responsibility, gives the juniors room to fail, and supplies a senior support layer rather than delivering the answer.

Ume's immediate problem is revealing. She can imagine endless training camps, but cannot spontaneously answer the broader question “what should the student council create?” Her strength is abundant motion inside a known objective; open-ended production requires a different cognitive tool.

## 2.2 China's privilege becomes usable infrastructure

Ume accidentally wanders onto a Kuramoto family property and discovers a small hot spring. China identifies it as belonging to her family's villa, then immediately offers it for the council event, subject to asking her grandfather.

The scene strengthens the Phase-3 **accountable inheritance** model without prematurely resolving its ethical tension.

China does not pretend that the resource is ordinary or self-earned. She identifies ownership, seeks family permission, and tries to convert inherited material capacity into a shared school benefit. At the same time, the event is only possible because one student can casually mobilize private land, a hot spring, household staff, food, massage expertise, and renovation capacity.

**STRENGTHEN:** China's privilege is productive infrastructure she can attempt to steward rather than merely consume.

**OPEN:** responsible use by one benevolent heir does not answer the distributive question of why this capacity is privately available in the first place.

## 2.3 Ume and China: divergent creativity without constraint

Their first ideas are spectacularly impractical:

- convert the bath into a 25-meter swimming-pool hot spring;
- turn the outflow into a hot-water waterfall;
- conduct contests, endurance games, treasure hunts, even class observation in the bath.

The important point is not that they are foolish. The ideas reveal **high divergent creativity with weak recipient filtering**.

Ume's instinct is to maximize activity. China's instinct is to mobilize resources immediately. Their combination is generative but potentially dangerous: Ume proposes construction; China says it can begin tomorrow.

Rinami therefore becomes the necessary third function.

## 2.4 Rinami teaches a production method, not an answer

Rinami's intervention is one of the clearest low-stakes examples of her Phase-3 mature care model.

She teaches a sequence:

1. decide **who** the event is for;
2. decide **how you want them to feel**;
3. then decide what action can produce that result.

She uses a concrete accessibility question—what happens if someone who cannot swim enters a deep pool—to force the team away from self-referential excitement.

Crucially, she does **not** conclude that Ume and China should stop ideating. When Ume becomes discouraged and suggests Rinami might as well design everything, Rinami refuses the substitution. Knowing a method does not mean she can generate their ideas. Their wild proposals create excitement she cannot produce herself.

Her formulation is effectively:

> **You generate; I constrain when the idea outruns its purpose.**

That is scaffolded authorship rather than maternal takeover.

**STRENGTHEN:** Rinami's care includes process design, boundary setting, and epistemic humility.

**STRENGTHEN:** soft delivery does not mean low authority; she can stop an entire project direction without appropriating ownership of the project.

## 2.5 Hiro supplies the missing user perspective

After prolonged brainstorming, Ume and China ask Hiro what she would want to do at a hot spring.

Hiro answers: **nothing**.

Her reasoning is embodied and ordinary. Hot baths promote circulation but are tiring; therefore she wants to be healed and left alone.

This apparently anti-event answer solves the design problem. Ume and China realize that their own happiest experience at the villa involved almost no programmed “activity”: bathing, resting, massage, sweets, tea, and being cared for.

The final concept becomes an extreme hospitality event in which participants should need to do almost nothing themselves.

Hiro's contribution matters because it demonstrates a recurring ensemble principle: **a weakness or preference that looks unproductive from the organizer's perspective can become valuable design information from the user's perspective.**

Her low-energy body is not transformed into a superior theory. It simply lets her articulate a need that the high-energy planners failed to imagine.

## 2.6 China discovers service as labor

The final event succeeds, but only through heavy work. China uses household staff and personally participates in hosting. Ume spends the day moving and giving massages. Rinami helps operate the event. China admits exhaustion while insisting that visitors' enjoyment makes the effort worthwhile.

Hiro's praise—she is glad she came—hits China especially hard. The person whose “do nothing” preference unlocked the project now validates the result.

The event therefore converts inherited hospitality from a background lifestyle into **deliberate labor for others**.

### Event 006 thesis

> **Creative energy becomes production only when it is answerable to a recipient.**

Ume provides motion, China provides resources and hospitality, Rinami provides recipient-centered method, and Hiro provides the decisive user perspective. None of the four functions is sufficient alone.

### Claim transitions

- **Ume — STRENGTHEN / REVISE:** raw propulsion includes genuine creative abundance, but open-ended planning benefits from explicit cognitive scaffolding; “direct” should not be modeled as “incapable of abstraction.”
- **China — STRENGTHEN:** inherited resources can be converted into accountable shared service; willingness to work personally alongside those resources matters.
- **Rinami — STRENGTHEN:** mature care is scaffolded self-authorship—teach the recipient-oriented method, constrain excess, preserve the junior's authorship.
- **Hiro — STRENGTHEN:** bodily limitation and low-energy preference can generate socially valuable perspective without being romanticized as hidden superiority.
- **Sena — STRENGTHEN:** generative authority often works through bounded delegation and the deliberate provision of differentiated support.

### AV requests

**P1:** `adv_event_006_main-03` — Ume's discouragement and Rinami's “that's what I'm here for” response. The balance among reassurance, professional confidence, and genuine admiration of Ume/China's creativity is performance-sensitive.

**P1:** `adv_event_006_main-04` — Hiro's deadpan “I want to do nothing” intervention and the moment the team recognizes it as the design answer.

# 3. Event 007 — Halloween work: the strongest member is not strong at everything

**Sources:** `adv_event_007_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** China, Temari, Hiro; Ume cameo; Misuzu in setup  
**Primary setting:** amusement-park Halloween promotional work and live  
**Conflict:** three idols with radically different competencies must perform public-facing labor before a live; Temari's technical superiority does not translate automatically into hospitality or social ease  
**Institutional function:** external paid/promotional work as a training environment for differentiated strengths

## 3.1 China enters work with realistic fear and serious intention

China is delighted to receive an idol job but immediately becomes nervous when she sees the crowd and unfamiliar peers. Her internal monologue distinguishes excitement from social confidence.

That matters because the event does not write her hospitality competence as effortless extroversion. She must act through fear.

Temari's first interaction is harsh. She calls China a `ぽんこつ`, notes her bottom-of-class ranking, and warns her not to become a burden. Hiro then arrives, having applied because she heard China would be there and was worried about her.

China jokingly names the trio the `ぽんこつグループ`; Temari refuses inclusion.

The joke becomes the event's structural irony.

## 3.2 Temari's professionalism is real even when her social competence is poor

Temari did not originally seek the job. Kotone was scheduled, became ill, and asked Temari to substitute at short notice. Temari complains, but she came.

She then states a governing rule:

> even as a substitute, she will do idol work seriously because that is her pride.

This is important evidence against reading Temari's sharpness as mere hostility. The standards are role-based. She is demanding because she believes idol labor warrants seriousness.

China answers the standard rather than rejecting it: she admits weak idol ability but promises full effort on the job. Temari says she will believe that promise.

## 3.3 China succeeds where Temari fails

China quickly discovers that greeting children, playing the Halloween role, and inviting people to the live suit her. Temari then tries to demonstrate “the idol example” and promptly frightens children.

The failure is not caused by bad intent. Temari's internal response shows embarrassment and confusion; she genuinely did not mean to scare anyone. Hiro and China identify the problem as **pressure**, not costume.

China's advice is excellent precisely because it does not ask Temari to become China:

> stop forcing the task you are bad at; use what you are already good at.

She names Temari's song.

Temari sings; people gather; China and Hiro handle the distributed social work. The trio becomes effective by **specializing rather than homogenizing**.

This is a direct ensemble correction to any model in which “strong idol” means globally superior performer.

## 3.4 The dream-origin exchange collapses false distance

Before the live, China admits fear of the stage and explains why she became an idol. She saw a wonderful idol live, wanted to shine like that, entered Hatsuboshi, realized how naive her initial understanding had been, and now feels responsible for the dream she chose.

Her line about taking **responsibility for the dream she had** is a compact formulation of accountable inheritance at the level of aspiration. A desire may begin lightly; continuing to claim it creates obligations.

Temari initially refuses to explain her own origin, then gives almost the same answer: she too saw a wonderful idol live and wanted to become one.

China had imagined Temari as categorically distant—a technically gifted person with whom she shared nothing. The shared origin reveals that technical inequality does not imply motivational alienness.

Temari then tells China that China has many things in which she is the superior one.

The event has already demonstrated what she means.

## 3.5 Blackout: vulnerability reverses the support direction

The blackout exposes a private Temari the public cannot see. Her internal monologue is terrified. She grabs China's hand, then rationalizes the action as safety.

Children begin crying. China proposes giving away the collected sweets because “idol work is making everyone smile.” Temari initially objects on professional-procedural grounds: they are on the job and should not act without authorization.

China's principle persuades her.

Hiro supplies a homemade 3000-lumen jack-o'-lantern, China distributes sweets, and Temari sings because her unamplified voice can reach the crowd.

Again, the trio works because each person contributes a different kind of capacity.

## 3.6 “You can drag me down. I will pull you.”

Before the final live, China worries that performing beside Temari will reduce Temari's reception. She suggests Temari would be better off singing alone.

Temari's answer is one of the strongest Phase-4 revisions in the corpus:

> `……引っぱっていい。`  
> `足、引っぱってもいいって言ってるの。`  
> `私が引っぱってあげるから、ついてきなさい！`

This does not mean Temari abandons standards. Her reason is that China has **worked hard for this live**.

At Event 001, Temari's default rule was “do not drag me down.” Event 007 gives the more mature conditional rule:

> **A person who is sincerely doing the work may be worth carrying until she can contribute at a higher level.**

That is a major distinction. Temari does not become indiscriminately gentle. She becomes more capable of recognizing effort as evidence of commitment and of treating strength as a responsibility toward a weaker teammate rather than merely a reason to exclude her.

### Event 007 thesis

> **Competence becomes relational when the strongest person stops demanding that everyone possess the same strength.**

China teaches Temari how to use song inside public service. Hiro supplies practical improvisation. Temari supplies musical force and eventually turns that force into support rather than gatekeeping.

### Claim transitions

- **China — STRENGTHEN:** accurate awareness of weakness coexists with genuine social talent; hospitality and audience reading are real idol competencies, not consolation prizes.
- **China — STRENGTHEN:** `立派なアイドル` increasingly means answering for a chosen dream, not merely improving parameters.
- **Temari — PROMOTE / REVISE:** professional severity is conditional rather than purely exclusionary. Serious effort can convert “don't burden me” into “I will pull you.”
- **Temari — STRENGTHEN:** public coolness and private fear coexist; fear does not prevent action but often leaks first through body/attachment before syntax.
- **Hiro — STRENGTHEN:** friendship motivates entry into ordinary paid idol work; she brings technical improvisation and accepts team obligation despite low stamina.
- **China ↔ Temari — PROMOTE:** relationship moves from intimidation and unequal judgment toward explicit mutual support and competence recognition.

### AV requests

**P0:** `adv_event_007_main-03` — Temari's failed Halloween greeting, shame, and China's transition from fear to advising her. Comic timing may conceal or intensify vulnerability.

**P0:** `adv_event_007_main-05` — blackout fear, hand-holding, China persuading Temari to sing, and especially `引っぱっていい / 私が引っぱってあげる`. Major performed relationship-state evidence.

**P1:** `adv_event_007_main-04` — China and Temari's shared idol-origin conversation and Temari's `あなたが勝ってるところだって、たくさんあるよ`.

# 4. Event 008 — Animate work: accidental authenticity becomes public capital

**Sources:** `adv_event_008_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Saki, Temari, Kotone; customers and store staff  
**Primary setting:** one-day Animate promotional assignment followed by a live  
**Conflict:** the trio wants to outperform Sena's prior-day draw while their unscripted personalities, mistakes, and internal friction become the thing that attracts an audience  
**Institutional function:** public-facing work, social-media virality, and conversion of incidental attention into performance audience

## 4.1 Three different concepts of “doing the job”

The opening establishes three professional instincts.

Saki treats the job as a competitive acquisition problem: yesterday Sena drew an overflow crowd, so today's goal should be to exceed her.

Temari treats it as a route to the stage. She is anxious about customer interaction but confident that singing and dancing are what matter most.

Kotone treats it as service work first. They are guests of the store; unusual behavior can impose costs on the client; therefore they should first perform the assigned task correctly.

None of these instincts is wholly wrong. The event's success comes from combining them:

- Saki supplies ambition and appetite for opportunity;
- Temari supplies musical credibility and an existing fan connection;
- Kotone supplies operational judgment and customer-service literacy.

## 4.2 Temari meets a SyngUp! fan

A customer recognizes Temari from SyngUp! and says she is glad Temari continued being an idol.

Temari's internal and external states diverge sharply. She is pleased and affected, but her spoken response threatens to terminate the interaction. Kotone and Saki intervene, help invite the fan to the afternoon live, and push Temari to say she would be happy if the fan came.

Temari then becomes possessive when Kotone jokes the fan might convert to Kotone.

This scene is a compact rehearsal of the later “gap” public image. The attractive contradiction is not fabricated from nothing: **the cool exterior, visible awkwardness, competitive possessiveness, and intense desire for recognition already coexist in ordinary interaction.**

**STRENGTHEN:** constructed public persona can be an edited amplification of recurring social truth rather than a false mask.

## 4.3 The public wants the mess they did not plan to show

Saki authorizes a customer to post video of the trio's banter. Kotone worries about reputational risk; Temari sides with Saki because exceeding Sena requires taking chances.

The uploaded clips go viral for precisely the wrong reasons from the trio's perspective:

- Temari nearly drives away her own fan and blushes while insisting she only panicked slightly;
- Saki proposes calling a television station;
- the trio's argument looks like comedy;
- Saki reveals her bizarre glowing `SSD` stamina drink.

People begin arriving to see whether the behavior is real.

The result is analytically important. The public is not attracted only to polished idol perfection. **Uncontrolled personality leakage becomes attention.** The idols' task is then to decide whether they can convert that attention into a legitimate stage encounter.

This should not be simplified into “authenticity always wins.” The audience response is contingent and partly mocking. The professional skill lies in converting accidental exposure into an address the performers can own.

## 4.4 Kotone turns labor history into operational competence

As the crowd grows, Kotone does what neither Saki nor Temari thinks to do: she asks store staff for an appropriate space, redirects traffic, protects merchandise, and begins carrying much of the crowd-management load.

Her employment history is therefore not merely tragic backstory. It has produced **real situational competence**.

But the same competence creates a familiar risk. Kotone assumes that because she knows how to manage the situation, she must keep managing it. She worries that if she rests, Saki and Temari will lose the crowd.

Saki notices her fatigue. Temari and Saki force her to sit. They enlist the audience itself to make rest socially possible.

This is a strong Phase-4 correction:

> **Kotone's ability to keep a system running is an asset; her belief that she must personally absorb every gap in the system is not.**

## 4.5 Saki and Temari learn to carry Kotone

Kotone finally explains that she is enduring because the three agreed to challenge Sena and because she is serious about becoming an idol.

Temari responds with exaggerated admiration—and immediately suggests Saki's `SSD` drink as a solution. The joke is abrasive, but the underlying movement is real: the two stronger-looking performers refuse to let Kotone's contribution remain invisible.

All three drink the terrible supplement together before the live. The comic ritual turns Saki's eccentric self-maintenance technology into shared group preparation.

More important is what happens after: Kotone thanks them, Saki offers a massage later, Temari worries whether they performed the store job properly, and the trio recognizes that the only thing they know for certain is that they worked as hard as they could with the situation they received.

### Event 008 thesis

> **Public image is partly authored and partly discovered under exposure. Professionalism consists in converting whatever becomes visible into a form the performer can answer for.**

The trio does not control why people arrive, but it can control what happens once they do.

### Claim transitions

- **Kotone — STRENGTHEN / REVISE:** service-work history is transferable professional capital; social intelligence includes operational escalation to staff, crowd management, and risk awareness. The same competence can trigger overfunctioning.
- **Temari — STRENGTHEN:** the cool/awkward “gap” exists before any deliberate branding; she wants fan connection but often cannot translate inner affect into social surface without scaffolding.
- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** competition pushes her toward opportunity capture and public experimentation; care remains bodily and practical when a teammate's limit becomes visible.
- **Class 1-1 trio — PROMOTE:** Saki, Temari, and Kotone can function as a high-friction but highly adaptive temporary work unit because their competencies are nonredundant.
- **Public-image model — STRENGTHEN:** audience attachment can form around error, contradiction, humor, and personality leakage rather than only perfected performance.

### AV requests

**P0:** `adv_event_008_main-02` — Temari's SyngUp! fan interaction, the internal/external affect gap, and Saki/Kotone's scaffolding.

**P1:** `adv_event_008_main-03` — viral-video reaction sequence; useful for distinguishing humiliation, comic anger, and competitive opportunity capture.

**P1:** `adv_event_008_main-04` and `main-05` — Kotone's fatigue, forced rest, confession of seriousness, and the trio's pre-live solidarity.

# 5. Event 009 — Christmas cake work: friendship as brokerage, not possession

**Sources:** `adv_event_009_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Kotone, Ume, Lilja; Sumika, Saki, China, Hiro, Misuzu, Temari in supporting roles  
**Primary setting:** paid Christmas-event cake-shop work  
**Conflict:** Kotone needs coworkers; Lilja wants both cultural participation and new friendship but struggles to enter an unfamiliar social triangle; Ume's enthusiasm overwhelms her until Kotone acts as bridge  
**Institutional function:** paid offstage labor as part of school event ecology

## 5.1 Kotone's first motive is money—and that is not the whole event

Kotone notices unusually good pay and immediately applies. When the shop owner says the event requires three workers, she aggressively recruits peers because she does not want to lose the income.

The text does not ask us to sanitize this motive. Money is real.

But once coworkers enter the situation, Kotone becomes responsible for their success. She worries whether Ume can handle cake-making, notices Lilja's silence, mediates social overload, trains both during the work, and ends the event pleased that they became closer.

This is a recurring Kotone structure:

> **commercial motive and relational care coexist without either cancelling the other.**

## 5.2 Sumika does not rescue Lilja from choosing

Lilja wants the job but repeatedly fails to get Kotone's attention. Once Ume fills one vacancy, Lilja becomes more anxious about joining a group containing someone she barely knows.

Sumika's response is unusually well calibrated. She does not push Lilja forward and does not tell her fear is irrational. She offers the real alternative: if the job is not worth this discomfort, Lilja can stop.

Lilja decides to chase Kotone again because she **does** want the experience.

Sumika says, effectively, that this is what she expected.

This strengthens the mature Sumika–Lilja relationship model. Sumika's support can consist of preserving an exit and waiting for Lilja to choose, not automatically pulling her through every threshold.

## 5.3 Lilja's Christmas desire is cultural, vocational, and relational at once

Lilja explains that Christmas in her home context is family-centered, while Japanese anime gave her an image of Christmas with friends. She wants to participate in the Hatsuboshi event.

But she is comfortable doing so from the back of the stage. If her cakes help create the event, that is meaningful enough.

She also wants to become closer to Kotone.

These motives matter together:

- cultural participation in a new environment;
- pleasure in baking;
- useful backstage contribution;
- deliberate expansion of friendship beyond Sumika.

The event therefore gives Lilja ordinary-life agency that is not reducible to “becoming braver on stage.” She is building a life in Japan.

## 5.4 Ume overwhelms; Kotone translates

Ume approaches Lilja with sincere enthusiasm and a barrage of technical questions because she wants to make Saki happy. Lilja overloads and leaves.

Ume immediately fears she has been disliked. Kotone correctly identifies the problem as **too much input, too fast**, not rejection.

Kotone then finds Lilja, identifies what she is struggling with, and gives her a specific relational permission:

> rely on me more; we're friends; I also want to know you better.

She does not permanently take over the conversation. She returns with Lilja and introduces one small teaching task at a time until Lilja can directly instruct Ume herself.

This is another excellent example of scaffolded self-authorship.

**STRENGTHEN:** Kotone's social intelligence is not omniscience—she literally forgot to recruit Lilja—but it includes rapid repair once another person's difficulty becomes visible.

**STRENGTHEN:** Lilja can expand her network through mediated entry and then convert the mediated relation into direct reciprocal friendship.

## 5.5 Ume's social world widens beyond Saki

Ume accepts the job partly because it sounds fun and partly because she wants to become closer to Kotone. She then becomes excited about friendship with Lilja, takes Lilja's baking instruction seriously, and agrees to bake together again later.

Saki's reaction is telling. She violates her own “cake is not idol food” rule because the cake is made by Ume, then feels complicated jealousy when she notices how close Ume and Lilja have become.

The scene is comic, but it confirms a structural fact: Ume's individuation involves acquiring relationships Saki cannot mediate or monopolize.

## 5.6 Sumika welcomes Lilja's expansion while joking about possession

When Sumika arrives, Lilja tells her that despite difficulty she became closer to Kotone and Ume and is glad she took the job.

Sumika's response is unambiguously pleased for her.

She later jokes to Kotone that she is not “giving” Lilja to her, prompting Kotone to call it possessiveness. The exchange is useful texture but should not be overpromoted. In this event, the operative behavior is **non-enclosure**: Sumika supported Lilja taking the risk and celebrates the new ties.

### Event 009 thesis

> **A close relationship becomes healthier when it can help someone acquire other relationships rather than becoming the condition that replaces them.**

Sumika lets Lilja choose. Kotone bridges Lilja into a new social configuration. Ume receives the new friendship directly. The result enlarges rather than threatens Lilja's relational world.

### Claim transitions

- **Kotone — STRENGTHEN:** commercial self-interest and genuine peer care are compatible; labor competence includes social brokerage and task decomposition.
- **Lilja — STRENGTHEN:** cultural adaptation is self-authored and pleasure-seeking as well as stressful; backstage contribution can satisfy her desire to participate.
- **Lilja — STRENGTHEN:** anxiety may require an entry scaffold, but once a relation becomes legible she can teach, joke, invite, and initiate future contact herself.
- **Ume — STRENGTHEN:** friendship-seeking is direct and expansive; individuation from Saki includes building peer relations on her own initiative.
- **Sumika — STRENGTHEN:** good support can mean offering an exit, refusing coercion, and letting Lilja expand beyond the dyad.
- **Saki — STRENGTHEN / OPEN:** sister attachment remains capable of mild jealousy even while she supports Ume's independent social life.

### AV requests

**P1:** `adv_event_009_main-04` — Lilja's overload, flight, Kotone's private intervention, and the return to direct Ume–Lilja teaching.

**P1:** `adv_event_009_main-05` — Saki's reaction to Ume/Lilja closeness and Sumika's response to Lilja's expanding friendships; useful for calibrating comic possessiveness versus genuine enclosure.

# 6. Event 010 — Valentine: Hiro learns that difficulty is not the recipient

**Sources:** `adv_event_010_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Hiro, Rinami, Sena; China, Ume, Misuzu, Temari, Kotone in framing/recipient roles  
**Primary setting:** Valentine gift-making and school Valentine festival  
**Conflict:** Hiro initially treats making a gift as another difficult technical problem; the social purpose of the gift eventually forces her to subordinate solitary process purity to recipient experience  
**Institutional function:** student-council festival; Sena's discretionary use of authority; friendship network made publicly performative

## 6.1 Hiro begins by maximizing theory

Hiro checks out—or attempts to check out—more than thirty confectionery books. After reading them, she combines every “best practice” she can find.

The result has no taste.

The joke is an unusually exact metaphor for a larger Gakumas problem: **optimization without a governing purpose can destroy the thing being optimized.**

Hiro has maximized theory but never asked what kind of chocolate her friends would actually enjoy.

Rinami supplies that missing question.

## 6.2 Rinami's method repeats Event 006 at the level of intimacy

Rinami asks Hiro to imagine the person receiving the gift:

- what would make that person happy?
- what taste, shape, size, color, wrapping, or ribbon would suit them?

This is the same production grammar she taught Ume and China in Event 006:

> **recipient first; method second.**

Rinami again offers help without insisting on control. Hiro says she wants to make the chocolate by herself because otherwise it would lack meaning. Rinami respects that choice.

Later, when Hiro asks Rinami to accompany her to buy cacao beans, Rinami goes. When Hiro says she still wants to make the chocolate alone, Rinami leaves the work to her. When Hiro finally asks for direct help, Rinami helps.

The sequence is one of the strongest examples in the corpus of **care that tracks consent dynamically**.

## 6.3 Sena gives Hiro the wrong lesson in the right direction

Sena agrees with Rinami that a gift is meaningful when it shows how much the giver thought about the recipient. She calls presents a form of affection.

Then she reveals what she is making: a highly detailed 3D model of Kotone derived from live footage, intended to become a 3D-printed Kotone chocolate.

The extremity of the example predictably captures Hiro's attention. Hiro concludes:

> affection means putting in labor.

This is not entirely wrong, but Hiro literalizes it. She decides that simply using chocolate is insufficient and seeks the “essence” of chocolate: cacao beans.

She considers Ghana, then Indonesia, before Rinami redirects her to a department store.

The comedy matters because it exposes a recurrent Hiro tendency: **a relational concept is first translated into a technically maximal challenge.**

## 6.4 The decisive correction: a fun failure is no longer enough

Hiro grinds cacao by hand until her palms are red and her arms nearly stop working. She describes the pain and difficulty as intensely fun.

The resulting chocolate is gritty and bad.

Early-Hiro logic could have made that outcome satisfactory: the process resisted her; therefore the process worked as hobby.

But this gift has a recipient.

Hiro says the process was fun, **but this is not good enough because the people she loves will not be happy with it**.

Then she does something Phase-3 Hiro could not be reduced to doing by a simple “loves difficulty” model:

> she asks Rinami to help.

The request is not surrender. It is a reordering of ends.

Difficulty remains enjoyable. But when difficulty conflicts with the value of the friend receiving the gift, **the friend takes priority over solitary purity**.

This is a major strengthening of `chosen impossibility becoming accountable attachment`.

## 6.5 Completion stops being empty

After they finish, Hiro notices something strange:

> usually, once she succeeds, she feels empty; this time, finishing was still fun.

That line is one of the most consequential ordinary-life findings in the Hiro corpus.

The old achievement cycle becomes unstable when the completed object remains connected to people outside the problem itself. The chocolate's value does not disappear when resistance disappears because its purpose survives in the friends who will receive it.

This offers a noncrisis answer to one of Phase 3's major open questions:

> **Can Hiro access durable value after the impossible task becomes possible?**

Event 010 cannot settle the entire question, but it provides a strong low-stakes affirmative mechanism: **relational afterlife**.

## 6.6 Friendship becomes a public gift

Hiro's intended recipients are China, Ume, and Misuzu—named internally as her `大切なお友達`.

Sena, grateful that Hiro helped improve the Kotone 3D model, gives Hiro a free stage slot so she can turn the chocolate delivery into a surprise live. Hiro explicitly asks whether this is abuse of authority. Sena replies that she can occasionally exercise presidential authority.

The final gift therefore becomes both private and performative: a song addressed specifically to friends, followed by handmade chocolate.

The recipients return friend chocolates of their own.

### Institutional caution

Sena's action is benevolent and narratively charming, but it is still evidence that **student-council discretion can be personalized**. This should be preserved as authority elasticity rather than inflated into a corruption thesis.

### Event 010 thesis

> **For Hiro, attachment creates an end that survives success.**

Pain remains pleasurable. Technical extremity remains attractive. But the friend receiving the result becomes a higher-order constraint capable of making Hiro accept collaboration and care about quality after the challenge itself is over.

### Claim transitions

- **Hiro — PROMOTE / STRENGTHEN:** “loves resistance” is further revised toward “loves resistance inside an expanding value structure.” Difficulty can be subordinated to recipient welfare without ceasing to be pleasurable.
- **Hiro — PROMOTE:** relational afterlife is a plausible noncrisis mechanism for sustaining value after completion; success need not produce emptiness when the finished act remains socially alive.
- **Hiro — STRENGTHEN:** China, Ume, and Misuzu form an explicitly named close-friend network.
- **Rinami — STRENGTHEN:** recipient-centered scaffolding generalizes from public event production to intimate gift-making; consent-sensitive help is a stable method.
- **Sena — STRENGTHEN / OPEN:** affection is expressed through spectacular labor and institutional facilitation; her willingness to personalize office power remains a bounded authority-risk signal.
- **Misuzu ↔ Temari — STRENGTHEN:** middle-school Valentine exchange provides ordinary-history evidence that Temari's refusal language can coexist with private reciprocal care.

### AV requests

**P0:** `adv_event_010_main-04` — Hiro's painful cacao-making, failed result, request for Rinami's help, and `いつもは、できちゃったら、虚しくなるのに……楽しかった`. Major character-state evidence.

**P0:** `adv_event_010_main-05` — Hiro's friend-directed live and chocolate delivery; useful for calibrating intimacy in her normally compressed voice.

**P1:** `adv_event_010_main-03` — Sena's Kotone chocolate, Hiro's interpretation of `愛情`, and the transition from relational advice into technical extremity.

# 7. Event 011 — Hinamatsuri: role flexibility without self-erasure

**Sources:** `adv_event_011_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Mao, Temari, China  
**Primary setting:** local Hinamatsuri event with reporting, customer interaction, short play, and live  
**Conflict:** the trio must serve a client, absorb late changes, and perform roles outside their habitual competencies; Mao is specifically asked to play the cute princess role she would not have chosen for herself  
**Institutional function:** external client work as ensemble training; audience enjoyment as performance criterion

## 7.1 Mao selects teammates by nonredundant strength

Mao personally recommends China and Temari.

China is selected because Mao believes her presence naturally makes people smile. Temari independently agrees and cites the Halloween job as evidence.

Temari is selected because Mao wants her voice to leave an impression on an audience that may know none of them.

The team is therefore assembled through **complementarity** rather than generic rank:

- China: hospitality and social atmosphere;
- Temari: musical force;
- Mao: senior coordination and theatrical experience.

China is startled that her “making people smile” capacity counts as a real talent. Temari's agreement matters because it comes from someone who once called her a bottom-ranked `ぽんこつ`.

This is a direct Event 007 → Event 011 development marker.

## 7.2 China and Temari now promise mutual rescue

Before the event, Temari admits that public customer work still makes her anxious. China says she is not actually confident either, despite people calling her good at it.

China promises to support Temari with whatever social gift she has. Temari promises that when China inevitably enters difficulty, she will help in return.

The relationship has therefore moved beyond “strong person carries weak person.” Each recognizes a domain in which the other may become the stronger actor.

That is a much more mature reciprocity model.

## 7.3 Mao accepts the client role—but acceptance is not the end of the question

The organizers suddenly add a ten-minute Hinamatsuri play. Mao's child-actor background makes her the natural teacher.

Then the client assigns Mao the `お雛様` role because of her past television work.

Mao has almost always played boys. She admits privately that being cast as the cute girl is a new experience. Temari offers to trade, but Mao initially refuses because responding to what the client asks is part of professional work.

This is exactly where a crude “authenticity means refuse the cute role” reading would fail.

Mao is capable of playing the princess without that performance invalidating the prince. The ethical issue is whether professional responsiveness becomes **exclusive category assignment**.

## 7.4 Temari sees the desire Mao is suppressing

During rehearsal, Mao's investment in the prince role becomes obvious. She knows the male-role movement grammar, gives Temari detailed instruction, and admits that she had hoped to perform that part when she first heard about theater work.

Temari does not tell Mao to reject the princess role. She says she wants to see Mao as the prince too because Mao's desire and expertise are real.

China finds the solution: **reverse the roles between morning and afternoon performances**.

The proposal is analytically perfect for Mao's Phase-3 thesis. The answer is not replacement but **refractive multiplication**:

- Mao can be princess and prince;
- Temari can be prince and princess;
- client demand and performer desire can both survive;
- the role switch itself becomes audience value.

Mao's authenticity is therefore not located in one gendered role. It is located in her ability to **choose, inhabit, revise, and connect roles without being imprisoned by any one of them**.

## 7.5 China becomes an operational coauthor

China is not merely the smiling junior in Mao's plan. She notices that both performers know enough of the opposite part, proposes the reversal, reasons that organizers gave them flexibility, predicts publicity value, and volunteers to negotiate approval.

This is a meaningful ensemble strengthening of receptive authority. China can take received constraints, identify latent degrees of freedom, and reorganize the system around a better outcome.

## 7.6 Theater is made by everyone

After the performance, China worries about mistakes. Mao rejects mistake count as the governing metric. The audience looked happy; therefore the performance succeeded.

Mao then says theater is made by everyone. She had to cover some junior mistakes, but Temari and China's fresh energy also supported her and reminded her of the beginning of her own acting life.

Later she names the origin explicitly:

> making the people watching the stage enjoy themselves.

She then distinguishes past and present without repudiation. She is not an actor now; the place she wants to shine is the idol stage they are about to enter.

This is a crucial anti-binary finding:

> **Mao does not become an idol by rejecting acting. She carries acting's audience ethic and role craft into a new chosen center of gravity.**

### Event 011 thesis

> **Professional flexibility becomes self-authored when external demand can be answered without making the demanded role the only role the performer is allowed to have.**

The role reversal is not a rebellion against the client. It is a negotiated enlargement of the work.

### Claim transitions

- **Mao — PROMOTE / STRENGTHEN:** refractive role authorship is confirmed in ordinary external work; princess and prince can both be real performances without a true-self/fake-role hierarchy.
- **Mao — STRENGTHEN:** child-actor craft survives as usable professional inheritance; audience pleasure remains an origin ethic even after idolhood becomes the chosen present vocation.
- **Temari — STRENGTHEN:** she can identify another person's suppressed desire and advocate for it; acting aptitude expands her possible professional repertoire without threatening singer identity.
- **China — PROMOTE:** receptive authority includes operational redesign and negotiation, not merely gracious reception; she can discover flexibility inside constraints and mobilize others around it.
- **China ↔ Temari — PROMOTE:** reciprocal support is now explicit and domain-sensitive.
- **Mao ↔ juniors — STRENGTHEN:** seniority functions best as transferable craft plus room for junior contribution, not one-way rescue.

### AV requests

**P0:** `adv_event_011_main-04` — Mao's admitted desire for the prince role, Temari's advocacy, China proposing the role reversal, and Mao accepting. Major role-authorship evidence.

**P1:** `adv_event_011_main-03` — Temari's fan interaction and China stepping in; useful comparison with Event 008.

**P1:** `adv_event_011_main-05` — Mao's `演劇は、みんなで作るもの` and statement of her audience-oriented acting origin before the idol live.

# 8. Event 012 — effort is not an absolution: Saki, Lilja, and Sumika build a reciprocal training system

**Sources:** `adv_event_012_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Saki, Lilja, Sumika; Temari, Kotone, Ume, trainer in reconstructed earlier states  
**Primary setting:** current temporary-unit preparation for a regional-promotion mini-live, interwoven with reconstruction of early first-year training crises  
**Conflict:** Lilja equates relentless effort with the right to remain beside Sumika; Sumika hides a dance-related fear/problem behind avoidance; Saki separately identifies both bad loops and turns them into structured work  
**Institutional function:** peer pedagogy as error correction when formal training alone does not solve individualized problems

## 8.1 Present Saki distinguishes leadership responsibility from friendship

At the current rehearsal, Lilja wants to continue after the others are exhausted because she still has things she cannot do.

Saki stops her and frames rest as professional responsibility. If Lilja injures herself or becomes sick, the consequences extend to organizers, school, collaborators, and fans.

Then she makes an important distinction:

> as unit leader, Lilja's collapse would be **Saki's responsibility** rather than a personal inconvenience; as a friend, Saki would simply be worried.

This is one of the clearest formulations in the tranche of **role-differentiated care**. Professional obligation and personal affection overlap but are not interchangeable.

## 8.2 The event retrospectively rewrites Class 1-1's early hierarchy

Sumika and Lilja remember early Saki as overwhelming: highly trained before admission, rapidly surpassing internal-progression students, and almost entirely focused on practice.

They also say that this was productive. Having the person at the front work harder than anyone else gave the rest of the class a reason to keep moving.

The retrospective correction is precise:

- Saki's early lack of cooperation was real;
- her example still produced external motivation;
- later she learned to make the same competitive energy directly useful to other people.

Saki answers that the effect is reciprocal: their effort now gives **her** strength too.

The top of the hierarchy has become more circulatory.

## 8.3 Lilja's early crisis: effort as `免罪符`

The reconstructed early Lilja material is among the strongest evidence in her corpus.

Lilja sees Sumika begin with far superior dance ability. Lilja fails a training test while everyone else passes. Then Sumika begins skipping practice and hiding the reason.

Lilja concludes that she has no right even to worry about Sumika until she catches up. The only thing she can do is keep running.

Her internal formulation is devastating:

> **continuing to run is the `免罪符` that lets her stand beside Sumika.**

Effort has stopped being merely instrumental. It has become moral permission to occupy the relationship.

Temari inadvertently reinforces the logic by saying that when people give up, others cannot do much; all one can do is continue running oneself.

Then Saki intervenes.

## 8.4 Saki attacks effort without attacking aspiration

Saki tells Lilja that her training is painfully inefficient and orders her to become Saki's disciple.

Lilja worries she would be a burden. Saki answers with a deliberately harsh distinction:

> if Lilja only wants the absolution of being able to say “I worked hard,” the offer is withdrawn; if she actually wants to become an idol and catch Sumika, she should take the available chance.

This is a core Phase-4 statement of Gakumas's effort ethic.

The text does **not** condemn extreme persistence. It condemns persistence detached from outcome, method, and feedback.

Saki offers optimization because she takes Lilja's desire seriously enough to judge the method.

Lilja accepts Saki as `師匠`.

**STRENGTHEN:** Saki's competitive rationality has become transmissible pedagogy.

**REVISE:** Lilja's courage should not be defined as “keeps trying no matter what.” Mature courage includes permitting another person to change how she tries.

## 8.5 Sumika's avoidance is not lack of desire

The event reconstructs Sumika's side of the same period.

At first she is highly capable, motivated, and visibly ahead. Then she experiences a growing bodily/psychological disturbance in dance: technical mistakes, fear, nausea, collapse, inability to continue intense movement. The event calls this a `症状` and later treats it as something being gradually overcome. It does not itself supply a medical diagnosis.

Sumika hides the problem because she feels obligated to fulfill the promise to become an idol with Lilja. She begins skipping training and lying about why.

Her avoidance is therefore not evidence that the dream meant nothing. It is evidence that the dream had become frightening enough to produce concealment.

This is exactly the kind of situation in which a surface behavioral reading would fail.

## 8.6 Saki notices the missing causal bridge

Saki says Sumika had looked like a compelling rival: excellent body, dance at or above Saki's level, high motivation. Then the motivation apparently vanished without a visible cause.

She has seen people give up before. What bothers her is that the usual causal sequence is missing.

She confronts Sumika's “liar's eyes” and demands the whole story.

Later Sumika says she is glad Saki did so because otherwise she might still be refusing to look at the problem.

This is an important strengthening of Saki's epistemic style. Competition trained her to notice **deviation from an opponent's expected behavior**. Mature care then lets her use that anomaly detection for another person's welfare.

## 8.7 One system, two opposite errors

Saki eventually trains all three together because Lilja and Sumika have opposite failure modes:

- Lilja responds to fear by **overworking without sufficient method**;
- Sumika responds to fear by **withdrawing and concealing the problem**.

Saki's solution differs accordingly.

For Lilja: optimize, structure, stop unsafe excess, create intermediate challenges such as competing with Ume.

For Sumika: expose the hidden constraint, consult formal trainers, choose lower-intensity choreography, and create repeated experiences of tolerable success.

This is a sophisticated example of differentiated pedagogy. “Work harder” would worsen both situations in different ways.

## 8.8 Saki deliberately chooses a performance Sumika can survive

In the present work state, Sumika notes that the mini-live song is not dance-intensive and that she is gradually becoming able to perform such material again.

Lilja realizes Saki chose the song.

Saki calls it professional necessity: the unit cannot afford Sumika dropping out.

That explanation is true and incomplete. Lilja recognizes a stable behavioral fact: Saki repeatedly makes choices that let Sumika continue.

The scene reinforces the distinction introduced at the opening. Saki does not need to choose between professional responsibility and friendship. The same action can answer both under different descriptions.

## 8.9 Lilja and Sumika finally make support reciprocal

Saki tells Lilja that Sumika will ultimately need **Lilja's** help to stand fully again.

Later Saki tells Sumika that Lilja also needs someone who can keep watching and supporting her. Saki cannot do it forever.

The event therefore refuses a one-way rescue model in both directions.

On the night before the live, Lilja and Sumika explicitly bargain for reciprocity.

Sumika says her true self is weak, cowardly, and small, but she wants to stand beside Lilja. She asks Lilja to support her.

Lilja agrees on one condition: Sumika must support **her** too—must pull the hand of the girl who only knows how to keep trying.

This is an exceptionally strong precursor to the later REVERSI formulation of **synchronized differentiation**.

Their bond is not mature because they have become equally strong in the same way. It is mature because each can name a weakness the other is being asked to help govern.

## 8.10 Sumika's “no more lies” needs precise interpretation

After Lilja falls ill from overexertion, Saki tells Sumika that Lilja needs someone beside her. Sumika doubts she can be that person while still unable to dance properly.

Saki challenges her to keep fighting. Sumika responds that she will no longer lie: she distinguishes her old concealment from the kind of self-construction she associates with Saki.

This must **not** be used to flatten every Sumika performance into a true-self/fake-mask binary.

The event is specifically rejecting **avoidance lies that prevent action on a known problem**. Later Sumika's authored ideal-self performance can still be genuine because it is used to fight, disclose, and create rather than to hide the existence of the wound from the people who need to know.

## 8.11 The unnamed resemblance remains OPEN

Ume asks Saki why she has become so invested in Lilja and Sumika. Saki says they seemed “a little similar” to someone, then refuses to specify whom.

It is tempting to map the pair directly onto Saki/Ume or another relationship. The event does not resolve the referent.

**OPEN:** preserve the resemblance as an intentional prompt; do not hard-code an identity without another source.

### Event 012 thesis

> **Effort becomes courage only when it is allowed to change form.**

Lilja must stop using exhaustion as moral proof. Sumika must stop using withdrawal as protection. Saki's value is not that she works harder than both; it is that she can diagnose two opposite error loops and build conditions under which each girl can continue without pretending her problem is the same as the other's.

### Claim transitions

- **Saki — PROMOTE:** competitive optimization functions as peer pedagogy; anomaly detection learned through rivalry can become care.
- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** professional responsibility and personal affection are explicitly differentiated rather than collapsed.
- **Lilja — PROMOTE / REVISE:** courage is not indiscriminate persistence. She explicitly used effort as relational absolution; maturation requires accepting method, rest, and reciprocal support.
- **Sumika — PROMOTE / STRENGTHEN:** apparent unseriousness can conceal high-stakes fear and continuing desire; recovery requires disclosure without abandoning self-authored brightness.
- **Lilja ↔ Sumika — PROMOTE:** reciprocal support is explicitly contractualized in the relationship; neither rescuer/rescued nor unilateral follower/leader is adequate.
- **Saki ↔ Lilja / Saki ↔ Sumika — PROMOTE:** Saki becomes a major peer-development node rather than only rival/classmate.

### AV requests

**P0:** `adv_event_012_main-02` — Lilja's `免罪符` monologue and Saki's disciple challenge. Major effort/courage evidence.

**P0:** `adv_event_012_main-03` — Sumika's symptom/avoidance reconstruction and Saki's `嘘つきの目` confrontation ending in `だって、わたしたちは――友達だもの！`.

**P0:** `adv_event_012_main-05` — Lilja/Sumika mutual-support bargain before the live. Major Phase-6 relationship evidence.

**P1:** `adv_event_012_main-04` — three-person training, Sumika's `もう、嘘はつかない`, Lilja falling ill, and Saki asking Sumika to become a sustaining partner.

# 10. Relationship-state deltas

## 10.1 Ume ↔ China

Event 006 promotes them from friendly peers/student-council recruits into **working collaborators**. Both are high-energy idea generators, but China contributes inherited resources and hospitality while Ume contributes divergent motion and willingness to test. Their similarity produces momentum; it also makes a third-party constraint function necessary.

Event 010 later shows easy Valentine exchange, supporting ordinary-life continuity of the friendship.

## 10.2 Ume + China ↔ Rinami

Rinami occupies an adviser relation that is neither peer equality nor command. Her characteristic function is **recipient orientation plus boundary control**. She tells them how to think, not what final idea to choose.

This is a reusable low-stakes reconstruction rule for Rinami around juniors.

## 10.3 China ↔ Hiro

Event 006 and Event 007 strengthen the relation in opposite directions. Hiro supplies the key design insight in Event 006 and validates China's hospitality. In Event 007 Hiro applies for work because she is worried about China and explicitly calls her a friend.

The relationship therefore combines affectionate surveillance, deadpan teasing, and practical contribution.

## 10.4 China ↔ Temari

This is the strongest relationship development in the tranche.

Event 007 begins with China frightened of Temari's pressure and Temari calling her a `ぽんこつ`. China then identifies Temari's social weakness, redirects her into song, discovers their shared idol origin, supports her through the blackout, and receives Temari's promise to pull her through the live.

Event 011 explicitly remembers that history. Temari now calls China's social gift a talent, China proactively offers to support Temari in customer interaction, and Temari promises to rescue China when her own limits appear.

**PROMOTE:** domain-sensitive reciprocal friendship.

## 10.5 Saki ↔ Temari ↔ Kotone

Event 008 demonstrates a temporary trio capable of high-friction adaptation.

- Kotone supplies service grammar and risk control.
- Saki supplies opportunity capture and competitive goal setting.
- Temari supplies musical authority and existing fan capital.

When Kotone overworks, Saki and Temari become the regulators. The social system is therefore capable of reversing who is “the capable one” depending on task.

## 10.6 Kotone ↔ Lilja

Event 009 promotes a direct friendship edge. Lilja explicitly wants to know Kotone better. Kotone forgets her initially but repairs the miss, gives relational permission to rely on her, and scaffolds Lilja into successful direct interaction with Ume.

The relation should not be reduced to “Kotone socially rescues shy girl.” Lilja chooses the job and later initiates future contact herself.

## 10.7 Lilja ↔ Ume

Event 009 converts light acquaintance into direct friendship through collaborative work. Ume's intensity initially overwhelms Lilja, but after Kotone decomposes the interaction, Lilja becomes teacher and future baking partner.

## 10.8 Lilja ↔ Sumika

# 11. Institutional findings

## 11.1 Student council as experiential production school

Event 006 shows the council assigning juniors an actual event-design problem, freeing time, attaching an adviser, permitting proposal development, and evaluating the result.

This resembles production education even though it is not Producer Course jurisdiction. The council can therefore function as a **secondary laboratory for planning, delegation, audience design, and resource coordination**.

## 11.2 External work is broader than stage performance

Events 007–012 repeatedly place idols in non-stage tasks:

- amusement-park greeting and ticket distribution;
- retail promotion and crowd handling;
- cake production and sales;
- local-event reporting and theatrical performance;
- regional-promotion temporary-unit work.

The franchise's professional model is therefore broader than “sing/dance well.” Idols must interface with clients, customers, logistics, public risk, service norms, and other workers.

This strengthens the argument that Kotone's work history, China's hospitality, Mao's acting craft, and Rinami's social-process skill are not peripheral to idol professionalism.

## 11.3 Publicity is partly uncontrollable

Event 008 gives a strong model of vernacular publicity. A bystander video—not official campaign planning—creates the crowd. The clips become popular because the idols look funny, awkward, or strange rather than because they perfectly execute a brand plan.

Institutional implication:

> public-facing idol labor creates exposure whose interpretation cannot be fully centralized.

The professional response is adaptive conversion, not total control.

## 11.4 Private wealth can enter school production

Event 006 demonstrates how easily China can turn Kuramoto property and household labor into student-council infrastructure.

The event portrays the result positively and China does substantial personal work. Still, the source should be retained in the institution ledger because it makes visible a material asymmetry that the narrative's ethics of “responsible use” do not erase.

## 11.5 Audience pleasure is repeatedly used as an outcome criterion

Rinami asks how participants should feel. China defines idol work as making people smile during the blackout. Mao tells China that audience enjoyment matters more than counting mistakes. Lilja wants her cake to support others' Christmas experience.

This does not mean popularity is the sole standard. It means **recipient experience is repeatedly treated as evidence that production succeeded**.

## 11.6 Client demand and performer authorship are negotiable

Event 011 is useful because the client is not demonized for requesting Mao as princess. The negotiated solution—role reversal between performances—creates more value for the client and more authorship for Mao.

The institution/work ecology is therefore capable of **constraint negotiation** rather than forcing a simple choose-between-obedience-and-selfhood binary.

## 11.7 Student-council authority remains elastic

Event 010 shows Sena arranging a stage slot as personal thanks and openly joking about using presidential authority.

This should be added to the existing authority-risk file established in Event 005. The action is low-stakes and benevolent, but it confirms that the council presidency provides discretionary resources that Sena can personalize.

# 12. Thematic findings

## 12.1 Effort requires a governing purpose

This is the tranche's central theme.

- Ume/China brainstorm hard without an audience model.
- Hiro studies and labors maximally without first asking what friends want.
- Lilja works until effort itself becomes moral permission.
- Sumika once tries to force through fear until she breaks, then avoids the problem entirely.
- Kotone keeps working because she knows how, even after fatigue makes rest necessary.

The stories do not oppose effort. They repeatedly **rescue effort from self-referentiality**.

## 12.2 Care is strongest when it preserves authorship

Rinami is the clearest repeated example:

- teaches Ume/China how to think rather than supplying the event;
- offers Hiro help, accepts refusal, returns when asked.

Kotone does the same with Lilja: she scaffolds the conversation until Lilja can speak and teach directly.

Saki does it more aggressively: she redesigns methods and demands disclosure, but the end remains Lilja/Sumika's chosen aspiration rather than Saki's replacement goal.

## 12.3 Strength is domain-specific

Event 007 is almost didactic on this point.

Temari is the strongest singer and initially assumes this justifies hierarchy. In hospitality she is the weakest member. China can do what Temari cannot. Hiro can solve a technical lighting problem neither can solve.

Event 008 repeats the model: Kotone dominates service logistics, Saki opportunity capture, Temari song.

The implication is not “everyone is equally good.” It is that **global social rank is a poor map of task-specific value**.

## 12.4 Failure is useful only if it is allowed to change method

Hiro's bad chocolate, Ume/China's absurd brainstorms, Temari's frightened children, Lilja's training failures, Sumika's collapse, and China's reporting mistakes all matter because someone turns them into information.

Failure fetish without revision would be empty. The tranche repeatedly demands **feedback uptake**.

## 12.5 Accidental exposure can become authored identity

Event 008 provides the strongest case. The public sees Temari's awkwardness, Saki's absurd supplement, and the trio's argument before the trio chooses those materials as public image.

Once exposed, the idols can either reject the interpretation or integrate it into performance. Authorship therefore sometimes begins **after** unwanted visibility rather than before it.

## 12.6 Gifts and performances are both recipient-oriented production

Event 006 event design, Event 010 chocolate, Event 011 theater, and Event 012 temporary-unit work share a production grammar:

> who is this for, what should it do for them, and what part of ourselves can produce that effect?

This is why gift-making is not analytically trivial in Event 010. It makes the same production ethics intimate.

## 12.7 Role flexibility is not identitylessness

Mao in Event 011 is the clearest case. Being able to play princess and prince does not mean roles are arbitrary. The prince role carries history and desire; the princess role carries professional challenge and new possibility.

Flexible authorship requires **having preferences without being reducible to them**.

## 12.8 Mutual dependence can increase differentiation

Lilja and Sumika's Event 012 bargain is not merger. Each names a different weakness and asks for a different kind of support. Their closeness becomes more mature precisely because their functions are not interchangeable.

This strongly prepares the later REVERSI teammate/rival structure.

# 13. Japanese voice/register findings

These are textual-register findings. Performance claims remain pending where queued.

## 13.1 Ume: propulsion turns into explicit learning language

Ume's base register remains direct, exclamatory, and action-first: `やったぁっ！`, `聞いて聞いて！`, rapid idea chaining, immediate commitment.

Event 006 adds a useful second state: after Rinami supplies method, Ume can explicitly reflect on why her ideas were failing and then re-enter high-energy ideation with a new constraint. Her spontaneity is therefore **teachable without becoming formalized into Rinami's voice**.

## 13.2 China: `わたくし / ですわ` carries fear, comedy, work, and authority

The register remains stable across panic, customer greeting, self-deprecation, negotiation, and leadership.

Particularly important is her appropriation of `ぽんこつ`. Temari uses it as hierarchy; China turns `ぽんこつグループ` into affiliation. Later Temari can reuse `ぽんこつ` teasingly inside a relation that now contains mutual support.

The word's social force therefore changes with relationship state even when lexical content does not.

## 13.3 Rinami: questions are a governance tool

Rinami rarely needs blunt imperatives. Her recurrent move is to ask a question that changes the problem representation:

- who is this for?
- how should they feel?
- what kind of chocolate would this person want?

Her soft register is therefore analytically active. **Questions are how she exerts structure while leaving the answer owned by someone else.**

## 13.4 Temari: internal affect remains warmer and more frightened than public syntax

Event 007 and Event 008 repeatedly split inner and outer language.

Outward Temari is short, evaluative, and often rude. Inward Temari is delighted by praise, terrified of darkness, embarrassed by social failure, and grateful for care.

The key mature change is not that the blunt syntax disappears. It acquires new relational actions:

- `私が引っぱってあげるから、ついてきなさい`
- `ぜったい、私が助けるから`

Care arrives as command.

## 13.5 Kotone: register mobility is operational competence

Kotone's inner voice remains blunt and money-conscious; customer-facing voice becomes highly polished and cute; peer voice can be abrasive, teasing, diagnostic, or reassuring within seconds.

Events 008 and 009 show that this is not merely performance flair. She uses register shifts to **manage crowds, calm errors, apologize, recruit, teach, and create interpersonal exits**.

## 13.6 Saki: imperative language becomes pedagogy

Saki's commands are pervasive:

- rest;
- take the chance;
- become my disciple;
- follow me;
- stop hiding the problem.

The same imperative form can be competitive, managerial, or caring. Event 012 is especially important because the command is paired with individualized reasoning. The grammar remains forceful while the social purpose becomes more responsive.

## 13.7 Hiro: technical literalization gradually acquires relational vocabulary

Hiro compresses abstractions into almost equation-like statements: `愛情とは――手間をかけるということ`.

Her humor often comes from taking a relational metaphor into technical extremity. But the event also gives simple, unusually direct friend language: `大切なお友達` and `友チョコ`.

This is valuable for reconstruction: intimacy does not make Hiro verbose. It makes the referent more explicit.

## 13.8 Mao: senior/prince poise contains professional self-suppression and playful flexibility

Mao speaks to juniors through calm explanation and encouragement. With clients she accepts role demand formally. Once the role-swap possibility becomes legitimate, the same composed register can accommodate visible desire.

The prince voice is therefore not a rigid defensive shell in this state. It is one selectable mode within broader theatrical competence.

## 13.9 Lilja: hesitation survives even after desire becomes explicit

Lilja still uses pauses, apologies, and self-lowering phrases in unfamiliar social situations. What changes is the firmness of the claims nested inside them:

- she wants the job;
- she wants to become closer to Kotone;
- she wants Saki as teacher;
- she wants Sumika beside her;
- she asks Sumika to support her in return.

Courage does not require fluency.

## 13.10 Sumika: casual brightness can both reveal and conceal

Sumika's light colloquial voice makes reassurance, teasing, and evasion sound superficially similar. Event 012 explicitly identifies a historical period in which the casual surface hid fear.

Later `もう、嘘はつかない` should therefore be heard as a claim about what the brightness will be used **for**, not necessarily a promise to stop performing strength.

## 13.11 Sena: polished authority can abruptly become fan-obsessive craft talk

Event 010 moves Sena from elegant generalization about love to intensely technical discussion of a Kotone 3D model. The register shift matters: fanhood and command are not separate personalities. Her institutional competence can be mobilized toward highly personal objects of attention.

## 13.12 Misuzu: ordinary affection remains soft and indirect

The Valentine recollection with Temari preserves Misuzu's gentle storytelling. The interesting register fact belongs partly to Temari: she rejects chocolate verbally, then secretly leaves her own in the shoe locker. Misuzu understands the contradiction without demanding verbal correction.

# 14. Contradictions and corrections to guard against

1. **Do not read Events 006–012 as one mechanically serialized calendar.** Use direct links and seasonal anchors only.
2. **Do not convert the tranche thesis into “effort is bad.”** The repeated correction is effort without direction, recipient, method, or feedback.
3. **Do not say Rinami solves Ume/China or Hiro's problems for them.** Her defining action is to preserve their authorship while supplying recipient-centered structure.
4. **Do not say China is secretly an elite performer because she succeeds socially.** Her technical weakness and social/hospitality strength coexist.

# 18. Phase-4 tranche conclusion

Events 006–012 make the ensemble project significantly more concrete.

The first tranche established **peer relation as production technology**. This tranche shows the operating mechanism of that technology:

> **people make one another's aspiration more accurate.**

They do so by supplying missing perspectives and constraints.

Rinami asks who the work is for. China tells Temari to stop imitating a social strength she does not possess and sing instead. Kotone notices that Lilja needs smaller social steps. Saki notices that Lilja's effort is inefficient and that Sumika's withdrawal has no visible causal explanation. Hiro discovers that a gift cannot be judged only by how interesting it was to make. Temari notices that Mao's professional compliance is hiding a real role desire. China finds a structural way to let both demands coexist.

This changes how several Phase-3 models should be carried forward.

The corpus increasingly rejects two simple developmental fantasies:

1. **the solitary-genius fantasy** — if desire is sincere enough, a person can solve herself;
2. **the rescuer fantasy** — if another person loves her enough, that person should solve her instead.

The more stable pattern is **distributed correction with retained authorship**.

That is especially consequential for the characters most vulnerable to excess:
