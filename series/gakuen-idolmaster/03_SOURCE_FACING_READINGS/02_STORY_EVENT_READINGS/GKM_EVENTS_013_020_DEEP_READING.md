# GKM EVENTS 013–020 DEEP READING

## 0. Scope, method, and principal finding

This is the third canonical Phase-4 numbered-event reading for the Gakuen Idolmaster V2 corpus. It follows `GKM_EVENTS_001_005_DEEP_READING.md` and `GKM_EVENTS_006_012_DEEP_READING.md` and preserves their source-facing function: test the thirteen Phase-3 character models in ensemble situations where peers, family, seniors, juniors, work, travel, publicity, class stakes, and ordinary life can expose behavior that character-centered routes do not necessarily reveal.

The source boundary is the dedicated `event_013-020.dialogue.txt` bundle: **40 unique source scripts / 2,570 messages**, exactly five scripts each for Events 013 through 020. There are no extra event-system introduction scripts inside this tranche.

The A2 dialogue layer is the primary reading surface. Every event retains A1 raw-script locators. No uninspected facial expression, animation, vocal nuance, camera movement, or rendered staging is promoted into a fact. Performance-sensitive discriminations are routed to the audiovisual verification queue.

### Principal tranche thesis

Events 001–005 established peer relation as a production technology. Events 006–012 sharpened that into peers as error-correction systems for aspiration. Events 013–020 add a third layer:

> **Peers and loved ones function as continuity-bearing witnesses.**

A character is repeatedly able to grow because another person remembers an earlier self, notices a hidden or ordinary self, or can hold several versions of the person together without declaring one of them false.

Across the tranche:

- Rinami remembers and regulates the violent social energy of the third-year class from its first day, while Sena later identifies Rinami precisely as someone who can perceive feelings that Sena and Tsubame miss.
- Mao remembers Sena's loneliness immediately after becoming Prima Stella and turns an old failure to answer her into a present vow to catch her.
- Tsubame recognizes Sena's unease before Sena explains it and refuses to let her replace personal rivalry with successor-management alone.
- Hiro discovers that difficulty changes meaning when failure burdens friends; Misuzu, in turn, permits friendship to motivate effort without pretending that effort has become her permanent preferred pace.
- Rinami's regional-live mentorship succeeds because China and Lilja cease to be merely recipients of senior guidance and become co-authors who can carry the senior through a crisis.
- Temari teaches Kotone's younger sister with language inherited almost verbatim from Rinha's earlier instruction, showing relation becoming portable behavioral memory.
- Saki has to accommodate Ume's widening social world; Kotone recognizes the same problem from the other side of sisterhood. The relationship survives because independence is not treated as abandonment.
- Rinami's publicity project turns the senior quartet's ordinary life into evidence that stage persona and daily self are not cleanly separable domains.
- Hiro approaches Mao and Sumika partly because she recognizes prior lives that resemble her own; reciprocal disclosure changes an initially one-sided act of observation into friendship.
- Saki, Temari, and Kotone discover that friendship can become operational trust: they can travel, sleep, work, improvise, and perform together without needing one member to dominate every domain.
- Lilja and Sumika explicitly imagine futures in which only one succeeds and reject those futures together. Their promise becomes not fusion but a jointly defended condition of differentiation: **both must get to become idols**.

The tranche therefore complicates any model in which maturity means self-sufficiency. The more mature characters become, the more they are capable of letting other people carry information about them across contexts. A durable idol self is not a sealed essence. It is a self that can remain recognizable while being remembered, corrected, teased, challenged, helped, seen off-stage, and re-encountered later.

# 2. Event 013 — the third-year quartet: rivalry as an answer to plateau

**Sources:** `adv_event_013_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Sena, Tsubame, Mao, Rinami; China/Misuzu in present-day student-council texture  
**Primary setting:** third-year Kyoto school trip with first-year flashback and prior-H.I.F. memory  
**Conflict:** the four seniors can enjoy a shared past only by confronting how their roles have diverged; Sena in particular believes she has reached the limit of idol growth  
**Institutional function:** connects class social formation, student-council selection, Prima Stella burden, mentorship, and H.I.F. rivalry

## 2.1 Rinami's social authority predates her senior persona

The first-day flashback is one of the strongest historical anchors for the senior quartet.

Mao arrives with child-actor experience and openly intends to become a top star. Tsubame immediately dismisses the incoming class and names Sena as her sole rival. Sena, already entrance-exam first, accepts challenges from the whole room. Mao refuses to disappear from the competitive field. The class atmosphere becomes openly combative.

Rinami, by contrast, introduces herself as an inexperienced student from Fukuoka. Her internal response is fear. She imagines idol school as sparkling and instead finds an atmosphere close to open conflict.

Yet she is the person who finally stops it.

Her sequence is revealing:

1. anxiety and failed soft mediation;
2. recognition that the conflict is no longer self-correcting;
3. sudden command: `いい加減にしなさーーーーいっ！`;
4. normative appeal not to harmony but to **the audience**—idols are supposed to make fans happy, and idols fighting cannot do that;
5. a calm `こほん。よろしい。` after the room yields.

The scene rejects a false binary between soft Rinami and authoritative Rinami. The authority is already present in first year; what changes later is the range and confidence with which she can deploy it.

Sena's later memory that Rinami became class leader and prevented class collapse is therefore not ornamental praise. It explains why Sena eventually recruits Rinami into student government.

### Claim transitions

- **Rinami — STRENGTHEN:** receptive softness and organized authority were co-present from the beginning. Later `receive → hold → offer → be answered → expand` is developmental refinement, not replacement of a passive original self.
- **Rinami — STRENGTHEN:** audience orientation is an early moral/professional criterion. She regulates peer conflict by asking what conduct does to the people idols are meant to serve.
- **Mao — STRENGTHEN:** aspiration to top-star status is old and sincere; later prince-role fixation cannot be reduced to post-puberty compensation.
- **Tsubame — STRENGTHEN:** exclusive Sena-rival fixation is explicitly early and socially aggressive.
- **Sena — STRENGTHEN:** public supremacy is already paired with an invitation to challenge, but early challenge is still vertical—everyone may attack the summit she assumes she occupies.

## 2.2 Rinami's student-council recruitment: emotional perception as institutional competence

The Event 013 flashback to Rinami's first-year live is equally important.

Rinami judges herself as out of sync with the other performers. Sena agrees that something did not fit but does not turn the problem into disqualification. Instead, she recruits Rinami to student government.

The stated reason is precise. Sena intends to become student-council president because she loves Hatsuboshi and believes top idols have an obligation to make the school better for juniors, including students who might otherwise give up on idolhood. But Sena also says she is only **acting as if she is perfect**. She cannot manage the institution alone. Tsubame is too similar to her in certain respects. Rinami, by contrast, is good at touching or perceiving people's feelings and may catch what the two stronger, harder seniors miss.

This is a major institutional correction to any merit model based only on visible performance parameters.

Rinami's early stage weakness and her social usefulness are simultaneously true. Sena recruits a weak idol because she identifies a form of competence the current hierarchy does not measure well.

### Claim transitions

- **Sena — STRENGTHEN:** generative supremacy includes the capacity to identify complementary competencies rather than simply reproduce herself.
- **Sena — REVISE:** “perfect-image discipline” should not be read as self-deception. Sena explicitly knows it is performed governance and knows where its blind spots are.
- **Rinami — STRENGTHEN:** care is institutionally legible competence. Her ability to perceive emotional residue becomes part of how Hatsuboshi is governed.
- **Institution — STRENGTHEN:** student government is developmental infrastructure, not merely ceremonial prestige; leadership selection can value relational perception alongside performance rank.

## 2.3 Mao's unanswered promise and Sena's loneliness at the summit

Mao's ghost story is not really a horror story. The frightening memory is Sena's earlier H.I.F. victory.

After Sena becomes Prima Stella, Mao finds her less joyful than expected. Sena says the title is gratifying but immediately reframes it as burden: becoming the star is easier than continuing to be the star everyone can admire. She asks Mao whether the person Mao once admired still shines. Mao answers that the person stopped being a star.

Sena then asks the question that Mao cannot answer:

> Who will reach into the light and keep chasing the star?

Mao later regrets not promising to catch her.

This makes Sena's plateau problem relational before it is technical. A summit without credible pursuers threatens to turn public admiration into isolation. The “perfect idol” can continue to illuminate everyone below while receiving no equal answer back.

### Claim transitions

- **Sena — STRENGTHEN:** plateau anxiety is bound to relational asymmetry. Her problem is not only “I cannot improve” but “no one can answer me from a position that forces improvement.”
- **Mao ↔ Sena — STRENGTHEN:** Mao's later rival/rescue language has an earlier wound: she once saw Sena's loneliness and failed to answer it.
- **Mao — STRENGTHEN:** princehood includes a real desire to answer another person's isolation, but Event 013 also shows how long Mao could remain too intimidated to act on that desire.

## 2.4 Tsubame reads Sena without requiring confession

In the souvenir scene, Sena is distracted by the work of producing first-year student-council idols. Tsubame immediately notices.

Her response is harsh but useful. She calls the producing project unlike Sena, warns that doing idol work while taking responsibility for others can become arrogance, and then asks the question Sena is avoiding: **what about Sena's own H.I.F.?**

Sena explains that she is producing juniors so new stars will remain after she leaves Hatsuboshi. She admits that her experience has transferred poorly and that she is repeatedly confronted by her own immaturity.

Tsubame accepts the successor work only after Sena promises full commitment to their own fight. Then she adds that Sena has more rivals than Tsubame alone.

This interaction is a clean instance of **heroic rivalry as anti-displacement**. Tsubame does not reject Sena's institutional vocation; she refuses to let it become a substitute for continuing to exist as a contestant herself.

## 2.5 The shrine: successor logic becomes rival logic

At the arts shrine, Sena wishes for her juniors to succeed at H.I.F. Mao and Rinami instead make declarations aimed at Sena. Mao vows to catch her. Rinami says she lacks Mao's confidence but still wants to confront Sena directly because Sena called her an `敵`/rival. Tsubame wishes that Sena will have grown overwhelmingly by the next H.I.F. because defeating a stagnant Sena would be meaningless.

Sena's inner monologue is the tranche's sharpest statement of her problem:

- she believes she can no longer grow as an idol;
- but if anything can make growth possible again,
- it may be an opponent who stands as an equal `敵`.

This is a major Phase-4 promotion.

> **The successor project and the rival project are not interchangeable.**

Successors answer institutional mortality: who shines after Sena leaves? Rivals answer developmental mortality: who can force Sena to become someone she has not already been?

### Event 013 thesis

> **A summit becomes developmental again when the people who have watched it longest stop accepting distance as the final form of admiration.**

Rinami regulates the group, Mao returns an old unanswered promise, Tsubame refuses succession as a substitute for combat, and Sena tentatively recognizes equality as the missing condition of further growth.

### Relationship promotions

- **Sena ↔ Rinami — PROMOTE:** institutional trust is rooted in Sena's explicit recognition of Rinami's emotional-perceptual competence and Rinami's long history of seeing Sena's less perfect states.
- **Sena ↔ Tsubame — STRENGTHEN:** childhood/long-term rivalry includes unusually accurate mood detection and the right to challenge Sena's self-displacement into mentorship.
- **Sena ↔ Mao — STRENGTHEN:** long asymmetry contains a specific unanswered plea to be pursued; Mao's current challenge is a delayed relational answer.
- **third-year quartet — PROMOTE:** their present mutual legibility is historically earned. Rinami is not simply “the nice one,” Mao not merely “the prince,” Tsubame not merely “the rival,” and Sena not merely “the leader”; each role has been renegotiated across years of shared observation.

### Japanese-register evidence

- Rinami moves from hesitant soft speech into imperative authority without changing identity.
- Mao uses first-name intimacy (`莉波`, `燕`, `星南`) in the present peer group.
- Tsubame's surname/direct style (`姫崎`, `有村`) and combative diction remain compatible with long familiarity.
- Sena's formal authority and teasing peer register coexist; the group does not require one fixed “private Sena” voice.

### AV requests

**P0:** `adv_event_013_main-01` — Rinami's first-year transition from frightened mediator to `いい加減にしなさーーーーいっ！` and calm post-command register.

**P0:** `adv_event_013_main-04` — post-H.I.F. Sena asking Mao whether she will keep chasing; discriminate loneliness, challenge, and performed composure.

**P0:** `adv_event_013_main-05` — Sena's internal plateau monologue and outward `叩き潰してあげるわ`; test whether renewed aggression is relief, excitement, performance, or all three.

# 3. Event 014 — the sports festival: difficulty changes when other people bear the cost

**Sources:** `adv_event_014_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Hiro, Misuzu, Ume, China; Saki, Temari, Lilja as Class 1-1 opponents  
**Primary setting:** nationally broadcast class sports festival  
**Conflict:** Hiro's severe physical weakness becomes a collective liability in a competition where classmates want the same reward  
**Institutional function:** turns physical limitation, class identity, broadcasting, professionalism, and peer obligation into one public test

## 3.1 The school makes weakness publicly consequential

The event rules matter. Participation is normally mandatory. The school chooses each student's event. The competition is nationally broadcast. Winning classes receive a class live.

The system therefore eliminates several protective strategies:

- Hiro cannot simply choose a difficult task that only affects herself.
- China cannot opt out because endurance is not her strength.
- Ume cannot guarantee a direct individual duel with Saki.
- Misuzu cannot quietly refuse without potentially harming the class.

The trainer explicitly warns Ume that overheard institutional information must not be casually spread: an idol must be able to keep confidential matters confidential. The sports festival is spectacle, but professional norms remain attached to it.

## 3.2 Hiro encounters a kind of suffering she does not enjoy

Hiro initially recognizes the event as exactly the sort of ordeal that would normally interest her. Yet the live reward changes the ethical structure. Her classmates are idols who want a rare chance to perform. Hiro knows she cannot reach their physical level by the event date and cannot avoid being a drag on the score.

Her solution is not self-punishment for its own sake. She separates what can and cannot be changed:

- practice her assigned event;
- train the class in efficient techniques, including using Saki as a model for rational tug-of-war form;
- rehearse the live;
- search for strategic ways Class 1-2 can compete.

The crucial admission comes to Misuzu. Hiro had always known that choosing an unusually weak body could someday impose costs on others. She thought she would simply accept that consequence when it came. Then she made friends.

> She can tolerate difficulty for herself. She cannot casually tolerate making friends lose because of her.

This is one of the strongest corrections to any “Hiro likes failure” model.

### Claim transitions

- **Hiro — STRENGTHEN:** resistance-seeking is recipient- and consequence-sensitive. Pain is not automatically pleasurable when its costs are externalized.
- **Hiro — REJECT:** “all hardship is a reward” fails here. She explicitly says this suffering is not the kind she likes.
- **Hiro — STRENGTHEN:** analytical intelligence becomes prosocial strategy rather than a way to escape bodily participation.

## 3.3 Ume's advice: shared defeat rather than rescue fantasy

Hiro asks Ume what happens when one's ability is genuinely insufficient.

Ume gives a strikingly unsentimental answer: sometimes there are very few choices; sometimes the team will suffer and lose. In that situation, **suffer together and be frustrated together**.

She uses sport logic: a player who did not score still has the right to celebrate a win; a player who scored brilliantly still has the right to grieve a loss.

For Hiro, who can rationalize herself out of ordinary emotional conventions, Ume intentionally refuses a comforting lie. Ume says she would phrase it differently for someone else, but Hiro is too smart for anything except the truth.

This is a strong example of Ume's relational intelligence being **recipient-specific rather than merely loud or motivational**.

### Claim transitions

- **Ume — STRENGTHEN:** reciprocal propulsion can include refusing false reassurance. She adapts motivational style to the recipient's epistemic needs.
- **Hiro ↔ Ume — STRENGTHEN:** Ume can occupy a rare role in which she gives Hiro emotionally consequential advice without needing to out-intellectualize her.

## 3.4 China performs morale as chosen professional labor

China is assigned endurance running, a terrible fit. Her response on the day is not denial. She knows she is likely to struggle and says that until the result is decided she will become an “idiot” who believes in victory and smiles because they are idols.

This is important for China's `ふにゃ／きり` governance. The bright public posture is neither spontaneous obliviousness nor pure falsification. It is a consciously chosen role performed for collective morale.

Saki later calls China the “boss” of Class 1-2 with genuine competitive respect. The joke works because China has become a moral center despite low physical rank.

## 3.5 Misuzu: friendship can motivate effort without converting her into an effort absolutist

Misuzu does not suddenly discover that sports festivals are wonderful. She repeatedly says they are troublesome. She trains only “a little.” Even at the decisive relay-like paired event she does not rewrite herself as someone who should have gone all-out from the start.

But Hiro reaches her.

When Hiro asks Misuzu to try, Misuzu accepts a bounded commitment. At the final obstacle segment, after Hiro has fallen badly behind, Misuzu picks Hiro up and runs while carrying her.

The point is not that Misuzu's “true self” is a hidden athlete waiting for friendship to unlock it. The event preserves her chosen economy of effort. What changes is **jurisdiction**: friendship gives this particular situation enough value that extraordinary exertion becomes worth choosing.

After losing, Hiro explicitly refuses to complain that Misuzu should have worked harder earlier. Misuzu says getting Hiro motivated was already enough to make Hiro the class MVP. Hiro answers that a permanently full-power Misuzu would not even feel like Misuzu.

This is mature differentiation: capability does not create an obligation to live permanently at maximum output.

## 3.6 Hiro learns shared disappointment

Before the final event Hiro says she has never been this afraid. The novelty should, by her old logic, be thrilling. It is not. She wants to win because the result belongs to other people too.

Class 1-2 loses.

The expected state exists: Hiro has failed in a public competition and still feels the uncomfortable consequences. But she is smiling. She cannot fully explain why. Misuzu gives the simplest relational answer: Hiro is smiling, so perhaps the loss can be borne.

Hiro then tells Misuzu directly:

> `あなたはわたしの……大切な友達。大好き。`

Misuzu reciprocates.

This is not merely friendship confirmation. It changes the structure of failure. Shared disappointment becomes emotionally different from isolated self-authored suffering.

### Event 014 thesis

> **Hiro's philosophy of difficulty changes when the cost of difficulty is no longer hers alone. Friendship does not remove suffering; it changes who has standing inside its meaning.**

### Relationship promotions

- **Hiro ↔ Misuzu — PROMOTE:** direct mutual `大切な友達` / `大好き` confirmation; Misuzu's exceptional effort and Hiro's refusal to demand a different Misuzu make the relation strongly reciprocal.
- **Class 1-2 collective — STRENGTHEN:** the Worst Three/affiliative culture becomes a class-wide performance ethic capable of facing public loss together.
- **Class 1-1 ↔ Class 1-2 — STRENGTHEN:** class rivalry is serious but bounded. Saki respects China; Temari recognizes Misuzu's threat; Lilja and Temari compete without dehumanization.

### Japanese-register evidence

- Hiro's minimal, low-affect grammar makes `大切な友達。大好き。` unusually high-information because it is not surrounded by elaborate emotional language.
- Misuzu retains polite `です／ます` even in intimate exchange; politeness is not evidence of emotional distance.
- China's `ですわ` register carries both panic and morale leadership.
- Ume's high-energy directness becomes unusually sober when giving Hiro sport-derived advice.

### AV requests

**P0:** `adv_event_014_main-03` — Hiro's confession that friends make imposed failure intolerable; Ume's “suffer together” advice.

**P0:** `adv_event_014_main-05` — Hiro's pre-race fear, Misuzu carrying Hiro, post-loss smile, and mutual `大好き`.

**P1:** `adv_event_014_main-04` — China's “be an idiot until the end” morale speech and Saki's “Class 1-2 boss” recognition.

# 4. Event 015 — Hiroshima escort live: mentorship becomes reciprocal production

**Sources:** `adv_event_015_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Rinami, China, Lilja  
**Primary setting:** third-year escort program; self-designed regional live in Hiroshima  
**Conflict:** Rinami believes she must carry the juniors because she is the senior, while China and Lilja increasingly demonstrate that useful leadership can move in both directions  
**Institutional function:** senior-led regional program teaches set-list design, stage adaptation, MC work, local research, crisis response, and junior initiative

## 4.1 Rinami selects juniors for differentiated reasons

Rinami's two invitations are not generic acts of niceness.

She chooses China because she has observed both her effort and her useful idea generation in student-council work. The Hiroshima live is self-produced from set list through staging, so China's creativity is relevant professional capacity.

Lilja enters differently. Rinami notices her reading the recruitment notice and approaches her. Lilja initially considers asking Sumika to join because Sumika is her familiar bridge, then catches herself: she relies on Sumika too often. When Rinami offers the opportunity, Lilja accepts on her own.

Rinami's internal response is revealing. She had considered merely watching from a distance but is glad she spoke because **she herself lacked the courage to take such an opportunity in first year**.

This is mentorship grounded in remembered vulnerability. Rinami does not help because she is a completed person instructing an incomplete junior. She recognizes a state she once inhabited and supplies the intervention she wishes had existed for her.

### Claim transitions

- **Rinami — STRENGTHEN:** mature care is historically reflexive. She uses remembered limitation to create opportunities without making the junior's choice for them.
- **Lilja — STRENGTHEN:** independence from Sumika develops through chosen action rather than relational severance. She can decline the easiest support path and still remain deeply attached.
- **China — STRENGTHEN:** relational legitimacy includes being selected for concrete creative utility, not merely goodwill or family resources.

## 4.2 The planning room distributes expertise

The set-list session immediately resists a one-way senior/junior hierarchy.

Rinami contributes experience with pacing, rest, and unit structure. China proposes altering day and night presentations so repeat attendees receive different experiences. Lilja thinks about encore planning. When Rinami suggests taking a harder section herself to reduce junior strain, Lilja refuses the assumption that she needs protection and points to her running practice. Rinami accepts the claim and gives her the responsibility.

The exchange is small but methodologically important:

> **Rinami tests capacity, receives an answer, and updates the allocation.**

She does not make “senior care” synonymous with preemptively doing more.

China then suggests researching Hiroshima itself so the MC can be locally responsive. The live becomes an interaction between performance technique and place rather than a generic traveling set.

## 4.3 Lilja's audience imagination becomes stagecraft

At the venue, Lilja notices that the stage is wider than expected and proposes changing gesture and fanservice scale so people in the back can see clearly.

This is a strong Phase-4 confirmation of Lilja's transition from execution-absorbed beginner to audience-directed performer. Her concern is not “can I perform the prepared movement correctly?” but “how does this movement reach a spectator located elsewhere in the room?”

The same pattern appears during Hiroshima sightseeing. Lilja's historical mistakes and cultural curiosity are not treated as embarrassing evidence that she does not belong. Rinami immediately reframes a mistaken “Ieyasu lived in Hiroshima Castle” assumption as potentially useful MC material because mutual cultural ignorance can be funny and humanizing.

### Claim transitions

- **Lilja — STRENGTHEN:** courage becoming circulatory now includes concrete audience-spatial judgment.
- **Lilja — STRENGTHEN:** foreignness is neither erased nor exoticized as essence; cultural misunderstanding can become reciprocal address rather than shame.

## 4.4 China takes over the crisis that Rinami thought she had to solve

The strongest scene occurs when the audio fails mid-live.

Rinami's internal reaction is immediate responsibility: she is the third-year leader and must keep talking. Before she can find the solution, China steps in. She announces that the group has prepared a local song and begins an a cappella Hiroshima baseball-team support song, bringing the audience into the gap until the equipment is restored.

Rinami is surprised specifically because **China is improvising**.

The incident matters for both characters.

China does not wait for senior permission because the situation requires an answer. Her earlier local-research idea becomes actionable public judgment. She is not merely “cute junior with ideas”; she can organize a room under uncertainty.

Rinami does not react defensively to the loss of control. After the live she acknowledges that the juniors carried her too.

> `3年生だから、ふたりを引っぱらなきゃ` becomes `私も、ふたりに、引っぱってもらってた`.

That is almost a textual gloss on **reversible hospitality**.

## 4.5 Seniority becomes a reversible relation

# 5. Event 016 — the sport-center outing: independence without abandonment

**Sources:** `adv_event_016_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Saki, Ume, Temari, Kotone, Kotone's younger sister; Rinha in Temari memory  
**Primary setting:** leisure outing initiated from Saki/Ume's split ticket prize  
**Conflict:** several characters confront what it means when a younger or dependent person develops relationships and competence outside the older caretaker's direct supervision  
**Institutional function:** minimal; the event is valuable precisely because school hierarchy recedes and ordinary friendship/family behavior becomes visible

## 5.1 Saki deliberately gives Ume a social world that does not include Saki

Saki wins sport-center tickets and gives half to Ume. Her stated reason is that Ume should spend time with her student-council friends **without her sister**.

This matters because Saki's identity was historically built around being the unbeatable older sister. Event 016 catches a mature contradictory state:

- she wants Ume to expand socially;
- she takes practical action to make that expansion happen;
- she is also surprised to discover that the result makes her lonely.

Kotone identifies the feeling before Saki does.

Saki's reaction is not possessive sabotage. It is shame at vulnerability. She says she cannot tell Ume that she is lonely because she cannot show Ume a “weak older sister”; in Saki's logic, if anyone is going to miss the other, Ume should miss Saki.

The event therefore reveals a residual constraint inside Saki's mature care:

> **she can permit Ume's independence more easily than she can permit herself to need Ume openly.**

### Claim transitions

- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** older-sister identity is genuinely generous; she actively creates independence opportunities for Ume.
- **Saki — OPEN:** the `無敵のお姉ちゃん` role still restricts reciprocal vulnerability. Supporting Ume's autonomy is ahead of Saki's ability to admit her own attachment needs.
- **Saki ↔ Ume — STRENGTHEN:** differentiation does not weaken rivalry or affection; it creates new material for playful competition.

## 5.2 Kotone's family labor is reciprocally visible

Kotone asks to bring her oldest younger sister because the child often stays home caring for younger siblings and does not ask for expensive leisure. The request shows familiar Kotone responsibility, but the sister's behavior prevents a one-way provider reading.

She is polite with Saki and Temari, wants to try batting, and later responds intensely to seeing Kotone perform. Her perspective matters because she receives Kotone's sacrifice but also wants Kotone to have an idol life worth watching.

By the end, Kotone understands Saki's loneliness somewhat better: after watching her younger sister bond enthusiastically with Temari and Saki, she feels the same mixed pride/displacement from the caretaker side.

This is a useful family correction. The younger sister is not only a burden or beneficiary. She has evaluative agency and can return recognition to Kotone.

## 5.3 Saki's coaching is excellent—and still excessive by default

Kotone praises Saki's teaching ability. Saki has studied current sports theory specifically to coach Ume and knows how to observe, diagnose, and praise.

Then she immediately says she will train Kotone until she can hit home runs consistently.

Kotone has to tell her that this is unnecessary.

The joke preserves an important truth: Saki's pedagogy is sophisticated, but her default horizon remains optimization. She is good at individualizing method while still needing other people to define when “better” is enough.

The later three-versus-one futsal match shows a more mature version. Saki gives the others a challenge they can work around. Kotone suspects Saki could have guaranteed victory but chose an all-offense approach that left openings. Saki refuses to call it “going easy”; she says overcoming a wall through effort is fun.

This is competitive pedagogy translated into leisure.

## 5.4 Temari transmits Rinha's care script

Temari's time with Kotone's younger sister is one of the most important side-character evidence moments in the event corpus so far.

Temari is anxious because she believes she is bad with children. She gives precise batting instructions, sees the child become frustrated, and remembers herself in the same position.

The memory gives Rinha's language:

- hold the grip like this;
- match the timing and swing through;
- do not quit yet;
- once you can hit, it becomes fun.

Temari then gives Kotone's sister essentially the same structure.

This is **portable relational memory**. Rinha is absent, but her earlier care remains behaviorally active because Temari can reproduce it for someone else.

The memory also complicates any simplified “Rinha was only harsh” or “Temari rejects dependency” account. The recalled Rinha is abrasive (`根性無し`) but stays, teaches technique, and attaches persistence to a future form of enjoyment.

### Rinha routing

This scene should be promoted to the mandatory Phase-6 Kaya Rinha distributed-character reconstruction:

> Rinha functioned as an embodied teacher whose harsh encouragement became part of Temari's later care repertoire. Influence survives even where the relationship itself is damaged or absent.

Do not generalize one baseball memory into a complete childhood-care model, but do not treat it as decorative texture either.

## 5.5 Temari and Kotone recognize each other through the sister

Kotone's sister tells Temari that Kotone privately describes her as an extraordinary singer, brutally self-demanding, actually kind, and someone she genuinely respects.

Temari is visibly delighted despite trying to remain casual.

The sister then asks what Kotone is like at school. Temari answers honestly: she initially judged Kotone badly because of the amount of part-time work, but now sees someone highly skilled at dance who works extraordinarily hard.

This is unusually clean reciprocity because neither peer is present to perform for the other. Each person's esteem is revealed through a third party.

It revises high-friction banter from “they tolerate one another” toward **mutual professional respect that both are embarrassed to state directly**.

## 5.6 Saki and Ume convert separation anxiety into rivalry play

At the end Saki tells Ume how much fun she had with Kotone's sister. Ume responds by emphasizing how much fun she had with her own friends. They become jealous of each other's independent day and immediately convert that jealousy into playful competitive boasting.

Saki ultimately tells Ume to go have even more fun with her friends.

The result is not emotional detachment. It is a new form of sibling bond in which both are allowed lives that the other did not organize.

### Event 016 thesis

> **Healthy separation does not require pretending that separation feels good.**

Saki, Kotone, Temari, and Ume all encounter the discomfort of another person becoming less dependent. The event's mature answer is neither enclosure nor indifference: preserve connection while allowing the other person to acquire skills, friends, memories, and pleasures elsewhere.

### Relationship promotions

- **Saki ↔ Ume — STRENGTHEN:** active support for Ume's independent peer world plus acknowledged-but-hidden separation anxiety.
- **Temari ↔ Kotone — STRENGTHEN:** third-party disclosures establish reciprocal professional admiration beneath blunt peer friction.
- **Temari ↔ Kotone's younger sister — PROMOTE:** short but substantive teacher/child relation; Temari successfully adapts a remembered pedagogy despite believing she is bad with children.

# 6. Event 017 — “idol daily life”: ordinary self is not the opposite of performance

**Sources:** `adv_event_017_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Rinami, Sena, Tsubame, Mao; Saki, Temari, Kotone and other students in texture  
**Primary setting:** publicity-club recruitment/PR video about the everyday lives of third-year idols  
**Conflict:** the seniors are accustomed to managing public image, but the assignment explicitly asks for ordinary life; Rinami must decide whether the off-stage person is less “idol” than the performed one  
**Institutional function:** makes everyday conduct, school governance, leisure, and recruitment media part of Hatsuboshi's idol-production ecology

## 6.1 The project deliberately destabilizes staged/public boundaries

The publicity club asks for a video about the third-years' `日常`. Staff specifically prefer Rinami to operate the camera because a friend who is normally present may capture more natural behavior than an external crew.

The project pays a small fee and lends equipment. It is recruitment content for prospective students. The institution therefore explicitly treats **ordinary student life as marketable evidence about idol education**.

Tsubame initially argues that idols have no “ordinary” life because `アイドルは常在戦場`; all time is part of the vocation. Sena makes a related but more polished claim: being an idol is inseparable from her daily life, so filming her normal self might not reveal anything new.

Rinami's project tests both propositions.

## 6.2 Mao's princehood survives contact with the mundane

Mao's morning sequence includes diet management, early training, dorm responsibility, helping a non-idol-course junior carry sports equipment, and trying to perform a black-coffee image she cannot comfortably sustain.

Rinami lightly exposes the gap by narrating that Mao's “black coffee” contains generous sugar and milk.

The correction is not that Mao's prince identity is fake. The very next behavior—interrupting filming to carry equipment for a struggling junior—makes the prince ethic more credible than the coffee affectation.

This is a useful distinction for Phase 7:

> **role performance can contain superficial posing and substantive vocation at the same time.**

The question is not whether every prince-coded behavior is natural. It is whether the role continues to organize real choices when nobody needs the performance.

## 6.3 Sena's daily governance clarifies why she loves the institution

Rinami films Sena doing student-council work after light morning exercise. Sena explains that the council plans live events, collaborates with Amakawa city government, and manages an idol-activity special budget in addition to more ordinary student-council functions.

More important is why she likes working in the morning. She listens to students training and club activities outside. Those sounds matter because she loves idols and loves the school that produces them.

This is one of the clearest low-drama statements of Sena's institutional vocation. Her leadership is not reducible to prestige or legacy control. She derives genuine pleasure from being near other people's effort and wants the school to remain generative.

## 6.4 Leisure reveals Sena without invalidating Sena

On the group day off, Sena becomes an exuberant idol fan, buys goods, knows her own licensed merchandise, wants to eat at the fast-food restaurant where Kotone works, and misunderstands a menu's “happy wink 0 yen” joke as an orderable item.

She gets sauce on her mouth. Rinami catches the lapse. Sena is embarrassed.

The event refuses the obvious “real Sena versus perfect Sena” binary. Sena herself says she is proud that idolhood consumes her life and does not experience perfect conduct as imprisonment. Yet she also admits that casually eating fast food and talking with friends was a small dream.

Her reason for valuing ordinary student life is professional as well as personal:

> idols sing about ordinary people's feelings; if idols lose contact with ordinary experience, they may lose the ability to create shared feeling with fans.

This is a powerful theoretical statement for the corpus. Ordinary life is not merely recuperation from idol work. It is **source material for idol address**.

### Claim transitions

- **Sena — REVISE:** perfect-image discipline and ordinary pleasure are not mutually exclusive selves. The private/ordinary register can become additional performance truth rather than evidence the public role is counterfeit.
- **Sena — STRENGTHEN:** love of idolhood is not only competitive appetite; it includes fandom, institutional stewardship, and interest in how ordinary emotion becomes public art.
- **Institution — STRENGTHEN:** Hatsuboshi's training ecology includes civic collaboration, publicity, budget governance, and the management of everyday student experience.

## 6.5 Mao tells Sena that the unguarded smile belongs on stage too

The paired amusement-park game produces a rare Mao–Sena conversation without Rinami or Tsubame mediating.

Mao internally admits that she has avoided looking directly at Sena because Sena resembles the star Mao once admired and lost. Sena, meanwhile, says that she has spent the day forgetting to maintain “perfect Juo Sena.” She does not describe this as liberation from a false identity; she remains proud of the disciplined form.

Mao answers with a performance proposition:

> the idol-loving, unguardedly happy Sena is also attractive; if that smile appeared on stage, people would love her too.

This is not a request that Sena stop being the crown. It is an invitation to **expand what the crown can contain**.

The logic parallels Mao's own integrated model: authenticity comes through the freedom to choose and integrate roles, not by stripping role away.

## 6.6 Rinami's camera turns observation into self-revision

Rinami begins the week worried that she may love idols less than the others because she feels stalled while Mao and Tsubame seem to move forward.

After filming them, her conclusion changes. She sees the senior quartet as idols not only on stage but in the way they pursue dreams, help juniors, work, play, and love other idols. Their ordinary lives remain luminous because vocation organizes what they do.

Her final definition is explicit:

> an idol is a star on stage, but also someone whose dream-pursuing daily life can shine and give other people energy.

Then the others turn the camera back on Rinami. The observer is made observable.

That reversal matters. Rinami cannot remain the invisible caretaker/editor who captures everyone else's charm. Her friends insist that **her ordinary self also belongs inside the record**.

### Event 017 thesis

> **The everyday self is neither backstage residue nor the “real person” behind a false idol. In Gakumas, ordinary life can be part of the material from which idol identity is authored.**

### Relationship promotions

- **third-year quartet — STRENGTHEN:** leisure and reciprocal observation expose private texture while preserving professional identities.
- **Mao ↔ Sena — STRENGTHEN:** Mao's role-integration insight becomes advice Sena can potentially use; the relation moves from intimidated observer toward peer interpreter.

# 7. Event 018 — Osaka escort live: role co-authorship and reciprocal disclosure

**Sources:** `adv_event_018_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Mao, Sumika, Hiro  
**Primary setting:** third-year escort program; Osaka regional live with a theatrical interlude  
**Conflict:** Mao wants to lead responsibly, Sumika is still negotiating fear around dance, and Hiro enters as an observer who knows more about the others than they know about her  
**Institutional function:** uses the regional-live program to test senior adaptation, embodied accessibility, cross-domain performance, and self-disclosure

## 7.1 Mao notices constraint before Sumika volunteers it

Mao invites Sumika first. Sumika accepts quickly and treats the Osaka trip as both work and something she can enjoy. During rehearsal, however, Mao notices that Sumika keeps protecting her knee and proposes reducing choreography difficulty.

Sumika initially hears this as criticism of dance quality. Mao clarifies that the problem is injury risk, not lack of talent.

Sumika then explains the real state: the knee is physically recovered, but intense movement still triggers a psychological block. She has been working through it gradually with Saki and Lilja.

Mao's response is strong senior practice:

- accept Sumika's account rather than override it;
- lower risk without treating her as incapable;
- prioritize quality over volume;
- prohibit further apology;
- frame the unit as mutual assistance rather than Sumika owing the others compensation.

This is a significant stress test for Mao because her senior/prince role could easily become paternalistic. Here she notices, asks, receives, and changes the plan.

### Claim transitions

- **Mao — STRENGTHEN:** prince/senior care can operate through adaptive design rather than rescue theater.
- **Sumika — STRENGTHEN:** dance fear is not laziness or lack of technical inheritance; she can choose ambitious work while still requiring graded exposure.
- **Mao ↔ Sumika — PROMOTE:** direct senior/junior trust around embodied vulnerability and production adjustment.

## 7.2 Hiro chooses the pair because their interrupted prior lives resemble her own

Hiro volunteers to join after hearing Mao and Sumika will travel together. Her surface explanation is simple: she likes them and wants to try the same kind of regional live China and the others are doing.

Her later disclosure adds a second motive.

Hiro already knew Mao had been a child actor and Sumika a ballet prodigy. She recognizes a structural resemblance: each had a substantial life before the present idol role, and each entered Hatsuboshi after a path that did not continue straightforwardly.

Hiro herself then reveals that she is already a university graduate.

This is not a claim that their biographies are “the same.” The important point is **recognition through discontinuity**. Hiro approaches people whose current first-year/third-year student identity visibly fails to exhaust their history.

### Claim transitions

- **Hiro — STRENGTHEN:** her curiosity about people is often structural. She is drawn to unusual constraint configurations and biographies that do not fit the obvious surface category.
- **Hiro — REVISE:** apparent spontaneous attachment may rest on substantial prior observation. “I like you” does not imply she entered the relation without analysis.

## 7.3 The theater idea makes each member produce the others

Hiro proposes inserting a play into the live because Mao has acting experience and Sumika has ballet-derived expressive capacity. She says she will write the script and initially assigns herself narration.

The script is designed to reveal Mao and Sumika's charm.

Mao and Sumika then alter it without telling Hiro: they pull the narrator into the story as a princess and insist that the happy ending include all three.

The sequence is analytically valuable because authorship moves in a circle:

1. Hiro reads Mao and Sumika and writes roles for them;
2. Mao and Sumika read Hiro and rewrite the ending to give her a role;
3. Hiro accepts the embarrassment rather than retreating into observer status;
4. the audience receives a jointly altered performance.

This is **reciprocal authorship**, not simple fanservice texture.

Mao also gets to play a prince in a context where the role is neither defensive nor imposed. She later says she had wanted to act again and enjoyed it. Event 018 therefore strengthens the claim that princehood remains a genuine positive desire after role integration.

Sumika's reaction to the romantic/theatrical language also matters. She can perform a vulnerable heroine while internally experiencing real embarrassment. Performance does not require emotional detachment from the material.

## 7.4 Sumika feared Hiro because Hiro threatens the usefulness of the mask

After the live, Hiro asks Sumika directly whether Sumika had been avoiding her.

Sumika says yes.

The reason is not dislike. She felt as though lies would not work on Hiro, and that made Hiro frightening.

This is one of the sharpest ensemble tests of Sumika's chosen brightness/avoidance structure. Hiro is dangerous not because she attacks the mask but because she seems able to see the mechanism by which it is being used.

The event's answer is not forced confession. Sumika has already voluntarily explained the knee issue; Hiro has voluntarily revealed her degree and motive for approaching them. Reciprocal disclosure makes the asymmetry less threatening.

Hiro then admits she had wanted to talk to Sumika for a long time, likes Sumika's fashion, and wants Sumika to make her into a gyaru. Her reason is characteristically Hiro-like: it seems not to suit her.

That last line preserves resistance-seeking inside a social/fashion domain. Hiro is interested in mismatch itself—but the mismatch is now pursued collaboratively rather than as private self-punishment.

### Event 018 thesis

> **People become safer to be known by when observation becomes reciprocal.**

Hiro begins with informational advantage. Mao and Sumika become more comfortable only after all three can author, expose, and alter one another in return.

### Relationship promotions

- **Hiro ↔ Sumika — PROMOTE:** initial fear of Hiro's perceptiveness is explicitly named and answered through mutual disclosure and future play.
- **Hiro ↔ Mao — PROMOTE:** Hiro's prior observation becomes direct respect; Mao receives Hiro's disclosure without treating her as aberrant.
- **Mao ↔ Sumika — STRENGTHEN:** Mao accommodates embodied fear without reducing Sumika to injury; Sumika trusts the unit with information relevant to safe production.
- **Mao–Sumika–Hiro trio — PROMOTE:** each member contributes a different authorship function—senior staging, embodied performance, structural observation/scriptwriting—and each becomes object as well as author.

### Japanese-register evidence

- Sumika's `まおっち先輩` keeps hierarchy playful rather than distant; `ひろっち` marks rapid casual familiarity.
- Hiro's plain `麻央` / `清夏` and compressed declaratives can carry high intimacy without honorific intensification.
- Mao's prince language inside the play is clearly marked theatrical speech; it should not be mined as ordinary conversational register.

### AV requests

**P1:** `adv_event_018_main-02` — Mao noticing Sumika's knee protection and shifting from performance evaluation to care/plan revision.

**P0:** `adv_event_018_main-04` — Hiro's surprise when the other two rewrite her into the play; useful for observer-to-participant transition.

# 8. Event 019 — the emergency Nagoya live: friendship becomes operational trust

**Sources:** `adv_event_019_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Saki, Temari, Kotone; Sena, Tsubame, Rinami in setup; Ume, Misuzu, Kotone's sister in departure texture  
**Primary setting:** emergency replacement within the third-year escort/regional-live program  
**Conflict:** a first-year is asked to replace an absent third-year leader on almost no notice and must construct a functioning unit from close peers  
**Institutional function:** tests contingency planning, first-year leadership, peer production, and whether the escort program's developmental function survives an exception to its normal hierarchy

## 8.1 Sena recruits Saki because adaptive leadership is already visible

The student council describes the problem as an emergency. A third-year leader cannot participate; the event is too close to cancel; replacing the leader means rewriting performance details quickly.

They select Saki because the replacement must:

- adapt rapidly;
- raise performance quality on a compressed timeline;
- lead other idols;
- tolerate changed staging and set-list requirements.

Saki immediately accepts once she realizes this is why she was chosen.

This is institutional recognition of a Phase-4 pattern already visible in her peer pedagogy: Saki can diagnose different people and organize around their strengths.

The event also exposes a limit. She accepts before consulting the people she intends to bring.

## 8.2 “Friends” are no longer decorative vocabulary for Saki

Saki announces that she will bring her `友達`: Temari and Kotone.

They are furious that she accepted first. Saki begins to back down and says she can ask others.

Both immediately shout that they are going.

The scene matters because the relationship is doing practical work before anyone gives a sentimental speech. Temari and Kotone reject Saki's unilateral procedure but not the underlying trust. Saki assumes they can succeed; they are angry that she did not grant them agency in accepting, yet they still choose the project.

This is an important distinction for Saki's care/leadership model:

> **accurate confidence in another person does not substitute for consent.**

The friends correct the process while affirming the relation.

## 8.3 Distance from old caretakers becomes proof of new capacity

Each member prepares by negotiating a prior attachment.

Ume worries that Saki is overextended; Saki has already prepared Ume's training plan and expresses unusual calm because Temari and Kotone are with her. Ume reacts competitively and decides to improve dramatically while Saki is away.

Kotone cancels family time. Her younger sister does not guilt her; she says seeing Kotone shine as an idol would make her happier than having the planned outing. Kotone is briefly surprised by how naturally she promises a top performance.

Temari receives packing help from Misuzu. Misuzu explicitly worries about Temari traveling far away without either Misuzu or Rinha. Temari answers that Saki and Kotone are capable and that she **will accomplish the trip without Misuzu's protection**.

Misuzu accepts the answer and asks for stories afterward.

This is one of the cleanest modular examples of Temari individuation from caretaker dependency. It does not repudiate Misuzu: the packing list Misuzu leaves is still useful, and Temari later carries it with embarrassed affection. But Temari can leave the dyad, function in another peer group, and return with the relation intact.

### Claim transitions

- **Temari — STRENGTHEN:** dependence can change form. She can accept background care while asserting independent competence away from Misuzu.
- **Misuzu — STRENGTHEN:** mature care can permit departure. She worries, checks, supplies practical support, and then stays home.
- **Kotone — STRENGTHEN:** family duty increasingly coexists with the family's active demand that she live as an idol, not only as provider.
- **Saki — STRENGTHEN:** trust in peers reduces her need to control every variable personally.

## 8.4 Saki's production logic is domain-differentiated

The planning scene shows why the seniors chose Saki.

She does not build a unit around “Saki plus two helpers.” She gives each member space corresponding to their strengths.

- Temari receives song-centered demands and recovery time consistent with her improved but still limited stamina.
- Kotone receives a long entrance/fanservice section because dance and audience interaction are core strengths.
- Saki promises to cover gaps if the others hit their limits.

Temari explicitly says she likes the arrangement because she can put everything into singing. Kotone notices that Saki has actually been watching them closely.

This is peer production in a very literal sense.

Saki is not their formal Producer, but she performs several producer-like operations: capability assessment, load balancing, set-list architecture, motivational framing, and contingency support.

## 8.5 Forced intimacy exposes hidden dependence without destroying dignity

The hotel has only two beds for three people.

The result is comic, but analytically dense.

Saki falls asleep instantly and becomes physically chaotic. Temari, who fears sleeping beside anyone except Misuzu, ends up sharing a bed with Kotone. Kotone later teases her for clinging in sleep. Temari retaliates by revealing that Kotone talks in her sleep as if comforting younger siblings and even pats Temari.

None of the three handles exposure gracefully. That is the point.

The event gives access to forms of dependency that daytime self-presentation suppresses:

- Temari seeks warmth/contact in sleep despite defensive independence;
- Kotone reproduces caretaker behavior unconsciously;
- Saki's optimized waking discipline vanishes into extremely unregulated sleep.

They then convert the embarrassment into MC material at the live.

Private awkwardness becomes shared “青春” offered to fans.

This directly echoes Event 017's ordinary-life thesis: the off-stage self can become part of idol address without being treated as shameful contamination of the public role.

## 8.6 The trio works because no one must become easy

The event does not smooth their personalities into a harmonious unit.

Temari still criticizes Saki's reckless acceptance. Kotone still mocks both. Saki still turns breakfast and even her father's bizarre collaboration menu into competition. Temari remains anxious about unfamiliar sleeping arrangements. Kotone remains financially/family aware.

Yet the live succeeds because the friction is now **predictable**.

They know enough about one another to build around the difficulty instead of requiring it to vanish.

### Event 019 thesis

> **Friendship becomes operational when people can rely on one another without requiring one another to become less difficult.**

Saki, Temari, and Kotone do not become temperamentally compatible. They become **legible enough to coordinate**.

- **Saki ↔ Temari ↔ Kotone — PROMOTE:** prior high-friction work trio becomes travel-capable operational friendship with explicit mutual trust and domain-differentiated performance roles.
- **Temari ↔ Misuzu — STRENGTHEN:** separation is now survivable and explicitly asserted; care persists without physical enclosure.
- **Kotone ↔ younger sister — STRENGTHEN:** younger sister actively legitimates idol work when it conflicts with planned family time.
- **Saki ↔ Ume — STRENGTHEN:** Saki's confidence in friends gives Ume room to respond with independent growth rather than needing to accompany her.

# 9. Event 020 — Lilja and Sumika: a shared future that does not require a shared self

**Sources:** `adv_event_020_main-01` through `main-05`  
**Participants:** Lilja, Sumika  
**Primary setting:** low-cost day out, picnic, shopping mall, anime shop, home questionnaire  
**Conflict:** mundane money trouble opens into a much deeper negotiation about shared history, hobby identity, migration, success, and the possibility that only one of them reaches the future they promised  
**Institutional function:** little direct school governance; unusually rich relationship/character material for Phase 6 and definitive monographs

## 9.1 Sumika's practical care is intimate but not infantilizing

The event begins because Lilja has almost exhausted her monthly discretionary money on anime goods, limited editions, and a cosplay event. Sumika scolds her but first confirms that living expenses are separately secure.

The distinction matters. This is not a crisis of material survival. It is ordinary young-person money management after leaving a household where Lilja's father often bought things for her.

Sumika's response combines teasing, accountability, and solution design. She does not give Lilja money or cancel the day. She redesigns the outing around things that cost little or nothing.

This is a useful low-stakes care model for both characters: Sumika can take a coordinating role without turning Lilja into a helpless dependent, and Lilja can accept correction without surrendering the preferences that caused the problem.

## 9.2 Sweden is reconstructed as a shared relation, not a single person's backstory

Their conversation fills in how friendship operated before Hatsuboshi.

Sumika often visited Lilja's home when ballet allowed because there were few same-age Japanese speakers around her. Lilja had few school friends and spent substantial time alone with anime and games. Each became a missing world for the other:

- Lilja gave Sumika access to hobbies and forms of ordinary childhood she had barely encountered inside ballet life;
- Sumika took Lilja outside and expanded the physical/social world around an indoor routine.

This reciprocity is important. Neither is merely rescuer.

Lilja's parents also become more dimensional: her Japanese father indulged hobby purchases, her Swedish mother taught her cooking and scolded both father and daughter over excessive hobby focus. Lilja wants eventually to invite both parents to a live as repayment/thanks for letting her abruptly pursue idolhood in Japan.

Sumika immediately expands the dream: once they are top idols, they should perform in Sweden together.

Lilja observes that Sumika repeatedly makes her dreams larger.

## 9.3 Sumika reclaims Sweden through Lilja

Lilja worries that Sumika may not want to return to Sweden because the ballet years were painful.

Sumika answers with one of the event's strongest relational claims.

Yes, she was often lonely and miserable. But she met Lilja there. Lilja's presence gave her a place to belong, and because of that she came to love Sweden too. If life began again, she would still go to Sweden because otherwise the two of them would not be together now.

Lilja answers symmetrically: if life began again, she would still come to Japan.

The scene should not be flattened into “destiny” as objective metaphysics. Its function is relational valuation:

> **each is willing to re-choose a painful migration history because the relation changes the value of the history that produced it.**

Lilja then says she wants to refill Sumika's Sweden memories with happy experiences the next time they visit.

This is not erasure of trauma. It is **memory expansion**: painful history remains true while new shared experience prevents it from owning the entire place.

### Claim transitions

- **Sumika — STRENGTHEN:** Sweden/ballet history is not a uniformly rejected past. Lilja becomes the reason Sumika can retain affection for the place where major suffering occurred.
- **Lilja — STRENGTHEN:** courage circulates bidirectionally long before idol school; Lilja did not only receive social opening from Sumika but gave Sumika belonging in return.
- **Lilja ↔ Sumika — STRENGTHEN:** attachment is reciprocal world-expansion, not one-way confident friend / timid friend structure.

## 9.4 Hobby identity becomes something Lilja can bring onto the idol surface

The mall/anime-shop section tests another apparent binary: serious idol versus otaku private self.

Lilja has been deliberately hiding hobby information because she fears `オタクバレ`. Sumika argues that the assumption is outdated and perhaps backwards in an idol school. Lilja's game knowledge, anime expertise, and high-quality cosplay could be public strengths.

Lilja tentatively decides to post cosplay photographs.

Then she immediately escalates toward a thirty-minute evangelism video, forcing Sumika to slow her down.

The comedy preserves the character mechanism. Lilja's restraint is often not lack of passion but governance of **very strong passion**. Once permission is granted, intensity appears quickly.

This is relevant to her performance model. The shy exterior is not evidence of low inner amplitude.

The same section contains a telling self-definition. When Sumika worries that idol work leaves less time for hobbies, Lilja says both dream and hobby matter and she intends to choose both, even if that is selfish or unrealistic. The most dramatic phrasing turns out to be an anime quotation, but that does not make the commitment false. Lilja borrows media language to articulate a genuine value.

### Claim transitions

- **Lilja — STRENGTHEN:** private fandom is a high-intensity identity domain with potential public-performance value.
- **Lilja — REVISE:** use of borrowed/quoted language should not be treated as lack of self-authorship. She can appropriate fictional speech as a genuine tool for self-expression.
- **Sumika — STRENGTHEN:** social intelligence includes translating a friend's private interest into plausible public opportunity while also constraining over-escalation.

## 9.5 They know each other's performed selves because they know the cost of performing them

A magazine questionnaire asks each to describe what she likes about her close friend.

Sumika names Lilja's bright eyes when discussing interests, cute pride when quoting anime, strong will, and unwavering trust.

Lilja names Sumika's joy around fashion and cosmetics, her bright social presentation, the fact that she is actually delicate and serious underneath the joking surface, and the way she continually leads Lilja into unfamiliar worlds.

This is unusually useful for reconstruction because the two are not simply describing traits. They are distinguishing **surface action from governing structure** while refusing to call the surface fake.

Lilja knows Sumika's brightness can coexist with sensitivity because she has seen both. Sumika knows Lilja's hesitation can coexist with stubborn will because she has seen both.

That is continuity-bearing witness in its most concentrated form.

## 9.6 The feared future is not failure; it is asymmetrical success

The final questionnaire asks what each would do if she were not an idol.

Lilja describes a recurring dream in which she graduates without becoming an idol, works in translation spreading anime/manga internationally, and one day sees Sumika on television with Saki, Temari, and Kotone. The four are introduced as classmates. Lilja is absent.

Sumika reveals a complementary dream: she never fully returns to dance, gives up idolhood, becomes a makeup artist, and eventually works on a now-famous idol Lilja. They politely reminisce as if their old promise no longer has force.

Both alternate lives are viable. Neither is portrayed as misery or moral failure. That is why the fear is sharper.

> The nightmare is not “I become nothing.” It is “I build a tolerable life while the person beside whom I imagined the future reaches that future without me.”

Lilja's answer is not to deny that such worlds could exist. She says they may be possible, but the present selves will **not choose them**.

`絶対に、アイドルになろう。`

Sumika answers: both of us.

This does not merge their destinies into one career outcome. It establishes a reciprocal demand that differentiation occur **inside shared success rather than through one person's disappearance**.

### Event 020 thesis

> **Lilja and Sumika's bond is not maintained by sameness. It is maintained by repeatedly widening each other's possible world while refusing a future in which only one is allowed to inhabit the shared dream.**

### Relationship promotions

- **Lilja ↔ Sumika — PROMOTE:** Sweden/Japan history, mutual world-expansion, ordinary financial care, public/private identity knowledge, and complementary failure dreams make this one of the strongest Phase-6 relationship dossiers in the corpus.
- **Lilja ↔ parents — STRENGTHEN:** parental support for migration/idol aspiration is directly connected to Lilja's desire to return gratitude through performance.
- **Sumika ↔ Sweden — REVISE:** painful ballet history and positive place attachment coexist because Lilja changes what Sweden means without erasing the injury.

### Japanese-register evidence

- Sumika's casual `リーリヤ～？`, playful `デート`, teasing, and rapid topic shifts coexist with serious care without a formal register boundary.
- Lilja's `清夏ちゃん` and soft hesitation remain present even during unusually forceful declarations.
- The anime quotation episode is important reconstruction evidence: Lilja can momentarily sound dramatically declarative because she has internalized fictional phrasing, then immediately reveal/acknowledge the source.

### AV requests

**P0:** `adv_event_020_main-02` — mutual “if life restarted” declarations and Sumika's account of Sweden as a chosen second home because of Lilja.

**P1:** `adv_event_020_main-04` — Lilja's dream+hobby declaration, anime-quote reveal, and decision to make otaku/cosplay identity more public.

**P0:** `adv_event_020_main-05` — complementary alternate-future dreams and `絶対に、アイドルになろう` / `ふたりとも` resolution.

# 10. Cross-event synthesis

## 10.1 Continuity-bearing witness as a third Phase-4 social mechanism

The first three event tranches now support a cumulative model:

1. **Events 001–005 — peer relation as production technology.** Characters develop through rivalry, class structure, work, seniority, and shared institutional tasks.
2. **Events 006–012 — peers as error-correction systems for aspiration.** Other people identify bad method, misdirected effort, hidden fear, and recipient-blind creativity.
3. **Events 013–020 — peers as continuity-bearing witnesses.** Other people preserve information about who a character was, who they are when unobserved, and what changes without declaring one state the only authentic self.

These functions overlap but should not be collapsed.

A person can correct method without knowing history. A person can remember history without having authority to correct. A durable close relation often does both.

The third function becomes especially important in a live-service corpus because the same character appears across branch variants, seasonal events, professional contexts, and radically different emotional states. The stories themselves repeatedly resist the idea that one surface should become definitive.

### Examples

- Rinami can remember Sena as a classmate before the current perfect presidency and still respect the public crown.
- Tsubame can remember Sena's long rivalry and therefore identify producing juniors as insufficient replacement for being challenged herself.
- Mao can remember the lonely Prima Stella while seeing the present institutional leader.
- Temari can reproduce Rinha's old instruction even though the current relation is no longer physically present in the scene.
- Lilja and Sumika know one another before idol school and can therefore read current bright/timid surfaces as genuine but incomplete.
- Misuzu knows Hiro's chosen philosophy of hardship well enough to notice when this fear is different.

This is not an argument for privileged “true selves” known only by intimates. The better formulation is:

> **close relations hold multiple valid states in memory and therefore reduce the pressure to make any one state total.**

## 10.2 Maturity means governing dependence, not eliminating it

Across the tranche, the most mature outcomes are not solitary.

Sena needs rivals if she is to escape developmental stasis. Rinami needs juniors capable of carrying a live when she freezes. Hiro needs friends to teach her that difficulty has external moral costs. Misuzu needs a reason she personally endorses before effort becomes worth spending. Sumika needs people who can know fear without turning it into a verdict. Lilja needs Sumika but also needs the ability to act without Sumika. Temari needs Misuzu's packing list while also needing to prove she can travel without Misuzu. Saki needs Ume to have a life beyond her and also needs permission to experience loneliness about it.

The governing distinction is therefore not dependence versus independence. It is:

- **enclosed dependence** — the relation reduces alternative action, alternative attachments, or the ability to leave;
- **governed dependence** — care remains available, refusal remains possible, the person can act elsewhere, and support can reverse direction.

Events 013–020 repeatedly reward the second form.

## 10.3 “Ordinary life” is productive material, not leftover non-idol time

Events 016, 017, 019, and 020 are especially important for behavioral reconstruction because the dramatic stakes are often low.

Characters shop, eat, tease, sleep, budget, babysit, play sports, talk about hobbies, worry about siblings, and fill out questionnaires. None of these scenes suspends idol identity.

Event 017 explicitly theorizes why. Sena argues that idols sing ordinary people's feelings and therefore need ordinary experience. Rinami concludes that dream-pursuing daily life can itself shine. Event 019 converts embarrassing sleep behavior into stage MC. Event 020 turns private fandom/cosplay into a possible public identity asset.

For reconstruction, this means:

> **do not simulate Gakumas characters as if their “real characterization” appears only under H.I.F.-level pressure.**

Their low-stakes conduct contains core mechanisms: who notices what, who accepts help, how quickly teasing escalates, where embarrassment appears, how professional language enters play, what kind of leisure restores them, and how public role bleeds into private habit.

## 10.4 Role authenticity is repeatedly additive rather than subtractive

This tranche adds several more anti-binary cases:

- Rinami can be soft and commanding.
- Sena can be perfect and ridiculous at a hamburger shop.
- Mao can enjoy princehood while being visibly bad at bitter coffee and roller coasters.
- Misuzu can dislike effort and still choose extreme exertion for a friend.
- Hiro can enjoy mismatch and still care about recipient satisfaction.
- Sumika's bright mask can be chosen and emotionally real while still protecting fear.
- Lilja can use an anime quotation and still mean the value she is expressing.
- Temari can assert independence and still sleep-seek warmth.

A useful Phase-7 guardrail is therefore:

> **When a scene exposes contradiction, first test whether the corpus is expanding the character's repertoire before concluding that one side is false.**

## 10.5 Rivalry has at least four distinct functions in the current corpus

Events 013–020 help separate rivalry mechanisms that should not be merged into one trait.

1. **Epistemic rivalry** — Saki uses competition to learn what another person can do.
2. **Pedagogical rivalry** — Saki constructs challenges that cause peers/juniors to develop.
3. **Identity rivalry** — Ume/Saki and Tsubame/Sena use the other person as a durable reference point while differentiating.
4. **Anti-stagnation rivalry** — Sena needs an equal opponent not to prove current superiority but to make further growth possible.

The fourth is especially important. It explains why succession cannot satisfy Sena's full problem. A successor may preserve the institution after her; only a rival can threaten the present self enough to create a new one.

## 10.6 Friendship becomes operational when friction becomes legible

Event 019 gives the clearest formulation, but the pattern spans the tranche.

Close groups do not become effective because conflict disappears. They become effective because members know how to interpret it.

- Saki knows Temari's limits and gives her a song-heavy role.
- Temari knows Saki's recklessness and corrects it without treating the relationship as broken.
- Kotone knows when to mock, when to work, and when to let Saki lead.
- Rinami knows Tsubame's hostility can contain care.
- Hiro knows Misuzu's low-effort preference is not the same as indifference.
- Sumika knows Lilja's dramatic quotation habits well enough to test whether a line is borrowed without dismissing the conviction.

For later relationship synthesis, **predictability of friction** should be tracked as a form of intimacy distinct from emotional warmth.

# 13. Institutional and system deltas

## 13.1 Senior escort / regional-live program

Events 015, 018, and 019 collectively establish a recurring program architecture:

- normally a third-year idol leads two first-year idols;
- the group travels to a regional venue;
- participants take meaningful responsibility for set list, staging, MC, and local adaptation;
- the program combines professional performance with sightseeing/local research that can feed the show;
- program administration can survive contingencies, including replacing an unavailable senior leader with an exceptional first-year;
- the pedagogical goal is not merely junior observation. Juniors are expected to produce, adapt, and sometimes lead.

This should be promoted to the institution ledger as a concrete mechanism for **distributed production education**.

## 13.2 Sports festival as spectacle and training

Event 014 establishes:

- near-universal participation rules;
- school-assigned competition events;
- national broadcast;
- a class-live reward;
- class-level scoring that makes individual weakness socially consequential;
- explicit confidentiality/professionalism expectations even around school-event planning.

The school deliberately makes non-idol athletic performance part of public idol education. The event therefore tests public composure, teamwork, class identity, and marketable personality alongside physical ability.

## 13.3 Publicity club and ordinary-life recruitment media

Event 017 establishes that:

- student publicity media are institutionally supported;
- performers may be paid even for school-linked PR work;
- the school lends production equipment;
- peer-operated cameras may be preferred to professional distance when “daily life” is the desired object;
- student-council work includes live-event planning, municipal collaboration, and special idol-activity budgeting.

The school does not merely sell stage excellence to prospective students. It sells an integrated life-world.

## 13.4 First-year leadership can be formally trusted under pressure

Event 019 is an important exception case. The escort program is senior-led by design, but the student council selects Saki as emergency substitute because her observed adaptive capacity outweighs nominal year hierarchy.

This strengthens an institutional pattern seen elsewhere: Hatsuboshi's hierarchy is real but not mechanically seniority-bound when performance evidence supports exceptional responsibility.
