# Who hears what in *Lycoris Recoil*

A girl tells her partner that she will live. A man wishes the same girl happiness and says that his death made her new life possible. Both statements reach us in E13. Chisato hears Takina’s reassurance. The decorated farewell from Yoshimatsu is in Mika’s hands. It is the viewer who gets Yoshimatsu’s words as voice-over. Give Chisato that voice as well and her choice on the beach changes meaning: she would be answering a claim of ownership she has never been shown receiving. Give Takina firsthand knowledge of the heart’s provenance and the girl who comes looking for her partner becomes a knowing keeper of Mika’s secret. The episode establishes neither. A performance can be meant for someone, spoken in their absence, preserved on paper and heard by a third party. Asking who hears it is part of interpreting the line. [E13 18:48–19:04 and 19:43–19:58, C0404–406/0422–425; first legible close view at 19:47.937; TV source recheck, E13.]

The same distinction matters in a joke. Takina proposes finishing the work promptly, and Chisato remarks on how keen she sounds. Takina admits that she too is excited about their trip. Chisato hears a personal admission from the girl whose call to the commander began with professional identification and an apology. Later, three people delighted to meet what they take for costumed performers hear an almost literal description of the girls’ work. The words are accurate enough to threaten disclosure; the listeners commend the performance. In Short 02 Chisato and Takina are able to hear each other anew. In Short 06, saying nearly the right thing to an unfamiliar audience succeeds as an entirely different speech act. The series repeatedly places recognizable voices before people with different reasons for listening. That structure explains more than arranging the cast from excitable to restrained. [Short 02 C041–59; Short 06 C012–20, S00E06-C01/C03.]

**Source and hearing.** This study covers the thirteen TV episodes and all six *Friends are thieves of time* shorts in Japanese. Plot, pronoun, speaker, perceptual delivery, acoustic property and interpretation are distinguished one claim at a time. I checked original ASS text, retained picture evidence, the reconciled continuous-observer accounts and source-native readings. I independently decoded 28 cue windows in ten Japanese FLACs for this study. I previously measured the S03/S06 recordings against E08. I have not listened continuously to the synchronized Japanese material. Where a listener reports a change in sound, their testimony is attributed. A full-mix level or a pattern of mouth positions cannot supply an ear I did not use. The accompanying source audit gives exact samples, methods, exposure, reopened speaker findings and unresolved listening referrals.

## A courteous sentence can do several things

**Takina’s formality has an addressee and a purpose.** At the café in E01 she introduces herself through what she is supposed to learn and where she hopes to return. Chisato calls the newcomer her partner before the two have earned much common experience. The earlier reading’s full-mix introduction samples put Chisato near −20.4 dBFS and Takina near −26.1. The lexical assignment of a relationship precedes any inference about the resemblance of their tones. The earlier 千束さん at 05:04.74 is addressed to Mizuki, whom Takina has mistaken for Chisato. At 07:33.06 she puts an actual question to Chisato using that form; E03 13:21.95 retains the suffix inside a distressed appeal. By E09 00:46.73 she calls the endangered Chisato twice by bare name. These are positive anchors for different forms in different relationships and emergencies. Counting printed names without their speaker and recipient would falsely date intimacy. Nor does Takina begin as a person incapable of play. At 12:08–13 she turns Chisato’s own description of a meaningless tower back on the speaker; Chisato answers with laughter. Addressed fragments and early imperatives already break a neat progression from complete polite sentences to later plain forms. On the train in E02, a practical reason for choosing a jelly drink coexists with opening her mouth to Chisato’s offered bite and pronouncing it good. The professional register survives a small, genuine change of mind. [E01 C0153, C0298–299, E01-CLM-004 and E01-C08; E02 05:15–06:25, E02-AUD-002/003.]

E03 puts stages of a loss into the mouths of more than one girl. Takina appeals to a superior. After the position is denied she breaks off at the thought of where she belongs. She attributes the loss to herself. Then she demands to know what she is supposed to do. Exact caption-window mono RMS for the self-blame at 14:02.66–05.45 and the demand at 14:52.21–53.63 is −37.63 and −25.58 dBFS in my decode. The later おあいこ after returning a blow to Fuki measures −31.35. Moving from a plea to a proposition about settling their score allows her to finish a sentence on terms she has helped to set. Neither accepting an invitation nor entering a mock match has given her one newly expressive voice for all occasions. Erika meanwhile names her own role against a room ready to blame Takina. Two reported listeners hear the later わたしじゃん, more clearly than the first overlapped pronoun around 16:32.7. Her defense can be understood from the exchange even where the first pronunciation remains unresolved. [E03 C0281/0301/0338–339/0433/0464, E03-CLM-009–016, verifier Q05/Q16.]

In E04 Takina’s かりんとう preference requires no institutional justification and no great increase in level. Its archived window is near −31.9 dBFS. Laughter late in the episode measures near −21.2. An early playmate might learn more from what she states quietly than from what she laughs at. At the aquarium she uses the conspicuously colloquial めっちゃ in a compliment to Chisato. The fish play gives the remark cover; her earlier interest in the pendant, Chisato’s movement at her neck and E13’s express recollection when she discards the corded token favor the pendant as the referent of それ. Naming the object is a contextual inference. The indirection helps explain why an intimate evaluation can embarrass the listener and leave the speaker room to laugh. The score reported to swell after the fish imitation comes well before the eventual compliment, not as a retrospective label for a particular feeling. [E04 C0183, C0276, C0293 and 14:46–54; E04-AUD-007/011–013; E04-C04; E13 20:12–35 and dated source recheck.]

What Takina does with another person’s answer is often more diagnostic than how much she speaks. In E05 the human premises of the client’s request prove false. Takina still tells Chisato that she really was a good guide. Her qualified judgment and Chisato’s thanks occupy separately measured full-mix windows both near −31.2 dBFS. Their quiet exchange accomplishes something the discovery of deceit threatened to erase. In E06 Mizuki presses Kurumi over the consequences of the hacking and names Takina’s dismissal in the third person. Kurumi apologizes in the later scene. Takina distinguishes Kurumi’s action from her own decision to fire and requests her continued cooperation. Captions, #000789/#001107 and the attributed E06 observer account converge on those assignments. The older E06-AUD-003 mixed measurement of the dismissal sentence supplies no speaker-specific result for Takina. The identified hold at 21:06.25–09.50 is low-level mixed signal, stereo RMS −67.96 dBFS, not digital zero or directly observed moral deliberation. In the same episode a small victory in rock-paper-scissors draws よしよしっ at −18.35 dBFS mono. Mizuki proposed changing the opening; Mika explained the zero chance under the old one. Takina’s delight records her application of help and her own competitive pleasure. It does not set a minimum volume for gratitude or responsibility. [E05-AUD-007–011; E06-C07, E06-CLM-014/015/021–023, 13:05–13:44, 15:17.65–15:24.99, 21:03.12–21:18.47 and C0488.]

**Chisato’s exuberance is also a choice of address.** She greets an anxious arrival like someone she can already welcome. She can stretch a greeting to amuse, speak gently to an enemy she is treating, expand her voice while imagining retaliation in E04, or understate a consequential choice. In E05 her account of wanting to help without taking a life is near −28.3 dBFS, appreciably more contained than the lively tour; in E09 her answer that killing the nurse would change nothing is near −29.2. In E11 she exports the café’s elongated いらっしゃいませ～ into combat (16:27.55–28.38). The same phrase can welcome somebody to a place where they belong, or address an adversary who emphatically does not belong there. A listener recognizing the café formula hears what Chisato can carry from one life into another. Her antagonist need not hear comfort. [E01-CLM-004/011; E04-AUD-010–012; E05-AUD-007/008; E09-AUD-006–008; E11 C0309, E11-AUD-008.]

She does not settle into solemn quiet whenever morality comes up. When Majima tries to turn their shared hostility to assigned purpose into equivalence, the refusal 一緒にすんな occurs at 21:23.93–25.10 alongside a gun report identified in the caption. I measure −18.08 dBFS mono for that mixed window. At Yoshimatsu’s proposed sacrifice, Chisato will not live as somebody other than herself. At 10:12.51–16.85 in E12 the window is −20.55. Takina’s plain insistence that she does not want Chisato to die is −29.95, 10:26.94–30.44. What matters to each woman is explicit in the wording. Chisato’s objection concerns who can define the life to be saved. Takina’s objection has a name in the middle of it. Calling the second emotion weaker because the recording is quieter misdescribes the scene. Calling the first delivery cold or timbrally hardened requires additional independent listening through the interfering effects. The words and the opposition survive that open question. [E11-AUD-013 and REV-E11-013; E12 C0186/0190, E12-AUD-012/013; full-window controls in the receipt.]

The two women can also use established expressions to tell each other where they belong without agreeing what to do. When E09’s long-planned day fails on its schedule, Takina asks if Chisato is having fun. The two voices share a roughly −27.9 dBFS window; an estimated fundamental for that mixed passage cannot be attached to one speaker. Later 分かりません (20:40.47–41.81) measures −33.80 in my mono window. It answers a question about whether she is pleased to have DA back, from the very person for whose sake she is returning. The going-out expression いってきます does different work from announcing an appointment or promising permanent residence; it opens the possibility of coming back. In E11 Chisato and Mika exchange their own departure formulas. In E13 Takina can quarrel vigorously after finding Chisato, relay what Mika reportedly brought and then give her own calm assurance. The first-person stakes and the grammar of report differ. One cannot infer foreknowledge of Mika’s separate actions from the certainty of あなたは死にません. At the end Chisato calls Takina 相棒 with a fuller, outward-looking invitation. The word introduced before they had a shared history now launches an activity they can choose. [E09-AUD-011–018; E11-CLM-021 and E11-AUD-007; E13 C0356/0404–406/0445, E13-AUD-014/022–024; dated E13 source recheck.]

## Registers that disappear if the café becomes a duet

**Mika and Yoshimatsu speak as adults before people who have different claims on them.** E07 lets Chisato overhear herself being discussed, recognize Yoshimatsu and speak to the benefactor for whom she has long been looking. Chisato’s exclamation on recognizing him (ヨシさん) measures around −23.4 dBFS in the cited window. Later Mika demands that Yoshimatsu release her from an assigned future. After the confrontation Mika admits that he lacked the resolve his threat presumed. In my cue-window control his demand at 18:06.98–08.86 is −25.50 dBFS mono and his later admission at 18:42.02–46.98 is −29.72. These windows have specified text and cuts; the archive’s −24.8 for the broader threat is a different selection. After Yoshimatsu’s farewell, Mika no longer has to persuade him that he will carry out the threat. Chisato’s reassurance to Mika belongs to a later exchange. At 19:59.01–20:01.89 she says that he kept his promise (C0408, around −33.2 dBFS in the archived window). The threat, Mika’s admission and the meaning Chisato gives his conduct each reach a different listener or speak to a different need. In E10 his disclosure to Chisato lets her call both men fathers and thank him for the choices still hers. In E13 the viewer, Mika and Chisato receive three different kinds of information. No word for father discharges the adults’ obligations of honesty. [E07 15:18–15:39, C0384–385/0395 at 18:06–18:47 and C0408 at 19:59–20:02, E07-AUD-006–011; E10-AUD-013–018; E13-C04/C08 and source recheck.]

In E08 Yoshimatsu can tell Himegama that choosing is impossible. In E12 he addresses Chisato through the authority of her alleged gift and offers his own life. With Mika in E13 two hearing accounts report あたし in two utterances at roughly 07:20 and 07:29, while the subtitle prints 私. The returning multimodal observer and zero-history audio-only listener provide corroboration for that scene; the first is not blind on Q13. Those tokens qualify an assumption that Yoshimatsu always uses a uniform public self-reference. They cannot diagnose sexuality, gender identity, or a stable pronoun for every intimate exchange. The ensuing exterior cut has very low but nonzero sound and a brief visible highlight. It lets audible words leave consequences without depicting the act that followed them. The later farewell on paper supplies the audience with an utterance whose moral claim can endure even without delivery to its intended recipient. [E08-AUD-001; E12-CLM-019–021; E13 Q13, 07:18–07:34/08:05–08:12; E13 C0422–425.]

**Fuki, Sakura and Erika share a profession, not a way of hearing Takina.** Fuki’s abruptness can enact supervisory responsibility, a personal grievance or permission at real cost. In E01 she seeks authorization and still has a standing order to wait. Erika asks for intervention, which makes her a participant in that crisis even though she cannot prescribe the method. In E03 Sakura’s abrasive account of Takina’s standing is different from Erika’s belated defense. In E11 Erika asks Fuki to let Takina go and offers to fill her position (C0190–191, 10:21.81–25.81). That offer makes the departure practicable before Fuki warns Takina what leaving will cost (C0202, 10:51.38). Takina’s acknowledgment at 10:56.09–57.05 measures −29.18 dBFS mono and her reason at 11:03.39–06.94 measures −24.80. Fuki finally gives permission with 行けよ (C0208, 11:11.28). Her short reply lets Takina act with the warning still in force. In E12 Takina’s dry agreement that Erika did something awful occurs in a sequence that also contains gratitude. A sympathetic paraphrase in which she entirely absolves Erika would erase the respect involved in answering the actual admission. [E01 02:06–02:53, REV-E01-003; E03-CLM-012/013 and Q16; E11 C0190–191/0202–208, E11-CLM-008–013; E12-CLM-032–034/043/044 and AUD-019.]

Short 05 offers a remarkably good control on attributing a fixed emotional color to Fuki. She invokes DA’s claim on the building. When Mika offers her a milkshake, she answers はい (C017, 00:37.43–38.22). He then tells her not to stay up too late, and she answers はいっ！ (C019, 00:41.18–42.06). The critic’s independent mono full-mix measurements are −31.97 and −27.40 dBFS respectively. My decode of C019 also gives −27.40. Soon Fuki is ordering her juniors about again. The earlier producer’s pitch estimates of roughly 464, 467 and 498 Hz belong to the bedtime response at C019 and the irritated responses at C024 and C043. My mono full-mix levels for those latter two cues are −22.88 and −22.54 dBFS. The irritated answers have the higher pitch estimates. Neither measurement supplies a romance. The actual listener, a long-standing form of address, the drink she accepts and her return to a different audience establish the register change. In S06 her attempts at 先生 break up when the one adult she praised for good sense appears in holiday clothes. Erika’s 残念です and laugh have their own addressee; the object of her private wish is unsettled. [Short 05 C016–019/024/043, S00E05-C04/SR05; Short 06 C067–86, own control receipt.]

**Kurumi and Mizuki can interpret a scene and also get carried away in it.** The revelation about Walnut in E02 leaves room for Kurumi’s relatively contained naming and a more activated invitation from Chisato. In E06 the person implicated in Takina’s expulsion speaks for herself and has work to do after Takina’s answer. Mizuki’s explanation of a game places her in the role of skilled teacher despite her willingness to complain, tease and drink. In E11 a departure conversation between Mizuki and Kurumi can be a challenge against giving up, rather than a declaration of uncomplicated loyalty; returning in E12 requires practical help. The shorts give them consequential tasks in the sound world. Mizuki takes the microphone at the very point when a different singer might normally be expected in S01. Kurumi calls out as an audience member. In S03 she names what the others have reacted to but not quite said, then hears the verdict meet laughter. S04 includes an implicated Mizuki seeking silence and a Kurumi urging Takina to look again. Someone capable of directing another person’s interpretation can still be one of the objects that interpretation fails to explain. [E02-AUD-009; E06 13:05–13:44/21:02–21:19; E11-CLM-004; E12-CLM-028; Short 01 C049–50, Short 03 C086–87, Short 04 C011–24/047–54/083–end.]

**Majima, Robota, Himegama and the people on a command channel.** Majima can publicly announce what the population must see, speak companionably about a film, then voice a claim of likeness Chisato rejects. The switch in footing matters more than a formula about a consistently low male voice. His label 黒いほう compares Takina with Chisato, as the E10 and E13 lines together establish. It supplies no description of rounds or weapons. Narrowly pitched sound near 3,147 Hz appears across E08 and E13; similar correlations can be obtained by aligning independently generated tones. Treating it as shared subjective hearing or the same device would insert an explanation absent from the work. Himegama can use the professional politeness of a caregiver while pursuing a purpose incompatible with the patient’s interest. Her contained E08 line (around −30.5 dBFS) is chilling in context because the viewer knows what care ought to mean, without needing an actor-specific diagnosis of a sinister timbre. At E02 11:26–29 Robota’s helmet, monitor reaction and explicit cue assignment identify him and strongly support the cry’s continuity. The proposed reattribution to people in the vehicle failed picture inspection even though the individual grunt syllables are uncertain. [E08-AUD-015; E08-C08/E13-C02 and Q18; E10 C0221/E13 C0082, Q19; E11-CLM-029/030 and AUD-013; E02 Q16.]

Radio makes this problem consequential for political authority. At E10 21:24–48 Kusunoki and Robota lay contrary claims on Chisato, one invoking the lives of many and the other Yoshimatsu’s. The published 300–3,500 Hz left-to-right energy differences switch from +19.15/+20.23 dB to −17.03/−19.05 dB in the relevant caller windows; Chisato’s answer recenters near +0.17. It gives the audience a way to keep two imperatives in earshot together. It does not claim a literal location inside Chisato’s head. At E12 19:52–20:09 the grammar of a name is insufficient to identify the speaker. C0419 carries the field lead’s call to command. C0420 names Nishikigi Chisato and asks whether to continue, over a wide view of headquarters. The existing continuous Gemini reader identifies the field captain reporting *her presence* to Itadori. The receiver’s pictures support that sequence without independently identifying the voice across the cut or every addressee. I reproduce −31.50 dBFS mono for C0420. The early gloss using this number as Chisato’s delivery must be withdrawn. At C0421 Itadori orders withdrawal; the field lead relays it at C0422. Kusunoki asks for Fuki and explicitly assumes command thereafter. The speaker with the quietest window need not be the person who gives an order, the person whom it benefits or the person named inside it. [E10-C08; E12 C0419–426, E12-C09/AUD-017; dated E12 source recheck; S05/06 for distinct ordinary authority.]

### What the credit and the recording room can tell us

There is source-proximate testimony about how the differences were made. Wakayama Shion remembers recording E09’s final line again after director Adachi Shingo explained its background. Anzai Chika does not remember that exchange. Their responses record two participants’ different recollections. Neither the retake nor a modest mixed level identifies which thought Takina had at the microphone’s equivalent inside the story. Koshimizu Ami distinguishes small adjustments to scripted lines from more extensive background improvisation. Kuno Misaki describes conversational direction and a suggested ad-lib in the trunk-reveal material. Sakaki Kōsuke recalls direction toward grounded playing, including how Mika welcomed Takina, while Matsuoka Yoshitsugu recalls learning that Majima would remain in the series. The way an actor expected a role to develop and the way a character knows a scene will end are different kinds of knowledge. Asaura’s acknowledgment that he cannot reproduce Anzai’s Chisato exactly in prose is a particularly good reason to preserve the difference between the words on a page and the variable duration, contour and overlap of a performed reply. These are production-reference observations. They supply no fresh hearing of an obscured line or new fictional event. [*Heroine Archive*, S104–109/p102–107, S110–121/p108–119 and S136–137/p134–135.]

## Hearing an ending before a story has ended

An ending theme announces an institutional certainty of another kind: the broadcast will eventually give way to titles. What remains unsettled is who can finish speaking first. In E01 Chisato’s next café line begins about 1.30 s after the matched recording starts; Yoshimatsu’s greeting to Mika begins about 17.40 s after it. The first illustrated credit is later still. In E03 Takina’s ええ is displayed from 22:19.19 to 22:20.15. The verifier hears it finish around 22:19.55. The matched recording begins at 22:19.680, leaving about 0.13 s between the reported voice ending and the matched entry, despite the continuing caption. The short answer can give way to music while the word remains available to the eye. In E09 snow and Chisato’s first response precede the matched entry; two verbal responses follow. In E11 the return to a pair working together is still an ongoing crisis when the music begins. Insisting that the most important syllable or cut is the same across all these endings would flatten four genuinely different uses of the same recording. [TV verifier Q02/Q05/Q08/Q11; E01 C0539/0546, E03 C0464, E09 C0382/0383, E11 C0430.]

These five placements use an E08 reference. The start of its first ten-millisecond stereo bin above −45 dBFS is 22:19.680. Correlation establishes where common recorded material lies. What a listener first recognizes through dissimilar mixes can be later. E04 reverses the order: the first credit image is 21:45.304; music enters near 21:45.400. Takina’s laugh and Chisato’s crosstalk around 21:39.53 already happened. The ending song cannot have masked those earlier syllables. In the same episode two comic bird sounds 16½ minutes apart match to .999216 over 460 ms, at a lag of −226 samples at 48 kHz. An audible resemblance is strongly attributable to a reused production recording. Neither listener attribution inside the scene nor a single private reaction follows from the reuse. The bird can punctuate two different failures of expectation. [E04-C08/C09, F2-05 and TV Q16; E01/03/07/09/11 dated music comparison.]

The less regular uses keep this comparison honest. E02’s apparent change of level under Yoshimatsu’s discovery includes dialogue in its windows; it cannot prove a 31 dB change of music alone. In E05 a reported piano entry follows the interval in which Chisato has refused the client’s request: her decision comes before the revelation of deception. The space may give her refusal some composure in the viewer’s experience, but “serenity” would be an interpretation. E06 begins with a binarizable silence whose end changes under quantization, and late in the episode a reported breath and the credit music are different sound sources. E08 crosses the failed check-in with the approach to a medical crisis. The early song makes a cliffhanger; a local ringback cannot establish that the remote person heard the phone. In E10 a reported piano/string entrance near 17:35.3–35.5 follows room sound, while disjoint quiet bins do not establish 32 unscored seconds under the disclosure. At E12 the expectant ending accompanies interrupted departure and Majima’s return. A song can hold a narrative possibility open for the viewer while nobody onscreen experiences it as an announcement. [E02-C07; E05-C05, 18:18–19:18; E06-C08; E08-C09; E10 Q10/C09; E12-C10.]

In E13 there are *two* identification problems. The return describes orchestral scoring over the opening rescue. Near 09:05 an audible sung entry is reported beneath the resumed encounter and its caption; that report can establish a useful contrast with the opening. A proposed E01 “opening-theme control” actually contains warehouse dialogue. An actual E02-to-E03 positive control matches near .99/.96 or better, while the E13 09:00–11:20 region yields no comparably compelling identification of the entrance. The scene can open out to a singer’s voice without assigning the exact track, lyric, arrangement or singer. Later, Chisato chooses a future, the ending sequence intervenes, and the mobile café can reopen in Hawaii. Neither the music nor Hawaii explains the unshown journey, resolves the ethics of rescue, or turns the farewell voice-over into playback for her. Director Adachi Shingo describes the series opening as music-clip-like and personally reads the lyrics of 花の塔 from Takina’s perspective. That is an attributed interpretation of the song. It does not make every appearance its character’s monologue. [E13-C01/C05/C08/C09, TV Q12 and dated E13 recheck; *Heroine Archive* S98/p096 and S100/p098.]

## Six shorts teach the audience to expect a voice

The shorts build a small musical argument across separate stories. An audience familiar with the TV ending can anticipate the place where instrumental material would give way to a singer; a new viewer can still hear a cue cut, restart or continue. Which expectation an individual viewer actually formed is unobserved. The six placements nevertheless have distinct, testable outcomes.

What makes the Japanese performances unusually consequential is the amount a listener can get wrong about an otherwise clear sentence. A short answer from Fuki can make it possible to leave. A recording can begin in the expected place and clear a space for Kurumi to tell her colleague exactly what she sees. To preserve those differences, keep the human action attached to the particular utterance, its listener, its source and the edit that lets us hear it.
