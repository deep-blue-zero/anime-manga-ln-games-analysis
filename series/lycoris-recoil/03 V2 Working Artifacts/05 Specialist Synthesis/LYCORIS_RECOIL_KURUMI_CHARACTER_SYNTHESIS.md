# Kurumi / Walnut

At an airport, Mizuki thanks Kurumi for a first-class ticket. “Yours is economy,” Kurumi tells her. They have just said something much harder. Kurumi has asked whether Mizuki ought to have stayed with Chisato to the end. Mizuki asks what Kurumi herself is holding back for, given how much at home she was at LycoReco. After a pause Kurumi admits that she had fun. The ticket joke gives them a way to part after an admission neither has quite managed to make on her own. A little later Kurumi is trying to get Mizuki off the plane. She has found something Chisato needs. Both the farewell and the change of plan are real. [E11 C0037–0053, 02:59.83–03:48.96; C0109–0140, 06:44.26–07:55.33.]

Kurumi (クルミ) is the name a small, fair-haired person gives her new companions in a Tokyo café. Walnut (ウォールナット) is the name under which she has clients, a reputation and people who want her dead. She can do difficult information work for money. She can also pay other people handsomely for what she needs. A growing collection of people can call her out to play a game, including people to whom she has no intention of telling her age. The history of a handle which people remember from decades ago is evidence about its reputation. It cannot establish when its present user was born, when she took up the name, or whether anyone else has used it. Childhood, legal name, family of origin and prior household remain unknown. She has good reason to be selective about who can put a recognizable face to either name. [E01 08:42.67–08:54.89; E02 06:28.95–06:44.55, 18:13.70–19:04.71, 22:11.35–22:28.99; E08 07:18.52–07:23.82.]

## Getting her name wrong

Walnut first tells us something about herself when a client mistakes her question for one about his fee. Yoshimatsu says he is satisfied with the job he commissioned against Direct Attack and is prepared to pay accordingly. Money is the wrong answer to the question at hand. Walnut wants to know why a man associated with Alan, known for supporting gifted people, has involved himself in an arms deal. She has already been surprised to discover that one of DA's reputed executioners is a girl. 無知であることは嫌いなんだ, she says: she hates being ignorant. Yoshimatsu ends the conversation with an explosion and his own maxim about the happiness of the ignorant. The professional who has secured an appointment with someone important has allowed curiosity to carry her beyond what her customer is willing to discuss. Curiosity here exposes her to danger. Being willing to take that risk for knowledge tells us something the later successes on behalf of friends cannot. [E01 C0495–0511, 20:26.83–21:16.76; E01-C06.]

Robota quotes the public account of Alan's philanthropy as a reason its people would be unlikely to kill. Walnut has good reason to dissent. The question for her is how her apartment was identified. Robota's boasting about his pictures and the drone he acquired for the occasion helps identify the party who sold her out. He wants the standing of the country's foremost hacker; she needs somewhere he will stop looking. At DA headquarters an aide points out that Walnut has been reported dead repeatedly over the past thirty years. Kusunoki would welcome the news, yet knows it is a rumor. There are several Walnuts in circulation already: a missing person, a professional rival, a name someone has contracted to kill. There is only one person here who needs to make it alive to the café. [E01 C0220–0227; E02 C0007–0027, 00:21.34–01:11.22; C0112–0117, 06:28.95–06:44.55.]

Walnut's new protectors see someone in a squirrel suit and hear the name. When Chisato and Takina believe the client has died in their care, their distress is genuine. The reveal distributes the ingenuity and the cost. Mizuki has been inside the suit. Kurumi emerges from the case, explains why it helps if pursuers believe her dead, credits Mika with the idea in response to Takina's question about deliberately taking fire, and regrets the more spectacular finale they no longer need. Mizuki says the pay was good. Mika is pleased that the affair ended sooner. Chisato wants to know why nobody told the two guards. Mizuki values precisely the natural reactions they gave, while Mika eventually acknowledges the deception. Kurumi admires the guards' handling of the unexpected. The group has saved her life by borrowing two other people's grief, and those two are entitled to different feelings about the job. Kurumi has been resourceful enough to ask for help and dependent enough to spend the job being carried. [E02 15:56–16:40; C0344–0387, 17:43.00–19:31.11; C0404–0405 at 20:15–20:21; E02-C06.]

The offer at LycoReco has terms. In return for shelter, she will help with the work; by her reckoning they are getting her cheap. Chisato holds up Saori’s photograph and asks her to find the small figure in the background. She has another request as well. She interrupts the beginning of “Walnut,” reminds her that this person has died and asks for her real name. Kurumi supplies the Japanese word for the same nut. Chisato laughs, says it suits her, uses it in her greeting, and invites her out for dango with Takina. Kurumi answers よろしく 千束, returning a first name for a first name. An outsider might find her answer evasive. It is also enough to begin a relation in which it will be her timer that rings for the café's game night. Neither Chisato nor the audience thereby obtains her civil identity. The local name lets her answer an invitation without giving up control of the rest of her history. [E02 C0415–0418, C0430–0434, C0446–0459, 20:47–20:55, 21:27–21:43 (frame #001195 at 21:38.756), 22:07–22:40; E03 C0036–0040.]

## What she is like when no one needs rescuing

The game-night alarm interrupts Kurumi and Mizuki looking at a photograph. Mizuki got DA intelligence to examine the image and hopes it might at least show body types. Kurumi shows what she has obtained and starts thinking about the advantage the café might have. Then Kitamura calls for her, Itō wants a match and she goes. She asks Takina if she will play, gets a refusal and shows no great distress about it. Near the end of the episode she asks Chisato whether she too is coming. Kurumi is already one of the people who can issue an invitation. In E04 she explains why the absent guns have supplied no useful lead on the black market. Later that day she is laying down cards and winning while the news plays. In E05 Chisato catches her gaming through a briefing. 聞いてるよ, she says, and supplies the unwieldy medical term Chisato has stumbled over. Her divided attention works well enough that even its apparent failure can be funny. It would be a mistake for her friends, or the viewer, to infer from these successes that she is always listening or already knows everything of consequence. [E03 02:12–03:50 and 22:33.29; E04 06:11–06:42 and 19:13–19:22, E04-CLM-018; E05 C0022–0033, 02:17.95–02:45; REL-CAFE-E03-001/E04-001/E05-001.]

Kurumi likes jobs with an interesting problem or an audience she can choose. Taking an order from a stranger takes practice. When the café is suddenly busy, Mizuki asks why she has come out to work. Kurumi says explicitly that *Chisato* told her to help. Her halting お… お待ちどう… gets an eager reception. The customers want to know how old the new girl is. 秘密だ, “It's a secret,” is a perfectly serviceable answer. Mizuki soon wants to know why she is grinning. She can enjoy being noticed while withholding exactly the fact her admirers want. A little later Takina says she is considering investing with Kurumi. It is a materially different kind of work with a different partner. This episode gives Takina the business initiative, Chisato the request to help serve, the customers their admiration and Kurumi her own response to it. By the finale Kurumi is taking Sakura's order and teasing Jin about who invented his nickname. The skills the café has acquired include knowing which particular person she can tease about what. [E08 C0123–0137, 06:59.88–07:29.37, C0210–0214, 10:27–10:39; E13 C0303–0314, 14:17.63–14:42.07.]

Kurumi can be rude. When Takina hopes for another way back to headquarters, Kurumi's crack about DA having no use for her earns a tap from a tray and her own economical verdict, 失言だった, a bad thing to have said. During the investigation of Mika's private meeting, Kurumi laughs at Takina's preposterous alias. A human attendant checks the name and lets the pair through. Kurumi sees in that an example of people trusting their records too readily. She then guesses what sort of rendezvous Mika is keeping from them, with far less grasp of his affairs than she has of the club's records. The story makes room for the curiosity of someone who jokes about other people's privacy and takes her own seriously. It supplies no secure romantic history, attraction or intimate partner of hers. A joke about Mika's love life and the mock daughter act she later uses at the airport are social performances with particular addressees and purposes. [E07 C0090–0097, 05:14–05:33; C0282–0309, 13:37–14:46; E11 C0116–0123, 06:57–07:16.]

## What she owes Takina

In E06 Kurumi identifies the four girls who were targeted in images from the earlier DA operation. She recognizes the connection because *she* took the contract to interfere with DA. She had wanted access to the client. When Mizuki intimates that some associate of hers must be responsible, Kurumi tells the room it was her. When accused of having supplied the guns, she disputes that account of her act. The accusation, its rebuttal and the known human cost all have to survive. A professional can accurately delimit the work she was paid to do and still have helped set in motion events she neither directed nor wished for. [E06 C0296–0325, 14:31.23–15:31.49; E01 C0501–0511.]

Who speaks here changes what Kurumi has to endure. As the earlier breach is connected to the missing guns, the line is that **Takina** was fired, in the third person; the picture puts Mizuki in the act of speaking, with Takina looking on. Mika tells Mizuki to stop. Takina's next question is whether that is all the footage. Kurumi asks where Chisato is. The rest of the footage makes the danger to her urgent. During the search Kurumi sends Chisato’s location to the others, who can physically reach her (18:21.75). Later, when Mizuki once more invites recrimination, Kurumi first points out that she helped. Then comes the apology *to* Takina. The picture stays on the kneeling Kurumi as she lowers her head; the original continuous observer also identifies her as the apologizer. A low-level interval follows, before Takina answers. Nothing in the measured quiet distinguishes shame, dread of expulsion or simply waiting to hear the verdict. What makes the apology matter is that Kurumi must hear what the injured person actually wants. [E06 C0314–0325, especially 15:17.65–15:25.00 and retained frame #000789 at 15:21.650; C0442–0455, frames #001102 and #001107 at 21:00.843 and 21:06.000; TV E06-C07 and original continuous observer E06, lines 86–87.]

Takina takes responsibility for the choice that led to her own transfer. She also requires Kurumi's cooperation to the end. Kurumi agrees, and immediately plays the material in which the men call their leader Majima, even joining in with his name. She has a gift for finding a new conversational footing once someone has told her what can be done. Here she need not earn the verdict that her intrusion was harmless, and Takina need not repay rescue with forgiveness for everything. Mizuki keeps the cost visible. Mika interrupts the piling-on. Takina specifies the form repair should take; Kurumi undertakes it. No line guarantees that the trust between them now extends to all secrets. [E06 C0443–0459, 20:44.60–21:26.81; E06-CLM-014/015; REL-TAK-KUR-E06-001.]

Her limits as a witness have practical significance well before this. During the E05 escort Kurumi tells the still-responsive Mizuki to hurry, then says how little she can do without the drone. Mizuki tells her to come herself. Only afterward does Mizuki go out of contact. Kurumi says the spare has no charge and later sends it, allowing her to give the others a location. In E06 she commits a drone to the café team's escape and loses it in the process. Neither incident makes her a competent eyewitness when she has lost her vantage point. She cannot give the group a reliable biography of “Matsushita,” the apparent client, from the wheelchair and the experience they have had escorting him. The later cut to equipment, with Yoshimatsu's words and Himegama's reply, gives the viewer grounds for an Alan-side explanation. The exact operator across the encounter remains open, and Kurumi has no automatic access to what the cut shows the audience. Conversely her account that DA used girls as bait in E06 is her in-story report. Mika's indignation on hearing it establishes his response. It adds no independent testimony on what each girl was told. The sheer variety of things accessible from her desk makes these boundaries particularly easy to miss. [E05 12:39.61–13:34, 18:18–20:30, E05-CLM-016–018; E06 17:43.04–17:51.93 and C0433–0437 at 20:10–20:23, TV E06-C01.]

## Whom she comes back for

Chisato's artificial heart gives Kurumi a question that impressive technical reach will scarcely touch. She first wants to know whether DA made it and Mika corrects her. Later she finds that the records concerning Alan and Yoshimatsu have been scrubbed. The important move is that she says so. She has made tea, an activity unfamiliar enough that she calls attention to it, for the person who can answer from experience. Mika says smoking lets him punish himself for his guilt. Kurumi thinks he should give it up if it tastes that bad. Then she asks for the account he can give because Chisato needs it. She can hear that he has cared for the girl for years and still ask if he too regarded her as a tool for killing. Hearing more about Yoshimatsu as a person is itself a lead. Takina and Mizuki overhear. When Takina says she had planned to refuse DA reinstatement, Kurumi is surprised: she knew Takina wanted it. Takina had acquired a quite different reason to go back. Here the narrative identifies exactly what a close colleague can fail to know. [E05 09:10–09:46; E09 C0157–0176, 09:15.33–10:13.59, C0209/C0227, 12:22.55–13:17.44, C0263–0285, 16:00.86–17:08.26; E09-CLM-019.]

She is skilled enough to formulate a testable hypothesis and interested enough to keep going after someone else's loss. At the beginning of E10 Kurumi and Mizuki consider why a person lacking ordinary civic registration would be vulnerable to exploitation in illicit medical research. The exposure of that vulnerability is their inference at the time. When Chisato closes the café, Kurumi says something rather plain: staying in Japan without Chisato would put *her* life in danger. Chisato suggests Germany, where she could go to a board-game competition. Kurumi asks Chisato to come and offers to arrange a passport. Chisato wants to stay with Mika and asks her to invite Takina someday. Later Kurumi tells Mizuki that closing was what Chisato wanted and that Takina is still there. She can respect a person's chosen separation while disliking the prospect and taking her own need for security seriously. Her airport admission that it was fun is no vow never to leave. [E10 C0002–0012, 00:06–00:30, C0037–0050, 02:47–03:23, C0110–0112, 06:30–06:39; E11 C0042–0053.]

What brings her back is a discovery. She pursues a lead while traveling, finds evidence of an improved heart and reports it first to the person who can use the news, Takina. In her telling the researcher made the device, Yoshimatsu took it away and **probably** still has it. She admits she does not know his recent whereabouts. There is plenty of comic selfishness in interrupting Mizuki's journey: the stunt with the purported long-lost daughter, the barked instructions and the reminder that Mizuki has yet to date the man in Vancouver. There is also friendship in being the person to whom Mizuki said goodbye, and enough respect for Takina's competence to make her a partner in the search. The plan matters precisely because a ticket, departure and another imaginable life have all been real possibilities. [E11 04:58.82–06:48.60, C0116–0147, 06:57.31–08:15.77; E11-CLM-003–008; REL-CAFE-E11-001.]

The success promptly produces a mistake. With Chisato's phone information and the direction of Mika's car, Kurumi infers that Chisato is likely heading toward the old tower and the person who has the heart. “Everything's solved,” she tells Takina. Takina thinks there may be a trap. Kurumi says Chisato will be all right. Her confidence in her friend has outrun what her evidence can establish about the intentions of the people waiting for her. Takina's refusal to be reassured is one of the best gifts anybody gives Kurumi in the series. She takes seriously the lead Kurumi found without having to trust the conclusion that Kurumi prefers. Kurumi's contribution survives being corrected. [E11 C0167–0182, 09:23.38–10:03.67; E11-CLM-007/008.]

## A lie people can live through

Kurumi arrives with Mizuki and calls down to the girls to ask if they are all right. The wind has dried her eyes and mouth. She has brought something Mika entrusted to her; when Chisato wants to know who asked for the rescue of the Lycoris, Kurumi tells her to ask Mika. The audience subsequently hears Mika confirm to Kusunoki that he concealed the identity of the requester. An exchange Kurumi is not shown hearing cannot retroactively turn her answer into a refusal to divulge a fact she certainly knew. She knows enough to tell the girls that their own organization means to dispose of their colleagues at Enkuboku. [E12 C0199–0221, 10:56.05–11:49.98; C0269–0275, 13:53.90–14:06.70.]

What she offers is a division of labor. People inside the tower can act where she cannot. Mizuki has limited time in the air. When Takina worries what can be done with Radiata down, Kurumi answers with the very professional name she laid to rest in E02: ウォールナットに任せろ, “Leave it to Walnut.” Chisato calls LycoReco back into business. Later Robota recognizes the voice of the rival he thought he had disposed of, and the people at DA recognize the handle without ceasing to imagine an elderly adversary. The return of the name carries a price, however satisfying it may be to anyone who has watched Robota boast. A reputation that could give cover when dead is now identifiable again in a place with reason to act against its owner. The episode does not show that identification turning into certain knowledge of the café employee's civil identity. The name has been offered to different people for different reasons. [E12 C0258–0267, 13:24.66–13:47.35, C0352–0367, 16:55.24–17:28.11, C0373–0392, 17:43.04–18:33.76.]

Kurumi identifies Robota's location and contacts the police. Abe reports the arrest. Headquarters recognizes Walnut as Radiata comes back under another party's control. The subsequent broadcast explains the violent images as part of an attraction. Kurumi has helped the girls escape an order that would have killed them for having been seen. She has also given the public a reason to discount evidence of what really happened. Those two consequences fall to different people. Itadori gives the withdrawal order, the field lead relays the cease-fire and Kusunoki takes command again. Kurumi supplies a changed information environment, without acquiring their authority or demonstrably choosing every element of the broadcast. The viewer sees her asleep after the work, while Mizuki still has flying to do. Even here nobody succeeds alone. Short 06 will bring three fans to the café who appreciate the Lycoris as characters in a film; the shorts give no evidence that Kurumi produced that film. [E12 C0373–0392, C0402–0412, C0419–0426, C0433–0436, 17:43–20:37; TV E12-C09 and SOURCE_RECHECK_20261123; SHORT06-CLM-001–004.]

In E13 the person who hated being ignorant has become somebody else's discreet associate. Kurumi asks Mika why he still uses the cane. He asks her to keep quiet. She says she has contracted with the surgeon, who is waiting. Their exchange is intercut with Chisato and Takina at the beach and with Yoshimatsu's written farewell. Mika holds the insert. The audience hears its words. Takina reports that Mika brought the case. Kurumi's practical knowledge is considerable, yet the cut supplies no warrant that she witnessed the lethal encounter, the acquisition of the particular organ, or the writing or reading of the entire message. Nor does Mika's request in direct response to the cane question specify every secret she is to keep. What she indisputably does for Chisato includes finding where she is. Takina credits Kurumi, and Saori's incidental image provides the lead even though Chisato chose a place she says has no network or cameras. Saori took the picture; Kurumi finds the clue; Takina goes. The friend Kurumi can trace remains someone she cannot make explain herself. [E13 C0374–0381, 17:26.57–17:49.09, C0402–0406, 18:48–19:03, C0417–0428, 19:33.99–20:10; E13-X02 and TV SOURCE_RECHECK_20261123.]

## The person beside the screen

Kurumi's language carries across the names. She uses ボク, addresses associates with お前, shortens an order, and teases without waiting for the respectful distance the listener may expect from someone of her appearance. She offers the ordinary reciprocation よろしく to Chisato, can admit 今んとこ お手上げ to Mika when a search has gone nowhere, and knows the perfectly conventional word for bringing an order to a café table even if it takes her a couple of starts to get it out. When she gives Takina the heart lead, 多分 matters as much as the triumphant announcement: she knows where her evidence ends even if her next deduction gets overconfident. Her clipped insistence that Mizuki hurry at the airport is written as a series of separated beats. Neither ボク nor an altered or recognizable professional voice is a birth certificate, proof of gender under a different handle, or proof that she cares less because she sounds matter-of-fact. [E02 C0010–0011/0448–0456; E08 C0129–0135; E09 C0162–0170; E11 C0121/0129–0138; E12 C0373–0376.]

Short 03 gives her a revealingly easy assignment. Asked what Takina's peach creation resembles, she says ケツじゃん. Having arrived later than the people who were trying not to laugh, she says plainly what the thing looks like to her. Takina rejects the comparison. There is no need for Kurumi to be a better interpreter of the maker's intention; the plate is available to her as a new observer. In Short 06 she can see the commercial possibilities of a themed café where others find the uniform embarrassing or the idea impractical. Chisato endorses the suggestion, then retracts it; no such business opens. When the ensemble prepares for travel, Kurumi proposes playing board games until morning so that she can sleep on the flight. She still wants other people around a table even when there is neither danger to avoid nor information she can sell. TV E13 does show her with the portable café in Hawaii; the short presents a plan and gives no independent travel date. [Short 03 C0083–0090 and #000188 at 03:10.540, SHORT03-CLM-005/006; Short 06 C0052–0061 and C0067–0075, SHORT06-CLM-004/005; E13 C0484 at 23:54.08.]

Short 04 gives her a much more complicated version of the same part. The person Takina and the audience see beside the monitors is presented as Kurumi. She says that the camera does not lie, and tells Takina to look again when a picture initially suggests a killing. Takina recognizes Mizuki. The replay also shows Chisato attending to Takina's hair and Mika performing deliberately for the camera. Accurate pictures can contain people at play or people bent on misleading whoever will look. The staged scare is explainable. A separate pale figure crosses behind the group. When Chisato asks where Kurumi is, Mika says she went out that morning and still hasn't returned. Takina insists she was there with her; the others dispute that account. The viewer's access to the depicted companion and the visible crossing settles neither the identity of that figure nor the physical presence of the actual Kurumi in the viewing room on the terms Takina remembers. Even her apparent assurance about the camera has to be read inside a story which can withhold who gave it. An accomplice, a further deception and the supernatural joke remain possible without being newly established facts about her powers or whereabouts. [Short 04 C0001–0010, C0033–0054, C0090–0105; #000074/088, #000187–192; SHORT04-CLM-001/003/004/006; S00E04-C06/C07.]

## How the print versions change the terms

In Asaura's *Recovery days*, manga artist Itō has a publication opportunity and a deadline. An item used as a reference discharges in Itō's own hands after Chisato and Takina have each counted on the other's precautions. Her tablet and wall are damaged. Kurumi helps recover the work while making limits on her expertise clear. When the editor reports car trouble, the thought that Kurumi also bought time belongs to Takina. File recovery, an editor's account of a delay and Takina's inference about it have different witnesses. Kurumi appears at the cinema elsewhere in this volume with her own reason for watching a splatter film whose illicit uploads she has taken down. She later goes for pancakes after the group's arcade game and debrief. Duty, a genre taste and an appetite are all sensible answers to why she is there at a particular moment. [A2 *Recovery days* 9.363–387, 10.559–610/637–702, 16.20–40, 18.25–40.]

Asaura's *Gluttony days* makes fear worth lingering on. Kurumi waits while Mika gives Detective Abe coffee and yōkan; Abe has the sense to leave without prying. A payment trail then makes Hikaru Akutsu look threatening. When he reaches toward her she freezes, and Mika intervenes. Hikaru says he was reaching to check what was displayed on her computer. Mika had doubted his intentions and later reproaches himself for having exposed Kurumi to the fright. Hikaru's explanation leaves open what he would actually have done. The alarm and Mika's intervention belong to the encounter itself. She can be perceptive about a transfer of money without feeling safe enough, or knowing enough, to interpret the hand coming her way. This is a particular test supplied by the prose. It gives no reason to make fear of all unfamiliar adults a rule for her anime self. [A2 *Gluttony days* 9.895–909, 10.577–592/615–623/961–975.]

Abe Kanari's *Recollect* makes patronage personal. Café regular Gotō pays for Kurumi's dessert and suggests she could meet people at the existing game night. Kurumi offers her own board games and wants people to play with. Takina demonstrates how the café treats a customer; Mika limits how much introduction will be imposed on his reluctant employee. Buying a dessert, inviting a person and offering one's games are separate acts performed by different people. The arrangement suits this Kurumi because it lets her control the activity through which she will be seen. The later yukata episodes distinguish her interest in the catalogue and the virtual fitting that she supplies from what she is subsequently shown wearing herself. Even the comic's greater attention to voluntary public participation does not permit someone else to presume she has agreed to be displayed on any occasion. [B1 *Recollect* V01 S25–40, S69–78 and S115–122; V02 §10 S17–22, R1-02/05.]

The anthology writers give her sharply different audiences. In 耳式's *クルミ my love*, Takkun has just spoiled other children's sandcastle. Kurumi invokes what his teacher admires about his drawing and clay work, and offers to take him on as her junior. He blushes and declines; Kurumi later refuses the teacher's own request as relayed by Takina. In いみぎむる's “ロボとリス,” Robota helps her reach a high shelf, sends along advice he received from Majima and misjudges the person before him. There is no discovery of her identity. In ikra's arcade story the screen-side arrangement lets a reader misattribute the losses, then gives Robota a single victory and a failed rematch. His return to Majima's car supplies his identity to the reader. The work does not establish what Kurumi knows of her opponent or that either cheated. These credited fictions can give an apparently all-knowing girl the pleasure or the irritation of being mistaken for someone else. None extends their city's record of Walnut's exploits or supplies the missing date of Kurumi's birth. [C1 *Reload 02*, 耳式, S69–78; *Reload 03*, いみぎむる, S12–14, and ikra, S83–90.]

## What to expect of her

Confront Kurumi with a discrepancy and give her a reason to care, and she will usually press past the first explanation. Give her a lead and an interested party who might know more, and even a confession of technical defeat can turn into an invitation to tea. Under accusation she can initially insist on the limits of what she did. A specific person asking for a particular repair has a better claim on her effort than a general demand to feel appropriately sorry. Her gift for misdirection survives her attachment to her friends. She can protect somebody with a fiction as costly to other people's understanding as those used against her. When she offers assurance, ask whether she has established the fact, inferred the likely position, or simply placed a great deal of faith in the person who has gone there. All three are recognizably hers, and Takina knows when the difference matters. [E01 20:46–21:16; E06 14:58–15:29 and 20:42–21:26; E09 09:27–10:13; E11 07:27–07:49 and 09:23–10:03; E12 17:21–20:36.]

Give her a chance to leave and she can take it. What the café has made possible is a return she can choose for reasons strong enough to inconvenience herself and delightfully inconvenient to Mizuki. A rough hello to a regular, a game with more people at the table, a chance to use Walnut's name on behalf of girls whose colleagues once hunted her: these are different ways of belonging. The first greeting in LycoReco still governs the proportions. When Chisato asks who she is now, Kurumi gives an answer true enough to live by and incomplete enough to remain hers. [E02 22:11–22:39; E03 02:46–03:31; E10 02:47–03:23; E11 03:09–03:49; E12 13:33–13:47 and 17:43–18:34; E13 14:17–14:42.]

### Authority and evidence note

The originating boundary is the 13 TV episodes and six shorts, with the separate credited print comparisons identified above.
