# Nishikigi Chisato

On the beach, Inoue Takina tells Chisato that she will live. Chisato takes the Alan pendant on its cord and throws it into the water. She hesitated, she says. In the same breath she explains that Takina had even called it めっちゃかわいい, and she teases Takina for denying she would ever say it (C0431–0439). The audience has learned of a death. Chisato has received a birthday greeting, the returned pendant and Takina’s assurance that she will live. The time the gift buys is hers to spend. The symbol can go. When the two wonder what to do with the future, Takina is the one who offers a starting point. It is hard to explain any one of these gestures without the others. Chisato can grieve a relationship, reject a claim on her, want reassurance that she is loved, and take genuine pleasure in discovering there will be another day. By the time she has that day, quite a few people have made decisions on her behalf. [E13, beach and epilogue, CLM-028–042, VIS-010/011]

Seventeen at her first meeting with the sixteen-year-old Takina (E01 06:19–06:25), Nishikigi Chisato (錦木千束) is the red-uniformed First Lycoris whom Direct Attack trained, and a worker at Café LycoReco, the small public-facing shop she established with Mika, the former DA instructor who named and raised her. Takina knows her by reputation as the Lycoris who defended Tokyo’s old radio tower alone against terrorists, famous even in the regions (E01 C0200–0201). Chisato’s first response to that fame is to deflect it. She was not the one who broke the tower, she says, and it ended up broken anyway (C0199, C0203). Earlier she has explained her posting with 問題児だからだよ, because she is a problem child (C0194). Others turn the legend into a name. Otome Sakura, the Second who has replaced Takina as Fuki’s partner, mocks her as 電波塔のヒーロー様, and Takina laughs at the 電波塔のリコリス who is afraid of injections (E03 C0241; E08 C0439). She grew up in DA’s Lycoris dormitory, where Fuki was once her roommate (E01 C0188), and she speaks of ten years of the same snack as a long acquaintance with the place (E04 C0185). Lycoris girls do the state's clandestine work. The shop serves ordinary people and takes requests alongside its DA business. The same hands can bring a customer tea, hold a hurt stranger still or inflict pain. Chisato has an artificial heart. Her position as a favorite of DA gives her bargaining room that girls trained alongside her do not necessarily possess. When she speaks about freedom, her listener sometimes knows its price better than she does. [E01 06:05–12:33, C0188, C0194–0203; E02-CLM-004, E03-CLM-004–009, E05-CLM-004/015, E08-CLM-005, E10-CLM-020–029]

This portrait follows what the thirteen television episodes and six *Friends are thieves of time* shorts give Chisato to do, know and say. The shorts supply their own encounters and seldom date them precisely. The anime’s final pre-print character baseline, CP5, incorporates the retrospective television and shorts audiovisual rulings. Those rulings specify what the recordings and images can sustain. The later sections turn separately to Asaura’s prose, Abe Kanari’s *Recollect* and the named anthology creators. Someone given Chisato’s thoughts by a novelist has access another character in the television series never had. The spoiler boundary is the complete television series, the six shorts and the identified licensed print works. Nothing here goes beyond the Hawaii epilogue.

In brief, Chisato is a seventeen-year-old elite operative who has chosen to spend her working life helping particular people from a café. She never kills, and she treats time, her own and other people’s, as the good most worth protecting. She opens relationships quickly and on her own terms. She reads bodies, sightlines and situations with exceptional accuracy, and reads motives, Takina’s included, less reliably than she believes. She keeps gratitude, care, trust and obedience as separate judgments, so she can thank a benefactor and refuse his plan for her in the same breath. Her nonkilling is a condition on her force, not a refusal of force. Her rounds hurt badly, and she will hit a hostile third party hard. Her characteristic failure is to protect other people from her own mortality without asking them. She withholds her condition, closes the shop and walks away to die rather than let anyone choose to accompany her. Her characteristic strength is to make another person’s next step possible without requiring that person to share her views. This portrait keeps five kinds of statement apart: what the episodes show her doing, what she says about herself, what another character or a narrator says about her, what this analysis infers, and what no source tells us. The anime (A1) governs. Asaura’s novels (A2) give Chisato narrated thought only where she focalizes. That happens in a few short passages of *Ordinary days* and *Gluttony days* and never in *Recovery days*. Abe Kanari’s *Recollect* (B1) and the anthology contributors (C1) are labelled where used. *Heroine Archive* testimony (P_PARATEXT) is reported as what a named person says. No print source rewrites an anime event.

## The two men who saved a child

The audience first meets Chisato as a girl who likes the quiet before a large city wakes. In the E01 opening a description of peaceful Tokyo gives way to the language of systematically erasing even the existence of danger. Her casual なんだってさ at the end marks the account as something she has heard. She knows that the peace is produced. She also likes the peace, the old tower with its strange, damaged shape, and the people who get up and live here. A person can hear the official story in her mouth and still hear the difference between her pleasure in a city and the authority’s explanation of it. Her café remains a DA branch that depends on DA funding even when she departs from its purposes. Mizuki, the café’s other adult staff member, explains in E07 that the branch cannot survive without its First (C0091–0092). In E02 she says she too would like to go abroad. When Walnut, the hacker who will shortly be renamed Kurumi, invites her along, she answers, with Takina beside her, that the girls have no family register and so cannot get passports (C0176–0178). The Hawaii epilogue returns to the passport question as a joke and leaves the administrative solution unstated. [E01 opening, C0038, CLM-001/013; E02-CLM-004, C0176–0178; E03-CLM-006; E07 C0091–0092; E08-CLM-005; E13 epilogue, C0460–0461]

The history of her heart reaches the audience by installments, and so does Chisato’s understanding of what she was given it for. In E04 she explains why she gives someone time. She dislikes taking it from them (12:32–12:48). Her reason for leaving DA includes a ten-year search for the man connected to her Alan pendant so she can thank him (13:38–14:17). When Takina asks what talent the Alan Institute saw in her, Chisato turns the question back. Does Takina know what her own talent is? Neither girl names one (C0281–0286). Within two minutes the episode cuts to Shinji Yoshimatsu, a café customer she will later learn is her benefactor, naming her a rare genius of killing (C0323). The pattern repeats. In E05, after a client calls her a fine guide, she tries out 才能あるかも～ in Takina’s hearing, and later the same day the fake client tells her what her life was given for (C0185–0186, C0367–0370). In E07 Takina names her bullet-dodging to her face. Chisato calls it a hunch (勘) and redirects it toward being of use to someone. About five minutes later Yoshimatsu tells Mika that the talent is for killing (C0269–0278, C0394). The audience hears the adults’ word for her gift long before she does.

At E05 she talks matter-of-factly to a terminally ill stranger about having a complete artificial heart, and Takina learns of it in that moment. When Takina starts to ask, Chisato calls her away to the tour. Mizuki asks whether she had not told Takina, and Mika says to leave it to Chisato (C0076–0083). The adults treat disclosure of her body as hers to manage. She tells the fake client that she does not want to take people’s lives and wants to help someone, like the person who gave her the heart (C0372–0375). At E07 she can finally address Shinji Yoshimatsu as the giver and speak of using the time she received to help other people (C0357). He begins pressing her to fulfill her role. At that encounter she still has not heard from him a completed, explicit formulation that her ordained work is killing. She also learns Mika protected a secret and can see an intelligible reason for his doing so. The terrorist Majima, set on exposing DA’s hidden violence, has a different experience of Alan, and in E08 it does little to dislodge her particular confidence in Yoshi-san. She tells Majima not to lump her in with him, because she really does help people, and that she will do as she likes (C0350, C0363). On the bench that evening she remarks to Takina that Yoshi-san has not come since (C0417–0418). In E09, just after Kurumi names Yoshimatsu as the likely mastermind, the edit cuts to Chisato fondly recognizing him in a photograph, ホントに ヨシさんだ (C0176–0179). Her willingness to recognize a stranger on first acquaintance coexists with a stubborn preference for the interpretation on which her own life was built. [E04 12:23–14:17, C0281–0286, C0323, CLM-009–012; E05 C0076–0083, C0185–0186, C0367–0375, CLM-004/014–017; E07 C0269–0278, C0357, C0394, CLM-015–021; E08 C0350, C0363, C0417–0418, CLM-015–018; E09 C0176–0179]

Only in E09 do we see what the child was told. Yoshimatsu has arranged a heart for a girl exhibiting remarkable aptitude. He speaks to Mika in the language of a personal promise and calls her their daughter. The adults mean to bring her capacity for killing into the world. To the child he speaks of a great mission and calls himself something like a savior. She seizes on 救世主. She sees a person about to help her and wants to be one too. Later, when Mika gives her a gun from the “savior,” she calls it a gun for helping people. The interpretation is hers before she is old enough to offer an informed objection to the bargain. It is also understandable given the words addressed to her. Mika names her and teaches her. Neither her early family circumstances nor her biological parentage is available to complete his gift of a name. The earlier assault on the old tower made a lasting personal impression on Majima. Years later, she does not remember him as an individual. Others have long had more reason to organize their lives around the legend of the little Lycoris than she has had to preserve their preferred account of who she was. [E08-CLM-015; E09-CLM-019–027; E10-CLM-020/023–027]

The foundation of her refusal to kill is unusually well distinguished from a plausible later influence. The child wants to imitate being saved (E09 C0241–0260). The older girl can say it feels bad to take someone’s time. The girl newly told she has about two months can speak about what she can still do. Later she tells Majima she wants to survive in somebody’s memory. These moments make mortality important at several ages without proving a single private origin for every act of mercy. A child making sense of her benefactor is already capable of authoring a meaning that her adult benefactor did not intend. When she learns her prognosis in E09 she says it was never going to be that long anyway (C0055), so the expectation of a short life predates the sabotage. The print sources add words on mortality without supplying a different origin. *Ordinary days* has Mika say that she treasures every minute, with no cause named (10.492–494, discussed below). In *Recollect* V02 she links her heart to her old indifference to her health (S116–117, B1, discussed below), consistent with E09 C0055. Neither passage reaches back to the child of E09. [E04-CLM-007/010/011; E09 C0055, C0241–0260, CLM-003/009/025–027/036–038; E13-CLM-013–015; *Ordinary days* 10.492–494; *Recollect* V02 S116–117, R2-14]

## The other side of “enemy”

At the E01 warehouse Erika, held at gunpoint, calls on her teammates to fire regardless, 構わず撃って (C0068), and Takina saves her by exposing her to grave danger. Chisato is on the outside stairs while it happens (C0067, C0075). Her first evaluative word to Takina at the café is admiration, この前のアレ すごかったねえ, followed by a question about the bruise (C0164–0165). A minute later, on the phone to Fuki, she objects that Fuki had no need to hit Takina and tells her to think for herself instead of repeating the commander (C0168, C0171). It is her first statement of a value the series keeps returning to, and she makes it in Takina’s presence and on the side of Takina’s disobedience. Chisato later asks what Takina wanted. When Takina offers an account of the most rational action, Chisato supplies the motive that her new partner is unwilling to celebrate. Saving a comrade took courage, 仲間を救った かっこいいって (C0347). That って, like the ones in E03, may report someone else’s words as well as her own view. She can distinguish a commendable purpose from an objectionable distribution of risk. The distinction returns with the civilian Saori, whose stalking complaint Detective Abe has brought to the café. Takina sends Saori ahead alone as bait so that her pursuers will show themselves, trusting her own marksmanship to keep the danger acceptable (E01-CLM-008). Chisato fights the attackers with her nonlethal rounds and sends Takina to Saori, たきな 沙保里さんを！ (C0478). Saori has not been given even Erika’s chance to say whether she accepts the risk. Competence matters to Chisato, but an outcome that happens to spare a life does not settle whether someone else was entitled to gamble with it. Afterwards she has Saori’s attackers taken away by a cleaner rather than handed to DA, because DA would kill them, ＤＡに渡したら殺されちゃうでしょ (C0524–0527). [E01 C0067–0075, C0164–0171, C0333–0347, C0478, C0524–0527; E01-CLM-006/008/009/011; REV-E01-003; TV audiovisual return Q01, Q15]

E02 makes the cost of her rule legible. An injured pursuer needs help during a protection job. Chisato stops, putting the party at risk and bringing Takina into a disagreement about the client to whom they already owe something. Takina’s point is a serious one. DA has authorized lethal force, they are working together, and taking care of one person can jeopardize others. Chisato works from the preventable death in front of her. The adversary subsequently warns her of another danger. That response is a realization of the future her decision preserved. She could not count on it when she stopped. Later she describes these people as enemies *this time*, 今回は敵だっただけ. Her care neither requires friendship beforehand nor purchases it afterward. The episode also gives her a limiting judgment about herself. She admits that a difficult task is better left to Takina and yields it to her. A celebrated fighter who is at ease saying she lacks confidence in a particular thing makes an unusually good partner in that moment. [E02 09:53–15:52 and late café exchange, C0401, CLM-005–007/011/012]

In E04 Takina gets Chisato’s account of the pain she is willing to cause. Chisato dislikes being made to feel bad by a bad person and is willing to hit them hard enough to hurt as long as they live. She treats Takina’s training as a capacity that can be directed toward a different result. Neither bodily harmlessness nor a generalized recoil from violence explains her conduct. The test in E05 is more exacting than a statement about ammunition. A seemingly dying client wants to see Tokyo. Chisato takes the wish seriously and sets out to give him a day worth living. When he invokes his murdered family and asks for revenge against Jin, the hired gunman, she refuses even though she has already used substantial force to stop Jin. The grievance still appears possible when she refuses. Its later exposure as a fraud cannot supply her earlier motive. She is willing to spend her time on another person’s last wish until its fulfillment would take a third person’s future. When she learns of the impersonation, Chisato thinks even the client’s praise of her work as a guide was a lie. Takina says she thinks Chisato really was a good guide. Chisato thanks her. The invented client no longer gets the last word on what she did that day, partly because Chisato can hear what her partner saw. [E04 12:32–12:48, CLM-001/010; E05 16:22–19:09, CLM-003/013–018, and 21:58–22:11, C0418–0421]

Her exceptional ability in action can conceal how much that rule costs and how easily it can fail. E03 establishes a perceptual skill under readable conditions. E06 shows her physically vulnerable when the relevant information is lost; Takina has to intervene. She is also careless with the routine care that protects her body. In E03 she has put off her health check and fitness test until the last day, because the trip so far into the mountains is a chore, ダルいし (C0073–0076). In E06 Takina has to relay the message from Yamagishi, Chisato’s doctor, that Chisato missed the monthly check-up the day before (C0017–0020). Later that day, under a known threat to Lycoris working alone, she declines Takina’s escort twice with ああ 大丈夫, tells Takina she is looking forward to her dinner again (C0293), and goes out alone on the delivery during which Majima ambushes her (C0281–0294). In E08, walking home, she tells Takina that it is not the check-up she dislikes. She is afraid of injections, because she cannot dodge them (C0430–0438). Minutes later, inside the clinic, she explains to the nurse, in fact Yoshimatsu’s agent Himegama in disguise, that she dislikes having something put into her body (C0446–0448). A feared injection in a place she has reason to trust becomes the means through which Himegama deprives her of her former medical future. What is frightening to an expert fighter need not be what frightens a less experienced person. Earlier in the same episode Takina has found ways to contain the cost of Chisato’s work. Chisato accepts an operational constraint and wants to know whether Takina approves. The café’s finances and her partner’s willingness to help sustain an ethic for which she is personally famous. [E03 C0073–0076, CLM-015; E06 C0017–0020, C0281–0294, CLM-017/018; E08 C0430–0438, C0446–0448, CLM-007/008/026–029; TV audit recheck, E08 addressee; TV Q06]

## What the rule permits, and what it costs

Chisato’s nonkilling is best stated as a condition she places on her own force. Nobody dies by her hand if she can stop them another way. It is not a refusal to hurt. Her E04 account, above, promises a bad person pain short of death, and Takina laughs because she had expected a more philanthropic reason (C0254–0260). On the first night Takina recognizes the ammunition before she asks whether 命 大事に includes enemies (E01 C0477, C0483). The red frangible rounds lose accuracy at range, so Chisato fights close and relies on reading where the shot will go. In E06 she keeps firing them while Majima taunts her with live rounds and injures her. In E11 she uses decisive force on the way up the tower while warning an opponent about a fatal fall. [E01 C0477–0483; E04 C0254–0262, CLM-001; E06-CLM-017/018, CHI-MB-E06-004; E11 CHI-MB-E11-001]

The novels make the cost concrete, and they do it through other people’s eyes. *Recovery days* never enters Chisato’s mind. In its story *Dog* the man she was hired to protect sees her work. Her rounds leave Kōsuke Mizukawa alive but vomiting blood with broken ribs (RD 11.329, 11.353, 11.408). Before she explains the commission, she shoots the threatening Ijūin in the side of the head, kicks him onto his back and shoots him again in the solar plexus without hesitation (11.360–379). Her stated objection to revenge is practical first. A killing would widen the trouble and make Mizukawa a target, which his boss wanted to avoid (11.392). She tells Takina that she need not shoot, after Takina’s live round has already grazed the man they were hired to protect (11.229, 11.241–253, 11.326). In *Ordinary days* Takina focalizes the action. She judges that Chisato never kills but goes to the edge of it without mercy, as when she shoots a downed man in the nape (OD 12.61–62). Asked why she does not use live rounds, Chisato answers 私は殺さないやり方がいーの, promises いつかわかるよ, and hands the job of disabling people with live rounds to Takina as their division of labor (12.77–95). After Takina’s rounds have maimed Bulldog’s knife hand, Chisato tells him gently, in English, that he must not die, promises him good coffee, and then empties a magazine into his mask (12.197–217). Her stated reason for pursuing the fugitive is operational. A live capture with little collateral harm is what makes the pair valuable to this commission, and the narration marks it as one of her reasons, not all of them (12.233–244). No waking scene in either novel gives her personal reason. Dream Chisato says she does not want to kill people and wants to stay herself to the end, but she speaks inside a dream that Takina focalizes, and her lines are not Chisato’s testimony (16.165–175).

The rule also allocates risk. In both novels Takina carries the lethal-class rounds. When Takina asks for calmer conduct in case of a mistake, Chisato answers that Takina will be there (OD 12.73–74). In *Recollect* V02 Chisato says training for accuracy is pointless when she never uses live rounds in real combat. In a smaller panel she admits that with Takina around she may be leaning on her a little (S8–10, B1). That is self-report in a comic register, and it states openly what the novels show. Her method depends on a partner whose method is different. Abe also tests the rule’s scope. Her rule, as she states it, concerns people. When Takina asks whether 命を大事に applies to a suspected café pest, Chisato deliberates visibly before conceding やむを得ん, then adds that putting it outside would be fine if possible (V02 S95–97). The supposed pest turns out to be Kurumi’s modified commercial robot, built to spray insecticide, and no animal is killed (S101–102). The scene shows her hesitation at the edge of the rule, not a practice either way.

E12 is the hardest test, and its record stays open where the picture is silent. The account of the tower below keeps apart the attribution of the shot, her unestablished intention and a director’s later moral summary. The portrait that follows from all this is less gentle than her slogan suggests. She will cause severe pain and hit a hostile third party hard. She will ask a partner to carry the lethal option. In the one instance where the edit points to her shooting the man she is trying to save, her intention remains open. No source depicts her deliberately killing anyone.

## Acquiring a partner

At the café in E01 Takina introduces herself by full name and explains the order that brought her. Chisato interrupts her formal greeting, calls her たきな, tells her to drop さん from *Chisato*, notices the bruise on her face and calls her 相棒, partner. She decides how close the conversation is going to sound before she knows whether Takina wants that closeness. The conspicuous musical length of 千束で～す and the clipped formality that meets it belong to two performers shaping a single exchange. At the end of the episode Chisato draws the café into a group photograph and posts it. It is her initiative. The evidence gives no comprehensive account of everyone’s wishes about publication. On the train in E02, after Takina initially declines some food, Chisato makes a more intimate invitation to take a bite. She can be delightful, persistent and a little presumptuous in the same few seconds. With Walnut, however, interrupting an introduction bridges an important loss. A professional alias has just “died”. The name Kurumi receives a greeting and an invitation to share dango. Kurumi’s first job for the café comes from Chisato in the same minute. She is to find the smaller man at the back of Saori’s photograph, the buyer whose trail is Takina’s route back to DA (C0433–0434). Takina, for her part, keeps the honorific Chisato told her to drop. She says 千束さん through E01, E02 and most of E03 (E02 C0210; E03 C0272). [E01 06:05–07:01, 22:00–22:43, C0161–0165, CHI-MB-E01-002/009; E02 05:55–06:11, 22:13–22:36, C0433–0434, CHI-MB-E02-002/006; TV Q03; TV audit recheck, address forms]

E03 finds the limit of immediately welcoming somebody. Chisato does not yet understand what Takina wants. Asked by Fuki why she brought her, she agrees that Takina wants to come back and wonders aloud why anyone would want this place (C0149). In the commander’s office she spends one of her harshest registers of the early episodes on Takina’s behalf. She tells Sakura to shut up, 黙れ 小僧, objects to being called “this”, and snaps 楠木さん！ and おい！ as Kusunoki, the DA commander, denies any promise of reinstatement and Sakura tells Takina to give up (C0224–0240). After the commander declines to restore Takina and Fuki tells her plainly she is no longer needed, Chisato points to all the people in town who need her. Takina says, in effect, that this is easy to say when DA still needs Chisato. It is the first thing the viewer needs to know about Chisato’s way of helping people who want what she herself could walk away from. Chisato listens through the accusation. She distinguishes Takina’s choice to help Erika from what DA decided to tell the rest of the organization, then brings the argument back to one personal consequence. They would never have met otherwise. She draws a surprised Takina into an embrace and says she is glad they met. Chisato initiates the contact. Takina is pictured receiving it. Chisato offers the time they might spend with the people at the café *as a trial*. Takina can still go back if that is where she wants to be. Then Chisato states her own rule, that she always puts what she wants to do first and fails often because of it, and names what she wants now. She wants to thrash the people who said cruel things to Takina, and she goes off to do it (C0323–0326). Her first worked example of doing what she wants is a partisan defence of her partner in a sanctioned fight. Two of her maxims in this scene end in って, which may mark words she received from someone else or may simply be emphatic (C0304, C0314). The record cannot settle which, so the question stays open. In the mock battle she covers Takina’s absence as resting the ace (C0352). Fuki says Takina is not someone who fights outside a mission, and Chisato tells her she cannot read people (C0353–0355). On the train home Takina calls her 非常識な人, an outrageous person, and for the first time says plain 千束 (C0462). She has used 千束さん for the last time that afternoon in the dorm (C0272), and she never uses it again. Chisato says that it still felt good, でも スカッとしたな, and Takina answers ええ (C0463–0464). That ええ, Takina’s first acknowledged pleasure in the series, is the measured answer that lies close to the music entry (TV Q05). Chisato’s question about the game night, どうする～？, comes after the music has entered and gets no spoken answer. Only the next picture shows Takina joining a game she had refused twice the evening before (C0057, C0067–0069, C0469). The episode has shown a voluntary next step, with the question of a permanent change of allegiance still ahead. [E03 07:52, 11:24–11:57, 13:21–15:58 and 22:08–22:45, C0149, C0224–0240, C0272–0326, C0352–0355, C0462–0469, CLM-003/008–011/016; TV Q05; TV audit recheck, E03 C0353 and address forms]

E04 shows Takina learning how to get a straight answer. At the aquarium, where Chisato holds an annual pass and offers the place to Takina if she likes it (C0216–0219), Takina asks whether there is a reason for the special rounds Mika made her. Chisato deflects twice. Is Takina interested in her? More than in a garden eel? Takina says that if Chisato is going to tease, never mind, ちゃかすなら もういいです, and Chisato answers seriously at once (C0244–0251). After the punitive explanation Takina calls her 謎だらけ, full of mysteries, and Chisato plays the mysterious girl (C0259–0262). Teasing is one of the ways Chisato decides how much of herself a conversation will reach. She uses it again when Takina asks what LilyBell do and Chisato asks whether she is interested in boys (E06 C0206–0209), and when she calls Takina away from her first question about the heart (E05 C0076–0079). Takina’s refusal in E04 is the first time the deflection fails, and Chisato yields. The same off-duty day shows the other side of her forwardness. She helps a French-speaking couple with the café menu unasked (C0197–0204). She checks Takina’s underwear after Kurumi has told her that what Takina wears is Takina’s business (C0061). She stops Takina from intervening in a station incident because out of uniform they are not Lycoris (CHI-MB-E04-003). [E04 C0061, C0197–0204, C0216–0219, C0244–0262; TV audit recheck, E04 C0247–0248]

E05 gives Chisato a bodily boundary of her own. When Takina wants to verify the absence of a heartbeat, Chisato objects to where she is trying it. Later Takina points out that nobody else is there and leans against Chisato’s chest, with her permission. Curiosity survives a refusal about place. ## When the person who needs rescuing has harmed her

After the damage to her heart, Chisato refuses revenge against Himegama. Killing the woman would change nothing about the prognosis. The claim concerns what that particular act could accomplish. She pursues practical help and continues ordinary work. Kusunoki tries to claim some of her remaining life for DA. Chisato asks for a real possibility for Takina and retains the right to decide what her own role will be. Toward the shop she is equally decisive. She closes LycoReco and encourages the others to take their time elsewhere. She wants the place to remain a happy one to the end. In the increasingly empty café, she also admits to Mika that she is lonely. Some customers turn up and object to losing the place in which *they* had made lives. She is pleased to hear it. Chisato is often good at imagining how a stranger might use another afternoon. Here she has gone some way toward deciding what counts as its best use for people who love her. She even assigns the destinations. She cheers Mizuki’s plan to meet a man in Vancouver, and when Kurumi says she must leave the country, Chisato tells her to go to Germany, home of the board games Kurumi loves. Kurumi offers to make her a passport and take her along. Chisato calls that an idea, then declines because Sensei would be lonely, and asks Kurumi to invite Takina someday (E10 C0033–0049). The reason she gives is Mika’s loneliness, not her own prognosis. [E09 C0057, CLM-006–017; E10 C0033–0049, C0155–0159, CLM-002–007/016–018; E10-VIS-002, approx. 08:05–08:50]

Mika brings out a box. In the place of the weapon she imagines, it contains her 晴れ着, the formal clothes he would have liked to see her wear at her coming of age. She dresses while there is still time and poses while Mika photographs her. When he answers that the picture came out well, she corrects him. She wants *his* opinion (C0268–0271). When he insists he has done little for her, her answer is concrete. He gave her a name, taught her to shoot, made the shop possible, let her meet Takina and, above all, searched for Yoshi-san for her sake (C0278–0280). Then she asks him to send the photograph to Yoshi-san (C0281). It is her last unqualified gesture toward her benefactor, and it is what breaks Mika: そうじゃないんだ！ (C0282). He tells her about the bargain and his involvement in it. His story changes what she knows about both men. It also makes room for a new act of trust on her part. She says that, had she known earlier, she might have done the work sullenly and blamed the adults for everything she hated. Instead, she chose her work and her shop. That counterfactual belongs to Chisato. It does not prove that withholding the truth from a child was the only way she could have become herself. She still calls both men her fathers. Then she asks, この千束はどう？ 好き？, asking Mika if he likes this Chisato, the person who actually came to exist. He answers that she is his pride as a daughter (C0336–0343). The most self-determining person in the room is the one who needs to hear those words. [E10 13:13–18:10, C0268–0343, VIS-003 as downgraded, CLM-019–030; TV Q10; TV audit recheck, E10 C0268–0271]

## Who can follow her

Majima asks for the right to interpret her. To him, the extraordinary child he faced at the tower remains an opponent who made him feel fear. For her he is also the unwanted visitor who turns out to like *Die Hard*. They can talk about Powell and the last scene over drinks. She rejects his inclusive 俺ら and does not remember him in the way he remembers her. Twice she refuses his comparison in the same words, 一緒にすんな. In E08 she adds that she really does help people, and in E11 she says it while still fighting him (E08 C0350; E11 C0414). In E11 his objections to other people imposing a purpose on them sound uncomfortably like her own. In E13 he calls himself a protector of the world, the person on the weaker side of any imbalance, even if someday that means helping DA. She recognizes he also believes himself in the right. Self-chosen purpose alone would license both of them. He forces civilians into his public experiment. She asks whom she can help and imagines the possibility of remaining in that particular person’s memory. The same capacity for freely deciding on a course of action leaves people with radically different obligations toward those caught in it. [E08-CLM-013–018; E11-CLM-028–033; E13-CLM-007–017]

The show puts the two briefly beside one another facing the city, as well as opposite one another in conflict. She offers him her drink, and when he finds it too sweet she tells him that he will be an old man before he has reshaped the world to his liking, おじいさんに なっちゃうぞ, and says there are plenty of things she loves as it is (C0180–0185). Unprompted, she lists the quiet before a big city starts to move, the shop she made with Sensei, the smell of coffee, customers, the people of the town, good food and beautiful places, companions and an earnest friend. That, she says, is all of her, and she does not care what happens to the world (C0185–0195). Majima calls it petty and asks whether she has no aspiration. Her answer to *that* is the being-needed statement (C0196–0200). To be needed in this sense means finding someone for whom she can do something, with no assurance that doing it will put the whole world right. It also has a selfish hope folded into it. Perhaps that person will remember her after she is gone. She can puncture his demand that the duel decide the true hero with a practical joke about who avoids falling from the building. Her mortality humor holds under the same pressure. Mid-fight she tells her failing heart to hold on a little longer, もうちょっと頑張んなさい (C0100). When Majima says he is her last client, she agrees that this really is the worst of it, クソッたれ (C0203). Felled, and told he is disappointed in her, she answers that one piece of junk is plenty, ポンコツは１つで十分なんだよ (C0224). The action nonetheless puts her in real need. Takina returns, catches her and saves her bodily. She receives what she has spent a lifetime insisting others are entitled to receive. Majima survives too. Early in the fight he tells her she is not as good a shot as 黒いほう, “the dark one”; he means Takina, and the line says nothing about which ammunition Chisato is carrying, which the scene does not settle (C0082). [E13 C0082, C0100, C0180–0203, C0224, VIS-003, CLM-009–023; TV Q19; REV-E13-015]

Her strongest failure of reciprocity happens after she wakes alone and in pain following surgery. Believing this means she is going to die, she leaves to find a good place while she can. She dislikes a damp, mournful farewell, 湿っぽいの やだし (C0390–0396). Analytically, this extends the one reason she gave in E09 for silence: she did not want Takina to start treating her differently (E09 C0077). Here she does not want anyone’s grief at all. She has previously given Takina the option of going or staying and Mika the chance to be heard. Here she does not give either of them a choice about accompanying her. She is taken aback that her friends found her. Saori’s civilian photograph supplies the clue beyond the reach of the usual technical search. The young woman whose safety Chisato took seriously in E01 has helped make her reachable when she would rather disappear. When they meet in the island forest, it is Takina who visibly fires first, toward a rustle in the trees; the later shots are obscured, and who fired them, with what, is not shown. Takina presses her objection until the chase gives way to the beach conversation. The friend who was once invited into a new life now insists on seeing the inviter, challenges her reasoning and gives Chisato information about her own future. In the middle of that reckoning Chisato points at the sea and says that this hour of the day is the one she loves best, この時間が 一番好き (C0397–0398). [E01, Saori sequence; E09 C0077; E13 16:05–16:24, C0386–0406, CLM-025–030/035–041; TV Q14]

What changes at the beach is the task she can set herself. She no longer has to arrange a goodbye in the time her heart has left. Takina suggests beginning with what Chisato had given up. Chisato can turn that thought into a destination, and the epilogue locates their mobile café in Hawaii. Kusunoki still calls with work and Chisato can still decline it. When Takina asks who called, Chisato says it was a fan of hers, 千束のファンから～ (C0463–0464). The residue of her habit of managing what Takina knows survives into the epilogue, in a play register. Earlier she bargained with Kusunoki for Takina’s option to return to DA. In E12 she helps defend the exposed Lycoris. Mizuki credits the older negotiation that stopped the threat to Chisato to **Mika and Kusunoki**, in talks with their superiors. Chisato names the ensuing obligation to help DA (E12 C0227–0231). The later withdrawal order comes from Itadori, the senior DA official who had ordered the exposed Lycoris disposed of (C0041–0043, C0421). Chisato does not institute transparent government in the process of recovering her life. When the fans in Short 06 drift close to the truth about the uniform, it is Chisato who interrupts Takina's accurate explanation and presents the issued uniform as a custom-made costume. Three enthusiastic viewers hear excellent role-play. One friend has learned to expose the limits of Chisato's preferred stories, while Chisato can still be very good at supplying an official fiction to someone else. Takina says as much, これが隠ぺいです (S06 C019). Mizuki once said the opposite of Chisato, that she is a bad actor, which is why the adults kept her uninformed about the staged death in E02 so that her relief would be real (E02 C0383–0387). The two judgments fit together. Chisato improvises a cover well when she chooses to, and performs badly when she does not know she is in a performance. ## Ordinary appetites and social power

Off duty in E04, Chisato insists on eating well because a lifetime contains only so many meals. If the dessert creates an extra fitness cost, she is willing to do the running. She also refuses to treat every disorder she sees out of uniform as a summons to exercise extrajudicial power. Pleasure need not be either an incentive for service or a reward for surviving it. Loving the morning quiet, the peculiar tower, a familiar film or the food that belongs to a train journey is already an answer to what there is to do with a life. E13’s world-in-miniature speech is credible because the audience has spent time with the things she names. The anime gives her a small, consistent set of tastes. She keeps an annual pass to the aquarium (E04 C0216–0219). She finds DA’s karinto boring after ten years, while the newcomer Takina likes it (E04 C0183–0185). She makes her own printed itinerary for a client’s Tokyo day, which Kurumi offers to send as data (E05 C0056–0057). She would like to go abroad (E02 C0175–0176). Her favourite time is the quiet before a big city wakes and, she says on the beach, the sunset hour (E01 00:48; E13 C0186, C0398). Films run through everything, from *Die Hard* with Majima to the shorts’ plans for Hawaii. Some of these are stable preferences, repeated across episodes and sources: films, food that belongs to an occasion, the morning quiet. Others, such as the guidebook, are choices made for a particular person. [E01 00:48 and CLM-013; E02 05:15–06:11, C0175–0176; E04 09:13, 09:24–09:37, 11:17–11:22, 11:58–12:48 and 18:45, CLM-007/008; E05 C0056–0057; E08-CLM-013; E13 C0186, C0398, CLM-013–015]

Ordinary life offers plenty of occasions on which her first explanation is wrong. At the hanami gathering she initially reads Takina’s proposal in terms of enjoying the blossoms. Takina supplies her own appreciably commercial reason. Later in S01 Chisato finds Takina armed for a festival day and chases her to take the weapon away. The danger, she says, is Takina herself, 危ないのはお前だ (C042). The same person can correct a partner’s judgment on one point and have hers corrected on another. In S02 Chisato imagines Waikiki, a photograph and a film to watch before the trip. When Kusunoki calls with a job, Chisato mimics her and goes on reading the guidebook, and she asks whether it is all right that she will do the job without killing, 殺さないですよぉ？ いいんですかぁ？; the commander’s answer is not given (C036). Takina’s private admission of excitement then turns travel from Chisato’s sales pitch into a desire they can share on different terms. The short ends with a robber overpowered in the closed café while Chisato greets him with the café’s welcome, 喫茶リコリコへようこそ～ (C064–075). The Hawaii plans in S02 and S06 do not show that every short takes place after the television finale. [SHORT01-CLM-002–006/014–017; SHORT02-CLM-001–006, C035–41/054–68; shorts SR01/SX01]

In Short 03, the strongest objection to making something pleasurable comes from the person who has to balance the books. Takina is trying to invent a dessert. Chisato first offers the familiar brown coil of Takina’s withdrawn E08 parfait, and when Takina refuses to go back there, teases that people find it hard to leave a past success (S03 C019–021). Chisato notices a word she recognizes from a film and asks her to say it again. A recipe has become a way of recognizing what someone did on her own. She supplies ideas from an old menu, including one Mizuki recalls was dropped for costing too much, while Takina credits Mika with realizing them. When Takina unveils her dish, Chisato and Mizuki, who have been handed drinks, spit them out at its look, and Kurumi says it looks like a backside; nobody is shown tasting it (C065–090). Chisato wants it sold anyway, because it is very funny, めっちゃ面白いのに, and Takina refuses over the cost on the receipt (C077–081). Short 04 is built around a camera recording. On it Chisato brushes Takina’s hair. E08 and the shorts make a full working portrait possible. Chisato delights in making something interesting and then in watching another person’s face. She can also claim attention by persistence, fiction or physical contact before the listener has decided whether to take part. ## Directed differences: Fuki, the café and the commander

Chisato behaves differently with each person around her, and the differences are consistent. With **Fuki**, her former roommate and, in Fuki’s narrated memory, the partner who left her (*Recovery days* 13.806), she drops every courtesy. They trade insults across the mock-battle floor and over a police sketch, and she can still lean on Fuki quietly, asking her in E12 to keep Kurumi out of Kusunoki’s knowledge because explaining would be a bother (E03 C0354–0361; E07 C0011–0020; E12 C0436–0438). The novels give Fuki’s side. In *Gluttony days* Fuki seeks her out to learn monjayaki. Chisato herself brings up a bunk-bed accident, and when Fuki says Chisato was the one who threw her off the bed, she answers そうだっけ？ (12.201–203); Fuki’s narrated view puts it ten years back (12.211). She also uses Fuki’s summons to escape a neighborhood cleanup (12.318–337). In *Recovery days* she teases Fuki on a rooftop and then repeats an offer of help without her bravado (13.759–829). Asaura says that early in development Chisato was close to Fuki in her sense of justice, and that Fuki had to be the responsible one beside her and was left behind when she left (*Heroine Archive* S136–137/p134–135). That is creator intention, not an A1 event. With **Mizuki** she is a sparring younger sister; Mizuki is the one who calls her a bad actor (E02, above). In *Ordinary days* Mizuki calls her too strict for waiting to be asked before helping Kana. In *Gluttony days* Chisato sells Mizuki out as her fellow wolf at Werewolf in order to win (E02 C0383–0387; OD 18.896–899; GD 9.2–88). Mizuki’s actor, Koshimizu Ami, says Chisato treats Mizuki like a badly made older sister and lets her words pass (*Heroine Archive* S111/p109). With **Kurumi** she gives a name, a first job, a destination and cover, and she also presses Kurumi into café service against her refusal (E02 C0433–0434; E10 C0041–0049; *Recollect* V01 S27–30). In *Recovery days* she games with Kurumi into the morning while supposedly ill (12.697–706). With **Kusunoki** she is a subordinate who bargains. She protests on Takina’s behalf, refuses the commander’s definition of her role, trades her own cooperation for Takina’s option to return, and in Hawaii simply declines the job (E03 C0237; E09 C0108–0112; E13 C0460–0462). The assistant director Maruyama reads the two as a mother and a rebellious daughter (S142/p140). With **customers and strangers** she is warm and forward. She helps the French couple, seats Kana and takes her free voucher without a question before asking for her order (OD 18.835–855), and promises Bulldog his coffee. She also misreads what Takina’s interest in the customer Doi means. Across these relationships the constant is that Chisato decides how close a conversation will be. What varies is how much room the other person has to answer back. Fuki and Takina use it. Kurumi and the customers often meet her initiative after it has already begun. ## What the Japanese performance lets another person hear

Chisato need not announce a mood for the audience to hear whom she is addressing. Chika Anzai gives the girl who stretches her own name at a first meeting a markedly different conversational task from the one who asks Mika if he likes her. With Takina, Chisato often occupies the space that would have allowed a formal answer to finish. In S03 Chisato asks Takina to repeat イノベーション, guesses correctly that she has watched *The Social Network*, then announces what her own Chisato Zuckerberg will do. She has heard a film in Takina’s choice of words and gives the two of them an exchange Takina could not have had with the screen. Elsewhere she drops an honorific, casts herself in an extravagant café welcome, or speaks for a superior on the telephone. In these exchanges Shion Wakayama gives Takina ways to participate without imitating Chisato’s manner. She can supply a dry correction, hold back a laugh, confess her own excitement in two phrases, or return a challenge. In S02 the extended beat between 私も and 浮かれてますから leaves room for us and for Chisato to discover who else is excited. The 2.17 seconds belong to the interval between captions. Mixed sound continues there. Chisato laughs, exclaims よっしゃー！ and starts planning how quickly they can finish the job and get ready for Hawaii. That is her answer to Takina. The explicitly marked echo belongs to her much later ホアチャ～!! In the TV cast conversation Wakayama describes Takina’s part as mostly returning what Chisato throws. Anzai hears a slightly Chisato-like nuance in Takina’s E06 「つ、つまらない？」, and Wakayama says Takina grew more Chisato-like from there. Those are the performers’ accounts of their work, not evidence of what Takina intends. [E01 06:05–07:01; SHORT02 C014–18/058–63/080, SR01 and shorts music register; SHORT03 C020–035; *Heroine Archive* S105/p103 (Wakayama on Takina’s part as returning Chisato’s lines), S106/p104 and S109/p107 (Anzai on the E06 line; Wakayama on Takina growing more Chisato-like; Adachi’s remark actor-reported)]

She also speaks *against* her usual invitation. The pleased ようこそ offered to a newcomer, her teasing of Fuki, and the grateful directness of ありがとう to Mika or Takina each distribute attention differently. Her 違う in E12 interrupts the benefactor who claims he acts for her. She calls him ヨシさん even when she says she dislikes him. Looking after his wound, anger at how carelessly he treats his life, the return of his pendant and an acknowledgment of what she owes all survive in the same address. With Fuki she trades challenges, and still works with her to identify Majima, first by arguing over whose sketch looks like him and then over the footage Fuki brings to the café (E07 C0011–0020, 10:21–11:37; E07-CLM-010). In E10 she can let the group go in broadly buoyant language and admit a little later, to her teacher, やっぱりさみしいですよ. Her ordinary first person 私 can place ownership where it belongs, as when she insists her work and shop were her choices. In the E13 beach exchange she turns Takina’s own めっちゃかわいい back on her (20:14–20:35). In E04 Takina had said “that” need not be hidden after Chisato’s hand went to her exposed pendant (14:46–14:54). The pendant is the strongest referent, and the exchange need not be a compliment on Chisato’s looks. The reference and the register matter more than a list of signature particles. [E07 C0011–0020; E10 C0159, AUD-006/007, CLM-016–030; E12 C0159–0166, CLM-017–027; E13 C0431–0439, CLM-038/039]

Her self-reference is unusually theatrical. Her ordinary first person is 私. In play or self-presentation she often names herself in the third person: 千束さん when she announces the snack time she has been waiting for or offers to pick Takina’s winter clothes (E04 C0165; E09 C0321), 千束流 for her way of enjoying trouble (E09 C0337), この千束 when she asks Mika whether he likes her (E10 C0341), 千束の相棒 and 千束のファン in E13 (C0370, C0464). *Recollect* V01 repeats the 千束さん habit (S14, B1). In a role she can slip into アタシ (*Recovery days* 10.46–48; *Recollect* V01 S120). Her terms of address map her relationships. Takina is plain たきな from the first minute, with mock deference when it suits her, たきな様 (E02 C0129). In the novels she becomes “our Takina”: うちのたきな when Chisato interrupts a duel in *Gluttony days* (10.564), and うちのたきなさん when she defends Takina to Fuki in *Recovery days* (13.771). Takina is 相棒 from E01 to the beach in E13, 行くぞ 相棒！ (C0445), and in *Gluttony days* the word covers Fuki as well, 私の相棒はこんなんばっかかよ (13.40). Mika is 先生, and in *Ordinary days* a regular notices that only Chisato calls him so (9.95). Kusunoki is 楠木さん, used for appeal and protest alike (E03 C0237). Her peers get their bare given names. Fuki is フキ (E03 C0251; E07 C0017, C0208), with a mock-polite フキさん when Chisato teases her (E03 C0127). Mizuki is ミズキ (E05 C0081, C0336), and Kurumi is クルミ from the moment Chisato approves the name (E02 C0455; E04 C0056; E08 C0123). Majima, in the one place she calls him by name, is a shouted bare surname, 真島！ (E11 C0363). Her registers range from long, sung vowels and mock formality in play, through stock phrases and English (おまかせあれ, OD 12.214; ワイハ, Short 02 C063; イエス・アイ・ドゥ, GD 14.482), to the clipped contempt she gives Robota and the street-fight bluster she trades with Fuki, てめ～ どこ中だ (E11 C0026; E03 C0358). Under mortal pressure she jokes (E09 C0090; E13 C0100, C0224). Under betrayal she turns sardonic (E12 C0083). When a conversation comes too close, she teases or leaves, until someone refuses the tease (E04, above).

The novels let a reader compare her outward speech with narrated thought, but only in a few passages. *Ordinary days* focalizes her at 10.178–182, 10.549–553, 10.584–591, 14.53–65 and 14.176–183. *Gluttony days* does so only in After Meal 2 and the final course (13.6–44, 17.2–54). *Recovery days* never does. In those passages, described below, her interior voice is quicker and more self-aware than her speech: a private resolve not to regret, an ignorance of Russian tea admitted only to the reader, a performer’s irritation over her monjayaki (OD 10.591; GD 17.23, 13.6–7). The gap is small and goes in one direction. What she presents outwardly as easy confidence can cover private uncertainty. In these few passages she is never shown privately thinking something cruel or contemptuous that she hides, though they are too few and too light to rule it out elsewhere. Written register is not performed voice. Asaura himself says that Anzai’s voice completes the anime Chisato and that she is too non-logical to reproduce exactly in prose (*Heroine Archive* S136/p134). The prose Chisato is a related realization, not a transcript.

Measured loudness in the full mix fits these differences without explaining them. Chisato’s E01 greeting runs louder than Takina’s formal reply (about −20.4 against −26.1 dBFS), and in E12 her objection to treating a life carelessly runs louder than her claim to have things more important than the world (about −22.1 against −30.0). Such figures describe the mix around a caption, not sincerity or a vocal habit. The credited *Heroine Archive* conversation at S106/p104 offers an actor’s account of how her work was made. Wakayama recalls that her first take of E09’s last line did not quite sit right (腑に落ちない), so she asked the director about its background and recorded it again. Anzai, and by the interviewer’s report Adachi, do not remember this; Anzai puts it down to their memories, and Wakayama allows it may be an illusion that still moved her most. The editors head the passage 第9話の収録で見た幻（？）. Anzai’s own choice of a favourite line is the E08 bench remark that Yoshi-san has not come since, which she hears as weak-spirited for Chisato and thinks would not have come out unless the partner were Takina (S106/p104). That is an actor’s interpretation, not Chisato’s motive. The episode itself still determines which line and image its audience receives. ## What changes when print grants another person the point of view

**Asaura’s prose.** Takina focalizes most of all three novels. Chisato is usually seen from outside, by Takina, a customer, a target or a friend, and the few passages inside her head are named where they occur. In *Ordinary days*, the regular Doi finds a reason to come out, walk, eat with others and take an interest in a future he had imagined closed. Chisato’s gift for connecting people is part of the pleasure. Her plan has two aims from the start: to advance what she takes to be Takina’s feeling and to give Doi something new that he will credit to Takina (10.266). Chisato’s theory starts from her own paraphrase of Takina’s question and hardens when Takina smiles, even though Kurumi raises the obvious possibility of asking Takina directly. She reports that Takina “let slip” an interest in Doi. Takina had in fact asked about his gloomy face, and Chisato’s next sentence concedes it sounded more like worry (10.90–128). Her ground is a rule about Takina: in her experience Takina takes an interest only in people with skills worth learning or in strong enemies (10.203). Her model for what should happen next comes from dating games (10.561–562). Takina’s own account, given to Mizuki and Kurumi, is that she disliked him for his low spend per head, his long hold on a seat and his gloom, and that his first order of sweets changed her mind. She disowns the outings as Chisato’s doing (10.616–636). Kurumi keeps that to herself, and Chisato’s wink three weeks later implies the theory still stands (10.641–643, 10.686, inferred). An adult encouraged to understand her concern as attraction asks the teenager to dinner. Although he sees the self-interest in his attempted justification, he goes ahead. The scene ends before her reply. Chisato’s actions help someone participate in life and expose a coworker to an unwanted inference. Being good at making an occasion is a poor guarantee of knowing whom a smile is for. The prose supplies the viewpoints needed to see both effects. Later the regular Tokuda walks in on what looks like the aftermath: Doi laughing strangely, Chisato prostrate and Takina irritated. The positions show which way the joke went without recovering Takina’s words (10.690, 19.7–22). [*Ordinary days* 10.90–128, 10.203, 10.211–213, 10.266, 10.335–355, 10.369–419, 10.525–599, 10.614–690, 19.7–22; OD-02, OD-09, OD-10]

Kana’s experience in the same book puts the case for hospitality at its strongest. Other people have claimed authority over her identity and body. When money is taken from her, the prospect of being unable to afford the modest coffee that gives her a seat at LycoReco feels like losing a life she could choose. She experiences Chisato as someone who takes genuine pleasure in her friendship. On the night Kana comes back, Chisato is alone at the café in her red uniform. She seats Kana at the counter and accepts the crumpled free voucher without a question about the late hour or the state Kana is in. She brews by siphon, passes on Mika’s saying that good coffee holds magic, and serves the cup from beside Kana, not across the counter (18.835–845). Then she explains that the voucher covers a coffee *set*, so one more order is allowed, anything at all, and asks ご注文は？ (18.850–855). The words for a request as dangerous as asking for help thus carry something of the ordinary ease of ordering. Kana asks, 助けて, reaches back into the embrace and says yes (18.858–875). Whether she recognized Kana in an alley on the night of the tea gathering is not said (18.396–411). Who fired the round that took Kana’s gun, and who attached the coffee voucher to her phone, are not shown (18.800–837). Yet before the request the café had already taken protective measures. The rule of acting only on request is the adults’ account of her. Mika calls the waiting her calculation, 千束なりの計算, and says she needs a rule or her helping would have no limit. Mizuki states its content, help only when asked; Kurumi calls it realism and Mizuki calls Chisato too strict. Chisato is absent from that conversation and never states the rule herself (18.892, 18.895–899; OD-16). Mizuki objects to how long they waited while Kana remained exposed. Chisato cannot receive general prior authorization from Kana’s later decision, any more than an observer can deduce from the voucher Kana finds exactly when and how it reached her phone. The same novel lets her speak over Takina and make it possible for Kana to speak. The interlocutor makes all the difference. [*Ordinary days* 17.73–91, 18.97–145, 18.279–407, 18.480–512, 18.800–910; OD-02/06/16]

*Ordinary days* also gives Chisato’s own narrated reasons about time. Regret wastes it, so having blurted out everything to Doi at once, she decides not to regret it: それが錦木千束という生き方なのだ (10.582–591). The meals left are numbered, nobody knows how many, and the protein shake Takina once served as lunch is a memory she will keep until she dies (14.175–183). Mika tells Doi that she treasures every minute and has had her share of trouble, and the book names no cause (10.492–494). With Mika himself she argues the opposite case for Doi. Twenty years to an average lifespan is plenty, she says, and Mika opened the café only ten years ago, badly at first (10.159–165). When he falters over telling her she will understand when she is older, she answers with a finger gun and a stage line. In her narrated thought, he used to look at her while seeing something else as well, and now he looks at her alone, which she credits to their time together, 少なくとも、千束はそう思っている (10.167–182; OD-08). The passage withholds what else he used to see. Her habits around danger are her own as well. Back from a night job she leaves the restocking Takina does first, because she does not want to live constantly conscious of combat and trusts one magazine in an emergency (10.549–553). Her first decision on a raid is about time. She refuses Kurumi’s offer to sacrifice a drone, first on cost and then with 時間の話だよ, because she wants to watch a zombie film with Takina before opening (12.30–36, 12.88–105). With Takina’s cooking, her private diagnosis is that Takina is too earnest to find a point where something is good enough, and that by the stated requirements the protein shake was correct (14.53–65). When Takina dismisses her own misshapen rice balls with どうせ, Chisato blocks the word three times and praises the taste, not the look (14.295, 14.359–376; OD-17). [*Ordinary days* 10.159–182, 10.492–494, 10.549–553, 10.582–591, 12.30–36, 12.88–105, 14.53–65, 14.175–183, 14.295–376; OD-07, OD-08, OD-13, OD-17]

Films and pictures are where her wishes for herself show most plainly in that book. She has just bought the *Alien* box set and says owning a disc makes a film one’s own and easy to lend (15.59–64). Takina, who once watched the loans with notes as if for a drill, still calls them a nuisance (15.66–72). Chisato wants zombie films lit well enough to see, and Takina calls that greed (12.301–306). Chisato wanted the café in a magazine feature for its business and for her own 人生の満足度, and when Mika refused she secured a photo shoot of herself and Takina at other cafés instead. She asked the writer whether the pictures made it in and calls the printed spread a treasure (9.120–135, 18.19–22, 19.39–46, 22.25, 22.32; OD-18). In the Bonus Track she imagines strangers of the distant future finding her picture in the National Diet Library and says simply that it would make her happy. Takina replies that they will certainly be dead by then (22.2–32). Whether that wish is ordinary vanity or something more, the book does not say, and the question stays open (22.11–16). A separate candid of Chisato feeding Takina pancake was taken by the photographer and printed, and nobody is recorded consenting to its publication (22.38–56, 19.69).

*Recovery days* contains no passage focalized through Chisato. Everything the reader learns of her motives there comes through her speech, her actions or other people’s readings, mostly Takina’s. In the story *Dog* she offers another possible interpretation of a dead man’s unfinished words to someone prepared to commit violence for him. Mizukawa last heard Okada invite him to eat ramen. His own boss heard part of something else, and Chisato relays the boss’s thought that Okada might have wanted Mizukawa stopped. She acknowledges that nobody can know, then expresses her conviction that the “owner” wanted his “dog” to be happy. She refuses to certify the words of the dead, yet lends her certainty to a claim about affection. Her practical objection to revenge and the force she has already used on Mizukawa and on Ijūin are set out above (11.353–392). Only after she leaves does Mizukawa recall what Okada taught him about living and face the boss who offers him a return home. The boss inflicts violence of his own. Chisato opened an alternative to revenge. She neither authored the man’s decision nor redeemed the organization to which he returned.

The rest of the book watches her at leisure and in bed. She argues Takina into the cinema with a claim to more than ten years of film-loving life, the principle that a film should be seen when one wants to see it because nobody knows what will happen, and the case for two hours given to one film and talked over afterwards (9.41, 9.144–149, 9.403–408). She books her favourite seats, in the middle and slightly forward, and when a job interrupts she tells Takina the deadline is the film (9.157–162, 9.237–240). She proposes that they try together the things Takina has never done, starting with a public bath (9.282–287). In Itō’s flat she outclasses Takina while modelling a scene, supplies the rescue beat for Itō’s plot, and with Takina makes the book’s most consequential mistake. Each assumed the other had cleared the chamber of a rifle taken on that night’s job, and it discharges in Itō’s hands. Takina first blames Chisato, who says she thought Takina had done it, then takes the larger share herself. Chisato acknowledges her own omission and turns to relief that nobody was hurt (10.244–291, 10.396–415, 10.559–590; RD-07, RD-12). She then pats the stunned artist to sleep with “it was all a dream” (10.572–578). Later an actual fever, recovery, late gaming with Kurumi and an attempt to fake another sick day put less flattering pressures on the host who wants to be cared for. She insists that infection is something everyone must deal with together (12.103), tells Takina that being worried about is pleasant (12.664), and lobbies for a spice-curry lunch and the next morning’s red rice (12.206–249). Mika says she has always had a hearty appetite (12.331). Takina suspects a motive for having been sent home, and Chisato gives another. Takina grants that only Chisato knows her intention (12.703–704). The narration identifies the later deception while leaving that earlier intention private. Several things stay open: what Mika means by her 万が一 (12.32), whether her flush when Takina sniffs her hair is fever or embarrassment (12.121–137), and what the *Crank* sequel means to her (12.187). At the end of the night in *One’s duties*, Chisato reports to Fuki that Kusunoki sent them although Chisato had said Fuki would not need them. She jokes that she wanted to see Fuki cry, defends うちのたきなさん when Fuki tells the sick Takina to go to bed, and repeats the offer of a ride without her bravado when Fuki refuses (13.759–829). [*Recovery days* 9.41–162, 9.237–240, 9.282–287, 9.403–408, 10.244–291, 10.396–415, 10.559–590, 11.53–83, 11.373–425, 12.32, 12.103–137, 12.147–155, 12.187, 12.206–249, 12.331, 12.662–724, 13.759–829; RD-01/03/04/07/08/12/13]

In *Gluttony days*, Takina again focalizes the job, and Chisato’s reading of it reaches the reader chiefly through her own speech and Takina’s hedged guesses. Her protection of the client rests on the girl’s ordinary vulnerability, not the contract. A girl alone in a strange place would be in danger, death threat or no (9.471). She tells Clarisse she thinks her father would come for her because they are father and daughter (9.480). At the ramen counter she orders a smaller serving for the frightened girl (9.511–517). Takina suspects she used the job as a pretext to try a shop she lacked the courage to visit alone, and Chisato denies it after long pauses (9.526–535). In Clarisse’s hearing she explains the man tailing them as a father’s concern for an only daughter, 親心. It was a hypothesis she held sincerely, and it was wrong: the tail was Madame’s partner, and she later admits she really had thought him Akutsu’s (9.716–722, 10.270–303, 10.844). When the tail asks whether they are guards, it is Chisato who confirms it, after Takina has cut her off once to protect the cover (9.729–734). Chisato’s warning that Takina may be in danger prompts the client’s confession at Deme’s house. Her staged threat to Clarisse follows and is aimed at Madame’s contractual duty to the client (10.572, 10.645, 10.710–712, 10.729–731). Whether Clarisse agreed to the staging is not stated. It is Clarisse who commissioned the attack, cancels it and says she paid. Madame takes her word. The child says the guards were kind and she feared Takina might be hurt. Takina’s thought that a shared struggle with ramen brought them together carries a different authority. Chisato’s voice, arriving with the smell of garlic, is what dulls Takina’s intent to kill. She tells Madame that “our Takina”, うちのたきな, is a hundred times prettier (10.561–570). At the back door earlier she had said 命、大事に without saying whose life she meant (10.203–207). She wipes Takina’s nosebleed with her own handkerchief, tells her there is nothing to apologize for and praises her effort (10.745–751). When Clarisse apologizes, Chisato lets Takina answer. Takina’s reassurance and Clarisse’s hug are theirs to give. Chisato pats Takina’s head, and Takina privately wants to brush the hand away (10.797–819). By her own account Chisato first doubted the official story when Madame’s opening shots went at the guards, and the truth reached her through Clarisse’s confession. That she saw through the day first is Takina’s verdict (10.850–861, 10.1008–1009; GD-07). Later, Chisato argues for the father’s care partly through the meaning of the girl’s name. Takina objects that etymology cannot establish his present behavior. Chisato answers with the proverb 噓も方便 and keeps only the etymology. The concession is implied, and its deliberateness rests on Takina’s reading of her face (10.900–921). Mika calls her broadly right, and he keeps the park’s timing from Clarisse (10.925–931, 10.946–948). The photograph shows Clarisse embracing her father, who booked the park **after** the emergency. It cannot prove she believed his account of having arranged the trip in advance. Chisato gives three reasons for not going: Takina was at the hospital, she would not intrude on a family’s time, and Takina had worked hardest. She would like a future outing when Takina can go too, and Takina is not displeased (10.935–960). She can be generous about an occasion. She can also misread a situation sincerely, as with the tail, and separately frame a father’s hidden love she cannot prove, conceding the framing with 噓も方便 when challenged. Akutsu and Mika keep convenient stories of their own. [*Gluttony days* 9.471–558, 9.716–734, 10.203–207, 10.549–573, 10.647–819, 10.844–960, 10.1008–1009; GD-01/02/07/08/14]

A less dangerous encounter in that novel is equally important. Chisato asks to dry Takina’s hair, receives two refusals and persists physically and verbally. Takina hands over the dryer because continued refusal seems troublesome. She finds the handling surprisingly gentle and becomes embarrassed. The drying stops when Chisato raises the question of food, and Takina finishes the job herself. Later she dries Chisato’s hair; Chisato praises their unspoken understanding and Takina says the cue was quite obvious. The body being attended to is the focalizer’s. The reader can know it felt different from doing the job herself without erasing her initial refusal. The prerecorded hair care in Short 04 supplies an anime image of intimacy, while this novel supplies interiority for *this prose encounter*. The latter cannot tell us how Takina silently felt in the former. A different sequel to an invitation in *Recovery days* has Takina realizing that she has been maneuvered into choosing the next film. She admits to herself that she enjoyed the day and is a little galled by it, then interrupts Chisato’s explicit offer of an exit to accept another outing on a condition (18.11–40). Takina is certain that Chisato knows she has noticed the steer, and at such moments she sees Chisato as an adult and herself as a child (18.20–22, 18.34–37; RD-13). The knowledge is assigned to Takina’s certainty, not to Chisato’s mind. Chisato’s delight in eliciting a yes is best read along with the actual terms in which her friend gives it. [*Gluttony days* 14.139–179, 14.218–276, GD-05; *Recovery days* 18.2–40, RD-01, RD-13; SHORT04-CLM-003]

The two short *Gluttony days* pieces told from Chisato’s side are domestic comedy, and they show what she enjoys when nothing is at stake. Serving monjayaki at the café, she is annoyed that the adults look away from her practised dike, and her greatest pleasure is seeing people go back for a second and third bite (13.6–7, 13.35). Takina’s remark about calories makes her imagine, as a joke, that Takina has been swapped for a mechanical double (13.16). When Takina asks the same practical question about the dike that Fuki asked, Chisato sighs, 私の相棒はこんなんばっかかよ (13.39–40). In the final course she admits to the reader that she does not know how Russian tea is served, guesses right about the flavor, and wonders whether parents give children more food because they understand how simply food gives a share of happiness. That is her surmise about Mika’s generosity, not his stated reason (17.17–54). She resolves to diet, and Takina offers to join her, reversing the earlier scene in which Takina resolved and Chisato offered (15.44–47, 17.54). Elsewhere in the book, outside her focalization, she keeps her hair short because long hair is a problem when dodging bullets, says she longs for hair long enough to braid, and brightens her tone when Takina has no reply (14.126–137). She argues that a life has only so many meals (14.196). She tells Takina that as a daughter she finds an overindulgent parent ideal, and calls Mika 先生 with a heart (10.1000–1005). **Abe Kanari’s *Recollect*.** Abe can draw a gesture from outside, let the next panel disclose its consequence to the reader, and keep its motive from the other girl. In V01 Chisato casts the café uniform as a Lycoris’s equipment and the smile as its weapon (S11). Takina has been performing the work without that smile, and she will only *treat* the reasoning as understood (S12). Chisato eventually admits she simply wants to see it, does not deny Takina’s charge of selfishness, and Takina says *she* smiles when she is enjoying herself (S12–13). Chisato turns that condition into a project of films and manga, and calls herself 千束さん as she does it (S14). Later, offered films for pleasure, Takina assumes enough work remains to justify skipping the credits. The panels show her absorbed. Her later explanation supplies the assumption about workload. Chisato assigned no written report or quota and insists that a film lasts until its credits end (S50–52). In the rain wager Chisato herself calls involving an unasked customer very rude and names Takina’s company as her reward (S88–91). At the pool she lets a child hit her and dodges every shot from Takina (S110–111). She also forbids Takina from teaching the children grip and aim (S109), chose the watermelon gift and Takina’s clothes for the day alone and in advance, and concedes the melon was a wrong choice when it turns out the nursery has no knife (S104, S112). Her rule for the day is that full effort in small things is the secret of enjoying life, and Takina says that is very like her (S106). She notices small changes in people, Takina’s shampoo and the hand in which Kurumi holds her spoon (S21, S36). She presses the uniform on Kurumi and forces open her closet door for the café’s cover (S27–30). In V02 the sofa images invert the problem. Chisato leaves to avoid seeing an intimate screen moment, and finds the image waiting when she returns. Takina says she paused as soon as Chisato went out, and accepts Chisato’s flat praise as a matter of course, 当然です (S66). Chisato’s own aside, away from Takina, names the awkwardness of watching the scene with her (S65). No parallel evidence grants a private motive to Takina for pressing pause, and whether Takina suspected why Chisato left is not lettered. Trying to make someone smile, misreading an assignment and wishing to avoid an image are different situations in which her expression fails to direct her audience. **Other licensed manga (C1).** Four small experiments by different contributors give unusually specific answers to the question of who may do what with the wish Chisato elicits. In GUNP’s *Repeat* she proposes a hug and offers a general rationale about soothing. Takina opens her arms; Chisato checks and Takina confirms. ## How she notices and decides

Chisato perceives bodies and objects with great precision. She dodges by reading the line and timing of fire. She beats Takina at rock-paper-scissors by reading the muscles before the throw. She loses much of that advantage when sight is degraded, which Majima exploits in the dark (E03-CLM-015; CHI-MB-E06-002/005, E11-003). She sees the anomaly in Saori’s photograph, the change in Takina’s shampoo and the hand Kurumi holds her spoon in (E01-CLM-006; *Recollect* V01 S21, S36). In *Recovery days* she reads Mizukawa’s bent sight from the angle between his gaze and his muzzle (11.303). In *Gluttony days* she infers the jammer from when communications came back (10.779). Mika says that if her eyes saw it, it was there (*Recollect* V02 S98). Her readings of motive are less reliable. She takes Takina’s concern for a customer as romance and a man tailing them as a father’s love, and in each case another person holds the correction (OD 10.90–128, 10.616–636; GD 9.716–722, 10.844). Her later argument from Clarisse’s name is different: a knowing framing she concedes with 噓も方便, not a misreading (GD 10.900–921). She does not know Takina has been treating her films as training until Takina says so (*Recollect* V01 S50–52). She is never shown testing such a theory by asking. Kurumi proposes the direct question once and nobody asks it (OD 10.338).

Her reasoning runs through particular people and finite time. She answers questions of principle with a concrete case: what this person wanted, what killing this woman would change, how many meals are left (E01 C0333–0347; E09 C0057; E04 C0254–0258). She keeps separate judgments separate: gratitude from obedience, rescue from agreement, care from trust (E10 C0333–0336; E11-CLM-021–032; E12 C0159–0166). Faced with what she cannot control, she accepts it and spends effort on what remains, 受け入れて 全力！ (E09 C0368). When obligations conflict, she usually chooses the person in front of her and accepts the cost. With the bleeding pursuer in E02 the cost is shared, since her stop puts the whole party at risk. With Yoshimatsu in E10–E11 she bears it herself. When the cost falls mainly on others she is less consistent. She lets Takina carry the lethal option, decides alone to close the shop and to disappear, and leaves her friends to settle the question of consent after the fact. Someone can argue her out of a position, but only by refusing her framing outright. Takina does it with ちゃかすなら, with her objection to the etymology, and on the beach (E04 C0248; GD 10.900–921; E13 C0386–0406).

## What changes, and what was only disclosed

Asaura says Chisato was fixed early as an already complete character who does not grow in the story, while Takina’s role was to grow into a partner who could help her in a pinch (*Heroine Archive* S136–137/p134–135, creator intention). The episodes largely bear this out for her values. Nonkilling, the preference for particular people over abstract causes, and the claim to author her own life are present in E01–E04 and unchanged in E13. Much of what looks like development is late revelation. The child’s reading of 救世主 in E09 explains a rule the audience has watched since E01. Mika’s confession in E10 changes what she knows, not what she values.

Three things do change. The first is her knowledge. Across E04–E12 she moves from searching for an unknown benefactor, to thanking him, to learning what he wanted her for and what Mika agreed to, to refusing him face to face. She ends knowing less than the audience about his death. The second is her relation to being helped. In E05 she keeps Takina’s questions about her heart at a distance. In E09 she says she did not want Takina to treat her differently. In E13 she disappears rather than be mourned. She ends the series found, caught and told about her own future by the person she tried to spare, and she accepts it. The epilogue’s joke about a fan’s call shows that the habit of managing what Takina knows has not vanished. The third is her horizon. Through E09–E13 she plans for an ending. On the beach she is asked what she wants to do next and takes up Takina’s suggestion to begin with what she had given up. The relationship with Takina changes in its balance more than its warmth. Chisato offers closeness from the first minute. What develops is Takina’s capacity to set terms on it, from ちゃかすなら in E04 to 嫌だ 千束が死ぬのは嫌だ in E12, and Chisato’s acceptance of those terms. *Recollect* V02’s bonus, B1-local, is the only source that shows her thinking about a long life, and it does so in a comic register while she eats a second snack (S116–118).

The profile should abstain where the sources do. It cannot say what she would do on learning the full account of Mika’s act, whether she would ever choose a killing to save many others, what adult life she would build, or whether she has romantic feelings she has not stated. It cannot supply her private reasons in *Recovery days*, and it cannot treat any dream line as hers.

## The reach of a portrait

When another person needs time, Chisato commonly converts her idea of help into something they can actually do, such as sharing food, going out or trying a different place. For an adversary it can mean surviving to make another choice. If they answer differently from what she expected, ask whose judgment she must revise. With Takina at DA she changes her offer and leaves another option intact. With Fuki in play she can persist beyond refusal. With a hostile stranger bleeding in front of her she will incur an immediate cost without the assurance of a return. With the father who has tried to own her future she can insist that he live and still reject what he wants her to become. With a caregiver who knows part of her childhood she can seek both the truth and a declaration that she is loved. The direction of disclosure changes once she is the person others wish to accompany in dying. Avoiding their grief becomes a reason to choose for them, and Takina has to compel another conversation. These claims have strongest support where another voice can answer hers. They become weaker wherever a gift or silence is allowed to speak for an absent person.

Several questions stay open, and the portrait should not close them. In A1, her intention in the E12 shot is unresolved. What she knows about Yoshimatsu’s death after E13 is unknown; Adachi’s suggestion that she may have noticed is a creator’s possibility. Two lines once given to her are not hers on present evidence. At the daycare in E01, the line introducing Takina to the children as their new friend たきなおねえちゃん (C0237) plays over Chisato and Takina but may be the daycare worker’s, and it awaits a listener. 狂ってる in E12 (C0115) leans toward Takina. Whether her maxims ending in って in E03 repeat someone else’s words is undecided. In the novels, what she understood at the back door when she said 命、大事に, and whose life she meant, is unknown (GD 10.203–214). So are her reason, while she herself was ill, for sending Takina home, the meaning of Mika’s 万が一, and what the *Crank* sequel means to her (RD 12.32, 12.187, 12.702–705). Whether she recognized Kana in the alley is not said (OD 18.396–411). All dream content, in *Ordinary days* and *Recovery days* alike, belongs to Takina-focalized dreams of open ownership. There are rival readings worth keeping. Her nonkilling can be read as mercy toward other people’s time, as a child’s imitation of the rescue she believed she received, or as a condition of staying herself (E04 C0250–0251; E09 C0241–0260; E12 C0186), and the sources support pieces of each without ranking them. The last rests on her E12 words about one extreme case; the novels’ version of it is spoken only by a dream figure. Her forwardness can be read as generosity or as a habit of deciding for others. The evidence shows both, and the second is clearest when the matter is her own death. Extrapolation beyond these sources, to her adult life, to how she would behave under a different heart, or to a romantic outcome, is speculation and should be labelled so.

Chisato says she wants to be remembered by people she has helped. She also wants to remain responsible for whom she becomes. It is also clear that she can disappoint, pressure, injure and be injured. The source withholds her response to a complete account of Mika’s actions, the eventual course of her new heart, her settled adult ambitions, and any definitive declaration about the romantic future of her partnership. An exceptionally self-directed girl still has to learn some things about herself from other people. The final chance Takina gives her is wonderfully ordinary in that respect. She has to decide what she wants to do tomorrow.
