# Nishikigi Chisato

On the beach, Inoue Takina tells Chisato that she will live. Chisato takes an Alan token attached to a cord and throws it into the water. She hesitated, she says. The audience has learned of a death. Chisato has received a birthday greeting, a token and Takina’s assurance that she will live. The time the gift buys is hers to spend. The symbol can go. A few moments later she recalls Takina calling something “extremely cute” and teases her for denying she would say it, and when the two wonder what to do with the future Takina is the one who offers a starting point. It is hard to explain any one of these gestures without the others. Chisato can grieve a relationship, reject a claim on her, want reassurance that she is loved, and take genuine pleasure in discovering there will be another day. By the time she has that day, quite a few people have made decisions on her behalf. [E13, beach and epilogue, CLM-028–042, VIS-010/011]

Seventeen at her first meeting with the sixteen-year-old Takina (E01 06:19–06:25), Chisato is the red-uniformed First Lycoris whom Direct Attack trained, the much-admired survivor of Tokyo’s old radio-tower incident, and a worker at Café LycoReco, the small public-facing shop she and Mika established. Lycoris girls do the state's clandestine work. The shop serves ordinary people and takes requests alongside its DA business. The same hands can bring a customer tea, hold a hurt stranger still or inflict pain. Chisato has an artificial heart. Her position as a favorite of DA gives her bargaining room that girls trained alongside her do not necessarily possess. When she speaks about freedom, her listener sometimes knows its price better than she does. [E01 06:05–12:33, E02-CLM-004, E03-CLM-004–009, E05-CLM-004/015, E08-CLM-005, E10-CLM-020–029]

This portrait follows what the thirteen television episodes and six *Friends are thieves of time* shorts give Chisato to do, know and say. The shorts supply their own encounters and seldom date them precisely. The anime’s final pre-print character baseline, CP5, incorporates the retrospective television and shorts audiovisual rulings. Those rulings specify what the recordings and images can sustain. The final sections turn separately to Asaura’s prose, Abe Kanari’s *Recollect* and the named anthology creators. Someone given Chisato’s thoughts by a novelist has access another character in the television series never had.

## The two men who saved a child

The audience first meets Chisato as a girl who likes the quiet before a large city wakes. In the E01 opening a description of peaceful Tokyo gives way to the language of systematically erasing even the existence of danger. Her casual なんだってさ at the end marks the account as something she has heard. She knows that the peace is produced. She also likes the peace, the old tower with its strange, damaged shape, and the people who get up and live here. A person can hear the official story in her mouth and still hear the difference between her pleasure in a city and the authority’s explanation of it. Her café remains entangled with DA for resources and licenses even when she departs from its purposes. She tells Takina in E02 that they have no ordinary family-register entry from which to get passports. The Hawaii epilogue returns to the passport question as a joke and leaves the administrative solution unstated. [E01 opening, CLM-010/013; E02-CLM-004; E03-CLM-006; E08-CLM-005; E13 epilogue]

The history of her heart reaches the audience by installments. In E04 she explains why she gives someone time. She dislikes taking it from them. Her reason for leaving DA includes a personal search for the man connected to her Alan pendant so she can thank him. At E05 she can talk matter-of-factly about having a complete artificial heart. She wants to become the sort of benefactor she believes gave her life. At E07 she can finally address Shinji Yoshimatsu as the giver and speak of using the time she received to help other people. He begins pressing her to fulfill her role. At that encounter she still has not heard from him a completed, explicit formulation that her ordained work is killing. She also learns Mika protected a secret and can see an intelligible reason for his doing so. Majima’s different experience of Alan in E08 does little to dislodge her particular confidence in Yoshi-san. Her willingness to recognize a stranger on first acquaintance coexists with a stubborn preference for the interpretation on which her own life was built. [E04 12:23–13:30, CLM-009–012; E05-CLM-004/014–017; E07-CLM-015–021; E08-CLM-015–018]

Only in E09 do we see what the child was told. Yoshimatsu has arranged a heart for a girl exhibiting remarkable aptitude. He speaks to Mika in the language of a personal promise and calls her their daughter. The adults mean to bring her capacity for killing into the world. To the child he speaks of a great mission and calls himself something like a savior. She seizes on 救世主. She sees a person about to help her and wants to be one too. Later, when Mika gives her a gun from the “savior,” she calls it a gun for helping people. The interpretation is hers before she is old enough to offer an informed objection to the bargain. It is also understandable given the words addressed to her. Mika names her and teaches her. Neither her early family circumstances nor her biological parentage is available to complete his gift of a name. The earlier assault on the old tower made a lasting personal impression on Majima. Years later, she does not remember him as an individual. Others have long had more reason to organize their lives around the legend of the little Lycoris than she has had to preserve their preferred account of who she was. [E08-CLM-015; E09-CLM-019–027; E10-CLM-020/023–027]

The foundation of her refusal to kill is unusually well distinguished from a plausible later influence. The child wants to imitate being saved. The older girl can say it feels bad to take someone’s time. The girl newly told she has about two months can speak about what she can still do. Later she tells Majima she wants to survive in somebody’s memory. These moments make mortality important at several ages without proving a single private origin for every act of mercy. A child making sense of her benefactor is already capable of authoring a meaning that her adult benefactor did not intend. [E04-CLM-007/010/011; E09-CLM-003/009/025–027/036–038; E13-CLM-013–015]

## The other side of “enemy”

At the E01 warehouse Erika calls for help, and Takina saves her by exposing her to grave danger. Chisato later asks what Takina wanted. When Takina offers an account of the most rational action, Chisato supplies the motive that her new partner is unwilling to celebrate. Saving a comrade took courage. She can distinguish a commendable purpose from an objectionable distribution of risk. The distinction returns with the civilian Saori. Takina thinks the accuracy of her judgment makes the danger she imposes tolerable. Saori has not been given even Erika’s chance to ask for help. Competence matters to Chisato, but an outcome that happens to spare a life does not settle whether someone else was entitled to gamble with it. She avoids a DA handoff precisely because she expects the adversaries to die. In E01 the visual record locates her changes of position across distinct shots. It cannot recover every movement that took her there. [E01-CLM-006/009/011; REV-E01-003; TV audiovisual return Q01, Q15]

E02 makes the cost of her rule legible. An injured pursuer needs help during a protection job. Chisato stops, putting the party at risk and bringing Takina into a disagreement about the client to whom they already owe something. Takina’s point is a serious one. DA has authorized lethal force, they are working together, and taking care of one person can jeopardize others. Chisato works from the preventable death in front of her. The adversary subsequently warns her of another danger. That response is a realization of the future her decision preserved. She could not count on it when she stopped. Later she describes these people as enemies *this time*, 今回は敵だっただけ. Her care neither requires friendship beforehand nor purchases it afterward. The episode also gives her a limiting judgment about herself. She admits that a difficult task is better left to Takina and yields it to her. A celebrated fighter who is at ease saying she lacks confidence in a particular thing makes an unusually good partner in that moment. [E02 09:53–15:52 and late café exchange, CLM-005–007/011/012]

In E04 Takina gets Chisato’s account of the pain she is willing to cause. Chisato dislikes being made to feel bad by a bad person and is willing to hit them hard enough to hurt as long as they live. She treats Takina’s training as a capacity that can be directed toward a different result. Neither bodily harmlessness nor a generalized recoil from violence explains her conduct. The test in E05 is more exacting than a statement about ammunition. A seemingly dying client wants to see Tokyo. Chisato takes the wish seriously and sets out to give him a day worth living. When he invokes his murdered family and asks for revenge against Jin, she refuses even though she has already used substantial force to stop him. The grievance still appears possible when she refuses. Its later exposure as a fraud cannot supply her earlier motive. She is willing to spend her time on another person’s last wish until its fulfillment would take a third person’s future. When she learns of the impersonation, Chisato thinks even the client’s praise of her work as a guide was a lie. Takina says she thinks Chisato really was a good guide. Chisato thanks her. The invented client no longer gets the last word on what she did that day, partly because Chisato can hear what her partner saw. [E04 12:32–12:48, CLM-001/010; E05 16:22–19:09, CLM-003/013–018, and 21:58–22:11, C0418–0421]

Her exceptional ability in action can conceal how much that rule costs and how easily it can fail. E03 establishes a perceptual skill under readable conditions. E06 shows her physically vulnerable when the relevant information is lost; Takina has to intervene. In E08 a feared injection in a place she has reason to trust becomes the means through which Himegama deprives her of her former medical future. What is frightening to an expert fighter need not be what frightens a less experienced person. Earlier in the same episode Takina has found ways to contain the cost of Chisato’s work. Chisato accepts an operational constraint and wants to know whether Takina approves. The café’s finances and her partner’s willingness to help sustain an ethic for which she is personally famous. The sound that accompanies distress in E08 and E13 has a close pitched resemblance. That resemblance cannot identify a shared medical mechanism. [E03-CLM-015; E06-CLM-017/018; E08-CLM-007/008/026–029; TV Q06/Q18]

## Acquiring a partner

At the café in E01 Takina introduces herself by full name and explains the order that brought her. Chisato interrupts her formal greeting, calls her たきな, tells her to drop さん from *Chisato*, notices the bruise on her face and calls her 相棒, partner. She decides how close the conversation is going to sound before she knows whether Takina wants that closeness. The conspicuous musical length of 千束で～す and the clipped formality that meets it belong to two performers shaping a single exchange. At the end of the episode Chisato draws the café into a group photograph and posts it. It is her initiative. The evidence gives no comprehensive account of everyone’s wishes about publication. On the train in E02, after Takina initially declines some food, Chisato makes a more intimate invitation to take a bite. She can be delightful, persistent and a little presumptuous in the same few seconds. With Walnut, however, interrupting an introduction bridges an important loss. A professional alias has just “died”. The name Kurumi receives a greeting and an invitation to share dango. [E01 06:05–07:01, 22:00–22:43, CHI-MB-E01-002/009; E02 05:55–06:11, 22:13–22:36, CHI-MB-E02-002/006; TV Q03]

E03 finds the limit of immediately welcoming somebody. After the commander declines to restore Takina and Fuki tells her plainly she is no longer needed, Chisato points to all the people in town who need her. Takina says, in effect, that this is easy to say when DA still needs Chisato. It is the first thing the viewer needs to know about Chisato’s way of helping people who want what she herself could walk away from. Chisato listens through the accusation. She distinguishes Takina’s choice to help Erika from what DA decided to tell the rest of the organization, then brings the argument back to one personal consequence. They would never have met otherwise. She draws a surprised Takina into an embrace and says she is glad they met. Chisato initiates the contact. Takina is pictured receiving it. Chisato offers the time they might spend with the people at the café *as a trial*. Takina can still go back if that is where she wants to be. The day ends with Takina accepting the activity she had refused that morning. Her measured answer and the music entry lie very close together. The episode has shown a voluntary next step, with the question of a permanent change of allegiance still ahead. [E03 13:21–15:52 and 22:15–22:45, CLM-003/008–011/016; TV Q05]

E05 gives Chisato a bodily boundary of her own. When Takina wants to verify the absence of a heartbeat, Chisato objects to where she is trying it. Later Takina points out that nobody else is there and leans against Chisato’s chest, with her permission. Curiosity survives a refusal about place. The person who set the condition sees it remembered. It is a valuable contrast to E03, where Takina receives an embrace she did not initiate, and to later moments when Chisato is less willing to hear a boundary from someone else. [E05 10:50–11:05, 22:21–22:33, CLM-010/011]

By E06, it is Takina who proposes living together because Chisato is threatened. The household begins under conditions. Chisato argues about how to divide chores, turns rock-paper-scissors into an asymmetrical game using a talent that gives her an advantage, and wants the arrangement to go on. Takina gets a say in when it ends. The competitive satisfaction of the game has weight of its own. A loud reaction there proves remarkably little about the relative depth of a later fear. Chisato can say in E07 that Takina’s departure would leave her lonely and still welcome the prospect of her return to DA. She can make room for different kinds of love when the girls speculate about Mika without supplying a confession of her own. In E08 Takina’s insistence on contact, medical appointments and solvent accounts acquires a meaning Chisato puts into words. Someone is prepared to come for her. Direct thanks for the work on the café reaches Takina through a different register from the teasing invitation to share a home. [E06-CLM-001/004/005/021/022; E07-CLM-002/005/013/014; E08-CLM-012/022/025/026]

When Chisato learns her damaged heart has about two months left, she still uses her access to Kusunoki to make a DA return possible for Takina. The audience comes to understand what the offer alone cannot tell Chisato. Takina sees that return as a way to investigate a route to her partner’s survival. Before they part, Takina plans the civilian day. Its alarms, shops, fishing and forecast snow bear her signature. She asks whether the fishing is fun. Chisato’s answer has its real object in the second clause, たきなといればさ, being there with Takina. She lets the failed schedule succeed on its maker’s terms by taking pleasure in the day she was given. The snowfall visibly starts later; Chisato reacts before the matched closing music enters around 21:43.23, and further responses follow. Takina’s gift already worked before the weather or the score could reward it. [E09-CLM-003/012/028/030–041; REL-CHI-TAK-E09-003; TV Q08]

These scenes give unusually strong evidence of a particular attachment. Chisato identifies Takina’s company as the source of pleasure, names meeting her as one of the goods of her entire life, and will go back for a bag she gave her. Comfort with physical proximity, cohabitation proposed for safety, remembered objects and a future chosen together sustain a romantic reading. None is a mutual declaration, and Fuki’s rank, Sakura’s interest in a uniform or a title suggesting first love cannot establish another girl’s private attraction. The sharper test of intimacy comes when Takina wants something for Chisato that Chisato cannot want for herself. [E09-CLM-033; E10-CLM-020/021; E12-CLM-023/025/046; SHORT05-CLM-002–005; shorts audiovisual SX02]

## When the person who needs rescuing has harmed her

After the damage to her heart, Chisato refuses revenge against Himegama. Killing the woman would change nothing about the prognosis. The claim concerns what that particular act could accomplish. She pursues practical help and continues ordinary work. Kusunoki tries to claim some of her remaining life for DA. Chisato asks for a real possibility for Takina and retains the right to decide what her own role will be. Toward the shop she is equally decisive. She closes LycoReco and encourages the others to take their time elsewhere. She wants the place to remain a happy one to the end. In the increasingly empty café, she also admits to Mika that she is lonely. Some customers turn up and object to losing the place in which *they* had made lives. She is pleased to hear it. Chisato is often good at imagining how a stranger might use another afternoon. Here she has gone some way toward deciding what counts as its best use for people who love her. [E09-CLM-006–017; E10-CLM-002–007/016–018; E10-VIS-002, approx. 08:07–08:50]

Mika brings out a box. In the place of the weapon she imagines, it contains her 晴れ着, the formal clothes he would have liked to see her wear at her coming of age. She dresses while there is still time, asks to be photographed, then asks for *his* opinion. When he insists he has done little for her, her answer is concrete. He gave her a name, taught her, helped make the café, gave her the chance to meet Takina and searched for Yoshi-san with her. Then he tells her about the bargain and his involvement in it. His story changes what she knows about both men. It also makes room for a new act of trust on her part. She says that, had she known earlier, she might have done the work sullenly and blamed the adults for everything she hated. Instead, she chose her work and her shop. That counterfactual belongs to Chisato. It does not prove that withholding the truth from a child was the only way she could have become herself. She still calls both men her fathers. Then she asks, この千束はどう？ 好き？, asking Mika if he likes this Chisato, the person who actually came to exist. He answers that she is his pride as a daughter. The most self-determining person in the room is the one who needs to hear those words. [E10 13:13–17:38, VIS-003, CLM-019–030; TV Q10]

News that Yoshimatsu has been taken puts her loyalty under pressure. She believes he owes her an answer. She has also learned more than enough to distrust his account of what is good for her. Yet that makes him neither disposable nor the only person in danger. She leaves the defense of Enkūboku to people she names and trusts, Takina and Fuki, and goes after him. The exchange of いってきます and いってこい with Mika before she enters sounds like leaving home. It also acknowledges the possibility of returning. Majima expects her to shield Yoshimatsu. Chisato interposes her satchel. Two visible impacts land on the bag around 19:06.20 and 19:06.84, without establishing a bodily wound. This is a material cost of protecting someone who has tried to direct her life. The benefit is his without being proof of his innocence. She can trust other people with a task, value a man enough to rescue him, and decline to take his word about her destiny. Those are three different judgments. [E10-CLM-039–044; E11-CLM-021–032; CHI-MB-E11-002, E11-VIS-010 and their TV audiovisual correction]

Yoshimatsu’s offer in E12 tries to collapse those judgments. He confronts her with a choice in which his death can procure both her survival and fulfillment of the vocation he assigns her. Takina has reason to think the replacement heart is in the case. He shows a scar and says it is in his body. The subsequent arrival of a replacement establishes material rescue but cannot settle every stage of custody. What matters to this confrontation is that three people act on different understandings of the price. He is prepared to give his own life. It would be a mistake to describe him as simply trying to save himself. Takina wants to secure the heart even if he must die. Chisato has to restrain the person trying hardest to save her. Yoshimatsu sees world-service, individual happiness and realizable talent as the same thing. For Chisato an extra span of life purchased by becoming his idea of her would cost precisely the life Takina is trying to preserve. [E11-CLM-005–008; E12 C0104–10, CLM-005–016/019–023; REV-E12-002]

Himegama attacks Takina. Yoshimatsu tells Chisato her friend will die. The edit goes outside the tower for the report, then returns to Chisato aiming and shows Yoshimatsu wounded. This gives strong grounds to attribute the intervention to her in defense of Takina. Neither contact nor a deliberate choice of a medically safe wound is visible. The later relief with which Chisato checks the injury cannot retrospectively establish certainty about its result. She was willing to use force that could injure the man she was trying to save. She checks him and tells him she hates people who treat their own lives carelessly. She can say directly to Yoshi-san that she dislikes him, thank him for the time he gave her and ask him to live on, all in one exchange. She gives back the pendant. The abruptness is part of the point. Gratitude for one action does not neutralize her anger about another. [E12 08:07–08:21 and aftermath, CLM-015–022; TV Q17]

Her protest to Takina is that survival by killing Yoshi-san would leave somebody who was already no longer herself. Takina’s reply reduces the issue to a person’s name and an unwanted fact. 嫌だ 千束が死ぬのは嫌だ means that she cannot bear Chisato dying. She does not concede Chisato’s ethic. Chisato thanks her, says the extra time let her meet Takina, and tells her that farewells come to everybody, *but today is not that day*. She asks for a return to action in the time they actually have. Takina is allowed to want her alive and to say just how much. She is prevented, physically as well as verbally, from obtaining that life on terms Chisato rejects. The disagreement has casualties even when nobody in this exchange dies. Takina must bear the helplessness of a love or loyalty fierce enough to motivate an act the intended beneficiary cannot accept. Chisato sometimes asks others to bear the cost of her self-authorship. She occasionally has very good reason. The anime also allows that cost to be visible from the other side. [E12-CLM-013/014/021–027/046; REL-CHI-TAK-E12-002; E12-AUD-012/013]

E13 declines to tie the moral threads into a clean recovery. In private confrontation Mika reaches Yoshimatsu, who accepts that the former partner has come for him. The anime later reports his death and Mika’s retrieval of the replacement heart. At the exterior cut, the image gives a brief change of light at the window. Two metallic-sounding events are reported before the cut. The verifier did not identify a clear report over the exterior. From the action around the ellipse one can infer Mika’s consequential intervention. A visible killing, an established single instrument or a fully reconstructed transfer would exceed it. Mika tells Kurumi to keep quiet. On the beach Chisato opens the outer greeting marked HAPPY NEW BIRTHDAY. Inside it the paper surfaces are blank, and her hand returns to the charm in the box. The decorated insert declaring that Yoshimatsu’s death completed her new beginning appears in Mika’s gloved hands in the earlier tower sequence (E13 19:14–19:20, 19:47.937–19:55.840). The soundtrack voices its written message for the audience. Chisato receives Takina’s assurance about the heart and the outer greeting. No encounter gives her the insert’s words or Mika’s full account. Accepting the extra time when she has not personally killed her benefactor fits her expressed position in E12. It does not answer what she would choose if she knew everything the audience suspects Mika had to do. The very person who loves her capacity to decide still shapes the information available to her. [E13-CLM-031–037; REL-MIK-YOS-E13-001; REV-E13-006; TV Q13]

## Who can follow her

Majima asks for the right to interpret her. To him, the extraordinary child he faced at the tower remains an opponent who made him feel fear. For her he is also the unwanted visitor who turns out to like *Die Hard*. They can talk about Powell and the last scene over drinks. She rejects his inclusive 俺ら and does not remember him in the way he remembers her. In E11 his objections to other people imposing a purpose on them sound uncomfortably like her own. In E13 he calls himself a protector of the world, the person on the weaker side of any imbalance, even if someday that means helping DA. She recognizes he also believes himself in the right. Self-chosen purpose alone would license both of them. He forces civilians into his public experiment. She asks whom she can help and imagines the possibility of remaining in that particular person’s memory. The same capacity for freely deciding on a course of action leaves people with radically different obligations toward those caught in it. [E08-CLM-013–018; E11-CLM-028–033; E13-CLM-007–017]

The show puts the two briefly beside one another facing the city, as well as opposite one another in conflict. Asked about ambition, she names Mika’s café, coffee, customers, townspeople, food, beautiful places, companions and her earnest friend. He can find little ambition in that list. To be needed in this sense means finding someone for whom she can do something, with no assurance that doing it will put the whole world right. It also has a selfish hope folded into it. Perhaps that person will remember her after she is gone. She can puncture his demand that the duel decide the true hero with a practical joke about who avoids falling from the building. The action nonetheless puts her in real need. Takina returns, catches her and saves her bodily. She receives what she has spent a lifetime insisting others are entitled to receive. Majima survives too. His comparison to “the dark one,” 黒いほう, concerns Takina, not an inscription of Chisato’s ammunition choice. Neither the appearance of her things nor a graze supplies missing knowledge of the load. [E13-VIS-003, CLM-009–023; TV Q19; REV-E13-015]

Her strongest failure of reciprocity happens after she wakes alone and in pain following surgery. Believing this means she is going to die, she leaves to find a good place while she can. She dislikes a damp, mournful farewell. She has previously given Takina the option of going or staying and Mika the chance to be heard. Here she does not give either of them a choice about accompanying her. She is taken aback that her friends found her. Saori’s civilian photograph supplies the clue beyond the reach of the usual technical search. The young woman whose safety Chisato took seriously in E01 has helped make her reachable when she would rather disappear. In the island encounter Takina makes the first *visible* discharge, a point the retrospective frames correct. The source of subsequent obscured events remains open. Takina presses her objection until the chase gives way to the beach conversation. The friend who was once invited into a new life now insists on seeing the inviter, challenges her reasoning and gives Chisato information about her own future. [E01, Saori sequence; E13 16:05–16:24, CLM-025–030/035–041; TV Q14]

What changes at the beach is the task she can set herself. She no longer has to arrange a goodbye in the time her heart has left. Takina suggests beginning with what Chisato had given up. Chisato can turn that thought into a destination, and the epilogue locates their mobile café in Hawaii. Kusunoki still calls with work and Chisato can still decline it. Earlier she bargained with Kusunoki for Takina’s option to return to DA. In E12 she helps defend the exposed Lycoris. Mizuki credits the older negotiation that stopped the threat to Chisato to **Mika and Kusunoki**, in talks with their superiors. Chisato names the ensuing obligation to help DA. The later withdrawal order comes from Itadori. Chisato does not institute transparent government in the process of recovering her life. When the fans in Short 06 drift close to the truth about the uniform, it is Chisato who interrupts Takina's accurate explanation and presents the issued uniform as a custom-made costume. Three enthusiastic viewers hear excellent role-play. One friend has learned to expose the limits of Chisato's preferred stories, while Chisato can still be very good at supplying an official fiction to someone else. [E12-CLM-030/041; E13-CLM-040–042/050; SHORT06-CLM-001–004, C012–20; shorts SX01/SX03]

## Ordinary appetites and social power

Off duty in E04, Chisato insists on eating well because a lifetime contains only so many meals. If the dessert creates an extra fitness cost, she is willing to do the running. At the aquarium she can turn a serious question about what interests Takina into a comparison with a seahorse and a garden eel. She also refuses to treat every disorder she sees out of uniform as a summons to exercise extrajudicial power. Pleasure need not be either an incentive for service or a reward for surviving it. Loving the morning quiet, the peculiar tower, a familiar film or the food that belongs to a train journey is already an answer to what there is to do with a life. E13’s world-in-miniature speech is credible because the audience has spent time with the things she names. [E01 00:48 and CLM-013; E02 05:15–06:11; E04 09:24–09:37, 11:58–12:48 and 18:45, CLM-007/008; E08-CLM-013; E13-CLM-013–015]

Ordinary life offers plenty of occasions on which her first explanation is wrong. At the hanami gathering she initially reads Takina’s proposal in terms of enjoying the blossoms. Takina supplies her own appreciably commercial reason. Later in S01 Chisato challenges Takina’s proposed response to an ordinary security problem. The same person can correct a partner’s judgment on one point and have hers corrected on another. In S02 Chisato imagines Waikiki, a photograph and a film to watch before the trip. Kusunoki’s interruption becomes material for imitation and a convenient way to keep reading the guide. Yet Chisato asks whether taking a task on a nonkilling condition is acceptable. The short supplies no explicit concession from the commander. Takina’s private admission of excitement then turns travel from Chisato’s sales pitch into a desire they can share on different terms. The punchline puts a robber in the closed café and a welcoming formula in Chisato’s mouth. S02’s anticipated departure and S06’s travel preparations do not date every short after the television finale. [SHORT01-CLM-002–006/014–017; SHORT02-CLM-001–006, C035–41/054–68; shorts SR01/SX01]

In Short 03, the strongest objection to making something pleasurable comes from the person who has to balance the books. Takina is trying to invent a dessert. Chisato notices a word she recognizes from a film and asks her to say it again. A recipe has become a way of recognizing what someone did on her own. She supplies ideas from an old menu, while Takina credits Mika with realizing them. Chisato wants the new creation sold. The receipt gives Takina a reason to refuse. The observers drink milk and react to the *sight* of Takina’s dish. Neither a depicted group tasting nor a visible price on the receipt resolves the question. Chisato actually feeds herself in the earlier passage. Her liking what something looks or tastes like remains quite compatible with her wanting to spend money another person has responsibility for. [SHORT03-CLM-001–006, C020–26/039–52/053–56/065–90; shorts SR02/SR03]

Short 04 shows her tending Takina’s hair on a recording, bargaining with Mizuki about a broken plate and the shop’s alcohol, and participating in a staged scare. The care is visible within a replay whose permitted uses are less certain. The frightened response and ensuing disruption put a bodily cost on the choice to make a friend part of a performance without telling her. Chisato subsequently imitates the scream. The prank that can be explained coexists with a later figure whose identity remains unsettled. Music from the ending is already present by about 02:36.4. Chisato’s comic interpretation is not a neutral account of everything the viewer has just seen or heard. S05 gives her a more generous authority to exercise. She offers Takina a place for as long as she wants and lets Sakura try *her* red uniform. Takina still intends to look for a room, and Fuki can refuse to lend her own garment. Chisato also persists in a joke Fuki declines to imitate. The interval around Sakura’s constraint is partly withheld. Neither Chisato’s proximity nor her friendliness proves she authorized or understood everything that happened to that witness. [SHORT04-CLM-001–006; shorts SR04/SX05; SHORT05-CLM-001–006; shorts SR05]

E08 and the shorts make a full working portrait possible. Chisato delights in making something interesting and then in watching another person’s face. She can recognize what a friend has contributed, say thank you directly, and let the friend veto what she wants. She can also claim attention by persistence, fiction or physical contact before the listener has decided whether they want to participate. Fuki answers back, Mizuki can bargain, Kurumi can join the game, Takina can refuse, and Mika can allow an occasion to be silly without giving up his authority over the shop. In S06 the costumed Mika merits a warm welcome precisely when Fuki has been insisting that her teacher alone is sensible. Chisato often makes it easier for other people to want things in public. The best check on what she has achieved is what those people actually go on to do with the opportunity. [E08-CLM-022/025/026; SHORT05-CLM-001–003/006; SHORT06-CLM-004–006]

## What the Japanese performance lets another person hear

Chisato need not announce a mood for the audience to hear whom she is addressing. Chika Anzai gives the girl who stretches her own name at a first meeting a markedly different conversational task from the one who asks Mika if he likes her. With Takina, Chisato often occupies the space that would have allowed a formal answer to finish. In S03 Chisato asks Takina to repeat イノベーション, guesses correctly that she has watched *The Social Network*, then announces what her own Chisato Zuckerberg will do. She has heard a film in Takina’s choice of words and gives the two of them an exchange Takina could not have had with the screen. Elsewhere she drops an honorific, casts herself in an extravagant café welcome, or speaks for a superior on the telephone. Shion Wakayama gives Takina ways to participate without imitating Chisato’s manner. She can supply a dry correction, hold back a laugh, confess her own excitement in two phrases, or return a challenge. In S02 the extended beat between 私も and 浮かれてますから leaves room for us and for Chisato to discover who else is excited. The 2.17 seconds belong to the interval between captions. Mixed sound continues there. Chisato laughs, exclaims よっしゃー！ and starts planning how quickly they can finish the job and get ready for Hawaii. That is her answer to Takina. The explicitly marked echo belongs to her much later ホアチャ～!! [E01 06:05–07:01; SHORT02 C014–18/058–63/080, SR01 and shorts music register; SHORT03 C020–035; *Heroine Archive* S104–109, cast discussion]

She also speaks *against* her usual invitation. The pleased ようこそ offered to a newcomer, her teasing of Fuki, and the grateful directness of ありがとう to Mika or Takina each distribute attention differently. Her 違う in E12 interrupts the benefactor who claims he acts for her. She calls him ヨシさん even when she says she dislikes him. Looking after his wound, anger at how carelessly he treats his life, the return of his pendant and an acknowledgment of what she owes all survive in the same address. With Fuki she trades challenges, yet she also names Fuki as someone she trusts and works with her to identify Majima (E07-CLM-010, E10-CLM-044). In E10 she can let the group go in broadly buoyant language and admit a little later, to her teacher, やっぱりさみしいですよ. Her ordinary first person 私 can place ownership where it belongs, as when she insists her work and shop were her choices. In the E13 beach exchange, she turns Takina’s own emphatic めっちゃかわいい back toward her. At the aquarium in E04 Takina had said that “that” need not be hidden. Chisato brought a hand to her exposed pendant before Takina called it cute (14:46–14:54). In E13 Chisato explicitly recalls those very words while explaining her hesitation over discarding the token (20:14–20:35). The pendant is the strongest referent. The playful exchange need not be a direct declaration about Chisato’s beauty. The reference and the register matter more than a list of signature particles. [E10-AUD-006/007, CLM-016–030; E12-CLM-017–027; E13-CLM-038/039]

Sound provides different degrees of access. The E01 introduction measures around −20.4 dBFS in Chisato’s greeting window and −26.1 in Takina’s formal response. That difference fits the unequal initiative already audible in their words. For two serious E12 lines the record measures roughly −22.1 dBFS around Chisato’s concrete objection to treating a life carelessly and −30.0 around her claim that she has things more important than “the world.” Her protest about losing herself and Takina’s quieter refusal to accept her death furnish yet another pairing, while Takina’s rock-paper-scissors response in E06 can be extremely animated in safety. These are observations about caption-bounded full mixes. They do not measure sincerity, universal vocal habits or how blame and grief sound in isolation. The two actors can share a crisis and perform different tasks within it. The credited *Heroine Archive* conversation at S106/p104 offers an actor’s account of how her work was made. Wakayama recalls a second take of E09’s last line after hearing the director’s explanation, while Anzai does not remember that exchange. The episode itself still determines which line and image its audience receives. [E01-CLM-004, E06-CLM-021, E12-AUD-008/009/012/013, E13-CLM-052; TV Q05/Q08/Q10/Q15]

## What changes when print grants another person the point of view

**Asaura’s prose.** In *Ordinary days*, the regular Doi finds a reason to come out, walk, eat with others and take an interest in a future he had imagined closed. Chisato’s gift for connecting people is part of the pleasure. When Takina smiles at him, Chisato supplies a romantic motive, even though Kurumi raises the obvious possibility of asking Takina directly. Takina’s account involves his spending, his seat, his improving mood and the outings the regulars have arranged. An adult encouraged to understand her concern as attraction asks the teenager to dinner. Although he sees the self-interest in his attempted justification, he goes ahead. The scene ends before her reply. Chisato’s actions help someone participate in life and expose a coworker to an unwanted inference. Being good at making an occasion is a poor guarantee of knowing whom a smile is for. The prose supplies the viewpoints needed to see both effects. [*Ordinary days* 10.211–213, 10.335–355, 10.369–419, 10.525–599, 10.614–690, 19.7–22; OD-02]

Kana’s experience in the same book puts the case for hospitality at its strongest. Other people have claimed authority over her identity and body. When money is taken from her, the prospect of being unable to afford the modest coffee that gives her a seat at LycoReco feels like losing a life she could choose. She experiences Chisato as someone who takes genuine pleasure in her friendship. When Chisato sits her down and takes an order, the available words for a request as dangerous as asking for help carry something of the ordinary ease of ordering a drink. Kana asks, reaches back into the embrace and says yes. Yet before the request the café had already taken protective measures. Mizuki objects to how long they waited while Kana remained exposed. Chisato cannot receive general prior authorization from Kana’s later decision, any more than an observer can deduce from the voucher Kana finds exactly when and how it reached her phone. The same novel lets her speak over Takina and make it possible for Kana to speak. The interlocutor makes all the difference. [*Ordinary days* 17.73–91, 18.97–145, 18.279–407, 18.480–512, 18.800–910; OD-02/06]

In *Recovery days*, Chisato offers another possible interpretation of a dead man’s unfinished words to someone prepared to commit violence for him. Mizukawa last heard Okada invite him to eat ramen. The boss heard part of something else, and Chisato relays the boss’s thought that Okada might have wanted Mizukawa stopped. She acknowledges that nobody can know, then expresses her conviction that the “owner” wanted his “dog” to be happy. She refuses to certify the words of the dead, yet lends her certainty to a claim about affection. Only after she leaves does Mizukawa recall what Okada taught him about living and face the boss who offers him a return home. The boss inflicts violence of his own. Chisato opened an alternative to revenge. She neither authored the man’s decision nor redeemed the organization to which he returned. Elsewhere an actual fever, recovery, late gaming with Kurumi and an attempt to fake another sick day put less flattering pressures on the host who wants to be cared for. Takina suspects a motive for having been sent home, and Chisato gives another. The narration identifies the later deception while leaving that earlier intention private. [*Recovery days* 11.53–83, 11.373–425, RD-03; 12.147–155, 12.662–724, RD-04]

In *Gluttony days*, Chisato’s apparent threat to Clarisse helps precipitate a dangerous client’s admission. It is Clarisse who commissioned the attack, cancels it and says she paid. Madame takes her word. The child says the guards were kind and she feared Takina might be hurt. Takina’s thought that a shared struggle with ramen brought them together carries a different authority. When Clarisse apologizes, Chisato lets Takina answer. Takina’s reassurance and Clarisse’s hug are theirs to give. Later, Chisato argues for the father’s care partly through the meaning of the girl’s name. Takina objects that etymology cannot establish his present behavior. The photograph shows Clarisse embracing her father, who booked the park **after** the emergency. It cannot prove she believed his account of having arranged the trip in advance. Chisato leaves room for the family and would like a future outing when Takina can go too. She can be both generous about an occasion and overconfident about the hidden love she thinks she has diagnosed. [*Gluttony days* 9.491–558, 10.549–573, 10.647–743, 10.790–819, 10.878–959; GD-01/02]

A less dangerous encounter in that novel is equally important. Chisato asks to dry Takina’s hair, receives two refusals and persists physically and verbally. Takina hands over the dryer because continued refusal seems troublesome. She finds the handling surprisingly gentle, becomes embarrassed, interrupts and finishes the job herself. Later she dries Chisato’s hair; Chisato praises their unspoken understanding and Takina says the cue was quite obvious. The body being attended to is the focalizer’s. The reader can know it felt different from doing the job herself without erasing her initial refusal. The prerecorded hair care in Short 04 supplies an anime image of intimacy, while this novel supplies interiority for *this prose encounter*. The latter cannot tell us how Takina silently felt in the former. A different sequel to an invitation in *Recovery days* has Takina feeling mildly maneuvered into the next film, smiling over the outing she has already had and setting a condition before accepting another. Chisato’s delight in eliciting a yes is best read along with the actual terms in which her friend gives it. [*Gluttony days* 14.139–162, 14.218–276, GD-05; *Recovery days* 18.2–40, RD-01; SHORT04-CLM-003]

**Abe Kanari’s *Recollect*.** Abe can draw a gesture from outside, let the next panel disclose its consequence to the reader, and keep its motive from the other girl. In V01 Chisato uses a Lycoris’s selection of tools for a job to recommend a smile in café service. Takina has been performing the work without that smile. Chisato eventually admits she wants to see it, and Takina says *she* smiles when she is enjoying herself. Later, offered films for pleasure, Takina assumes enough work remains to justify skipping the credits. The panels show her absorbed. Her next-day explanation supplies the assumption about workload. Chisato assigned no written report or quota. In V02 the sofa images invert the problem. Chisato leaves to avoid seeing an intimate screen moment, and finds the image waiting when she returns. Takina says she paused as soon as Chisato went out. The reader can infer that Chisato is embarrassed from what she saw, said and did. No parallel evidence grants a private motive to Takina for pressing pause. Trying to make someone smile, misreading an assignment and wishing to avoid an image are different situations in which her expression fails to direct her audience. [*Recollect* V01 S7–14, 43–52; V02 S64–70, R1-01/R2-01]

Two further sequences grant bounds the television series never tests. When a supposed pest is loose in the café, Chisato first allows that action might be necessary and later prefers release if possible. The object is a modified commercial device. An unwillingness to kill a person cannot be extended to every living thing by citing an animal nobody in this story actually kills. In V02’s bonus a gesture at her chest accompanies a “gift” and a changed willingness to think about the future. She is also defending her second snack break of the day. The heart is a strong local reading. A donor identity and detailed prognosis remain unavailable in these panels. In the preceding chapter guest Takina makes soba in Chisato’s home. Weather spoils the moon-viewing, and Takina asks Chisato to cook next year. A longer horizon can be made intimate by assigning a household task to a future version of someone. The chapter prints a proposal, with no need to imagine the meal has already happened. [*Recollect* V02 S87–102, 105–112, 115–118, R2-05/06]

**Other licensed manga.** Four small experiments by different contributors give unusually specific answers to the question of who may do what with the wish Chisato elicits. In GUNP’s *Repeat* she proposes a hug and offers a general rationale about soothing. Takina opens her arms; Chisato checks and Takina confirms. After the embrace each attributes the desire for comfort to the other before Takina admits she too felt soothed. Chisato makes her second request directly, obtains assent and embraces her again. A separate creator comment takes up friendship. In 苗川采’s *Reload*, repeatedly petting a devoted Takina belongs to Chisato’s dream. Awake, she compares Takina to a dog and encounters a threat. In 稲岡和佐’s *Repeat 03*, Takina asks Chisato to take her photograph without announcing the instant of the shutter. They help a woman, who accepts a café invitation, and Chisato catches an unguarded image of **Takina**. The later posed picture of the two girls follows its own request. In くゥ’s contribution to the same volume, Takina refuses a photograph for Chisato’s wallpaper even under an assurance of privacy. Takina does take a picture of Chisato. Chisato says she missed the expression she wanted. These independently authored circumstances make the affirmative, reluctant, imagined and refused acts individually legible. They supply no incidence rate for Chisato’s behavior. [GUNP, *Repeat* S151–159; 苗川采, *Reload* S43–54; 稲岡和佐, *Repeat 03* S7–23; くゥ, *Repeat 03* S129–136]

Other contributors divide even the experience of being remembered. 大倉ナタ’s Chisato calls Fuki from a beach, concerned that she left Takina lonely. Takina’s wish for access to graves, refusal of the protected information, and Fuki’s securing permission for a different memorial visit take place in Fuki’s recalled account. ほげらむ lets Takina imagine what it costs the person who *leaves others behind*. Chisato immediately suspects illness or a hurt she caused. Takina rejects those explanations. Her newfound insight into someone else’s position and a new diagnosis are quite different things. 竹本蒼太郎’s solitary Mika can picture holding a child’s hands with Shinji. That imagined scene gives the reader his yearning. It gives Chisato neither a shared memory nor new knowledge of Mika’s confrontation with Yoshimatsu. Each contributor is borrowing a recognizable relationship or predicament to do particular work. An inherited tower, heart, photograph or beach supplies no independent corroboration of events omitted by the anime. [大倉ナタ, *Reload 02* S129–136; ほげらむ, *Reload 03* S115–124; 竹本蒼太郎, *Reload* S135–142]

## The reach of a portrait

When another person needs time, Chisato commonly converts her idea of help into something they can actually do, such as sharing food, going out or trying a different place. For an adversary it can mean surviving to make another choice. If they answer differently from what she expected, ask whose judgment she must revise. With Takina at DA she changes her offer and leaves another option intact. With Fuki in play she can persist beyond refusal. With a hostile stranger bleeding in front of her she will incur an immediate cost without the assurance of a return. With the father who has tried to own her future she can insist that he live and still reject what he wants her to become. With a caregiver who knows part of her childhood she can seek both the truth and a declaration that she is loved. The direction of disclosure changes once she is the person others wish to accompany in dying. Avoiding their grief becomes a reason to choose for them, and Takina has to compel another conversation. These claims have strongest support where another voice can answer hers. They become weaker wherever a gift or silence is allowed to speak for an absent person.

Chisato says she wants to be remembered by people she has helped. She also wants to remain responsible for whom she becomes. It is also clear that she can disappoint, pressure, injure and be injured. The source withholds her response to a complete account of Mika’s actions, the eventual course of her new heart, her settled adult ambitions, and any definitive declaration about the romantic future of her partnership. An exceptionally self-directed girl still has to learn some things about herself from other people. The final chance Takina gives her is wonderfully ordinary in that respect. She has to decide what she wants to do tomorrow.
