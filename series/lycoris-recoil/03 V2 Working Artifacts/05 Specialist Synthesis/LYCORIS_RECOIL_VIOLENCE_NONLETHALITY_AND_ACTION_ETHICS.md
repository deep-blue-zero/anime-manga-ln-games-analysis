# Violence, nonlethality, and the authority to run a risk

Source boundary: TV E01–E13 and *Friends are thieves of time* Shorts 01–06, at the frozen CP5 anime horizon with attributable V2 revisions. Bounded comparisons draw on Asaura's three admitted novels, *Recollect*, and individually credited anthology stories. This study uses Japanese text, scenes and frames available in the accepted readings and reports native synchronized viewing only by attribution. The institutional studies carry political structure and the Alan bargain in greater depth. This study follows who decides, who bears the danger, and what the result allows anyone to claim.

## The two rescues in the pilot

The warehouse and street in E01 look like repetitions because Takina uses the same defense in each. In the first she fires while Erika is held. In the second she describes a plan in which she lets a protected civilian walk ahead in order to draw out pursuers. She is confident that she can finish the confrontation. In the van Saori actually endures confinement and interrogation about her photograph (17:30.86–17:42.00). The pursuers elect to threaten her and the earlier decision helped expose her to them. Her success in saving Erika is the fact she puts to Fuki. Her ability to hit from that distance is what she offers Chisato. Both answers treat likely execution and a desired outcome as decisive under pressure. The events give different grounds for questioning that confidence. Erika is already in mortal peril. She says 構わず撃って at 02:39.93. Her call for action remains part of the scene even though she cannot freely specify a method and does not control when it begins. Saori has come to the café for help and has supplied an image whose timing matters to DA. Takina chooses an avoidable additional exposure without a depicted opportunity for Saori to weigh that purpose. Erika can ask to be helped; neither woman's relationship to the person taking action gives that person unlimited permission. [E01 02:39.93–03:21.97 and 16:11–18:47, E01-CLM-002/007/008, REV-E01-003, TV E01-C02.]

There is no uninjured status quo to which Takina could return in the warehouse. Kusunoki prefers to take the merchants alive. The system interruption changes command's opportunity to help. At 02:44.27 Fuki restates the standing order to wait and has a reason to fear the irreversible consequence of a teammate's shot. To award the entire moral question to Fuki because an order was disobeyed is to bypass the time-sensitive danger and Erika's call. To award it to Takina because Erika lives is to erase the merchant's death and the risk she accepted for her teammate. In E03 Erika challenges the convenient account that Takina deserves all the blame for trying to save her. [E01-CLM-002/003; E03 16:17–16:42, 21:31–21:45 and E03-CLM-012; E12 21:08.20–21:25.80 and E12-CLM-043/044.]

In the street, Chisato demands to know why the person being guarded was used to expose the men after her. [E06-CLM-005/006/017–019/023; TV E06-C03/C09 and Q06.]

The café's costs put another limit on treating virtue as a private accomplishment. In E08 Takina takes responsibility for the accounts. Its comic lightness gives a useful countercase to the idea that Chisato's speed always allows her to determine how others should respond. [E08-CLM-005–008.]

Competence and authority come apart at a larger scale as well. When disclosure makes the agency's operatives publicly legible in E11, what they can do and what command allows them to do diverge. Majima engineers exposure and calls the availability of coercive capacity freedom. The people endangered by it did not jointly determine its purpose, timing or costs. [E11-CLM-014/016–020; E12-CLM-003/004/031; E06-CLM-009–011; E08-CLM-017.]

## Supplementary comparisons within their own boundaries

Asaura's Kana in *Ordinary days* tests who gets to ask and who decides how much she must endure before adults act. Pressure through threatening images has already raised the cost of being able to go to the café. The physical assault by Ishihara is a separate act. After earlier protective work, Chisato makes an intelligible request possible, and Kana speaks it. She returns and accepts the contact Chisato offers. That meaningful assent does not establish that the intervention began only with her request, that all surveillance was welcome or that requiring a commission was the right way to allocate continued harm. Mizuki directly challenges the delay. Mika gives reasons for the formal arrangement. Kurumi's projected image cleanup extends beyond the result she can establish. The novel supplies a favorable outcome, a genuinely chosen request and an unresolved question about what the adults could have done earlier. The three can be true without being one verdict. [OD-06, *Ordinary days* 18.97–145, 18.279–407, 18.480–512, 18.800–910.]

The viewpoint in *Recovery days* follows a man imagining authorization from a benefactor who can no longer answer. Mizukawa's last personally heard words from Okada concern a future meal. The boss supplies a different report and Mizukawa imagines that his own name meant a demand for retaliation. Chisato has been hired by that boss to keep Mizukawa alive. She relays the boss’s rival reconstruction of the unfinished sentence. She expressly says that nobody can learn its exact ending and personally vouches for the happiness Okada would have wanted. Takina sees securing the protected person as completing the commission. Chisato extends her time with him in the hope of leaving another interpretation available. Mizukawa later actually picks up the blade. His recollection that Okada taught him how to live as a person rather than how to kill helps change his choice. The boss later hurts Ijūin and even proposes displacing the blame onto the girl. The concession that one interpretation can preserve a life carries no promise that the surrounding organization is about to stop doing violence. The story is valuable because it separates the protagonist’s attributed interior decision from the boss’s proposed completion and Chisato’s own claim about affection. [RD-02, *Recovery days*, “Dog,” 11.53–83, 11.207–215, 11.373–425.]

The novel also puts competent partners inside a more ordinary failure. In the manuscript commission, a reference object discharges while Itō holds it. The separation between immediate act, checks the professionals omitted, tangible harm, repair labor and disclosure prevents their solution from absorbing all ethical credit. *Gluttony days* gives the partners a different opportunity to disagree. Takina expects that she may need to act at a level Chisato would object to and uses the assignment to separate from her.
