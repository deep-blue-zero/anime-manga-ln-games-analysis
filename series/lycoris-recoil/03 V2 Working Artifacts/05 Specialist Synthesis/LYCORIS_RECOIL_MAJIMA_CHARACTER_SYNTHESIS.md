# Majima and the opponent who remembers

*Lycoris Recoil* · Full spoilers for the Japanese TV series through E13. The final section on the Japanese manga anthologies names each contributor and the limits of that contributor’s portrayal.

Majima wants to know whether Chisato Nishikigi remembers him. She remembers him spitting on her. He means ten years earlier, at the old radio tower, when he was an armed attacker and she was a little girl. The fear he felt has survived the intervening decade. She gives him no sign she remembers him from that day. He tells the story at some length. She objects to being described as a monster and says she does not know him from that encounter. Majima laughs. He can bear being much less important in her past than she is in his. He finds it considerably harder to bear the thought that his future importance to her might also be hers to decide. [E08 C0293–0299 and C0329–0343, 13:57.29–14:14.19 and 15:28.59–16:20.31](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E08_DEEP_READING.md). [E13 C0112–0120, 05:05.87–05:25.06](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E13_DEEP_READING.md).

Majima is the green-haired man who turns a Tokyo subway battle and the inauguration of the new Enkuboku tower into confrontations over who has the right to keep Japan at peace. His adversaries include Direct Attack, the organization that secretly deploys girls called Lycoris, and Shinji Yoshimatsu, an agent of the Alan Institute who believes exceptional talent places its possessor under an obligation. Majima himself leads men who will fight and die on his instructions. He accepts paid work. When Commander Kusunoki asks whether the money is what he wants, he answers quite seriously that his comrades have a living to make. He adds that curiosity about the people who pass for defenders of justice interested him more. Kusunoki describes an international career and a commission from criminal organizations. His own answers let us distinguish what someone hired him to do from the parts of the job he would pursue for his own reasons. [E10 C0052–0065, 03:27.31–04:11.02, and C0172–0185, 09:25.75–10:06.38](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E10_DEEP_READING.md).

## What happens to the people on his side

His first description of Tokyo’s peace begins with a smell. He draws breath and says the place smells of a lie, bleached and disinfected, healthy-looking and unhealthy. During the subway fight several of his men fall. Afterward, alone, he says he has once again been the one left. He also finds the adversary interesting. Both responses precede his discovery that the public is being told there was an accident. “Accident” comes back in his speech three times. The deaths he lived through can disappear into a word and the citizens whom he thinks he is addressing need never know that he or his comrades were there. The encounter gives him a reason to distrust the public record. His response is to do something so large that lying about it will be impossible. The decision about how to be heard is his own. [E04 C0327–0332, C0348–0366 and C0435–0452, 16:28.88–16:45.65, 17:43.92–18:31.30 and 23:05.07–23:58.96 · MAJ-MB-E04-001/002](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E04_DEEP_READING.md).

He keeps count of people on his own side. Later, facing his hacker associate Robota, he specifies that twenty-six comrades died while carrying out Robota’s directions. This is Majima’s reported count. The story gives us neither a roll of those men nor a warrant to allocate all twenty-six deaths to the fight we saw. Robota says the losses were unanticipated and the proposed course was quickest. Majima says *his* side did what was asked. The comparison is between the price already paid and what Robota has delivered in return. His way of saying “my comrades” (俺の仲間) places them in a more personal relation to him than the “pawns” he dismisses in the same conversation. He also wants to know what happened to hired fighters who went missing in Japan. There is substance to the loyalty here. Remembering the dead makes a demand on the living. It also becomes a means of putting a frightened associate under a three-day deadline. Even the recollection which most honors his men can serve him as a threat to somebody else. [E06 C0093–0119, 05:31.85–06:47.72, and C0214–0236, 11:13.15–12:10.47 · REV-E06-007](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E06_DEEP_READING.md).

His followers call his name and cheer as he beats Chisato. Their enthusiasm tells us something about the man they want to see in action and something less certain about how close any of them are to him once the audience disperses. To Robota he can be bullying, dismissive, appreciative and quite deliberately inclusive. He hangs up on Robota’s first presentation of himself as the world’s best hacker. He checks whether Robota can state *his* objective, and dismisses an individual Lycoris recommended to him as a disposable pawn. Then he changes his mind about the particular girl Robota has found. During the pursuit he praises Robota for locating her again. After facing her, he tells Robota he has revised his opinion of him and asks for more about the girl. The praise is real, as specific as the earlier dismissal. It arrives in a room Robota has just complained about being broken into again. [E04 C0442–0450, 23:26.09–23:49.45](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E04_DEEP_READING.md). [E06 C0351–0359, C0385–0398 and C0460–0472, especially 21:45.74–21:59.34](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E06_DEEP_READING.md).

When Robota later speaks about “your plan,” Majima corrects him to “ours.” He can invite someone of very different abilities into the enterprise and leave that person space to do work he cheerfully admits he would fail to understand. He sometimes asks for an explanation, then cuts the explanation short, saying he probably would not understand it. At a later meeting, after Robota answers a question about Yoshimatsu, Majima calls him by his name. Still later comes the proprietary endearment “my hacker.” Those have different effects from another threat. They give the subordinate a stake and a place. Majima continues to decide what that place is. The limits of the partnership are plain at the end, when taking time alone with Chisato matters to him more than retaining anyone else’s connection to the scene. ## What makes him look twice

Majima initially fails to connect the Lycoris he encounters in the present with the child who frightened him ten years earlier. In E05 another young Lycoris has a far shorter encounter with him. He calls her the first and kills her. He does not ask what she wants, whom she serves willingly or what her distinctive abilities might be. In E06, with Chisato injured in front of him, Majima first taunts her over her choice of nonlethal ammunition. Then he asks about her mission, points out what she is wearing and calls her an Alan Lycoris. The TV verification favors recognition of her pendant at that point. His source of prior knowledge and his own affiliation were still uncertain when the episode ended. She gives him no verbal reply to the mission question before the encounter is interrupted. Robota has already shown him footage of her. Fighting the person in that footage gives him a reason to take a more particular interest. He tells Robota that it takes *her* to balance him. The dead girl’s lack of a similar opportunity is a standing objection to his belief that the weaker side can count on him. [E05 C0413–0417, 21:33.27–21:54.54 · E05-CLM-019](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E05_DEEP_READING.md). [E06 C0224–0236, C0389–0399 and C0465–0471](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E06_DEEP_READING.md). He does some genuine thinking before calling the meeting fate. Watching evidence from the fight, Robota wonders aloud whether Chisato’s apparent evasiveness might simply come down to Majima being a poor shot. Majima points out that she returns his fire without hesitation. He thinks this means she knew he would miss. Later Robota brings a rumor about a person who used nonlethal rounds at the old tower. Majima can supply what the researcher lacks. He was there. The resemblance brings back a small figure against whom, he says, he could do nothing. When the scene renders the child monstrous, it is giving form to the adult man’s memory of being afraid. In his telling a comrade called on him to bring down the old tower. Years later the uniform, refusal to kill and uncanny assurance allow him to recognize the opponent. They give him no basis to expect Chisato to remember him. He goes to Chisato to ask for it anyway. The visit starts at gunpoint. Chisato asks whether she may take her ringing phone. Majima agrees. Her partner Takina asks about a missed medical checkup. Majima remarks that health matters to people in their line of work. Chisato immediately objects to his easy plural and makes him account for his “we.” This is one of his most intimate habits and one of his most presumptuous. He can put two people into the same grammatical category in the time it takes the other to ask who authorized it. For a few moments they find a subject on which no authorization is needed. He notices *Die Hard*. He asks which character she likes. She names Powell, and he knows she means the cop. The talk turns to Powell and McClane becoming partners before they have even met. Both start to mention the last scene at once. Majima can be interested in the answer someone else will give, and the pleasure he takes in it is quite legible. To him the meeting of two people at a distance matters in a movie as well as in his account of his own life. Reading his entire pursuit as an imitation of the film would turn a resonance into a motive he never declares. [E08 C0257–0290, 12:16.57–13:47.37, especially C0281–0287 · REL-CHI-MAJ-E08-001](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E08_DEEP_READING.md).

Chisato offers to make coffee. Majima says he cannot handle bitterness and asks whether she has something else. At 13:53.958 and 13:55.170 the retained pictures show them with cups. What she eventually served him is unspecified. In a confrontation otherwise full of demands, he can make an ordinary request and leave the decision to the host. The ease has limits. He has shown up armed and taken an unwelcome interest in her heart. Chisato asks why he uses his talent to make people unhappy. When he groups her among killers she answers that she helps people. He shows his own Alan pendant, questions her belief that the Institute promotes peace and pursues the “Yoshi” she mentions. She withholds more information. When Takina approaches he announces that her partner is coming and takes his leave. Takina’s intervention and his farewell preserve a danger which their delight in the film did nothing to settle. A capacity for ordinary company is one of Majima’s gifts. He also decides unilaterally when other people are to receive it. ## Who gets to call a world peaceful

Majima’s political quarrel is sharpest when a particular person answers back. He confirms through Robota that “Yoshi” is the Alan agent who supported Chisato, goes to meet Shinji Yoshimatsu, and greets him by her familiar name for him. Discussing the artificial heart, he recognizes that the girl’s admiration rests on a painful misunderstanding and says he feels for her. Her refusal to kill also helped him survive their first encounter. Yoshimatsu calls Majima an excellent Alan child and asks whether a thousand guns were enough. Majima objects to being reminded of the favor. He might use his freedom to kill the benefactor himself. Yoshimatsu answers that he would give his own life for the Institute’s ideal. Majima recoils from the idea. What disgusts him about Alan and Direct Attack alike is authority concealed from the people it arranges the world for. He wants to know where the power resides. He can identify an affront to Chisato’s freedom even while planning to use her feeling for Yoshimatsu to make her come to him. [E09 C0290–0296 and C0395–0396](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E09_DEEP_READING.md). [E10 C0066–0096, 04:13.69–05:39.15](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E10_DEEP_READING.md).

Commander Kusunoki is a different audience. He greets the girls dispatched against him as though they were on a school trip and interprets their commander as the chaperone. His ease with a joke deprives their ranks and official vocabulary of the respect they usually command. His next question is serious. If modern society measures wrongdoing by law, does Direct Attack exist under law? Kusunoki invokes an organization older than the present law, responsible for order across political regimes. She can cite results. Majima asks how the public would judge the arrangements if it knew about them. Kusunoki says real peace is a world in which people need never feel the existence of malice. Majima thinks people shielded from a terrible reality may forget that peace means something and has to be won. He says an institution like hers should not be able to put its thumb on the scales, whichever direction they tip. There is no automatic answer to his question in the fact that he commits atrocities. The person raising it would also deny her the power to describe *him* out of existence. Her threat that he will vanish from everyone’s memory gives him reason to keep talking. [E10 C0169–0218, 09:16.83–11:34.59](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E10_DEEP_READING.md).

His argument changes when the audience gets bigger. He addresses the public contemptuously and tells citizens he has scattered guns throughout Tokyo. A man who wonders whether he has picked up a toy suddenly faces a police officer. Majima tells people that what they do is up to them, immediately after urging them to shoot as they please and protect themselves. The man in the street had no part in choosing the conditions of this lesson. Neither did the girl in uniform later recognized and shot at in public. Majima anticipates a perfectly reasonable objection to his broadcast. Pictures can be fabricated. People who have witnessed the confrontation in front of them should believe their own eyes. He has found a way to manufacture the experience on which he then asks them to exercise independent judgment. He even cites three thousand annual disappearances and *asks* how many should be attributed to Lycoris. The question is an insinuation. The series provides no audit of that number or of how many disappearances Direct Attack caused. The existence of a hidden service really is evidence against the public account of peace. It cannot make every association he invites his listeners to draw true. [E10 C0356–0387, 18:43.06–20:19.99](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E10_DEEP_READING.md). [E11 C0240–0282, 13:00.68–15:01.09](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E11_DEEP_READING.md).

He tells several people that he cares how *they* see. With Yoshimatsu he imagines how disappointed Chisato would be if she knew whom she admired, and says he sympathizes. With Kusunoki he wonders what other eyes would see in her work. With the public he contrasts the fallibility of screens and the experience of a person in the street. Then he calls Chisato a hero and uses Yoshimatsu’s position to constrain her movements. He tells her Yoshimatsu will be hurt if she gets out of the way. What matters to him now is the fact that another person can see a choice so clearly he knows which one she will make. Chisato’s protectiveness is quite genuine. So is the harm of being made to demonstrate it for his benefit. [E10 C0066–0073, 04:13.69–04:37.49](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E10_DEEP_READING.md). ## The terms of a fair fight

In the dark at the old tower Chisato asks whether Majima can see. He says he can hear. She later identifies sound as the operative difference and he says she is right. The TV story gives him exceptional orientation and lets him notice a change in her condition in their later encounter. It offers no clinical history for this ability. A measured transient in the mix is evidence of a sound at a particular time. Its physical source and Majima’s formative history are separate questions. The flashback at the old tower shows his skill already in use years earlier. Its depiction of what he apprehends belongs to the telling of his experience, not a map of everything a bystander could have perceived. [E08 C0301–0319, 14:23.20–15:06.74](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E08_DEEP_READING.md). Majima has interpreted the part of her resistance that resembles his own and ridden straight past the part that would forbid the partnership. ## What other people want from him

The manga anthologies give his followers an opportunity the TV series largely withholds. They get to explain why they want him around. In 如月南極’s 「礼賛」, men who celebrate Majima’s remarkable survival have heard of a belief about the good fortune attending an infant held by an exceptional person. Admiration, increasingly elaborate in their own telling, covers a perfectly intelligible fear that they themselves will die. Grown men come asking their leader to hold them. Majima asks about their fatigue and resists the request. He orders them to bed and fires, drawing apologies and alarm at the ricochet. The comedy shows no injury. After hearing about their superstition, he agrees to a less ceremonious compromise. He shakes hands with them. The followers’ reading of his affection belongs to them. His response gives them both something frightening and a reassurance they actually sought. The verdict on their chances of survival still belongs to the men hoping to live. This contributor allows him a small kindness to men who have made an embarrassing request, a choice the TV account of the twenty-six dead cannot settle for us. [如月南極, *Reload 02*, S47–56, especially S49–51 and S54–56](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Manga/Anthologies/LYCORIS_RECOIL_RELOAD02_CONTRIBUTOR_READINGS.md).

すのはら風香’s 「あなたの好きが好き」 gives him an ordinary demand made in an unmistakably Majima fashion. A disguised customer wants the best film the rental shop can offer. He disallows the easy answer from the more experienced Chisato and insists Takina choose one. It is an intelligent invitation to attend to a less practiced person’s judgment. The answer also takes the occasion away from him. Takina can recognize flaws in the film she picks while remembering how much she liked *Chisato* as they watched it together. Majima has succeeded in getting a personal answer, and it is about somebody else. His final thought turns to using Robota’s streaming account. The story shows neither a rental nor permission from Robota. In 魚住さかな’s separate Halloween story, a bandaged visitor presents himself to Chisato as a customer. She recognizes Majima. He even objects that Mika should wear a costume like everyone else. He thanks the staff as he leaves, and another person says he ate a parfait. For one creator he can be sidelined in a conversation about his favorite medium. For the other he can join a café custom, complete his visit and go home. Neither scene needs a political reconciliation to make a social encounter possible. Two brief appearances in *Reload 03* put him on the other side of the encounter. In いみぎむる’s collection, Robota remembers Majima telling him to eat vegetables. Robota passes the advice on to a young-looking stranger at the snack shelf. In ikra’s arcade story Majima knows the result and teases Robota about losing to a girl. Robota starts asking if he saw it. Exactly how Majima learned it is implicit. The private adviser and the amused colleague both do something more particular than an employer might require. Each belongs to that creator’s situation. The TV series itself has supplied a film enthusiast, a visitor who dislikes bitter drinks, a leader mindful of his men’s livelihoods, a man who recalls fear and another person’s capacity with unusual accuracy. It leaves his precise age, early family life, intimate history and domestic life outside the conflict largely undisclosed. His investment in Chisato has declarations of fear, affinity and personal interest behind it. The evidence for romance is much thinner, and Chisato repeatedly contests his claim to speak for them both. [いみぎむる, 「ロボとリス」, S12–14, and ikra, 「VS.天才ハッカー！」, S83–90, especially S89–90](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Manga/Anthologies/LYCORIS_RECOIL_RELOAD03_CONTRIBUTOR_READINGS.md).

Imagine Majima on a neighborhood film jury. An organizer privately asks him to smooth over an unpopular verdict for the sake of the event. A quiet juror can explain precisely what the judges saw differently. I would expect Majima to want that difference aired, and to be curious enough to hear the juror out. He has questioned concealed authority, asked for another person’s taste and admitted that a report improved his opinion of the person who brought it. I would be much less confident that he would protect the juror’s wish for anonymity once he thought public acknowledgment mattered. He has a history of assigning others the burden of making a truth visible. If the juror turned out simply to share the majority’s view, the apparently weaker side would have vanished from the example. His declaration that he always supports the weak could not tell us whether he would accept the outcome, pursue another opponent or just enjoy the film.

That is the unresolved part of the man Chisato meets. Majima and Chisato can enjoy discovering that they both want to talk about the same last scene in a film. The later pause has another reason. A sick opponent would make their contest uninteresting, and he grants the break before she tells him what she values. He listens when they disagree about what to live for and calls her aim small. The harder question is whether she gets to leave when she has finished speaking. She has given him an excellent account of what she means to do with her remaining life. He listens closely enough to cast himself as the person she must spend it helping. The contest reaches its end through a partner coming back for her, the very bond he asked her to set aside.
