# Detective Abe

In Asaura’s *Gluttony days*, Detective Abe is finishing his coffee when Kurumi comes out to speak to Mika. She breaks off when she sees who is sitting at the counter. Mika says his name and starts an apology. That is all the explanation Abe gets. He drains the cup, gets up and reaches inside his clothes. Mika offers to waive the charge. Abe says he drank it, after all, and it tasted good today too. Mika gives him ground coffee and homemade yōkan before he leaves. Abe protests that this is too generous, scratches the back of his head and promptly accepts the box with his other hand. Kurumi, who urgently needs the room, has time to observe how pleased he looks. He takes the hint and leaves with the ground coffee and the yōkan. The request stays unfinished, and the two men retain the ordinary pleasure of the visit. [*Gluttony days* 9.886–910.]

When Chisato introduces him to Takina in the first TV episode, Abe identifies himself as a member of Tokyo’s Metropolitan Police Department. He is a regular at Café LycoReco, where some of the people who know his order and expect him at games have considerably more to conceal than he can demonstrate. *Recovery days* describes him as a man in his late forties. The novels give him several occasions to decide what to overlook at the counter. The TV series gives him a colleague, Mitani, entitled to ask what his discretion costs other people. These are separate portrayals. The novels never provide the missing private account of what Abe believes about the girls on the TV series. [TV E01 C0307–0315, 12:45–13:03; E04 C0388–0395; E05 C0117–0157; *Recovery days* 9.132–145.]

## A man who will interrupt

The embarrassment in Abe’s first substantial prose appearance belongs initially to Chisato. Kurumi has read out the cinema’s restriction on underage patrons at screenings ending after eleven. Chisato says she could go in ordinary clothes and nobody would catch her. A new customer makes a conspicuously artificial throat-clearing sound. Once Chisato looks up, Abe scratches his head and says that would be a little hard to overlook. She wants to know what he is doing here at just this moment. He came for coffee. Was he not supposed to? He laughs and suggests tomorrow. Chisato protests that you should see a film when you want to see it, since you never know what will happen. Abe sits down and orders iced coffee, which Mika serves him as the girls sort out the next day. Her argument about living now is real to her. Abe, who has heard a teenager propose how she might get past an age restriction, offers an alternative that lets the outing go ahead. He does not have her whole horizon in view. [*Recovery days*, 『そして、幕が開く』, 9.129–145, 9.155–166.]

Abe’s phrasing matters. The novel gives him 見過ごせない, “can’t overlook it,” softened by ちょっと and a drawn-out ねぇ. His cough lets Chisato recognize the difficulty for herself before he names it. There is no demand for her ID and no report of an offense. She has voiced an intention in front of someone she knows is a detective. Elsewhere in this book an entirely different sort of cough makes him ask, coffee cup in hand, whether she has a cold. Chisato thinks she will be fine. Mizuki brings a limited-time kiwi parfait, and the narration likens the alteration of his smile to a child’s. Takina asks about symptoms, Mika proposes calling the doctor, and Takina directs Chisato to rest. Abe gets one familiar question and a dessert he evidently looks forward to. The source has given him an appetite as well as a conscience. It leaves the care decisions to the people responding to Chisato's different circumstances. [*Recovery days*, 『Cough』, 12.6–38.]

His professional identity also comes in handy to people who are joking in his absence. In *Ordinary days*, Itō teases the reporter Tokuda that he has joined the criminal classes; Yoneoka pretends to lament having to report an acquaintance and offers him the chance to turn himself in to Detective Abe first. They have overheard Chisato ask about some “stuff.” They laugh. It is Yoneoka who imagines calling Abe. There is no call, investigation or evidence here about what Abe knows of the later account of Kana. He is familiar enough that naming him completes the joke. [*Ordinary days*, 『ご注文は？』, 18.19–36.]

## What a paying customer gives back

The favor Mika starts to ask of Abe in *Gluttony days* is almost the inverse of Chisato's audible plan about the late show. Kurumi has said something is odd, and then that she cannot confirm it yet. She has not explained the interrupted communications to Abe. With Mika she can have the conversation once he has gone. The box Mika puts together gives Abe a gracious way to stand by the value of a visit cut short. His words 今日もおいしかった, “it was good today too,” acknowledge the work as the work of a familiar host. The apologetic smile, the hand at his head and the other hand already accepting the gift allow pleasure to survive embarrassment. The commercial transaction still matters to him. There is also something here he is willing to give Mika for free, namely the space to have a conversation whose contents he does not know. [*Gluttony days*, 『ファントム・メナス』, 9.891–910.]

This is Kurumi’s view of events. The text wonders whether he sensed something. Kurumi thinks a naturally cheerful man can be quick to pick things up and supposes his conduct here speaks well of his work as a detective. She also thinks about the value of exchanges she would once have found economically absurd. Her assessment is revealing because she knows just how much depends on this one visitor's response. It leaves open how much he guessed. An ordinary regular, hearing the proprietor start to ask him a favor, could have made the same exit. The readers know whose safety is at issue. Abe heard enough to leave and too little to have made an informed choice to suppress the case. Kurumi’s teasing description of the two middle-aged men after he goes belongs to her. There is no evidence here of a romantic attachment or a private pact between them. [*Gluttony days* 9.894–909, 9.910–919.]

## What he will show Mitani

On television Abe does something that a general theory of his reluctance to get involved would have trouble explaining. He brings Chisato and the newly introduced Takina a stalking complaint outside his own assignment. The police are slow to act, he says, and he thinks Saori Shinohara may speak more easily to other girls. He asks them to hear her out and offers to pay well. Saori independently tells them the police treated it as a lovers’ quarrel even though she has no former partner to explain it. She later asks the girls to thank the detective. Abe's small decision gives her a hearing. Chisato and Takina subsequently perceive something in her photograph that makes the danger far greater than the request suggests. Abe neither presents that discovery nor claims credit for making it. His familiarity with the café has made an alternative available when his office offers little. It has also moved the work of a first interview onto two young people who subsequently encounter a danger Abe has never named. The helpful result does not dispose of that cost. [E01 C0307–0325, 12:45–13:27; C0351–0358, C0371–0380, 14:51–15:59.]

Mitani gets a less comfortable favor. At the closed railway site he asks why even the police cannot go in and how there can be no witnesses. Abe gives him いろいろ あんだよ きっとな, a deliberately unspecific answer about the kinds of things that go on. Yet he subsequently searches the wreckage and tells Mitani that he wanted to show him what he found. A bullet brings the official account of an accident into question. Abe says others have already been recovered, that incidents have been turned into accidents since the old tower attack, and that he cannot tell whether somebody is solving them or simply covering them up. It is Mitani who wants to tell the chief. Abe believes the chief is acting under orders and considers it dangerous to challenge whoever can keep so much out of sight. A workman finds the two men where they have no business being. Abe urges flight, and afterward admits the trouble with trespassing. He has violated the boundary of the site to educate another policeman about the limits of being a policeman. He has also decided for himself where that education should stop. [E04 C0383–0395, 19:26–20:03; E05 C0114–0143, 07:06–08:15, E05-CLM-006/007.]

His next explanation gives his choice a human face. Chisato calls to him. He notices Takina too, asks if the two of them are going to a festival, and praises them when Chisato says they are guiding a customer. Once she is gone, he tells Mitani that if a girl like that can live in safety, what does it matter who has covered up what? This appeal follows his admission of real danger to anyone who inquires. Self-preservation and concern for familiar people can both be genuine reasons. The irony the viewer can see is that Chisato herself goes into danger to maintain the everyday world whose existence Abe finds so persuasive. She gave him a truthful description of one part of her day. He has no demonstrated access to the rest. Mitani's dissatisfaction matters because the young colleague has looked at the same evidence and found a different responsibility in it. [E05 C0128–0135, C0143–0157, 07:39–07:59 and 08:13–08:51; REV-E05-006.]

## The ordinary life he invokes

The café patrons watching the train story on television recognize him on the news. Yoneoka’s immediate conclusion is that he will not come in that day. When the regulars get together to play, Abe admits he is actually on duty. He beckons Takina over. She declines the various invitations, and he wonders aloud if there are too many middle-aged men. Itō supplies her own explanation. Neither speaks for Takina, and Abe does not press her to prove him wrong. At a later game gathering he asks after Chisato, welcomes her and urges her to hurry because his lunch break will end. In E08 he sounds interested in the possibility of a daytime drink; Mitani reminds him he is on duty. The scene gives him the question and gives Mitani the correction. It does not show Abe drinking. The colleague who had to be taught the limits of the job is perfectly capable of reminding his senior of a simple one. These jokes give a measure to what “ordinary” means in Abe’s defense of secrecy. He has something he likes to do at lunch and people who notice when work keeps him away. [E03 C0040–0060, 02:54–03:38; E04 C0388–0395; E07 C0035–0041, C0416–0419 and C0446–0451, 02:53, 20:27 and 21:25–21:37; E08 C0017–0022, 01:04–01:17.]

This does not make him indifferent to a concrete police job. When Kurumi locates Robota and the tip reaches the police, Abe radios that the reported culprit has been secured. The information came from somebody else; the police made the arrest. Later Mitani calls Abe’s attention to the lights going out at Enkūboku. Abe immediately asks Robota if he has done something again and demands he remove his headgear. The suspect denies knowing anything and resists having his face exposed. Here is an important counterexample to the considerate customer at Mika’s counter. Faced with an apparent fresh emergency and a suspect already in custody, Abe presses for an answer. Suspicion is visible; proof that Robota caused the blackout does not follow. [E12 C0373–0386, 17:43–18:18, E12-C08; E13 C0106–0111, 04:52–05:02.]

In the café afterward, a phone displays an image of a red-uniformed figure. Abe is still looking at it. Mitani's “again?” makes this repeated attention, and Abe asks whether his colleague is already satisfied. Mitani says that the commendation for Robota's capture has made him the chief's dog. He gives back a version of Abe’s own いろいろ, all the things that go on. Abe repeats the word and concedes the point. Mitani has acquired a professional reward he can name, a reason to be less insistent, and the vocabulary with which his senior once fended him off. His irony leaves room to doubt how content either man is. Abe gets a second look, not a confession of changed allegiance. The phone does not establish that he can identify that figure, has penetrated DA, or has decided to disclose anything. The lasting change we can actually see is between the two men. The one who had to be shown can now invoke his teacher's words and leave him thinking. [E13 C0319–0324, 14:53.54–15:05; retained pictures #000802 at 14:52.642, #000804 at 14:54.977, #000807 at 14:59.420 and #000809 at 15:02.568.]

## What to expect when he returns

Give Asaura’s Abe a familiar host, an unfinished request for privacy and no firsthand evidence of somebody in immediate danger. A plausible ordinary choice would be to finish his drink, insist the host's work still has value, and make it easy to see him again. The source supplies one especially good example and one countercondition. If a young regular has just announced a concrete intention he thinks he ought to discourage, he can amiably interrupt and offer her another way to do what she wants. How much trouble either choice would cost his career has never been tested in the novels. [*Gluttony days* 9.895–909; *Recovery days* 9.132–145.]

TV Abe can similarly look beyond his assignment to someone who needs to be heard. He may take considerable trouble to show a colleague a fact while resisting its open pursuit. When an identified suspect and an actionable report are available, the series lets him perform ordinary police work and ask an uncomfortable question. None of those situations tests a demand to betray Mika, a direct objection from Saori to the referral, or a verified revelation about Chisato's own work. His upbringing, family and intimate life, the origin of his friendship with the staff, his precise rank, and what he might risk once a personally known victim makes continued silence untenable all remain open. The question worth carrying forward is whether Abe would still reach for his answer about ordinary safety if the person he meant to protect were present to dispute it. [E01 C0311–0321; E05 C0123–0157; E12 C0377–0385; E13 C0319–0324.]
