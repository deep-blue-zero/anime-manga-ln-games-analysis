# Itō

The regulars put down their guns when the woman with the camera calls the scene finished. Itō thanks them and gathers them around her laptop. She has taken stills and video for a difficult scene in her manga. She intends to trace much of what she has captured. Is there anything anyone wants her to change? Doi would like to look more distinguished. A middle school girl would like to look cool. The two café workers, Chisato and Takina, have played the heroine and her rival. Chisato wants a correction to somebody *else's* hand. She demonstrates the difference, Itō immediately makes a note, and the group reassures the embarrassed writer Tokuda that his performance was fine. Itō will draw the scene. To get it right she needs these people to be available to her as bodies, critics and occasionally rather vain collaborators. Her question about revisions lets each of them become something more than a figure she can trace. [*Ordinary days*, introduction 4, 15.14–51.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_ORDINARY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md)

Itō (伊藤) is a working manga artist around thirty who uses Café LycoReco as an extension of her studio. She likes a table wide enough for a notebook and pens or a drawing tablet. In *Recovery days*, Takina knows this habit well enough to notice the evening Itō arrives empty-handed, takes a counter seat and barely looks at the menu. She has come to hire help. A customer who seems to be asking a café employee if she knows how to use a gun is in fact a woman who needs two drawn faces to come alive on a page. Some of the people she is asking really do carry guns. Their occupations are a secret from her. That imbalance makes for good comedy until the night the difference puts a hole through her work. [*Recovery days*, “Safety work,” 10.2–55, 10.559–589.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_RECOVERY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md)

## What a page needs

Itō works alone at a desk in the apartment where she lives. Shelves and boxes of books take up much of the space. She inks characters on paper and finishes digitally, including the backgrounds. A completely digital workflow is beyond her current skill, while doing everything by hand has become wearing and traditional screen tones are harder to buy. When Chisato suggests computer graphics to solve a reference problem, Itō says quite plainly that she can't do that. She has borrowed a model through an acquaintance, would consider buying the right replacement for future use, and dislikes the prospect of being accused of tracing somebody else's online photograph. She has already found the angle she wants. The problem is how to put the particular thing she needs in front of her from that angle. The distinction matters alongside her declared intention in *Ordinary days* to trace photographs she took at a cooperative session. Here she has made her own reference and can discuss it with the subjects. Online she expects to have neither exactly the view she needs nor an answer to accusations about copying. In another part of her practice she has already gone ahead before asking. [*Recovery days* 10.159–175, 10.318–345.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_RECOVERY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md) [*Ordinary days* 15.30–39.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_ORDINARY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md)

In “Safety work,” Itō has **already used** Chisato and Takina as character models when she explains that she would like their permission. She jokes that the situation calls for getting down on her knees to obtain it after the fact. Chisato joins enthusiastically. Takina's initial objection takes place in her head. First she thinks permission should come before use, then she wonders how much she knows about either manga or ordinary social expectations. When Chisato accepts the job, Takina understands that she is going along and still doesn't know quite what it entails. In Itō's apartment, after Takina twice directs the talk back to the work, Itō makes a much more precise request. She would like them to pose for a scene that is difficult to draw and let her photograph the pose. She even switches to polite Japanese. When Takina asks why she is using polite speech, Itō replies “just because.” Takina does pose, and later reminds Itō to take the picture. Successive acts of cooperation are visible here. An advance agreement to use Takina's likeness, a license for every future appearance and Takina's private approval of Chisato answering for her are different questions. [*Recovery days* 10.55–89, 10.165–186, 10.239–252.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_RECOVERY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md)

The assignment is a thirty-six-page one-shot, with most of the inking done and the backgrounds ready. Itō lacks a decisive image of the two heroines. She has invented Chise, a schoolgirl who kills for a living, and Tamaki, the heiress Chise initially mistakes for someone needing rescue. The pair will meet again at school. Chisato can already see the pleasure of a serial in which the ostensible enemies have to act together. She and Itō enjoy improvising future developments while Takina is still holding the pose. The fictional setting and the heroines' purposes are Itō's. Chisato's appetite for genre helps her imagine the partnership's future. Takina sees something else in the rough drawing. One panel has already let her infer an entire preceding movement. To a reader accustomed to actual danger the situation is legible. Itō's answer is that legibility is only the beginning. She wants 迫力, a force in the picture that will make a reader feel the moment the two young women discover what each is capable of. [*Recovery days* 10.75–89, 10.215–243, 10.279–298.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_RECOVERY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md)

When Takina and Chisato enact the meeting, the impact against the bookshelf startles Itō. She takes out her phone at Takina's prompting, then stops before taking the picture to ask whether Chisato is all right. Once reassured, she circles them with the phone. She knows immediately which expressions she wants. A detail of Chisato's hand looks wrong to her until Chisato explains what her character could be doing. Itō reverses her judgment and proposes an additional close-up of the hand, so readers can see the balance of confidence and skill change. She asks the two to hold the position while she sketches it. Later a comment from Takina makes Itō stop her pen and swivel her chair. The reference item she has borrowed looks out of place in the sale depicted in her story. Takina herself allows that the choice is possible. Itō hears the makings of the kind of mistake readers will pillory online. Chisato's attempt to defend a little artistic license goes badly when she, too, uses the word *mistake*. Itō can change a panel on the spot when a better idea excites her. Being asked to settle for one she no longer believes in is harder. [*Recovery days* 10.239–280, 10.298–345.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_RECOVERY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md)

## How long an editor can wait

There is money in the distinction. The magazine's lead artist has hurt their back. The empty pages could go to Itō now or to somebody else with a finished manuscript. She has no current serial in this story. If this particular piece goes down well, there is a possibility of serialization. Waiting months to find out could keep her from pitching it elsewhere and leave her relying on illustration jobs or short pieces with little prospect of producing collected-volume income. A prompt rejection would at least free her to draw something else. Takina thinks Itō should decide which matters more, the best possible reference or delivering on time. Itō walks back to her tablet. To Takina it looks like a decision to prioritize the deadline that Itō hasn't quite made her peace with. That is the young visitor's reading of a working artist facing two losses. Itō has already concisely explained why “there will be other chances” does little for her. [*Recovery days* 10.348–369.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_RECOVERY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md)

Her friends' solutions create fresh demands on the person they're trying to help. Itō asks Chisato and Takina to keep their voices down when their debate about what to feed her becomes too loud. She gives them leave to use what is in the refrigerator. At the meal she is the one who realizes that *tamago fuwafuwa* is the actual name Takina keeps giving Chisato for the dish. She thanks both women, laughs when Takina points out how much time Chisato spent reading in bed, and tells them they make a good pair. She enjoys company, has had little conversation lately and recognizes how easily Chisato gets her talking. She can also tell both visitors to be quiet in her home. Looking into her refrigerator, the girls cannot tell whether the sparse ingredients reflect her working hours or how she personally likes to eat. Itō's pleasure in this particular meal is on much firmer ground. [*Recovery days* 10.168–180, 10.421–463, 10.491–519, 10.529–558.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_RECOVERY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md)

The replacement reference the two bring her is a real firearm obtained on a separate assignment. Chisato tells Itō that a gun enthusiast lent them a remarkably realistic model. Itō notices its oily, industrial smell, has Takina pose again and photographs her. Later she can report concrete progress. The big scene is nearly drawn and she has a few smaller views left to correct. She thanks them for the “model gun,” then brings it back in her own hands. It discharges. Her tablet breaks and there is a hole in the wall. Nobody is injured by the shot. The girls who brought the object discover that each had expected the other to complete the safety check. Itō's first bewildered words are about the noise, the broken tablet, and the manuscript data she has only on that device. There is an explanation for her trust that doesn't require her to be foolish. She asked the two for a reference, told them a replica would do, and accepted what Chisato said they had supplied. [*Recovery days* 10.329–345, 10.369–395, 10.405–423, 10.559–592.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_RECOVERY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md)

The helpers can tell one another the truth. Takina apologizes to Chisato and Chisato acknowledges her own omission. Chisato tells Itō that the discharge was a dream. The account they give Itō after she wakes attributes the broken tablet to a dropped heavy metal replica. While Itō sleeps, Kurumi gets the damaged device working again and copies the data to her own computer. Chisato later pours hot coffee into the half-awake woman's mouth, and the heat rouses her. Itō resumes drawing on the damaged tablet. Her editor eventually reports car trouble. Takina has inferred that Kurumi intends to delay the editor, whose name and number Kurumi asked for. Itō has no scene of discovering the firearm's origin, approving that interference or learning the full terms of her rescue. When the cheerleading becomes so noisy that Takina thinks it might be impeding the work, Itō says nothing and goes on drawing. Her spoken resolve to finish for the people cheering her is better evidence of what their presence means to her than the mere absence of complaint. [*Recovery days* 10.570–610, 10.637–675, 10.698–702.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_RECOVERY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md)

The editor receives the pages. Itō has finished them herself. Kurumi restored the files, Mika brought the coffee, and Chisato and Takina contributed poses, criticism and a good deal of avoidable trouble. When the group disperses, payment has been put off for later. The story specifies neither an amount nor a settled agreement that the labor was free. Mizuki's solo café shift is beginning around the time they finish. In the subsequent illness story Chisato recalls going to a movie, returning to the café and finding Mizuki still there needing help. When Chisato falls ill, Itō sends forehead cooling sheets. Mika says Itō came into the café and quickly supplied them from the things she had on hand. Chisato opens them and puts one on. This is a small, specific return of care from a person who has also spent long hours working through exhaustion. Nothing in it suggests that she learned the truth about her tablet. [*Recovery days* 10.675–702, 12.105–116, 12.393–404.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_RECOVERY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md)

## The girl beside the drawing tablet

Some of the most revealing evidence about Itō comes when she has barely looked at the person she is addressing. In *Ordinary days* she is struggling with a color illustration for a collected-volume cover. At a neighboring table a middle school customer, Kana, is working out how to eat an affogato. Itō tells her how while continuing to draw. She recognizes Kana's school uniform because she used to live nearby. She knows how far it is from the café, wonders if Kana travels for lessons and then guesses at dance, voice or performance training. She asks the girl to tell her more because she might get an idea out of it. Tokuda can see Kana recoil. From where he sits it seems that Itō, still looking at the tablet, hasn't noticed. When she finally looks up, she sees how pale Kana has gone. Chisato's arrival relieves the room. The narration stays with Tokuda as he notices that Kana's spoon has come to a standstill while the ice cream melts. The experienced observer whose pictures can capture a shift in expression has talked straight past one. [*Ordinary days* 17.10–16, 17.36–46, 17.59–91.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_ORDINARY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md)

After closing, Itō has finished the line art. Time and stamina have run short and a designer will handle the coloring. The narration now gives us her thoughts. She appreciates Chisato for modeling, for giving honest comments on her rough storyboards while always finding something to praise, for helping without waiting to be asked and for easing a back stiff from a day drawing. The same passage lets her look at the writer Yoneoka, who has cheerfully decided that his own deadline is physically impossible, and think, *I mustn't end up like that.* The grown woman who can talk herself into staying up most of a night has delegated part of this job and feels some pride in having finished her share. There is camaraderie here and an edge of professional judgment. [*Ordinary days* 18.2–18.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_ORDINARY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md)

Freed from the cover, she does think about Kana again. Itō's question this time concerns money. Refreshments and return fare add up, the journey takes time, and Kana's irregular visits make regular lessons an inadequate explanation. Itō has noticed enough to challenge two alternatives her fellow customers propose. She has also made an assumption about what a girl with spare money would spend on clothes, hair and cosmetics. Yoneoka disputes that line of reasoning. Mika suggests they stop prying into someone else's privacy. Later Chisato asks if Itō might write a mystery for a quarterly that has commissioned a one-shot. Itō has never drawn one and points out how difficult the plotting would be. She doesn't understand why Chisato thought of her. The next viewpoint belongs to Kana, who really is distressed that someone recognized the uniform. The reader sees both the accuracy of Itō's attention and the limits of her right to pursue it. She has identified a costly journey and an inadequate explanation. Kana's private circumstances and her fear that Itō might come to look for her or investigate who she is are unavailable to Itō. [*Ordinary days* 18.33–63, 18.70–99.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_ORDINARY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md)

## Who gets to use the picture

In the group shoot from *Ordinary days*, the people Itō photographs have agreed to help with reference for a current serial. She tells them what she will do with their poses and invites requests for changes. Her informal, appreciative Japanese suits people who are willing to play along. In *Recovery days* her easiest companion in the room is Chisato, who can make a game of a desperate pitch. With the less easily diverted Takina she can ask for a pose formally, receive a correction she dreads and thank her for the meal. Her 「私」 belongs both to the artist who states her technical limits and the shocked woman who can barely finish a sentence about *my* tablet and *my* pages. The variations are in written speech and narrative timing. The novels provide a civilian's range of contact with the café while withholding the café's operational life from her. Her playful matchmaking and a far-fetched wish that she could marry Chisato to a son *if she had one* add color to her fondness for the girl. They establish neither an actual son nor a romantic history for Itō herself. [*Ordinary days* 15.30–58, 15.65–85, 18.9–18.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_ORDINARY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md) [*Recovery days* 10.34–58, 10.175–186, 10.329–345, 10.559–576.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_RECOVERY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md)

The series of pictures Itō takes is separate from the magazine feature for which Tokuda arranges a shoot. At the latter, after Tokuda announces shooting is over, the magazine photographer takes the candid picture of Chisato feeding Takina a bite of pancake. Tokuda expects it to be printed and the issue bears him out. Itō neither takes that picture nor obtains its release. Her own session makes an especially good comparison because she explicitly invites her subjects to have a say in how she will draw them. The photographed group's cooperation does not settle whether everyone would agree to public distribution of the raw files, future reuse or someone else's candid image. Similarly, *Ordinary days* gives Itō a current serial with a Chisato-modeled lead and a rival played by Takina, whereas *Recovery days* gives her a one-shot with comparable roles and a genuine hope of serialization. The resemblance is grounds to consider a connection. There is no named publication or editor's acceptance identifying the former as the confirmed outcome of the latter. The separately mentioned quarterly commission supplies a third description, without a settled genre. [*Ordinary days* 15.30–39, 18.74–80, 19.28–45, 19.69, 22.2–32, 22.38–56.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_ORDINARY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md) [*Recovery days* 10.279–294, 10.351–363, 10.698–700.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_RECOVERY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md)

In *Gluttony days* Chisato quotes Itō in her absence. According to the artist, tomorrow doesn't start until the editor comes into work in the morning. Takina dismisses that as a special case when Chisato invokes it in their argument over staying up late. In “Safety work,” Itō herself had distinguished last night's nominal deadline from the morning hour when this particular editor usually comes in. As an observation born of necessity it is exact enough to help her deliver a manuscript. As a rule for somebody else's bedtime it is splendidly unpersuasive. [*Gluttony days* 14.199–215.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_GLUTTONY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md) [*Recovery days* 10.659–675.](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Prose/LYCORIS_RECOIL_RECOVERY_DAYS_DEEP_READING.md)

Itō is most likely to revise when another person can show her what a reader will see differently. If the change threatens a deadline, the risk of getting the picture wrong and the need to earn a living both press on her. Her real concessions include handing coloring to a designer and taking help from people with skills unlike hers. Her real obstinacy includes insisting on the angle after an apparently easier solution appears. If a regular showed her a better expression with a stand-in to which she had permission, there is good reason to expect her to weigh what the expression adds before deciding if the change will fit the deadline. The novel gives a photographed pose and a revised hand detail to support that expectation. No scene tests how she would respond if the regular revoked approval after the page was finished. She can check whether a model got hurt before taking the shot, and she can press a child for an anecdote while missing the child's distress. Trust in a friend can buy her a memorable image and expose her to a danger the friend conceals. The next material test would be a disclosure about the accident, a model explicitly withholding permission, or an editor's actual decision about a serial. Her response to any of those remains open. She deserves the attribution the ending of “Safety work” gives her in practice. After everyone else's intervention, somebody still has to sit down at the damaged tablet and finish the pages.
