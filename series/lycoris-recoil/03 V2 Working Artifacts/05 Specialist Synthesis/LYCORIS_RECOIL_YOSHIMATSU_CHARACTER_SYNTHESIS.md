# Shinji Yoshimatsu · Promises to the people he thinks he knows

This private portrait follows 吉松シンジ, the man Chisato knows as ヨシさん, across *Lycoris Recoil* TV E01–E13. It refers to the six *Friends are thieves of time* shorts as the rest of the frozen anime boundary, with no new trial of his conduct there, and identifies separately the licensed comics that portray him or remember him. It contains full spoilers. The broader study of the Alan Institute follows its publicly described philanthropy and the institutional conditions of the gifts. Here the question is what Yoshimatsu does when somebody he likes fails to want the life he has chosen for them.

## The customer and the man watching

Chisato welcomes an ordinary customer in E02. She notices he has not been there for a month. “You remembered,” he says. At first she jokes that the shop has so few customers that he could hardly be forgotten, then gives another reason. He was Takina’s first. Yoshimatsu says he has been to Russia and gives her a souvenir. She asks whether Russia was also where he met Mika. Mika interrupts to get her ready for work. Yoshimatsu laughs and puts the answer off. The episode gives him an evident interest in being remembered, a taste for presenting an object after a trip, and an opportunity to keep a history private without withdrawing his warmth. He is capable of making a social situation pleasurable for someone whose presence matters to him. Takina's first customer is also someone with the means to arrange a life-saving intervention, and Chisato knows neither that particular history nor the project he intends to make of it. [E02 03:23.98–04:07.98, REL-CHI-YOS-E02-001.]

Another visit gives him a name for the kind of work that put him there. He calls it 掃除, cleaning, saying it took longer than expected. E01 has already shown him confirming that he commissioned Walnut's DA breach and promising the agreed payment. He then calls ignorance happiness. He has reason to contain somebody who knows more than he wants known. In the later visit he believes the problem has been taken care of. When Robota thinks he has eliminated Walnut, Yoshimatsu praises what he finds useful in him and describes him as tool-like after the exchange. Walnut is alive in the same café. Yoshimatsu can act on knowledge inaccessible to the daily people at the counter and still have an unexamined view of the outcome they know to be false. In the same visit he says he likes Mika’s ohagi. He remembers when Mika could scarcely brew coffee, and Mika replies that ten years will change things. The foods and jokes are small, directly evidenced comforts in a private life the show otherwise gives sparingly. Their ease does not require supposing that the faintly observant regular is a false role. Both the appetite and the amount of information he withholds belong to this man. [E01 C0504 at 20:52.94 and 21:16.76, YOS-MB-E01-001; E02 16:51–17:25 and 20:55.86–21:20, E02-CLM-008/010.]

His route into Alan and earlier life remain mostly unavailable. The name Alan Adams circulates in a public account of philanthropy. It is neither supplied as his legal identity nor an exact explanation of his position. He can speak in the organization's name, obtain access to exceptional devices and enlist accomplices. The episodes show what power he can put to use without enumerating his rank, succession rights, legal jurisdiction, family origin or private training. The formative history that is recoverable concerns Mika and the child, and it is revealed in a different order to different people. In E04 he says he returned because he wanted to see Mika. Mika calls it teasing and assumes Chisato is the motive. Yoshimatsu acknowledges that the girl did not recognize him. He invokes a rule against involvement with recipients when Mika asks why he never told her who saved her. When Mika points to the contradiction with his frequent visits, Yoshimatsu asks if he is being told to disappear. The friend denies it. Yoshimatsu brings the conversation back to the promise. The shifts are observable. The source gives little ground for assigning his visit to a single settled motive. [E04 15:06.64–16:04.19, YOS-MB-E04-001, REL-MIK-YOS-E04-001.]

## Who hears the promise

We hear the substance of the adults’ promise late, through Mika’s account to Kurumi in E09. An artificial heart might keep the child alive to adulthood. Yoshimatsu sets out what it offers and admits its limited durability. The younger Mika begins to talk about how long the active life of a Lycoris ordinarily lasts. Yoshimatsu makes explicit the part that the other man has left under an ellipsis. Would that time allow her to kill enough? He names realization of the talent for killing as the essential condition. Mika says he will meet the expectation. The specific numbers and the technical advantages come from what these men say. They agree about enough of the purpose to make the resulting decade difficult for both of them to look back on. [E09 11:28.63–12:27.56, E09-CLM-020/021.]

The conversation that turns an arrangement into a relation tests whether there is anything else that matters to the giver. Yoshimatsu asks whether Mika gets along with children. Mika says taking care of them is work and suggests he would be doing something else if it were possible. When Yoshimatsu asks him to help the girl use her talent after surgery, Mika answers that DA will take responsibility. Yoshimatsu says he is asking *him*. He calls her already “our daughter,” then asks whether that is unacceptable. Mika calls it an unfamiliar mission. Yoshimatsu objects to the noun and calls it a promise between the two of them. The institution's backing is available and fails to answer the personal question he is asking. He wants Mika himself to take the girl into his life. That makes the request more comprehensible without making it innocent. The conditions of help were voiced before it, and the child has no part in deciding how to divide gratitude, childrearing and a career among her adults. Mika later recalls the undertaking with embarrassment and guilt, calling the resulting family 親子ごっこ. Yoshimatsu’s affection is substantial enough to make a comparison with mere institutional compliance fail. The tender possessive does nothing to enlarge the child's power over the proposal. [E09 12:34.32–13:26, YOS-MB-E09-001, REL-MIK-YOS-E09-001.]

The child recognizes him before she knows why he arranged it. She asks directly if he is the one who will save her. He claims to be a member of staff and she says he is lying, citing his conspicuous suit. He laughs and thanks her. When she asks what she can do in return he says she has a great mission. He says he has in effect become a savior in order to help bring it about. The child repeats 救世主 and tells him she will become a savior too. A postoperative gift later reaches her through Mika. She reasons from the giver that this is a gun for saving people. The gift and the way she interprets it are different steps. Her conclusion is a coherent response to the experience of being helped. It does not need the audience to believe that he gave her a script for this interpretation. For roughly ten years he will have the consequences of what the adults arranged without either a beneficiary who knowingly accepted it or a pupil who wants what he wanted. [E09 13:44.97–14:46.07 and 15:19.44–15:52.47, E09-CLM-025–027; E10-CLM-022–029.]

The evidence for a deeply intimate history with Mika is stronger than the evidence for any official name it had. Yoshimatsu tells Mika in E07 that his heart leapt when he first visited his café, as it had the day they met years earlier. The scene recalls their first formal introductions and the confidence of two men with secrets that they could get along. The later café familiarity and the parental language let the narrative make room for affection even under threat. Chisato and the others speculate about Mika and Kusunoki earlier. Their observations when Yoshimatsu arrives concern the way Mika behaves around this particular visitor. In the E13 room Mika says there are things one wants to hide, especially from those one loves, after Yoshimatsu sees what his leg can do. The dialogue supplies intimacy in the men's own terms without supplying a name for the relationship. Conclude that an intimate history and a shared sense of parenting matter to his decisions. Abstain on a particular legal status, sexual history or shared domestic schedule. [E07 06:13–06:30, 14:22–15:16 and 18:00.14–18:42.02, REL-MIK-YOS-E07-001/002; E09 12:57.09–13:13; E13 07:04–07:12.]

## Recognition as a way of making a claim

Yoshimatsu's most useful conversational skill is to make another person feel he has identified something valuable about them. Himegama cooks him a meal in E08. He thanks her, says it was good and tells her she has talent as a cook. She asks whether Alan would have supported that path if she had chosen it. That is the first part of the sentence he answers. The gift comes from God, he says, and the person’s role is indicated at birth. Himegama supplies a consequence. Nobody then needs to search for a meaning to life. Yoshimatsu calls that happiness. She later says she cannot promise to treat Chisato gently, given the circumstances. He tells her that *she* can do it, and she answers yes. By the end of the episode medical trust gives her an opportunity to place Chisato in danger. The details of that intervention cannot all be read back into his order. What the scene gives us is how he handles both the cook's pleasure and the agent's objection. A compliment is easier for him to offer than jurisdiction over what her talent means or the conditions under which the job may be possible. [E08 00:08.84–01:03.86 and 21:16–21:43, YOS-MB-E08-001/CLM-003/004.]

His language with Robota has a recognizable asymmetry. He can applaud a useful outcome in polite terms without treating the person who supplies it as an equal. In E07 his address to Takina as たきなちゃん moves in a more intimate direction. She means to repair a bad encounter by saying Chisato had been glad to see him and asking him to come to the café. He interrupts her incomplete attempt to tell him how long Chisato has wanted to see him. He says she should understand that Chisato belongs somewhere else and that he expects something of her. He has accurately identified an attachment. He translates it prematurely into an expectation of service. His honorific for her cannot make that distinction disappear any more than her courtesy to him ratifies what he is proposing. By E12 this same attachment supplies a person who is prepared to act with terrible urgency independently of him. [E02 16:51–17:25, E02-CLM-010; E07 18:52.20–19:19, YOS-MB-E07-002 and REL-TAK-YOS-E07-001; E12 05:41–07:16.]

He also speaks differently to Chisato and to Mika about the same event. When Chisato at last recognizes her benefactor and thanks him, he says that Alan children have roles and directs her to speak to Mika. He leaves the table before the claim is stated to her in full. To Mika in private he explains that talent belongs neither to the person who carries it nor to the people who support it. It belongs to God. “We promised” is the term to which he returns when Mika pleads that he give Chisato the time she wants. Mika threatens him and later admits that the resolve he claimed out loud was unavailable to him. The unequal access to the crucial words matters. Chisato can understand Yoshimatsu as a giver, reject his disappointment and still lack the claim about killing the adults have exchanged. Mika knows the exact intended role. He is the man Yoshimatsu expects to translate an abstraction into a duty for a child. In E10 Mika finally discloses his own part to Chisato. As a commander he asked Shinji for the surgery with an interest in what a strong Lycoris would mean for him. In Mika's account Yoshimatsu personally stipulates that she be raised as the strongest killer. Chisato initially has trouble accepting that account of her savior. She then thanks Mika for letting her choose and imagines what a different upbringing might have made of her. By E12 she knows the terms, who proposed them and which adult left her room to choose. Her gratitude to the giver can therefore coexist with an informed refusal of his demand. [E07 15:18–18:47, REL-CHI-YOS-E07-001 and MIK-MB-E07-001; E10 15:45–16:14 and 17:07–17:30, E10-CLM-022–029.]

His sense of being a father can coexist with the larger principle. Majima claims to be another person who lives as he chooses and threatens Yoshimatsu. Yoshimatsu praises him as an outstanding Alan Child and points to the help he supplied. He says he would offer his life to realize the ideal. Given the opportunity to complain that someone might kill him, he frames that possible loss as another resource for the purpose he serves. Meanwhile he calls Chisato's departure from her supposed path his own mistake. The fact that he can call it his failure protects an important part of the portrait. A man who considers himself personally responsible has a reason to persist even when it will cost more than money or control. He can accept blame for failed execution while keeping almost no room open for the possibility that he was wrong about what Chisato owed. [E10 04:18.28–05:24, E10-CLM-013–015, YOS-MB-E10-001.]

## Failure changes his instruments faster than his criterion

He can take a slight against a mission very personally. In E06 Himegama relays Yoshimatsu's encouragement to try hard to Robota, then makes a polite demand of her own. In E06 and E08 the people carrying his wishes have purposes and objections of their own. Robota initially says that Majima is uninterested, and Himegama tells him to continue. Majima's interest eventually turns into a challenge with aims of his own. In E08 Himegama carries out an intervention that damages Chisato's ability to recharge her heart. A wish to make the girl exercise her lethal skill produces actual bodily harm. The loss of future time is testified to in E09 by Yamagishi, with the estimate contingent on exertion. Yoshimatsu's words connect him to the direction of the operation, while the exact clinical plan and his private understanding of every consequence remain open. What matters for his portrait is the transition from being disappointed by Mika to increasing pressure on a person whose trust is available through an ordinary appointment. [E06 C0127–0129 and 12:17–12:25, E06-CLM-012; E08 00:45–01:04 and 21:16–21:43; E09 03:25–04:44.]

The fake client in E05 permits an earlier look at the same habit with less direct certainty about its personnel. Chisato spends time showing an apparent client around and hears a demand for revenge later. The demand uses her life-gift to argue she should kill Jin. The display of the tour, Yoshimatsu's subsequent instruction and Himegama's answer give strong evidence of his side arranging a test. A unique remote operator is never established. It attempts to use an existing gratitude for being alive and her concern for the man she believes she has guided. The hours of guiding and caring have already happened and retain a value of their own. Chisato refuses the demand while the circumstances behind it are still obscure to her. Later, when Chisato doubts even the praise she received for guiding the visitor, Takina says what she did well could still be true. A day the planner wanted to make into leverage has yielded two other people's experience of useful work and care. He can give the apparent client an argument. He does not determine the only meaning the people in the room find in what happened to them. [E05 16:22–21:07 and 21:58–22:11, E05-CLM-013–018/020; E12 05:41–06:17, CLM-008.]

In E11 Yoshimatsu's earlier and later conversations with the heart researcher become recoverable through material Kurumi analyzes. He originally describes having found a good subject. In a subsequent conversation the scientist credits Alan. Yoshimatsu responds that “we only helped,” then calls one girl's life a small cost if the man's talent comes to fruition. The scientist asks that the girl be given the new device. Yoshimatsu urges him to think of future patients and to entrust the subject to him. His modesty about technical accomplishment and his confidence about which life counts both matter. The researcher knows enough to make a different decision about one patient. Yoshimatsu can applaud the man whose skill produced the device without granting him authority to act on what he has learned. The case gives a positive limit on his behavior beyond private cruelty. He can make a case about the people a device might one day help and use that prospect to assert control over the patient who needs it now. The conversation establishes his appeal to future patients. It does not establish who actually receives this device. The device's existence becomes a lead for Kurumi. Its position later remains separately uncertain. [E11 05:35.57–06:44.26, E11-CLM-005–008, YOS-MB-E11-001.]

## The price he offers to pay

The E12 confrontation begins with him wanting to know whether Chisato killed the person she went to rescue him from. He asks it after she has interposed herself to protect him in the previous episode. The return of a life she evidently values reaches him as a missed opportunity to exercise the talent. When she tells him she cannot take a life with a life somebody saved, he answers that very few people have a role so clear and calls that happiness. Then she tells him plainly that she has been quite happy, helping as he helped her. He says he did not wind a dying doll for that purpose. Chisato repeats “a doll” and remarks on how aptly he has put it. Her reply makes it possible to hear the label as an account of how he saw her. It is one of his least guarded answers. Her testimony gives him the information he would need to reconsider. His next response asserts his purpose as prior to her judgment. [E11 18:58–19:18, E11-CLM-024–027; E12 04:13.65–05:12.79, E12-CLM-005–007.]

Takina first treats the case as the lead to a way of saving Chisato. Yoshimatsu smiles at what she knows, then claims the heart that will let Chisato live is now in his own chest. He names what he is willing to pay and asks that Chisato kill him to regain her value and her life. The scar and the claim give the offer vivid force. The offer's bodily premises remain unverified. Takina's desire to obtain the device outweighs her interest in punishing the man. Chisato refuses. When Takina offers to act for her, Yoshimatsu predicts that in front of Chisato she will be unable to shoot him. Chisato physically stops Takina; Himegama then attacks, and he invokes the threat of a friend's death to renew the demand to kill. The progression tests different people against different wants. A wish for one's own survival proves insufficient. The other girl's danger might exert a pressure that Yoshimatsu's own death has not. The giver's willingness to die is evidence of the depth of his commitment. It also raises the cost of the ultimatum for somebody he has helped. [E12 05:41–07:39, C0104–0110 and E12-CLM-008–016, REV-E12-002.]

The cut and bodily aftermath supply a boundary he cannot impose himself. The depicted order supports Chisato shooting Yoshimatsu to protect Takina. The exact contact, load and intended severity remain open. When Chisato checks him afterward she says the projectile went through and believes he will live. She returns his pendant and tells him to stay well instead of dying for her. In one conversation she tells him she hates what he does with his own life, says she dislikes him and says that she is genuinely grateful. On being told he is doing it for her, she asks whether he means the world. He replies that it is the same thing. This is one of the clearest limits to the charitable interpretation of his behavior. Affection on his own terms may be sincere. A person he loves can state a different account of her happiness and receive no independent authority over her own answer. [E12 08:07–09:39, TV E12-C04/Q17, REV-E13-015; E12-CLM-017–020.]

## The man left with the returned token

At the last meeting Yoshimatsu discovers how much Mika has withheld about his capacity to act. He answers by calling him a person who tells lies. Mika's appeal to what they have both done for Chisato meets two first-person statements. Yoshimatsu says that he was not understood and shows the returned pendant, then says he seems no longer needed. The Japanese ASS writes 私 in both lines. Two attributed Japanese listener reports hear あたしは locally near 07:20 and 07:29. What is written and what those viewers hear support a contrast with the earlier corporate “we” and the expectation he expresses as a patron. The two disputed readings supply neither an invariant pronunciation nor a personal category. His account of feeling misunderstood and unneeded, along with the returned token, supports hearing hurt in the exchange. [E13 07:04.32–07:44.28, REL-MIK-YOS-E13-001 and TV Q13.]

Mika says children can lead adults into a world they do not know and adults should leave them that choice. Then he asks about what is supposedly there. Yoshimatsu says Chisato did not believe him. Mika tells him that Shinji does not lie. The claim is possible as a profession of trust and literally incomplete as biography. The child had already seen through his claim to work at the hospital. His reaction now is to say it is too late for that to make him happy, then admit she has also driven Mika off the course they had understood. Mika agrees. Yoshimatsu can recognize the scale of a change in somebody he knows well. The exchange provides no unequivocal recantation, return to an earlier domestic relation or assurance that his description of the device can be verified. [E09 13:44.97–13:57.65; E13 07:29.89–08:06, E13-CLM-031–033.]

The exterior image with shifting illumination is the last direct circumstance around their confrontation given to the audience. Eight presentations between 08:09.572 and 08:09.864 support a changed window. The localized audio and viewer checks do not resolve an unseen shooter or act. Later Chisato learns from Takina that Mika brought the case and hears her assurance about a new chance to live. She actually receives the birthday greeting and charm. Mika holds the decorated farewell. Yoshimatsu's attributed voice in the finished episode communicates the text to viewers. It joins a claim that his death completes her new beginning to a wish that she be happy. The fact of reported death is stronger than any precise account of its physical cause, retrieval or consensual basis. The two cards give an ordinary object to Chisato and a much larger account of its meaning to the audience. It remains possible for Yoshimatsu's last language to express continued affection even if his final interpretation still makes his act the author of her new life. Chisato's acceptance of that interpretation is unshown. [E13 08:05–08:12 and 18:52.65–19:58.22, Q13 and REV-E13-006, TV source recheck.]

## A personal future others imagine

Two creators give his absence different objects. In 竹本蒼太郎’s *Reload* S135–142, a child's drawing labels a figure as a savior without a personal name. Mika imagines a particular family arrangement that places Shinji beside her. A spent casing and the arrangement of figures put further consequence outside the pictures. It is Mika's act of imagining, leaving the identification in a child's drawing and the TV lethal event unsettled. In 大倉ナタ’s *Reload 02* S129–136, Chisato sees a drawing within that separate story and calls the man who harmed and helped her family and benefactor. Her subsequent call is to Fuki and concerns Takina's loneliness. Fuki recalls Takina's request for classified grave information and helps route a different request for Enkūboku through Kusunoki. Chisato's terms for naming her benefactor and Takina's request to honor fallen companions remain separately attributable. One cannot use the panels as Yoshimatsu's own private expectation of surviving his relationship with Chisato. [C1 named contributor readings.]

An ordinary parallel is available in ぎんもく's opening *Repeat*. Yoshimatsu comes for Mika's ohagi, repeating a taste stated in the television café. The snack keeps a specific way he likes to see someone present without constituting new evidence of his entire domestic habits. Abe's *Recollect* V02 S115–118 lets a chest gesture suggest something about Chisato's time without independently identifying a giver or explaining the means. Such portrayals preserve the difference between a gift that enables a future and the relationship one artist lets a person keep with its giver. They do not supply Shinji's unknown ordinary life away from family, work and café. [C1 ぎんもく, *Repeat* S9–20; B1 *Recollect* V02 S115–118; E02 20:55.86–21:15.]

## Predictable choices and where to abstain

If an interlocutor can do something he believes matters, Yoshimatsu is likely to notice a capacity and speak to the worth of realizing it. In a friendly situation he can mark a pleasure he really has, praise and give without immediately mentioning his purpose. The food offered by Mika and the cooking praise to Himegama support that reading. If the interlocutor makes an apparently self-directed choice that opposes his purpose, his language is likely to tighten around the capacity he named. With a known adult whose cooperation he needs, a reminder of a personal promise is better supported than a simple threat. With a professional subordinate he can combine appreciation, a constrained choice and an expectation that they can accomplish it. With a beneficiary demanding control over her life his first response is likely to interpret what she wants through what he thinks her talent means. The violence of the evidence increases as he tries to make his prediction come true. Extrapolating which threat he would select in a wholly new case would exceed what the source gives.

As the person whose affection is being asked about, he is more readable in dialogue than in interiors. He expresses pleasure at being remembered and recalls a visit that delighted him. His words about being misunderstood and unneeded convey hurt when the token comes back. He can say plainly that he will die for a purpose, which changes what a rational choice about cost can mean for him. These are positive tests against reducing him to a man who wants wealth, self-preservation or companionship alone. They leave considerable room for not knowing what would persuade him to take a recipient's testimony as decisive. A hypothetical recipient who declines a gift freely, money he has promised to someone without exceptional talent, his family obligations before meeting Mika, and the terms of later consent after the tower are untested. The reading of Yoshimatsu closes where he has to relinquish possession of a listener's future, and where the edit relinquishes possession of some of the facts we would want about his last act.
