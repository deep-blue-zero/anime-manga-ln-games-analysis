# Otome Sakura. Who gets a place beside Fuki

In the DA hallway a girl named Sakura announces that she will fill the gap Takina left. She tells Takina to rest assured, names her own appointment as Fuki's partner, and adds that Takina has no seat anymore. The reassurance is pointed at somebody who plainly seeks to return. Before that she has said that the story being told about Takina makes her sound like a movie murderer, the kind she says she likes. It is an ugly compliment with an immediate use. Sakura gets to be amused by the scandal while presenting herself as the respectable answer to it. Erika later challenges the story from inside it. Erika says she is alive, says she was the one captured, and denies that Takina was at fault. What happened to Erika, what Sakura can repeat about it, and whose job it is to fill the ensuing vacancy all have different witnesses. Sakura already knows she has the job. The episode gives her no comparable access to Takina's calculation in the field. [E03 10:41.50–11:22.71, C0204 and C0214–0222; 16:17.17–16:41.65, C0333–0342.]

She can change her address in the middle of a provocation. The stranger who intervenes is first somebody whose identity she demands. When Fuki names Chisato, Sakura abruptly notices her senior and the commander. The stranger becomes the famous girl from the old tower. She proceeds to tease that hero from the favorable position of somebody already under recognized supervision, and she changes again when Fuki calls her away for training. Her bored response to the command indicates room for display within obedience. Her familiarity with the story of the tower cannot tell her how Chisato will fight, choose a partner or respond to a subordinate who has made the wrong guess. At the mock battle she speculates about the missing competitor, then calls attention to how well the bickering seniors get along. A sardonic observer can be quick about one social texture and disastrously confident about another. [E03 C0223–0244 at 11:22.79–12:07.82, C0350–0362 at 17:03–17:35.]

## What exactness can cost

The mock battle catches her being good at something. She trusts her shooting. Chisato says she has decent skill, even as she exploits inexperience and repeatedly stays outside what Sakura can accomplish. Sakura presses after the advice to stop. Takina enters after the early exchanges, and Sakura and Fuki lose the match. Afterward Sakura apologizes to Fuki for poor shooting and for embarrassing her senior. Fuki corrects her precisely. Her accuracy helped make her readable to that opponent. A defender of the rank system can still give useful feedback about the limits of a skill it rewards. Sakura's startled question about actually dodging shots, something she associated with manga, tests the proposition she has just heard. The exchange offers a glimpse of a person adjusting the boundaries of what she thought possible, even after the desire to prove herself had obstructed correction. Fuki vows they will not lose next time. She gives Sakura another attempt to imagine as her partner. The outcome can teach her something without settling whether she has learned it. [E03 C0375–0420 at 18:07.03–20:05.77 and C0438–0447 at 21:06–21:29, E03-CLM-016–019.]

There is an ordinary price to following a senior, too. In E07 Sakura helps Fuki bring the café information from the police station. Her curt assumption that of course the news has a cover story is spoken in the presence of a former DA employee who objects that their job is to get ahead of the violence. Sakura has the confidence of a young worker who understands the accepted practice. The exchange gives no evidence of what she would say if she had to defend that cover story to a survivor. She requests the matcha dango special before Chisato points out Majima and Takina confirms the identification. Fuki later reports their finding to the commander. Fuki declines because she is on duty and calls her back out before the food arrives. Sakura keeps protesting beyond the café and accuses Fuki of putting on airs in front of Mika. An accurate sense that a senior changes her register with a teacher and a young woman's irritation about losing dessert can occur in the same breath. No access to Fuki's private reason follows. [E07 C0205–0246 at 10:16.93–11:57.]

At E09 she asks for whichever parfait can come quickly. Fuki has come with news of Takina's possible return and immediately wants to leave. When Sakura says “good for you” to Takina there is little immediate response. Outside, she complains that somebody who wanted DA back ought to be happier. In E03 she had protected her new place by emphasizing Takina's lack of one. The shared purpose of an operation and the possibility of reinstatement are enough for an overt congratulation from the same woman. She still has exactly the wrong account of how much has changed in Takina's day. Sakura has overheard the official reason for the visit, seen the response and drawn an inference. The viewer has heard the negotiation Chisato conducted with Kusunoki that she did not attend and is following the effects of Chisato's illness. The gap keeps the greeting legible without turning it into disingenuousness or proof they have become friends. [E09 C0086–0113, C0119–0139 at 07:46.57–08:34.44.]

## The cost of winning and leaving

Sakura tries to hurry her teammates in E11 because she thinks other teams will beat hers. When Takina intends to go to Chisato, Sakura's “again?” puts the new decision in the category of the original departure. She tells Erika she cannot replace Takina and blames Erika's old mistake for the vacancy. Erika unexpectedly takes up the accusation and insists on making an apology of her own. Fuki permits the departure after spelling out its cost. Sakura asks how they are to report it. Later, as another team's progress becomes apparent, she still protests that they have taken the credit. Hibana says this is no competition and Sakura answers that it is. These are two disagreements. She prefers to retain a teammate whose absence might cost them a result, and sees opportunities for recognition inside the institution that another teammate regards as collective work. Even when her first reaction is to blame the person, she also asks the practical supervisory question. [E11 C0181–0212 at 10:01.41–11:22.83 and C0214–0219 at 11:46.81–11:58.91.]

E12 makes her incentives unusually plain. With the government preparing to dispose of the surviving Lycoris and Fuki deciding to move, Sakura initially says she does not want to disobey. She wants to earn credit and become a First. Fuki assigns Erika and Hibana their evacuation work and takes Sakura herself for the control room. Sakura answers as though that assignment is just what she wanted. That response cannot supply a tractable doctrine of when orders deserve obedience. It shows the circumstances in which she acts despite her stated objection. She will join the senior with a difficult task in front of them. Later, Chisato asks Fuki to keep Kurumi’s part from Kusunoki. Sakura asks whether this means the affair will be treated as though it never happened. Her relief comes with an express reason. She says it affects her prospects. A hidden system can reward her aspirations while making her disposable. The viewer has been shown more of that contradiction than her joke has to acknowledge. [E12 C0233–0254 at 12:22.14–13:16, E12-CLM-032/033, and C0437–0441 at 20:46.77–20:59.94.]

Her bluntness can also disclose a debt. Takina thanks Erika, who answers that she is the one who should be grateful. Sakura says Erika got to stay because Takina was thrown out and tells Erika to give proper thanks. That is Sakura's explanation of their relative positions. It tells Takina what the scene shows she was slow to understand without proving that every command decision happened for that one reason. Erika then says she should have spoken to the commander. Sakura had used the same past to make Erika look incompetent under immediate pressure in E11. The new audience and direction matter. Her directness now gets recognition to a person whose action has been easy to tell as a story about disobedience. Erika has already begun to say what she owes, and she goes on to name something she should have done herself. [E12 C0442–0455 at 20:59.95–21:33.14, E12-CLM-043/044.]

The finale makes Sakura the particular person a senior has to help. Fuki calls to her about the rescue team below. The retained picture at 00:55.389 shows Sakura bloodied, eyes closed. Later, when Takina asks to go to Chisato, she says that she is the one who can save Chisato and Fuki is the one who can save Sakura. That is an appeal to a uniquely situated partner, concurrent with the group’s effort to get casualties out. It leaves Sakura's sensory access and exact thoughts at the moment open. When the café is working normally again, Sakura is back and has one heard wish fulfilled at least as far as placing her order. She orders the special and says she can finally eat it. Fuki wears the red First uniform beside her and Sakura wears a dark uniform. The attendant remark comparing a uniform to Chisato’s belongs to Fuki’s visible red. There is no demonstrated promotion for Sakura. The junior gets the chance she kept trying to find without the institution having to reward her. [E13 C0024–0027 at 00:52.91–01:01, C0072–0082 at 03:18.05–03:51, C0310–0317 at 14:33.85–14:49, E13-CLM-003–005 and E13-VIS-006; #000045, #000792/795/798.]

## The right to try on red

The overnight visit in Short 05 gives that ambition a more private object. Sakura asks “the tower” for a moment with her red uniform. Fuki and Takina refuse at once, but Chisato is free to lend her own. Wearing it permits Sakura to call this the view from First. She proceeds to mention having tried secretly to wear Fuki’s garment before, a real boundary even if the explanation about the fit becomes a joke. Fuki is entitled to refuse. Chisato's permission carries no authority over Fuki’s belongings. The same junior who enjoys the permission knows that trying on an office and reaching it are different activities. A garment can be a status object, a physical fit and another person's property on the same evening. [Short 05 C0030–0043 at 01:03.17–01:33.94, #000059/061 and #000065/066, SHORT05-CLM-003.]

Later Sakura happens upon a privately red-clad Takina, the person who joined the prohibition. The frames give Sakura looking and laughing. The release of that laugh makes it plain that Takina’s audience is no longer the one she chose. The shot cue and the calls about a robber resolve into Sakura visibly gagged and restrained. The cut leaves the exact offscreen mechanics open. Takina asks, with a polite negative question, for a version of events in which Sakura saw nothing. The witness is in no position to refuse or answer normally. Because the short has already shown voluntary permission for Sakura to put on the uniform, it can show sharply different freedom when she sees somebody else’s fitting. Neither her laugh nor the muffled answer is assent to restraint. [Short 05 C0051–0071 at 01:47.50–02:51.32, frames #000114/118/123/126 and #000132/134/143, SHORT05-CLM-004/005.]

Short 06 gives her a smaller absence. Fuki says she is out sick. The picture cuts to her in bed, where Sakura’s own separately labeled words are that she wants a parfait. Erika laughs and registers regret in a way Chisato calls unconvincing. Each is a claim or act with its own audience. Nothing in Sakura's line shows that she hears Erika. The short provides no diagnosis from her wish, and Erika's reaction does not reveal a private rivalry over a relationship. It gives an ordinary taste and the absence of a usually troublesome participant, which her peers experience in their own ways. That appetite also has independent TV evidence. [Short 06 C0062–0067 at 02:43.22–02:51.27, #000156 at 02:46.375, SHORT06-CLM-005; E09 C0120; E13 C0313/0315.]

## What a partner can still say

In Asaura's *Recovery days* Sakura has lost contact with Fuki and reaches her under her own power before the café crew gets there. Fuki calls her shot precise. Sakura says she is glad her senior is safe and wants to know how Fuki knew she was there. The answer gives the achievement weight in a different register. Fuki can recognize her partner's footsteps. Sakura repeats “partner,” smiles, and voices the wish to learn Fuki's as well. The precision Fuki once described as a liability against a particular person can help when the partner both knows and needs it. The prose gives that reversal in its own action. It also allows a later divergence. Fuki refuses the ride the café crew offers. Sakura calls out and afterward says openly that she wanted to go on the helicopter. Fuki's inward sense that there was no seat for her describes a stance toward former partners and her teacher. Sakura says she wanted to take the ride. She has already relayed Kusunoki’s conditional lift for medical need and hears the distinct invitations by Mika and Chisato. Nothing in Fuki’s reaction takes away Sakura's standing as her current partner. [A2 *Recovery days* 13.709–750 and 13.781–829, RD-05.]

In *Gluttony days* Sakura's competence has been inconvenient to hear. Fuki tells Chisato that Sakura had been trained in cold conditions and tried to advise her, that she rushed past the advice and that Sakura then saved her. Her memory ends in the feeling of being held by her junior. She tries to thank her with a dish intended as a first experience. Sakura already had it with visiting former companions from Hokkaido. Hibana and Erika know she has eaten it when they join the dorm scene. Fuki can feel dismayed when she learns this and take the silence as an effort to spare her. Sakura herself apologizes for keeping quiet, then goes after her senior and says what the prepared dish means. The restaurant food was good, fumbling through a first attempt with visiting friends was fun, and she is happier that Fuki made the second dish for her. That is work Sakura performs in words. She brings her own pleasure into a disappointing moment without pretending the previous one had no value. Fuki stops withdrawing and hands Sakura a bowl of chopped cabbage. She says it is enough for four. The surrounding thought experiment about how much partners can read is Fuki’s, including her retreat from the idea that the wish can make it true. Sakura's choice to persist and her stated distinction are independently there. [A2 *Gluttony days* 12.169–178, 12.75, 12.213–277, 12.539, 12.618–627 and 12.637–749, GD-04.]

幌田’s *Every dog has its day* gives Sakura a chance at a duel she thinks she might win. She says she has recovered, challenges Fuki and later guesses that her own injury is what troubles the First. Fuki corrects her. The senior admits debt to Chisato's group and still judges its departure from DA. Sakura says she should not try to carry the problem alone. Fuki offers to train with her. An independent ambition and an imperfect willingness to see a senior as somebody who could need help can share a scene. *Recollect* V02 offers a tempering instance. Sakura dismisses previous victims' competence too readily, and Fuki includes herself among those who might be overwhelmed. Horita’s Sakura can see Fuki as someone who might need help. In Abe Kanari’s version Sakura discounts other victims, until Fuki includes herself among the vulnerable. The television supplies its own pattern of mockery, underestimation, help and practical discipline. [C1 幌田, *Reload 02* S119–128; B1 *Recollect* V02 §11 S37–41.]

## What she may do next

Sakura gets impatient when there is talk about who deserves a place and nobody has shown what they can do. In a new training session led by Fuki, if praise falls to a junior who did less visible work, she could ask what part the junior actually handled. If the outcome shows she was wrong, her response could still be competitive before it is gracious. E03 supports her accepting concrete correction from Fuki, and E12 supports her giving a colleague credit in a blunt form. They do not establish that she would volunteer the same repair to a stranger or defend a victim in a formal review. In the novels, a junior whom the senior has dismissed may also persist long enough to state an objection or a preference clearly. Her answer to a familiar partner is stronger evidence for that act than for her behavior with a commander. Keep the novel example within its source.

Away from a rank contest, a postponed dessert remains a reason Sakura will ask for another minute. If she is allowed to stay and her senior has no urgent task for her, staying to enjoy the treat fits the visits. If a departure is ordered, she can protest loudly and go. A stated appetite and practical compliance give more grounds for this expectation than any unshown interior. The larger decisions remain contingent. Her age and ordinary history before DA apart from the source-specific training report, what she would choose if the First uniform ceased to matter, and her independent view of institutional secrecy under personal harm remain open. There is enough in the portrait to recognize what it would cost her to give a seat up. Nobody has yet asked her to do it in circumstances that let her answer freely.
