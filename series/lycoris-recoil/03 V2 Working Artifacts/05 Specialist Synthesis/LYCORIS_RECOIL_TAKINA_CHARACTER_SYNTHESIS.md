# Inoue Takina and the right to decide what counts

At the beginning of *Lycoris Recoil*, Inoue Takina wants a decision reversed. She broke a direct order to save her teammate Erika. Erika lived. Headquarters transferred Takina out of her unit. Takina believes there ought to be some performance good enough to earn a return. Toward the end of the series she has the post back and Fuki warns that walking away now will cost her the chance to return. She walks away. The difference turns on a sentence she can now finish. She knows what she needs the institution *for*, and there is somebody it cannot help her save. An equally revealing change occurs outside work. Once she needed to know whether civilian clothing had a specification. Later, handed a problem no one requires her to solve, she writes an itinerary for a day with Chisato. The day goes badly by the criteria she gave it. She has to listen to somebody else tell her what she got right. [E03-CLM-002/008–010; E04-CLM-004; E09-CLM-028–035; E11-CLM-008–012.]

**Scope.** Takina (井ノ上たきな) is an adolescent Lycoris raised within Direct Attack (DA), transferred to Café LycoReco, and partnered with Nishikigi Chisato. This study contains spoilers through the thirteen TV episodes and the six *Friends are thieves of time* shorts. Those nineteen films establish her anime history. Asaura’s three *days* novels give access to thought available in those stories. Abe Kanari’s two *Recollect* volumes and the seven licensed anthologies give an artist or credited contributor the freedom to change the encounter, the joke and who knows its answer. The shorts return to familiar situations without furnishing a common calendar. The novels and comics have their own order of disclosure. S-numbers after comic titles identify the one-based spine leaf. A number after a prose title gives the Japanese EPUB spine and paragraph. Anime IDs identify the unit reading or the attributed reconciliation.

## What an institution gives a child

Fuki explains the force’s claim on the girls from the inside. DA saved orphans and raised them. It provides the dormitory, uniform, instruction, a hierarchy of seniors and a vocabulary of usefulness. Chisato speaks of their missing family registration and the resulting obstacle to obtaining a passport. Takina did not first choose DA on the terms on which a civilian might choose a profession. For a girl formed there, exclusion puts housing, peers, a future, and the conventional proof that she deserves any of them under one authority. The films give this shared upbringing and particular evidence of Takina’s identification with it. Her birth family, memories before DA and the decisive experience that first linked worth to usefulness remain unavailable. “Needed” is powerful here because people she knows still have the power to need her. [E02-CLM-004; E03-CLM-004/005/008/009; E07-CLM-003.]

When a captive Erika calls for intervention, Fuki is waiting on orders. Takina acts. She has the technical confidence to believe she can save her teammate in the middle of the danger. Erika’s survival is the center of Takina’s defense. For Fuki, the possibility that Takina might also have killed Erika survives that successful result. The record matters down to one distinction. Erika had spoken. That makes her a participant in the decision to call for help, without giving Takina a blank authorization for every method. Saori, the civilian Takina later lets walk ahead so her pursuers will reveal themselves, has no comparable opportunity to choose the exposure. When Chisato challenges the method, Takina can explain her probability judgment and capacity to intervene. Neither answers for Saori. DA meanwhile needs a different fact kept quiet, the compromise of its own systems. Holding Takina accountable for her decision and using the transfer to protect the institution can both be true. [E01 01:49–03:21, 16:31–18:47; E01-CLM-002/003/006; attributable correction REV-E01-003; E03-CLM-012.]

In E03, when Chisato can walk into the old institution and be wanted there, Takina’s complaint about her own lost place breaks off at 私の居場所は. Chisato has the option of turning down the recognition Takina cannot secure. It would be cruelly easy for the successful girl to pronounce that sort of recognition worthless. Instead she offers Takina a place from which she can still go back. The ensuing mock match reveals what Takina values *besides* a victory headquarters could count. Having received a blow from Fuki, she chooses to return it and calls them even, おあいこ. She also makes a risky play on her confidence that Chisato can evade. The conditions which made her a difficult subordinate still operate as part of her new partnership. The small assent at the close, agreeing to join the others off duty, matters as an additional choice she can make today. No scene there makes the dormitory cease to matter. [E03 13:46–13:59, 20:48.94, 22:15–22:45; E03-CLM-009/010/013/014/016; TV verifier Q05.]

## What good judgment would have to include

Takina is often the person who asks what happens to everyone else while Chisato helps the person in front of her. During the Walnut escort Chisato stops to treat an injured attacker. In the interval the team is divided and those with the client face an ambush. The wound matters to Chisato even if its bearer was an enemy. The separation matters to Takina even once the client’s apparent death is revealed as deception. The injured person also gives Chisato a warning. No tidy hypothetical can subtract the stop and tell us how the whole rescue would then have gone. Earlier in the same episode Chisato delegates a difficult shot to the partner she considers better suited to it. Takina accepts and executes. Each young woman can recognize a particular capacity in the other without accepting her definition of the whole job. [E02-CLM-005–008; REL-CHI-TAK-E02-002/003/005.]

Some of the strongest evidence of Takina’s own conception of responsibility concerns Kurumi. The young hacker’s work helped cause the crisis which cost Takina her post. A convenient way of putting things back together would be to blame Kurumi for the expulsion. Takina insists the choice to fire belonged to her. She also requires Kurumi to help catch the attacker. The gift she makes Kurumi is an exact one. Kurumi is allowed to be responsible for what she did, free of authorship of another person’s decision, and still obliged to help put things right. This is the same Takina who could tell Chisato that a foreseeable rescue was adequate grounds for placing Saori at risk. She can disentangle accountability after one event more finely than she solicits consent before another. Harm done by her friends and harm she believes she can control put different demands on her reasoning. [E06-CLM-014/015; E01-CLM-002/003 and REV-E01-003.]

Ordinary contact changes the purposes available to her expertise. Chisato points out that the accuracy required to hit a vital point could be used to avoid one. On another day Takina takes the active protection role herself so that threat response and getting a client away can be divided. She can distinguish the false premise of an assignment from things the girls actually learned or experienced within it. E06 offers a serious instance of discriminating rescue fire. E07 gives a playful countercase when she endorses killing a bad person in fiction and Chisato immediately disagrees. The life of the person under her protection, the survival of a witness, the success of an assigned extraction and her own investment in a particular person can all matter to her. The TV series supplies no all-purpose rule ranking them. [E04-CLM-001/002; E05-CLM-010–012/020; E06-CLM-019; E07-CLM-022.]

## Learning to want a thing

A journey and a shopping trip give Takina quite different ways of finding out that she likes something. On the train a jelly drink fits a transfer of less than ten minutes. Chisato’s boxed lunch makes visible what that perfectly competent choice omitted. Takina declines the offer, then opens her mouth to the direct invitation and gives her own verdict on the bite. During the shopping trip she has interpreted Mika’s advice about underwear as a designation and wonders if civilian clothing has a specification too. She can outsource a choice to a friend. She can also keep pursuing an embarrassingly literal question about which underwear one can safely let someone see. During the outing she names a taste without justifying it, a particular *karintō* sweet. At the aquarium she can puncture Chisato’s invitation to take a personal interest in her by asking about the garden eel, then follow their imitations with a suggestion that Chisato should leave “that” visible and the emphatic verdict めっちゃかわいい. The earlier conversation concerned the pendant. In E13, after dropping the corded keepsake, Chisato explicitly recalls Takina’s words. The pendant is the strongest referent of それ. The preceding fish play lends the compliment affectionate indirection. Chisato reacts with embarrassment and Takina laughs. Practical intelligence supplied a point of entry. It did not dictate the sweet, the joke, the compliment or its recipient. [E02 05:15–06:25 and REL-CHI-TAK-E02-001; E04-CLM-004–006/009/013, 12:28 and 14:46–54; E13 20:12–20:35.]

Watch how she learns to win. She proposes temporary cohabitation when Lycoris are being targeted, down to shifts for sleeping. She has a routine for allocating housework. Chisato says it is boring. Takina offers rock-paper-scissors. Chisato’s ability to read bodily preparation makes the apparently fair contest conspicuously unfair. Repeated losses lead Takina to ask *how* she is losing. Mika explains why she has no chance under the usual opening. Mizuki gives her a way to change it. Takina puts their advice into practice. A victory in a game can call forth a larger response than a sentence with much greater personal stakes. It matters that there is pleasure in winning, and that the outcome of their wager also leaves Takina free to end the arrangement. She has learned something Chisato can do, answered it on her own terms and enjoyed the answer. Chisato’s eagerness for more domestic time and Takina’s attachment remain real on either side of the result. [E06-CLM-001/002/004–006/021–023; 13:05–13:44.]

By E07 she has commitments in her own calendar and can turn something down. After an electronic lock admits the girls, Takina gives the absurd alias 蒲焼太郎 to a human attendant. He checks it and welcomes her, while Mizuki protests and Kurumi jokes about people who believe only the data. She can also step back and let Chisato talk to Yoshimatsu in private. Later, when she takes responsibility for the café accounts, the shop gains a manager who notices the connection between expensive field habits and a business haemorrhaging money. Her care reaches outward into teaching, practical work and a small gift from the childcare assignment. Her original chocolate parfait makes a much less flattering visual impression than she expected. A creation can attract customers while meaning something to them that its maker had not seen. Chisato recognizes the work as something Takina has done for a place important to her. Takina has acquired people with reasons to thank her that headquarters never defined. A scene of being thanked does not by itself fix the first date on which this became her home. [E07-CLM-001–005/011/014, 13:37–14:07; E08-CLM-006–012/025/026.]

The shorts give these tastes consequences. During cherry-blossom season, Takina anticipates supply needs. Chisato thinks she may like flower viewing. Takina is interested in the commercial opportunity and proposes selling yakitori. She also still thinks of her armed competence when security comes up. In the second short she proposes getting the job done so there will be time to prepare for a trip. In private she admits to Chisato that she too is excited. The third opens with three unanswered greetings and a glass. Chisato speculates about psychic powers. Takina is considering a menu item. A word borrowed from a film, イノベーション, invites a teasing request to repeat it. She gives the manager credit for earlier ideas, asks for the peach receipt, and eventually refuses to sell a dish at that cost. Her own design draws a blunt anatomical comparison from Kurumi which she rejects. The person who introduces the design problem, makes the dish and sets a financial limit still cannot tell other people what shape they see. Nor does a profitable calculation in one short make profit her private motive for planning a trip in another. [SHORT01-CLM-003–013; SHORT02-CLM-001–005; SHORT03-CLM-001–006, C013–26, C039–56, C077–90.]

## What it means to spend a day, or a life

Takina once treated DA as the place that could certify a life worth living. After Chisato’s artificial heart is damaged, restoring Takina to DA is one of the things Chisato still wants to do for her. The prospect leaves Takina divided. Reinstatement gives her a way to pursue a lead on Yoshimatsu. It also means leaving Chisato with very little expected time left. On first learning the prognosis Takina proposed killing the nurse. Chisato answered that killing her would change nothing. Mobilizing when there is a useful lead and wanting a response to an irreversible injury can coexist. Now Takina wants to tell Chisato of the move herself. She plans a day down to an event no itinerary can make happen, snow. Chisato can count a failed activity as time successfully spent with Takina. Takina’s 分かりません leaves room for her own uncertainty about what “going back” now means. When she takes leave with いってきます, the ordinary expression used on departing a place to which one expects to return, she acknowledges two directions of belonging. The eventual snowfall does not retroactively rescue her planning. In the closing edit, snow and Chisato’s reaction precede the matched ending music at 21:43.230. Two responses follow it before the credit cut. The events make time for what the plan could not control. The emotional work of the sequence is an interpretation of their order. [E09-CLM-005–007/012–017/028–041; TV verifier Q08, 21:37–22:09.]

Once back inside DA, Takina can tell a captive that she found life outside fairly enjoyable. The threat depends on a genuine pleasure. If expulsion can no longer take everything from her, it also has less power to restrain her behavior. She inflicts a serious injury during an interrogation in pursuit of the lead. The captive survives. The scene supports her ability to control violence in service of an end, and makes visible what it costs someone with little say in that end. In E11 she initially seeks a way to investigate Chisato’s unexplained absence and still rejoin her unit. Takina wants to believe the news of another heart and still asks whose purpose a rescue attempt would serve if the information were a trap. When remaining at the assigned post and rescuing Chisato are finally incompatible, she accepts Fuki’s warning about the price of leaving. ここでは千束を救えない identifies a place as the problem. *Here* she cannot save Chisato. She has not pronounced on every future intervention DA might make. [E10-CLM-008–012, E10-VIS-001; E11-CLM-001/002/008–013.]

Yoshimatsu treats the life that he helped provide as a claim on Chisato’s vocation. In the tower confrontation he offers a lethal way for her to go on living. Takina is ready to act on it. Chisato’s refusal puts Takina opposite the intended beneficiary of the rescue. Their disagreement cannot be explained away as bad information which a more candid conversation would necessarily cure. Chisato would count killing this man as a destruction of what she has spent her life trying to do. Takina can hear her and still say 嫌だ　千束が死ぬのは嫌だ. The sentence is both spare and extraordinarily particular. She does not want this girl to die. She has no argument left which could make that a right over Chisato or another person’s life. A comparative mix measure puts this insistence below Chisato’s louder ethical refusal. That is valuable evidence against reading Takina’s strongest feeling off the peak of her voice. After Chisato restrains her and refuses the sacrifice, Takina still takes up the practical work of looking after her. An embrace, a declaration of shared doctrine, and an agreed account of whose danger counts are all unnecessary for that work to resume. The exact intended result of the wound and medical mechanism of any transfer remain bounded by the verifier. [E12-CLM-008/009/013–018/023–029/048; E12-AUD-012/013; REV-E12-002; TV verifier Q17, 08:07–08:21.]

In the finale Takina helps with the wounded. She tells Fuki that she can reach Chisato and that Fuki is the one who can help Sakura. 私たちで決めましょう puts that proposal in the plural, giving both women a part in the decision. Fuki entrusts Chisato to her. Takina can work from comparative competence as she did at the start and still give another agent a voice in allocating the risk. She saves Chisato bodily after their severe disagreement. Later, her partner has hidden herself from those who care what becomes of her. Takina finds her, objects and offers reassurance. She says Mika brought Yoshimatsu’s case and uses そうです for the report of what it contained with the heart. Her own conclusion follows. Chisato will live. Mika is shown holding the inscribed farewell card. The viewer is given its words. Takina has no depicted access to the card, the circumstances in which Mika obtained the heart or the exact act left offscreen. Her assurance therefore gives no grounds for treating her as a knowing party to Mika’s concealment. In the island encounter the first **visible** discharge comes from Takina’s raised weapon at 16:07.675–.717. Later obscured powder and reports have no comparably secure attribution. Chisato’s account of what she thought had happened is distinct from both. Takina’s future proposal is as characteristic as her reproach: begin with one of the things Chisato gave up when she thought she had no time. That gives the next choice back to Chisato. Chisato takes up the suggestion and chooses an adventure. Together they can now make a plan whose particular pleasures neither could prescribe alone. [E13-CLM-001/003–005/018/019/029/030/039–041; TV verifier Q14, 16:05–16:24; E13 18:48–19:02/19:43–19:56, retained frame 001087 at 19:47.937.]

## The people to whom she talks

**Fuki and Erika receive different replies to the warehouse.** Fuki is a former superior with substantial power to rebuke Takina and a personal history that survives the rebuke. Takina once insisted on settling their score through the body. Their shared institutional formation weighs more in the later scenes than affection announced in dialogue. Fuki’s recognition of what leaving will cost gives weight to 行けよ, her release in E11, and to the responsibility she later hands Takina. Erika, who was endangered and is ashamed of the consequences, defends Takina in her absence in E03. She apologizes in E11 and can take on the work Takina leaves. In E12 Takina actually thanks her. Once Erika admits what she withheld, Takina’s dry 確かに　ひどいヤツだ can coexist with that gratitude. Sakura supplies part of the unglamorous causal history aloud. A reader expecting a tender reassurance as the only possible evidence of repair would miss that Erika is being taken seriously enough to answer. The series leaves Takina’s wider circle of lasting confidants outside these depicted exchanges relatively unknown. [E03-CLM-012/013; E11-CLM-009–013; E12-CLM-032–034/043/044; REL-TAK-FUK-E13-001.]

**Kurumi, Mizuki and Mika can correct her.** Kurumi has technical knowledge Takina needs, her own stake in the institutional harm and, in Short 03, the inconvenient independence to name the resemblance the others have been laughing at. In Short 04 Takina initially reads a figure in monitored footage as a possible killing. A second look at Kurumi’s prompting brings recognition of Mizuki. The later scare produces a scream and gunshot cue. When the joke is uncovered Takina still asks for the broken plate and shop alcohol to be paid for. She can be frightened, mistaken and right about a debt all in the same scene. The ending challenges even her certainty about who had sat beside her. The image on the monitor is evidence of an image, and the prank does not explain every image. Mika, in a quieter register, supplies a manager’s precedent. Asked by Chisato about an old design in Short 03, Takina gives him credit and proposes seeking his advice. Across these scenes other café members possess knowledge independent of hers. They can answer, contribute, refuse or laugh at a proposed solution. [E06-CLM-014/015; SHORT03-CLM-003–006; SHORT04-CLM-001–006, C047–54/087–105.]

**Sakura and unfamiliar guests show the limits of courtesy.** Fuki and Takina object together when Sakura asks to wear the red First uniform. It belongs to Chisato, who consents to the trial. At night Takina privately tries red herself. When Sakura sees her and laughs, an edit withholds what happens before the girl appears restrained and gagged. Takina asks politely for agreement that Sakura saw nothing. This is formal language offered to someone with very little power to decline. Wanting to see herself in red can mean aspiration, curiosity, an experiment or embarrassment at being seen. We know she tried it and then tried to control the witness. None of those facts constitutes a newly declared career plan. In Short 06 the precise answer to an excited fan about an issued uniform starts to expose a secret. Chisato interrupts with an improvised cover. The fans praise their convincing role-play. Takina calls the intervention 隠ぺい. Her candor here yields agreement on entirely unintended grounds. Outside this particular audience the same degree of disclosure might have a different result. [SHORT05-CLM-003–006, C032–43/057–70; SHORT06-CLM-001–003, C012–20.]

**Chisato receives permissions nobody else automatically inherits.** Early on, Takina reaches for a way of checking the artificial heart in public. Chisato makes the privacy condition intelligible and the question can later be revisited privately. In E07, Takina can be the partner who leaves the room. By E09, she is the person who tries to limit Chisato’s activities because she understands the danger and has a different view of what to do with the remaining time. At the end of TV she follows Chisato precisely because withdrawal has costs for the people left behind. After the tower crisis Chisato turns back for a bag precisely because Takina gave it to her. The shorts show Chisato tending her hair. They also let Takina keep looking for her own room after being offered an indefinite stay. Domestic intimacy, bodily knowledge, arguments, play, rescue and wanted company support a reading of romantic attachment as well as one of intense chosen partnership. The acts she actually permits deserve to be named before any conclusion about the bond. The series gives no settled declaration of exclusivity, sexual history, or reliable account of how she would respond to unrelated romantic attention. [REL-CHI-TAK-E05-001/004; E07-CLM-014; E09-CLM-016/017; E12-CLM-046; E13-CLM-029/040/041; SHORT04-CLM-003; SHORT05-CLM-002.]

## The sound of giving a reason

Takina can put delight, warning or disagreement into much the same grammatical frame. Her ordinary first person is 私. She uses 千束 directly with her partner and can retain a polite ending even as she asks a very intrusive question. In E04 指定 asks whether a requirement exists. 私、あのかりんとう好きです names a taste with no need for one. The particle and stance change when she puts the brusque little めっちゃ next to a polite compliment. In E03 the repeated attempt at 私の居場所 stalls at the point where the missing place would have to be named. E09’s 分かりません answers the person entitled to hear how she feels about getting what she once wanted. E13’s 私たちで決めましょう puts a senior she has fought beside inside the deciding “we.” Translating all of these as variations on formal dutifulness would flatten distinctions the Japanese already makes. [E03 13:46–13:59; E04-CLM-004/006/013; E09-CLM-035; E13-CLM-004.]

The audience changes what an apparently courteous sentence does. To Kusunoki on the phone Takina identifies herself by full name and apologizes for the disruption. To Chisato immediately afterward she splits 私も from 浮かれてますから. A bureaucratic reason for finishing work acquires a personal subject. To captive Sakura a polite negative question asks for an answer already prescribed. To an enemy threatening Chisato she can dispense with professional distance. The sharp お前 in her E11 intervention contrasts with her controlled acknowledgment to Fuki that returning to DA may become impossible. A frightened Takina can scream, a competitive Takina can celebrate, a threatened Takina can become terribly plain. Quieter speech can be an admission or a rebuke. There is consequently no reliable instruction to make her increasingly breathy, flat, loud or casual as her familiarity with the listener increases. [SHORT02-CLM-002–004, C041–59; SHORT05-CLM-005, C070; E06-CLM-020/021; E11-CLM-009/034–036; E12-AUD-012/013.]

What we can say about timing depends on the witness. The Japanese caption and matching cue in E03 place the close of her answer beside the entry of the ending. The attributed continuous listener reports that her ええ finishes about 0.13 seconds before that music. The subtitle remains visible afterward. In E01 an 80-millisecond written gap and listeners’ different estimates of the audible pause do not measure the time it takes Takina to feel something. In the menu short, the first イノベーション and its embarrassed repetition have similar full-mix levels. Context does more interpretive work than absolute loudness. Wakayama Shion recalls recording E09’s final line again after director Adachi explained its background. Anzai Chika does not recall that exchange. That recollection is about the making of a performance. It gives neither new fictional dialogue nor transmission of Takina’s thoughts to her partner. This manuscript uses those measured and attributed results. Its author did not independently audition the uninterrupted Japanese performances for this synthesis. [TV verifier Q05/Q15; E12-AUD-013; SHORT03-CLM-002, C022/026; *Heroine Archive* S106, printed p104.]

## Asaura gives Takina a private answer

The prose can supply something unavailable to a camera which only sees a girl turn to the sink. In *Ordinary days*, a complaint about how staff meals take away service time gives Takina a defensible reason to offer a more efficient means of feeding the staff. A connection through Doi allows her to secure good food made by somebody else. Then she makes a meal herself. Its awkward shapes provoke laughter. What gets to her is that Chisato is already eating the rice ball when she says how good it tastes. A sense of being embraced is in Takina’s perception. At the sink she is close to flushing, pleased, frustrated and embarrassed. 千束はズルイ is the judgment she cannot quite explain. It matters that she made the thing whose value Chisato recognized. Procuring an excellent meal through someone else would not expose the same part of herself. Takina’s response to Doi’s own attention supplies a corrective. She actually does smile at him. Her concern for a paying customer, a seat and the atmosphere of the shop gives that smile grounds. Chisato supplies the courtship interpretation, Kurumi suggests asking Takina, Doi eventually asks, and the chapter cuts before she answers. The woman who wants her rice ball recognized may have very different reasons for extending a real smile to a customer. [*Ordinary days* 10.533–599, 10.614–690, 14.27–87, 14.190–249, 14.327–385.]

Takina also has an imagination capable of testing incompatible futures. In the zombie dream of *Ordinary days*, she considers work, food and a life together and eventually says that she would not mind. So much time has passed that it feels like talking to herself. The imagined Chisato has waited and answers. When offered flight from danger Takina separately insists on heading for DA. Over an espresso pot that makes enough for two, she considers answering the earlier question differently. Following Chisato’s example can mean doing what she privately wants or sustaining a limit despite the cost. On waking, she pushes the sleeping Chisato away and later gives the dream a qualified favorable appraisal. A different dream in *Recovery days* makes declaring that the two are dating into an alleged requirement for survival. This time Takina questions the reasoning and never supplies that declaration. She wakes without remembering. Their hands overlap and the narration leaves intention open. Private wishes and the imagined speech of a version of her partner are rich material for reading the first story. They give no waking agreement, identical dream or future schedule in either continuity. [*Ordinary days* 16.351–431, 16.519–559, 16.587–617; *Recovery days* 14.233–359.]

In *Recovery days* she has reason to value being competent at care. The person she nurses has a measured fever. Takina responds to a particular request about the following morning’s red rice, arranges udon for dinner and makes room for privacy when asked. She also orders enough for her own appetite. Chisato later recovers, games overnight and tries to warm the thermometer. Whatever the motive for sending Takina home was, the attempt to feign illness is real. Later a different set of gifts arrives for the now-sick Takina. She initially takes them to be Chisato’s, until Mizuki corrects her. The regulars have anticipated one of *her* needs. Nobody has to instruct her in what sort of face a grateful person makes for that discovery to matter. In the same book, both she and Chisato make a dangerous assumption about who has checked the manga artist Itō’s reference object. They damage her tablet and wall, acknowledge faults, then work to recover the manuscript while concealing part of what happened. Kurumi recovers files and Mizuki helps keep the café running. The people bearing the cost of a repair are not always the two who made the decision. [*Recovery days* 10.55–89, 10.559–610, 10.637–702, 12.147–155, 12.228–249, 12.382–420, 12.662–724, 14.344–371.]

The book lets her be particular about the movies as well. Streaming would be more convenient. A trailer catches her interest. At the first ending Chisato counts five attempts from five tickets. Takina sees that their chances improve if they try together. She proposes the arcade before the others go their separate ways. At the second ending a penguin sits on her lap after the outing. She can find the film too gory, like a design and suspect Chisato maneuvered her into considering another outing. A trailer she saw before the feature has already caught her interest. When Chisato leaves the next outing up to her, Takina says she wants to go again. She will investigate further information as it becomes available before choosing the title. Chisato asks her to say if the film looks promising and suggests going together. With her face half hidden against the penguin, Takina agrees. The first outing took place, and the second screening remains ahead of the text. In *Gluttony days* she moves from moderating Werewolf, with access to the hidden roles, to playing from a seat where she lacks that knowledge. She laughs at how difficult it is to understand people. Efficiency was available in both situations. She volunteered for a game involving uncertainty and chose company which would not guarantee the film she preferred. [*Recovery days* 9.135–164/363–428, 16.17–39, 18.11–40; *Gluttony days* 9.2–88, 10.982–1018.]

*Gluttony days* supplies three especially sharp qualifications. First, Takina can spend a cost on pleasure knowingly. She weighs sleep, a birthday booking the next day and hunger, chooses to eat and suggests *Gremlins*. Tomorrow’s Takina, she recognizes, will pay for it. When off duty she also calls the café うち. Chisato tells her why hearing this pleases her, while Takina suspects she has said it before. One character hearing the word as a discovery does not date the other’s first sense of belonging. Second, with Clarisse she can recognize the possible necessity of severe force and engineer separation from the partner most likely to oppose it. Her subsequent mistake about the effect of revealing herself as a guard raises the danger. Clarisse cancels the commission, undertakes payment and later reports that she has completed the transfer. Madame declines to verify it. The child names fear for Takina and the guards’ kindness. The account of a shared ramen meal changing their allegiance is Takina’s theory. In the later apology scene Takina owns her inexperience, smiles and asks Clarisse not to repeat the act. Clarisse *then* shows relief, cries and hugs her. Takina pats her back. Assigning the child’s decision to the adult’s later reassurance would erase both girls’ actions. [*Gluttony days* 10.203–233, 10.483–558, 10.647–715, 10.791–819, 11.15–59, 14.340–486, 14.626–700.]

Third, the same author can stage bodily closeness and give the reader evidence of reluctance. Takina twice refuses Chisato’s offer to dry her hair and yields after persistence to save time. The handling is gentle. We get ticklishness, embarrassment and a wish to end the experience, followed later by an appreciation of the difference between their ways of doing it and by Takina drying Chisato’s hair. Chisato characterizes the latter exchange as tacit understanding. Takina says the sign was obvious. The reciprocity is genuine. Her embarrassment neither invalidates her first answer nor proves a secret wish at the moment she gave it. The filmed hair care in Short 04 has its own evidentiary ceiling. No one in that camera view can hear an internal justification from Asaura’s separate story. [*Gluttony days* 14.139–162/218–242/259–276; SHORT04-CLM-003.]

## What the comics can change

Abe Kanari gives Takina some of her clearest opportunities to ask *whose wish* a request represents. In *Recollect*, Chisato equates a smile in café service with choosing the right equipment for a mission. Takina points out that she has already served customers without smiling. Once Chisato names the smile as something she personally wants, Takina can explain the circumstances under which she smiles on her own. She smiles when she is having fun. A movie assignment repeats the mistake from the other side. Her body leans with the action. She skips the credits because of her idea of how much viewing she has to finish. Chisato supplied no deadline or report. In a later film scene Chisato stands up to avoid an intimate moment. Only when she returns does a panel disclose that Takina paused the film when she left. Consideration, literal joint viewing and Takina’s understanding of their agreement are all possible. An invented intimate motive would undo what the page turn does: we discover an act without gaining complete access to her reasons. [Abe Kanari, *Recollect* V01 S7–14/43–52; V02 S51–70.]

Abe’s Takina enjoys being the author of a joke. To satisfy a challenge to make Chisato say *doki*, she presents a piece about Jōmon pottery, 土器. Chisato objects to winning the word without provoking the feeling. Takina invokes Chisato’s earlier advantage at rock-paper-scissors. To children at a pool she participates in a game whose outcome she can see Chisato manipulating for their benefit. When they give a whole watermelon, she asks how the nursery will cut it. She also challenges drawing the café’s next customer into a wager, helps set a different condition for a newcomer, then personally exploits a gap in the game’s terms when a manga artist arrives. The girl who notices the limit can occasionally want to win quite as badly as the girl who invented the game. She can cook the moon-viewing soba and assign her partner next year’s turn after clouds spoil their view. These details matter to a portrait of humor and ordinary agency. They tell us what Abe gave this pair to do after returning to familiar anime premises, and how an artist can reveal comprehension through a counterexample or a delayed panel. [*Recollect* V01 S81–94/97–112; V02 S73–84/105–112; compare E06-CLM-021/023.]

Two other comics show why it matters whose attention a character can recognize. In ぶりすけ’s *Ignorance is bliss*, Takina starts to leave Itō’s reference session, hears the regular’s request and agrees to assist. When Kurumi offers to record the pair with her own camera, both object. One accepted likeness gives no permission for the second use. 葉来緑’s *しっぽのきもち* gives the reader privileged access to a disguised Erika. Takina vetoes the discontinued parfait, offers a squirrel dessert and receives Erika’s thanks without established recognition. In A1 she eventually thanks Erika by name and can answer the apology. The C1 encounter gives her a different customer-facing task. Kindly service does not require magically knowing the person in front of her. [*Reload 02* S59–68; *React* S17–30; E03-CLM-012; E11-CLM-011; E12-CLM-043/044.]

The anthology contributors disagree fruitfully about what Takina wants from games. In ikra’s *れっつぷれい！* she asks for three days’ notice to practice and initiates a rematch. In 秋タカ’s *ゲームしようよ* she recalls losing the anime’s chore game and turns a winning streak into cooperative play so Chisato can enjoy it too. In 久川はる’s *Repeat 03* comic she already frequents the ramen shop on her own, reports five visits a week and helps a newcomer. This time Chisato declines consecutive nights. That particular food habit belongs to the artist’s Takina. Its value for reading the anime is that an appetite of her own and an invitation that Chisato can refuse are both imaginable. Neither the frequency nor the ramen itself needs to be inherited. [*Reload* S15–22/63–70; *Repeat 03* S101–114; E04-CLM-006; E06-CLM-021/023.]

There are equally substantial disagreements about intimacy. 花田ももせ’s Takina chooses the dessert outing and twice refuses to share a bed. さくらうめ gives her a night when a horror film makes proximity welcome. Chisato objects to company in the bathroom, Takina waits, and sleeping together is agreed separately. GUNP lets Chisato propose a hug on general grounds about its soothing effect. Takina offers her arms and confirms. Afterward each initially locates the desire for comfort in the other. Takina acknowledges the effect on herself. A second request and consent can now be spoken directly. 若鶏にこみ, by contrast, makes the wish to care for Chisato into a literal restraint she objects to and escapes. The graphic joke works because a sincere offer of kindness has become an alarming experience for its recipient. Add the dream dog whose obedience belongs to *Chisato’s* imagination in 苗川采’s story, and counting panels of physical affection becomes an exceptionally poor way to infer a waking norm. [*Reload* S43–54/99–108; *Repeat* S97–108/151–158; *Repeat 03* S155–162.]

A final comic comparison matters beyond romance. In しーめ’s *In perfect harmony* Chisato twice summons Takina and, from the customer now present or the circumstances after closing, Takina supplies two quite different readings of what she wants. Kurumi and Mizuki hear Takina’s explanations. The tiny face inside the balloon on S102 belongs to Chisato. The exchange gives no demonstration of equivalent shorthand *from Kurumi*, and no claim to exclusive access to what Chisato thinks. In 弐尉マルコ’s *Repeat 02* comic, Takina explains how she worked out Chisato’s actual location, then guesses what she is thinking and gets an immediate denial. Even the correct inference needs its own ground. Distinct forms of attention can coexist in late, intimate, seemingly effortless coordination. [*Reload 03* S93–102; *Repeat 02* S129–138; E11-CLM-008; E12-CLM-002/014.]

## What her conduct allows a reader to expect

Give early Takina an institutionally meaningful solution and she will ask what the result can restore. Give her the same offer after Chisato’s prognosis and she has a different question for it. Give her a practical civilian problem whose objective is intelligible and she may volunteer a solution nobody asked for. Before making her veto an expense, ask who incurs it. A shop’s unworkable margin, a gift that creates labor for somebody else and a meal paid for by her own future tiredness produce three different responses. What follows a friend’s objection depends on its substance. She can redesign the chore routine, accept privacy in a particular setting, stand by a price ceiling or keep pressing for a life the intended beneficiary will not buy on those terms. These conditional expectations have different support. The variation in what prompts a practical refusal recurs throughout the originating series. Privately appraising tomorrow’s cost in those terms belongs to *Gluttony days*. [E05-CLM-011; E06-CLM-004; E08-CLM-006; E09-CLM-028–035; E12-CLM-013/014; SHORT03-CLM-004; *Gluttony days* 14.340–486.]

There is no warranted default romantic script for an unfamiliar admirer. Doi’s unanswered question is an unusually exact example of why. Nor can one transfer Takina’s behavior with a captive witness to a customer she can serve and hear, or her lethal threshold for Chisato to a stranger whose needs are unclear. Evidence for public competence is much stronger than evidence for her private life before DA. Evidence for choosing the café is stronger than evidence that she discarded every institutional loyalty. Evidence for a future directed toward Chisato is stronger than evidence of what she would call that future. The formal reconstruction model can test how these differences change an action sequence or a line of Japanese. This portrait confers no performance rating and supplies no independently withheld test result.

The enduring change is legible on a scale smaller than an ideology. Once Takina could imagine what she might do well enough to be admitted again. Later she can ask what her friend had given up doing, with enough confidence in a possible tomorrow to suggest they start there. She has learned that a decision can be hers and still remain unfinished until someone else answers it.
