# Inoue Takina and the right to decide what counts

At the beginning of *Lycoris Recoil*, Inoue Takina wants a decision reversed. She broke a direct order to save her teammate Erika. Erika lived. Headquarters transferred Takina out of her unit. Takina believes there ought to be some performance good enough to earn a return. When she sees her seat filled, she finishes a sentence about it in the negative: her place is no longer there. Toward the end of the series she has the post back and Fuki warns that walking away now will cost her the chance to return. She walks away with a sentence about a different lack. *Here* she cannot save Chisato. Between the two sentences she has learned what she needs the institution *for*, and found somebody it cannot help her save. An equally revealing change occurs outside work. Once she needed to know whether civilian clothing had a specification. Later, handed a problem no one requires her to solve, she writes an itinerary for a day with Chisato. The day goes badly by the criteria she gave it. She has to listen to somebody else tell her what she got right. [E03 C0277–0278; E03-CLM-002/008–010; E04-CLM-004; E09-CLM-028–035; E11 C0202–0206; E11-CLM-008–012.]

**Scope.** Takina (井ノ上たきな) is an adolescent Lycoris raised within Direct Attack (DA), transferred to Café LycoReco, and partnered with Nishikigi Chisato. This study contains spoilers through the thirteen TV episodes and the six *Friends are thieves of time* shorts. Those nineteen films establish her anime history. They are the governing source (A1), and the frozen CP5 baseline records how they were read before any supplementary narrative was opened. Asaura’s three *days* novels (A2) give access to thought available in those stories. Takina focalizes most of all three, so they carry most of this portrait’s evidence about her interior. Abe Kanari’s two *Recollect* volumes (B1) and the seven licensed anthologies (C1) give an artist or credited contributor the freedom to change the encounter, the joke and who knows its answer. *Heroine Archive* (P_PARATEXT) contributes attributed production testimony and nothing else. The shorts return to familiar situations without furnishing a common calendar. The novels and comics have their own order of disclosure, and none of them fixes an exact slot among the TV episodes. S-numbers after comic titles identify the one-based spine leaf. A number after a prose title gives the Japanese EPUB spine and paragraph. In *Heroine Archive* citations, the p-number after the S-number is the printed page, S minus 2. A cue number such as E03 C0462 counts the dialogue lines of that episode’s corrected Japanese subtitle file. Anime IDs identify the unit reading or the attributed reconciliation.

**How to read the evidence.** A1 governs. Every supplementary finding below is retrospective and labelled with its source. Where it bears on the anime it is stated as one of the project’s comparative rulings: it can SPECIFY, EXTEND or PRESSURE a CP5 claim, remain SOURCE_SPECIFIC, or be CARICATURE. None rewrites an A1 observation. The text keeps apart what Takina is shown doing, what she says, what a narrator tells us she thinks, what other characters say about her, what this portrait infers and what is unknown. Dream passages in the novels stay dream-framed, for her lines and for the imagined Chisato’s. Production testimony appears only as what a named person, or the book’s editors, say.

**Orientation.** Takina is sixteen, a year younger than Chisato (E01 C0159–0160; *Recovery days* 18.34–37). She wears the navy uniform, not the red of a First such as Chisato or Fuki, and she tries a red uniform on only in private (SHORT05). She was transferred from Kyoto only the year before (E01 C0157), and in the novels Kyoto is where she comes from (*Ordinary days* 14.330). Chisato calls her たきな and, when she enforces sickroom discipline, スパルたきな (*Recovery days* 12.55). Children call her たきなおねーちゃん (*Recollect* V01 S102), and a regular in the novels says たきなちゃん (*Ordinary days* 10.216). The evidence supports a working sketch before any argument is made. She is precise, cost-conscious and literal-minded, trained for assassination and physically fearless. She speaks politely by default and drops into plain or rough forms under threat or in play. She has a dry wit that surprises people who expect none. She is willing to use lethal force for a person she has chosen, and in another setting cannot bring herself to fire at a woman who resembles a café customer. Her appetite for food, coffee and films is real. She is slow to say aloud that she enjoys something and quicker to act on it. Each of those terms is earned below, with its counterexamples.

## What an institution gives a child

Fuki explains the force’s claim on the girls from the inside. DA saved orphans and raised them. It provides the dormitory, uniform, instruction, a hierarchy of seniors and a vocabulary of usefulness. Chisato speaks of their missing family registration and the resulting obstacle to obtaining a passport. Takina did not first choose DA on the terms on which a civilian might choose a profession. For a girl formed there, exclusion puts housing, peers, a future, and the conventional proof that she deserves any of them under one authority. The films give this shared upbringing and particular evidence of Takina’s identification with it. Her birth family, memories before DA and the decisive experience that first linked worth to usefulness remain unavailable. “Needed” is powerful here because people she knows still have the power to need her. [E02-CLM-004; E03-CLM-004/005/008/009; E07-CLM-003.]

What the sources add about her own past comes almost entirely from her side, and it is sparse. Until the day before she met Chisato she shared a dormitory room with Fuki (E01 C0188–0189). In *Ordinary days* she remembers Fuki as someone who once looked after her, and measures her own speed against both Fuki and Chisato (12.64–72). She went through the same training school as Chisato, so Chisato can vouch that she speaks the languages Chisato speaks, and Takina answers in Russian on request (E01 C0242–0244). On her first day she tells Chisato that she was transferred from Kyoto only the year before, and Chisato takes the transfer as a mark of excellence (E01 C0157–0158). The novels give her a Kyoto background. She rolls a Kansai-style omelette because 自分は京都の出だ (*Ordinary days* 14.330), cooks from a Kansai stock as her baseline (*Recovery days* 12.272), and was taught a dish in general-education classes and how to care for her hair in Kyoto (*Recovery days* 10.517–518; *Gluttony days* 14.116). Only the *Ordinary days* dream narration says she moved to the capital region to train (16.182). In *Recollect* she points out that DA never asked her for a smile (V01 S10). In *Recovery days* the narration, focalized through her, calls the transfer a demotion, 左遷 (10.122). Her own A1 description of it is offered to a captive she is hurting: she was once fired from DA for killing too many (E10 C0127). No admitted source shows her birth family, her life before DA, or the experience that first tied her worth to being useful. In *Heroine Archive* Anzai Chika, Chisato’s actor, says she would like to see episodes from Takina’s Kyoto days, since everyone but Chisato remains a mystery (S109/p107). That is a statement about absence, not evidence about content.

When a captive Erika calls for intervention, Fuki is waiting on orders. Takina acts. She has the technical confidence to believe she can save her teammate in the middle of the danger. Erika’s survival is the center of Takina’s defense. For Fuki, the possibility that Takina might also have killed Erika survives that successful result. The record matters down to one distinction. Erika had spoken. That makes her a participant in the decision to call for help, without giving Takina a blank authorization for every method. Saori, the civilian Takina later lets walk ahead so her pursuers will reveal themselves, has no comparable opportunity to choose the exposure. When Chisato challenges the method, Takina can explain her probability judgment and capacity to intervene. Neither answers for Saori. DA meanwhile needs a different fact kept quiet, the compromise of its own systems. Holding Takina accountable for her decision and using the transfer to protect the institution can both be true. [E01 01:49–03:21, 16:31–18:47; E01-CLM-002/003/006; attributable correction REV-E01-003; E03-CLM-012.]

In E03, when Chisato can walk into the old institution and be wanted there, Takina’s complaint turns on her. She addresses Chisato as あなた: *you* are needed by DA, so it is fine for you (C0276). Her own sentence breaks at 私には！ and resumes as a completed negative, 私の居場所は… / もう ここにはない, her place is no longer here, followed by an apology (C0277–0279). The sentence does not stall on the word “place”. What goes unsaid is where her place might be instead. A few lines earlier she had still called her partner 千束さん, for the last time (C0272). Chisato has the option of turning down the recognition Takina cannot secure. It would be cruelly easy for the successful girl to pronounce that sort of recognition worthless. Instead she offers Takina a place from which she can still go back. The ensuing mock match reveals what Takina values *besides* a victory headquarters could count. Having received a blow from Fuki, she chooses to return it and calls them even, おあいこ. She also makes a risky play on her confidence that Chisato can evade (きっと よけると思いましたから, C0460). The conditions which made her a difficult subordinate still operate as part of her new partnership. On the train home she calls Chisato 非常識な人, and the name comes bare for the first time: 千束 (C0462). Chisato says the fight felt good, and Takina answers ええ (C0463–0464), her first acknowledged shared pleasure in the series. The closing images then place her at the group game she had refused that morning, another choice she can make today. No scene there makes the dormitory cease to matter. [E03 13:21–13:59, 20:48.94, 22:02–22:19; E03-CLM-009/010/013/014/016; TAK-MB-E03-003–006; TV verifier Q05, which times the ええ against the ending music; audit recheck on C0272 and C0462.]

## What good judgment would have to include

Takina is often the person who asks what happens to everyone else while Chisato helps the person in front of her. During the Walnut escort Chisato stops to treat an injured attacker. In the interval the team is divided and those with the client face an ambush. The wound matters to Chisato even if its bearer was an enemy. The separation matters to Takina even once the client’s apparent death is revealed as deception. The injured person also gives Chisato a warning. No tidy hypothetical can subtract the stop and tell us how the whole rescue would then have gone. Earlier in the same episode Chisato delegates a difficult shot to the partner she considers better suited to it. Takina accepts and executes. Each young woman can recognize a particular capacity in the other without accepting her definition of the whole job. [E02-CLM-005–008; REL-CHI-TAK-E02-002/003/005.]

What Takina notices first is usually mechanism. In E01 she names Chisato’s ammunition, 非殺傷弾, before she asks whether “life matters” covers enemies too (C0477, C0483). Her first answer to Chisato’s request for help is the dry あり過ぎますね: she has too many questions (C0306). In E02 she claims the wheel because a Lycoris who could not drive could not have become one, and prefers the plain car to Chisato’s supercar because it attracts less attention (C0133–0151). In E06 she asks *how* she keeps losing at rock-paper-scissors. In *Recollect* her narration boxes test the same losing streak against a control trial with Kurumi, to whom she does not keep losing, before she plans to consult the adults (V02 S54–56; R2-10). In *Gluttony days* she identifies a tail by his unchanged shoes and works out the ramen toppings from a posted guide (9.518–521, 9.629–665). The habit is diagnostic and literal, and it can miss a joke. She earnestly asks whether an armored gunman is Kurumi’s acquaintance (*Ordinary days* 12.128–130). She asks where cameras that damage ghosts are issued (*Recollect* V02 S31). At the café’s cry of 敵襲 she draws a pistol on what turns out to be an insect-shaped drone (V02 S91–97; R2-11, CARICATURE). She also knows where the habit falls short. In *Gluttony days* she rates Chisato’s reading of people above her own and names her own inexperience more than once (10.191, 10.258–269, 10.784–790). After the Clarisse job she decides that Werewolf is not a game for her, then takes a player’s seat anyway (10.862–868, 10.1008–1009; GD-07).

Some of the strongest evidence of Takina’s own conception of responsibility concerns Kurumi. The young hacker’s work helped cause the crisis which cost Takina her post. When Kurumi confesses, the angry voice in the room is Mizuki’s. Mizuki asks whether Kurumi is the one who put guns in terrorists’ hands and says that Takina was fired because of it (E06 C0315–0320), until Mika tells her to stop. Takina’s first words are a practical question, whether that is all the footage (映像は それで全部ですか？, C0322), followed by a report that Chisato is out on a delivery (C0324). An earlier ledger row and acoustic measurement gave Mizuki’s outburst to Takina as her peak activation. That measurement is of Mizuki’s voice. Takina’s composure is the observed fact. Later Mizuki twice urges Takina, as the injured party, to retaliate (C0446–0448), and Kurumi kneels and apologizes to her (C0450). A convenient way of putting things back together would be to blame Kurumi for the expulsion. Takina says the outcome was the result of her own action and not Kurumi’s fault. She also requires Kurumi to help catch the attacker to the end (C0451–0454). The gift she makes Kurumi is an exact one. Kurumi is allowed to be responsible for what she did, free of authorship of another person’s decision, and still obliged to help put things right. In E03, before she knew of the hack, Takina had called the loss 全部 自分のせい, all her own fault (TAK-MB-E03-003). The E06 sentence keeps the authorship and makes room for a cause she could not have seen. This is the same Takina who could tell Chisato that a foreseeable rescue was adequate grounds for placing Saori at risk. She can disentangle accountability after one event more finely than she solicits consent before another. Harm done by her friends and harm she believes she can control put different demands on her reasoning. [E06-CLM-014/015; audit recheck E06 C0318–0320 and C0450; E01-CLM-002/003 and REV-E01-003.]

Ordinary contact changes the purposes available to her expertise. Chisato points out that the accuracy required to hit a vital point could be used to avoid one. On another day Takina takes the active protection role herself so that threat response and getting a client away can be divided. She can distinguish the false premise of an assignment from things the girls actually learned or experienced within it. E06 offers a serious instance of discriminating rescue fire. E07 gives a lighter countercase. Asked by the manga artist whether bad people should be killed, she answers べきですね without hedging (C0424–0425). Chisato’s ダメだよ 殺しちゃ comes almost a minute later and answers the finished manga, not Takina (C0453). The life of the person under her protection, the survival of a witness, the success of an assigned extraction and her own investment in a particular person can all matter to her. The TV series supplies no all-purpose rule ranking them. [E04-CLM-001/002; E05-CLM-010–012/020; E06-CLM-019; E07-CLM-022.]

The series lets Takina form her own view of Chisato’s ability before any adult names it. At the aquarium she asks what talent the Alan pendant recognized. Chisato turns the question back on her, and Takina says she hopes she has some talent herself (なんかあるといいですけど, E04 C0281–0286). That modest hope is her only self-assessment of aptitude in E01–E07. In the car in E07 she tells Chisato that dodging bullets is not something just anyone can do. Chisato calls it 勘, intuition, and turns it toward being useful to someone (C0269–0278). Takina’s description is a technician’s: she names the observable skill. The darker name for the talent, a genius for killing, reaches the audience through Yoshimatsu within minutes of each conversation and never through Takina (E04 C0323; E07 C0394).

## Learning to want a thing

A journey and a shopping trip give Takina quite different ways of finding out that she likes something. On the train a jelly drink fits a transfer of less than ten minutes. Chisato’s boxed lunch makes visible what that perfectly competent choice omitted. Takina declines the offer, then opens her mouth to the direct invitation and gives her own verdict on the bite. During the shopping trip she has interpreted Mika’s advice about underwear as a designation and wonders if civilian clothing has a specification too. She can outsource a choice to a friend. She can also keep pursuing an embarrassingly literal question about which underwear one can safely let someone see. During the outing she names a taste without justifying it, a particular *karintō* sweet. At the aquarium Chisato deflects a personal question with 何？ 私に興味あんの？. Takina answers that she is interested more than in the seahorses (タツノオトシゴ以上には, C0246). When Chisato teases again with チンアナゴよりも？, Takina refuses the joke: if Chisato is going to make fun of it, never mind (ちゃかすなら もういいです, C0248). Chisato answers seriously at once. Killing puts her in a bad mood, because it takes someone’s time. Takina laughs, says she had expected a more philanthropic reason, and calls her full of mysteries (千束は謎だらけです, C0249–0261). After their imitations of the garden eels she suggests that Chisato should leave “that” visible and adds the emphatic verdict めっちゃかわいい (C0297–0300). The earlier conversation concerned the pendant. In E13, after dropping the corded keepsake into the sea, Chisato explicitly recalls Takina’s words. The pendant is the strongest referent of それ. Takina denies having said anything so embarrassing (言わないですよ 恥ずかしい, E13 C0433–0438). The preceding fish play lends the compliment affectionate indirection. In E04 Chisato reacts with embarrassment and Takina laughs. [E02 05:15–06:25 and REL-CHI-TAK-E02-001; E04-CLM-004–006/009/013, E04 C0244–0262 and 14:46–54; E13 20:12–20:35; audit recheck E04 C0247–0248.]

Watch how she learns to win. She proposes temporary cohabitation when Lycoris are being targeted, down to shifts for sleeping: until safety is secured she will be with Chisato twenty-four hours a day (C0021–0027). In the same scene she relays the doctor’s message that Chisato’s monthly check-up was the day before, and says flatly that Chisato did not go (C0017–0020). The protective rule has a limit she cannot enforce. When Chisato leaves on a delivery that afternoon, Takina twice offers to come and twice hears ああ 大丈夫. Chisato goes out saying she is looking forward to Takina’s dinner, and Majima’s ambush finds her alone (C0281–0293). She has a routine for allocating housework. Chisato says it is boring. Takina offers rock-paper-scissors. Chisato’s ability to read bodily preparation makes the apparently fair contest conspicuously unfair. Repeated losses lead Takina to ask *how* she is losing. Mika explains why she has no chance under the usual opening. Mizuki gives her a way to change it. Takina puts their advice into practice. A victory in a game can call forth a larger response than a sentence with much greater personal stakes. It matters that there is pleasure in winning, and that the outcome of their wager also leaves Takina free to end the arrangement. She has learned something Chisato can do, answered it on her own terms and enjoyed the answer. Chisato’s eagerness for more domestic time and Takina’s attachment remain real on either side of the result. Adachi Shingo, the series director, says that, with the episode director, he modelled her delight at the final win on a video of a girl whose joy spills out despite herself (*Heroine Archive* S83/p081, creator intention). [E06-CLM-001/002/004–006/021–023; 13:05–13:44.]

By E07 she has commitments in her own calendar and can turn something down. She notices that Chisato is off before Chisato says why (なんか 今日は変ですよ, C0068–0069). She turns what she learned of Chisato’s eyes in E06 into a joke: good eyes see things they should not (C0081). When Chisato apologizes to Mika for reading his mail, Takina adds her own reason for looking, so that the intrusion is shared (司令と会うのかと, C0340). After an electronic lock admits the girls, Takina gives the absurd alias 蒲焼太郎 to a human attendant. He checks it and welcomes her, while Mizuki protests and Kurumi jokes about people who believe only the data. She can also step back and let Chisato talk to Yoshimatsu in private. Later, when she takes responsibility for the café accounts, the shop gains a manager who notices the connection between expensive field habits and a business haemorrhaging money. The management reaches Chisato’s body. When the clinic rings Chisato’s phone, Takina takes it from her hand, books the check-up over her protest and calls the crew’s health her job (E08 C0215–0222). Her care reaches outward into teaching, practical work and a small gift from the childcare assignment. The gift has a history in the same episode. At the nursery Chisato asks publicly for a present from Takina too, and Takina snaps 子供ですか！ (C0190–0191). That evening she hands Chisato a nursery treat bag with 欲しかったんでしょ？. Inside is a pug charm (C0419–0424). Then she explains her work for the café by what the place means to Chisato, 大切な場所なんでしょ？ (C0427). Rebuffed in public, the request is remembered and fulfilled in private. Her original chocolate parfait makes a much less flattering visual impression than she expected. Chisato swallows her first reaction and offers a hesitant いいんじゃないかな？ (C0110–0114). Takina drops the dish herself after a toilet-flush gag makes the resemblance unmistakable (C0238–0241). A creation can attract customers while meaning something to them that its maker had not seen. Chisato recognizes the work as something Takina has done for a place important to her. Takina has acquired people with reasons to thank her that headquarters never defined. A scene of being thanked does not by itself fix the first date on which this became her home. [E07-CLM-001–005/011/014, 13:37–14:07; E08-CLM-006–012/025/026; audit recheck E08 C0215–0222 and C0190 → C0419–0424.]

The shorts give these tastes consequences. During cherry-blossom season, Takina has already placed an extra alcohol order before Chisato asks (Short 01 C021). Chisato thinks she may like flower viewing. Takina would like the event every week because alcohol is profitable. She proposes yakitori for next year, only to learn that the idea had already been proposed and turned down (ウチは居酒屋じゃない, C026–032). She also still thinks of her armed competence when security comes up, and answers Chisato’s alarm at the gun under her festival dress with 上手にやりますから (C041–043). In the second short she proposes getting the job done so there will be time to prepare for a trip. In private she admits to Chisato that she too is excited. The third opens with three unanswered greetings and a glass. Chisato speculates about psychic powers. Takina is considering a menu item. When Chisato offers the familiar brown swirl, Takina asks to move away from it, declining to repeat her own E08 embarrassment, and Chisato teases that people find it hard to leave their successes behind (C019–021). A word borrowed from a film, イノベーション, invites a teasing request to repeat it. She gives the manager credit for earlier ideas, asks for the peach receipt, and eventually refuses to sell a dish at that cost. Her own design draws a blunt anatomical comparison from Kurumi which she rejects. [SHORT01-CLM-003–013; audit recheck Short 01 C031–0032; SHORT02-CLM-001–005; SHORT03-CLM-001–006, C013–26, C039–56, C077–90.]

## What it means to spend a day, or a life

Takina once treated DA as the place that could certify a life worth living. After Chisato’s artificial heart is damaged, restoring Takina to DA is one of the things Chisato still wants to do for her. She makes it her price. When Kusunoki orders Chisato herself back to DA, Chisato answers that she might think about it if Takina is returned (ＤＡに戻れ; たきなを ここに戻してあげて, C0088, C0111–0112). The prospect leaves Takina divided. She says she had meant to refuse (断ろうと思ってました), until she overheard that capturing Majima might tell them about Chisato’s heart (C0272–0280). Reinstatement gives her a way to pursue a lead on Yoshimatsu. It also means leaving Chisato with very little expected time left. On first learning the prognosis Takina proposed killing the nurse, Himegama in disguise. Chisato answered that killing her would change nothing. Mobilizing when there is a useful lead and wanting a response to an irreversible injury can coexist. Her care changes register at once. She apologizes for having made Chisato run and calls after her not to (C0148, C0154). Out of Takina’s hearing, Chisato tells Mizuki that Takina has become self-conscious around her, and that this is why she had not wanted to tell her (C0075–0077). Takina’s changed manner is visible enough to be the reason Chisato gives for her secrecy. Now Takina wants to tell Chisato of the move herself. She plans a day down to an event no itinerary can make happen, snow. The plan shows its costs on her own body. She is wearing the summer outfit Chisato chose for her and owns nothing warmer (これ 千束が選んだやつですよ / ないです, C0317–0319). Her own schedule alarm cuts off the coat Chisato starts choosing for her (C0323–0326). When she sneezes, Chisato puts a scarf on her, and at parting gives it to her as a farewell gift (C0346–0350, C0378). Chisato can count a failed activity as time successfully spent with Takina. Takina’s 分かりません leaves room for her own uncertainty about what “going back” now means (C0363). She then says 理不尽なことばかりです, so much is unreasonable, and asks whether Chisato does not think so (C0366). Chisato answers with acceptance and full effort, praises the plan and offers a fist, which Takina meets. The accompanying やったぜ has no speaker tag and stays unattributed (C0367–0374). Adachi says the 理不尽 Takina voices in E09 differs from the one she felt in E03, which concerned only herself, and that he hoped the repeated word would express her change (*Heroine Archive* S88–S89/p086–087, creator intention). When she takes leave with いってきます, the ordinary expression used on departing a place to which one expects to return, she acknowledges two directions of belonging (C0379). The eventual snowfall does not retroactively rescue her planning. In the closing edit, snow and Chisato’s reaction precede the matched ending music at 21:43.230. Two responses follow it before the credit cut. The events make time for what the plan could not control. The emotional work of the sequence is an interpretation of their order. Maruyama Yūsuke, credited on the series as a director (演出), says chief animation director 山本由美子 added Takina’s reddened nose in the park cut as an ad-lib, that it shows she had been holding back tears, and that it is the only cut in the series in which her nose is red (S142/p140). The redness is visible. Its meaning is the staff’s account. [E09-CLM-005–007/012–017/028–041; TV verifier Q08, 21:37–22:09; audit recheck E09 C0075–0077.]

Once back inside DA, Takina can tell a captive that she found life outside fairly enjoyable. Her full sentence describes her own record bluntly. She was fired from this place once for killing too many, life outside was rather fun, and being fired again might be fine (C0127–0129). The threat depends on a genuine pleasure. If expulsion can no longer take everything from her, it also has less power to restrain her behavior. She inflicts a serious injury during an interrogation in pursuit of the lead. The captive survives. The scene supports her ability to control violence in service of an end, and makes visible what it costs someone with little say in that end. To Majima she says she has no interest in him, only in where Yoshimatsu is (C0226). In E11 she initially seeks a way to investigate Chisato’s unexplained absence and still rejoin her unit (作戦までには戻ります, C0020). Her first institutional choice under pressure is compliance. On the phone she tells Kusunoki that Chisato is missing and has left her phone behind. Asked about Yoshimatsu, she says he is Chisato’s benefactor, confirms he is the heart donor and adds that Majima knew Chisato was looking for him. Told to ask Majima before he dies, she says すみません 戻ります and goes back (C0063–0078). She has disclosed her partner’s private history to the commander in order to get help, and has not yet left. She then takes Chisato’s abandoned phone to Kurumi for analysis, the first link in the search that leads to the old tower (C0146). When Kurumi calls the news of the heart a complete solution, Takina doubts it. She has a bad feeling, it may be a trap, and something is surely there (そうでしょうか？; 罠かもしれない; 絶対 何かある, C0175–0180). When remaining at the assigned post and rescuing Chisato are finally incompatible, she accepts Fuki’s warning about the price of leaving with a quiet 分かってます. ここでは千束を救えない identifies a place as the problem. *Here* she cannot save Chisato. She has not pronounced on every future intervention DA might make. Fuki’s 行けよ releases her (C0202–0208). In the closing fight she has no spoken line. The お前には用がない once given to her there is Majima’s (C0429–0430). [E10-CLM-008–012, E10-VIS-001; E11-CLM-001/002/008–013/034–036; audit recheck E11 C0430.]

Yoshimatsu treats the life that he helped provide as a claim on Chisato’s vocation. Takina arrives with a polite threat, 動くな 次は眉間に撃ち込みますよ, and then turns to お前 and mostly plain forms for an itemized indictment that begins 吉松 お前が and ends with his breaking Chisato’s heart (C0093–0102). She discards it at once. None of that matters any more if she can get the case, because Kurumi has found that Chisato’s life is inside it (C0103–0105). Adachi says that at the storyboard stage he had Takina overhear the earlier conversation between Chisato and Yoshimatsu, to raise her hatred of him (*Heroine Archive* S94–S95/p092–093). What she hears on screen is limited to what the shots show. In the tower confrontation Yoshimatsu offers a lethal way for Chisato to go on living. The 狂ってる heard as he finishes falls in a Takina close-up with no speaker tag. It leans toward Takina and stays unattributed (C0115). Takina is ready to act on the offer: if Chisato cannot do it, she will (千束ができないなら 私がやります, C0121). Chisato’s refusal puts Takina opposite the intended beneficiary of the rescue. When Himegama’s attack leaves Takina hanging from the edge, her first words send Chisato on to Yoshimatsu, 行って！ (C0153). It is the one moment in the tower when she gives way to Chisato’s priority instead of overriding it. When the wounded man flees, her cry is that the heart is getting away (心臓が逃げる！, C0183). Their disagreement cannot be explained away as bad information which a more candid conversation would necessarily cure. Chisato would count killing this man as a destruction of what she has spent her life trying to do. Takina can hear her and still say 嫌だ　千束が死ぬのは嫌だ. The sentence is both spare and extraordinarily particular. She does not want this girl to die. She has no argument left which could make that a right over Chisato or another person’s life. A comparative mix measure puts this insistence below Chisato’s louder ethical refusal. That is valuable evidence against reading Takina’s strongest feeling off the peak of her voice. Wakayama Shion, who voices Takina, says she was glad to show Takina’s human, soft side in the stretch from 心臓が逃げる！ to 千束が死ぬのは嫌だ (S109/p107). That is the actor’s account of a performance. After Chisato restrains her and refuses the sacrifice, Takina still takes up the practical work of looking after her. An embrace, a declaration of shared doctrine, and an agreed account of whose danger counts are all unnecessary for that work to resume. The exact intended result of the wound and medical mechanism of any transfer remain bounded by the verifier. [E12-CLM-008/009/013–018/023–029/048; E12-AUD-012/013; REV-E12-002; TV verifier Q17, 08:07–08:21; audit recheck E12 C0115; C0153 at frame #000566.]

In the finale Takina helps with the wounded. Treating Sakura, she tells Erika by name to keep pressure on the wound. When Erika asks for gauze she says the rest is for Chisato (C0006–0008). Care for a peer and a share kept back for one person happen in the same breath. She tells Fuki that she can reach Chisato and that Fuki is the one who can help Sakura. 私たちで決めましょう puts that proposal in the plural, giving both women a part in the decision. Fuki entrusts Chisato to her. Takina can work from comparative competence as she did at the start and still give another agent a voice in allocating the risk. She saves Chisato bodily after their severe disagreement. Later, her partner has hidden herself from those who care what becomes of her. Takina credits Kurumi with finding the place from Saori’s incidental photograph. She makes the trip herself, objects to Chisato’s disappearance and offers reassurance. The forest chase gives her some of the plainest speech she uses with Chisato. She demands to know why Chisato is running, calls the hit on Chisato’s shoulder proof that she has gone rusty, says she was never trained to call out first, and answers Chisato’s アホか with アホは そっちでしょ (C0350–0357). On the beach Chisato twice claims her aloud as her partner (私の相棒; 千束の相棒) before Takina has explained anything (C0369–0370). Takina says Mika brought Yoshimatsu’s case and uses そうです for the report of what it contained with the heart. Her own conclusion follows, in the formal second person she rarely uses to Chisato: そういうわけで あなたは死にません (C0402–0406). Mika is shown holding the inscribed farewell card. The viewer is given its words. Takina has no depicted access to the card, the circumstances in which Mika obtained the heart or the exact act left offscreen. Her assurance therefore gives no grounds for treating her as a knowing party to Mika’s concealment. In the encounter on the island where Chisato has hidden, the first **visible** discharge comes from Takina’s raised weapon at 16:07.675–.717. Chisato takes up the suggestion and chooses an adventure. Together they can now make a plan whose particular pleasures neither could prescribe alone. In the Hawaii epilogue Chisato hangs up on a call and tells Takina it was a fan (C0463–0464). At the TV endpoint Chisato still keeps a little from her, in a playful key. ## The people to whom she talks

**Fuki and Erika receive different replies to the warehouse.** Fuki is a former superior with substantial power to rebuke Takina and a personal history that survives the rebuke. Takina once insisted on settling their score through the body. Their shared institutional formation weighs more in the later scenes than affection announced in dialogue. Fuki’s recognition of what leaving will cost gives weight to 行けよ, her release in E11, and to the responsibility she later hands Takina. The supplementary sources add perspective without adding a reconciliation scene. Beyond Takina’s *Ordinary days* memory that Fuki once looked after her, *Recollect* V01 has Hibana warn that Takina’s name is taboo around Fuki, while Chisato reminds Takina that something she said once got her hit by Fuki. Takina answers that she only stated facts (S62, S91; R1-10). In *Recovery days* Fuki focalizes, not Takina. Fuki privately ranks her previous partner’s shooting above Sakura’s while counting that partner among those who will not follow orders, and she asks Chisato whether things are working with “that one”. Reading 前の相棒 as Takina rests on the order of a list. Takina, ill, sits silent in the helicopter, and the book never says what she thinks of Fuki (13.52, 13.64–65, 13.764–771, 13.805–807; RD-15). Erika, who was endangered and is ashamed of the consequences, defends Takina in her absence in E03. She apologizes in E11 and can take on the work Takina leaves. Asaura says that in that E11 encounter Takina’s mind is elsewhere because Chisato is in danger, and that he would like to write a calm conversation between the two (*Heroine Archive* S136–S137/p134–135, creator interpretation). In E12 Takina actually thanks her, with ありがとう あのとき and no name (C0442). Once Erika admits what she withheld, Takina’s dry 確かに　ひどいヤツだ can coexist with that gratitude (C0454). Sakura supplies part of the unglamorous causal history aloud. A reader expecting a tender reassurance as the only possible evidence of repair would miss that Erika is being taken seriously enough to answer. In E13 Takina calls her by name as they treat Sakura together (C0006). The series leaves Takina’s wider circle of lasting confidants outside these depicted exchanges relatively unknown. [E03-CLM-012/013; E11-CLM-009–013; E12-CLM-032–034/043/044; REL-TAK-FUK-E13-001.]

**Kurumi, Mizuki and Mika can correct her.** Kurumi has technical knowledge Takina needs, her own stake in the institutional harm and, in Short 03, the inconvenient independence to name the resemblance the others have been laughing at. In Short 04 Takina initially reads a figure in monitored footage as a possible killing. A second look at Kurumi’s prompting brings recognition of Mizuki. The later scare produces a scream and gunshot cue. When the prank is revealed she says she thought she would die (死ぬかと思いましたよ, C078), and Mika remarks that her scream was not the voice of someone who disbelieves in ghosts (C085). She still asks for the broken plate and shop alcohol to be paid for. She can be frightened, mistaken and right about a debt all in the same scene. The ending challenges even her certainty about who had sat beside her. The image on the monitor is evidence of an image, and the prank does not explain every image. Mika, in a quieter register, supplies a manager’s precedent. When Chisato digs out early menu sketches in Short 03, Takina credits the manager with them and proposes asking him to make one (店長にお願いしましょうよ, C044–052). She calls him 店長 throughout, where Chisato says 先生 (lettered センセ in *Recollect* V02) and Fuki, on the telephone, 先生 (V02 S46, S91–98). In *Recollect* she writes daily coffee notes under his arrangement (V01 S17–19; R1-07). In *Recovery days* his coffee arrives before she can decline a break, and she thinks she would have kept working had he asked first (9.8–31). She once pictured him and Chisato understanding each other almost perfectly, then deflated the picture by working out that Chisato could have seen him at the shop (10.637–643). In *Gluttony days*, when Mika phones regulars back for a rematch, she remarks that so indulgent a parent is questionable, and Chisato answers, as his daughter, that it is ideal (10.1000–1005). Mizuki is the colleague to whom Takina explains herself. She tells Mizuki and Kurumi her commercial dislike of Doi (*Ordinary days* 10.616–632). She slaps the sink harder than she meant at Mizuki’s word 卑屈 (14.266–271). Mizuki tells her the regulars’ gifts are for her (*Recovery days* 14.353–356). Kurumi hears Takina’s account of Doi and keeps it from Chisato (*Ordinary days* 10.641–643). In *Gluttony days* Takina guesses that Kurumi may use the word うち differently from her (11.53). Across these scenes other café members possess knowledge independent of hers. They can answer, contribute, refuse or laugh at a proposed solution. [E06-CLM-014/015; SHORT03-CLM-003–006; SHORT04-CLM-001–006, C047–54/078–105.]

**Sakura and unfamiliar guests show the limits of courtesy.** Fuki and Takina object together when Sakura asks to wear the red First uniform. It belongs to Chisato, who consents to the trial. At night Takina privately tries red herself. When Sakura sees her and laughs, an edit withholds what happens before the girl appears restrained and gagged. Takina asks politely for agreement that Sakura saw nothing. This is formal language offered to someone with very little power to decline. Wanting to see herself in red can mean aspiration, curiosity, an experiment or embarrassment at being seen. We know she tried it and then tried to control the witness. None of those facts constitutes a newly declared career plan. In Short 06 she first tries a cover story, a denial captioned in stilted katakana, ソンナワケ ナイジャナイ デスカァ (C007). When that fails, the precise answer to an excited fan about an issued uniform starts to expose a secret. Chisato interrupts with an improvised cover. The fans praise their convincing role-play. Takina calls the intervention 隠ぺい, the word she had used against Mizuki’s bargain in Short 04 (C019; Short 04 C026). She cannot perform a lie, so she tells the literal truth and names the other person’s cover. Her candor here yields agreement on entirely unintended grounds. Outside this particular audience the same degree of disclosure might have a different result. [SHORT05-CLM-003–006, C032–43/057–70; SHORT06-CLM-001–003, C007–20.]

**Clients, customers and opponents meet a professional first.** With people she serves, Takina’s courtesy is complete and her criteria are her own. To Doi she keeps offering service, asking for his order although his blend is ready, because it is work (*Ordinary days* 10.214–220). Her private ledger of regulars is commercial. A seat held for hours over one coffee is a cost, and a writer who orders sweets and espressos is a top contributor (10.620, 10.629). With Clarisse in *Gluttony days* she is blunt about danger and practical about appetite. She asks whether the girl’s father would really come for her, tells her she would certainly die if the guards withdrew, and orders her ramen without the garlic she cannot face (9.476–479, 9.543–558, 10.120–121). When Clarisse’s confession comes out, Takina first meets her glance coolly and cries out that some things must not be done. At Chisato’s small shake of the head she holds her tongue, and she later answers the girl’s apology kindly (10.692–699, 10.726–730, 10.797–819). With Madame she can find a mortal standoff clean and even pleasant, recognize in an older killer the same eyes as her own, and hear the woman’s parting words as praise (10.253–256, 10.542–560, 10.794). With Itō, the manga artist, she manages the work. She cuts into the small talk because there is no time, objects to an incongruous reference weapon, and thinks Itō should state her priority aloud (*Recovery days* 10.167–189, 10.300–368). With a captive she wants something from, in E10 and in the *Gluttony days* capture of a tail, she can hurt and threaten (9.673–682). With Kana in *Ordinary days*, as Mizuki reports it, she wanted the abusive teacher dead and offered to take the consequences of Kana’s shot on herself, and Mizuki stopped both (18.887–889). Kana herself sees Takina as a well-honed blade with no malice (18.97–145). There is no single rule for strangers. The service relationship, the protective contract, the professional opponent and the captive each receive a different Takina.

**Chisato receives permissions nobody else automatically inherits.** Early on, Takina reaches for a way of checking the artificial heart in public. Her first question about it is cut off when Chisato calls her away to the tour, and Mika’s policy is to leave the telling to Chisato (E05 C0076–0083). She asks again directly. Chisato makes the privacy condition intelligible and the question can later be revisited privately. Alone with her, Takina says no one else is here now, listens at her chest and confirms there is no heartbeat (今は ほかの人いませんよ; 本当に鼓動 ないんですね, C0423–0425). Adachi says he wanted the episode to end on the impression of Takina listening to the sound of Chisato’s chest, 胸の音 (*Heroine Archive* S80–S81/p078–079). In E07, Takina can be the partner who leaves the room. By E09, she is the person who tries to limit Chisato’s activities because she understands the danger and has a different view of what to do with the remaining time. At the end of TV she follows Chisato precisely because withdrawal has costs for the people left behind. After the tower crisis Chisato turns back for her own bag and names the charm on it, Takina’s E08 gift, as the reason (E12 C0471–0473). The shorts show Chisato tending her hair. Asked by Fuki whether they live together, Takina says she is looking for a room (部屋 探し中です), and Chisato answers that she may stay as long as she likes (SHORT05 C027–029). [REL-CHI-TAK-E05-001/004; E07-CLM-014; E09-CLM-016/017; E12-CLM-046; E13-CLM-029/040/041; SHORT04-CLM-003; SHORT05-CLM-002; audit recheck on the bag and charm.]

Takina keeps a working model of Chisato and revises it. In A1 she calls her 非常識な人 and 謎だらけ (E03 C0462; E04 C0261). The novels give the model in narration. In *Recovery days* Chisato is greedy, extravagant and selfish, childish and yet sometimes detached beyond her years: a strange person, 変な人 (9.401–402). She is the strongest Lycoris and has become someone the public likes, loves and worries over, and Takina cannot tell whether that is good or bad for a Lycoris (12.495–500). At moments Chisato seems to her an adult and herself a child, more than a single year’s seniority would explain (18.34–37). After a short acquaintance Takina claims to understand her well enough to repeat an order to stay in bed (12.142–146). In *Gluttony days* she judges that Chisato reads people better than she does (10.258–269, 10.1008–1009). The texts mark where the model is hers. She is certain that Chisato stepped back into a bookcase on purpose so that a pose would match a manga panel, and resents being outclassed even in play (悔しさ, *Recovery days* 10.254–278). She suspects Chisato of using a job to visit a ramen shop she had lacked the courage to try, and Chisato never admits it (*Gluttony days* 9.526–535). In *Recollect* she names Chisato’s motto, やりたいこと最優先, and turns it against the director’s career Chisato has just imagined for herself (V02 S62–63). Chisato’s model of Takina can be wrong too. In *Ordinary days* Chisato builds a romantic theory from her own paraphrase of Takina’s interest in a customer and from her rule that Takina notices only skill or threat (10.120–128, 10.203; OD-09).

Takina’s words for the relationship are sparing. In *Recovery days* she tells a stranger at a market stall that the red rice is for 寝込んでいる友人, a friend who is ill in bed (12.571). It is the only time in that book she names the relation aloud. In *Recovery days* 相棒 occurs in her focalized narration and in dream reasoning, while aloud the word is Chisato’s (9.300, 9.321, 14.310). In *Gluttony days*, refusing to let Chisato dry her hair, she says the partnership has nothing to do with it, 相棒は関係ない (14.143–145). Her interrupted plans say more. Leaving the sickroom she works out a walk combining the spice café with the shopping street. Her thought that it would be more fun with Chisato breaks off (千束となら、もっと──). She turns back to propose it and gets as far as 千束、今思ったんですが before she finds Chisato warming the thermometer (*Recovery days* 12.678–691). In A1 the partnership is claimed in the first person by Chisato, not by Takina (E13 C0369–0370, C0445).

The forms of her care are concrete and often administrative: schedules, a three-ring telephone rule, a booked check-up, gauze held back (E08-CLM-025/026; TAK-CARE-ADMIN-01; E13 C0008). In the novels they extend to sniffing Chisato’s hair to check whether she has bathed, buying red rice at dawn, pressing a handkerchief to her sweating brow and reading the contents of a vitamin pack Chisato swallows unread (*Recovery days* 12.121–127, 12.406–411, 12.520–593; *Gluttony days* 10.117). Receiving care is harder for her. She expects to be of little concern to anyone and is caught out when the regulars send her gifts (*Recovery days* 12.669–673, 14.353–358). When Chisato pats her head in front of Clarisse she privately wants to brush the hand away and lets it stay for the child’s sake (*Gluttony days* 10.815–819).

## Closeness without a settled name

Takina is a minor, and so is Chisato. This portrait limits itself to what the sources depict. The evidence supports a particular, reciprocated attachment and does not settle its name. Domestic intimacy, bodily knowledge, arguments, play, rescue and wanted company support a reading of romantic attachment as well as one of intense chosen partnership. The acts she actually permits deserve to be named before any conclusion about the bond. The series gives no settled declaration of exclusivity, sexual history, or reliable account of how she would respond to unrelated romantic attention.

Evidence of romantic interest elsewhere is thin and runs against it. Asked in E06 about the male counterpart organization, she wants to know what its members do, and Chisato’s tease about boys gets そういうことじゃないです (C0206–0209). In *Ordinary days* the café’s theory that she is interested in a customer collapses when she explains her commercial dislike of him. Asked whether she saw him as a man, she asks whether he was a woman (10.616–632). Doi’s private dinner invitation ends the chapter before she answers. The narrator calls what followed needless to tell, and the outro finds Doi laughing strangely, Chisato prostrate and Takina irritated: the direction of the joke, without her words (10.688–690; 19.11–12). In *Recollect* V02 she says Lycoris have no need for romance, and asks why the films Chisato lends her keep containing kiss scenes, which makes Chisato fidget (S68, S76). The moments of closeness in the novels are dream-framed or carefully bounded. In the *Ordinary days* dream, minutes after the imagined Chisato’s question, she murmurs as if to herself that she would not mind the life at Chisato’s side. When the imagined Chisato proposes going away together, she drives on to DA (16.374–406). In the *Recovery days* dream she declines to repeat a dream Chisato’s declaration that they are dating (14.244–312). Awake in *Recovery days*, their hands overlap at the futons’ heads with intent left open (14.336–341). In *Gluttony days* she tells Chisato not to come into her futon, and Kurumi later finds the two asleep in different halves of one set of bedding (14.384–388, 14.684–689). In *Recollect* she pauses a film when Chisato leaves the room, without learning why Chisato left (V02 S64–66). The anthology contributors take this material in incompatible directions, as the comics section shows. None of these sources gives a waking declaration from Takina, and a portrait should not supply one.

## The sound of giving a reason

Takina can put delight, warning or disagreement into much the same grammatical frame. Her ordinary first person in the anime is 私. In all three novels her spoken first person is written in kana, わたし, with a handful of kanji exceptions that the readings leave open as orthography or register (*Ordinary days* 14.218, 16.479; *Gluttony days* 14.270, 14.366). Her narrated self in the novels is 自分 (*Gluttony days* 10.254). Her address to her partner has a dated change. Chisato asks on the first day to be called without さん (E01 C0161–0162). Takina keeps 千束さん through E01 and E02 and into E03’s dormitory scene, where the last instance falls (C0272). On the train home that evening she says bare 千束 for the first time (C0462), and no later 千束さん from Takina occurs in the TV episodes or shorts. *Recollect* V01 does not date a progression. Takina says 千束さん once, with a regular present, shouts ちさと in kana at S64 and uses bare 千束 from S90 on (S39, S64, S90). In one *Repeat 03* comic she apologizes as 千束さん after a blurred photograph, and a burst balloon, by its content Chisato’s, registers that the address has slipped back (稲岡和佐, S13; C1). The plain name does not bring plain speech with it. She keeps です/ます with Chisato in most exchanges and can retain a polite ending even as she asks a very intrusive question. In *Recollect* V02 she speaks to Chisato in です/ます in every setting, at home after a bath and in the bonus. Her plain form appears only in narration boxes, thought balloons and hand-lettered asides (S54–56, S94, S108; R2-10). In E04 指定 asks whether a requirement exists. 私、あのかりんとう好きです names a taste with no need for one. The particle and stance change when she puts the brusque little めっちゃ next to a polite compliment. In E03 her sentence about a lost place finishes in the negative, もう ここにはない, and leaves unspoken where she might belong instead. E09’s 分かりません answers the person entitled to hear how she feels about getting what she once wanted. E13’s 私たちで決めましょう puts a senior she has fought beside inside the deciding “we.” Translating all of these as variations on formal dutifulness would flatten distinctions the Japanese already makes. [E03 C0272–0279, C0462; E04-CLM-004/006/013; E09-CLM-035; E13-CLM-004; audit recheck on address forms.]

Her second person and her plain forms mark pressure more than intimacy. She calls Chisato あなた at two A1 peaks. One is the E03 dormitory, where Chisato’s being needed by DA is the grievance (C0276). The other is the E13 beach, where she tells her she will not die (C0406). The only あなた to Chisato in *Ordinary days* is a reproof inside the dream (16.128). お前 is for people threatening Chisato or under her hand: Himegama, disguised as the clinic nurse, in E09 (お前… 何をした！, C0012), Yoshimatsu in E12 (C0095–0102) and, in *Gluttony days*, the tail she questions at pen-point after a polite opening (9.673–682). Sakaki Kōsuke, Mika’s actor, cites her turn to お前 with Yoshimatsu as the “mad-dog Takina” he loves (*Heroine Archive* S118/p116). With Majima she keeps a cold あなた (E10 C0226). With the café staff and her former senior she keeps さん: ミズキさん (E05 C0018; E08 C0102; E09 C0275), フキさん (E13 C0072) and, once, クルミさん (E08 C0213), though Kurumi is a bare クルミ when Takina speaks of her (E06 C0451; E13 C0375). Erika is a plain エリカ (E13 C0006). Mika is always 店長, and Kusunoki is 司令 (E07 C0340). Plain endings break through with Chisato at particular moments: でしょ with the gift and the café (E08 C0421, C0427), 嫌だ in the tower, アホは そっちでしょ in the forest (E13 C0357). Thanks can be a plain ありがとう, to Chisato at parting and to Erika (E09 C0379; E12 C0442). Institutional vocabulary follows her into domestic talk. The café staff are her 組員 (E08 C0222), eating is 摂取 (*Ordinary days* 14.19–31), a household ruling gets 了解 (*Recollect* V02 S96), and her uniform was 支給, issued (Short 06 C012).

Her humor is dry, compressed and often mistaken for literalism. In E01 she turns Chisato’s 意味不明 back on her and answers an invitation to ask questions with あり過ぎますね (TAK-MB-E01-003; C0306). In E02, told by Walnut that a schoolgirl killer is the stranger sight, she answers that one is more rational than a bear hacker. Chisato calls the costume a dog, and Walnut corrects them both: a squirrel (C0162–0165). In E07 good eyes see things they should not (C0081). In E12 she agrees that Erika was a terrible person (C0454). In *Recollect* she answers Chisato’s riddle about a heavy round bag with 首ですね, a head, and claims it as her own joke. Chisato registers that she has started to talk back (言うようになったな) and says it does not sound like a joke from her (V01 S100–101). In *Ordinary days* she labels a joke as a joke when she suspects it will be missed (22.2–32). Adachi warns against overreading one of her E01 lines: her 知らないですよ about Saori’s selfie is a reflexive push-back, like a Kansai そんなん知らんわ, not a statement about what she knows (*Heroine Archive* S124/p122). The other side of the humor is a refusal to be teased off a serious point (ちゃかすなら もういいです, E04 C0248) and a stiff, unconvincing attempt at lying when she needs one (Short 06 C007).

The audience changes what an apparently courteous sentence does. In Short 02 Takina identifies herself to Kusunoki on the phone by full name and apologizes. She says she is a little giddy (少し 浮かれていまして…), protests on her own behalf (いえっ 私はっ！) and assures her commander that all is well (C041–047). Some thirty seconds later, to Chisato, she says she too is giddy: そりゃあ 私も / 浮かれてますから (C058–059). The private 私も reverses the public position she took with her superior, and a bureaucratic reason for finishing work acquires a personal subject. To captive Sakura a polite negative question asks for an answer already prescribed. To an enemy threatening Chisato she can dispense with professional distance, as in E09 and E12. A frightened Takina can scream, a competitive Takina can celebrate, a threatened Takina can become terribly plain. Quieter speech can be an admission or a rebuke. Her controlled 分かってます to Fuki carries the decision to give up DA (E11 C0203). There is consequently no reliable instruction to make her increasingly breathy, flat, loud or casual as her familiarity with the listener increases. [SHORT02-CLM-002–004, C041–59; SHORT05-CLM-005, C070; E06-CLM-020/021; E09 C0012; E11-CLM-009; E12 C0095–0102; E12-AUD-012/013.]

The novels add a layer the camera cannot. Takina’s spoken reasons are often hygienic, commercial or procedural, while the narration gives a concern or pleasure behind them. She tells Chisato she is not worried, only inconvenienced, because a food business cannot keep a sick employee serving (心配はしてません, *Recovery days* 12.44–46). Asked which night was more fun, she will say only which was harder, and then that she does not know (10.689–697). Yet when Chisato arranges for her to join the game night, she answers 知ってますよ: she knows it will be fun (12.477). After the cinema she admits to herself that she enjoyed the day, so she does not mind having been maneuvered, and that this is a little galling. Aloud she gives a trailer and future research as her reasons for going again (18.26–32). She dislikes Doi in the present tense, 嫌いです, and then, once he orders sweets, in the past, 嫌いでした (*Ordinary days* 10.616, 10.632). The change of tense records a judgment revised by evidence. In *Gluttony days* she answers Chisato’s 命、大事に with 了解です while already resolved to kill (10.203–231). Spoken assent and private rule can diverge, and a reader cannot recover the rule from the assent. RD-09 (SPECIFY) and GD-06 (PRESSURE and SPECIFY) are the project’s rulings on this pattern. A1, which never narrates her thoughts, can neither confirm nor refute it there. What the anime does show is that her stated reasons are sometimes the only ones she gives.

What we can say about timing depends on the witness. The Japanese caption and matching cue in E03 place the close of her answer beside the entry of the ending. The attributed continuous listener reports that her ええ finishes about 0.13 seconds before that music. The subtitle remains visible afterward. In E01 an 80-millisecond written gap and listeners’ different estimates of the audible pause do not measure the time it takes Takina to feel something. In the menu short, the first イノベーション and its embarrassed repetition have similar full-mix levels. Context does more interpretive work than absolute loudness. Production testimony describes the performance, not the character. Adachi describes Takina, set against an unwavering Chisato, as 真面目で、純粋で、冷酷, earnest, pure and cold. He first imagined a low voice for her, so that a late emotional explosion would show her change, and dropped the idea after Wakayama’s audition (*Heroine Archive* S123/p121; the interviewer’s example is E12’s 心臓が逃げる！). Wakayama says Takina’s part was mostly returning what Chisato threw at her (S105/p103). Wakayama Shion recalls that her first take of E09’s last scene did not quite sit right with her (腑に落ちない), that she asked director Adachi about the scene’s background and recorded it again. Anzai Chika does not recall that exchange. The interviewer reports that Adachi, asked shortly before, does not remember it either. Wakayama jokingly asks whether it was all her illusion, Anzai puts the gap down to her own and Adachi’s memory, and Wakayama holds that the scene moved her most even if it was an illusion. The editors head the passage 第9話の収録で見た幻（？） (S106/p104). That recollection is about the making of a performance. It gives neither new fictional dialogue nor transmission of Takina’s thoughts to her partner. In the same conversation Anzai hears a slightly Chisato-like nuance in Takina’s E06 つ、つまらない？, and Wakayama says Takina grew more Chisato-like from there, reporting Adachi’s remark to that effect (S106/p104, S109/p107). Written register in the novels and comics is not performed voice at all. This manuscript uses those measured and attributed results. Its author did not independently audition the uninterrupted Japanese performances for this synthesis. [TV verifier Q05/Q15; E12-AUD-013; SHORT03-CLM-002, C022/026; *Heroine Archive* pages S106 and S109 inspected for this revision.]

## Asaura gives Takina a private answer

Takina focalizes most of all three novels in close third person. In *Ordinary days* she carries Story 2 from its first sentence, the third section of Story 3 and the whole of the Story 4 dream. Story 1 never enters her head, and Story 5 opens through Itō and then follows Kana. In *Recovery days* she carries the opening, *Safety work*, *Cough*, the dream of *Common occurrence* and both cinema endings. Mizukawa and Fuki carry the other two stories, and no passage is focalized through Chisato. In *Gluttony days* she carries most of the first and third courses and two of the After Meals, while Fuki, Kurumi and, briefly, Chisato have the rest. The novels therefore let a reader watch her reason, misjudge, enjoy and hide things, but only inside the scenes she narrates. What other people intend reaches her, and us, as her inference. [OD reading, Stories 1–4; RD reading, *Dog*, *One’s duties* and RD-09; GD focalization table.]

### *Ordinary days*

The prose can supply something unavailable to a camera which only sees a girl turn to the sink. In *Ordinary days*, a complaint about how staff meals take away service time gives Takina a defensible reason to offer a more efficient means of feeding the staff. Her protein shake, taken between jobs, is correct by the stated requirements; only shared taste rules it out (14.19–31, 14.53–65). A connection through Doi allows her to secure good food made by somebody else. Then she makes a meal herself. She cooks because she treats an agreement with Chisato as a rule, and she went along with Chisato’s trick only because Chisato very occasionally shows her a view she had not known (14.281–297). She tests the fridge, makes her first rolled omelette Kansai-style from a phone video and shapes rice balls she thought would be easy (14.308–355). Their awkward shapes provoke laughter. She admits to herself that she trusted manuals and her own dexterity too far (14.366–370). What gets to her is that Chisato is already eating the rice ball when she says how good it tastes. A sense of being embraced is in Takina’s perception, not in the scene. At the sink she is close to flushing, pleased, frustrated and embarrassed. 千束はズルイ is the judgment she cannot quite explain (14.374–385). It matters that she made the thing whose value Chisato recognized. Procuring an excellent meal through someone else would not expose the same part of herself. Takina’s response to Doi’s own attention supplies a corrective. Back at the café she smiles a little while telling the staff that Doi laughed and seemed less gloomy (10.343–344). Chisato reads it as the rare smile Takina gives a good result at work (10.349–352). Asked by Mizuki, she says she dislikes him, 嫌いです. He held a seat at busy times over one coffee, spent the least of any customer and darkened the room. Once he ordered sweets she revised it to 嫌いでした and expected to dislike him less (10.616–632). She disowns the outings as Chisato’s doing and asks what they were for. Concern for the man himself, which Chisato heard as worry about his gloomy face, remains available beside that commercial account (10.97, 10.128). Chisato supplies the courtship interpretation, Kurumi suggests asking Takina, Doi eventually asks her to a private dinner, and the chapter cuts before she answers. The woman who wants her rice ball recognized may have very different reasons for smiling over a customer’s brighter mood. [*Ordinary days* 10.90–128, 10.214–220, 10.335–355, 10.533–599, 10.614–690, 14.27–87, 14.190–249, 14.259–385; OD-04, OD-09, OD-10, OD-17.]

The same book fixes tastes no anime scene tests. At a sushi bar she identifies sea bream and milt by taste, gently corrects Chisato’s bluff and asks how the tempura was made (14.111–159). She recommends a particular shop’s ume inari from her own knowledge (10.246, 10.271). The narration states as waking habit that the café makes espresso in a stovetop pot, that Takina particularly likes it, and that the partners drink sugared espresso before every night job (16.449–450, 16.490). She defends darkness in zombie films as a trigger of instinctive fear and calls Chisato’s wish to see more greed (12.301–306). She once watched the films Chisato lent her with notes, as if they were a drill, now pities her earlier self for it, and still calls the loans a nuisance (15.66–72). She tries to pay for her own lunch and turns down an offer to exercise together (14.78–88, 14.169). When Chisato says that life is short and that this protein lunch is a memory she will keep, Takina says she will think about it (14.175–188). [OD-14, OD-15.]

Story 2 shows her lethal capability inside café life, from her side. She judges that Chisato never kills but goes to the edge of it without mercy. Her own speed has risen under Chisato’s pull, yet she feels she lacks some inhuman quality Chisato has (12.61–72). She resents having to wait for an explanation of Chisato’s method and points out that live rounds can disable without killing; Chisato hands that job to her as their division of labor (12.77–95). Against the armored Bulldog she proposes withdrawal, concedes when Chisato charges, and covers her (12.134–148). When he draws a knife on Chisato she cries Chisato’s name, slides to her knees and empties her magazine into the blade, tearing off his fingers, then shoots his hip joints so that he cannot rise. Her lethal-class rounds maim to protect Chisato, and nobody dies (12.171–204). She does not dislike the way a defeated guard stood in the way for someone else (12.232). She suggests leaving the escaped target to the police, sees the value of a live capture once Chisato explains it, infers why DA keeps Chisato, and calls off the film they had planned (12.233–255). On the train she warns the fugitive, in the same calm tone as their film talk, that her full-metal-jacket rounds will be fired only if Chisato fails (12.315–316). OD-12 and OD-20, both SPECIFY rulings, record this. Her readiness with force is undiminished by café life, and her habit is to name the cost or propose the safe plan, then carry out Chisato’s.

Takina also has an imagination capable of testing incompatible futures. In the zombie dream of *Ordinary days*, focalized through her and of open ownership, she considers work, food and a life together. Since her expulsion she has been a Lycoris for individuals and a café worker at Chisato’s side, a life she would once have dismissed. She murmurs that she would not mind (嫌じゃないですけどね, 16.374–379). So much time has passed that it feels like talking to herself, and she has no wish to convey her feelings (16.380–385). The imagined Chisato has waited and asks whether they should go away together. Takina cannot account for her own reaction, or say how she would have looked away had she not been driving. She refuses the change of course: they will go to DA, in order to survive (ダメです。ＤＡに行きますよ, 16.386–406). Over an espresso pot that makes enough for two, she considers answering the earlier question differently. Following Chisato’s example can mean doing what she privately wants or sustaining a limit despite the cost. The text leaves the object of her wish unnamed (16.519–559). Inside the dream she reasons that it is not hers, because dream Chisato uses a technique Takina did not know (16.143–148). Asked about her own dream, she begins to say she wants to work as a First Lycoris and is cut off, and she privately wonders whether she will one day wear the red uniform (16.96, 16.196–197). That is SOURCE_SPECIFIC and dream-framed (OD-11). CP5’s reading of the Short 05 uniform trial, curiosity and embarrassment without a declared ambition, is unchanged. The cast page’s note that she wants results in café work is publisher paratext and concerns the café, not rank. On waking, she pushes the sleeping Chisato away and later gives the dream a qualified favorable appraisal: it was not so bad (16.587–617). Private wishes and the imagined speech of a version of her partner are rich material for reading the first story. They give no waking agreement or future schedule. [*Ordinary days* 16.96, 16.143–148, 16.196–197, 16.351–431, 16.519–559, 16.587–617; OD-05, OD-11.]

### *Recovery days*

*Recovery days* opens on a pleasure she has acquired. Mika’s coffee arrives before she can decline a break. The narration gives the cup its longest sensory passage, acidity, bitterness and a late sweetness, and she now understands a magazine line about coffee that meant nothing to her when she came (9.8–31). Her objections to the cinema are costs: the price, some forty extra minutes that turn two hours into half a day, the efficiency of streaming or postal rental (9.105–112). She would rather be working harder (もっとバリバリに, 9.78). She invites the others along to dilute Chisato, and finds it almost pleasant to hear someone tell Chisato what to do (9.181–206). At the cinema she prefers a different trailer, and agrees to see both films so readily that it surprises her. She covers the surprise with the straw and finds she is drinking the cola. Then she notices she had said a line about the shop from inside it, as one of its people (9.410–418). RD-11, an EXTEND ruling, sets that Kyoto and DA baseline against the changes the café has made.

In *Recovery days* she has reason to value being competent at care. She identifies a cough by its sound before its source and sends Chisato off the floor. Teased for being worried, she denies it on hygienic and commercial grounds (12.6–46). The person she nurses has a measured fever. Takina responds to a particular request about the following morning’s red rice, arranges udon for dinner and makes room for privacy when asked. She also orders enough for her own appetite. On the dawn walk she judges that Chisato would go down a ten-metre slide and adds that she will not (12.535–544). When Chisato says that being worried about is pleasant, Takina privately places herself among the ordinary people nobody much worries about. She keeps the thought to herself: 特別な人間に、凡人の気持ちはわからない (12.664–673). Chisato arranges, through a video greeting to the regulars, for Takina to join the game night. Takina sees the second purpose and answers that she knows it will be fun (12.471–479). Chisato later recovers, games overnight and tries to warm the thermometer. Takina suspects she was sent home so as not to stop the game, and grants that only Chisato knows her intention (12.697–705). Whatever the motive for sending Takina home was, the attempt to feign illness is real. Takina coughs in the middle of her lecture on discipline. Later Mizuki tells the now-sick Takina that the regulars sent more gifts. Takina asks whether they are really for her and offers Chisato as another possible recipient. Mizuki says they are all for Takina. Takina now understands what Chisato meant about being worried over, and the understanding itches (むずがゆさ, 14.353–358). The regulars have anticipated one of *her* needs. Nobody has to instruct her in what sort of face a grateful person makes for that discovery to matter. [*Recovery days* 12.6–155, 12.228–249, 12.382–420, 12.471–479, 12.520–724, 14.344–371; RD-04, RD-09.]

*Safety work* is the book’s clearest record of her adjustment, and of a shared failure. Asked after the fact to serve as a manga artist’s model, she first finds the request high-handed, then reconsiders. Since leaving DA she has learned that her common sense is not the world’s. She recalls an underwear incident and a cream-heavy pancake, pleasures she could not have imagined at DA, and wonders, hedging, whether Kusunoki’s order to learn from Chisato meant this (10.57–63). At Itō’s flat she manages the time, holds to her rule that every muzzle is a killing beam, and admires Chisato checking a revolver by touch while still talking (10.167–214). Posing for a panel, she attacks half in earnest and finds Chisato’s revolver already at her temple. She cannot hide her frustration at being outclassed, even in play with a toy (10.244–278). Then, for the first time, she makes たまごふわふわ, a dish a teacher in Kyoto had described. She is proud of it and suppresses the smug face as unseemly (10.420–544). The rifle the partners brought as a reference was taken on that night’s job. Each assumed the other had cleared the chamber. It discharges in Itō’s hands, damaging her tablet and wall, and nobody is hurt. Takina first rounds on Chisato, then reconstructs the handover. With a civilian as its destination, she concludes, the mistake was hers, and she apologizes (10.559–590). They work to recover the manuscript while concealing part of what happened. Kurumi recovers files and Mizuki keeps the café running alone. The people bearing the cost of a repair are not always the two who made the decision. [RD-02, RD-07, RD-11, RD-12.]

*Dog* gives the book’s only external portrait of her in combat, through the man she shoots. Mizukawa sees a black-haired girl in a waiter’s outfit fire from behind a bar without aversion, like flicking a light switch, and vault it without her eyes or muzzle wavering. Her live round grazes his arm, and he is the man the café was hired to protect. Chisato tells her she need not shoot. Takina’s protest is cut off, and she hauls the bound informant to the lift. Afterwards she proposes securing the protected man as the formal end of the job, and Chisato silences her with a smile (11.227–253, 11.326, 11.385, 11.402–406). Whether she meant to stop him firing on someone else or fired by default, the text gives only his perception (RD-08).

*Common occurrence* is a dream, focalized through her and framed as one. Inside it she proposes that they are dreaming and that the dreamer is she. She fights the slasher, wrenching a machete from a corpse and aiming for the knee (14.64–67, 14.188–211). Dream Chisato announces that they are dating, as a way of surviving the plot, and asks Takina to say it with her. Takina questions whether the killer would listen and never supplies that declaration (14.244–270). She reasons that so much attachment to rules sounds more like herself than like the selfish, free Chisato she knows. The rule-keeper at the café, she concludes, is Inoue Takina. She ends the plot by an act of will framed as efficiency, and then allows that the dream need not be hers (14.277–312; RD-14, SOURCE_SPECIFIC). Climbing into the rescue truck, she had offered Chisato her hand, the reverse of Chisato pulling her along at the cinema (14.295–297; 9.321). She wakes without remembering. Their hands overlap at the futons’ heads, and the narration leaves intention open (14.336–341). These are a dreamer’s models of herself and of her partner, not testimony about Chisato’s wishes.

The book lets her be particular about the movies as well. A trailer catches her interest. At the first ending she asks why people stay through the credits, and concludes for her own reason that, having paid properly, one should take in the whole work (16.2–19). Chisato counts five attempts from five tickets, an untagged line assigned by context (16.29). Takina sees that their chances improve if they try together. She proposes the arcade before the others go their separate ways. Her stated reasons are loss and odds, and they deliver the togetherness she has just seen Chisato wanted (16.34–40). At the second ending a penguin sits on her lap after the outing. She can find the film too gory and like the killer’s design. She realizes that Chisato has maneuvered her into choosing the next film, and is certain that Chisato knows she has noticed (18.11–22). She admits to herself that she enjoyed the day, so she does not mind, and that this is a little galling (18.26–29). When Chisato offers an explicit way out, Takina interrupts to say she wants to go again. She will investigate further information as it becomes available before choosing the title. Chisato asks her to say if the film looks promising and suggests going together. With her face half hidden against the penguin, Takina agrees (18.30–40). The first outing took place, and the second screening remains ahead of the text. [*Recovery days* 9.135–164/363–428, 16.2–40, 18.4–40; RD-01, RD-10, RD-13.]

### *Gluttony days*

In *Gluttony days* she moves from moderating Werewolf, with access to the hidden roles, to playing from a seat where she lacks that knowledge. The opening fixes her working pleasures. Her favourite moment in siphon brewing is the draw-down, when the earlier work bears fruit at once, and she is glad when a pause means the coffee will be drunk warm. She is ashamed of inferring from Mika’s face during a phone call that someone may be in danger, because the inference is bad manners (9.39–71). At the ramen counter she judges her bowl manageable, regrets skipping the garlic that would have sharpened the soup and ties back her hair to save time. She understands from Chisato’s stuffed cheeks that Chisato wants a spare tie (9.561–611). Afterwards she feels something nearer accomplishment than satisfaction (9.614–627). In the after-hours scene she calls the café うち, and Chisato tells her she is glad to hear it. Takina suspects she has said it before and is embarrassed (11.15–59). One character hearing the word as a discovery does not date the other’s first sense of belonging. Her question about selling food is commercial, and Mika reverses her criterion. Whoever comes for coffee and sweets, which nobody needs, has chosen the place. She follows the thought to its end: a place people could do without and still want is loved (11.18–44). By the end of the first course she laughs at how complicated, troublesome and interesting people are, and leaves the moderator’s chair for a player’s (10.982–1018).

*Gluttony days* supplies three especially sharp qualifications. First, Takina can spend a cost on pleasure knowingly. Her stated rule is that night meals supply only what a mission needs (14.183). Against it she weighs sleep, a regular’s child’s birthday party the next day and hunger. She reasons that low blood sugar might make eating necessary and assumes a First would never sanction such indulgence. Once roast beef is mentioned, she says she will eat (14.456–486). She suggests *Gremlins*. Asked whom she is wronging, she names the person she will be tomorrow (14.645–665). Her breakfast plan, rice, miso soup, pickles, natto, good nori and grilled salmon, shows what the rule was holding back (14.302). Second, with Clarisse she can recognize the possible necessity of severe force and engineer separation from the partner most likely to oppose it. Her first failure to fire comes earlier. The shooter looks like a plump woman in her fifties, the kind of customer who might bring friends to the café, and Takina is vexed to find herself more steeped in ordinary life than she knew (世俗に染まっている, 9.827–836). At the back door of the house where they shelter, she answers Chisato’s 命、大事に with 了解です while already resolved to kill. She dates the decision to a look exchanged with the woman from the doorway. Whose life 命、大事に meant is not said. Privately she puts it bluntly: 殺すしかない, and Chisato was in the way, because nobody may die on Chisato’s job (10.203–231, 10.253–256). In the fight she tells herself that being killed does not matter, only the mission’s failure, because Chisato alone might not be able to protect Clarisse (10.489–506). Facing Madame with raised pistols, she finds the moment clean and even pleasant. Then the smell of garlic and Chisato’s うちのたきな reach her. Her head starts to turn like a dog’s at its name, and her intent to kill dulls (10.542–570). Afterward she concludes that her capture of the tail let the attackers confirm who the guards were. The spoken confirmation was Chisato’s, and Takina does not count it (9.729–734, 10.704–709). Clarisse cancels the commission, undertakes payment and later reports that she has completed the transfer. Madame declines to verify it. The child names fear for Takina and the guards’ kindness. Takina’s theory is that sharing the ramen meal helped Clarisse change her mind. In the later apology scene Takina owns her inexperience, smiles and asks Clarisse not to repeat the act. Clarisse *then* shows relief, cries and hugs her. Takina pats her back. Assigning the child’s decision to the adult’s later reassurance would erase both girls’ actions. [*Gluttony days* 9.827–836, 10.195–256, 10.483–570, 10.647–743, 10.791–819, 14.183, 14.302, 14.456–486, 14.626–665; GD-01, GD-05, GD-06, GD-10.]

Third, the same author can stage bodily closeness and give the reader evidence of reluctance. Takina twice refuses Chisato’s offer to dry her hair. The partnership, she says, has nothing to do with it (相棒は関係ない, 14.143–145). She yields after persistence to save time. The handling is gentler and more attentive than she expected, and the strokes feel like being patted and praised. We get ticklishness and an embarrassment she hopes will pass for the bath’s flush (14.157–162). The drying stops when Chisato raises the question of food. Later comes an appreciation of the difference between their ways of doing it, noticed by stroking her own head, and Takina drying Chisato’s hair (14.163–179, 14.218–276). Chisato characterizes the latter exchange as tacit understanding. Takina says the sign was obvious. The reciprocity is genuine. Her embarrassment neither invalidates her first answer nor proves a secret wish at the moment she gave it. The book ends with the two trading diet resolutions after a sukiyaki dinner, each offering to join the other (15.44–47, 17.54). The filmed hair care in Short 04 has its own evidentiary ceiling. No one in that camera view can hear an internal justification from Asaura’s separate story. [*Gluttony days* 14.139–179, 14.218–276; SHORT04-CLM-003.]

## What the comics can change

Abe Kanari gives Takina some of her clearest opportunities to ask *whose wish* a request represents. In *Recollect*, Chisato makes the café uniform the equipment and the smile the weapon of a mission. Takina accepts the reasoning only provisionally (わかったことにしておきます) and points out that she has already served customers without smiling. Once Chisato names the smile as something she personally wants, Takina can explain the circumstances under which she smiles on her own. She smiles when she is having fun (V01 S11–13). Later panels show her smiling after a ruling in her favor, after her own joke and while predicting Chisato’s evasion, never on request. Whether any of them meets her stated condition is the reader’s inference (R1-06). A movie assignment repeats the mistake from the other side. Her body leans with the action, and a notebook labelled 映画ノート lies on her table (S49). She skips the credits because of her idea of how much viewing she has to finish. Chisato supplied no deadline or report. In a later film scene Chisato stands up to avoid an intimate moment. Only when she returns does a panel disclose that Takina paused the film when she left. Takina accepts Chisato’s flat praise as a matter of course (当然です), and Chisato’s reason for leaving reached her only as a trip to the toilet (V02 S64–66). Consideration, literal joint viewing and Takina’s understanding of their agreement are all possible. An invented intimate motive would undo what the page turn does: we discover an act without gaining complete access to her reasons. [Abe Kanari, *Recollect* V01 S7–14/43–52; V02 S51–70; R1-01, R1-06, R1-07, R2-10.]

*Recollect* keeps her DA goal live beside the café. Told that partners cover each other’s gaps, she asks what happens when she leaves, states that returning to headquarters means leaving, and then denies having any gaps at all (V02 S10–13). Asked about travel, she has no particular wish to go, allows that it would probably be fun, and says that for now she would rather return to DA (S69–70). At Chisato’s home, having cooked the moon-viewing soba because she lost at rock-paper-scissors, she vows that next year Chisato will cook it (来年こそ…作らせます, S108–112). No line reconciles the vow with the goal, so neither commitment nor departure follows (R2-13). In the bonus she has noticed that Chisato snacks less, asks whether something is wrong, and catches Chisato dressing up a change of subject as a good story (S115–118). Abe gives her a scrupulous watcher’s attention to Chisato’s habits and no access to the heart’s history (R2-14).

Abe’s Takina enjoys being the author of a joke. Challenged to make Chisato’s heart race, she first objects that nobody could judge it. Chisato then sets the rule that she will say ドキッ when it happens. Takina tries a water pistol, reasoning that a muzzle quickens her own pulse, and judges it ineffective. Then she presents a piece about Jōmon pottery, 土器, which wins under Chisato’s rule. Chisato objects to winning the word without provoking the feeling. Takina says she too is learning and invokes Chisato’s near-cheating at rock-paper-scissors (V02 S76–84; R2-04). At a nursery pool she offers to teach the children grip and aim, which Chisato forbids. After a child lands a hit, she fires at Chisato herself, saying she cannot accept the result and wants to score. Chisato dodges everything, and whether Takina sees her losing to the child on purpose is not stated (V01 S109–111; R1-09). When the whole watermelon is brought out, she asks whether it will be cut, and the reply is that a nursery has no kitchen knife (S112). On a rainy day Chisato proposes a wager on the next customer, though she herself concedes that involving a customer who has not been asked is rude. Takina objects in turn to its timing, its purpose, its cost to the takings and the case of a customer who cannot be appeased, and asks what Chisato gains. She declines to offer anything (いえ なにも), and Chisato names Takina’s playing along as her reward. When a manga artist arrives, Takina exploits a gap in the terms (V01 S85–94; R1-08). The girl who notices the limit can occasionally want to win quite as badly as the girl who invented the game. These scenes show what Abe gave this pair to do after returning to familiar anime premises, and how an artist can reveal comprehension through a counterexample or a delayed panel. [*Recollect* V01 S81–94/97–112; V02 S73–84/105–112; compare E06-CLM-021/023.]

Two other comics show why it matters whose attention a character can recognize. In ぶりすけ’s *Ignorance is bliss*, Takina starts to leave Itō’s reference session, hears the regular’s request and agrees to assist. When Kurumi offers to show what her own camera has captured, both girls object, and Takina warns her at gunpoint and fires while Chisato cheers (*Reload 02* S68). One accepted likeness gives no permission for the second use. 葉来緑’s *しっぽのきもち* gives the reader privileged access to a disguised Erika. Takina vetoes the discontinued parfait and offers a squirrel dessert. She says it is meant to erase the image of her previous menu item, that Kurumi’s squirrel avatar gave her the idea, and that she had secretly thought it a little cute (*React* S24). Her remark that the squirrel’s tail cannot be replaced is the occasion of Erika’s thanks, made with no sign that she recognizes the customer (S29–30). In A1 she eventually thanks Erika, with ありがとう あのとき and no name (E12 C0442), and can answer the apology. The C1 encounter gives her a different customer-facing task. Kindly service does not require magically knowing the person in front of her. [*Reload 02* S59–68; *React* S17–30; E03-CLM-012; E11-CLM-011; E12-CLM-043/044.]

The anthology contributors disagree fruitfully about what Takina wants from games. In ikra’s *れっつぷれい！* she asks for three days’ notice to practice and initiates a rematch. Chisato puts the request down to her hating to lose (負けず嫌い). Takina answers that the point is a real contest (勝負にならないと), Kurumi says that is still hating to lose, and the comic leaves their reading contested (*Reload* S20–22). In 秋タカ’s *ゲームしようよ* her fifteen-nil run against Chisato is deliberate payback for her chore-rota losses. An inset shows a chore chart with her name in every slot, and she asks whether Chisato understands now. She then thinks she may have overdone it and asks Kurumi for another game. The cooperative one Kurumi supplies has her playing *with* Chisato (S65–67). In 久川はる’s *Repeat 03* comic she already frequents the ramen shop on her own, reports five visits a week and helps a newcomer. This time Chisato declines consecutive nights. That particular food habit belongs to the artist’s Takina. Its value for reading the anime is that an appetite of her own and an invitation that Chisato can refuse are both imaginable. Neither the frequency nor the ramen itself needs to be inherited. [*Reload* S15–22/63–70; *Repeat 03* S101–114; E04-CLM-006; E06-CLM-021/023.]

There are equally substantial disagreements about intimacy. 花田ももせ’s Takina chooses the dessert outing and twice refuses to share a bed. さくらうめ gives her a night when a horror film makes proximity welcome. Chisato objects to company in the bathroom, Takina waits, and sleeping together is agreed separately. GUNP lets Chisato propose a hug on general grounds about its soothing effect. Takina offers her arms and confirms. Afterward each initially locates the desire for comfort in the other. Takina acknowledges the effect on herself. A second request and consent can now be spoken directly. 若鶏にこみ, by contrast, follows a doctor’s warning that Chisato will last a little longer if she does not run about. When Chisato asks Takina to treat her as normally as the others do, Takina answers that the others’ normality is the real kindness, and that she, unfortunately, is not kind. Chisato ends up wired to a tree, objects and escapes, and Takina insists that she *is* kind (*Repeat* S97–108). The graphic joke works because a sincere protective impulse has become an alarming experience for its recipient. ぺけ’s *Reload* comic is a milder case: by Takina’s own account she moved the sick Chisato to the café by force (強制収容), and she hides the medicine Chisato refuses in porridge (S79–81). Add the dream dog whose obedience belongs to *Chisato’s* imagination in 苗川采’s story, and counting panels of physical affection becomes an exceptionally poor way to infer a waking norm. [*Reload* S43–54/79–81/99–108; *Repeat* S97–108/151–158; *Repeat 03* S155–162.]

A final comic comparison matters beyond romance. In しーめ’s *In perfect harmony* Chisato twice summons Takina by name at the café, and Takina supplies two quite different readings of what she wants: a drink, because someone seemed thirsty, and later a question about joining a game after closing. Kurumi and Mizuki hear Takina’s explanations. The tiny face inside the balloon on S102 belongs to Chisato. The exchange gives no demonstration of equivalent shorthand *from Kurumi*, and no claim to exclusive access to what Chisato thinks. In 弐尉マルコ’s *Repeat 02* comic, Takina explains how she worked out Chisato’s actual location, then guesses what she is thinking and gets an immediate denial. Even the correct inference needs its own ground. Distinct forms of attention can coexist in late, intimate, seemingly effortless coordination. In the same しーめ comic Kurumi doubts that experience explains the coordination, since their time together should be short, and Mizuki proposes that Takina is coming to resemble Chisato. Takina rejects the label and promptly answers the next call (*Reload 03* S100–102). [*Reload 03* S93–102; *Repeat 02* S129–138; E11-CLM-008; E12-CLM-002/014.]

The anthologies also let contributors state Takina’s change in so many words, and those statements stay with their contributors. In はも’s *Repeat 03* comic Mizuki says Takina has become much rounder, and Kurumi says the earlier Takina would not have come to a sleepover. When Chisato is sorry the day is ending, Takina says that only means it was that much fun, then hits her with a pillow and tells her not to say things unlike herself (S46, S51). In 久川はる’s comic Takina recalls a pancake meal when she did not yet understand, and says she now understands a little the feeling of wanting to eat something good with someone (S112). In みかづち’s *Repeat* comic she says she has grown used to Chisato’s practice but finds caring even for enemies’ lives unreasonable. Her はいはい acknowledges a condition she has said does not convince her (S63, S68). In 弐尉マルコ’s *Repeat 02* comic, told by Chisato that she can no longer escape Takina, Takina answers that she really will not let her get away again, and Chisato, flustered, asks her not to turn so serious so suddenly (S137). Each is one creator’s Takina. Together they show which anime traits the contributors found most portable: literalism, a cost-conscious care, an unconverted ethic and a possessive watchfulness about Chisato’s disappearing.

## What she can do, and what she will

Her professional skill is the most heavily evidenced fact about her. She is an exceptional shot at range. Chisato delegates the drone shot to her in E02, her rescue fire from cover in E06 leaves an attacker crying 脚が！, and in *Recollect* she puts a tight group into a silhouette target (E02-CLM-005–008; TAK-MB-E06-005; V02 S7–9). She drives, speaks Russian and manages a firefight’s logistics, from calling 弾切れです！ to directing a client’s evacuation (E02 C0133–0135; E01 C0242–0244; E05-CLM-010–012). The novels add close-quarters violence: a kick from a steel-capped loafer that breaks ribs, a deliberately triggered ballistic airbag against an older professional, a pen at a man’s skull (*Gluttony days* 10.395–506, 9.673–682). Her civilian skills are learned and real. She runs the café’s accounts and cuts its costs, designs menu items, keeps coffee notes, cooks dishes she has only read or heard about, and tastes precisely (E08-CLM-006–012; *Recollect* V01 S17–19; *Recovery days* 10.420–544; *Ordinary days* 14.111–159).

Her limits are as specific. Chisato outclasses her in close combat, evasion and rock-paper-scissors, and Takina knows it (*Ordinary days* 12.64–72; *Recovery days* 10.254–278; *Recollect* V01 S111). She cannot keep up with Chisato’s sprint across a park, and reads it as a glimpse of real ability at a moment with nothing to do with work (*Recovery days* 9.333–338). By her own estimate she reads people less well than Chisato, and after the Clarisse job she judges, in hindsight, that she missed the hints and that her capture of the tail set the attack in motion. She does not count Chisato’s spoken confirmation that they were guards (*Gluttony days* 9.728–734, 10.704–709, 10.784–790). She cannot lie convincingly (Short 06 C007). Her errors under pressure include a jointly unchecked rifle chamber, tunnel vision that Chisato has to correct, and, in a dream, a crashed van she calls inexcusable for a professional (*Recovery days* 10.559–590; *Gluttony days* 10.127–133; *Ordinary days* 16.407–437).

Capacity and willingness separate most clearly around killing. She was trained to kill, says so, and in E12 is ready to kill Yoshimatsu (E10 C0127; E12 C0121). The novels show restraint beside the readiness. She hesitates to fire at a woman who looks like a customer, her rounds maim rather than kill Bulldog, and the smell of garlic and Chisato’s voice dull her intent to kill Madame (*Gluttony days* 9.827–836, 10.561–570; *Ordinary days* 12.171–204). CP5’s finding stands. There is no global conversion in her use of force, and no evidence that she has given up the capacity.

## How she changes

A development account has to separate change from the late disclosure of a trait already there. Several of Takina’s traits are present from E01 and only gain new objects. The dry wit is there on the first day. So are care that can override orders, the habit of asking what a rule covers, and risk imposed on others on the strength of her own competence (TAK-MB-E01-003; NEG-E01-TAK-001; TAK-CLARIFY-01; TAK-OPS-RISK-01). Her appetite and her literalism are not converted by the café. They are given more to work on.

What changes in A1 can be dated. Through E03, DA is the measure of her worth, and leisure is useful if it helps her return (TAK-MB-E03-001). E03 breaks that monopoly without ending the wish. She loses the seat, returns a blow, says ええ to a shared pleasure, joins a game she refused and drops the honorific from Chisato’s name. From E04 to E08 she learns to state preferences, to set a condition on Chisato’s teasing, to build household and safety systems, to own a workplace’s accounts and to make things for people. In E07 a return to DA is still something she can offer to make (じゃ 私が戻りますよ, TAK-MB-E07-002). E09 turns DA into an instrument. She had meant to refuse reinstatement and accepts it because it may help Chisato live. E10 shows the instrument held loosely: outside life was fun, and another firing would be tolerable. In E11 she gives it up. E12 shows the limit of the change. Her care for one person overrides Chisato’s ethic, and only Chisato’s body and refusal stop her. E13 shows the same competence put to a different use. It is negotiated with Fuki, aimed at finding a person through an ordinary photograph, and ends in a proposal that returns the next choice to Chisato. The shorts then test the endpoint in mundane forms: commerce, a trip, a menu, a ghost hunt, a borrowed room, a pair of fans (TAK ledger snapshots E01–SHORT06).

The supplementary sources cannot be placed on that calendar, but they show the same contrast between baseline and change from inside. *Recovery days* lets her notice what the café has altered: coffee she now understands, common sense she has learned is not the world’s, a line about the shop said from inside it (RD-11). *Gluttony days* has her wonder twice whether she has been steeped in ordinary life, once in vexation at a missed shot and once while understanding why Chisato would find DA hard to stay in (9.835, 14.517–522). *Recollect* keeps the DA goal and the domestic vow side by side without resolving them (R2-13). The anthology statements of her change belong to their contributors.

Production testimony describes an arc from outside. Asaura says Takina’s role was to grow, and that he wanted her to become, in the end, a partner able to help Chisato (*Heroine Archive* S136–S137/p134–135). Wakayama’s sense of Takina growing more Chisato-like from E06 on, given above with the performance evidence, belongs to the same register (S109/p107). These are the makers’ intentions and impressions. Within the films, the change most firmly supported is not resemblance to Chisato. It is a change in what Takina uses her own competence for, and in who gets a say in its use.

## Where the portrait stops

Several of the most interesting questions about Takina are open in every admitted source, and a faithful account keeps them open.

*Her past.* Nothing shows her life before DA, her family, how she entered DA, why she was transferred from Kyoto, or what first tied her worth to usefulness. A1 gives her age once: she tells Chisato she is sixteen, a year younger than Chisato (E01 C0159–0160).

*Her private reasons in A1.* The anime never narrates her thoughts. Why she tried on the red uniform, what she felt in the snow, what she knew of Mika’s concealment and what she intends by a sudden plain ending are inferences. The novels’ interior views are source-local. They can SPECIFY or PRESSURE an anime reading without proving that the anime’s Takina thought the same.

*Lines whose speaker is unsettled.* The E12 狂ってる leans toward Takina but is untagged and unheard (C0115). The E09 やったぜ under the fist bump has no speaker tag (C0374). The E05 任せてって言ったのに after the fight with Jin, the veteran hired against their client, answers her earlier 私に任せてください but needs a listener to assign it (C0243, C0358). Whether Chisato or a nursery worker introduces たきなおねえちゃん to the children in E01 is unsettled (C0237). None of these is used here as evidence of her character.

*Dreams.* Whose dream the *Ordinary days* zombie story is stays open among three readings, and the *Recovery days* slasher dream leaves the same question. Her dream statements about a life together, about becoming a First and about the red uniform are hers only within the dream (OD-05, OD-11, RD-06, RD-14).

*Open events in the novels and comics.* Her words in reply to Doi’s dinner invitation; the outro shows only the aftermath (*Ordinary days* 10.690, 19.11–12). Her intent when firing at Mizukawa (*Recovery days* 11.229). Why she flew while ill (*Recovery days* 13.771). What Chisato understood at the back door, and whose life 命、大事に meant (*Gluttony days* 10.204–214). Whether she suspected why Chisato left the film (*Recollect* V02 S64–66). How she learned the nature of Chisato’s rock-paper-scissors wins (V02 S56, S84).

*Rival readings that remain live.* Her attentiveness to Doi can be commerce or concern for the man, and the text keeps both (OD-10). Her pause of the film can be courtesy, literal shared viewing or adherence to their arrangement. Her proposal of the arcade can be pure odds or a way to give Chisato the togetherness she wanted. Her conduct in the E12 tower can be read as attachment overriding ethics, or as an ethic of her own in which one particular life outweighs a doctrine. The films let both stand.

*Labels.* Whether to call the attachment romantic, a friendship (her own word to a stranger, 友人) or a partnership (Chisato’s word, 相棒) is not settled by any waking line of hers. The *Heroine Archive* editors call her Chisato’s 無二の親友 (S26/p024). That is the editors’ word.

*Extrapolation.* The anime’s endpoint establishes a future directed toward Chisato. It does not establish what Takina would call that future, how long it lasts or what she would do if Chisato chose against her. *Recollect* keeps a return to DA in play. The shorts and novels supply ordinary cases without a calendar, so they cannot show a settled later life. Beyond the sources, predictions about her conduct should follow the conditional profile below and say where it runs out.

## What her conduct allows a reader to expect

Give early Takina an institutionally meaningful solution and she will ask what the result can restore. Give her the same offer after Chisato’s prognosis and she has a different question for it. Give her a practical civilian problem whose objective is intelligible and she may volunteer a solution nobody asked for. Before making her veto an expense, ask who incurs it. A shop’s unworkable margin, a gift that creates labor for somebody else and a meal paid for by her own future tiredness produce three different responses. What follows a friend’s objection depends on its substance. She can redesign the chore routine, accept privacy in a particular setting, stand by a price ceiling or keep pressing for a life the intended beneficiary will not buy on those terms. These conditional expectations have different support. The variation in what prompts a practical refusal recurs throughout the originating series. Privately appraising tomorrow’s cost in those terms belongs to *Gluttony days*. [E05-CLM-011; E06-CLM-004; E08-CLM-006; E09-CLM-028–035; E12-CLM-013/014; SHORT03-CLM-004; *Gluttony days* 14.340–486.]

The table compresses those expectations. **Strong** support means repeated A1 evidence with consistent supplementary support. **Moderate** means A1 evidence in one or two settings, or A1 with source-local support. **Source-local** means the expectation rests on A2 or B1 evidence that the anime does not test. The table describes likely conduct for a reader or model. It is not a validated reconstruction.

| Situation | Likely perception and response | Countercondition or limit | Support |
|---|---|---|---|
| A rule, assignment or norm lacks an explicit criterion | Asks what it covers or requires, then follows a stated criterion to the letter (指定; 命 大事にって 敵もですか; the staff-meal shake) | Revises once someone supplies the missing criterion (OD-04, OD-17) | Strong |
| Chisato is under direct threat | Mobilizes at once, armed; drops courtesy toward the threat (お前); will consider lethal force | Chisato’s physical restraint and refusal can stop her (E12); in A2 Chisato’s voice dulls her intent (*Gluttony days* 10.561–570) | Strong |
| A protective task conflicts with Chisato’s no-kill practice | May separate from Chisato and give a spoken assent she does not mean to keep (了解です) | A1 shows the disagreement openly instead; the private rule is A2 evidence (GD-06) | Source-local; A1 supports the disagreement |
| A loss she had a hand in | Claims authorship of her own choice, distinguishes others’ causal share, asks for repair rather than blame | Without full information she over-attributes to herself (E03 全部 自分のせい) | Strong |
| A friend’s error that harmed her | Declines to scapegoat; requires help putting it right | Untested against deliberate betrayal | Moderate |
| An ordinary task with an intelligible goal | Volunteers a solution, usually costed; may originate a product | Misreads how others will see it (E08 parfait, Short 03); withdraws or holds a cost veto | Strong |
| A proposed expense | Asks who pays and whether it serves the café; vetoes on cost | Knowingly spends her own future comfort on pleasure (*Gluttony days* 14.645–665) | Strong; the self-cost is source-local |
| A game she keeps losing | Analyzes the mechanism, changes the procedure, delights in winning | Will exploit a gap in terms she did not set (R1-08, R2-04) | Strong |
| Chisato teases to deflect a serious question | Refuses the joke and restates the point (ちゃかすなら もういいです) | Answers teasing in a safe frame with her own dry jab | Moderate |
| Asked whether she enjoyed something | Gives a practical reason or says she does not know; admits enjoyment privately or obliquely (知ってますよ) | Can say plainly she is excited when Chisato already shares it (Short 02) | Moderate in A1, strong in A2 |
| She receives care or praise | Embarrassed and deflecting; refuses first, then accepts for practical reasons or for a third party’s sake | Later reciprocates the same care | Source-local, compatible with A1 |
| An unfamiliar customer or client | Polite, efficient service; candid about danger; practical help unasked | Captives and opponents meet a different Takina; no default script for an admirer | Moderate |
| Asked to lie or keep a cover | Cannot perform it; falls back on literal truth or silence | Can withhold by not volunteering | Moderate |
| An institutional offer conflicts with rescuing Chisato | Uses the institution while it helps; leaves when it obstructs (E09–E11) | Her first move under pressure can still be compliance (E11 C0078) | Strong for Chisato; untested for others |

Abstain where the sources do. Do not supply her pre-DA history, her family, a declared romantic attachment, a waking ambition to become a First, her reply to Doi or her private view of Fuki, and do not turn a dream’s dialogue into waking speech.

There is no warranted default romantic script for an unfamiliar admirer. Doi’s invitation, whose answer is never reported, is an unusually exact example of why. Nor can one transfer Takina’s behavior with a captive witness to a customer she can serve and hear, or her lethal threshold for Chisato to a stranger whose needs are unclear. Evidence for public competence is much stronger than evidence for her private life before DA. Evidence for choosing the café is stronger than evidence that she discarded every institutional loyalty. Evidence for a future directed toward Chisato is stronger than evidence of what she would call that future. The formal reconstruction model can test how these differences change an action sequence or a line of Japanese. This portrait confers no performance rating and supplies no independently withheld test result.

The enduring change is legible on a scale smaller than an ideology. Once Takina could imagine what she might do well enough to be admitted again. Later, when Chisato asks what to do next, Takina can suggest they start with something her friend had given up. Chisato names the destination herself. Takina has learned that a decision can be hers and still remain unfinished until someone else answers it.
