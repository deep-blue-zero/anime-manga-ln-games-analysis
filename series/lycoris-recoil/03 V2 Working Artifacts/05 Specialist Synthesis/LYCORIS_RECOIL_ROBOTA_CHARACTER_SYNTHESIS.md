# Robota and the pleasure of being recognized

*Lycoris Recoil* · Japanese TV series, through E13. The final two stories discussed below belong to individual contributors to the Japanese manga anthology *Reload 03*. Their encounters have their own premises.

Chisato Nishikigi is trying to save someone when she says Robota’s name. He has asked whether she left her phone behind, heard her say yes, restricted her to his channel and told her to follow his directions. She wants him to hurry. He hears something much more agreeable in her impatience. “You know who I am?” he asks, and launches into what a great hacker he has become. There is a lovely absurdity in the choice of audience. Of all people, why would the girl he is threatening pause to admire his achievement? For a few seconds the mere fact that she knows his name seems enough. The retained pictures give one side of the encounter a sound-only display and the other an impatient Chisato beside her café manager, Mika. Robota can control the terms of contact without having much say in what his name means on the receiving end. [E11 C0022–0031, 00:52.62–01:12.18, especially pictures #000042 and #000043, in the episode reading](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E11_DEEP_READING.md).

Robota is the box-helmeted figure who helps the armed agitator Majima bring Tokyo’s hidden security organization, Direct Attack, into public view. Robota’s Japanese handle is ロボ太. His helmet has round eyes, a small triangular nose and a rectangular grille for a mouth. It makes him identifiable even to a viewer who has never seen his face. Majima knows where to find the man inside it well before the police receive that information. That combination suits someone who wants a famous name and a private face. Fame is his avowed wish. Control over who can see him is an inference with a narrower basis, particularly his protest when Detective Abe tries to make him remove the helmet. The TV story follows a piece of his working life. It offers little biographical explanation for how he came to want these things together. [E06 picture #001148, 21:44.660](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E06_DEEP_READING.md). [E13 C0106–0111, 04:52.69–05:02.82](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E13_DEEP_READING.md).

## The rival he would like to succeed

Robota begins by showing off to Walnut, a celebrated hacker who wants to know how someone found the apartment from which Walnut was operating. He starts with an account of the Alan Institute, famous for supporting talented people. Walnut has heard the elementary facts already. When Robota presents images of an explosion, Walnut compliments the picture. Robota takes the compliment readily. He says he got a new drone for the occasion. He even asks whether Walnut’s present location is another dummy. The danger of being fooled occurs to him at the beginning of the story. A few lines later he declares the advent of a new top hacker in Japan and bids the “old-timer” farewell. His imagined succession calls for an audience of one. He wants Walnut to know who has displaced Walnut. [E02 C0001–0024, 00:02.44–01:06, in the episode reading](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E02_DEEP_READING.md).

Walnut has something better than a compliment on a picture to give him. During the later pursuit, the vehicle carrying Walnut’s party stops obeying the driver. Walnut recognizes the opponent by name, says his skill has improved, and reckons that simply recovering control will give only temporary relief. Robota comes back with the conversational equivalent of *exactly right*. Soon afterward the party escapes his control. The two judgments about him belong together. Someone with grounds for assessing his work acknowledges real progress, and people working together can still frustrate him. Robota’s preferred measure, a single title changing hands, has no place for this perfectly intelligible outcome. [E02 C0197–0204 and C0215–0223, 10:25.77–12:02 onward](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E02_DEEP_READING.md).

Later the hired gunmen think they have killed Walnut. Chisato’s partner Takina reports their charge dead. The person inside the bullet-resistant costume is actually Mizuki, the café employee who helped stage the death. Walnut, whom the viewer comes to know as Kurumi, is alive. Robota asks his client Shinji Yoshimatsu whether he should have had the guards killed too. The question says something uncomfortable about his professional courtesy. He offers up the escorts for the client’s judgment as if checking an omitted detail. It would overshoot the evidence to give him a personal desire to see those two girls dead. Yoshimatsu says the job was well done, closes the assignment that began the previous month, and leaves the prospect of future work. Robota answers in a business register, presents himself as Japan’s best and offers to be available again. After his farewell, the scene gives Yoshimatsu a further thought about what a good tool Robota makes. The favorable words Robota received and the valuation the viewer hears afterward do very different work. [E02 C0319–0340, 16:09.70–17:25, and C0354–0368, 18:04.19–18:40; E02-CLM-010](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E02_DEEP_READING.md).

Kurumi generally gets the better of him on the question he cares most about. Robota thinks her death settled his rank. She understands that letting him think her dead might buy her a life outside the chase. She can give him credit for a piece of work without offering her continued existence as the prize. Robota keeps talking about Walnut long after he believes the person is gone. That persistence remains visible in the way he presents himself to others. What he says about the title is also how he measures himself when his audience has changed.

## Getting Majima to listen

At the end of E04 Robota introduces himself to Majima as the world’s best hacker. He proposes putting his brains and Majima’s power together. Majima cuts the call short before Robota can finish. The distinction between Japan’s best and the world’s best belongs to Robota’s own sales talk. E04 introduces these men to each other. Walnut has already said Robota’s name in E02. For Majima, neither the name nor the superlative supplies a reason to keep listening. [E04 C0444–0450, 23:32.81–23:49.45](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E04_DEEP_READING.md). [TV reconciliation E04-X01](../07%20Evidence%20and%20Indexes/TV%20Audiovisual%20Reconciliation/CLAIM_MATRIX.md).

Their next sustained disagreement shows how much Robota takes in and how selectively he acts on it. He proposes a particular Lycoris, one of the young agents Direct Attack uses, as the next target. Majima wants to know why hired fighters keep disappearing after entering Japan and wants their hidden adversary found. Asked, Robota recites the objective accurately. He is also carrying a second assignment from Yoshimatsu’s side, to interest Majima in that particular girl, Chisato. Majima says that twenty-six of his own comrades have died following Robota’s direction. Robota says the casualties were unanticipated, defends his choice as the quickest way, and offers the girl’s potential interest again. Majima gives him three more days to find Direct Attack’s base. A retained image at 06:36.771 puts a gun in the foreground as Majima sets the deadline. The next sounds captioned are Robota’s cries and groans. Having two clients gives him two sets of expectations and precious little protection when either becomes unreasonable. The deaths are Majima’s count. Robota’s answer shows the distance he keeps from the cost even on Majima’s account. [E06 C0088–0119, 05:18.30–06:47.72, pictures #000375 and #000381](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E06_DEEP_READING.md).

He makes a reasonable attempt to renegotiate. He tells Himegama, Yoshimatsu’s assistant, that Majima has no interest and he thinks the additional task may be impossible. After putting him on hold, she relays her employer’s exhortation to try harder and adds her own polite encouragement. Robota protests that he is already trying. The scene grants him an understandable grievance against people who buy an outcome and offer exhortation in payment for his difficulty. It also shows what he does next. He goes looking for help. On receiving an offer from another hacker he thanks the person at once. He is perfectly capable of acknowledging need when someone appears likely to meet it. When he later sees images of Chisato getting the better of a group of attackers, he wonders whether showing them to Majima would get his attention. He has found evidence that might change this particular man’s mind, after his account of Chisato’s rank failed to do it. [E06 C0121–0149, 06:49.81–08:09.39, and C0210–0212, 11:04.64–11:09 onward; REV-E06-008](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E06_DEEP_READING.md).

Majima does eventually see something in Chisato, with a consequence Robota could hardly have dictated. During their pursuit he locates her again after the men on the ground have lost her. Majima praises the help. After his own bruising encounter he comes back to Robota, and Robota’s first complaint is about the door being broken *again*. Then he greets the party as survivors. The helmet faces Majima at close quarters. Robota’s short, startled replies continue even as Majima tells him his opinion of him has improved. Robota found someone sufficiently interesting for Majima himself. What Majima wants now is more about her. There is a moment of recognition here, but Chisato is its center. Robota has earned new work as well as praise. [E06 C0351–0359, 16:35.31–17:02.21, and C0460–0472, 21:30.10–22:02.47, especially #001148](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E06_DEEP_READING.md).

## What he can tell, what he takes credit for

Once Majima listens to him, Robota is often a more interesting conversational partner. Looking at Chisato’s uncanny ability to avoid being hit, he ventures a deliciously unflattering alternative. Perhaps Majima is a bad shot. Majima answers from experience and from how promptly she returns fire. Later Robota brings him what he starts to call a record of someone who used nonlethal rounds, then downgrades to a rumor about the old radio tower. Majima has personal knowledge of that event and says he brought the tower down. Robota wants to know what he means. The man who can find a likeness across otherwise disconnected reports has just discovered he was speaking to a witness. His initial uncertainty was warranted. Majima supplies his own recognition of the little opponent from years ago. It takes the two men’s different kinds of attention to make that connection. [E07 C0023–0027, 00:58.00–01:10, and C0124–0143, 06:34.00–07:29](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E07_DEEP_READING.md).

He is a better judge of some practical limits than his grand declarations suggest. During the police-station operation he warns Majima that he can buy only an initial delay and that Majima must hurry. Direct Attack’s personnel do respond late and then recognize the diversion. When Majima asks for an explanation and then cuts it off, Robota gets little satisfaction from being right. He also objects afterward that the spectacle was excessive and was covered up anyway. Majima congratulates the useful result and wants something more spectacular next time. Robota can question his associate’s aim and keep contributing to it. His willingness to continue does not turn Majima’s public cause into Robota’s private political conviction. [E07 C0145–0169, 07:33.43–08:42, C0177–0190, 09:06.07–09:40.26, and C0470–0475, 23:33.06–23:46 onward](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E07_DEEP_READING.md).

Their forms of address change. At first Majima calls him “hacker,” sometimes arranging further work with a pointed “top hacker.” In E07 Robota calls the plan *yours*. Majima corrects him to *ours*. In E09 Majima asks who the “Yoshi” Chisato mentioned is. Caught by surprise, Robota first professes ignorance, then identifies him as the Alan agent who supported her. Majima calls him Robota by name and announces that he has thought of a plan. By E10 he can send him back to work as “my hacker.” By E11 Robota himself says “we can do this.” The shift in pronoun gives him a place beside Majima in his own account. The particulars of his loyalty remain unclear. He also protests to Yoshimatsu that he warned the Alan side and has not betrayed it. Pressure from Majima, professional pride and investment in shared success can account for different parts of the same history. None establishes friendship, a transfer of ultimate loyalty or equal power between partners. [E07 C0163–0167, 08:19.85–08:34.08](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E07_DEEP_READING.md). [E09 C0288–0296, 17:15.26–17:34.66](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E09_DEEP_READING.md). [E10 C0074–0075 and C0301–0303](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E10_DEEP_READING.md). [E11 C0148–0155, 08:24.86–08:39](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E11_DEEP_READING.md).

There is an ethical consequence to that developing “we.” Robota presents Yoshimatsu as a hostage at the old tower and tells Chisato that Yoshimatsu will die if she approaches Enkuboku, the new tower, and that there is a one-hour deadline. Then he demands control of their communications. He correctly expects that threatening Yoshimatsu will get Chisato to follow his directions. When Chisato calls him by name he welcomes it almost as though she were a fan. By the time Majima makes his public broadcast, Robota has helped prevent Direct Attack from promptly taking it down. Its staff confirm the disruption while Majima identifies the young agents before the public. Later an announcer describes a man being shot. Robota anticipates that more fighting will liven things up. The pleasure of having an audience has turned cruel. Even so, the person making the broadcast’s claims is Majima. Robota’s responsibility follows from his own actions and comments, without attributing every public statement or subsequent injury to him. [E10 C0415–0429, 21:22.76–21:59.63](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E10_DEEP_READING.md). [E11 C0226–0249, 12:25.31–13:35.25](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E11_DEEP_READING.md). ## The person in the box

One way to recognize Robota is by what interrupts his performance. He keeps track of expenses. The opening drone was acquired for the occasion; when another falls in E11, his first comment is how expensive it was. He minds the door to his room. He can start to greet the men who have come back alive and trail off at their arrival. It would take more than that greeting to decide how deeply he cares for Majima’s followers. He wants help and gives thanks when it is offered. His speech to powerful people can turn formally accommodating. The offer of his future services to Yoshimatsu uses the deferential ご用命. Elsewhere he commonly calls himself 僕. With Majima a deferential answer can give way to a bewildered “eh?” within a few lines. His helmet furnishes the same broad visual expression whatever he says. The words and the other person’s answer have to bear more of the difference. The Japanese cues support these changes in register, the interruptions and the written laughter. Exact inflection, pitch and felt emotion exceed those textual and still-picture witnesses. [E02 C0014–0016 and C0335–0340](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E02_DEEP_READING.md). [E06 C0142–0143 and C0460–0469](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E06_DEEP_READING.md). [E11 C0299–0302, 15:50.26–15:56.52](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E11_DEEP_READING.md).

His last big confrontation is with a person he thought he had disposed of, who calls him by the very name he wanted people to remember. Kurumi has gotten help from Chisato, Fuki and the others on site. She contacts Robota and announces herself as Walnut. After he recognizes her, she declares that she has located him. The subtitle gives him a hum just before he realizes that something is wrong. First comes his incredulity that Walnut can be alive, then his insistence that he himself is the world’s best. An image at 17:48.672 fills the frame with his helmet as the claim appears. Kurumi identifies his location and phones the police. Detective Abe reports the arrest. That broken door earns another protest. Consider all the different things “winning” here might have meant to Robota. He had made the state’s system fail at a crucial moment. The disruption really happened. It neither establishes his chosen rank nor protects his house when an adverse witness can tell the police where to go. He did not plan an adequate answer to someone he had already written out of the competition. [E12 C0354–0390, 17:00.21–18:31.26, especially C0373–0385 and #001088](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E12_DEEP_READING.md).

There is life after this defeat, even in the small amount the series allows him. The lights go out at Enkuboku. Majima means to have time alone with Chisato and observes that hackers need electricity too. In police custody, Robota says he knows nothing about it when Abe accuses him. Abe wants his headgear off; Robota begs him to leave his face alone. The retained still at 04:56.000 shows the two men close together and the helmet on. It would be facile to treat each denial as another evasion simply because earlier he did evade Majima. This time the series has provided a better explanation for the outage. Later Commander Kusunoki cites Robota’s testimony for the total of 1,151 guns. She gives 211 as the number her people recovered. Detective Mitani remarks that he was commended for capturing Robota. His arrest has become both evidence for an inquiry and a line on another man’s career. The scene of testimony itself is absent. The report establishes that he supplied information, leaving the inducement, his relationship to the police afterward, and any change of heart open. [E13 C0027–0032, C0106–0111, C0274–0279 and C0319–0324, with #000302](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Anime/LYCORIS_RECOIL_E13_DEEP_READING.md).

## Two afternoons away from the job

What could he want in the absence of an assignment? Two different contributors to *Reload 03* give particularly good answers because each lets him meet Kurumi with the professional rivalry in abeyance. In いみぎむる’s 「ロボとリス」, Robota goes to the snack aisle and notices the potato chips. Majima’s advice to eat vegetables comes back to him. Then he sees a small person staring up at a high shelf. He offers to reach something for her and hands her the packet she wants. This is Kurumi. To him she is a child with surprisingly old-fashioned tastes. She tells him to mind his own business when he comments on her choice. As she leaves he passes on the injunction about vegetables. The scene lets him be considerate and a little annoying in the space of a single errand. It is his hand that reaches up, his judgment of her that goes awry and his remembered scrap of Majima’s solicitude that he spends on somebody else. He offers the help without naming himself or asking her for credit. His decision tells us more about this contributor’s Robota than a general declaration that he must have a hidden heart of gold. [いみぎむる, *Short story collection*, Act 3, S12–14, in the Japanese *Reload 03* reading](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Manga/Anthologies/LYCORIS_RECOIL_RELOAD03_CONTRIBUTOR_READINGS.md).

In ikra’s 「VS.天才ハッカー！」 Kurumi is killing time at an arcade. Her opponent sits at the other side of the cabinet. His first three losses appear alongside her expressions, so the facing players’ experiences are easy to switch in the telling. He complains, wins one game, celebrates and accepts another round. He loses the rematch. Before leaving, Kurumi gives him credit for taking a game from her. There is no prize and the compliment is hardly unqualified; she considers herself the better player. Yet the exact concession has a sweetness which her professional rival’s claim to the whole world could never earn. The reader learns who played when he gets into Majima’s car. Majima has seen enough to ask how a hacker could have lost to a little girl. Robota’s surprise at being observed is one more reversal. The occasion on which a witness actually watched his work is one he might rather have kept private. Kurumi, meanwhile, remembers an entertaining stranger who might have helped her pass more time. Her thoughts give no indication that she has recognized the hacker. This game settles neither player’s professional standing. These two creators have furnished possible ordinary encounters, each with its own ending; the television characters acquire no shared shopping or arcade memory from them. [ikra, S83–90, especially the reversal at S85–88 and the car and final wish at S89–90](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/Manga/Anthologies/LYCORIS_RECOIL_RELOAD03_CONTRIBUTOR_READINGS.md).

## When someone needs an answer from him

Imagine a harmless exhibition where Robota has built something with another contributor. Minutes before the demonstration, his host offers to introduce him as the best in the business. A fault has appeared which he can probably contain only briefly. On TV evidence I would expect him to welcome the billing and try to keep the demonstration in his hands. I would also take seriously a quiet warning from him to the person on the floor that time is short. He has made both sorts of statement to Majima about the same undertaking. If the other contributor offered help that might save the demonstration, accepting it would be quite compatible with his pride. It is much harder to say how he would credit the other contributor afterward. We have evidence that he asks for help and gives immediate thanks, much less that he publicly shares a coveted distinction. A public slight with no job at stake, a request from an intimate or an enduring duty of care would test other motives. The TV story supplies too little to predict his choices there.

He may take pleasure in games and snacks in the anthology premises. His expenditure on equipment and bodily wish to be left in his helmet have TV evidence. His legal name, age, home circumstances growing up, training, unguarded friendships and romantic life remain undisclosed. Nothing in the rivalry requires an old personal wound or romantic jealousy to make sense. What we can know has more texture than a backstory guessed from his conceit. He can tell someone exactly what he can manage for the next few minutes and still believe he deserves the whole profession’s crown. He can do good work for people who see a disposable tool, take pleasure in hearing his name from a girl he is pressuring, and miss the importance of an opponent who once knew enough to tell him he had improved. Walnut’s early compliment remains one of the fairest assessments he receives. It leaves him room to become better, a prospect he nearly loses sight of in his eagerness to say that he already is the best.
