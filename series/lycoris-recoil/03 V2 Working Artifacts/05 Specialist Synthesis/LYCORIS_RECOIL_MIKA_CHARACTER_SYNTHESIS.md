# Mika

Chisato asks Mika how she looks. He assures her the photograph came out properly. She has to ask again. She wants *his* opinion. He tells her she looks wonderful, quite a grown woman. The distinction between the two questions would matter to any girl showing a parent her formal clothes. It matters particularly between these two. Mika has put a coming-of-age garment into Chisato’s hands because he doubts that she will live to wear it at the usual time. He can arrange the occasion, pay attention to the camera and still miss the part she needs him to supply in words. A few minutes later she asks a harder version of the same question. Does he like the person she has become? This time he answers as her father. [E10 13:15–14:05, 17:42–18:09, MIK-MB-E10-001]

Mika runs Café LycoReco, the neighborhood business where Nishikigi Chisato and Inoue Takina serve customers and undertake work that can put them in danger. He is a Black man with glasses and long hair tied behind him. At the counter he wears a kimono. His cane, his food and the ease with which the girls call him 先生 become familiar well before the series explains what kind of a teacher he was. His past includes dangerous professional work with a man called Jin, training girls for Direct Attack, and a sustained intimate bond with Shinji Yoshimatsu, who arranged the childhood operation that saved Chisato. His own parents, nationality, early education and route into DA remain largely beyond the TV story. Mika has spent much of his adult life making decisions about young people from whom the reasons for those decisions are kept. The hard question about his fatherhood is how far a man formed in that work can let one of those young people become the better judge. [E01 05:28–07:07; E05 19:47–20:17; E09 10:20–13:26; E10-CLM-019–030]

## What the instructor agreed to

The earliest fixed point is a shared past, without a flashback. After the supposed client Matsushita accuses Jin of an old killing, Mika goes to ask Jin directly. Jin says the two of them were together twenty years ago and asks what happened to Mika’s leg. Their familiarity makes Jin’s answer a consequential objection to the client’s story. It does not give us the particulars of their work in those years or answer the question about the leg. In E13 the café staff are able to treat this old associate as a customer. Kurumi asks whether “Silent Jin” chose his own nickname. The man who arrived in the plot as a possible murderer can order anmitsu and coffee in a place run by someone with a memory long enough to challenge the charge. [E05 19:47–20:28, CLM-016–018; E13 14:17–14:35]

Ten years before the present, the child Chisato collapsed after demonstrating her extraordinary ability. On the tablet it is Fuki’s voice calling for her. Mika tells Yoshimatsu she has a congenital heart condition and perhaps half a year to live. Yoshimatsu has access to an artificial heart which may last until adulthood. A Lycoris is active only until about eighteen, says Mika, beginning to add that such a span would suffice. Yoshimatsu supplies the missing end of the proposition by asking whether she would kill enough. Mika promises to meet his expectation. In E10 he will tell Chisato that he sought the operation in his interest as a commander, at least at the time. His shock at hearing the consequence stated so plainly is consistent with his acceptance of it. The man does not get a kinder past by virtue of becoming someone gentler later. [E09 10:20–12:27, MIK-MB-E09-001; E10 15:45–16:18, CLM-022/023]

The rest of the agreement matters as much to Mika's history as the official condition. Asked whether he is good with children, he says 苦手だな, that he finds it difficult. He is regularly surrounded by them because it is his job. He says he would not be doing that work if it were not for his leg. When Yoshimatsu asks for his help with the girl after surgery, Mika initially gives a commander’s reassurance that DA will raise her responsibly. Yoshimatsu wants *Mika*. He calls her “our daughter” and asks for a promise between the two men. Mika calls the prospect an unfamiliar mission. Yoshimatsu corrects the noun. The difference will continue to divide them. A mission could have ended when the subject of the operation had spent her useful life. A personal promise lives on in the voice of the man who asked for it and in the child whose name Mika gives her. The series shows what Mika said about the leg then and what he can do later. It does not license a retrospective diagnosis, a date of recovery or the belief that every use of the cane over that decade was a sham. [E09 12:32–13:26, REL-MIK-YOS-E09-001; E10 14:08–14:26; E13 07:04–07:15, Q13]

Mika remembers Chisato saying she is frightened before surgery. He tells her she will get better. Yoshimatsu is the savior she has identified, though he first told the child she had mistaken him for someone who worked there. When Mika identifies a post-operative present as coming from the savior, Chisato reasons that it must be for helping people. She has never heard the compact between the two adults. Mika later describes the beginning of his relationship with her as 親子ごっこ, playing at parent and child. He uses that phrase to Kurumi while answering what happened; it would be an unusually cruel summary of the life he subsequently gave Chisato if it were taken as the series’ final word. He named her, taught her, built the café with her and introduced her to Takina. He also let her live for years with a mistaken understanding of what his old friend wanted. Both lists are indispensable. [E09 13:14–13:26/13:44–15:55, CLM-022–027; E10 14:08–14:32, 16:26–17:48]

## What he makes and who gets to decide

The first coffee Mika serves Takina carries a different kind of authority. He has just told her and Chisato that they are partners and should get along. Chisato has rushed the introduction, done away with an honorific and given Takina a great deal to answer. Mika simply offers the cup. A frame at E01 07:07 places the man behind the bar with one hand open toward the cup and lets the newcomer turn toward him. It is recognizable service in a shop where everything else about the job remains unclear. When he invites Takina to join the game in E03, she asks whether doing so would get her back into DA. His sigh leaves the answer intact. He does not promise a return as a reward for sociability. Minutes later he reminds Chisato that the deadline for the medical examination and fitness assessment is real if she wants to keep working. Being free to choose a job is compatible, in his practice, with meeting its conditions. At the subsequent game it is Mika himself who shouts 取ったどお～！ A proprietor can recommend a pleasure he means to enjoy too. [E01 06:02–07:07, REL-CHI-MIK-E01-001; E03 03:30–04:50/22:24–22:37, REL-CAFE-E03-001]

What he passes on to each student differs. Chisato says Mika made the special rounds she wanted at the old radio tower. He had invested in a place where practice could continue without disturbing the neighborhood and says that good work requires regular training. Her eventual way of working, of which she says she made the choice, depended on someone taking the material implications of that choice seriously. Takina's introduction to another side of his advice is comic. When she needed to bring her own underwear, she asked Mika about his preference. He gave it. She took men's trunks as a plausible specification for the job, then found practical things to recommend about them. Mika explains that he told her to bring her own and answered the question she asked. Chisato sees how much social context was lost between the two instructions. A man trained to give orders can fail to notice that a technically personal answer carries a supervisor’s authority for the new employee. His individual preference and Takina’s later reasoning about ventilation and movement have different owners. [E04 00:42–00:47/04:28–04:54/07:30–07:39/12:10–12:22, CHI-MB-E04-001]

In the household dispute over rock-paper-scissors, Mika and Mizuki know enough about Chisato’s anticipation of another person’s movement to explain why Takina’s opening convention works so poorly. He contributes one part of the explanation, she another. The people who ordinarily manage dangerous work are available to explain an unfair distribution of chores. Elsewhere Mika's experience does not secure instant cooperation. When DA keeps information about the attacks secret, he makes the case for sharing it to prevent another victim and comes back with a refusal. When he learns Lycoris have been exposed as bait he voices disapproval; when Chisato is in immediate trouble he joins the rescue. He can have access to Kusunoki, recognize what a situation calls for and find that a critical part of it is beyond his authority. [E06 04:54–05:09/13:07–13:39/17:51–20:24, E06-CLM-009/012–014]

At the stove there are other reasons to be exact. At hanami he hears an order for five more servings of cherry-blossom tea and remarks how much tea they are shifting. Chisato tells him it is being mixed with alcohol. He tells her not to let Mizuki drink too much; she wants him to tell Mizuki himself. Later he tells the girls to hurry because the dango will harden. The cook knows how little time a good texture lasts even when his adult colleague is disinclined to take a limit on drinking from him. In E06 he answers Mizuki’s objection to buying too much watermelon by proposing juice, since it is fashionable. E08 finds him calling orders and asking Mizuki to cover for him. S03 gives Takina a precise correction to Chisato’s pride in an old menu: the manager was the person who realized those ideas. Mika is sufficiently versed in what is currently popular to justify a purchase with it and sufficiently traditional in his craft to care what happens between the pan and the table. Whether the overstock was a good business decision is another matter. [SHORT01 00:13–00:34/01:52–01:56, MIK-MB-SHORT01-001; E06 04:12–04:23; E08 06:46–07:50; SHORT03 02:00–02:15, CLM-003/004]

## How the shop looks from the other side of the counter

Fuki calls him 先生 too. When she brings Sakura to the café on official business in E07, Mika calls them unusual guests and takes Sakura’s order for a matcha dango set. He asks Fuki what she wants. She refuses because she is on duty. In E09 he makes another offer; she says she must leave and addresses him courteously before taking Sakura away. By Short 05, staying overnight at the café, she is pleased to answer that she will collect what he has prepared. “Milkshake for you, right?” She answers yes. He tells her not to stay up too late. She says yes again, in a register conspicuously different from the one she uses on Sakura or Chisato. Mika does not need to ask what sort of person Fuki is each time she comes in; he does need to leave room for her to go back to work. In Short 06 Fuki’s confident belief that he alone is sensible collapses at the sight of his vacation outfit. His pleasure cuts across the authoritative image his pupil would like to preserve. We have warmer evidence of how Fuki sees her teacher than of a corresponding preference on his part for one former student over another. [E07 10:50–11:23; E09 07:51–08:19; SHORT05 00:27–00:42, CLM-001; SHORT06 03:24–03:53, CLM-006]

Takina tends to call him 店長, the manager. The name describes where she encountered his authority. With her he can offer coffee, invite her into play, explain a skill and give permission to be a colleague. He does not become her father by becoming Chisato’s. Her E09 knowledge of the old bargain is an accident of the room. Kurumi makes Mika tea, asks for the history she cannot recover online and listens to his memories. When she asks when he ceased to think of the girl as an instrument, Mika lets the remembered conversation continue. After he remarks that he may have included extraneous details, Kurumi says the man's character is also a clue. His account of an intimacy, potentially embarrassing to tell, has become useful to someone with a different kind of competence. Then Takina asks whether capturing Majima would tell them something about Chisato’s heart. Mika is surprised to discover she listened. She adds that Mizuki heard too, to Mizuki’s displeasure. Only then does Kurumi explain that Majima’s conspicuous movements offer the quickest lead when Yoshimatsu himself cannot be traced. Chisato, whose life they have discussed, has yet to hear what her father has just told the others. [E05 09:18–09:43, MIK-MB-E05-001; E09 09:15–09:56/12:18–13:26/16:07–16:56]

Kurumi’s first encounter with the café team provides an early case where Mika has to answer for withholding what he knows. With dangerous pursuers after Walnut in E02, the adults help stage a false death; Chisato believes for a time that somebody she meant to protect has died. When the truth comes out Mika initially tells her to cheer up. Eventually he acknowledges the adults’ responsibility for a plan which deceived the girls. Then he offers something sweet. Chisato calls it bribery and accepts. Her willingness to eat with him can repair the present moment without retroactively endorsing the choice to exclude her. With Mizuki the arrangement runs the other way during the E10 farewell. She leaves Chisato in his charge and tells Chisato not to demand too much of the aging man. They are used to covering for one another and prepared to object, joke, drink or depart. Later, in E13, Kurumi’s discoveries and her call to a surgeon give Mika possibilities the solitary guardian could never have obtained. His authority works through people who can answer him back. [E02 17:50–20:29, CLM-008–012; E10 05:53–06:11; E11 05:35–06:44, CLM-005–008; E13 19:23–19:48]

There are days when the sensible grownup enjoys being the joke. In S04 playback from the café cameras reveals a shirtless, flexing Mika looking into the lens. He calls out ナイス バルクッ！ and チョモランマ～. In the later staged fright he has to identify the pranksters to an alarmed Takina and worries aloud that he may have hurt his back. He can still tease her over the sound of the scream. His explanation of where Kurumi has been does not solve the closing puzzle of whom Takina saw. In Short 06 Fuki falters at the sight of Mika’s vacation outfit. Mizuki and Chisato laugh. Mizuki teases him about getting carried away, Chisato tells her teacher that is just how he should be, and Mika answers with a little laugh of his own. A man can enjoy being watched by the girls who work for him and still be intensely private about a meeting with someone he loves. The admitted shorts document the preparations and the reactions. They give scant grounds for assigning the whole group a common taste in clothes or for hearing the vacation laugh as proof that grief has ended. [SHORT04 01:24–01:35/02:44–03:18/03:30–03:49, CLM-004–006; SHORT06 03:24–03:54, CLM-006]


## Two men who have trouble saying goodbye

When Yoshimatsu turns up at the shop, there is an older, less guarded man available in Mika’s presence. Shinji likes his ohagi. He can remember a time when Mika could hardly make coffee. Mika observes that ten years make a difference. Chisato teases them about having met in Russia after the customer brings back a souvenir from a visit there. Mika directs her to get ready; Yoshimatsu laughs and postpones an answer. The trip is a fact and the meeting place is a question. When they are alone in E04 Mika asks why the other man is back. To see Mika, says Shinji. Don't tease, Mika answers, it’s Chisato. He is quite willing to dispute Alan’s prohibition on contact by pointing to the frequency of those visits. He is much less willing to say that he wants the visits to stop. When Shinji asks, “Should I disappear?”, Mika begins to take back the implication. Before he can finish, his old friend asks whether he has kept their promise. The skill with which Yoshimatsu changes the subject works because Mika wants to keep more of that relationship than its contractual terms. [E02 03:23–04:10/20:55–21:15; E04 15:06–16:04, REL-MIK-YOS-E04-001]

E07 lets the younger people try to interpret his evening. Mika takes care over his appearance; an original frame at 14:25 shows him alone at the bar in a light jacket, a very different silhouette from the proprietor behind his counter. Takina had wondered whether his appointment with Kusunoki was an affair. The group gets the identity of his visitor wrong. When she sees Yoshimatsu arrive, Chisato reads a date, says they should not intrude and tells Takina that love takes different forms. The warmth, the men’s prior intimacy, the shared language about a daughter and Mika’s inability to treat Shinji as expendable have substantial support beyond an onlooker’s guess. No one supplies a history of their private household or explains the relationship under a definitive legal or sexual category. Mika gets to have an adult attachment that matters to him apart from being a clue in a girl’s life. His loss has more weight if we let him have wanted to see the man. [E07 06:15/14:20–14:55, CLM-013–021; E09 12:32–13:26; E13 07:04–08:06]

Mika has already told Kurumi, in Chisato’s absence, that Chisato decides what her own mission is. Saying it to a curious third party costs relatively little. Saying it to the person who once made him promise the opposite costs much more. After Yoshimatsu has told Chisato that Alan's children have roles, and left her, Mika asks him to give Chisato the time she wants. It is *Mika* who now hears that talent is God’s property. He demands the girl's freedom, threatens his intimate and claims that he is prepared to shoot him. Then he is alone and admits 覚悟なんかあるわけないだろ, that of course he had no such resolve. The claim of readiness and its revocation are both his. Between them the anime recalls Yoshimatsu's first visit, his exhilaration, a successful operation and the promise. Mika wants two incompatible things. He wants the man who made fatherhood possible to desist. He wants that man to remain in the world with him. His inability to sever the attachment in E07 is a substantial part of who has to act in E13. [E05 09:20–09:43, MIK-MB-E05-001; E07 17:19–18:47, MIK-MB-E07-001, REL-MIK-YOS-E07-002]

The adult who cannot yet take that action can inflict a smaller punishment on himself. Rain is heard in E09. Kurumi catches him smoking; the frame at 09:19 holds a cup beside his hand and an ashtray. He tells her the desire to smoke comes when he feels guilt and that it is convenient for hurting himself. She tells him to quit if he is going to smoke as though it tastes that bad. She has gone to the trouble of making tea, something she says she does not usually do. This is one of the strongest pieces of evidence about Mika's inward life because *he* supplies the explanation and somebody who cares about him declines to admire it. It would be a mistake to distribute the statement across every occasion when he is quiet, or to diagnose a condition from a cigarette. Here guilt is consuming time that could also be used for investigation. Kurumi insists he should have consulted her sooner. Mika begins to tell her what the network could not. [E09 09:07–10:20/13:14–13:26, MIK-MB-E09-002]

## A daughter's opinion of her father

After the shop has emptied in E10, Chisato asks whether he could take on part-time staff and keep the place. Mika asks if that is what she wants, tells her not to strain, and offers to make coffee when she admits that she feels lonely. There is no grand paternal insight in knowing exactly what she wants; he asks in order to find out. The same restraint stumbles minutes later over the camera. He has already given her the ceremonial clothes, chosen the moment and taken the picture. She has to insist that his own response belongs to the occasion too. Chisato thanks him. When Mika disclaims having done anything worthy of thanks, she credits him with her name, her training, their shop, the chance to meet Takina and his search for the man who could save her. Only then does she ask him to send Yoshi-san the photograph. Mika interrupts her next words loudly enough for her to remark on his raised voice. He has heard what the picture would mean to her and can no longer bear to let her gratitude stand in place of the history. [E10 08:03–08:57/13:15–14:37, CLM-016–024]

His account is more exacting than “I wanted to protect you.” He had wanted a benefit as a commander when he sought the original operation. He could see Yoshimatsu becoming an ideal that helped Chisato bear an uncertain life. He found that sight dazzling and fragile. Should he have told her her way of living was wrong, and suggested that Shinji would save her again if she killed? He asks **Chisato** to tell him. There is a real recognition that her understanding matters. There is also something unfair in presenting his choices as if the only alternatives had been years of silence or telling a child that the killer's vocation was right. She could conceivably have heard the facts without his endorsing the condition. The scene allows Chisato to invent a counterfactual of her own: perhaps, if she had known, she would have gone along miserably and blamed the two men. She thanks Mika for letting her decide and calls both men her fathers. Her answer belongs to her. She can tell him what his conduct meant to her without certifying what every child placed under the same constraint would have wanted. [E10 15:45–18:09, CHI-MB-E10-002/003, MIK-MB-E10-001]

He apologizes repeatedly and cries. Chisato has already made clear that she likes the life she chose. She still wants to hear whether he likes *this Chisato*. His 自慢の娘だ calls her the daughter of whom he is proud. There is an old professional standard against which that is an extraordinary answer. She has declined to become the person he promised to produce. His new standard begins with what she made of the time. The camera can turn the child into a lasting picture for the man who may lose her, yet she wants to know how it feels to him, a living person, to look at her now. Much later somebody else will take a picture of Mika and give him a halo. It is Chisato’s question here, in the room with him, that gives us the more difficult portrait. [E10 13:46–14:05/17:30–18:09, CLM-028–030; compare the separate portraits in キ村由宇, *Repeat 02* S27–34]

At the old tower he asks whether she really intends to go alone. Chisato says he should go back and have tea. His answer includes a greeting for Shinji. After a report is heard, she says いってきます, the everyday formula for setting out from a place to which one belongs. Mika returns いってこい. The words allow her to go and continue to imagine her coming home. He could take over, forbid her or treat her as a subordinate. At this threshold he chooses the ordinary relation created by their decade together. It is an instance of what he can do when the decision belongs to her and he is present to see her make it. It will not be his only way of protecting her. [E11 15:31–15:48, MIK-MB-E11-001, REL-CHI-MIK-E11-001]

## What Mika decides in someone else's absence

Mika never becomes an outsider to DA. Mizuki says in E12 that Mika and Kusunoki negotiated with upper command after earlier attacks on Chisato. Chisato adds the cost of the arrangement: she had to help DA. Later, when the young agents themselves are in danger, the café group answers the call for help. Mika tells Kusunoki he kept her identity as the requester from them. Itadori gives the order for the later withdrawal, a field captain relays it, and Kusunoki resumes command. None of these distinct acts belongs to Mika alone. What he knows how to do is make use of confidential institutional relationships at a moment when secrecy is putting the girls at risk. He neither abolishes the institution nor gives the people acting for him a complete account of who asked them. His ethical proximity to a commander and his continuing ability to keep a confidence can enlarge young people's options while setting the terms on which they understand them. [E12 11:20–12:12/13:53–14:02, REL-MIK-KUS-E12-001; attributed TV source recheck, E12-CLM-030/041]

By E13 the possibility of saving Chisato brings him face to face with Shinji again. Mika overcomes Himegama, who had attacked Chisato; Yoshimatsu asks about his leg. Mika says a warrior does not show everything, especially to someone he loves. Calling this disclosure evidence of concealed capability is more precise than supplying an undocumented medical history. Yoshimatsu retaliates with a charge of habitual lying. Mika answers that it was all for Chisato and appeals to Shinji to recognize their shared cause. Shinji holds up the returned charm. He says he was not understood and seems no longer needed. We hear two adults who have lost the position they once thought they occupied in a child's life. Only one of them can say that the loss of that authority is part of what a parent owes. [E13 06:22–07:33, MIK-MB-E13-001, REL-MIK-YOS-E13-001; TV Q13]

Mika says children lead their elders into a world the adults do not know and that adults must not obstruct their choices. He then asks whether the heart is there. Shinji tells him that Chisato did not believe him. Mika replies that Shinji does not lie. Once, when a frightened child correctly identified the man who had come to save her, Shinji pretended to be a member of staff and she called him a liar. Mika's late assurance speaks to a bond under strain; it cannot prove what was in another man’s body. As Yoshimatsu observes, the assurance of trust comes horribly late. When he tells Mika that Chisato has driven him off course too, Mika accepts the judgment. This is his last firsthand exchange with the man whose presence he could not relinquish in E07. The talk of children leading adults is earned in an admission that Mika himself has been led somewhere he did not intend to go. [E09 13:44–13:58; E13 07:29–08:06, CLM-031–033]

What happens after the exchange is deliberately harder to assign. Two metallic-sounding events precede the cut to the exterior at 08:09.197. Counting the faint edges, a small light appears across eight source-image presentations from 08:09.572 to .864. The owner hears no distinct report in the [exterior window](../07%20Evidence%20and%20Indexes/TV%20Audiovisual%20Reconciliation/OWNER_LISTENING_RETURN_20261123.md#sound-performance-and-action). The mix retains faint signal. In separately attributed production testimony, Chika Anzai describes a soundless shooting. Later material ties Mika’s intervention to Shinji’s death and the delivery of the heart. These are consequential facts about his choice and about Chisato's survival. No image of the precise lethal act, its agent at the instant or the mechanics of removal is supplied by the flash, two sounds or the later case. In particular, the Q17 event near E12 08:10 is a *different* exterior, a separate perceptible report, Chisato’s intervention under immediate threat to Takina and a wound which she subsequently treats. The two scenes give Mika and Chisato different opportunities to choose and the audience different degrees of access. [E12 08:07–08:21, TV Q17; E13 08:05–08:12, TV Q13, REV-E13-006]

On the island Takina tells Chisato that Mika brought Shinji’s case and that the present came with the heart, according to what she heard. Chisato handles the outer birthday greeting and the corded charm. The edit then takes the viewer to a separate, decorated farewell insert in Mika's gloved hands at the tower. In Yoshimatsu's voice the audience hears the claim that his death has completed her new beginning and the wish that she be happy. The recipient of that text in the pictures is Mika. The girl whose future it purports to describe receives the other paper. Kurumi notices he still has the cane, he asks for discretion, and she says she has engaged a surgeon. The cut does not show the exact act of separating the papers, give us everything Kurumi saw or make Chisato the recipient of a recorded farewell. It gives the father more knowledge about the cost of his daughter's future than it gives her. He can choose to carry the burden, and still have made a choice *for her* about the truth she could bear. [E13 18:48–19:21/19:23–19:58, CLM-034–036/048, TV source recheck E13; retained images 001049/001051, 001087/001089/001093]

Director Adachi Shingo later describes the decision to put a burden on Mika and allows that Chisato might suspect the truth. The account helps explain a choice in making the work. It does not tell us that Chisato knows the wording of the letter, and the uncertainty about the exact act survives the director’s account. There is a genuine cost to Mika in surviving a man he loves with knowledge his daughter does not possess. There is also a genuine cost to Chisato in having no opportunity to say how she wanted that knowledge handled. The café can keep a person alive by controlling information much as the institutions it criticizes do. The fact that Mika can explain why he did it prevents the comparison from making him interchangeable with those institutions. It gives the reader somebody particular to hold responsible. [E13-CLM-034–037/048; *Heroine Archive*, Adachi interview S127/p125]

## Company Mika seeks for himself

His customers are good judges of one of his talents and occasional poor judges of his biography. In Asaura's *Ordinary days*, the magazine reporter Tokuda is genuinely welcome. Mika remembers his drink, turns down the proposal to feature the café, and offers him a fresh monaka. Asked about his background, he can talk about making sweets and coffee while steering around former employment. Declining to put the café in a magazine does not require sending the hopeful writer away hungry. Another regular, Doi, objects that telling someone their time is short can be dispiriting. Mika concedes that it could make some people give up and asks what *this particular man* would do now. He credits Chisato with living this way. The customer has already changed his habits, begun to walk and looked forward to another meeting. Mika makes an appeal to a life in progress and lets Doi answer from where he stands. The advice later has a consequence Mika did not propose. Chisato has encouraged the fifty-five-year-old Doi to take Takina’s attentiveness for romantic interest. As Doi considers pursuing her, he recognizes how convenient that reading is for him. He remembers Mika’s words, applies for a gym membership and heads straight to a men’s salon. Three weeks later he asks Takina to have dinner alone with him after work. The chapter ends before her reply. Mika gave Doi a reason to act while he had time. What he would act upon came from Chisato’s reading of Takina and Doi’s own wishes. [*Ordinary days* 9.97–178, 10.211–213/335–355/369–419/469–497/614–690, 13.5–21]

*Recovery days* gives Mika a reason for joining a cinema outing that a portrait confined to his paternal function would never have predicted. He admits he would be lonely as the one left out. The same novel begins with coffee already in front of Takina before she can decline a break. She values the care and privately thinks she would have gone on working if he had asked first. There are two people's wants here. His intervention can be welcome and still preempt her answer. At the cinema's end Mika wants to visit the spa. Takina proposes using the group's game chances together before they separate, and the later account confirms he goes to the spa. In another story he brings gifts collected for the sick Chisato, and gifts later arrive for Takina, surprising her. Those offerings belong to a community with more than one needy member; a gift delivered by Mika need not have been chosen by him. The owner can spare his staff the burden of asking for a break, miss their company and wish for an hour of his own. [*Recovery days* 9.8–31/363–387, 12.393–425, 14.344–359, 16.2–40, 18.2–25]

Asaura gives Fuki direct inward access in a different story in the same book. She finds a pretext to see Mika. On the rooftop she sees the three figures of Chisato, Takina and her teacher together and thinks there is no place for her. She actually receives offers of transport and turns them down; Sakura would have liked the ride. Counting available seats would miss what Fuki is saying about herself. Treating her feeling as proof that Mika deliberately excluded her would put her thought into the wrong mind. Abe Kanari takes a different route in *Recollect* V02. Mika thanks a caller, says Kusunoki has already given him information and invites her to have sweets. The page delays identifying Fuki. Later Erika sees Fuki’s flushed face and wonders if she has a cold. Neither the whole content of the call nor Fuki’s private explanation is printed. Both works allow Mika to be particularly important to a former pupil without assigning him the feeling that the reader finds in that pupil or a motive Erika merely suspects. [*Recovery days* 13.709–748/781–829; *Recollect* V02 S38–48; compare SHORT05-CLM-001]

Mika can read adults he knows very well and can misread them, too. In *Gluttony days* Kurumi waits to report something while Mika sees Detective Abe out. Abe catches Mika’s cue, finishes his coffee and leaves without prying. Mika sends him off with a box of coffee grounds and homemade yōkan. Later a payment trail raises suspicions about Hikaru Akutsu. When Hikaru reaches toward Kurumi, she freezes and Mika intervenes. Hikaru says he wanted to see the screen. Mika had doubted his intentions too and later reproaches himself for the fright Kurumi suffered. After Akutsu’s daughter Clarisse leaves, Mika tells Takina that her father booked the park just after the emergency was resolved. Akutsu claims he planned the outing long beforehand to make up for time he had missed with her. The reader first sees the picture on Chisato’s phone, with Clarisse smiling as she embraces her father, then learns what Mika had told Takina. Clarisse went smiling and let the people around her believe she accepted her father’s account. Whether she privately did remains open. Toward the end of the book Kurumi tells Mika that the person who can get used to indulging children may be the parent. Chisato separately wonders why parents want to give so abundantly. Kurumi’s warning and Chisato’s private thought give us two different responses to the man who offers each person something they like. Mika himself has said only that an indulgent day is fine once in a while. [*Gluttony days* 9.895–909, 10.575–625/878–975, 14.675–700, 17.21–54]

The manga can show an adult being seen and leave him fewer words of his own. In *Recollect* V01 Mika offers a season in which the staff may wear yukata. Kurumi requests the catalogue after having resisted the proposal; the request belongs to her, and wearing the garment is a further choice. At S66 Mizuki comments on the ease between Chisato and Takina. Mika agrees and asks her to leave off teasing them. In キ村由宇’s *Repeat 02* story a halo accompanies Fuki’s portrait of her teacher. Sakura draws for likeness and Kurumi expresses simple thanks. Chisato privately prizes Kurumi’s picture; no group winner is chosen. We learn about different people looking at Mika and the satisfaction of giving an adult something. In 高野いつき’s *Repeat*, he makes a contrary choice of his own. He objects on ethical grounds to a promotional proposal from Kurumi, while later taking up a prompt to make the squirrel-and-acorn latte art the customers actually see. In ぎんもく’s contribution he submits reluctantly to a change of hair and Yoshimatsu arrives for his ohagi. These are various authors’ ways of making him a participant in contemporary fashions, sometimes constrained by them and sometimes able to change the terms. They cannot establish a frequency of assent to whatever brings in customers. [*Recollect* V01 S64–66/69–78; *Repeat 02*, キ村由宇 S27–34; *Repeat*, 高野いつき S47–54 and ぎんもく S9–20]

In 竹本蒼太郎’s *Reload*, the child’s drawing represents a benefactor generically. It is the adult Mika who can imagine a family with Shinji and the girl. The closing spent casing and a solitary figure against the skyline preserve the gulf between the family he might picture and the act the audience was never shown. 幌田, in *Reload 03*, stages a much more modest refusal. Presented with the opportunity to confess something small, Mika declines to name a suitably small sin even under an offer of confidentiality. The reader who knows the television ending has ample reason to feel the weight of that answer. The contributor has declined to supply a particular confession. One artist shows what a man might long to have seen; another gives him a right to leave a sentence unsaid. Neither signed story returns an absent recollection to the anime. [*Reload*, 竹本蒼太郎 S135–142; *Reload 03*, 幌田 S39–48]

## Hearing the teacher

Mika speaks to Shinji by given name, carries out negotiations with Kusunoki in short, familiar phrases, and can direct a room of girls without a speech about authority. His first-person 私 in describing an agreement and his お前 when addressing someone known to him do different work from the familiar title his pupils return. In a light interval he can answer a new worker's uncertainty with the politely spare どうぞ, make another sweet available with a teasing いらないか, or acknowledge one of the girls’ shrewder observations with a laugh. In a harder one he can repeat すまない after Chisato has tried to release him from the need to apologize. The loud interruption in E10 matters precisely because she notices the change. In E13 he can use practically the same familiar manner to ask Kurumi to keep quiet. In S04, by contrast, he knowingly poses for an audience. The credited actor, Sakaki Kōsuke (さかき孝輔), recalls being directed toward grounded playing and discusses how to greet Takina. The recollection gives a maker’s perspective on the welcome. The short’s bodily flourish, Fuki’s eagerness, Chisato’s surprised answer and the quietness of the café are distinct pieces of performed evidence, none a diagnosis of his private emotions by sound level. [E01 07:07; E02 20:15–20:26; E07 20:10–20:20; E10 14:26–14:37/17:52–18:09; E13 19:33–19:41; SHORT04 01:24–01:35; *Heroine Archive* S116–118]

If a pupil is choosing a harmless pleasure, Mika is strongly disposed to provide the material occasion and let them discover what to do with it; the E03 invitation that Takina declines and the S05 drink Fuki accepts represent genuinely different outcomes. If a trusted younger person can act and her values are clear to him, he can equip her, contest an order on her behalf and give her a home to leave from. If he believes knowledge or an unavoidable personal cost will burden her, silence, an arrangement made elsewhere or action she can no longer refuse becomes much more plausible. Yoshimatsu can make him hesitate where another opponent cannot. A manager minding the accounts, a father facing a daughter, a lover addressing a man who once disappeared and a smoker allowing a young hacker to make him tea are all the same person. There is no secure single response to an invented situation in which Chisato learns the whole truth and asks him what he did. That moment would give him the question he has so far prevented her from asking. [E03 03:30–03:50, E05-CLM-014, MIK-MB-E07-001/E09-002/E11-001/E13-001, TV Q13 and E13 source recheck]

When the café reopens, Fuki asks after Takina and Mika says she is working. The work is connected to finding Chisato. In Hawaii he calls for a café au lait as Takina works beside him and Chisato takes the telephone. There is no speech certifying that he has recovered from the loss of Shinji. There is a working man who still knows what to hand to another person across the counter, in a place that none of the three adults in the original bargain could have prescribed for the child. Chisato once asked him for the opinion no photograph could give. His answer reversed the judgment he once made as a commander. He could be proud of a daughter whose future no longer belonged to its planners. Yet the knowledge he carries on her behalf also keeps some of the meaning of that future beyond her reach. [E13 14:17–15:40/18:48–19:58/22:25–22:42]
