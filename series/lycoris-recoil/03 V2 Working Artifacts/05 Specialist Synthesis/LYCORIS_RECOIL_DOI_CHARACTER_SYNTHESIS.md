# Doi. A regular's new appetite

The first thing Yoshiharu Doi wants from Café LycoReco is a quick way out. He has worked in the Kinshichō area for years and did not know this place was here. Once inside he thinks he has made the kind of mistake a formerly discerning man makes when his senses are going. A child is laying out games, somebody in school clothes is sprawled beside her, and Mizuki’s loosely worn uniform offends his sense of a proper shop. He expects a business for people who take pictures of their food. If he orders only a blend, he can look as if that was all he came for. Asaura lets him make a complicated calculation about how to leave a room without requiring any other character to know what he is thinking. The seat at one end of the counter puts a little distance between him and the first employee he saw. Takina actually brings the menu and takes the order. Her clear voice impresses him. He thinks she is too young for him, then imagines that it might have been otherwise had they been born closer together. Both judgments precede anything a coworker later tells him. [A2 *Ordinary days*, 10.3–32.]

The people who come in give him better reasons to stay. A man he would not have expected to sit down for games knows Kurumi and takes a place at her table. The other faces are of several ages. The younger man eating a wafer sweet says he shared Doi’s first impression of a stylish hideaway and discovered a warm, mixed crowd. Before long there is even someone else alone with a coffee and racing newspaper. The café actually makes room for more kinds of person than Doi supposed. That possibility gives him another reason to look at the face in his cup. People once said he resembled Akutagawa. Now he feels older than his fifty-five years, remembers having retired early intending to enjoy freedom, and describes the days he is spending on walkabouts, movies that barely interest him, and drinks to get the evening over with. He cannot even tell whether he was as absorbed in things when he was young as the people around him are now. His account of a closing life starts in a case he can treat as settled only because nobody else is supplying evidence about it. [10.32–57, 10.648–652.]

## The man whose order can be anticipated

Over subsequent visits the corner becomes something like his regular seat, and Mika starts preparing the blend before he sits down. Doi smiles at Mika and appreciates the attention. Takina still asks him what he wants. Chisato watches the routine, notices where his smiles go, and guesses that Mika stands in the way of a romance between Takina and the customer. Doi knows her coworker gives him time and forms a preference around the pleasure of being recognized. He has no reason at this stage to understand every extra question at the counter as part of a plan. When Chisato tries asking him to lunch, Takina suspects she means to have him pay. Doi stops Mika from intervening and offers to cover lunch. He says he likes sushi and offers to pay for delivery before he knows that going out is Chisato's actual objective. His money initially makes him useful without requiring a private meaning for the gift. Chisato leads the pair on a separate inari-sushi outing. They eat while walking near the shrine and she brings some back to the café. There is no need to give that meal the same route or consequence as the exercise visit that follows. [10.211–267, especially 10.213–225 and 10.239–260; 10.268–330.]

The ten-kilometer proposal is substantially less considerate. Doi mentions discomfort and Chisato decides that he needs exercise. She sets up the second outing before he has a chance to answer. She gets him walking ten kilometers along the river in leather shoes. He gets painful blisters and spends three days in bed, first with exhaustion and then soreness. His response becomes an individual action after the bad fit of that walk. He starts leaving home with sneakers, a towel and clothes to move in. He still needs something formal because on another day it was a film and a restaurant at the top of a hotel. He works out that neither outfit handles every outing, and observes how the girls' uniforms fit settings that make him self-conscious. He thinks their look keeps other people from suspecting them. He still calls it school uniform in his thoughts. The reader can register the insight and the gap in what he knows at once. Chisato has made activity possible and repeatedly imposed her way of getting there. Doi's eagerness to see what they will think up next gives both the imposition and the invitation consequences. [10.369–401.]

The change comes into view on a day the girls are absent. Mika says they are away on work. Doi guesses it might be a delivery or schoolwork, then realizes that he is a little relieved and a little disappointed. Some regulars are carrying plates. He knows their faces now and helps when he sees a chance. He can take a place among them without being the customer the girls must keep amused. He tells Mika that building new ties has come to feel unusual. He thinks he had mostly lost acquaintances since his thirties while his world was getting smaller. The quiet day gives a measure of change that does not make his life depend on Takina or on any one invitation. Their work stays mysterious to him. The person beside him serving somebody else does not. [10.401–419.]

## What Mika actually offered

Mika has a way to ask an older man to try something without giving him the usual formula about still being young. He serves an ohagi set and a lighter cup made with Mandheling. Doi can taste a difference because his usual order has given him something against which to compare it. The first portion alters the coffee. The salted cherry-blossom and matcha variants give him different sensations. He finds that either direction across the plate takes him from familiar bean paste through two surprises and back to a familiar ending. That ordering is Doi’s account as someone tasting it. His pleasure has room for a personal insight instead of making him thank a host for an improvement he cannot name. The man who was reluctant to order a snack discovers a reason to taste several and compare them. [10.420–467.]

Mika asks him to think of his life as short and getting shorter. Doi immediately objects that these words could make somebody miserable. Mika agrees that some people would give up. He is asking how *this* man, after what has already changed for him, would react to the idea. Doi entertains the possibility that he himself would have heard it differently a little earlier. Now he has a list of things he wants to do, starting with trying the shop’s full range of sweets and coffee. Mika credits the attitude to how Chisato already lives and says she has her reasons. Doi can admire the concentration of a young person’s future without being told the medical history that informs it. He admires the café manager's smile and muses that it would win him over if he were a woman. Asaura gives him a self-conscious thought about being charmed by another adult; it is hardly a dossier on whom Doi has desired in a life largely outside the book. He is less likely to drift past things he wants to investigate now. His own objection about the unequal effect of the advice stays available even after he finds a use for it. [10.469–497.]

By the time Doi is drinking the coffee with ohagi he already knows how useful one of his ordinary ties can be. In a separate account of a lunch Chisato proposes, Doi contributes the sushi shop he knows. He eats there with Chisato and Takina and can join the joke when a flavor identification goes wrong. The proprietor calls some unusual tempura a service for a regular bringing young visitors, then remarks how young they are. Doi says that the outing is not that sort of affair. Chisato still reads it as a romantic arrangement. The pair of women later thank him and leave while he mentions wanting a drink. Once Takina has learned what the shared meal can provide, she independently contacts that same chef to make a similar staff meal. The chef supplies his skill and says there is no charge. He told the acquaintance who caught the fish that he wanted the young people to eat it. The acquaintance let him take it. Doi’s introduction has created a genuine possibility without making him the person who arranged the new meal or paid for it. Knowing a good place and another adult willing to help becomes his contribution to a workplace from which he had initially hoped to disappear. [10.389–390; 14.69–101, 14.101–168 and 14.190–234.]

A later scene in the book gives him a more deliberately playful way to contribute. He joins the café group when Itō photographs and films them posing for her manga. Tokuda believes he looks unusually composed and wonders if he has experience of danger. He reports that Doi apparently used to run shops in Kinshichō. Neither the pose nor the conjecture establishes how he got that composure. When Itō invites drawing revisions, Doi asks her to make him more dashing. It is a small reversal for the man who expected the people with cameras to be the reason he did not belong here. [15.10–11, 15.24–39.]

## A face that cannot do all that work

When Takina notices Doi has finished the ohagi, Mika identifies the preparation as hers. Doi imagines her slender hands responsible for the small finished pieces. In the other account Takina remembers leaving before the final shaping and expects that Mika will finish shaping them. Her own work has indeed gone into the plate. The lovely specificity Doi grants it is partly his invention. He praises the food and says he intends to try more of the sweets. Takina smiles and thanks him. She is pleased with the prospect of a returning customer ordering sweets, and soon tells her coworkers in unmistakable terms why it matters to her. Doi has been taking a seat for hours at busy times with one drink and in a mood she considers bad for the shop. Kurumi proposes that Yoneoka also occupies the counter for long periods. Takina points out that he buys a great deal. Takina says she disliked Doi and expects to dislike him less now that he is more open to ordering. She has genuinely done her work and received a welcome result. Doi has received genuine attention without thereby learning something about a young woman's attraction. [10.525–540, 10.614–634 and 12.254–255.]

Chisato is faster to act than the people who might put the two meanings together. Kurumi earlier proposes asking Takina what she actually thinks. Chisato thinks a smile answers the question. Later she catches Doi outside, says Takina loves him, and guarantees it. He is surprised and asks if it is true. The routine requests for orders and encouragement during the outings now look to him like hints he has failed to notice. These are the same incidents whose different purpose Takina soon makes explicit to Mizuki and Kurumi. Doi does not hear their conversation. The novel permits him to take what he received as a sincerely expressed assurance from someone who knows Takina. It also gives him his own time to consider a claim about a person who has never made it to him. He guesses she is in her mid to late teens and expressly does not know her exact age. His own age is fifty-five. He believes he has substantial assets and has no need to ask a teenage employee making his food to confirm a coworker’s theory in the course of her shift. [10.335–355, 10.560–599, 10.614–646.]

Doi has read romances in historical fiction in which older men had relationships with much younger women and recruits them as precedent. His lines about what was normal in earlier Japan are part of this man’s argument with himself. He actually asks whether the interpretation conveniently serves his own interests. He then imagines offering expensive experiences, educating a younger person in his world, and leaving her with memories, all inside a version of the affair in which he is the one sure what would benefit both. He invokes the very words Mika used to encourage discovery. The proposal to notice what one wants has acquired a new object, another person whose wishes Doi has mostly heard about from Chisato. Mika’s confidence in his capacity to move is borne out in an ordinary way by the gym and the attention he gives to his appearance. His new confidence can change his own habits. Takina still has her own answer to give. Three weeks pass. Doi returns in carefully chosen adult clothes after his changes. Chisato winks. He orders three-color dango and an iced coffee to go with it, then adds an invitation to eat alone together after work. A real departure from his old order and an assumption about his server come in one breath. [10.648–690.]

The chapter stops at the invitation. Much later in the reading order, Tokuda enters to a curious gathering. Doi is laughing in a way the new observer finds disturbing, Chisato is prostrate and Takina looks irritated. Doi goes quiet and looks down once Kurumi complains. Takina lets Tokuda order coffee. The book invites us to read this as the immediate fallout without giving the exact answer. Tokuda says he does not know what happened. Doi is seen at his usual seat in this gathering. Subsequent visits, any apology, what he understood, and the later shape of his relationship with the staff remain open. The scene of discomfort belongs in the portrait without assigning anybody a private resolution or treating the café counter as proof of restored trust. [10.688–690 and 19.7–27.]

## A different invitation

If the café suggested a weekend group visit to a market, Doi might ask what they are doing before choosing his shoes or recommend a stall he knows. Specific participation, preparation and putting a familiar place at another person's disposal all have precedents. If the younger organizer has expressed nothing about wanting to see him alone, he has no reason from her own voice to turn a group outing into dinner. Whether, after the last scene, he would ask the right question, carefully decline to press her, or still let Chisato tell him what he wants to hear remains open. Both accepting help and being susceptible to a self-serving explanation are within the man Asaura gave us. It would take a later deliberate choice in which he can hear the other person's answer to know how much the embarrassment taught him. The fellow regular handing someone a plate and the man making a young employee answer for a wish ascribed to her have both made decisions he could not have pictured when he first ordered his blend.
