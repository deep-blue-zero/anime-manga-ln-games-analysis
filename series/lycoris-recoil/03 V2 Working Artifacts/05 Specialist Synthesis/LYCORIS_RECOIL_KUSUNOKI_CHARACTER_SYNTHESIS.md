# Commander Kusunoki

When Mika tells Commander Kusunoki that the rescue party has gone, he adds that he kept her name out of it. She thanks him, gives him the time before LilyBell arrives, and her aide fends off a suspicious officer by saying the commander always takes a long time in the lavatory. A few minutes of screen time later, Kusunoki asks to be put through to Fuki. This time the girl at the other end hears exactly who is speaking. Both decisions matter. A woman who can put her own authority behind an order also knows when other people will have a better chance of living if she acts without taking credit. The girl transferred out of headquarters in the first episode has already experienced what it costs somebody else when Kusunoki keeps a name or a fact out of the account. [E01 08:42–09:13; E12 13:53.90–14:06.70, 20:05.48–20:11.06.]

Kusunoki (楠木) commands the Lycoris of Direct Attack, the girls whose work is supposed to let people go about their lives unaware of the danger. She has superiors. She has a long enough working acquaintance with Mika, who runs the café LycoReco, a small DA branch, to speak candidly and ask a favor that could compromise them both. She knows former residents of the Lycoris dormitory by name, can summon them, and can still fail to make one sit down. The series gives us her military and professional circumstances, the people who can contradict her and some of the things she will do for them. Its first sustained view of her is already at work. Her given name, childhood, recruitment, ascent to command and life at home remain outside this account. One consequential piece of her earlier history reaches us through people who lived with its effects. [E01 01:40–02:06; E03 08:37–09:53; E07 05:17.38–05:24.26; E09 07:17–07:34; E12 11:51–12:15.]

## Whose mistake can be named

The old radio tower bothers Kusunoki. An eyesore, she calls it, which is now apparently a guardian of peace. Her aide answers that getting the public to believe the myth is part of their duty. The difference in diction is small. Kusunoki can experience the building as an annoyance while admitting what others have come to see in it. Later she will defend a citizen's freedom from the felt presence of malice in words of her own. The early remark gives her a mundane judgment about a familiar landmark, with room between her taste and her profession. It supplies little basis for making her a skeptic about that profession. [E01 C0042–0045, 01:40.91–01:49; E10 C0193–0200, 10:23–10:40.]

In the warehouse crisis Mika tells her a Lycoris is in danger and asks whether to shoot. Kusunoki wants the dealers alive. She will wait for Chisato. Fuki, the field leader, thinks her own team can do the job and requests permission. Headquarters loses contact before she gets a new decision. Fuki still has the standby order. Takina disobeys it and gets Erika out alive. There are real costs to what Takina does, including the dead dealers, and a real person saved. There was a choice at headquarters, too. Kusunoki has decided to wait for one exceptional girl. Fuki voices a different assessment only afterward, and the contact failure prevents us from learning how the commander would have answered her. She had intelligible reasons, including the need for witnesses. Her subsequent account leaves Fuki and Takina with little means of raising her share in the outcome. [E01 01:50.63–03:30, especially C0047–0057 and Fuki at 02:44.27; E03 C0172–0193, 08:52–09:45.]

When the aide identifies the hacker as Walnut, Kusunoki infers an older person from the name's long history, orders that Walnut be found and killed, and asks after the missing guns. The order from above is to keep the breach secret. The aide asks how to report the failure. The next conversation already has an answer in circulation, the independent action of a Lycoris. It is an economical injustice. Takina really did act alone against orders, so no one needs to invent her action. What vanishes is the information anyone considering her punishment would need to give it proportion. The cut omits the authorship of the final report. Kusunoki enforces the distinction between what her side knows and what it will acknowledge to the girls. [E01 C0220–0230, 08:42.67–09:12.79; KUS-MB-E01-001.]

In private her assessment is more exacting. Her aide brings her the rumor of Walnut's death and reminds her that the hacker has repeatedly been reported dead over thirty years. Kusunoki would be glad to have both the hacker and the stolen information disappear, yet declines to rely on the rumor. Hearing that the café's picture places the transaction three hours earlier, she remarks that **we** have lost our edge, then asks whether anyone in the blurred picture can be identified. The café had already worked out the time discrepancy; her people confirm it. A commander capable of skepticism about good news and self-criticism about bad news meets Chisato's question about responsibility quite differently. She asks Chisato to do more work for her license, stills Fuki with a single use of her name as the broken communications come up, and calls the problem merely technical. *We* is available with the aide. It would have a different cost if spoken in front of the person whose career has suffered. [E01 21:38.94–21:50.04; E02 C0112–0124, 06:28.95–07:06.62; E03 C0172–0193.]

Takina submits the photograph and asks whether this achievement can get her back into DA. Kusunoki says she has no recollection of saying anything of the sort. In fact it was Chisato who had held out that possibility. The reply's sting lies in its accuracy. Kusunoki answers the question of who promised a return and offers no standard by which Takina might earn one. Later she presides over the exercise with Fuki and Sakura and tells both sides to treat it like the real thing. A girl can show what she is capable of here. There is no corresponding promise of reinstatement if she does. The commander controls the terms on which competence becomes consequential. She need not dispute the competence itself. [E01 22:00–22:07; E03 C0232–0239, 11:38.72–11:54.61, and C0363–0369, 17:35.12–17:49.72.]

## What Chisato can bring out in her

A poor likeness of Majima from people who have actually seen him draws a short question from Kusunoki and permission to go home. Other people in the room laugh and dispute the drawings. Later Fuki takes an image to the café. Chisato identifies the man and Takina confirms the identification. Fuki reports it. Kusunoki accepts it and tells her to come back for the day. The young witnesses have scarcely become more congenial between these encounters. The useful change is the task. Recognizing a man in a picture makes fewer demands on them than drawing him for somebody else. Kusunoki can accept a correction from the very people whose earlier effort she dismissed; Fuki and the café do the work that brings it within her reach. [E07 C0004–0017, 00:12.37–00:37.73; 10:40–11:44, especially Takina at 11:09.94 and C0240–0241.]

When the artificial heart can no longer recharge, Chisato learns that it may sustain her for only two more months. Kusunoki’s part in their subsequent meeting is hard to credit to any single motive. She opens with the observation that someone about to die looks well, and tells Chisato to return to DA. Chisato coughs theatrically. Two meetings with Majima become, in Kusunoki's reckoning, two occasions on which Chisato let him get away. Chisato says catching him was never her job. Yet Kusunoki has brought something the girl has long wanted back. It is her camera. The commander says it had been taken to prevent a leak; Chisato calls her a thief, and thanks her for it on the way out. Later Chisato looks at the image of her benefactor. A thing that had value to an information custodian as a risk has value to its owner as a way to see someone again. Kusunoki returns it before Chisato agrees to anything. We cannot tell whether she knows precisely what the image means to the girl. [E09 C0086–0113, 06:16.15–07:30.72, 10:22–10:33; E09-CLM-003/004/010–013; E09 continuous-observer unit reading, camera visible around 06:45 and 10:24.]

Many people, Kusunoki goes on, worked to make Chisato an exceptional Lycoris; she is going to die without properly fulfilling her role. The rebuke borrows the imminence of death to press a claim of debt. Chisato lets the contributions stand and disputes who gets to say what her role is. When Kusunoki tells her to sit, she leaves after asking for Takina's return. Even her offer to reconsider is conditional. Fuki subsequently delivers what she believes to be the reinstatement order and cites a need for personnel. Takina eventually takes it for reasons bound up with saving Chisato. The succession of scenes makes Chisato's intervention significant without supplying Kusunoki's private calculation, a completed bargain at the table or knowledge of Takina's change of purpose. Affection for Chisato, an attempt to draw her back and the demands of staffing a real operation could lead to some of the same acts. The return of the camera is concrete. Her appeal to a dying girl's supposed obligations is equally concrete. [E09 C0102–0113 and C0123–0129, 06:57–07:33 and 07:56–08:11; E09-CLM-010–014/028–029/035.]

Mika has seen an earlier version of this difficulty. As Mizuki later tells the café group, LilyBell used to visit to kill Chisato; Mika and Kusunoki negotiated with those above them. Kurumi appears to be the one who calls Kusunoki a good person: the original caption leaves the speaker unnamed, and the sampled picture at 12:10 shows Kurumi open-mouthed beside her laptop. Chisato adds that she had to help DA as part of the outcome. Kurumi’s judgment and Chisato’s experience have different grounds. Preserving a child's life was a substantial intervention. It also gave the institution a continuing claim on her work. Neither an unconditional rescue nor an ingenious plan to stockpile an operative does justice to the arrangement as its beneficiary describes it. By the time we learn about this history, we have already watched Kusunoki try to collect on it. It is one reason she and Mika have a channel available in E12; none of the participants gives its entire history. [E06 10:44–10:49; E09 C0088/0102–0108; E12 C0223–0232, 11:53.69–12:15.26; E12-CLM-030.]

## The argument she chooses and the order she receives

Majima gives Kusunoki an opportunity to speak beyond the needs of a briefing. She first asks whether his motive is money. He asks her by what right an organization that decides good and evil exists outside the law. Kusunoki gives him the long view of her work. DA predates the present law, she says, has survived changes of regime, and has helped cultivate security and morals. She refuses to explain everything to him. The outcome, she insists, has protected the country, and genuine peace would be a world in which people do not even feel the presence of malice. She is an articulate interpreter of her own conduct. Calling her a mere servant of someone else's doctrine misses what she chooses to defend in her own words. Her historical and political account supplies no evidence that the public has granted her that authority. She promises Majima that he too can disappear from memory; her account of what it feels like to live in peace has entailed deciding whose experience may be made to disappear. [E10 C0175–0206, 09:35–10:59; E10-CLM-031–033; KUS-MB-E10-001.]

She grasps the point of the distributed guns quickly. She orders the girls to avoid armed civilians and absolutely forbids firing. The prohibition can spare the person holding a gun and keep a Lycoris from identifying herself to the crowd. The scene allows both consequences; Kusunoki explicitly identifies exposure as Majima's object. Later the assault briefing authorizes killing everyone in the target group. Once a Lycoris has been recognized in the street, headquarters insists on the prohibition again. Attributing each line of an operations briefing to her private appetite for violence would obscure her distinct choice in responding to Majima's public trap. Equally, the prohibition under exposure cannot be transferred to a principled aversion to killing. [E10 C0374–0377, 19:36.36–19:44.12; E11 09:09.11–09:16.49, 13:55.40–14:17.63; E11-CLM-014/019.]

She tells her superior Itadori that Radiata can control the information. He tells her the fact of exposure is already a failure and asks why she has not used Chisato. When she politely requests discretion to plan the operation, he repeats the order. Minutes later she asks Mika politely to put Chisato on, then uses the imperative to demand the girl come to headquarters. Many lives are at stake, she adds. The viewer hears a second call threatening one particular life if Chisato obeys. Kusunoki only asks whether someone else is there. Nothing licenses making her a party to the threat or granting her the viewer's knowledge. The exchange gives the woman in command an experience familiar to the girls: to be judged on results by a superior who will also prescribe her choice. Her response is to carry the demand downward. [E10 C0389–0399, 20:22.28–20:51.94; C0407–0426, 21:08.29–21:48.87.]

Takina later presses her about the absence of Chisato and Mika. In a meeting Kusunoki says they cannot be reached, the reason is unknown and it is no concern of theirs. Later, Takina answers an incoming call ordering her back for the operation. When she reports finding Chisato’s abandoned phone, Kusunoki asks what connects the disappearance to Majima, who Yoshimatsu is, and whether he provided the heart. Takina confirms the last point. Kusunoki directs her to ask Majima himself before he dies; Takina agrees to come back. Kusunoki is capable of a pertinent follow-up when she has just given the appearance of complete indifference. It is still a direction back into the operation. The exchange supplies neither permission for an independent search nor proof she has already solved the predicament. [E11 C0008–0018, 00:25.55–00:46.36; C0061–0078, 04:13.15–04:53.82.]

## The girl she asks for by name

When Itadori announces the decision to dispose of the exposed Lycoris, the order runs over Kusunoki's head. Her aide encounters armed opposition inside headquarters. The incoming force is directed at the surviving girls at Enkuboku. Hearing Itadori say the bodies can be left where they fall, the viewer is given a single name, Fuki, in the command-room exchange. The stronger evidence of Kusunoki's response comes from what she arranges. Mika tells her he has sent the others and has concealed the fact that the request came from her. He asks when LilyBell will arrive. She has an estimate. Her aide buys her time. The adults who know what she has done can protect her identity as well as the girls. The rescuers can know what help is needed while remaining unaware who asked Mika for it. Mika has preserved exactly that distinction. [E12 C0040–0051, 03:01–03:36; C0233–0237, 12:22–12:36; C0269–0275, 13:53–14:06; KUS-MB-E12-001; REL-MIK-KUS-E12-001.]

Her motive remains a difficult question for a good reason. Saving experienced personnel preserves the institution she believes in. Recovering her position would serve her personally. Wanting Fuki and the others to live could motivate the same call. What those explanations have to account for is her choice to assist before she can expect credit and while the explicit upper order endangers those very girls. An obedient custodian could have remained where she was. A public dissenter would have had to survive a command room already occupied by the other side. Her actual resource is a relationship: Mika can be relied on to act and to withhold his source. Secrecy, whose cost fell on Takina in E01, here makes action against the wishes of Kusunoki's superiors possible. Her willingness to use secrecy in both directions is more consistent than any judgment that everything it serves is good. [E01 C0226–0230; E12 03:05–03:44, 12:22–12:36, 13:53–14:06.]

Later the public account of what happened has changed again, and Chisato has appeared. The LilyBell field lead is the strongly supported speaker reporting Chisato and asking command whether to continue. Itadori orders withdrawal. The field lead relays the cease-fire. Only then does Kusunoki ask for Fuki, say she will take command from here, and identify herself. The correction matters to the person as much as to the command chart. Giving Kusunoki the withdrawal would turn her into the superior with the power to rescind the danger. Giving her only the subsequent lawful command would erase the help she gave when she lacked that power. Both the identity of the person who made the covert request and the moment she can speak in her own name survive the more exact attribution. The scene leaves the relative weight of Chisato's presence and the restored cover in Itadori's decision undetermined. [E12 C0419–0426, 19:53.34–20:11.06; E12-C09; TV audiovisual reconciliation, SOURCE_RECHECK_20261123, E12 row and speaker audit.]

Nor does the rescue turn Kusunoki into someone willing to see every decision made by the girls themselves. When Fuki wants to go up the tower, Kusunoki orders withdrawal and tells her to leave no further trace of the Lycoris. What about Majima? Chisato is still there. Fuki tries to object; Kusunoki says she will not say it twice. Chisato really can do things other people cannot; leaving it to her places the risk on a person who has already disagreed explicitly with Kusunoki over what her abilities oblige her to do. Takina subsequently makes a distinct appeal to Fuki. She can reach Chisato and Fuki can save Sakura. Fuki entrusts Chisato to Takina. Their division of responsibility is the field leader's decision with her teammate. There is no indication that Kusunoki secretly sanctioned it. She has acted to preserve the lives of people who remain capable of choosing against the limits she sets them. [E13 C0051–0081, 02:28.67–03:51.09; E09 C0091–0096/0105–0112.]

## A voice at the other end of the line

Kusunoki's register changes with her addressee. With her aide she can voice doubt about their joint performance. With her superior she uses the courteous お任せください when attempting to keep control of planning. Mika gets an initial ください on the telephone and a brief どうも when he has kept her confidence. With Fuki she can say the girl's name and have an indiscretion stop, or use the full weight of 命令だ when Fuki wants to continue. Chisato gets many of her bluntest imperatives and is unusually free in what she does with them. This is partly professional hierarchy and partly their history of surviving one another's demands. It says nothing precise about a pitch, a breath or a particular performer's intention. [E02 C0121–0124; E03 C0188; E09 C0110–0113; E10 C0396/0409/0415; E12 C0270–0273; E13 C0061–0071.]

The shorts give that last relationship an ordinary inconvenience. In Short 02 Kusunoki begins to give the two nearest operatives a robbery call. Chisato is talking to Takina about a hotel and an ocean view. Kusunoki uses Chisato's name, asks her to listen to the end of the instructions, and reaches the much plainer 聞いてほしい, *I'd like you to listen*. Chisato at last checks what the job was, states that she will not kill, gets sidetracked again, and hands over the call when asked. Takina identifies herself and apologizes. In 少し浮かれていまして, being a bit carried away has no stated subject; when she starts to speak for herself she assures the commander that she is quite all right. Only after the call does she tell Chisato explicitly that she, too, is excited. Kusunoki hears the apology; Chisato hears the later confidence. The viewer can also see what is distracting them. The scene leaves Kusunoki’s response to the no-kill condition unstated. For once there is no institutional crisis to explain the effect. No degree of formal rank can guarantee the first practical requirement of an order: a person who listens all the way through. [Short 02 C0023–0047, 00:48.00–01:45.10, including C0029/0032/0036/0039–0047; C0058–0059, 02:05.62–02:11.67.]

The TV finale makes a smaller use of the same possibility. After the tower, she reports Robota's figure of 1,151 guns and the 211 DA recovered; her aide supplies the separate figure of 323 and Itadori speaks for LilyBell's action. The work continues with a material discrepancy still to account for. Later Kusunoki calls the café. Recognizing Chisato's voice, she remarks that she sounds well. It recalls the greeting to a girl who was expected to die. This time she says she is calling about work and asks for Mika. Chisato answers that they cannot take the job because they are in Hawaii. The good-health greeting can carry pleasure or relief, yet another unembarrassed request for work follows immediately. The scene can bear both. Chisato then echoes a passport question, asks her caller to spare her the awkward inquiry and jokes about LilyBell. Her side of the conversation suggests what Kusunoki has asked. Her actual words at the other end and her private reaction to the holiday remain unavailable. [E13 C0274–0283, 12:54.21–13:24; C0453–0463, 22:31.54–22:57.23; REL-CHI-KUS-E13-001.]

The series gives her scarcely any independent leisure, domestic routine or bodily self-description. It does give her a professional friendship or alliance with Mika that other people can misread: Takina asks whether the two are lovers while the café group tries to guess whom Mika is meeting. The meeting they actually discover is with Yoshimatsu. It proves nothing about attraction, partners, jealousy or Kusunoki's private life. Likewise the preference for a functional photograph over a poor drawing is evidence about what she will work with, and her reaction to the tower is a small aesthetic judgment. Neither will sustain an invented biography. The other shorts give the café and its visitors lives beyond work; their attitudes to a uniform or to rank cannot be assigned to the woman who issues orders. [E07 04:57.90–06:18.48, 14:28–15:59; Short 02 C0023–0043; Short 06 public uniform encounter.]

## Two manga artists put discretion in different places

In 野上武志's *楠木司令の植木箱* in *React*, Kusunoki's horticultural comparison distinguishes the common stock from the unusual reserve. The image gives her a way to think about disparate people under common management. Her aide extends it into the proposition that putting Takina with Chisato was deliberate. Kusunoki's doubtful answer leaves the idea with the person who proposed it. The story also sets Kusunoki's image of Walnut as an older man beside what she is told of Kurumi as a girl who likes games. Some of the facts most relevant to the café remain out of her reach. Nogami's version supplies an apt alternative to a commander's all-seeing retrospective design. The planter belongs to this artist's portrayal. A habitual TV gardener or a secret plan for the partnership would each require evidence the comic does not supply. [C1 *React*, OPF S5–14, story art S7–14, contributor note S15; compare A1 E01 C0220–0226.]

大倉ナタ uses her more sparingly in *安心しとけバーカ* in *Reload 02*. Fuki refuses to give Takina the classified grave locations. Takina separately asks to visit Enkuboku so she can express her thanks to the fallen. The visit happens, and Takina acknowledges that Fuki took the matter to Kusunoki and obtained approval. Fuki's advocacy and Takina's desire to mourn give the permission its meaning. Kusunoki can help without surrendering the restricted information. The approval comes to us through another girl's account. The artist provides no scene of Kusunoki grieving in private, and the TV series does not inherit a memorial policy from this story. The contrast with Nogami is instructive: one artist asks what the commander can know of the people she manages; the other lets someone who needs a real accommodation benefit from a decision made out of sight. [C1 *Reload 02*, OPF S129–136, especially S131–135.]

## Where her portrait holds

Given a failed operation and a protected fact, expect Kusunoki to ask people she trusts for better information and to ration what the affected girls are allowed to contest. Given an operative she cannot replace, expect her to try duty, debt, an imperative or an intermediary; Chisato's refusal can still stand. Given a superior prepared to sacrifice her people, she has demonstrated a willingness to help without acknowledgment when someone outside headquarters can act for her. None of these conditions predicts that she will disobey every destructive order. Fuki has heard her put secrecy first even after being rescued. We know less about what Kusunoki wants for herself than about the people from whom she will risk asking something. What changes across the series is partly our chance to see her choose at those different costs. It is possible to recognize her late courage without having to believe she had a just answer ready when Takina asked what it would take to come back. [E02 06:28–07:06; E03 11:38–11:53; E09 06:16–07:34; E12 13:53–14:06, 20:05–20:11; E13 02:47–03:12; Short 02 00:48–01:45.]
