## Attributed claims worth carrying forward

- Adachi Shingo says E01 uses Takina’s viewpoint to leave viewers unsure how competent Chisato is. He speaks about the bench positions as a way of conveying outlook (S73/p071). The adjacent S72 unsigned explanation about prioritizing an enemy’s life is editorial evidence. This distinction matters to a view of opening recognition and competence.
- Adachi describes the opening as music-clip-like. He attributes the mutual kick to a later production request inspired by *Stand by Me* (S98/p096). He interprets the lyrics of 花の塔 as Takina’s point of view, without claiming the artist told him so (S100/p098). This bears on interpretive reception. The waveform comparisons in the shorts record independently identify music use.
- Anzai Chika and Wakayama Shion discuss recording procedure and freshness (S104–109). Wakayama recalls that her first take of E09’s final line did not quite sit right with her (腑に落ちない), that she asked Adachi about the scene’s background and recorded it again. Anzai does not remember the exchange, and the interviewer reports that Adachi, asked shortly before, does not remember it either. Wakayama jokingly asks whether it was all her illusion; Anzai puts it down to her own and Adachi’s memory, and Wakayama holds that the scene moved her most even if it was an illusion. The editors head the section 第9話の収録で見た幻（？） (S106/p104). Their imagined future teamwork between adversaries belongs to the interview’s hypothetical register (S109/p107).
- Koshimizu Ami distinguishes small alterations to scripted dialogue from more extensive background improvisation. Kuno Misaki describes conversational direction and her partner’s suggested trunk-reveal ad-lib in the E04 material (S110–115). Sakaki Kōsuke recalls direction toward grounded playing and a particular approach to welcoming Takina (S116–118). Matsuoka Yoshitsugu recalls expecting a short-lived antagonist and being told Majima would remain (S119–121). These recollections can explain some delivery choices without verifying the phonetics of a disputed short line.
- Adachi distinguishes Asaura’s heart-plot premise, his own wish for a happy conclusion and the decision to put the burden on Mika (S127/p125). Adachi says Chisato might suspect the truth. That possibility has creator-context value. It does not complete the offscreen action or make particular knowledge in Chisato certain at the end of E13.
- Imigimuru describes Takina’s long black hair as an early decision and discusses suggestions that helped settle Chisato’s design (S129/p127). Asaura says Anzai’s voice makes exact replication of Chisato in prose impossible. His later explanation of Takina’s trajectory explains development as conceived during production (S136–137/p134–135). This is direct support for treating the prose realization as its own medium.
- Maruyama Yūsuke gives a personal reading of Kusunoki’s goodwill and attributes the addition of Takina’s reddened nose in the E09 park material to Yamamoto Yumiko (S142/p140). Sawada Kenji, credited 沢田犬二, discusses the production supervision of depicted props and action (S143–145). Neither is a new witness to a fictional character’s unspoken private motive.

The interview names printed in order are 安済知佳 and 若山詩音, 小清水亜美, 久野美咲, さかき孝輔, 松岡禎丞, 足立慎吾, いみぎむる, アサウラ, 丸山裕介 and 沢田犬二. The colophon credits writing to 岡本大介, 田中尚道 and 富田英樹, and editing to 山本貴志 and 串田誠. To attribute a designer or interviewee, prefer the page’s role label to the volume’s umbrella editorial credit.

### Internal inconsistency

The Art Design caption for Okinawa and Hawaii (S70/p068) says Chisato was found by chance on **石垣島** and offers a hedged motive ("perhaps thinking her death was near"). The Story Digest caption (S96/p094) and Asaura (S138/p136) name **宮古島**, and Asaura says he visited the actual café there. Do not cite S70 for the island. The S96 synopsis also gives an editorial motive (死を覚悟して); neither motive is an A1 fact.
