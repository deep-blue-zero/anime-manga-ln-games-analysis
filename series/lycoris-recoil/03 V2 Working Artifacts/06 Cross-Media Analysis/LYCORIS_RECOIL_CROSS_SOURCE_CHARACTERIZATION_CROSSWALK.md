### Scope for character and relationship work

Abe supplies a **selectable Recollect realization** in which Takina can identify a false premise, take pleasure while disputing its rationale, initiate a game and voice a plan that places labor on each partner. Chisato can use a playful offer to get close, discover she was asking for something personally important, then change what she requests or fail to notice the burden it creates. Their recognizable speech rhythms on the page are best reconstructed from who proposes, who narrows the proposition and who gets the last word. Treating that distribution as a fixed “warm initiator / literal respondent” pair misses the partner who invents a lexical trap, the guest who cooks and assigns the next turn, and the host who would like to leave an awkward film scene. Close attention, romantic possibility and independent grounds for the same act all survive. Only the corresponding anime passages can establish the anime's performed registers and the answers Chisato and Takina actually give there.

No B1 finding here meets the conditions for a direct contradiction of a high-confidence A1 rule. Where the manga is closest to an anime premise, the device, disclosures, and character knowledge often differ; where the manga adds real new conduct, the relationship, stakes or genre change. The unfamiliar customer's unresolved choice, Takina's treatment of Sakura in Short 05, the private response to the screen, and Chisato's untested pest-control limit remain counterexamples or open discriminators in their own right. This comparison supplies **no independent prospective validation**. It assigns no agreement count across the two Abe volumes, no extra credit through their licensed status and no claim about the frequency of these acts among other creators. ### Cross-contributor comparisons

Four patterns recur across independent contributors. Auditor `claude/anthology-auditor-c` proposed them on 23 November 2026, and critic `claude/c1-critic-c` qualified each against the pages. They compare creators' independent choices. None is frequency evidence, each ruling is local to these comics, and none revises A1 or CP5.

**P1 · Chisato deflects Takina's probing about her own risk or fear.** In みかづち’s *Repeat* S63–67, Takina says the risk falls on Chisato too and that she might cry. Chisato answers with an embrace and thanks, turns to the job, and Takina says she feels dodged (はぐらかされた). In 弐尉マルコ’s *Repeat 02* S135, Takina teases Chisato for being relaxed under fire yet afraid of an injection, and Chisato switches to praising Takina's hair. The objects differ: concern for Chisato's safety in one, an incongruous fear in the other. Only みかづち's Takina names the evasion. **SPECIFY** for how the relationship handles Chisato's risk; **OPEN** for motive.

**P2 · Takina applies Lycoris training and armed procedure to ordinary situations.** In sugar.’s *Repeat 02* S37–45, she draws a gun on a vending machine and reads lottery beeps as a time bomb. In にいな涼’s *Repeat* S89–92, she draws a concealed handgun on a café customer she suspects of taking Chisato's underwear, citing Lycoris hunting and a possible stalker. In 森永ミキ’s *Repeat 02* S79–80, she treats a stiff expression with a hundred push-ups and sit-ups, attributing her stamina to being a Lycoris. Three contributors independently exaggerate the same disposition: **CARICATURE** for each. The comparison cannot raise the A1 frequency of such behavior.

**P3 · Takina values the unusual life in which she met Chisato: once in her own words, once by implication.** In 涼海来夏’s *Repeat* S43–44, she says that because they are not ordinary she could meet Chisato, and prefers their own kind of normal (こっちの普通), admitting she borrowed Chisato's line (パクリ). In しろううらやま’s *Repeat 02* S99–100, her answer about the mermaid touching an unimagined world and gaining many irreplaceable experiences sits in a panel of the two in uniform. **EXTEND**: the first is direct self-report. The second is an implied parallel the comic does not state.

**P4 · Takina's stance on nonkilling.** In みかづち’s *Repeat* S63/S68, she finds sparing even enemies' lives unreasonable and is not convinced, though she understands Chisato's view, then answers はいはい to Chisato's condition. In にいな涼’s *Repeat* S92, holding a suspected thief at gunpoint, she volunteers that she does not intend to kill, paired with a threat (痛い目みる前に). **SPECIFY**: verbal acceptance of Chisato's condition and a volunteered disclaimer of intent to kill coexist with stated disagreement. Whether the limit is internalized, and how Takina acts under it, is **OPEN**. For context, cite the crosswalk's “The reach of nonkilling” paragraph (A1 E02-CLM-006/011–012, E05-CLM-017, E12-CLM-006/013/018, REL-CHI-TAK-E12-002/003). It concerns Chisato's commitment, not a match for Takina's stance.


### What may enter later reconstruction

Chisato’s ordinary invitations and Takina’s practical framing can be selected as starting possibilities when the speaker, setting and stakes fit. The C1 works supply specific alternatives to that familiar sequence. Takina sometimes initiates the pleasure; Chisato sometimes declines the next invitation. Takina can accept an embrace, reject an image use or answer an unwanted intrusion. Mika can be the subject of a portrait, the person who decorates a drink, the parent grieving an impossible household, or the practical arbiter of a work proposal. Fuki’s care reaches a former partner, her current junior, Mika and Takina in different registers and under different official permissions. Which option belongs in a generated scene depends on its source-local realization. Romantic possibility remains available to interpretation alongside the participants’ stated reasons and the opt-outs they actually exercise.

For a stranger, the strongest direct C1 comparisons concern a labeled purpose: a competitive child rejects Kurumi’s recruitment after receiving useful advice; the woman the pair helps accepts Chisato’s separate invitation to visit the café; a café operator may bear the cost of a popular competition. For a partner, care can persist through disagreement and may even create an unwanted burden. For an official, permission to visit one site or keep a kit has a bounded object and recipient. For an observer, the default should be to report a remark, facial expression or revealed object and state exactly whose explanation is available. None of the seven books recalibrates native frequencies of intrusion, intimate contact, exceptional skill, public revelation, permanent residence or deaths. The older positive example of Takina understanding Kurumi’s shorthand was withdrawn after source review. The scope of that comparison is narrower. The historical V1 transition and originating evidence retain their own basis.
