# Gluttony days · prose reading

The lunch Clarisse finds almost too frightening to order becomes Takina’s best explanation for why the girl later tries to save her. Clarisse herself gives a simpler reason. She came to know the guards as kind people and heard Takina was in danger. The disagreement is characteristic of a book in which food matters intensely without giving its maker, recipient or narrator an infallible reading of anyone’s intentions. Fuki wants her partner to taste monjayaki for the first time. What Sakura values is that Fuki cooked for her. Takina makes a salad to answer her misgivings about eating late, then wants the pizza while it is hot. The After Meals keep the question of what each person wanted alive beyond the apparent conclusion of each course.

## Source-native reading

### 1 · ファントム・メナス

Takina moderates Werewolf while serving coffee. The assignment gives her exact knowledge of the two wolves and none of the same certainty about why Mika’s face changes during a telephone call. She notices the change, considers what it could mean, then decides pursuing it would be bad manners. At the table Chisato claims to be the seer and gains enough credibility to win for the wolves by **selling out her fellow wolf Mizuki** (9.2–88). It is a revelation strategically made by Chisato. This opening gives the reader a rare look at a game from the seat with access to the role cards.

In the job that follows, the supposed security threat, the assignment the adults know they have staged and the danger that actually materializes have different authors. Hikaru Akutsu’s company traces his daughter Clarisse’s threat to her own computer. The adults agree to put on the appearance of hiring guards while the café takes her out for the day. The narrative waits until the girls have spent time with her, and an actual watcher has appeared, to explain what their apparent lack of vigilance meant (9.222–304, 9.638–653). The adults’ discovery of the first ruse gives them unwarranted confidence about what comes next. Chisato’s proposal that the father might have supplied the watcher is another provisional account. She later admits an initial misidentification (9.701–729, 9.931–947, 10.843–851).

At the ramen restaurant 弐郎, the three girls come to need each other before any violence. Chisato has wanted to try the place and has doubts of her own about its customs. Clarisse fears getting the order or the unwritten rules wrong. Getting adjacent seats, reducing her serving and helping her keep something she wants do more than announcing that she has nothing to fear. Each girl can see the other two eating with her. The meal brings visible relief and thanks. Full stomachs and garlic breath then stay with them, impeding Chisato and eventually identifying her by smell before speech (9.354–395, 9.491–558, 9.601–653, 10.48–90, 10.559–567). Asaura lets the bodily consequences of lunch follow the characters into what appears to be a different genre.

Takina consciously uses protecting Clarisse as a reason to separate from Chisato. She expects a fight in which she cannot afford restraint and where a death would violate her partner’s practice. The look she remembers from Chisato makes her suspect her purpose was understood. That memory is Takina’s (10.203–233). Takina and Madame suspend their confrontation in the presence of a child. Asked who was following the girls, Madame identifies Monsieur as her partner (10.270–303). Concern for the person she has undertaken to protect stays central to Takina’s accounting of success, alongside professional pride, recognition of an accomplished opponent and a willingness to face the possibility of dying (10.483–558). The story avoids making her either a dispassionate functionary or a convert to Chisato’s rule.

Back at the café Kurumi waits to speak while Mika sees Detective Abe out with ground coffee and homemade yōkan. Abe takes Mika’s hint without asking what happened (9.895–909). Kurumi has to reconsider the company’s earlier assessment. A payment trail associated with Hikaru makes him look implicated. When Hikaru reaches toward her, Kurumi freezes and Mika intervenes. Hikaru says he wanted to see her screen. Even Mika had doubted his intentions. Mika later faults himself for the fright Kurumi suffered. None of them can establish who ordered what from the account holder’s name alone (9.886–947, 10.323–391, 10.575–625, 10.961–977). Their separate scenes matter because even the people doing research out of harm’s way have something at stake.

Chisato interrupts the standoff with an apparent threat to Clarisse. The girl then cancels the contract she herself placed with Madame and Monsieur. It targeted the guards and expressly protected her. She offers the outstanding success fee as compensation for cancellation (10.647–685). Later she reports that the transfer is complete. Madame declines to check because she trusts her (10.790–795). Clarisse wanted a neglected daughter’s chance to bring her father running. She had counted on professional guards staying alive long enough for her to call the whole thing off (10.686–715). Takina realizes that Monsieur had been waiting to establish who the guards were. Identifying herself helped trigger Madame’s attack (10.674–706). When Madame asks why Clarisse confessed, Clarisse describes the guards’ kindness and her fear for Takina after Chisato warned her. She also nods at Madame’s suggestion that the two girls’ appearance changed her feelings. Takina doubts that explanation and imagines the ordeal of ordering and eating ramen made them comrades (10.727–743). The girl’s words, her nod and Takina’s explanation all deserve separate weight.

When Clarisse apologizes, Chisato leaves Takina to answer. Takina crouches below the girl’s eye level, credits her own inexperience, smiles and asks her never to do this again. Clarisse looks up, sees the smile, shows relief, then begins to cry and hugs her. Takina pats her back, and Chisato pats Takina’s head. Takina privately wants to brush the hand away and refrains for Clarisse’s sake (10.797–819). The same gesture that looks simply affectionate to a bystander has an immediate cost to its recipient.

In the car Chisato makes a case for connecting the meaning of Clarisse’s name with her father’s. Takina objects that the etymology could not show whether he neglects her *now*. Clarisse points out he does not know Latin. Chisato stands by the word’s meaning. Mika vouches for Hikaru’s habit of making efforts people do not see (10.878–931). The later photograph on Chisato’s phone shows Clarisse smiling, with both arms around her father and costumed characters nearby. The narrated account first lets the reader take in their pleasure. It then reports that **Akutsu rented the park after the emergency**. Mika had already told Takina this after Clarisse left. The father’s claim that he had arranged it long beforehand is false. Clarisse went smiling and let everyone think she believed him. Whether she privately did stays open (10.933–951). Chisato gives the family a private outing and later says that if she goes, she would like to go when Takina can go too. Takina had been sent for a medical examination. Mika also attributes Madame’s reluctance to kill children to her own bereavement. The attribution is Mika’s testimony (10.952–977).

At the next game Takina leaves the moderator’s place for a player’s. People strike her as complicated, troublesome and interesting, and she laughs (10.982–1018). She has deliberately given up a position from which the distribution of roles would have been known.

### After Meal 1

Takina’s idea for a meal the shop might sell produces a distinction between eating what one needs and choosing a café for sweets or coffee when it is possible to go elsewhere. Mika values that freedom to choose. Chisato hears Takina refer to the shop as うち, “our place,” after hours. She tells her she is glad. Takina thinks she has probably said it before, feels embarrassed and entertains an explanation for why Kurumi might use that word differently (11.15–59). The remark establishes that Chisato wants to hear it and that Takina is affected by her response. It cannot date Takina’s first sense of belonging or disclose Kurumi’s private feelings.

### 2 · フキの恩返し

The first scene shows the consequence before it reveals the telling. Sakura warns Fuki about breathing in the cold warehouse. Fuki dismisses her, then finds herself unable to breathe or voice the praise she owes while Sakura supports her. Only afterward does the narrative move to the park and disclose that Fuki has been telling yesterday’s story to Chisato (12.61–89, 12.169–223). She wants to thank Sakura. She also has something to apologize for. Fuki is competent at owning an error before a superior, even one someone under her made. She has a harder time speaking across the difference in rank in the other direction. Asking her former partner how to make a good meal is a practical way to begin (12.213–277).

Cooking brings its own training. Fuki and Chisato sometimes arrive at the same thought. Fuki conjectures that sharing so much of childhood accounts for it. She can make the monjayaki by a fourth practice round and still wants to look things up. At the dorm she gets permission for a hotplate, sets it at the one table within reach of a socket, and has Sakura come after afternoon training. She expressly calls the food an afternoon snack. She has assumed that for someone from Hokkaido this would be a first taste (12.347–364, 12.465–498, 12.519–535).

Fuki does tell Sakura that she appreciated the rescue and should have listened. Her attempt to finish apologizing for that error is interrupted at **すま──**. The narration revisits the fact that the apology was only beginning to come out (12.549–570). Learning that Sakura had monjayaki at a restaurant the day before makes Fuki feel foolish. Erika almost exposes what the others hoped to smooth over. Hibana manages the awkward moment and subsequently apologizes simply because she wants to say it. That matters to Fuki. The person she finds pitiable is herself. She hates that the others are having to spare her feelings over a repayment that might have been an imposition. She would like to tell the anxious Sakura that she is blameless and cannot get those words out. She does apologize aloud about serving the food two days in a row and retracts her own bitter remark (12.607–685).

Sakura gives the two meals different values. Yesterday’s restaurant tasted good and eating there with the others was fun. Having her senior make today’s meal for her makes her *glad*. Fuki credits the look in her eyes. She finds herself wishing partners could read each other’s minds, then rejects the temptation to skip the work. She returns with ingredients for four, and the narrative leaves Sakura laughing. Fuki can trust what Sakura said without possessing her complete private verdict (12.686–749).

### After Meal 2

Publication order briefly reverses event order. The café gathering at 13.7 takes place on the day Chisato taught Fuki. Fuki’s dorm snack follows on the **next** day at 12.477–490. Chisato serves the café meal for dinner. Takina wonders whether this food is normally closer to a snack. Her silence makes Chisato imagine that the real Takina might have been swapped out for a mechanical double. She checks her pulse. The eventual question concerns the practical point of making the barrier in the batter. Fuki had asked a similar question. The coincidence can amuse Chisato without making the two people interchangeable. They keep eating (13.6–44).

### 3 · ジ・アンリマーカブル・ナイト・イン・リコリコ

The long job that puts the pair in the shop late required neither girl to fire. Baths, hair, the order of sleeping and what they might eat receive more space than that assignment. Chisato offers Takina the first bath because her hair takes longer to dry. Takina recognizes a labored pun and declines to reward the setup. She describes what she learned about hair care in Kyoto and can imagine long hair on Chisato. Chisato’s practical reason for keeping it short makes Takina hesitate (14.35–58, 14.112–138).

When Chisato asks to dry her hair, Takina twice says no. Being partners has no bearing on the request, she says. Chisato shakes her shoulders and persists. Takina hands over the dryer because continued refusal is troublesome and wastes time. The actual handling is gentler and more attentive than she expected. The strokes feel like being patted and praised. She feels ticklish in body and mind, embarrassed, conscious of blushing and inclined to hide her face (14.139–162). She interrupts, finishes her own hair, then notices a difference between doing it herself and having Chisato do it (14.218–242). Later she dries Chisato’s hair. Chisato calls this wordless understanding. Takina points out how unmistakable the cue was (14.259–276). The pleasure in this intimacy is a possible reading of the scene. The narrated feelings are more specific, and what happens after she yields cannot be used as evidence that she had privately assented from the start.

Each proposal to go to sleep generates a more vivid account of what the hungry pair could eat instead. Takina has tastes of her own before Chisato begins the sales pitch. When her stomach audibly growls, Chisato pretends to sleep long enough for Takina to realize what she is sparing her. Kurumi finally suggests that, if eating before bed is the objection, they could stay awake. Chisato seizes the suggestion and proposes taking turns napping the next day. Takina objects on grounds of work and an emergency call, then finds herself practically considering how to serve the food. Chisato offers her the option of sleeping. The prospect of adding roast beef changes what happens, and Takina says out loud that she will eat (14.244–329, 14.340–386, 14.398–486). The proposed rota has a speaker. Its execution is never shown.

Kurumi had acquired this beef with points accumulated through shopping. Chisato adds cheese and Kurumi suggests roast beef. Takina makes salad as well, and her account of why the pizza succeeds gives the pumpkin, sauce and texture of the thin slices specific jobs. They all reach for more pizza before the salad. Takina herself proposes watching *Gremlins*. Asked to whom she is doing wrong, she identifies the person she will be tomorrow. She agrees with Chisato that the decision was hers. Knowing the cost is precisely what unsettles her (14.493–569, 14.579–599, 14.626–665).

They watch both films. The café’s work and the birthday party for a regular’s five-year-old child (14.10) go ahead, and Chisato and Takina are found asleep together. When Mika says that a day like this is occasionally fine, Kurumi warns that spoiling children can become a habit *for the parent*. She goes back to her own room, listens to the sleepers and strikes her keys a little more gently (14.675–700). Her own softening is carried by that last description. Nothing requires her to withdraw her objection to Mika.

### After Meal 3 and 満腹の後に

After they have overslept, Takina tries to reconstruct with her eyes shut how the two could have fought over the bedding. The imagined reconstruction cannot verify the movements in their sleep. Another meal is on offer. This A5 beef comes from a party guest, separately from the roast beef Kurumi obtained through her shopping points the night before. The prospect of dinner helps postpone restraint once more. At 15.44–47 Takina makes the dieting resolution and Chisato offers to join her (15.7–12, 15.25–47).

In the final course the adults have brandy for their tea, and everyone gets a spoon. Takina recognizes Russian tea but gives the wrong account of what the present service requires. Kurumi says the jam is tasted separately and Mika confirms it. Eating and drinking in succession lets Chisato experience how the aroma and strength of the sweetness change. Her historical explanation for the custom is her own inference. Mika says the diners can adjust what they take to their appetites. He made the jam himself and still offers the flavor Chisato has not tasted. She takes the apple jam while wondering whether parents give children more food because they understand how simply it can give them a share of happiness. This is her interpretation of his abundance. Now *Chisato* resolves to diet and *Takina* offers to join her (17.17–54). Both directions of the invitation are present. The novel ends with the plan and the pleasure about which they made it.
