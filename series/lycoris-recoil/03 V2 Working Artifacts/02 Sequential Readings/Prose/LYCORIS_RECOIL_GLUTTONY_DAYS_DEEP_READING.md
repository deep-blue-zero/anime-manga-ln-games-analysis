# Gluttony days · prose reading

Each course starts with a practical definition that proves too narrow. A guard job turns into a test of who has accepted whose story. A thank-you meal becomes an apology and then asks whether enjoyment can take the place of words. An inelegant night after work gradually acquires a reason to continue. The After Meals preserve objections after the main action is over. In the last course, refreshments go beyond anyone’s stated need and Chisato imagines why a parent might do that.

## Source-native reading

### 1 · ファントム・メナス

A Werewolf game gives Takina formal access to who plays each role. That knowledge cannot certify what Mika’s expression means. Chisato’s team turns Mizuki’s apparent betrayal into credibility (9.2–88). The actual café job reverses the arrangement. The apparent client and object of concern are visible, but people have different accounts of the same threat. Hikaru and the café stage protection as the setting for an outing for his daughter Clarisse. The delayed account of the staged commission makes readers revise earlier professional language (9.286–304, 9.638–653).

The ramen scene gives the misunderstanding a mundane counterpart. Clarisse is intimidated by the shop and an imagined penalty for ordering badly. Chisato and Takina notice different needs, adjust the order and let Clarisse keep something she wants. Clarisse visibly relaxes and thanks them. Chisato has chosen the venue partly to satisfy her own curiosity, a possibility Takina notices. The excessive meal then impedes Chisato more than the genre would predict (9.503–558, 9.601–653, 10.48–90). Feeding, pleasure and inconvenience belong to the same cause.

Takina separates from Chisato when she expects morally incompatible work. Kurumi meanwhile questions a company assessment and develops plausible rival explanations. Inside the café, she experiences Hikaru’s approach as threatening before his purpose is known. Mika protects her. Later evidence shows why the apparent origin of payment could mislead (10.1–47, 10.226–233, 10.323–391, 10.575–625). Expertise reveals a gap, and each new inference still has a person’s point of view behind it.

Clarisse commissioned the danger to the guards as a way to get Hikaru’s attention, assuming they would last until she could stop it. The girl who appears to need rescue must now stop a real threat that grew out of a manufactured one. Chisato turns up with what looks like a threat to Clarisse. Clarisse herself cancels the engagement and expresses fear for Takina. Takina sees how her intervention contributed to the conflict. She gives shared food a place in her account of the confession, whereas Clarisse describes the girls’ kindness more generally (10.549–573, 10.627–715, 10.725–743). Chisato later admits that she too misidentified a watcher at first. Mika backs part of her kind interpretation of Hikaru. He says the apparent proof of a prearranged father–daughter outing was created after the emergency. Whether Clarisse believes that story stays uncertain (10.843–859, 10.889–951). The novel repairs part of a relationship without proving that the adults’ consoling account was true.

At the later game Takina opts to play rather than moderate. She finds people difficult and interesting. The form lets her choose the position with less guaranteed information (10.982–1018). In After Meal 1, she proposes a commercial meal. Mika distinguishes demand for something sweet from acquiring necessary nutrition. Chisato hears affiliation because Takina uses うち after hours. Takina suspects she has said it before and is embarrassed by the acknowledgment (11.15–59). A lexical event matters to the hearer without establishing the first date of a mental change.

### 2 · フキの恩返し

Fuki ignores Sakura’s practical advice before Sakura protects her. In telling Chisato what happened she calls a meal gratitude, recognizes an apology she finds difficult and asks a former partner for concrete help. She can assume responsibility upward without knowing how to apologize sideways or downward (12.61–87, 12.169–223, 12.259–277). The use of the dorm dining room makes a meal with its own conventions an exception to daily institutional routines. Fuki wants Sakura to enjoy a first monjayaki. An earlier pleasure with acquaintances makes her premise wrong. An offer of care can still contain its giver’s need for a particular response.

After Fuki’s embarrassment spills into irritation, Sakura explains that yesterday’s restaurant was good and today’s homemade meal is good in another way. It matters that her senior cooked it for her. Fuki sees an opportunity to trust her and entertains a wish that partners could read each other. She rejects that shortcut in her own thoughts before returning to cook for the four of them (12.549–574, 12.607–714, 12.719–749). The apology for the original error begins and is interrupted. Her later apologies address the meal and her irritation. The last laugh is visible. Fuki never obtains a full verdict from Sakura. After Meal 2 returns to the café on the day Chisato taught Fuki, before the following day’s dorm dinner (12.475–479, 13.7). It replays a small part of the misunderstanding. Chisato thinks Takina’s silence might indicate pleasure or fancifully a mechanical substitute. Takina asks whether the showy part of cooking has a practical point. This is a specific resemblance to Fuki’s earlier objection. It coexists with the group continuing to eat (13.6–44).

### 3 · ジ・アンリマーカブル・ナイト・イン・リコリコ

After a routine job takes too long, bath arrangements, a changing schedule and small bids for attention supply the occasion. Takina initially refuses help drying her hair and disputes whether partnership entitles Chisato to touch it. Chisato persists. Takina eventually yields on grounds of time and privately enjoys the care. Later she reciprocates. Chisato calls this understanding without words, and Takina notices how conspicuous the signal was (14.35–58, 14.112–162, 14.220–243, 14.261–276). Taking turns lends the night an order, but talking about a particular food makes postponing it harder. Takina has a previously formed preference and can name it before the exchange becomes a debate about what tomorrow’s selves should bear (14.244–329, 14.340–485). Kurumi proposes staying awake and Chisato offers to make it practical with a rota. Takina keeps objecting on performance grounds and also begins to provision the meal. Chisato eventually offers to let her sleep, before a roast-beef topping changes both their minds. Takina explicitly owns her decision and the cost to her future self (14.460–486, 14.645–665).

The morning follows a kitchen collaboration and two films. Others come in and tolerate the unusual sleep schedule. Kurumi’s criticism that parents can get into the habit of spoiling children is directed at Mika; after looking in on the sleepers the narration gives her own keystrokes a slightly gentler rhythm (14.494–569, 14.675–700). That is prose’s figurative access to a character-inflected action, without a promise of indulgent behavior in every situation. After Meal 3 postpones dieting again when the prospect of an A5 dinner arises. Chisato agrees to join Takina in her resolution (15.25–47). The final course adds jams and differently served drinks. Chisato wonders whether offering abundance is a parent’s way of offering a share of happiness. Takina now agrees to accompany Chisato’s renewed resolution (17.21–54). Participation makes excess mutual and reversible in plans, if not already resolved in action.
