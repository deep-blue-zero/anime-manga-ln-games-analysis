# Gluttony days · prose reading

The lunch Clarisse finds almost too frightening to order becomes Takina’s best explanation for why the girl later tries to save her. Clarisse herself gives a simpler reason. She came to know the guards as kind people and heard Takina was in danger. The disagreement is characteristic of a book in which food matters intensely without giving its maker, recipient or narrator an infallible reading of anyone’s intentions. Fuki wants her partner to taste monjayaki for the first time. What Sakura values is that Fuki cooked for her. Takina makes a salad to answer her misgivings about eating late, then wants the pizza while it is hot. The After Meals keep the question of what each person wanted alive beyond the apparent conclusion of each course.

## Source-native reading

The point of view changes by course. Takina focalizes most of the first and third stories, Fuki the second, and Chisato only After Meal 2 and the final course. Kurumi takes the café scenes of the first story and the coda of the third. Chisato’s narrated interior is therefore confined to two short pieces of domestic comedy that S20 lists as new for the volume (13.6–44, 17.2–54). Her reading of the Clarisse job reaches the reader chiefly through her own speech, at the capture of the tail, at the standoff and in the car (9.700–738, 10.653–712, 10.779, 10.850–869), and through Takina’s hedged guesses about her (9.720–722, 10.191, 10.210–211, 10.231, 10.698–699).

### 1 · ファントム・メナス

Takina moderates Werewolf while serving coffee. The assignment gives her exact knowledge of the two wolves and none of the same certainty about why Mika’s face changes during a telephone call. She notices the change, considers what it could mean, then decides pursuing it would be bad manners. At the table Chisato claims to be the seer and gains enough credibility to win for the wolves by **selling out her fellow wolf Mizuki** (9.2–88). It is a revelation strategically made by Chisato. This opening gives the reader a rare look at a game from the seat with access to the role cards.

The same scene fixes several of Takina’s working pleasures and scruples before the job begins. A moderator has nothing to do during the debate, so she goes to the kitchen and brews refills for two regulars in the siphon (9.12–19). Her favorite moment in siphon brewing is the one in which the coffee draws back down from the funnel. It feels as though all the earlier work bears fruit at once (9.39–41). She is glad when a toilet break means the coffee will be drunk while it is warm (9.71). Her training in peripheral vision cannot be switched off, so she catches Mika’s expression without meaning to. The part she finds shameful is the inference she then draws, that someone may be in danger or dead. She rebukes herself in a string of short judgments (9.43–47, 9.67). She also knows that a trained person can smile while doing violence, so Mika’s relaxed face after the call proves nothing (9.65–66). Watching Chisato and Mizuki improvise as if by prior arrangement, she privately thinks さすがだ (9.60–62). When Mizuki hopes the caller is a handsome man, Takina’s dry remark that she would argue the opposite for a young client earns a glare, which Takina ignores (9.83–84). Mika says only that the next day was to have been a holiday and that he wants to take an old acquaintance’s job (9.74).

In the job that follows, the supposed security threat, the assignment the adults know they have staged and the danger that actually materializes have different authors. Hikaru Akutsu’s company traces his daughter Clarisse’s threat to her own computer. The adults agree to put on the appearance of hiring guards while the café takes her out for the day. The narrative waits until the girls have spent time with her, and an actual watcher has appeared, to explain what their apparent lack of vigilance meant (9.222–304, 9.638–653). The adults’ discovery of the first ruse gives them unwarranted confidence about what comes next. Chisato’s proposal that the father might have supplied the watcher is another provisional account. She later admits an initial misidentification (9.701–729, 9.931–947, 10.843–851).

Takina’s account of the morning shows how little she is told and how she reads around it. They are told to dress for combat with almost no information. Kurumi’s absence bothers her because Kurumi’s backup raises the odds of success (9.142–147). Akutsu and Mika meet after ten years. Takina relays what she heard the night before, that Mika knew him in his wilder days, and Mizuki places that before DA. Akutsu remarks that Mika’s coffee was awful the last time he tasted it (9.115–125). When the client’s daughter gives her name, Chisato at once connects it and the girl’s red hair to a film heroine. Akutsu confirms the guess and Mika says 千束は、そういう子だ. Takina understands none of it, and nobody explains (9.157–167). Clarisse calls Chisato a geek (9.169). Takina learns why only on the drive back. Chisato names Clarice Starling of *The Silence of the Lambs* and *Hannibal*, and Clarisse supplies the red hair and the Mustang. Takina had been shown the film by Chisato as a classic and could vaguely recall the heroine’s name, but nothing about the car (10.881–887). Mika tests Chisato with a real punch from Akutsu. Chisato throws him, and Takina, without a word between them, pins his free wrist under her foot and aims her M&P at his forehead. She lets go when Chisato says her name (9.175–193). Chisato repeats her introduction word for word, and this time Clarisse takes her hand (9.195–198). When Akutsu says he will not bow to a threat over a small contract, Chisato restates his choice somewhat coolly (9.214–222). Kurumi warns that a predictable response to threats is the danger. Takina glosses it with the rakugo tale 『まんじゅう怖い』, and Kurumi agrees (9.226–232).

The real assignment reaches Takina in the car as a headset briefing from Mika, with the instruction not to let Clarisse notice. Mizuki says only たきな to stop her reacting. Takina, calling herself slow on the uptake, infers that Chisato’s loud talk in the back seat covers any sound leaking from the earpieces. The narration says only that Chisato talked as if she could not hear the channel (9.284–298). The reader learns in the car that the job is a dummy and that a real threat did reach the company (9.299–301). The details come only after lunch. The threat was sent from Clarisse’s home and her own computer through servers in China and the Netherlands, which the company’s cyber team established in under an hour. The girls are to spend a day playing with her under the appearance of a skilled guard (9.643–652). The adults judged that exposing her would be unkind and that ignoring the danger she had alleged would earn her displeasure (9.651–652). This is the adults’ account of her plan, and it proves incomplete.

Before the meal Clarisse twice tells the guards to cancel. Her argument is tactical. They have no plan and have sent the car away, and anyone targeting her father and her would be first-rate. Two needless deaths, she says, would trouble her sleep (9.456–470). Chisato refuses on grounds unrelated to the contract. A girl alone in a strange place would be in danger, death threat or no (殺害予告とか関係なしに, 9.471). Takina asks bluntly whether her father would really skip his business to fetch her. Clarisse answers that he has always put work first and her below his cars, then begins to say that if she were truly in danger he would surely come (9.476–479). Chisato replies that she thinks he would come because they are father and daughter (9.480). The premise of Clarisse’s commission is visible in these lines before anyone but Clarisse knows it exists.

At the ramen restaurant 弐郎, the three girls come to need each other before any violence. Takina suspects that Chisato has wanted to try the place, and Chisato has doubts of her own about its customs. Clarisse fears getting the order or the unwritten rules wrong. Three adjacent seats come free by luck (9.510). Reducing her serving and declining on her behalf the raw garlic she does not want do more than announcing that she has nothing to fear. Each girl can see the other two eating with her. The meal brings visible relief and thanks. Full stomachs and garlic breath then stay with them, impeding Chisato and eventually identifying her by smell before speech (9.354–395, 9.491–558, 9.601–653, 10.48–90, 10.559–567). Asaura lets the bodily consequences of lunch follow the characters into what appears to be a different genre.

Clarisse’s preferences come first. Tempura and sushi would leave her unmoved, yakitori, udon, soba and natto are nothing new, and a ryōtei meal would be dull (9.253–265). Takina, feeling that nothing she could say would land, looks to Kurumi and Mizuki for help (9.266–274). Chisato’s decision follows as a challenge, おいガキンチョ、覚悟しやがれ (9.275–282), and Kurumi later works out the target from the car’s position and the conversation (9.369). By the time they reach the ramen street the shop is settled. Takina had expected Chisato to choose among its shops to suit the girl, and Chisato says it is her first visit too (9.348–360). Takina notices tension in her partner, which she finds rare in someone who jokes with a muzzle at her brow, and asks whether the place is really dangerous. Chisato says it is (9.361–366). On the way Chisato leads them past a soba shop and praises its nabeyaki udon for another day, while Takina is tempted by the smell of Kantō dashi and envies a motorcyclist who seems to be lunching there (9.378–383). In the queue Takina backs Chisato’s claim to be watching for snipers as professional craft, though she knows the job is a pretense. She reasons that Akutsu, and probably Mika, chose them for the outing, since 遊びに関してなら千束だ (9.447–455). Kurumi supplies the etiquette by headset, watching through cameras. Only Chisato and Takina hear her say that the stories of punishment for leftovers are exaggerated. Clarisse has thrown her headset to Takina in irritation, so she is left with Takina’s half-remembered rumor and the sight of a huge shop master holding a cleaver (9.460, 9.487–506).

At the counter each partner rescues Clarisse once, and she sits between them. When she cannot speak, Chisato orders a third of the noodles for her, and Clarisse gives Chisato a weak, relieved smile (9.511–517). Asked about garlic, Takina declines it for herself and Chisato orders every topping increased. Clarisse stammers that she cannot manage raw garlic, and Takina orders for her: no garlic, everything else standard. Clarisse thanks her with a small smile, and Takina answers with the thumbs-up (9.543–558). The wait also produces an interrogation. Takina asks whether Chisato came only to say マシマシ, and whether she used the job as a pretext for a visit she had lacked the courage to make. Chisato denies it after long pauses and never admits it, so the motive stays Takina’s suspicion (9.526–535). She then says that tension and release are entertainment, which she heard on television (9.539). Takina is about to remind her that their work is guarding. For a moment she does not, because she is considering the idea (9.541).

Takina’s own meal is narrated as a sequence of discoveries. She works out the toppings from the posted guide, notes that asking for less fat yields slightly more than asking for nothing, and judges her bowl manageable (9.518–521, 9.561–564). She decodes Chisato’s うはぁ〜 before her mountain as delight, fear and relief that this branch has no マシマシ (9.565–569). She tastes vegetables, noodles, soup and pork in turn, and regrets having skipped the garlic that would have sharpened the soup (9.573–600). She ties back her hair to save time. Chisato wordlessly holds out a hand for a spare tie, and Takina understands from her stuffed cheeks what she wants. Clarisse copies them, which Takina reads as the effort of a girl who works hard at learning the rules, and she silently urges her on (9.603–611). Outside, full to the point of pain, Takina feels something nearer accomplishment than satisfaction. She concludes that Chisato’s remark about entertainment may have been serious (9.614–627). The second illustration in S9 gives only Chisato’s starry-eyed joy before her bowl. The prose adds the fear and the relief.

The tail is Takina’s discovery and Takina’s act. Waiting across the road so as not to block the queue, she recognizes the motorcyclist in different clothes. He could change his clothes but not his shoes (9.629–665). Kurumi agrees he is probably following them. Takina announces that she will capture and question him and is already moving when Chisato tells her to wait (9.666–671). She forces him to his knees with a pen point just breaking the skin at the base of his skull and questions him, after a polite opening, with a spoken お前 she uses toward no one else in the book (9.673–682). Chisato, still breathless from the meal, shields the scene from passers-by with her body and asks Takina to stop pressing him (9.685–700). Chisato then suggests that he shares their client. She had seen him following them from the café and assumed Takina had too. Takina had in fact been glancing at the two in the back seat, not checking for a tail. Knowing the job was a pretense, she had not been on guard (9.702–707). In Clarisse’s hearing, Chisato presents the surveillance as a father’s concern for an only daughter. Takina privately thinks Akutsu may simply have distrusted Mika, but she sees the words work on Clarisse and judges them deft. She guesses (多分) that Chisato wants to mend things between father and daughter (9.716–722).

When the man asks whether they are guards, Chisato begins with そうだけど and an indignant question. Takina cuts her off with her name, because Clarisse must go on believing in the guard job and the watcher may know only that they are guides. Chisato then answers that they are guards, very skilled ones (9.729–734). Takina later blames herself for the attack because her capture let the man confirm who the guards were (10.704–709). Chisato’s spoken confirmation is also on the page, and Takina does not count it. Once he has gone, Takina grimaces and turns away. Chisato reads her face as a warning that cannot be spoken aloud and tenses, so Takina has to explain that the problem is garlic breath. Chisato blushes, reads Takina’s suspicion that the man fled from the smell straight off her face, and attacks her with her breath until Clarisse asks them to stop dancing (9.741–772).

Clarisse then asks whether sending the watcher away was wise in case the two guards were killed and she needed her father. Even trusting them, she says, she is uneasy (9.774–778). Chisato kneels to her eye level, puts a hand on her head and says 大丈夫. Takina hears in the voice a limitless kindness with something firm at its core, and notes that the moment was badly chosen, because the garlic makes Clarisse turn away (9.779–785). Chisato falls silent in embarrassment, and Takina takes over. She proposes Asakusa. When Clarisse says she cannot ride in a car, Takina runs through Katsushika, Ryōgoku and Akihabara on her mental map and settles on a walk to the station (9.786–801).

The ambush tests the habits the café has given Takina. Communications have gone dead, and Chisato leads them north into a residential lane (9.808–822). At a woman’s ハイ, Chisato pushes Takina aside and falls with Clarisse in her arms. Takina could have fired as she fell. She hesitates because the shooter looks like a plump woman in her mid-fifties, the kind of customer who might bring friends to the café, and she is vexed to find herself more steeped in ordinary life than she knew (9.823–836). The exchange through a block wall shows the better shooter. Madame answers Takina’s rounds through the very holes they make, and Takina’s fire becomes uncharacteristically wild (9.840–864). With her pistol empty, Takina stops herself from a second pursuit because the first had led into an ambush. Had she had rounds left, she thinks, she might have chased by reflex (9.865–872). She then finds Chisato still lying in the open, which in a firefight would be suicide (9.876–884). In S10 the cause turns out to be lunch. Chisato cannot be carried without the meal coming back up, and she declares throwing up a crime that would forfeit the right to eat at 弐郎 (10.66–89).

Takina’s management of the retreat is blunt and competent. She tells Clarisse to be ready for the worst, and Chisato asks her not to say such frightening things (10.113–115). When Clarisse offers once more to cancel the guard job so that they can flee, Takina answers that Clarisse would certainly die (10.120–121). Chisato stops her from using a glass telephone box in an open park, and Takina recognizes her own tunnel vision (10.127–133). Chisato recognizes the house of Deme-san, a regular who won enough on currency trading to buy a house and now seldom visits. She announces herself at the intercom as the café’s 看板娘 (10.137–147).

Takina consciously uses protecting Clarisse as a reason to separate from Chisato. She expects a fight in which she cannot afford restraint and where a death would violate her partner’s practice. The look she remembers from Chisato makes her suspect her purpose was understood. That memory is Takina’s (10.203–233). Takina and Madame suspend their confrontation in the presence of a child. Asked who was following the girls, Madame identifies Monsieur as her partner (10.270–303). Concern for the person she has undertaken to protect stays central to Takina’s accounting of success, alongside professional pride, recognition of an accomplished opponent and a willingness to face the possibility of dying (10.483–558). The story avoids making her either a dispassionate functionary or a convert to Chisato’s rule.

The parting has a spoken and an unspoken layer, and the resolve comes first. Takina believes Clarisse has already read in her face a switch to the offensive and “a certain resolve”; that reading is Takina’s (10.195–199). Takina later dates her decision to kill to the look she exchanged with the woman on the bench from Deme’s doorway (10.253–256). At the back door Chisato says 命、大事に, without saying whose life she means, and Takina answers 了解です. Chisato begins to offer help once the shop can be reached, and Takina cuts in to ask her to lock the door (10.203–207). Takina wonders whether Chisato has judged that she might lose or has seen her real purpose. She then turns Chisato’s own word for throwing up the meal against her. Chisato can hardly move yet, and should the worst happen, that would be ギルティ (10.208–212; cf. 10.86). Chisato gives the troubled smile of someone facing a willful child, swallows something and agrees (10.213–214). Outside, her private reasoning names it bluntly: 殺すしかない, and Chisato was 邪魔 (10.226–230). She pushes the memory of Chisato’s look out of her mind (10.231–233). What Chisato understood at the door stays open.

The walk with Madame turns on a misunderstood question and on talk that leaks information. Madame asks whether Takina is the child she looks. Takina says yes and asks “and you?”, meaning her age, but Madame gives her working name (10.239–248). Takina gives only her surname (10.251). Madame says she wanted the guards one at a time and saw in Takina the eyes of her own side. Takina had felt the same at the doorway and accepts the description for herself and, correcting the word, for her kind: 自分……いや、自分達と同じ目 (10.252–256). Madame cannot place Chisato. Her movements are too natural, and she even disguises the weight of her bag. Takina agrees that Chisato is probably the better of them. Trained only for offense, she had never thought that a guard must hide her own information, and she concludes 自分はまだまだだ (10.258–269). She lets Chisato’s name slip and corrects herself (10.292). Madame remarks that she talks a lot, and that talk leaks information both ways unless one is trained in plausible lies. Takina links this to Werewolf and briefly wonders whether the café’s game nights are covert training (10.297–303). Madame’s regret at having to kill so young a child plants the inconsistency Takina will later resolve (10.283–285, 10.309–313).

The fight is told through Takina’s assessments. She takes Madame’s full-body punch on her bag and is thrown four meters (10.395–412). She identifies Madame’s pistol as an old Smith & Wesson 422 and reasons that a veteran who still carries it will fire quickly at her head. She shields her head with the bag and feels no fear (10.432–455). A kick from her steel-capped loafer breaks ribs (10.469–471). When distance would favor Madame, she tells herself that being killed does not matter. Mission failure does, because Chisato alone might not protect Clarisse from this opponent (10.489–493). She clinches and fires the bag’s ballistic airbag between their bodies, aiming the pressure at the broken ribs, without fear or hesitation and on the assumption that Chisato can protect Clarisse afterward whatever happens to her (10.494–506, 10.525–526). Waking with a nosebleed and half-torn nails, she judges that Madame’s lung is pierced, that the job is done and that only her own survival remains (10.507–531). Facing each other with raised pistols, both smile faintly. Takina finds the moment clean and even pleasant, feels her resolve understood, and hears Madame’s comparison with her younger self as praise and possibly last words (10.542–560).

The smell of garlic arrives before the voice. Takina’s finger slackens at it. Then Chisato says that their Takina is a hundred times prettier, using うちのたきな. Takina’s head starts to turn by reflex, like a dog hearing its name, and she feels her intent to kill dull. Madame too looks away from Takina toward Chisato (10.561–570).

Back at the café Kurumi waits to speak while Mika sees Detective Abe out with ground coffee and homemade yōkan. Abe takes Mika’s hint without asking what happened (9.895–909). Kurumi has to reconsider the company’s earlier assessment. A payment trail associated with Hikaru makes him look implicated. When Hikaru reaches toward her, Kurumi freezes and Mika intervenes. Hikaru says he wanted to see her screen. Even Mika had doubted his intentions. Mika later faults himself for the fright Kurumi suffered. None of them can establish who ordered what from the account holder’s name alone (9.886–947, 10.323–391, 10.575–625, 10.961–977). Their separate scenes matter because even the people doing research out of harm’s way have something at stake.

Kurumi, the focalizer of these café scenes, tends to think in numbers. She judges Mika’s gift to Abe an impossible service, yet now understands that exchanges between people matter most in a privately run shop, and stays silent (9.896–905). Her hypotheses about rival parties and an insider faking a prank are labelled as hypotheses and prove wrong (9.940–947, 10.1–8). Mika’s attempt to reach Akutsu by posing as a company executive fails, because Akutsu runs a one-man firm and puts off a call from his own company. Kurumi watches Mika dig himself deeper, like a Werewolf player who cannot withdraw a claim (10.9–14). When her drone is shot down, she likens not knowing to a locked box she cannot accept being unable to open (10.33–40). She sets off fire alarms on Akutsu’s floor, in data only, so that he will turn on his phone. She would have done the whole building if Mika had not stopped her (10.324–336). She traces the tail through a dealer in false number plates. Mizuki, out driving in search of たこわさ, stops him by hitting him with her car (10.339–365). Before confronting Akutsu, Kurumi asks Mika whether he trusts her. His 仲間じゃないか leaves her feeling ticklish (10.373–375).

After Akutsu’s lunge Kurumi judges herself. Online she can laugh off anyone, but within arm’s reach she is small and weak, and she froze (10.579–591). Mika’s first caution is addressed to her. She should be careful with words and with suspicion, because without Chisato’s transmission someone would have been hurt. She accepts the lesson (10.615–623). Mika asks Akutsu to trust the girls as he trusts Mika himself and promises that all three will come back. Akutsu leaves to show that he can still overdo things (10.604–613). That the park rental is what he meant is an inference from 10.612–613 and 10.948. Mika’s later self-reproach covers the name Madame & Monsieur as well as Kurumi’s fright. He had not seen from the name that they would never accept a contract to kill a child (10.963–967).

Chisato interrupts the standoff with an apparent threat to Clarisse. The girl then cancels the contract she herself placed with Madame and Monsieur. It targeted the guards and expressly protected her. She offers the outstanding success fee as compensation for cancellation (10.647–685). Later she reports that the transfer is complete. Madame declines to check because she trusts her (10.790–795). Clarisse wanted a neglected daughter’s chance to bring her father running. She had counted on professional guards staying alive long enough for her to call the whole thing off (10.686–715). Takina realizes that Monsieur had been waiting to establish who the guards were. She concludes that her capture of him let him confirm it. The spoken confirmation was Chisato’s (9.729–734, 10.674–709). When Madame asks why Clarisse confessed, Clarisse describes the guards’ kindness and her fear for Takina after Chisato warned her. She also nods at Madame’s suggestion that the two girls’ appearance changed her feelings. Takina doubts that explanation and imagines the ordeal of ordering and eating ramen made them comrades (10.727–743). The girl’s words, her nod and Takina’s explanation all deserve separate weight.

The order of confession and threat matters. Clarisse confessed to Chisato at Deme’s house, after Takina had left by the sequence of events, and Chisato had already reproached her (10.710–712). Clarisse tells Madame it was Chisato’s warning that Takina might be in danger that made her confess (10.729–731). The jammer had gone with Takina’s bag, so communications returned and Chisato told the café everything (10.392, 10.620, 10.779). The gun at Clarisse’s head is therefore a performance aimed at Madame’s contractual duty never to harm the client, as Chisato’s taunt about the job failing makes plain (10.572, 10.645). It precedes only the formal cancellation. Whether Clarisse agreed to the staging is not stated. She weeps and apologizes to Takina (10.632–639). Takina knows none of this. She is the one person left without understanding and exchanges a look of mutual bafflement with Madame (10.628–638, 10.659). She works out Madame’s real target by recalling the earlier inconsistency. A killer hired to kill Clarisse would not regret having to kill someone older than her (10.660–674).

Takina’s reply to the explanation is an outburst of moral indignation. She cries out that some things simply must not be done. She wants to say more but stops at Chisato’s small shake of the head, inferring that Chisato has already dealt with it (10.692–699). Concluding, by her own admission with hindsight (結果論ではあるが), that she made the trigger, she feels dizzy (10.704–709). Her kindness toward Clarisse has a colder prelude. She assumes the girl never cared what became of the guards and meets her glance with a cool look (醒めた目) before the apology (10.726–730). Madame tells Clarisse that adults always underestimate children, their own most of all. Takina asks about Madame’s own child, and Madame says only that it slipped out of reach before she knew it (10.756–761). The jammer had been in Takina’s bag, planted by Monsieur when she captured him. Chisato had suspected as much because communications returned once Takina left (10.768–779). Takina feels that the hints were all there and that she caught none of them (10.784–790). Madame’s parting advice is that twenty more years will make her strong (10.794).

When Clarisse apologizes, Chisato leaves Takina to answer. Takina crouches below the girl’s eye level, credits her own inexperience, smiles and asks her never to do this again. Clarisse looks up, sees the smile, shows relief, then begins to cry and hugs her. Takina pats her back, and Chisato pats Takina’s head. Takina privately wants to brush the hand away and refrains for Clarisse’s sake (10.797–819). The same gesture that looks simply affectionate to a bystander has an immediate cost to its recipient.

Chisato’s care before the apology is practical. She wipes Takina’s nosebleed with her own handkerchief, refolds it and holds it to her nose. She tells her the blood spoils her looks. When Takina apologizes, Chisato says there is nothing to apologize for and praises her effort, and Takina answers はい (10.745–751). When Takina offers to wash the handkerchief and return it, Chisato snatches it back and points her chin at Clarisse (10.803–806). The head-patting comes with praise for Takina’s grown-up handling of the girl. Takina finds the satisfied smile slightly galling (10.815–819). The care runs both ways. During the retreat Takina had pressed her own handkerchief to Chisato’s sweating brow (10.117).

In the car Chisato makes a case for connecting the meaning of Clarisse’s name with her father’s. Takina objects that the etymology could not show whether he neglects her *now*. Clarisse points out he does not know Latin. Chisato stands by the word’s meaning. Mika vouches for Hikaru’s habit of making efforts people do not see (10.878–931). The later photograph on Chisato’s phone shows Clarisse smiling, with both arms around her father and costumed characters nearby. The narrated account first lets the reader take in their pleasure. It then reports that **Akutsu rented the park after the emergency**. Mika had already told Takina this after Clarisse left. The father’s claim that he had arranged it long beforehand is false. Clarisse went smiling and let everyone think she believed him. Whether she privately did stays open (10.933–951). Chisato leaves the family their private outing and later says that if she goes, she would like to go when Takina can go too. Takina had been sent for a medical examination. Mika also attributes Madame’s reluctance to kill children to her own bereavement. The attribution is Mika’s testimony (10.952–977).

The car scene shows two attitudes to what a child should be told. The narration, focalized through Takina, has the original pretense told to Clarisse without naming the teller; the default reading is Takina (10.845). When Clarisse concludes that she was underestimated, Takina says her follow-through was weak and she should be thorough next time. Chisato tells her not to teach the girl such things (10.846–848). When Clarisse proposes doing it properly next time, Chisato and Mizuki answer together ちゃんとすんな, and Takina laughs a little (10.872–875). Chisato says she began to doubt the official story when Madame’s first shots went at the two guards rather than the client (10.850–861). Takina privately concludes that Werewolf is not a game for her (10.862–868). Once Takina has objected that the name proves nothing about present care, Mizuki tells her to keep quiet. Takina says Chisato’s face shows she knew the argument did not hold, and Clarisse sides with Takina. Chisato answers with the proverb 噓も方便 and, asked about the Latin, says that part is true. The narration calls this giving herself away (10.900–921). The concession is implied, not stated, and its deliberateness rests on Takina’s reading of her face and her flustered half-denial (10.906–908). Chisato’s earlier 親心 account of the tail was a hypothesis she held sincerely (9.717, 10.844). The adults keep convenient stories too. Mika’s verdict that Chisato was broadly right comes after the concession, and Akutsu tells Clarisse the park was long planned. Mika tells Takina the truth only after Clarisse has gone (10.925–931, 10.946–948).

Chisato gives three reasons for declining the park. Takina was at the hospital, Chisato would not intrude on a family’s time together, and Takina had worked hardest (10.935, 10.941, 10.953). Takina thinks this unnecessary and is still not displeased that Chisato did it for her sake (10.960). After Mika’s account of Madame’s bereavement, Takina rereads Madame’s words as past tense. The rereading construes a conditional (10.285) together with 10.289 and 10.761. Madame’s own words are consistent with the bereavement without stating it (10.967–970). Takina reflects that everyone sees the world only from their own point of view (10.982). When Mika phones a group of regulars to come for a rematch, Takina remarks that an overindulgent parent is questionable. Chisato replies that as a daughter she finds it ideal and calls him 先生 with a heart (10.1000–1005). Takina decides to play instead of moderating. Her reason is her own verdict that Chisato, the strong Werewolf player, saw through the day first (10.1008–1009). Chisato’s account is narrower. She believed the tail was Akutsu’s and first doubted when Madame’s opening shots went at the guards, and the truth reached her through Clarisse’s confession (10.710, 10.844, 10.850). When Chisato claims to be the seer again, Takina proposes to ignore her (10.1012–1014).

At the next game Takina leaves the moderator’s place for a player’s. People strike her as complicated, troublesome and interesting, and she laughs (10.982–1018). She has deliberately given up a position from which the distribution of roles would have been known.

### After Meal 1

Takina’s idea for a meal the shop might sell produces a distinction between eating what one needs and choosing a café for sweets or coffee when it is possible to go elsewhere. Mika values that freedom to choose. Chisato hears Takina refer to the shop as うち, “our place,” after hours. She tells her she is glad. Takina thinks she has probably said it before, feels embarrassed and entertains an explanation for why Kurumi might use that word differently (11.15–59). The remark establishes that Chisato wants to hear it and that Takina is affected by her response. It cannot date Takina’s first sense of belonging or disclose Kurumi’s private feelings.

The scene opens with care that Takina tries to refuse. Chisato takes the cleaning cloth from her because of her nails and calls her hands too pretty to waste. Takina thinks that people who handle guns cannot keep pretty hands and that her moderate hand care is enough for a Lycoris (11.5–11). Her question about food comes from commercial reasoning. Pasta and sandwiches would bring new customers and profit, and a Japanese meal might have settled the Clarisse problem (11.18–19). Kurumi predicts that food would bring Mizuki’s drinking snacks and then an izakaya. Takina admits to herself that she would probably help, since an izakaya earns more per customer, and catches herself already working it out (11.24–27). Mika’s answer reverses her criterion. Coffee and sweets are unnecessary, so whoever comes for them has chosen the shop (11.31). Takina follows the thought to its end. A place people could do without and still want is one that is loved (11.33–41). She then wonders whether Chisato shares the idea or whether it was Chisato’s in the first place (11.43–44). In this book Takina’s first うち for the café is 11.15. Chisato herself had called the café うち on the morning of the job and Takina うちのたきな in the duel (9.251, 9.704, 10.564). Takina’s embarrassment ends in a joke at Chisato’s expense, since the words came from someone reeking of garlic (11.59). The illustration shows Takina blushing with plasters on her cheek. ### 2 · フキの恩返し

The first scene shows the consequence before it reveals the telling. Sakura warns Fuki about breathing in the cold warehouse. Fuki dismisses her, then finds herself unable to breathe or voice the praise she owes while Sakura supports her. Only afterward does the narrative move to the park and disclose that Fuki has been telling yesterday’s story to Chisato (12.61–89, 12.169–223). She wants to thank Sakura. She also has something to apologize for. Fuki is competent at owning an error before a superior, even one someone under her made. She has a harder time apologizing to peers and juniors (12.220–222). Asking her former partner how to make a good meal is a practical way to begin (12.213–277).

The warehouse prologue defines the debt precisely. Kusunoki sends in Fuki and Sakura alone, and Fuki infers that the commander wants speed and few losses (12.2–24). Fuki values Sakura as a partner who follows without needing instructions (12.27–28). Sakura’s warning about breathing is cut off by Fuki’s 話は後だ (12.84–88). Fuki outruns her, freezes her own airways with a deep breath, survives a shotgun ambush and an exchange of head-butts, and is about to be shot by a frightened old man when Sakura kills him (12.114–172). Fuki realizes that she had left Sakura behind out of overconfidence. She thinks 大した奴だ and loses consciousness before she can say it (12.175–177). The frame then reveals that Fuki, on a day off for a medical check, has summoned Chisato to a bench in Kinshi Park (12.179–194). The narration calls Chisato her former partner (12.187).

The exchange between them is a long rivalry carried in bodies and banter. Chisato reaches for Fuki’s famously hard forehead and is slapped away. They argue over a bunk-bed accident that Fuki remembers and dates to ten years back, and that Chisato claims not to remember (12.201–211). Fuki’s seated barrage of blows is parried throughout and stops only when Chisato points out students watching from the gymnasium (12.195–238). The first S12 illustration draws their mock-thug exchange of glares (12.243–252). Chisato’s じゃ、フキの恩返しってわけね gives the story its title as a gibe (12.224). The narrator explains why Fuki says 礼 rather than 謝罪. She will bow to superiors and even take the blame for comrades’ errors, but she resists apologizing to peers and juniors, and does not know herself whether pride or the duty of leading is responsible. Calling it thanks lets her reconcile the two half-consciously (12.220–222). Fuki comes to Chisato because her sources on monjayaki disagreed and Chisato, by Fuki’s count, has lived outside for ten years. When Chisato teases her about falling behind inside DA, Fuki privately concedes that Lycoris lag behind the world (12.386–391). Fuki has never eaten the dish herself (12.273). Chisato favors a squid-ink monjayaki at a restaurant that opens at the hour Fuki must leave for curfew. Fuki rejects anything she cannot reproduce in the dormitory (12.291–304).

At the closed café Fuki panics at the prospect of meeting Mika, whom she calls 先生, until she learns he is out on a neighborhood cleanup. Chisato had used Fuki’s summons to escape it (12.318–337). Before cooking, Fuki runs a finger along the polished edge of the sink and remembers that the desk of “that person” was always as tidy when he taught her (12.341–343). A small figure, in fact Kurumi, peeks in, and Fuki never learns who it is. Chisato calls it the house’s 座敷童, who probably does not want to meet “you lot,” which Fuki guesses means DA or the Lycoris (12.425–428). Told that the dike in the batter is the dish’s 醍醐味, Fuki refuses to accept that as an explanation (12.417–421, 12.472–473). Tasting, she reaches unconsciously for a second bite, flushes at Chisato’s smug face and says ……うめぇよ (12.452–463).

Cooking brings its own training. Fuki and Chisato sometimes arrive at the same thought. Fuki conjectures that sharing so much of childhood accounts for it. She can make the monjayaki by a fourth practice round and still wants to look things up. At the dorm she gets permission for a hotplate, sets it at the one table within reach of a socket, and has Sakura come after afternoon training. She expressly calls the food an afternoon snack. She has assumed that for someone from Hokkaido this would be a first taste (12.347–364, 12.465–498, 12.519–535).

Fuki does tell Sakura that she appreciated the rescue and should have listened. Her attempt to finish apologizing for that error is interrupted at **すま──**. The narration revisits the fact that the apology was only beginning to come out (12.549–570). Learning that Sakura had monjayaki at a restaurant the day before makes Fuki feel foolish. Erika almost exposes what the others hoped to smooth over. Hibana manages the awkward moment and subsequently apologizes simply because she wants to say it. That matters to Fuki. The person she finds pitiable is herself. She hates that the others are having to spare her feelings over a repayment that might have been an imposition. She would like to tell the anxious Sakura that she is blameless and cannot get those words out. She does apologize aloud about serving the food two days in a row and retracts her own bitter remark (12.607–685).

Earlier in the dormitory scene there are signs that Fuki misses until later. Sakura hesitates when told she cannot know how monjayaki is made. She says やっぱ when the snack noodles go in, and she laughs only after a pause when Fuki is glad the dish is a novelty (12.497–505, 12.542–544). Fuki explains the dike from library research. She prefers the theory that it kept each child’s batter from mixing with another’s, so that children could eat together and go off to play (12.520–523). When Sakura happily takes her for a fruit-milk drinker, Fuki is irritated with herself for repeating Chisato’s analogy and says she drinks plain milk (12.513–518). After Sakura burns her mouth, Fuki recognizes that she had wanted to apologize all along and now has no wish to go on (12.564–566). Sakura names them 相棒 and says no thanks are needed (12.567). Fuki later realizes she could not have asked about Sakura’s day, having avoided talk the night before rather than admit training with Chisato (12.633–636).

The turn from anger to apology happens within a few lines. Fuki says she is sorry for serving the dish two days running, then hears herself say 言えよ and recognizes that she is about to take her mood out on Sakura. She turns it into a request to be told next time and privately congratulates herself (12.637–646). Hibana’s apology, offered only because she wanted to say it, stops Fuki at the kitchen door. It makes her ask whether her own thanks and apology were also only things she wanted to say (12.653–662). She withdraws at once a sharper remark about having fed Sakura an amateur’s dish the day after a restaurant’s. She then despises herself and takes it out on the cabbage (12.681–687).

Sakura gives the two meals different values. Yesterday’s restaurant tasted good and eating there with the others was fun. Having her senior make today’s meal for her makes her *glad*. Fuki credits the look in her eyes. She finds herself wishing partners could read each other’s minds, then rejects the temptation to skip the work. She returns with ingredients for four, and the narrative leaves Sakura laughing. Fuki can trust what Sakura said without possessing her complete private verdict (12.686–749).

Sakura’s distinction sits beside real pleasure in the previous day. The restaurant was good, an old comrade had come, and the struggle of first-timers was fun. Fuki’s dish, like home cooking against a shop’s, made her glad (12.690–696). Fuki analyzes why the word works. 嬉しい declines the comparison that politeness would otherwise have forced, and Sakura’s straight look, which Fuki likens to a dog looking at its owner, lets her believe it (12.706–710). Being outplayed feels good (12.713–714). Her wish that a partner could read her mind ends close to where Takina ends the first story. One has to say things, and say them well, and people are troublesome (12.725–733; cf. 10.985). She laughs alone at how simple she is (12.734–736). The second round is an Italian monjayaki for four, cooked by Fuki (12.740–742). The second S12 illustration, Fuki in apron and headscarf holding a knife with a startled Sakura behind her, belongs to the moment the snack is announced (12.484–495).

### 3 · ジ・アンリマーカブル・ナイト・イン・リコリコ

The long job that puts the pair in the shop late required neither girl to fire. Baths, hair, the order of sleeping and what they might eat receive more space than that assignment. Chisato offers Takina the first bath because her hair takes longer to dry. Takina recognizes a labored pun and declines to reward the setup. She describes what she learned about hair care in Kyoto and can imagine long hair on Chisato. Chisato’s practical reason for keeping it short makes Takina hesitate (14.35–58, 14.112–138).

Both head for the café half-unconsciously, each knowing the other means to sleep there (14.8–9). Takina notes that Chisato, whose pistol holds six rounds, stores empty magazines and loads them just before use (14.25–32). Each has a name-tagged overnight bag in the closet (14.33). Being looked after over the bath makes Takina feel diffident (気が引ける, 14.42). Her refusal of the pun is principled: being waited for feels like walking into a trap, and Chisato considers this and agrees (14.47–56). Takina finds baths tedious beforehand and never regrets them afterward (14.60–64). Overhearing Kurumi humiliate a braggart hacker, she worries that Walnut, who faked death for safety and freedom, might be exposed as alive (14.84–87). She dries her hair outside the changing room so that Chisato can bathe undisturbed (14.101–106). Her compliment on Chisato’s hair is sincere. She thinks it would be beautiful long (14.121–124). Chisato says only that dodging bullets is the problem. The mechanism, moving before the shot, and the frayed ends Takina has often seen are Takina’s reasoning and memory (14.126–133). When Chisato says she longs for hair long enough to braid, Takina cannot find words. When Chisato brightens her tone, Takina guesses she has sensed the awkwardness (14.134–137).

When Chisato asks to dry her hair, Takina twice says no. Being partners has no bearing on the request, she says. Chisato shakes her shoulders and persists. Takina hands over the dryer because continued refusal is troublesome and wastes time. The actual handling is gentler and more attentive than she expected. The strokes feel like being patted and praised. She feels ticklish in body and mind, embarrassed, conscious of blushing and inclined to hide her face (14.139–162). The drying stops when Chisato raises the question of food (14.163–179). Takina sends her to bathe, finishes her own hair, then notices a difference between doing it herself and having Chisato do it (14.218–242). Later she dries Chisato’s hair. Chisato calls this wordless understanding. Takina points out how unmistakable the cue was (14.259–276). The pleasure in this intimacy is a possible reading of the scene. The narrated feelings are more specific, and what happens after she yields cannot be used as evidence that she had privately assented from the start.

The details sharpen both sides of the exchange. Takina answers the offer only after a long silence, asking why, and Chisato says she wants to try (14.139–141). Takina’s second refusal answers Chisato’s appeal to their partnership: 相棒は関係ない (14.143–145). Under the dryer she hopes her blush will pass for the bath’s heat (14.160–162). Chisato’s whispered これからどうしようか makes Takina wonder whether she means to restyle her hair. She means dinner (14.163–170). Once Chisato has gone to the bath, Takina compares herself to someone keeping a dog and notes that at play Chisato is nothing like her ferocity at work (14.234–235). She registers the difference in drying by stroking her own head (14.241–242). The reciprocal drying is prompted by Chisato’s sidelong looks from the futon. Takina asks whether she needs hair oil and says she uses it every day unless something prevents her. Chisato, visibly happy and swaying from side to side, is hard to dry (14.259–276). The first S14 illustration shows both with eyes closed and Takina composed. The narrated embarrassment is not in the picture.

Each proposal to go to sleep generates a more vivid account of what the hungry pair could eat instead. Takina has tastes of her own before Chisato begins the sales pitch. When her stomach audibly growls, Chisato pretends to sleep long enough for Takina to realize what she is sparing her. Kurumi finally suggests that, if eating before bed is the objection, they could stay awake. Chisato seizes the suggestion and proposes taking turns napping the next day. Takina objects on grounds of work and an emergency call, then finds herself reasoning that low blood sugar might make eating necessary. Chisato offers her the option of sleeping. The prospect of adding roast beef changes what happens, and Takina says out loud that she will eat (14.244–329, 14.340–386, 14.398–486). The proposed rota has a speaker. Only its first step is reported: after the party Takina goes to the back room saying she has reached her limit (14.682, 15.5–7). The swap never comes, because Chisato lies down too (15.8–10, 15.23).

The appetite argument has two explicit positions. Takina says Lycoris night meals supply only what a mission needs (14.183; cf. 13.15). Chisato counters that a life has only so many meals (14.196). Inside this volume that is a stock phrase in a comic argument. Takina’s tastes come first all the same. Asked about breakfast, she plans rice, miso soup, pickles, natto, good nori and grilled salmon (14.302). When Chisato suggests congee, Takina is embarrassed to think she alone was greedy, until Chisato names a Chinese congee shop near Kinshichō station (14.307–315). Takina has chosen the herbal YAKUZEN bowl ever since she first ate it, in her recorded case as takeout, though she has tasted the others from Chisato and the rest (14.314). The pine nuts please her and the low calories reassure her (14.328–334). The shop opens at 10:30, so Chisato has roused her appetite for nothing, and Takina sits up to protest (14.338–344). Chisato’s real wish, fried dough dunked in congee, makes Takina writhe at the remembered pleasure (14.351–358). Takina swallows a complaint she knows would be unfair. When her stomach growls and Chisato feigns sleep, Takina asks her to laugh instead. Chisato asks her not to be angry and reaches an arm into Takina’s bedding, and Takina tells her not to come into her futon (14.364–388). Kurumi’s light and Kurumi’s pizza interrupt (14.389–399).

Kurumi’s 寝なきゃいいだろ sets off a mock-philosophical spiral in Takina’s sleepy mind (14.411–430). Chisato’s teasing 一緒に? during the rota proposal leaves Takina flustered (14.448–455). Chisato strokes her head, says she was half joking and offers to move the bedding so that they can sleep. Takina is relieved and reflects that a First, whom everyone looks up to, would never sanction such indulgence (14.460–470). Kurumi’s roast beef ends that. Chisato tells Takina to go to bed alone because she will settle the matter here, and answers Takina’s paraphrase with イエス・アイ・ドゥ。アンド・ユー? (14.478–483). Takina’s わたしも食べます is reported as something she could only say with a sigh (14.485–486).

Kurumi had acquired this beef with points accumulated through shopping. Chisato adds cheese and Kurumi suggests roast beef. Takina makes salad as well, and her account of why the pizza succeeds gives the pumpkin, sauce and texture of the thin slices specific jobs. They all reach for more pizza before the salad. Takina herself proposes watching *Gremlins*. Asked to whom she is doing wrong, she identifies the person she will be tomorrow. She agrees with Chisato that the decision was hers. Knowing the cost is precisely what unsettles her (14.493–569, 14.579–599, 14.626–665).

In the kitchen Takina’s scruples and skills run together. She dislikes the waste of thawing under running water while knowing the cost is trivial. She generalizes that growing up means accepting the gap between one’s ideal and one’s actual position (14.508–516). The same thought lets her understand why the self-directed Chisato would find DA uncomfortable to stay in (居づらい). That understanding is Takina’s, not an account of why Chisato left. She wonders whether keeping Chisato company has steeped her in ordinary life as well (14.517–522; cf. 9.835). She says the salad partly eases their guilt, and Chisato’s brief, suppressed answer tells her that Chisato is not wholly at peace either (14.533–535). She defers to Kurumi on thin slices, hones the knife and cuts the beef half-frozen. Noticing that she would normally have asked for a thickness in millimeters, she takes the omission as a sign of fatigue (14.540–565). Her tasting credits each person’s part. Chisato’s extra cheese and Kurumi’s beef made the slice strong, and the pumpkin balanced it by luck (14.621–633). She has never seen *Gremlins* and is puzzled by the others’ hesitation over its gory scene (14.199–200, 14.582–595). Chisato lies on the quilt smiling at her sulkiness (14.666). The second S14 illustration catches Chisato drooling and Takina looking sidelong at the moment the roast beef is mentioned.

They watch both films. The café’s work and the birthday party for a regular’s five-year-old child (14.10) go ahead, and Chisato and Takina are found asleep together. When Mika says that a day like this is occasionally fine, Kurumi warns that spoiling children can become a habit *for the parent*. She goes back to her own room, listens to the sleepers and strikes her keys a little more gently (14.675–700). Her own softening is carried by that last description. Nothing requires her to withdraw her objection to Mika.

The coda is Kurumi’s. Mika reports that Takina went to the back room saying she had reached her limit (14.682). Kurumi finds the two asleep, each wrapped like a cocoon in a different half of the one set of bedding. She predicts heavy stomachs and self-reproach from people who ate pizza, a party meal and cake before sleeping (14.684–689). Her 親の方がだ echoes Takina’s earlier remark about an overindulgent parent (10.1002). Mika falls silent with the face of a dog whose treat has been taken away (14.694–697).

### After Meal 3 and 満腹の後に

After they have overslept, Takina sits up and tries to reconstruct how the two could have fought over the bedding. The imagined reconstruction cannot verify the movements in their sleep. Another meal is on offer. This A5 beef comes from a party guest, separately from the roast beef Kurumi obtained through her shopping points the night before. The prospect of dinner helps postpone restraint once more. At 15.44–47 Takina makes the dieting resolution and Chisato offers to join her (15.7–12, 15.25–47).

Chisato’s half-awake words confirm only that she had lain down beside Takina (15.23). Takina pushes away Chisato’s sleeping attempt to use her lap as a pillow (15.14). When Mika looks in, she apologizes and says she can work (15.20). Dinner turns out to be sukiyaki. Chisato says they are not of an age to get excited about it, until Mika mentions A5 Wagyu given as thanks for the party (15.32–34). Both know the danger, having eaten A5 the night before, and Chisato yields first with ……食べるか (15.35–39).

In the final course the adults have brandy for their tea, and everyone gets a spoon. Takina recognizes Russian tea but gives the wrong account of what the present service requires. Kurumi says the jam is tasted separately and Mika confirms it. Eating and drinking in succession lets Chisato experience how the aroma and strength of the sweetness change. Her historical explanation for the custom is her own inference. Mika says the diners can adjust what they take to their appetites. He made the jam himself and still offers the flavor Chisato has not tasted. **Illustrations and paratext.** These add framing but no narrative facts. The color spread of three staff members in work kimono illustrates no narrated scene. The character page and the jacket blurb belong to the publisher. The blurb calls Fuki and Chisato 犬猿の仲 and presents the stories as newly written, which the S20 credit qualifies. Where a picture differs from the prose, the prose governs. Two examples are the composed Takina of the hair-drying illustration and the phone she holds where 9.12 has a stopwatch.

On this record I accept the expanded reading for transfer to the integrator.
