# Ordinary days · prose reading

A regular can enter LycoReco, be recognized and still misread what happens there. Tokuda notices the silence that falls when he mentions a job, then laughs off his own remarkably accurate suspicion. Takina smiles at Doi when he says he will try more sweets. Chisato persuades him to read romantic interest into her attentiveness. Kana sees the partners turn their backs precisely when she most wants to catch their eyes. Each visitor really has a place in the café. Belonging gives none of them a complete view of another person. Asaura builds the volume around the difference, returning to incidents through people who saw other parts of them.

## Source-native narrative

### Five introductions and an exit

Introduction 1 is Tokuda’s throughout. He is twenty-eight, divorced and childless, a magazine writer who takes “any order” for little money (9.2–4). His pitch is unusual for him. He wants the small satisfaction of having found and introduced the shop, not extra pay (9.9–11). At 9.12–36 Tokuda reads the area north of Kinshichō station through a reporter’s sense of what a family might enjoy. The centre of the counter is his favourite seat, and Mika has offered him “いつもの” only since about the previous week (9.29–33). What he sees is accurate in detail and wrong in frame. He notices that only Chisato calls Mika 先生 and cannot explain it (9.95). When Chisato guesses that Tokuda has come about “work” and he confirms it, the room stills, Kurumi included, until he explains he wants to cover the shop (9.96–112). He senses a place that takes other kinds of job, then dismisses the idea as something out of a manga (9.111–112, 9.146–147). He supposes Mizuki really runs the shop because Mika’s leg is bad (9.41), and she loses interest in him once he admits he is freelance (9.113–118). Kurumi rebukes her prying with “デリカシーがないぞ” (9.55).

Mika declines the publicity and offers a freshly made monaka (9.126–178). Mika keeps the shop out of the magazine and makes Tokuda welcome as a customer. The refusal is shared out. Chisato wants the feature, plans a salon booking and argues for business, fans across the country and her own “人生の満足度” (9.120–135). Kurumi says a popular shop only makes their work harder (9.133–136). Mika gives a policy Tokuda can accept. The shop takes no interviews and wants to stay quiet, and its social-media account exists for the regulars and because “あの子がやりたがる” (9.138–143). Mizuki says the main reason is something else, and Mika does not answer (9.144–145). The monaka come as an apology, and Mika’s question whether Tokuda will stop coming shows what the refusal is meant to keep (9.153–156). Tokuda guesses that the stick shape was a woman’s idea. The book never says whose (9.167). The other visits test what a reporter might reasonably discover.

Introduction 2 belongs to Bulldog. His memories of his grandmother and good coffee grow out of his dissatisfaction with the canned coffee his employer supplies (11.4–21). She told him from her sickbed that good coffee has magic and that happiness waits at its end (11.12–14). Mika uses nearly the same words when he mentions a country where people say good coffee holds magic (10.446). Chisato later repeats the saying to Kana and credits it to Mika (18.842). Neither of them knows Bulldog’s version. Bulldog admires his employer’s nerve in calling himself アジア人 (11.26–30). On the monitor he sees a man of about twenty with a gentle face and blond-and-black hair, whose small group sells through a special “herbal tea” route (11.33–36). His expectations about the people who hired him and the people coming for them are an employee’s reconstruction (11.31–48). The rumor that dealing groups vanish without arrests or bodies is underworld legend, and the report that the attackers are cute girls sounds to him like a drugged fantasy (11.38–41, 11.66–69). That earlier intimacy gives his later request to Chisato a history that she never hears.

Introduction 3 finds the feature alive in another form. It is now an issue on Kinshichō and Kameido for women, with Tokuda as main writer of a ten-page café section (13.5–7). Mika tells Tokuda about sweets and coffee and steers around his former employment (13.5–21). Asked privately why the shop is Japanese in style, he says it is his own taste. A former workplace that valued old Japanese tradition always served the best wagashi, and he prefers coffee to tea (13.15). When Tokuda asks what that workplace was, Mika turns away and smiles. Tokuda, now a regular, reads the smile as “no comment” (13.16–19, 13.48). Mika recommends the hot cakes at a nearby coffee shop, Tomy, and Chisato says she loves them (13.20–23). The takoyaki is Chisato’s turn at staff lunch, cooked on a bargain griddle from Orimoto’s recycling shop (13.24–29). A takoyaki party gives the regulars something delicious and gives the shop a smell that overpowers the coffee. Takina’s one concern is that smell, and Mika sends her to eat (13.41–43). Tokuda actually burns his mouth. His pleasure survives the discomfort (13.23–72). Mika says that unusual staff meals happen only on Chisato’s turn, then qualifies that for the others (13.46).

The fourth introduction opens as an armed stand-off seen by a man unsure whether he can still handle a gun (15.2–9). It delays identifying the woman behind the camera. She is Itō, the manga artist, photographing the armed poses as creative reference for a series whose protagonist is modeled on Chisato (15.30–36). Takina speaks the rival’s lines and Chisato the hero’s, and five customers, Kana among them, cover Chisato with Mika and his shotgun (15.17–27). Chisato alone corrects someone else. Takina names Tokuda’s grip “カップ＆ソーサ”, a habit he picked up with an airsoft gun in his late teens (15.39–50). Asked why she knows guns, Chisato gives a laugh that sounds false to Tokuda and credits films (15.53–56). Talk of film collections and borrowed discs is genuine. Chisato has just bought the *Alien* box set and says a disc makes a film one’s own and easy to lend (15.59–64). Takina pities her earlier self for watching the loaned films with notes as if they were a mission or drill, and she still calls the loans a nuisance (15.66–72). So is the talk about work after dark. Takina declines an evening screening because tonight is ordinary work. Chisato tells Kana that the two of them do “裏のお仕事” at night, and Kurumi corrects the men’s unspoken guess with “ゴミ掃除…日本のな” (15.84–94). Tokuda studies a picture of Mika crouched behind the counter with only the shotgun and his eyes showing, and guesses that Mika grew up abroad among guns (15.96–104). Chisato asks Mika for “いつもの” before night work, and Mika describes deep-roasted beans ground very fine (15.106–110). The witnesses have different access even in a single group portrait.

The fifth introduction supplies a less comfortable test. Kana has been a regular since about the time Tokuda became one (17.39). The adults treat her to her first espresso, and Mika adds vanilla ice cream because she dislikes bitterness. She hesitates to pour hot coffee over cold ice cream, and Mika tells her that is how it is done (17.41–48). An affogato tastes best at a particular stage of melting. At the café Kana plays the cheerful junior and calls Itō 伊藤先生 (17.67–70). When Itō recognizes Kana’s school uniform and presses for her reason for visiting, Tokuda notices Kana recoil while Itō is still looking at her drawing tablet (17.10, 17.74–81). Chisato’s arrival revives the room. Tokuda answers her question about his work with “良くはないかな。いろいろとね” while looking back at Kana. He sees her spoon has stopped while the ice cream continues to melt (17.36–61, 17.73–91). The pause belongs to one customer, easily lost in the group’s relief.

The printed magazine brings the frame back to Tokuda. He arrives in high summer to a scene he cannot read. Doi is laughing like a broken toy, Chisato is prostrate, and Mika and the regulars look down. An irritated Takina says it is nothing, knows his usual and passes it to 店長 (19.2, 19.11–17). Kurumi silences Doi and calls the day ordinary (19.21–26). Tokuda’s “例のブツ” is the issue Chisato had asked after (18.19–22, 19.28). The photographs feature the girls at other cafés, from a shoot arranged at her request (19.39–42, 22.25, 22.32). She calls them a treasure (19.46). An untagged voice in Takina’s register asks whether that is how the photographs are used (19.41). Kurumi wonders how permission was granted, and Mika complains that he has told them not to stand out (19.44, 19.50). Tokuda is glad the regulars love the spread and hurt that nobody reads his words. He still has a little of the wish to be the one who discovered LycoReco, though he now treasures being able to come here himself. His 世界, “world,” carries the reading カフェ at 19.62. Mika answers with his own wish that this ordinary daily life continue, and serves cold-brew coffee over coffee ice (19.65–66). As the bell rings someone else may be about to become a regular, and Chisato and Takina go to greet them (19.28–78). His change of heart allows for both professional vanity and genuine attachment. The spread that follows shows four of the staff greeting at night, Mika not among them (S20).

### 1 · 閉じ行く人生にスイーツを

Doi feels conspicuous on arrival, a man of fifty-five who supposes the possibilities of his life are closing. The story opens through him on the afternoon of Introduction 1. The young man eating monaka at the counter is unnamed here and very probably Tokuda (10.7, 10.37–38; 9.159–188). Doi takes the shop for a concept café and declines Takina’s “他にご注文は？” (10.9–28). He retired three years ago, fills his days with walks, films and drink, and is sure his life will not shine again (10.47–57). Recognition at the counter turns into invitations to walk, eat and talk. The ten-kilometre walk in his leather shoes, which Chisato proposes on hearing he lacks exercise, puts him in bed for three days. Chisato tells him their loafers could handle Hollywood gun action, a truth he takes as a joke (10.380). On later visits he is better equipped for an outing, looks forward to the next one and helps serve alongside the regulars when the staff are out (10.3–57, 10.211–213, 10.369–419). One outing is a sushi bar’s staff lunch, the meal Story 3 narrates from Chisato’s side (10.389, 14.73–167). He notices that a school uniform suits anywhere and makes its wearer unremarkable, without knowing he is describing a cover (10.392–397).

Chisato’s first section shows her habits before her theory. She is late after cooking rice a customer sent from Niigata with grilled salted mackerel, and after watching a newly arrived *Dynamite Police 2* disc and then the first film (10.62–66). She greets every regular in the full shop alike, from Kana to Gotō, and wears her red First uniform to work (10.79–86). Takina’s kimono is soaked because she caught Kurumi when Kurumi stumbled (10.88). Takina raises Doi in the changing room. He keeps coming, looks dark, orders only blend coffee and sits as if enduring something. She is not satisfied with Chisato’s relay of Mizuki’s guess about money. Chisato notices her puzzlement without asking about it (10.90–103). The romantic theory begins with Chisato’s report that Takina “let slip” 「土井さんが気になる」 (10.120). Those words do not occur at 10.90–98, and Chisato’s next sentence corrects her: it sounded more like worry about his gloomy face (10.128). Kurumi pulls up Doi’s records so casually that Mizuki likens it to checking the weather (10.144–150). Chisato rests the theory on Takina’s rare interest in other people. In her experience Takina notices only people with skills worth learning or strong enemies (10.203). Her model of what should happen next comes from dating games (10.561–562). Mika is not neutral. He says falling in love is no bad thing. His one focalized passage allows that a first love may be unknown even to the person who feels it, though he also warns against deciding too early (10.127, 10.141, 10.198, 10.293–297). Kurumi keeps steering the talk back to Takina and to what a café can do (10.188–193). Chisato counters with a working rule of her own: a good café worker tries to brighten a gloomy regular (10.189).

Mika urges Doi to think of time as scarce. Doi objects that these words might depress someone. Mika agrees that some people might give up, and asks what *this* man would do now (10.469–497). His advice depends on recognizing how much Doi has already changed. Mika also tells Doi that the outlook is Chisato’s way of living. She treasures every minute, and she has had her share of trouble (10.492–494). The book names no cause. Chisato’s earlier reading of Mika’s parental gaze similarly tells us how she receives it (10.158–183). Mika describes ageing as a curtain closing on possibility. Chisato counts twenty years to an average lifespan and reminds him that he opened the café ten years ago and was bad at it at first (10.159–165). He falters when he begins to say that she will understand when she is older. She answers with a finger gun and a stage line (10.167–173). The narrator says he closes his eyes as if thinking of her fate (10.174). Chisato’s narrated thought gives her own account. He used to look at her while seeing something else as well. Now he looks at her alone, which she credits to their time together, “少なくとも、千束はそう思っている” (10.178–182). The passage withholds what else Mika used to see and why he falters.

The comic reversal gives the same advice a consequence Mika did not propose. Chisato’s plan has two aims from the start. She wants to advance what she takes to be Takina’s feeling and to give Doi something new that he will credit to Takina (10.266). Takina keeps asking Doi for his order although his blend is made before he sits. When Chisato suggests she stop, she answers “……仕事ですから” (10.214–220). Each time Chisato pulls her out, she names the cost, work, and goes (10.220, 10.280, 10.373). At lunch she suspects Chisato of angling for a free meal. Then she supplies the decisive recommendation from her own knowledge: Hana-inari by Kameido Tenjin, whose ume inari she likes (10.246, 10.271). Chisato’s narration is full of rules of manner (“ざる蕎麦につゆ”, 10.249–250). Seven of the sixteen inari bought for the evening are ume because Chisato chose them. Kurumi eats the plain one first because she starts with the standard (10.307–322). Takina is glad to see a customer in better spirits. Chisato takes the smile as evidence of romantic interest. Kurumi proposes asking what Takina thinks, then lets the smile give some credence to the group’s guess (10.335–355). The narration says why the smile misleads. Takina smiles like that after a good result at work, so Doi’s enjoyment counts as one (10.349–351). Nobody remembers the shopping that was the pretext for the outing (10.357).

Back from the night’s work, Takina restocks ammunition first. Chisato knows that is correct and would leave it, because she does not want to live constantly conscious of combat and trusts one magazine in an emergency (10.549–553). Chisato has already encouraged Doi to act on someone else’s supposed feelings (10.542–599). Her reasons are her own. It is better to act and fail than not to act, and Takina’s happiness would start Doi’s new life, a WIN-WIN. When she realizes she has said everything at once, she decides not to regret it, “それが錦木千束という生き方なのだ” (10.582–591). Her promise to herself, “おまかせあれ!!”, is the formula of Tokuda’s trade (10.601, 9.4). Kurumi’s section supplies what Chisato never learns. Asked by Mizuki, Takina answers in the present tense, 嫌いです (10.616). Her reasons are a seat held at busy times over one coffee, the lowest spend per customer, and a gloom that darkens the room. Yoneoka, by contrast, orders sweets and several espressos (10.620–629). Asked whether she saw Doi as a man, she asks whether he had been a woman (10.626). She then revises to the past tense, 嫌いでした. He has begun ordering sweets, and she expects to dislike him less (10.632). The change of tense records that the sweets changed her judgment. She disowns the outings as Chisato’s doing and asks what they were for. Nobody tells her (10.634–636). Kurumi concludes “千束が全部悪い” and keeps what she heard to herself (10.641–643). Chisato’s wink three weeks later implies the theory still stands (10.686). Doi imagines he can justify courting a teenager through precedent from period novels. He catches himself wondering whose interest that interpretation serves and nonetheless acts on it (10.648–674). At 10.688 he places his food order and adds a private dinner invitation. The chapter ends at 10.690 before Takina replies. Tokuda later arrives to find Doi laughing strangely, Chisato prostrate and Takina irritated (19.7–22). Their positions give the reader the direction of the joke while leaving her exact words unrecoverable. Takina’s commercial account is explicit. A concern for the man himself, which Chisato heard as worry, remains available beside it (10.97, 10.128, 10.343).

Doi also imagines the ohagi reflects the size of Takina’s hands. In another story Takina remembers doing the preparation, leaving before the finish, and expects the manager to shape it (10.525–537, 12.254–255). Doi is right that she worked on his food and in no position to identify whose hands gave it its finished form. The novel makes his gratitude intelligible even as it corrects his account. That morning Mika tells Doi the girls are away on “別件の仕事”. Doi guesses a coffee delivery or school and takes Kurumi and Mizuki to be out as well, while Kurumi has been supporting the pair from the closet (10.403–408, 10.554). Mika serves the ohagi set with a light-roast Mandheling American, because both began as beans (10.424–446). When the girls return, Chisato’s first request is a generous pack of the shop’s best beans for a new delivery order (10.523). The text names no recipient. Takina finds Doi’s empty plate, asks whether he liked them, smiles at his answer and promises more sweets (10.525–538). Her later account to Mizuki fits that pleasure (10.632).

Takina never focalizes in this story, and she keeps to polite service language with customers. Chisato varies hers with mock formality (“たきなさん”, “～のかね、ワトソン君”) and the stock “おまかせあれ”. Doi calls the girls “たきなちゃん” and “千束ちゃん” and Mika “マスター” (10.216, 10.576). The three eye-contact lines at 10.346–348 are untagged and can be assigned only by register. Kurumi mentions that Otome Sakura visits the café now and then (10.113). The illustrations show Takina flat-faced in twin tails under Chisato’s sidelong smile, and Chisato and Kurumi eating the inari after hours (i-037, i-056).

### 2 · ガンファイトとコーヒーと千束の赤いアレ

The story is Takina’s from its first sentence, and so is its technical knowledge. The account of the uniform, loafers and satchel, of Chisato’s spiked compensator as a striking tool, and of the red frangible rounds and the stance built around them, is what Takina knows, has seen or infers. She offers two explanations of Chisato’s two-eyed aim, and neither is confirmed (12.2–12, 12.78–87). Chisato’s first decision concerns time. She refuses Kurumi’s offer to sacrifice a drone, first on cost and then with “時間の話だよ”, and she releases a stun grenade’s lever before throwing it (12.30–36). Takina judges that Chisato never kills but goes to the edge of it without mercy, as when Chisato shoots a downed man in the nape (12.61–62). Takina measures herself against Chisato and against Fuki, who once looked after her. Her speed has risen under Chisato’s pull, but she feels she lacks some inhuman quality (12.64–72). When Takina asks for calmer conduct in case of a mistake, Chisato answers that Takina will be there (12.73–74).

Takina does not initially understand Chisato’s chosen method, challenges it and resents having to wait for an explanation. She says live rounds would end each fight in one shot. Chisato answers “私は殺さないやり方がいーの”, promises “いつかわかるよ” and says she likes to delay a reveal. Takina is irritated (12.77–93). When Takina adds that Chisato could disable people without killing even with live rounds, Chisato hands that job to Takina as their division of labor (12.94–95). Chisato has an ordinary reason for wanting to finish quickly. She hopes to watch an earlier zombie movie with Takina before café opening, the first film of the series whose sequel they saw with Doi (12.88–105). Takina’s “違います” ends Chisato’s teasing about wanting more time with her, and Chisato correctly guesses Takina’s next thought (12.98–105).

Against Bulldog each partner keeps her own method. Takina’s 9 mm subsonic rounds cannot pierce his armor, and she proposes withdrawal as the rational course. When Chisato decides to charge, she concedes and covers her (12.134–148). Her earnest question whether Bulldog is Kurumi’s acquaintance misses a joke (12.128–130). Chisato notices that he does not advance, waits out the smoke and counts the rounds left in his machine gun (12.141–163). This fight is the book’s only scene in which Takina fires her lethal-class rounds at a person. Her first shots strike his armored abdomen and head without effect. She then shoots at the machine gun and keeps firing at the drum as she runs, though a hit is unlikely (12.171–183). When he draws a knife on Chisato, Takina cries her name, drops into a sliding kneel and empties her magazine into the blade. It shatters, his fingers are torn off, and the fight’s only real blood flows (12.197–199). She then shoots his hip joints so that he cannot rise (12.204). Her fire maims his weapon hand to protect Chisato. It kills no one. Her treatment of Bulldog brings work and hospitality into the same moment. After serious injury, Chisato tells him gently, in English, that he must not die. His question whether holding on earns a reward leads to her offer, his request for good coffee and her “おまかせあれ” (12.202–214). The text passes straight from the promise to more painful force, a full magazine into his mask, and then the next scene (12.215–217). Whether he actually receives the coffee is open (11.4–21, 12.202–217). The bean order Chisato places on reaching the café names no recipient and cannot settle it (10.523).

Takina does not dislike the way the defeated guard stood in the way for someone else. She reads him as a decoy ready to die to buy time (12.229–232). Kurumi apologizes at once for missing the vent route. Chisato is delighted that a film-lover’s dream escape turned out to be real (12.219–228). When the target escapes, Takina notes that pursuit ends the hope of an early return and suggests leaving it to the police. Chisato refuses because he may be armed. She explains that a live capture with little collateral harm is what makes the pair particularly valuable for this commission (12.233–242). Takina takes this as the reveal Chisato had delayed, and the narration calls it one of Chisato’s reasons, not all of them (12.243–244). Takina sees the benefit for tracing the drug distribution route and infers why DA holds on to Chisato. She accepts it for now. Chisato resolves to pursue, and Takina calls off their movie plan (12.245–255). Takina can appreciate live capture and still describe herself as armed with lethal ammunition on the train. Her judgment here has a history and a limit.

The fugitive, who calls himself アジア人, sees a timid-looking schoolgirl, is relieved when she gets off and relaxes again when the next two passengers start talking about zombie films. He has pushed his six-shot pistol into the seat (12.289–298). He misjudges his position, assuming an unofficial enemy cannot trace him (12.276–278). He is tense when the two schoolgirls board, relaxes at their film talk and learns who they are only when they flank him (12.299–311). His interest in the discussion is real before he realizes the pair have him under guard. Chisato wants zombie films lit well enough to see. Takina defends darkness as a trigger of instinctive fear and calls Chisato’s view greed (12.301–306). Chisato addresses him by his chosen name (12.309). Takina warns that her full-metal-jacket rounds will be fired only if Chisato fails, in the same calm tone as the film talk (12.315–316). He carries on afterward, offering the action/horror distinction that the partners had missed (12.290–319). For a few minutes he has something to teach the people detaining him. The change of focalizer grants him a taste of his own and the audience a glimpse of Kana before either understands what the other has left in that carriage.

### 3 · Takina’s cooking

The first complaint about Takina’s cooking concerns the time spent in the kitchen while customers need service. Chisato focalizes the first two sections and Takina the third. The staff-lunch rota is described from Chisato’s side. Mika is plain and quick. Chisato is adventurous. Mizuki cooks for drink and keeps the surplus. Kurumi orders snacks or frozen food (14.4–9). Takina has been at the café for several months (14.3). She has shown she can cook with time to spare. Chisato remembers her simmered taro, homemade daikon-leaf furikake, miso soup and hijiki (14.37). Her efficient substitute, a protein shake taken (“摂取”) between jobs, makes sense against the criticism she actually received (14.19–31, 14.42–45). Chisato notices that the shake tastes better than the protein she usually drinks (14.28). Mika recognizes that Takina is following Mizuki’s complaint to the letter and that nobody explained what a staff meal should offer the people eating it (14.39, 14.59). Chisato’s own diagnosis is that Takina is too earnest to find a point where something is good enough. By the stated requirements, she sees, the shake is correct, and only shared taste rules it out (14.53–65). Showing her a meal elsewhere supplies that missing criterion. Chisato also takes the opportunity to advance her theory about Doi (14.69–100, 14.169–187). A helpful invitation can serve a second, mistaken purpose.

Takina’s palate is the surprise of the sushi-bar lunch. Chisato silently guesses yellowtail. Takina names sea bream, gently teases Chisato’s bluff about the hiramasa and then concedes “正解という事で” (14.111–132). She identifies the tempura as milt and asks how it is made (14.155–159). She accepts Chisato’s case for a second lunch as preparedness for sudden action, refuses an offer to exercise together, and tries to pay for herself (14.78–88, 14.169). On the walk back she asks what the right answer is. Chisato tells her that her way was fun in its own fashion. Chisato’s narrated thought gives the reason: life is short, nobody knows how many meals are left, and the protein lunch is a memory she will keep until she dies (14.175–183). Takina says she will think about it (14.188).

Doi’s connection to a chef lets Takina obtain a welcome meal before she learns to make one herself (14.190–249). She judged that reproducing it was unrealistic on cost and skill, so she consulted the chef. He came and cooked without charge, using a sea bream an angler had given him (14.228–231). She credits Chisato’s lesson and rejects any suggestion that she spent café money. She notices only that Chisato and Mika look pained (14.208–212, 14.241). Mika bows deeply to the chef (14.248).

In her own attempt, a recipe cannot supply the feel of rolling an omelet or shaping a rice ball. Takina’s section shows her reasoning step by step. Busy service comes first. She expects complaints whatever she cooks, and she refuses (14.259–265). Mizuki’s word “卑屈” provokes a harder slap on the sink than she intended (14.266–271). She goes along with Chisato’s puffed-cheek trick only because Chisato very occasionally shows her a view she had not known, “あの、噴水の前の時のように”. This time she bet on the unlikely case and lost (14.281–290). That earlier conversation is not narrated in this book. What makes her cook is the agreement, which she treats as a rule (14.296–297). She tests the fridge against every requirement. She cuts red sausages into octopuses with sesame eyes. She makes tamagoyaki for the first time from a phone video, Kansai-style because “自分は京都の出だ”. She shapes rice balls she thought would be easy because she shapes ohagi (14.308–341, 14.355). Mizuki laughs at the misshapen pyramids, then concedes the egg tastes better than its appearance promises (14.353, 14.364). Takina admits to herself that she trusted manuals and her own dexterity too far (14.366–370). Chisato blocks her “どうせ” three times. Then she calls Takina by name with a partly eaten rice ball in her hand and praises its taste (14.295, 14.359–360, 14.371–376). Takina turns back to the sink. The narration gives the pleasure, embarrassment and frustrated pride that the others cannot see there. She feels *as if* Chisato had accepted and embraced her. She cannot explain what feels so “unfair” (14.327–385). That difference between a bite actually eaten and an embrace privately felt matters to the scene. ### 4 · リコリコ・オブ・ザ・デッド

Takina first builds the shop out of what she can smell, feel and hear. An outbreak announced on a broadcast gives the story the furniture of a public emergency, from a private consciousness already entertaining the possibility that it is dreaming (16.2–147). The dream is focalized through Takina from first to last, and the text leaves open whose dream it is. Inside it she tests the hypothesis. The café is lit by sunset though its lamps are always on, her phone has no signal, and the radio’s timing is impossible. She concludes “夢、か” (16.15–19, 16.112–119). When dream Chisato drills holes in a bat before driving nails, the narration notes that Takina did not know that technique. Takina reasons that the dream is at least not hers and wonders whose figure she might be (16.143–148). She suspects Kurumi is not part of this dream because Kurumi’s machines are off (16.241). A giant squirrel with Kurumi’s voice appears later (16.556–559). Zombie conventions give her and the imagined Chisato occasion for closeness, disagreement and horror. Dream Chisato argues zombie lore from cremation customs. Takina answers from a zombie film she saw with Doi in a cinema (16.65–66).

Takina’s habits show in her in-dream choices, and those choices carry the same dream frame. Asked whether she has a dream, she begins to say she wants to work as a First Lycoris and is cut off (16.96). She privately wonders whether she will one day wear the red uniform and learn what DA does not teach (16.196–197). She chooses hollow points against flesh and head shots where the range is sure (16.157, 16.264), and she makes and admits tactical errors (16.270, 16.298–315). She argues for DA headquarters. It is in the capital region, where Chisato grew up and where Takina moved to train, and it was once secondary to a Kyoto centre. Its fighters regard killing the defenceless who must be killed as ordinary work (16.179–186). Her inference that DA’s name implies sister organizations is hers alone (16.188–193). She reproves dream Chisato’s solo foray with “あなた”, the only time the book has her use the word to Chisato (16.128). Dream Chisato speaks within the same frame. She does not know whether she could shoot, does not want to kill people, would keep her usual rounds and wants to stay herself to the end (16.165–175). She balks at striking human-looking zombies, then strikes zombie Mizuki with “夢だしね” (16.272–287). None of these lines is waking testimony from either partner.

When Chisato presses against her neck, Takina asks her to stop. In that night scene dream Chisato has pushed the futons together and asked whether Takina likes anyone, and Takina has given a dry refusal (16.224–248). When the closeness goes further than she wants, she tells Chisato to stop and decides to push her away. The zombies break in first (16.251–259). A later violent encounter with an imagined Mizuki belongs to the same dream (16.242–292).

The van conversation supplies two answers within the dream. Asked how she would feel about staying alone with Chisato, Takina first calculates what two people can survive (16.365–369). She then decides the question is meant literally, because in her view Chisato puts enjoyment before the best plan (16.370–373). She imagines the life that would have continued without the pandemic. Since being “expelled” from DA, she has been a Lycoris for individuals and a café worker at Chisato’s side, a life she would once have dismissed (16.374–378). She murmurs that she would not mind (16.379). The delay feels to her like several minutes, perhaps over ten. She would prefer to count the words as a private remark, and she has no wish to convey her feelings (16.380–385). Dream Chisato has waited and asks whether they should go away together (16.386–394; i-217). Takina cannot account for her own reaction, or say how she would have looked away had she not been driving (16.401). She turns back to the road and says no to that change of course. They will go to DA because she wants them to survive (16.395–406). She asks whether Chisato dislikes DA as the place she “threw away” (16.408). Her qualified answer about being together and her decision about where to drive can both stand.

Chisato’s dislike of zombie films that turn into fights among human factions then gives Takina a less personal explanation for the invitation. Embarrassed, she pulls away when Chisato touches her face and tells her to stop. Dream Chisato reads her heat as fever. The van crashes, a failure Takina calls inexcusable for a professional (16.407–437). A two-serving espresso pot makes the personal explanation occur to Takina again, and she dismisses it again (16.443–461). Here the narration states waking habits. The café makes its espresso in a stovetop pot, Takina particularly likes it, and the partners drink sugared espresso before every night job (16.449–450, 16.490). The rest is dream event. Takina refuses milk, tells Chisato that she likes her coffee too, likes the Alfort and trusts Chisato’s palate (16.466–479, 16.495–500). She recalls that the film set Chisato gave her when she first came included *The Boondock Saints*. Dream Chisato says she chose it hoping they would become such a pair (16.510–516). Over the coffee she thinks that now she might assent regardless of what Chisato meant. The phrase “like Chisato” occurs on both sides of her own argument. She could follow what she wants, or honor her own limits even when doing so costs her. She still wants a push and wonders what it means that the only person present to give it is Chisato. Takina redirects the conversation toward dawn before the giant creature appears (16.519–559). Neither the genre explanation nor the personal reading empties the other of force, and the text leaves unnamed what Takina might want (16.546). The kaiju, the tank and Mika end the night. Takina agrees to come along and thinks that this noisy life has been ordinary for several months and is not bad (16.575–585).

On waking after the DA handoff, Takina pushes the sleeping Chisato toward the door and is irritated when she slumps back. Later she asks Mizuki to forget the sounds she made in her sleep. Asked if it was a bad dream, she says it was not really so bad (16.587–617). Mizuki’s remark that Takina looked as if she were dreaming and sometimes moaned shows that Takina did dream (16.605–607). The prose gives these bodily and verbal responses different objects. Chisato’s sleep-talk rhymes with the dream ending. Three lines, about setting off, shooting and the peace of the world, echo the tank scene (16.587, 16.601, 16.616; 16.571, 16.580). Her waking wishes and the contents of her own dream remain inaccessible. The text allows three readings of the dream’s ownership: Takina’s dream, Chisato’s with Takina as a figure in it, or a genre device with no owner. It does not choose. The waking frame places the ride on the return from a drug-route job after a long DA handover (16.590–597). The close match with Story 2’s job makes that its return. That the abandoned zombie screening shaped the dream is an inference, not a statement.

### 5 · ご注文は？

The story opens on the night of Introduction 5, through Itō. She values Chisato as a model, a frank critic and a helper on deadline nights (18.12–13). Chisato asks Tokuda whether “例のブツ” made it in. The outro shows that it means the photographs (18.19–22, 19.28). Chisato is oddly flustered when the others joke that it means drugs (18.25–32). Itō’s suspicion about Kana is practical. A visit costs about two thousand yen with the fare from Saitama. Kana comes on irregular days and spends nothing on clothes (18.40–55). Chisato does not answer. Mika closes the subject as prying, and Takina and Mizuki are away on “別件の仕事”. Chisato tells Itō she might be good at writing mysteries (18.61–79). Mizuki’s later report suggests that the watch on Kana had already begun (18.887). The girl who introduces herself as Kana at the café is named 堅香子, read Katashi Kyōko, in the narration (18.97). The narrator calls the stooped girl with hidden eyes her true form. Kana thinks the café Kana is her real self (18.85–86, 18.164–166). At home and school others routinely tell her who she is. Her chosen name, the seat she can return to and the staff who recognize her make another life possible. She pays for it with the weekday lunch allowance, saved by skipping lunch and walking home (18.127, 18.228, 18.385). Her comparisons to the family she would like tell us what each person offers *her*. Kurumi first invites her to play. Takina looks to her like a well-honed blade with no malice. Chisato seems to take such pleasure in friendship herself that Kana can accept what she gives and thank her freely. In Kana’s view, Chisato does not sacrifice herself (18.97–145, 18.163–251, 18.247). Kana’s wish for a family gives us no legal relationship between her father and his partner. An earlier rumor about Ishihara remains a rumor. His present assault on Kana is an event she directly experiences (18.480–512).

Ruri Mizokakushi (溝隠瑠璃) invites Kana to the tea gathering with Kadowaki (門脇). Mizokakushi’s hold over Kana is images taken without her consent (18.172–190). Kadowaki’s gathering is a drug and exploitation operation aimed at minors, which this reading names and does not describe (18.284–365). Kana pretends to drink before she understands the danger, because she will not take anything of theirs into her body (18.296–298). Kadowaki demands payment for what she never ordered (18.333–341). Mizokakushi takes out her phone, and Kana expects her to produce the images used against her. Kana reaches for the gun and stops, because only the first name on her list and an unplanned extra are present. She pays with the money she had saved (18.148, 18.279–305, 18.335–365). Kadowaki matches the man on Bulldog’s monitor, and the talk at the gathering mentions a recent sharp fall in supply. No line identifies him or says Story 2 cut his route (11.33–36, 18.276–277, 18.300). What that loss means to her emerges afterward, when even her inexpensive coffee and the time she can spend being someone at LycoReco seem out of reach (18.294–365, 18.383–407). Her changing reasons for failing to shoot also belong to her. She keeps a list of five people and carries a pistol with five rounds (18.133–134, 18.191, 18.562). She thinks about the number and order of the intended victims, confronts the possibility of fear, later freezes and turns the failure against herself (18.559–608, 18.700–780). In the guidance room she holds Ishihara at gunpoint, in the stance Takina taught her at the manga shoot, and does not fire. Shooting him would cost her father’s place on the list (18.533–566). The alarm and the woman in a white coat come from Mizuki, though Kana never learns it. The call for a stretcher is an untagged voice, probably hers (18.567–580, 18.603, 18.887–889). After failing to fire at Mizokakushi, Kana turns the gun on herself and fires (18.780–803). Her survival does not require attributing a stable principle of nonviolence to her.

The train scene is explicitly recalled from around two weeks earlier (18.610–664). In the first telling the fugitive discounts the sailor-uniformed girl who leaves. He tenses at the partners who board, then relaxes at their film talk (12.293–319). Kana is the girl who got off. She wanted the partners to see her, followed the train to another stop, found them gone and wondered if the man had been a boyfriend (18.618–640). At the same moment they were addressing him under guard. She found the pistol in the seat gap where they had sat and test-fired it in a wood (18.638–658). Mika later identifies it as the gun the fugitive left (18.892). Replaying the encounter gives her a plausible mistake, the gun a route into a different story, and the reader two accounts that fit without either witness seeing the whole.

Kana later experiences a discharge, an impact, red rubber-smelling residue, a missing gun and the discovery of a handwritten coffee-set voucher stuck to her phone. Her impressions leave the mechanics and the placement of the voucher unseen (18.800–837). The residue and the bat-like impact match Takina’s earlier account of Chisato’s rounds. Who fired, and at what, is not shown (12.79–83, 18.810–818). Chisato has appeared twice before in Kana’s view. Two weeks earlier she boarded the train. On the night of the tea gathering she found Kana in an alley, and Kana hid her face and ran. Whether Chisato recognized her there is not said (18.618–631, 18.396–411). At the café, Chisato is alone in her red uniform. She asks nothing, brews by siphon and sits beside Kana. She passes on Mika’s saying about coffee’s magic and adds that coffee at night is special (18.833–845). She seats her and serves coffee before asking what else she would like to order, as the voucher’s terms allow (18.850–855). Kana asks for help. Chisato embraces her, and Kana puts her own arms around her. Kana cannot see how Chisato seems to know what she has been through. The reader has Mizuki’s report (18.864–867). The teacher also asked Kana to entrust matters to him while he assaulted her. The difference is in the actions around the words. Here Kana chooses her order, hears it accepted, reaches back and says yes (18.507–512, 18.829–875).

The people who helped bring Kana back to the café still disagree about its price. Mizuki reports the prior surveillance, Takina’s wish to kill the teacher and her later proposal to claim responsibility if Kana shoots. Mizuki herself set off the alarm and acknowledges physically punishing the teacher. Twice she asks why they could not have spared Kana more suffering by acting sooner. Kurumi argues that the quick route, recovering the gun quietly and reporting to child services and the police, would have ended in anti-bullying handouts (18.884). Mika calls the waiting “千束なりの計算”. A formal commission, and a gun from the earlier case, made DA responsible for costs and able to act (18.892). Mika says Chisato needs a rule or her helping would have no limit (18.895). Mizuki gives its content as acting only on request (18.896). Kurumi calls that realism, and Mizuki calls Chisato too strict (18.898–899). Chisato is absent and states neither rule nor reasons anywhere in the book. Kurumi reports the raid on a drug route and predicts online deletion of the images after obtaining the original data from Mizokakushi’s phone. A reported operation, a predicted cleanup and Kana’s immediate relief have different evidentiary standing (18.877–910). The fee, Mika says, is the smile of Chisato’s friend. An untagged voice adds that it is a loss, at least for the adults (18.907–910). The formal order matters enormously. The café had already offered friendship and acted on Kana’s behalf before she gave it. Mizuki’s objection survives the successful request.

### Afterword and Bonus Track

Asaura calls the afterword revised. He describes adapting his material to prose, drawing on elements the anime could not cover, and imagining a book with the variety of a mixed snack packet (21.2–11). He also states the book’s concept. It turns the director’s earliest promotional copy, which hid every gun-action element and which he calls a probably whimsical “lie(?)”, into a work (21.9). The passage grounds the extraordinary shift from staff-meal comedy to Kana’s terror in the author’s account of composition. That stated concept can establish neither an in-world date nor an omitted TV event. The fan-letter leaf states that the book is entirely newly written (S24), and the jacket copy glosses ご注文 as オーダー a fourth time (S25).

The Bonus Track takes place after the posed shoot and before the issue reaches the café. Chisato imagines strangers of the distant future seeing her picture through the National Diet Library, and says simply that it would make her happy. Takina replies that they will certainly be dead by then. She answers Chisato’s exaggerated complaint with a joke about a cow and identifies it as a joke (22.2–32). The shoot was Chisato’s doing. Tokuda tells the photographer that the girls badly wanted to appear, and he agreed because Chisato asked (22.25, 22.32). The hot cake comes from a shop new to Chisato, so it is probably not the Tomy hot cake she already loved (22.40, 13.23). Takina calls her greedy for wanting to eat the prop before it cools (22.34). When Chisato offers her a forkful of pancake, Takina starts to demur, then takes the bite. Tokuda interprets the exchange as explaining why the two look so good together. The photographer has independently moved behind Tokuda and taken the shot. Tokuda asks whether he got it and expects the editor to use it (22.38–56). The printed issue includes the feeding image, which a voice at the café recognizes as taken after the shoot (19.28–45, 19.69). Participation in the posed shoot, acceptance of the offered food, delight among the readers and permission to publish this separate candid are four different matters. The final one remains open.
