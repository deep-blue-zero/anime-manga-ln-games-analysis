# Ordinary days · prose reading

A regular can enter LycoReco, be recognized and still misread what happens there. Tokuda notices the silence that falls when he mentions a job, then laughs off his own remarkably accurate suspicion. Takina smiles at Doi when he says he will try more sweets. Chisato persuades him to read romantic interest into her attentiveness. Kana sees the partners turn their backs precisely when she most wants to catch their eyes. Each visitor really has a place in the café. Belonging gives none of them a complete view of another person. Asaura builds the volume around the difference, returning to incidents through people who saw other parts of them.

## Source-native narrative

### Five introductions and an exit

At 9.12–36 Tokuda reads the area north of Kinshichō station through a reporter’s sense of what a family might enjoy. Mika remembers his drink. When Tokuda asks about “work,” the room stills until he explains he wants to cover the shop (9.97–112). Mika declines the publicity and offers a freshly made monaka (9.126–178). Mika keeps the shop out of the magazine and makes Tokuda welcome as a customer. The other visits test what a reporter might reasonably discover. Bulldog’s memories of his grandmother and good coffee grow out of his dissatisfaction with the coffee at work (11.4–21). His expectations about the people who hired him and the people coming for them are an employee’s reconstruction (11.31–48). That earlier intimacy gives his later request to Chisato a history that she never hears. Mika tells Tokuda about sweets and coffee and steers around his former employment (13.5–21). A takoyaki party gives the regulars something delicious and gives the shop a smell that overpowers the coffee. Tokuda actually burns his mouth. His pleasure survives the discomfort (13.23–72).

The fourth introduction delays identifying the woman behind the camera. She is Itō, the manga artist, photographing the armed poses as creative reference (15.30–34). Talk of film collections and borrowed discs is genuine. So is the talk about work after dark. Tokuda studies a picture of Mika’s stance and entertains a training history of his own (15.2–38, 15.52–106). The witnesses have different access even in a single group portrait. The fifth introduction supplies a less comfortable test. An affogato tastes best at a particular stage of melting. When Itō recognizes Kana’s school uniform and presses for her reason for visiting, Tokuda notices Kana recoil while Itō is still looking at her drawing tablet (17.10, 17.74–81). Chisato’s arrival revives the room. Tokuda looks back at Kana and sees her spoon has stopped while the ice cream continues to melt (17.36–61, 17.73–91). The pause belongs to one customer, easily lost in the group’s relief.

The printed magazine brings the frame back to Tokuda. The photographs feature the girls at other cafés. He is glad the regulars love the spread and hurt that nobody reads his words. He still has a little of the wish to be the one who discovered LycoReco, though he now treasures being able to come here himself. His 世界, “world,” carries the reading カフェ at 19.62. As the bell rings someone else may be about to become a regular (19.28–75). His change of heart allows for both professional vanity and genuine attachment.

### 1 · 閉じ行く人生にスイーツを

Doi feels conspicuous on arrival, a man of fifty-five who supposes the possibilities of his life are closing. Recognition at the counter turns into invitations to walk, eat and talk. The ten-kilometre walk in his leather shoes puts him in bed for three days. On later visits he is better equipped for an outing, looks forward to the next one and starts doing things for regulars himself (10.3–57, 10.211–213, 10.369–419). Mika urges him to think of time as scarce. Doi objects that these words might depress someone. Mika agrees that some people might give up, and asks what *this* man would do now (10.469–497). His advice depends on recognizing how much Doi has already changed. Chisato’s earlier reading of Mika’s parental gaze similarly tells us how she receives it (10.158–183).

The comic reversal gives the same advice a consequence Mika did not propose. Takina is glad to see a customer in better spirits. Chisato takes the smile as evidence of romantic interest. Kurumi proposes asking what Takina thinks, then lets the smile give some credence to the group’s guess (10.335–355). Takina later explains her concern in terms of Doi’s spending, seat, mood and the outings laid on for him. Chisato has already encouraged him to act on someone else’s supposed feelings (10.542–599, 10.614–646). Doi imagines he can justify courting a teenager through precedent from period novels. He catches himself wondering whose interest that interpretation serves and nonetheless acts on it (10.648–674). At 10.688 he places his food order and adds a private dinner invitation. The chapter ends at 10.690 before Takina replies. Tokuda later arrives to find Doi laughing strangely, Chisato prostrate and Takina irritated (19.7–22). Their positions give the reader the direction of the joke while leaving her exact words unrecoverable.

Doi also imagines the ohagi reflects the size of Takina’s hands. In another story Takina remembers doing the preparation, leaving before the finish, and expects the manager to shape it (10.525–537, 12.254–255). Doi is right that she worked on his food and in no position to identify whose hands gave it its finished form. The novel makes his gratitude intelligible even as it corrects his account.

### 2 · ガンファイトとコーヒーと千束の赤いアレ

Takina does not initially understand Chisato’s chosen method, challenges it and resents having to wait for an explanation. Chisato has an ordinary reason for wanting to finish quickly. She hopes to watch an earlier zombie movie with Takina before café opening (12.88–105). Her treatment of Bulldog brings work and hospitality into the same moment. After serious injury, he asks her for good coffee and she agrees. The text passes straight from the promise to more painful force and then the next scene. Whether he actually receives the coffee is open (11.4–21, 12.202–217).

Takina recognizes the defeated guard’s willingness to stand in the way for someone else. When the target escapes, Chisato explains that a live capture with little collateral harm is what makes the pair particularly valuable for this commission. Takina sees the benefit for tracing the drug distribution route and infers why DA holds on to Chisato. Chisato agrees to pursue, and Takina calls off their movie plan (12.219–255). Takina can respect the professional she opposed, appreciate live capture and still describe herself as armed with lethal ammunition on the train. Her judgment here has a history and a limit.

The fugitive sees a timid-looking schoolgirl, is relieved when she gets off and relaxes again when the next two passengers start talking about zombie films. His interest in the discussion is real before he realizes the pair have him under guard. He carries on afterward, offering the action/horror distinction that the partners had missed (12.290–319). For a few minutes he has something to teach the people detaining him. The change of focalizer grants him a taste of his own and the audience a glimpse of Kana before either understands what the other has left in that carriage.

### 3 · Takina’s cooking

The first complaint about Takina’s cooking concerns the time spent in the kitchen while customers need service. She has shown she can cook with time to spare. Her efficient substitute makes sense against the criticism she actually received. Chisato realizes that nobody explained what a staff meal should offer the people eating it (14.27–87). Showing her a meal elsewhere supplies that missing criterion. Chisato also takes the opportunity to advance her theory about Doi (14.69–100, 14.169–187). A helpful invitation can serve a second, mistaken purpose.

Doi’s connection to a chef lets Takina obtain a welcome meal before she learns to make one herself (14.190–249). In her own attempt, a recipe cannot supply the feel of rolling an omelet or shaping a rice ball. Mizuki laughs at the misshapen pyramids, then concedes the egg tastes better than its appearance promises. Chisato calls Takina by name with a partly eaten rice ball in her hand and praises its taste. Takina turns back to the sink. The narration gives the pleasure, embarrassment and frustrated pride that the others cannot see there. She feels *as if* Chisato had accepted and embraced her. She cannot explain what feels so “unfair” (14.327–385). That difference between a bite actually eaten and an embrace privately felt matters to the scene’s intimacy.

### 4 · リコリコ・オブ・ザ・デッド

Takina first builds the shop out of what she can smell, feel and hear. An outbreak announced on a broadcast gives the story the furniture of a public emergency, from a private consciousness already entertaining the possibility that it is dreaming (16.2–147). Zombie conventions give her and the imagined Chisato occasion for intimacy, disagreement and horror. When Chisato presses against her neck, Takina asks her to stop. A later violent encounter with an imagined Mizuki belongs to the same dream (16.242–292).

The van conversation supplies two actual answers. Asked how she would feel about staying alone with Chisato, Takina first calculates what two people can survive. She then imagines their life together, working for individuals and sharing the café. She murmurs that she would not mind (16.351–379). The delay feels to her like several minutes, perhaps over ten. She would prefer to count the words as a private remark. Dream Chisato has waited and asks whether they should go away together (16.380–394). Takina turns back to the road and says no to that change of course. They will go to DA because she wants them to survive (16.395–406). Her qualified answer about being together and her decision about where to drive can both stand.

Chisato’s dislike of zombie films that turn into fights among human factions then gives Takina a less personal explanation for the invitation. Embarrassed, she pulls away when Chisato touches her face and tells her to stop. The van crashes (16.407–431). A two-serving espresso pot makes the personal explanation occur to Takina again, and she dismisses it again (16.443–461). Over the coffee she thinks that now she might assent regardless of what Chisato meant. The phrase “like Chisato” occurs on both sides of her own argument. She could follow what she wants, or honor her own limits even when doing so costs her. She still wants a push and wonders what it means that the only person present to give it is Chisato. Takina redirects the conversation toward dawn before the giant creature appears (16.519–559). Neither the genre explanation nor the possibility of attraction empties the other of force.

On waking after the DA handoff, Takina pushes the sleeping Chisato toward the door and is irritated when she slumps back. Later she asks Mizuki to forget the sounds she made in her sleep. Asked if it was a bad dream, she says it was not really so bad (16.587–617). The prose gives these bodily and verbal responses different objects. Chisato’s sleep-talk rhymes with the dream ending. Her waking wishes and the contents of her own dream remain inaccessible.

### 5 · ご注文は？

The girl who introduces herself as Kana at the café is named 堅香子 in the narration (18.97). At home and school others routinely tell her who she is. Her chosen name, the seat she can return to and the staff who recognize her make another life possible. Her comparisons to the family she would like tell us what each person offers *her*. Kurumi first invites her to play. Takina looks to her like a well-honed blade with no malice. Chisato seems to take such pleasure in friendship herself that Kana can accept what she gives and thank her freely (18.97–145, 18.163–251). Kana’s wish for a family gives us no legal relationship between her father and his partner. An earlier rumor about Ishihara remains a rumor. His present assault on Kana is an event she directly experiences (18.480–512).

Ruri Mizokakushi (溝隠瑠璃) invites Kana to the tea gathering with Kadowaki (門脇). Kana pretends to drink before she understands the danger. Kadowaki demands payment for what she never ordered. Mizokakushi takes out her phone, and Kana expects her to produce the images used against her. Kana pays with the money she had saved (18.148, 18.279–305, 18.335–365). What that loss means to her emerges afterward, when even her inexpensive coffee and the time she can spend being someone at LycoReco seem out of reach (18.294–365, 18.383–407). Her changing reasons for failing to shoot also belong to her. She thinks about the number and order of the intended victims, confronts the possibility of fear, later freezes and turns the failure against herself (18.559–608, 18.700–780). Her survival does not require attributing a stable principle of nonviolence to her.

The train scene is explicitly recalled from around two weeks earlier (18.610–664). In the first telling the fugitive discounts the sailor-uniformed girl who leaves, then recognizes the partners who board (12.293–319). Kana is the girl who got off. She wanted the partners to see her, followed the train to another stop, found them gone and wondered if the man had been a boyfriend (18.618–640). At the same moment they were addressing him under guard. Replaying the encounter gives her a plausible mistake, the gun a route into a different story, and the reader two accounts that fit without either witness seeing the whole.

Kana later experiences a discharge, an impact, red rubber-smelling residue, a missing gun and the discovery of a handwritten coffee-set voucher stuck to her phone. Her impressions leave the mechanics and the placement of the voucher unseen (18.800–837). At the café, Chisato seats her and serves coffee before asking what else she would like to order. Kana asks for help. Chisato embraces her, and Kana puts her own arms around her. The teacher also asked Kana to entrust matters to him, as he forced himself on her. The difference is in the actions around the words. Here Kana chooses her order, hears it accepted, reaches back and says yes (18.507–512, 18.829–875).

The people who helped bring Kana back to the café still disagree about its price. Mizuki reports the prior surveillance, Takina’s wish to kill the teacher and her later proposal to claim responsibility if Kana shoots. Mizuki herself set off the alarm and acknowledges physically punishing the teacher. Twice she asks why they could not have spared Kana more suffering by acting sooner. Mika explains how a formal commission and a connection to the earlier case made DA responsible for costs and able to act. Kurumi reports the raid on a drug route and predicts online deletion of the images after obtaining the original data from Mizokakushi’s phone. A reported operation, a predicted cleanup and Kana’s immediate relief have different evidentiary standing (18.877–910). The formal order matters enormously. The café had already offered friendship and acted on Kana’s behalf before she gave it. Mizuki’s objection survives the successful request.

### Afterword and Bonus Track

Asaura calls the afterword revised. He describes adapting his material to prose, drawing on elements the anime could not cover, and imagining a book with the variety of a mixed snack packet (21.2–11). The passage grounds the extraordinary shift from staff-meal comedy to Kana’s terror in the author’s account of composition. His joke about the earliest promotional copy can establish neither an in-world date nor an omitted TV event.

The Bonus Track takes place after the posed shoot and before the issue reaches the café. Chisato imagines strangers of the distant future seeing her picture. Takina replies to Chisato’s exaggerated complaint with a joke about a cow and identifies it as a joke (22.2–32). When Chisato offers her a forkful of pancake, Takina starts to demur, then takes the bite. Tokuda interprets the exchange as explaining why the two look so good together. The photographer has independently moved behind Tokuda and taken the shot. Tokuda asks whether he got it and expects the editor to use it (22.38–56). The printed issue includes the feeding image, which a voice at the café recognizes as taken after the shoot (19.28–45, 19.69). Participation in the posed shoot, acceptance of the offered food, delight among the readers and permission to publish this separate candid are four different matters. The final one remains open.
