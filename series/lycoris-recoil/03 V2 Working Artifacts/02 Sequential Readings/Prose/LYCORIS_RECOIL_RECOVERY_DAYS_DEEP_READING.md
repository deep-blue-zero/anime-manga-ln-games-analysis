# Recovery days · prose reading

Chisato gives Takina an argument for going to the cinema. Buying the ticket sets aside two hours in which they will do nothing else, and afterward there will be someone to talk to about the film. The novel lets Takina dislike much of what they actually watch. She wants to go again anyway. The cases between the opening and those two endings repeatedly let a character revise what she thinks she agreed to. Recovering an artist’s work, bringing someone home alive and convincing a patient to rest can all succeed while leaving a real dispute about how the result was obtained.

## Source-native reading

### 0 · そして、幕が開く

Mika’s coffee is already there before Takina can decline a break. She appreciates the care and thinks that, had he asked before serving it, she would have stayed at work. That unused alternative is hers to imagine (9.8–31). Chisato’s invitation to the cinema gives Takina more room to object, though she privately feels that her schedule for the next day has already been decided for her (9.155–168). The contrast matters because the novel ends with an invitation to which Takina supplies a reason of her own.

Takina focalizes the whole opening in close third person, beginning with the longest sensory passage in the story: acidity, bitterness, aroma, a late sweetness, and a cup that is not too sour (9.16–31). An unscheduled break would cost her a little guilt, and Mika’s timing removes it (9.14–15). She remembers a magazine line about coffee that she did not understand when she came to the café and understands now (9.20–21). Before the cinema dispute starts, the novel has recorded a pleasure she has acquired.

Her first answer to Chisato is a flat 別に and the statement that she does not go to cinemas (9.51). Asked where she is, she answers literally: the café, Tokyo, a little north of Kinshichō station. Asked why she lives there, she says Kusunoki told her to go (9.61–68). She adds that she would rather work harder (もっとバリバリに, 9.78), and guesses that the area’s merit is its good places to eat (9.82). Her reasons against cinemas come in answer to the regulars Kitamura and Yoneoka, with Chisato interjecting: the price, about forty extra minutes that can turn a two-hour film into half a day, and the efficiency of streaming or postal rental (9.105–112). She already watches the Blu-rays Chisato presses on her (押しつけられます), and Chisato’s reply is 見せてます (9.102–104). The objection concerns the venue and its costs, not films. When Kurumi finds a 21:00 showing, Takina privately decides she could go after closing (9.130). The late show fails for another reason. Minors are barred from screenings that end after 23:00, Chisato proposes plain clothes, and Detective Abe (阿部), who has come in for coffee, will not overlook it (9.134–145). Chisato books the film’s last Kinshichō showing for the next day, in her favourite seats in the middle, slightly forward (9.157–162).

Chisato’s case for the cinema has several parts, and the chapter keeps them distinct. She claims a cinema-going life of more than ten years (映画好き人生, 9.41). She holds that a film should be seen when one wants to see it, because nobody knows what will happen in life (9.144). She concedes that she also watches films on her phone in spare moments (9.148), and thinks convenience thins out feeling and gratitude (ありがたみ, 9.149). The next day she lectures from morning on trailers, actors and dubbing while Takina wipes tables and thinks of her as a haunting spirit (背後霊). Takina thinks Chisato could at least clean or study coffee-making (9.172–179). She invites the others, explicitly to dilute Chisato (うざったいので, 9.181), and all three decline (9.182–190). When Kusunoki ignores Chisato’s protests on the phone, Takina finds it almost pleasant to hear someone tell Chisato what must be done, after so many people who indulge her (9.206). Takina’s own summary of the indulgence, that the only bitter thing in the shop is the coffee, is a line she will later turn on herself (9.165–168).

Café hours and the call from Kusunoki keep the movie clock running. A Third fired two shots at close range and failed to kill an industrial spy with a special-forces past, who has fled with documents and a handgun (9.208–209). Kusunoki says frontal combat with such a man is beyond a Third, and the narration explains that Lycoris are built for unsuspected assassination (9.211–215). She expects Chisato to hand him to a cleaner rather than kill him, and makes the cleaner’s fee a chargeable expense if the documents and gun are recovered (9.216). The narration derives the concession from DA’s aim, a country everyone believes peaceful (9.219–221). Chisato’s eyes widen at the place name 横川 (9.224). The lines that follow, about regulars walking to the café and children going home on those roads, are unattributed narration placed after her reaction (9.225–228). They can be read as Takina’s construal or as narrator comment; they are not given as Chisato’s thought. The possibility of an armed fugitive on the streets their customers use changes what Chisato is willing to interrupt the outing for (9.229–230). Mizuki turns the sign and Kurumi sets her own AI on the street cameras. Mika phones someone, and Takina guesses that he is warning neighbours who know what the café really is (9.232–234). Takina unties the twin tails she wears for café work and checks her chamber (9.236). One exchange shows the partners’ different clocks. Chisato wants the job done within an hour, Takina agrees because rush hour will make capture harder, and Chisato corrects her: the deadline is the film (9.237–240). Chisato’s fury at Kurumi’s offer to download a pirated copy has a consequence collected later, since Kurumi spends the day taking the uploads down (9.252–254, 9.373). The operation itself is told after its conclusion. The usual backup and luring procedure is skipped. They strip the target of everything he carries, have him treated, duct-tape him, hand him to the cleaner and send the materials to Kusunoki by courier from a convenience store. The rush makes their handling of the assignment look reckless even in success (9.258–266).

The novel spends more time on making the screening because Chisato has booked the other cinema (9.299–318). Not long Chisato’s partner, Takina still knows this particular あっ means trouble (9.300–301). At the wrong building she is ready to ask how late they may enter; Chisato takes her hand with 行くぞ、相棒！ (9.319–321). In Kinshi Park Chisato lets go, sprints, waves at a large dog, and arrives without losing her breath. Takina, running flat out, cannot keep up. She reads the moment as a glimpse of Chisato’s real ability at a time that has nothing to do with work (9.333–338). Nothing in the passage connects that stamina to anything beyond itself. Chisato’s offer to treat her (奢ってあげる) exposes the ordinary experiences Takina lacks. Chisato compares cinema popcorn to bottled milk at a public bath, cup noodles outdoors in winter and churros at a theme park. Takina has done none of these, but grasps the idea as value added by special circumstances, and Chisato proposes that they try them together, starting with a public bath (9.282–287). Even at the right building, Chisato still stops for the popcorn and drinks she meant to share: a cola and an oolong tea for Takina to choose between, caramel and buttered salt to eat together (9.346–355).

The turnout looks like a unanimous endorsement until each colleague supplies a different reason for being there. Mizuki imagines talking about the film with an actor she admires, and Takina tells her the odds of that fateful meeting look hopelessly low (9.368–371). Kurumi has come to see the splatter film after taking down the illicit uploads; prompted by Chisato, she decodes the title as a *Friday the 13th* homage (四 plus 九, and 黄金) (9.373–384). Chisato asks her 先生 for his reason, and Mika admits that he would be lonely as the one left out. Takina privately laughs at the sweetest reason of the three (9.386–388). During the trailers Takina suggests that someone who wants to talk through a film should watch at home, and adds that the two of them cannot easily go abroad. Chisato answers that they should go together some day, and that she likes every kind of fun (9.393–400). Takina’s private verdict is that Chisato is greedy, extravagant and selfish, childish and yet sometimes detached beyond her years: still a strange person (変な人, 9.401–402). Chisato argues for sustained attention and the memory of time spent together, two hours given to one film and talked over afterwards (9.403–408). Takina prefers a different trailer, Chisato proposes seeing both, and Takina agrees so readily that it surprises her. The soda straw gives her a moment to cover the surprise; only then does she find she is drinking the cola (9.410–415). As the lights go down she thinks she may be one of the people who indulge Chisato, and notices that she had said the coffee line from inside the shop, as one of its people (9.416–418). Her distinct taste has already entered the arrangement. In the dark she has been watching Chisato, who turns to her with はじまるよ, and she smiles a little (9.419–426). The chapter establishes an accepted outing and a second film agreed almost without deciding. It does not yet establish that she enjoys cinemas. Illustration i-015 shows Chisato clutching a startled Takina at the counter (9.54–57), and i-049 the pair in their seats with the adults behind.

### 1 · Safety work

The manga artist Itō has already used Chisato and Takina as character models by the time she asks them. Chisato readily accepts. Takina briefly thinks she may have too little experience of everyday life to judge even this irregular request. Her thought that Kusunoki might have sent her to learn such things breaks off. It gives no new order from her commander (10.55–89). Itō’s professional difficulty has a shape of its own. The opening in the publication schedule is scarce, much of the drawing already exists, and a dramatic pose still has to read on the page. Takina pictures what would actually happen in the position Itō proposes. Chisato helps make a viable composition and supplies the grip detail that Itō adds as a close-up panel. Takina’s body-check sets the pose Itō photographs, and her technical contribution is an objection to the rifle itself, which sends the partners after a different reference (10.191–216, 10.221–275, 10.300–395). The girls’ ordinary trained movements and the artist’s needs cross without becoming interchangeable.

Takina focalizes the whole story. A haggard, empty-handed Itō at the counter instead of her usual desk-sized table is a signal (10.10–14), and Takina’s first plan is a service plan, the season’s grapefruit daifuku (10.29–33). Itō’s grave request for 仕事 makes Takina tense, because regulars know only the café’s front, while Chisato keeps massaging and smiling. Takina registers that as skill (10.35–38). Asked to play a reluctant killer, Chisato shifts into melodrama and, in the role, into アタシ (10.46–48). Mika serves the sweet Takina had in mind. She explains that the tsubu-an helps with fatigue, and the narration gives her reasoning about vitamin B1 and citric acid (10.95–102). She had prepared the grapefruit that morning, so Itō’s praise pleases her doubly (10.112–114). She decides for herself that a darker, more bitter coffee suits the sweet, and she would have eaten more (10.148–152). She has known Mika, a veteran of violence who is delicate in confectionery, only since her recent “demotion” (左遷, 10.122–126).

The pause in which Takina judges Itō’s request is the volume’s clearest retrospective on her adjustment. Asking after the fact seems high-handed to her, but she reconsiders: the manga world may simply work that way, and since leaving DA she has learned that her common sense is not the world’s (10.57–59). She recalls a recent incident with men’s underwear and eating a cream-heavy pancake in the spring breeze, pleasures she could not have imagined at DA (10.60–61). The underwear incident is compatible with A1 E04; the novel itself dates neither memory. She wonders, hedging (かもしれない), whether Chisato’s standing as the strongest First rests partly on knowing and doing such things, and whether Kusunoki’s order to learn from Chisato meant this (10.62–63). Itō’s call for Takina interrupts the thought (10.64). The inference stays hers.

Once at Itō’s flat Takina manages the work. She waits silently while Chisato and Itō chat, then cuts in with 時間がないんですよね!? Itō and Chisato both kneel, and Takina tells Chisato that they have not come to play (10.167–189). The gun props give Takina’s doctrine room. Itō carries a revolver with her finger on the trigger and lets the muzzle swing toward Chisato. Takina moves by reflex, but Chisato is faster and takes the gun so naturally that Itō thinks she handed it over (10.194–206). Takina’s rules follow as narrated conviction: treat every muzzle as if it fired a continuous killing beam, and assume error and malfunction (10.199–203). She admires Chisato checking the revolver by touch while still looking at Itō, first-rate Lycoris work woven into ordinary movement (10.211–214). She also works out the drawn panel as a sequence of real decisions (10.221–238).

The modelling session gives Takina a private defeat. She attacks half in earnest, ramming the AK into Chisato’s stomach as the panel requires, and Chisato’s revolver is already at her temple (10.244–248). Takina is certain Chisato stepped back into the bookcase on purpose so that the pose would match; her counterfactual is that in a real fight Chisato would have sidestepped and shot her (10.254–260). They exchange やりますね、千束 and だろ？ (10.261–262). Then Takina sees that Chisato’s trigger is already pulled, with the hammer held by her thumb, and senses (ようにも感じる) that Chisato counts on her to understand and stop. The narration glosses こちら with the ruby たきな, Takina modelling Chisato’s expectation in her own deixis (10.267–271). Knowing that this is play with a toy, Takina still cannot keep her frustration from showing at being outclassed (悔しさ, 10.274, 10.278). Chisato’s explanation of the grip is adopted as a close-up (10.272–275). Itō’s planned series runs from rival assassins to schoolmates to a temporary alliance against a common enemy. Chisato adds the rescue beat (“nobody kills her but me”), and Itō supplies the proud reply (10.281–293).

Takina’s second contribution is critical. She remarks that a modernized custom AK is an odd sample for a dealer selling in bulk, compares it to gaming chairs in a rural classroom, and insists the choice is incongruous rather than wrong (10.300–318). When Chisato appeals to her (気にならないよね！), she answers 気になりますね, then softens it: the panels are not yet drawn (10.326–329). Itō cannot draw guns without a model. The deadline cannot move, because the author of the magazine’s flagship series has thrown out their back, and Itō has no current serialization (10.334–362). Takina thinks Itō should settle her priority aloud instead of half-choosing (10.365–368). Mika’s call interrupts her (10.369). From his hesitation Takina infers that the request comes not from DA but from an acquaintance who knows the café’s other side (10.378–379). *Dog* later shows that acquaintance to be an intermediary, not the client (11.265, 11.387). A young gangster has taken guns from a secret safe. At the word AK47 Takina calls Chisato’s name, and Chisato accepts the job as killing two birds with one stone (10.383–388).

Feeding an exhausted person produces a smaller argument about energy, appetite, pantry supplies and time. Takina wants something gentle on the stomach, Chisato something for stamina (10.427). Chisato’s menu is meat with plenty of garlic and ginger, then fruit piled with cream, which Takina calls violence. Takina can name only rice porridge, and Chisato calls it hospital food (10.431–438). Chisato proposes a cook-off and promises to eat whatever Itō leaves of Takina’s dish. Takina takes a moment to understand, accepts it as better than waste, and then sees that winning would leave her eating Chisato’s food (10.448–455). Itō’s refrigerator holds condiments, fermented foods and eggs. Takina finds she is reluctant to make porridge from instant rice and cannot say why (10.459–467). Chisato declines a trip to the all-night supermarket, and Takina reads fatigue after the night’s fight in that refusal (10.471–474). Takina makes たまごふわふわ, drawing on a dish a Shizuoka teacher had taught her about in Kyoto. This is her first practical attempt. She is proud of the result and tries to hide it (10.420–478, 10.482–544). She makes do with granulated stock where she would prefer bonito, and adds a pinch of sugar once the egg begins to hold air (10.481–493, 10.540–542). The name causes a conversation at cross purposes, which Takina says is common with Chisato (10.506–516). Praised, she suppresses a smug face as unseemly with this dish and answers coolly that she is glad (10.529–532). The earlier novel’s uncooperative rice balls cannot establish a general incapacity to cook. Chisato has spent the cleaning time reading manga on Itō’s bed (10.495–499). Itō calls them a good combination of opposites, and Takina ignores Chisato’s wink (10.555–558).

The partners make a consequential mistake precisely where each expects the other to have checked. The reference gun is a weapon they took on the night’s job and are passing off as a prop. They hide it in newspaper, paracord and a garbage bag (10.396–403), and Chisato tells Itō it was borrowed from a gun-enthusiast friend (10.413). Shielded by Chisato, Takina empties the magazine of its last three rounds, but neither clears the chamber (10.415). A real gun brought as a reference discharges in Itō’s hands. The tablet and wall are damaged. Nobody is injured (10.559–590). Takina first rounds on Chisato for not unloading it, and Chisato says she thought Takina had (10.579–580). Takina then reconstructs the handover. She took out the magazine and gave Chisato the rifle mid-procedure, so she should have worked the charging handle then. With a civilian as its destination, she concludes, the mistake is hers, and she apologizes (10.581–587). Chisato acknowledges her own omission and turns to relief that nobody was hurt (10.588). The bullet stopped in the building’s steel frame (10.590).

They offer Itō a misleading account of what has happened even as they set to work saving her manuscript. Chisato covers her eyes and pats her to sleep with “it was all a dream” (10.572–578). The cover story later becomes a heavy model gun that fell (10.658). Kurumi explains the limits of her usual work, then recovers the files (10.596–609). Takina drags the unwakeable Itō off the bed and suggests tossing her in the air. Chisato, who earlier refused even the idea of striking Itō’s neck, vetoes that as potentially fatal (10.616–629). Takina’s impression that Mika and Chisato understand each other almost perfectly changes when she recalls Chisato went back to fetch Kurumi. Takina infers Chisato could have seen Mika still at the shop, and deflates her own parent-and-child image (10.639–643). The restorative coffee wakes the artist by being hot; Takina calls it torture, Mika says the taste is reliable, and Takina reflects that plain hot water would have done (10.645–656). Takina names the morning a total effort by the whole café (10.682–683). The editor reports car trouble and receives the finished pages. Takina has inferred Kurumi’s part in the delay (10.672–675, 10.699). Kurumi asks about pay; Chisato calls it helping out, and Mika thinks a family-restaurant lunch may come of it (10.684–688). Payment is put off and Mizuki gets the café’s shift alone (10.700–702). The manuscript reaches its editor. A future serialization is still a possibility.

The chapter’s last exchange is one of the clearest pieces of evidence for Takina in the book. She calls the night hard and unprofitable, and starts to say that ordinary Lycoris work would have been far better. Chisato asks which was more fun (10.689–691). Takina knows that work is not chosen by whether it is fun, and the narration leaves her thought hanging on けれど (10.692–693). She answers that this was the harder one. Chisato says she asked about fun. Takina says she does not know, and Chisato tells her she must, since it is about herself (10.694–697). The narration never answers for her. Two illustrations sit in the story. i-063 shows Takina hiding her mouth beside a chewing Kurumi. i-097 shows Chisato staring at the clay pot and Takina wearing a closed-eyed look of quiet pride. The pot carries a garnish that the prose never mentions.

### 2 · Dog

Mizukawa’s own boss, the unnamed Oyaji, calls Kōsuke Mizukawa (水川コウスケ) the guard dog of Okada, the man who brought him into the organization (11.3–5, 11.18–32, 11.53). Mizukawa owes Okada a refuge and a way of living, so the description fits well enough to hurt. Asked about his patron’s last words, he recalls being invited to eat ramen when Okada returns. These were the last words *he personally heard*. The boss tells him that the dying man called his name. Mizukawa imagines a threat to Ijūin or an instruction to take revenge. Neither is a recovered utterance (11.53–83). He resists thinking of himself as someone who wants indiscriminate harm while accepting its possibility on the way to the man he wants (11.207–215).

Mizukawa focalizes the whole story. With *One’s duties*, it is one of two stories in which neither partner focalizes, and Mizukawa’s perceptions of both are external. Read against *Safety work*, it narrates from the target’s side the job that story elides. Neither story states the identification. The reader assembles it from convergent detail. It is the same night: Mizukawa reaches his boss after 23:00 (11.20), and Mika calls late at night (10.341, 10.371). A young gang member takes an AK47 from a secret safe (10.383–385; 11.124–139). The request reaches the café through someone who knows its back face (10.379; 11.265). The man is subdued and handed over (10.394; 11.403, 11.418–425). Takina picks up the rifle, and the pair bring one back (11.402; 10.394). It is a greased, unlicensed copy (10.395, 10.412; 11.145–149, 11.297) with a partly spent magazine (10.415). Inside that identification, the rifle that discharges in Itō’s flat is Mizukawa’s. The 組長 to whom Chisato asks about taking coffee in the opening (9.193) is plausibly the same coffee-loving Kinshichō boss, but that link is also inference. Mizukawa knows him only through underworld rumour, which says he reformed his business about ten years ago, around the old radio-tower accident, and has ties to some public body (11.170–177). That is a rumour carried by a focal character, not a fact the book establishes.

Mizukawa, twenty-two and stooped, does not think about what he cannot understand and fights to kill or maim from the first move (11.6–10, 11.33–38). With the boss he uses 自分 and formal speech (11.22, 11.29), and the narration glosses Okada’s name with the ruby アニキ (11.143). The boss, about seventy, has had heart trouble for some years (11.24–25), the book’s only explicit heart condition. He draws from seiza aiming only at Mizukawa’s legs, then gives up (11.92–123). In the club Mizukawa forgets his bent sight and misses Ijūin (11.218–223). A black-haired girl in a waiter’s outfit fires from behind the bar and grazes his arm with a live round (11.227–229, 11.326). His perception of Takina is the book’s only external portrait of her in combat: she fires without aversion, like flicking a light switch, and vaults the bar without her eyes or muzzle wavering (11.238–240, 11.250). The man she hits is the man the team was hired to protect (11.385). Whether she meant to stop him firing on Ijūin or fired by default, the text gives only his perception. Chisato tells her not to shoot (たきなは撃たなくていい). Takina’s しかし千束 is cut off, and she drags the bound and gagged informant to the lift (11.241–253). Mizukawa repeats both names to memorize them, a courtesy Okada taught him (11.247). Chisato’s outfit, chosen by Mizuki over the headset, earns his verdict that it does not suit her (11.255–263). Her name for him, 忠犬, is a clue he notices only afterwards (11.281, 11.390–391). She reads his bent sight from the angle between his gaze and his muzzle (11.303). Her frangible rounds leave him alive but vomiting blood with broken ribs (11.329, 11.353, 11.408).

A protective commission appears to place the café on the other side. Mizukawa first guesses that Ijūin paid the coffee-loving boss to broker protection (11.273). Chisato gives the commission’s actual object at 11.383–389. The boss hired the girls to keep Mizukawa alive. As Chisato tells it, the client is Mizukawa’s own boss, and the coffee-loving regular only passed the job on (11.265, 11.387). Even her earlier way of addressing him as a loyal dog could have told him something about who hired her. Before she explains, she has already shot the threatening Ijūin in the side of the head, kicked him onto his back with her heel and shot him again in the solar plexus without hesitation (11.360–379). Her stated reason against revenge is practical first: a killing would enlarge the trouble and make Mizukawa a target, which his boss wanted to avoid (11.392). The fuller reported fragment, 水川を……, still leaves Okada’s sentence unfinished. The boss’s reconstruction, which Chisato offers to Mizukawa, is that Okada wanted him *stopped*. Chisato adds her own gloss, that Okada would not want him to spend his life avenging an accident, marked as conjecture with 言いたかったんじゃない？. She explicitly admits nobody can know (11.396–400). What she will guarantee is the wish of an owner for his dog’s happiness. The change from a conjecture about words to an assurance about affection makes her intervention possible without making the conjecture true. Takina, changed into her school uniform, picks up the AK and considers securing the protected person the formal end of the job. Chisato silences her with a smile and stays a little longer to speak to him (11.373–406). The prose gives Takina’s stated reason and no other. Illustration i-147 shows Takina still in the waiter’s vest as they leave, a detail the prose contradicts.

Once the pair have left, Mizukawa reaches for the blade, Ijūin’s dosu, and actually picks it up. He remembers that Okada never taught him to kill. What Okada taught him was how to live as a person. Only then does the boss arrive, hurt the already beaten Ijūin himself and suggest letting the girl take the blame. He tells Mizukawa to come home. After a long silence Mizukawa says yes (11.407–425). Recollection has changed the meaning of loyalty enough for him to go with the boss. The revenge scene also shows the violence the organization remains willing to displace onto somebody else.

### 3 · Cough

Takina sends Chisato off the floor (12.36–41). She has identified the cough by sound before its source: a damp cough followed by throat-clearing (12.6–16). Mika offers to call a Yamagishi whom he calls 先生. Takina does not know the name and guesses a family doctor. Mika adds that Chisato’s case carries a 万が一 (12.29–32). The text does not say what that risk is, and Takina reads only his usual overprotection (12.34). When Chisato teases her for being worried, Takina denies it outright (心配はしてません): it is merely a nuisance, and a sick employee cannot keep serving (12.42–46). Chisato’s nickname for this regime, スパルたきな, recurs three times, the last as Takina lectures (12.55, 12.149, 12.714). Worried that Chisato may have gone two days without a bath after a gunfight, Takina sniffs her hair. It does not smell, she reports, and then that it smells good (12.121–127). Chisato’s reply is slow and stiff. Under Takina’s hand her forehead grows hot, she looks away and her face reddens. Takina diagnoses the start of a fever (12.128–137). A reader can see embarrassment where Takina sees symptoms, and illustration i-159 draws Chisato flustered. Takina states her basis for firmness: their acquaintance is not long, but she already understands Chisato well enough to repeat the order to stay put (12.142–146). The thermometer later reads 37.9°C. Takina’s account of what the body is doing with rest is an interpretation of that reading (12.147–155).

The sickroom conversation supplies institutional and ordinary detail. Lycoris receive many vaccines from early childhood, because close-range shooting exposes them to the blood of smuggled-in offenders. Some children fall ill from the shots (12.99–102). Chisato insists that infection is something everyone must deal with together (12.103). After the manga deadline, Chisato went to a film on her own and then helped Mizuki, who had been running the café alone. She then slept in her underwear on her sofa (12.105–118). The solo visit does not date the group screening. Takina rests her case on hygiene: mask, rubber gloves and an alcohol spray (12.158–163). On Kurumi’s tablet Chisato abandons *Contagion* and *28 Days Later* at Takina’s insistence and settles on *Crank*. She says its sequel gets her いろんな意味で, a remark the text leaves unexplained and Takina does not take up (12.174–187). She feels guilty for streaming a film she owns on Blu-ray. Takina comforts her with 見たい時に見られるのが一番ですよ (12.180–181). That is Chisato’s own maxim from the opening (9.144) on Takina’s convenience reading of it (9.112, 9.146), not a change of position. Takina also observes that about thirty percent of the films Chisato has lent her star balding men (12.188–190).

Food is negotiated as care. Chisato delivers a long tribute to a spice-curry café, and Takina refuses curry for a patient and rejects the idea that spices are medicine. She agrees to the lunch there only once Chisato is better (12.206–216). Concerned about asking for an early walk, Chisato hesitates before naming the fresh red rice she would like from the next morning’s market. Takina agrees to fetch it and points out they still need dinner (12.228–249). Thanked before she has cooked anything, Takina finds the word ticklish and tells Chisato to thank her if it tastes good (12.259–262). In the kitchen she departs from her usual Kansai-style stock, talks Mizuki out of a good kamaboko mainly by looking disappointed, and grills mochi cubes with great care (12.272–318). Mika remarks that Chisato has always had a hearty appetite. He finds her asleep, and Takina defers cooking, wishes the sleeping Chisato good night and closes the door without a sound (12.323–346). Chisato later lifts the clay pot to drink the broth to the last drop (12.349–350). Takina turns around when Chisato asks for privacy while changing (12.360–371). She can care firmly while making room for a specific refusal.

Mika brings Chisato bananas from Gotō, cooling sheets from Itō and vitamins from Kitamura. The box of jelly drinks is Yamadera’s. The three boxes of sweets from the writer Yoneoka are for Kurumi, who rescued his computer, a distinction Mika catches before handing them over (12.13, 12.393–420). Chisato swallows a vitamin without reading the pack, and Takina checks the contents for her (12.406–411). Takina regrets bringing no gift. Chisato counts the nursing and the udon as more than enough, and Takina is a little embarrassed (12.428–433). Takina catches the pajama hem to stop Chisato thanking the customers in person (12.435–440). She buttons the pajama before the video is recorded (12.454–456). The patient can also arrange something for her caregiver. A video spares Chisato the need to dress and greet the regulars, and puts Takina in their game night. Takina recognizes the second purpose. When Chisato says it will be fun, Takina laughs and answers 知ってますよ. Then she goes (12.471–477). She thinks it is no longer clear which of them is caring for the other (12.479). Spoken to Chisato, 知ってますよ is as close as Takina comes to saying an activity is fun. Her other admissions of pleasure are milder (12.624, 12.642) or narrated (12.682, 18.26–29). The customers’ shouts include a warning that Takina may take Chisato’s place as the café’s star waitress (12.491). Takina reflects that the strongest Lycoris has become someone the public likes, loves and worries over. Calling her special, Takina cannot tell whether that is good or bad for a Lycoris (12.495–500). A regular calls Takina to a table, and the narration closes on her rolling dice (12.510–518).

On the Sunday walk Takina gets lost, uses her phone and imagines what Chisato would do (12.520–559). She sets out at 6:30 in her Lycoris uniform. The street is some two kilometres east, and the season is early summer with spring still lingering (12.520–524). At a ten-metre slide in Kyōjima-minami Park she judges that Chisato would have gone down it, and adds that she will not (12.535–544). She prays at a small shrine and finds the shopping street’s signs reminiscent of Kansai (12.546–550). A soup stall tempts her. Chisato might have bought soup anyway as a once-in-a-lifetime chance, but carrying it two kilometres settles the matter (12.552–559). In the queue an older man asks whether the rice is for her. Takina says a friend who is ill in bed wanted it (寝込んでいる友人, 12.569–571). This is the only time in the volume that Takina names her relation to Chisato aloud, and she chooses 友人. She never says 相棒 aloud. It appears in her focalized narration (9.300) and in her dream reasoning (14.310), and aloud it is Chisato’s word (9.321). Buying two large portions serves Takina’s own appetite too (12.572–583). She decides she is glad to have come before eating anything (12.587–593).

At the meal neither promptly proposes the second helping that both have left room for (12.598–657). Takina says the walk was pleasant (12.624). She thinks she enjoys the rice more than Chisato does because of it (12.626–628). Chisato is startled that she did not try the slide (12.630–634). When Chisato says that being worried about is pleasant, Takina privately reflects that she is not much (あまり) a person others worry about. She treats being worried over, and leaning on it, as the privilege of special people, who often think themselves ordinary. She ends on 特別な人間に、凡人の気持ちはわからない and keeps the thought to herself (12.664–673). She uses the promised spice-café lunch as an incentive for Chisato to recover (12.674–676). Leaving the room, she works out that the café is near the shopping street and plans to combine the two on a walk. Her thought that it would be more fun with Chisato breaks off (千束となら、もっと──). She turns back to propose it, but the proposal is never made (12.678–684).

Takina returns to propose another walk and catches Chisato warming the thermometer by rubbing it (12.685–691). Chisato had recovered after the udon and a nap, then played with Kurumi into the morning. Takina suspects she had been sent home so that she would not stop the game. Chisato cites concern for her. Takina grants that her suspicion may be overthinking and that only Chisato knows her intention (12.697–705). The narration leaves *that* motive private, even as it identifies the later effort to feign illness as an attempt to gain another day’s sleep (12.706). Takina still claims the right to be angry (12.705). Only Mika moves to forgive Chisato’s mangled apology (12.707–710). Takina coughs in the middle of a lecture on discipline, claiming that a Lycoris who catches cold is simply slack (12.711–722). The subsequent story establishes that she developed a fever. Who passed it to whom remains unestablished.

### 4 · One’s duties

DA’s ranks describe assignments as well as prestige. Thirds supply numbers across the country, Seconds matter especially in cities and Firsts can supervise without taking all of the everyday work (13.4–32). This much is narrator exposition, as are the expendability of Thirds (13.7–9) and the Lycoris vaccinations of *Cough* (12.100–102). Lilybell’s greater means and threshold for deployment are Fuki’s belief (13.117–120). So is the cover story DA would give a civilian killing (13.521–522). Choice of gun by aptitude in Kyoto and the regions is hearsay (らしい, 13.605). Fuki values Sakura’s obedience despite her complaints, recognizes how a partner’s manners affect an assignment and suspects reasons for the pairing that Kusunoki has never confirmed (13.46–67, 13.193–205). A high-five can mean something quite different under pressure (13.141–161). This episode of institutional life supplies the novel’s own account of how people are used. It supplies no numerical census of the anime organization.

When Sakura disappears, Fuki wants to go to her immediately but obeys an order to identify an adversary. Her inner argument lets her see reasons for obedience and still shows how hard she has to work to follow it (13.391–402). She can understand the gunman’s explanation of his pleasure without sharing it. Fuki distinguishes him from the rider and the hunter in her account of the encounter (13.515–533). Fatigue and relief attend the outcome of her own work. Her life in it began before she could choose it (13.513–545, 13.617–636). Later she moves off without her usual precaution in her haste to reach Sakura. Surprised at her own action, she infers how much she has been worrying about her (13.653–659). She also tries to infer a woman’s relationship to the dead gunman after the woman asks whether Fuki killed him. The woman’s grief and their supposed history together remain conjectures from Fuki’s position (13.662–695).

After the exposition (13.2–32) Fuki focalizes the story, and her private comparisons reach both protagonists without naming them. Sakura’s shooting is good but below that of Fuki’s 前の相棒 (13.52). The same previous partner is among those who will not or cannot follow orders (13.64–65). Fuki also remembers an older partner, the 古い相棒 who walks through gunfire dodging (13.179), and that partner’s favoured close-quarters C.A.R. technique (13.563). The rooftop list assigns the partner who left Fuki and the partner she discarded in the order Chisato, Takina (13.805–807). Reading 前の相棒 as Takina and 古い相棒 as Chisato therefore rests on that list order and on the difference between 前 and 古い. The dodging matches Chisato’s conduct in *Dog* (11.299–302). Her register divides by audience: rough speech and ワタシ with Sakura (13.46), clipped acknowledgements to Kusunoki (13.225), a formal, expressionless はい to Mika (13.781). She reads Sakura generously. An eager message sent by phone rather than over the command channel, so as not to sound like defiance, shows a delicacy beneath the bluster (13.228–232). She risks giving away her position to warn Sakura by radio about the trap (13.290–291). She keeps the gunner talking so that his voice covers her reload. She answers his creed with 自分の頭でも撃ってろよ and praises him only for sparing civilians. Her なるほどな is narrated as understanding, not agreement (13.482–525). Her indifference to which gun she is issued is a working ethic: whatever is handed to her, she will master (13.608–616).

Sakura saves her. Fuki praises her accuracy and says she can distinguish her partner’s footfall. Sakura repeats the word “partner” and smiles. It matters who arrived first. The crew with Mika reaches the scene after this intervention (13.709–745). Fuki believes Mika has come to rescue her and feels a ticklishness (くすぐったさ) that she suppresses by clenching her teeth (13.741–742). It is the word Takina uses for Chisato’s thanks (12.261). Chisato’s report is different: she told Kusunoki that Fuki would not need them and was sent anyway (13.759). Neither account is narrated fact. Fuki then invents a reason to go up to the helicopter. The narration supplies the simpler wish to see her teacher (13.745–748). Chisato jokes that she wanted to see Fuki cry (13.761). Takina stays in the helicopter watching Fuki and never speaks (13.764–766, 13.803). Fuki notices that she seems drained of energy but cannot read her complexion, because it is dark and Takina is naturally fair (13.765–766). Fuki’s one question to Chisato concerns the partnership, whether she is getting on with “that one” (13.767). Chisato tells her Takina is unwell and wanted to come anyway (13.769–771). Fuki privately wonders whether Takina came despite her illness, and aloud tells them to go home to bed. Chisato defends うちのたきなさん (13.770–771). Nothing in the story tells us what Takina thinks of Fuki or why she came.

Mika and Chisato actually offer the ride. Fuki refuses, and Chisato asks again, this time without her bravado. Fuki sees Chisato, the partner who left her, Takina, the partner she says she discarded, and Mika, the teacher who mattered so much to her. She feels she has no place in that vehicle. Sakura, beside her, would have liked a helicopter ride. Fuki’s metaphor and her reason for going up to the roof tell us different things about where she wants to be. She leaves with the present partner she addresses directly (13.781–829). Affection for Mika, pain about the former partnerships and a real attachment to Sakura have different evidence in this scene.

A repeated sentence marks the night’s pressure. After killing the gunner, Fuki finds no pleasure, only fatigue. The narration says the work was fixed for her before she could remember, and that she now feels nothing about it (13.632–636). After the helicopter leaves, the same thought returns with はずだった: there *should* be nothing left for her to feel (13.818–819). Mika is a “special former teacher” at 13.740 and a teacher who “was special” in the parallel list at 13.808. Her wish to see him (13.748) shows no cooling, so the wording difference records nothing more. She leaves with 帰んぞ、相棒 (13.828). Illustration i-277 shows her looking back with Sakura beside her.

### 5 · Common occurrence

A memory gap starts Takina testing the world she is in. Chisato’s horror vocabulary seems an explanation until Takina notices how faithfully this version of her obeys genre convention. The threat changes shape according to what they expect. Mizuki, found asleep with a sake bottle, dresses for the part of the first victim. Instead Mika is found dead under a tree, spouting far more blood than a body holds, and the parts have to be reassigned (14.97–117, 14.143–167). Mizuki is separated from them later in the forest, and only her bloodied glasses return (14.213–218, 14.251). Thoughts of killing a supposed survivor likewise belong to the dreamer (14.13–90, 14.119–178).

Inside the dream Takina proposes that they are dreaming, and that the dreamer is she (14.64–67). Dream Chisato replies in a form close to waking Chisato’s maxims (9.144, 9.400): dream or real, not enjoying the present is a loss (14.70). Her reflex punch at the window is followed by guilty creeping, for fear she has broken the stranger’s neck (14.26–34). Takina’s first move is to look for their satchels and guns (14.41–44). Her view of horror protagonists is tactical; she watches them make bad moves (14.57–59). She reasons that the word for gore-free slashers may exist for viewers who want killers without gore (14.89). She is the one who fights. She braces a foot on dead Mika’s shoulder to pull out the machete and aims at the knee because the neck is out of reach. The blade fails to bite and shatters (14.188–203). She wants a second strike, and gives it up only when Chisato tells her that this kind of killer cannot be beaten by ordinary means (14.204–211). Dream Chisato sniffs Mika’s pillow to identify him. Takina objects to the method (14.129–131), although she has used it herself (12.124) and will again on waking (14.369). Takina drops her one ruthless thought, removing the final girl first, partly because that logic would end with her fighting Chisato (14.178).

Dream Chisato announces that they are dating because she thinks that might let them live through a slasher plot. She asks Takina to say it with her. Takina has already begun to wonder what makes two people lovers, a declaration or perhaps a kiss, and broke off because something was wrong (14.244–250). She asks whether the killer would listen and does not join the declaration (14.259–270). She reasons that this excessive attachment to rules sounds more like herself than the Chisato she knows. The Chisato she knows is selfish, free and does as she pleases, and might end the plot with a bald, divorced muscleman arriving by helicopter, an image built on Takina’s own sickroom remark (14.277–278; 12.188–190). The rule-keeper at the café, she concludes, is Inoue Takina (14.280). She dislikes the convenient rules that make the killer invincible. Anything alive can be killed, she says, and she ends the plot because fighting is a bother (面倒なので). A container truck driven by Kurumi flattens the killer at once (14.281–300). The truck arrives as she wished, and she counts it for her thesis (14.306). What complicates the explanation is dream Chisato’s claim that their declaration counted (14.302–305). Takina entertains a further possibility that a “couple” might only have to be two people working together. If so, the dream need not be hers at all (14.309–312). Climbing into the truck, she had offered Chisato her hand, the reverse of 9.321 (14.295–297). She never settles what caused them to survive or whose dream it was (14.233–323). Too tired to let go, she feels the hand’s warmth to the end, while heat, stickiness and thirst leak in from her body (14.315–323). These are a dreamer’s models, not testimony about Chisato’s wishes. Dream Chisato’s claim that Mika has had a stalker (14.165) has the same standing.

What survives the cut for the reader is the sensation of a hand. The waking Takina remembers getting sick and Chisato putting her to bed in return for the earlier nursing. She remembers an intervening deployment, the night of *One’s duties* by the evidence of 13.769–771, and Chisato showing films with running commentary (14.334). A chainsaw slasher is still playing on the tablet (14.330). By her pillow is an unopened can of ゴキゲン, whose mascot was the killer’s mask (14.342; 14.195). She cannot recall the dream. The two futons meet at their heads and their hands overlap. For a moment she thinks it is like the dream, and then the dream is gone. Intent is expressly open (14.336–341). Mizuki says the get-well presents from the regulars are now for Takina. Takina first guesses they may be Chisato’s (14.355). Then she recognizes something of the care she had thought exceptional in Chisato’s case, with an understanding that itches (むずがゆさ, 14.357–358). This answers her belief at 12.669–673. Mizuki’s 油断大敵 repeats Takina’s own warning to Chisato (14.350; 12.357). Mizuki also calls some of Chisato’s activity playing, and asks whether she managed a bath. Takina’s check of Chisato’s hair supplies the final joke (14.344–371). Receiving care does not require either girl to repeat a dream declaration awake. Illustration i-307 draws Chisato’s sleeping hand over Takina’s raised one and leaves intent where the prose leaves it.

### Two endings · そして、また and the unlisted continuation

The lights come on at the cinema. Takina, who focalizes both scenes, thinks of the auditorium as a world one step short of the real one (16.4). She asks why so many people stay through the credits. Chisato pretends the question is hard (またキミはそうやって). She says that she stays through the credits for the music and the afterglow, sometimes spotting familiar names. A scene after the credits would be a pleasure, and this film does not have one (16.2–19). Takina concludes for her own reason: having paid properly, one should take in the whole work (16.19). The book has given its own reader a reason to stay. Chisato explains that their tickets buy a free crane-game attempt each, and Takina is first to ask whether that is not a very good deal (16.20–25). **Chisato counts five people and five attempts.** The line is untagged. It answers Mizuki’s doubt and precedes Mizuki’s own アタシ line, so it goes to Chisato from context (16.28–30). Mizuki questions their ability to win and wants a drink, Kurumi wants something sweet, and Mika would like to try the spa, which Mizuki notes is for men only (16.30–36). Takina registers that Chisato wanted them all to go round together (16.34). **Takina proposes that they go to the arcade together first and separate afterward**, since combined attempts give them better odds (16.20–40). Her stated reasons are only loss and odds, and they deliver the togetherness she has just seen Chisato wanted. The text does not say she wants it for its own sake. Chisato’s cry to sortie (出撃) is the line the jacket blurb quotes (16.40; S21). The S17 group illustration gives their machine a visible setting.

S18 opens with the scene separator ● used within stories (18.1). It reads most naturally as the second scene of 『そして、また』, set apart from S16 by the S17 spread and without a heading of its own. S18.25 confirms they all went to the arcade and then discussed the film over coffee before separating. Mizuki gets her beer, Mika goes to the spa, Kurumi goes for pancakes, and the partners eat together. The penguin won by the group is on Takina’s lap. Takina criticizes the excessive gore in the movie and finds a little to like in the killer’s design. Films, she says, should be chosen after gathering information, or money and time are wasted (18.4–10). When Chisato invites her to choose the next film, Takina realizes her criticism has somehow committed her to going out again. She is certain Chisato knows she has noticed (18.11–22). She feels maneuvered and takes up the invitation on a condition of her own. Chisato teases her that her pretty face has turned cute with sulking (18.23). Takina admits to herself that she enjoyed the day, so she does not mind, and that this is a little galling (嫌じゃなかった … ちょっと悔しい, 18.26–29). Chisato then offers an explicit way out: no pressure, it is up to Takina (18.30–31). Takina interrupts her to say she wants to go again (18.32). A trailer she saw before the feature caught her interest. She will decide which film to choose after more information comes out and she has looked into it. Chisato asks her to let her know if it looks promising and suggests they go together (18.33). At such moments, Takina thinks, Chisato seems an adult and herself a child, more than being one year ahead (一つ先輩) or the strongest Lycoris would explain; the smile is not her usual sunflower one (18.34–37). With half her face against the penguin, Takina squeezes out her agreement (18.38–40). The prize and shared time are accomplished facts. The proposed new screening is still ahead. The book places its acknowledgements where film credits would stand (S15) before this scene. Reading the volume as a film with a post-credits scene is an interpretation the structure invites, not a fact it states.
