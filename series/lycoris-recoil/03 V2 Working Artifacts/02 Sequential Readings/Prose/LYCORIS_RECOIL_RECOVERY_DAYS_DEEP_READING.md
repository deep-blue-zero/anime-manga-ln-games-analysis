# Recovery days · prose reading

A late cinema ticket organizes the beginning and ending of the book. In between, a script, a professional duty, a revenge story, an illness and the conventions of a horror film each promise to tell a person what should happen next. The prose repeatedly grants a focalizer confidence in the rule and then puts some desired person or activity outside it. Recovery includes manuscript files, rest, a literal return to the café and the ability to entertain another plan. Each takes work from someone who might have preferred to leave it alone.

## Source-native reading

### 0 · そして、幕が開く

Mika brings Takina coffee before she can decline it. In Takina’s view, a simple offer would have let her insist on staying at work. The narrated counterfactual shows how she understands a tactic for reaching her. It cannot show exactly what she would have done without it (9.2–29). Chisato’s wish to catch a film meets operating hours, a visitor from the police and a DA call. Kusunoki needs material recovered and explicitly authorizes Chisato’s alternative arrangement conditional on getting it back (9.190–235). Takina enjoys a commander holding firm with Chisato. The suspect survives, receives treatment and is handed over. The narration compresses this outcome but spends scene time on Chisato’s discovery that she booked the other cinema (9.259–271, 9.299–353). This reversal makes the ordinary stakes legible instead of assuming that work is the only consequential activity.

The group finally obtains time to attend together. Mika joins because he dislikes being left out. Chisato names an experience with attention and memory unlike watching at home. Takina surprises herself by agreeing to come again, with a film of her own in mind (9.386–428). The sequence distinguishes acquiring a preference from having no reservations about the person who encouraged it.

### 1 · Safety work

Itō has already modeled characters on the girls when she asks for permission. Chisato agrees readily. Takina privately wonders if working with Chisato is meant to teach her things ordinary life requires. Kusunoki does not make that intention explicit here (10.55–89). Then the prose gives Itō control of the account of a precarious deadline. The café’s voluntary help answers real stakes in someone else’s profession (10.336–395). Takina successfully prepares たまごふわふわ on a first practical attempt, drawing on something a Shizuoka teacher taught her about in Kyoto. It corrects any broad inference from the earlier lunch story that she simply lacks cooking skill (10.420–446, 10.482–541).

The joke about material looking authentic becomes a failure of mutual checking. Both partners assume the other dealt with a real object brought into a workplace. A discharge destroys the artist’s tablet. Nobody is injured and each acknowledges a share of responsibility (10.559–592). The repairs also conceal the cause from Itō. Kurumi identifies a limit to her normal work and still retrieves the files. Mika offers comfort, and an early inference by Takina about almost perfect understanding between Mika and Chisato gives way to an ordinary explanation involving prior sight (10.596–610, 10.637–659). Delivery happens. Payment is postponed, the editor complains about a damaged car, and Mizuki covers the café alone (10.665–702). The source does not show a serialization award.

### 2 · Dog

Mizukawa takes his debt to Okada as a reason to avenge him. He supplies the missing purpose of Okada’s final words himself. His focalization allows the reader to mistake who stands on which side of the café’s protective commission (11.53–83, 11.241–280). Chisato eventually identifies Mizukawa as the person she was paid to keep safe. She offers the boss’s possible completion of Okada’s words and says openly that she cannot know (11.373–405). Her appeal works by giving an avenger a reason to keep living that does not require the certainty he sought. Takina initially identifies securing the person with completion of the task and stays while Chisato talks.

The later return with his boss revisits what Mizukawa learned from Okada. It gives evidence of reconsideration, but the boss still attacks the restrained adversary and proposes shifting responsibility (11.408–425). Chisato’s intervention stops one form of harm at that moment. It cannot reimburse the relationship or the surrounding organization with innocence.

### 3 · Cough

Takina removes an actually ill Chisato from service. The manga deadline and Chisato’s solo cinema visit bound prior events but do not identify the group screening in chapter 0 (12.35–75, 12.105–140). Takina offers food that balances appetite, rest and work, and can adapt to a boundary when Chisato objects to being watched changing (12.231–266, 12.323–383). Regulars and staff find occasions to play around the patient. At a moment of gratitude Takina thinks that Chisato is the sort of exceptional person others are glad to worry about. Her own experience of such care seems thinner (12.483–517, 12.662–673).

Takina researches a place she would like to visit together. Chisato is caught warming the thermometer and admits she had recovered enough to play with Kurumi. The prose explicitly withholds why she sent Takina home. Later deception does not undo the first fever. Takina’s own cough interrupts a lecture about discipline before a disease or chain of transmission is established (12.662–724).

### 4 · One’s duties

The account of DA’s ranks distinguishes prestige from how the organization uses people. Thirds supply numerical capacity, Seconds matter particularly in cities and Firsts can supervise without constituting the majority of operations (13.4–32). This is exposition specific to the novel, not an A1 staffing census. Fuki initially commends Sakura’s dependable response and suspects a reason their assignment works. Under pressure to check on Sakura she has to argue with herself to follow the order (13.46–67, 13.193–205, 13.395–402). She understands an adversary’s logic and feels repelled by his pleasure. Her work brings fatigue and relief, and she remembers having been assigned it before she could choose (13.519–540, 13.617–636). The novel separates competence, obligation, pleasure and assent.

Sakura saves Fuki. Fuki knows her footsteps and names her as her partner (13.711–735). Fuki meanwhile makes up an excuse to see Mika and finds his manner encompassing when they meet. Both Mika and Chisato offer a place in the vehicle. Fuki refuses because she feels there is no seat for her beside the former partner who left, the one she says she discarded and her teacher (13.738–829). That is her metaphor alongside the literal offer. It does not imply collective exclusion, a romantic confession, or a decision to abandon Sakura. Her final invitation to go home addresses Sakura.

### 5 · Common occurrence

The dream begins with Takina testing a memory gap. Its Chisato treats a disreputable horror convention as an obligation, and Takina calls the apparent Chisato’s inflexibility into question. A declaration of dating is proposed as an in-world means of survival. Takina asks whether the pair have the relationship this implies and whether the condition requires anything besides two partners (14.64–89, 14.233–323). The narrator grants her successive hypotheses. It leaves their world unset until she wakes.

The waking scene confirms warmth and hands in contact. Takina cannot remember the dream and does not establish whether the contact was intended (14.325–341). The regulars’ gifts then appear to have been meant for her. She can reconsider her earlier assumption that concern belongs to exceptional people like Chisato. Mizuki dismisses part of Chisato’s activity as playing. The hair-smell joke gives the interaction a mundane end (14.344–371). Real care and a privately experienced dream therefore contribute different things.

### Production and ending scenes

The author’s afterword identifies a mixture of official-note serialization and original additions without identifying the genealogy of every individual story (15.4–8). The printed film frame resumes in the listed scene. Members of the group propose different ways to spend their remaining time. Takina’s calculation of what five attempts are worth gives them a collective first destination and still permits later separation (16.20–40). In the unlisted continuation, Takina recalls both dissatisfaction with the movie and pleasure in the day. She feels Chisato arranged the idea of going again, then explicitly accepts an offer to help choose after checking the next release (18.11–40). Takina can feel steered and also choose.
