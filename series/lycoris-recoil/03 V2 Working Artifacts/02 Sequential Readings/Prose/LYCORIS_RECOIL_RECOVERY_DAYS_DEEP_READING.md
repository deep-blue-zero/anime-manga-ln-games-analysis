# Recovery days · prose reading

Chisato gives Takina an argument for going to the cinema. Buying the ticket sets aside two hours in which they will do nothing else, and afterward there will be someone to talk to about the film. The novel lets Takina dislike much of what they actually watch. She wants to go again anyway. The cases between the opening and those two endings repeatedly let a character revise what she thinks she agreed to. Recovering an artist’s work, bringing someone home alive and convincing a patient to rest can all succeed while leaving a real dispute about how the result was obtained.

## Source-native reading

### 0 · そして、幕が開く

Mika’s coffee is already there before Takina can decline a break. She appreciates the care and thinks that, had he asked before serving it, she would have stayed at work. That unused alternative is hers to imagine (9.8–31). Chisato’s invitation to the cinema gives Takina more room to object, though she privately feels her evening has already been spoken for (9.154–168). The contrast matters because the novel ends with an invitation to which Takina supplies a reason of her own.

Detective Abe (阿部) comes in for coffee and hears Chisato arguing about the late show (9.135–145). Café hours and a call from Kusunoki keep the movie clock running. Kusunoki allows Chisato to use a cleaner and charge the expense if the materials and arms are recovered. Takina enjoys hearing someone tell Chisato what must be done. The possibility of an armed fugitive moving along the streets their customers use changes what Chisato is willing to interrupt the outing for (9.190–235). The operation itself is told after its conclusion. They secure the items, have the suspect treated, hand him to the cleaner and ship what they recovered to Kusunoki. The rush makes their handling of the assignment look reckless even in success (9.258–266). The novel spends more time on making the screening because Chisato has booked the other cinema. Even at the right building, she still stops for the popcorn and drinks she meant to share (9.299–355).

The turnout looks like a unanimous endorsement until each colleague supplies a different reason for being there. Mizuki imagines talking about the film with an actor she admires. Kurumi has come to see the splatter film after taking down the illicit uploads. Mika admits that he would be lonely as the one left out (9.363–387). Chisato argues for sustained attention and the memory of time spent together. Takina prefers a different trailer, Chisato proposes seeing both, and Takina agrees so readily that it surprises her. The soda straw gives her a moment to cover the surprise. As the lights go down she thinks she may be one of the people who indulge Chisato (9.397–428). Her distinct taste has already entered the arrangement.

### 1 · Safety work

The manga artist Itō has already used Chisato and Takina as character models by the time she asks them. Chisato readily accepts. Takina briefly thinks she may have too little experience of everyday life to judge even this irregular request. Her thought that Kusunoki might have sent her to learn such things breaks off. It gives no new order from her commander (10.55–89). Itō’s professional difficulty has a shape of its own. The opening in the publication schedule is scarce, much of the drawing already exists, and a dramatic pose still has to read on the page. Takina pictures what would actually happen in the position Itō proposes. Chisato helps make a viable composition, and Itō uses Takina’s technical idea for another view (10.191–216, 10.221–275, 10.336–395). The girls’ ordinary trained movements and the artist’s needs cross without becoming interchangeable.

Feeding an exhausted person produces a smaller argument about energy, appetite, pantry supplies and time. Takina makes たまごふわふわ, drawing on a dish a Shizuoka teacher had taught her about in Kyoto. This is her first practical attempt. She is proud of the result and tries to hide it (10.420–478, 10.482–544). The earlier novel’s uncooperative rice balls cannot establish a general incapacity to cook.

The partners make a consequential mistake precisely where each expects the other to have checked. A real gun brought as a reference discharges in Itō’s hands. The tablet and wall are damaged. Nobody is injured. Takina apologizes and Chisato acknowledges her own omission (10.559–592). They offer Itō a misleading account of what has happened even as they set to work saving her manuscript. Kurumi explains the limits of her usual work, then recovers the files. Takina’s impression that Mika and Chisato understand each other almost perfectly changes when she recalls Chisato went back to fetch Kurumi. Takina infers Chisato could have seen Mika still at the shop. The restorative coffee wakes the artist by being hot (10.596–610, 10.637–659). The editor reports car trouble and receives the finished pages. Takina has inferred Kurumi’s part in the delay. Payment is put off and Mizuki gets the café’s shift alone (10.669–702). The manuscript reaches its editor. A future serialization is still a possibility.

### 2 · Dog

The gang boss calls Kōsuke Mizukawa (水川コウスケ) the guard dog of Okada, the man who brought him into the organization (11.3–5, 11.18–32, 11.53). Mizukawa owes Okada a refuge and a way of living, so the description fits well enough to hurt. Asked about his patron’s last words, he recalls being invited to eat ramen when Okada returns. These were the last words *he personally heard*. The boss tells him that the dying man called his name. Mizukawa imagines a threat to Ijūin or an instruction to take revenge. Neither is a recovered utterance (11.53–83). He resists thinking of himself as someone who wants indiscriminate harm while accepting its possibility on the way to the man he wants (11.207–215).

A protective commission appears to place the café on the other side. Chisato gives its actual object at 11.383–389. The boss hired the girls to keep Mizukawa alive. Even her earlier way of addressing him as a loyal dog could have told him something about who hired her. The fuller reported fragment still leaves Okada’s sentence unfinished. The boss’s reconstruction, which Chisato offers to Mizukawa, is that Okada wanted him *stopped*. She explicitly admits nobody can know. What she will guarantee is the wish of an owner for his dog’s happiness. The change from a conjecture about words to an assurance about affection makes her intervention possible without making the conjecture true. Takina considers securing the protected person the formal end of the job. Chisato stays a little longer to speak to him (11.373–406).

Once the pair have left, Mizukawa reaches for the blade and actually picks it up. He remembers that Okada never taught him to kill. What Okada taught him was how to live as a person. Only then does the boss arrive, hurt the already beaten Ijūin himself and suggest letting the girl take the blame. He tells Mizukawa to come home. After a long silence Mizukawa says yes (11.407–425). Recollection has changed the meaning of loyalty enough for him to go with the boss. The revenge scene also shows the violence the organization remains willing to displace onto somebody else.

### 3 · Cough

Takina sends Chisato off the floor for the customers’ sake as well as her own. After the manga deadline, Chisato went to a film on her own and then helped Mizuki, who had been running the café alone. The solo visit does not date the group screening (12.26–75, 12.99–140). The thermometer later reads 37.9°C. Takina’s account of what the body is doing with rest is an interpretation of that reading (12.147–155). Concerned about asking for an early walk, Chisato hesitates before naming the fresh red rice she would like from the next morning’s market. Takina agrees to fetch it and points out they still need dinner (12.228–249). She finds a clay-pot udon solution with Mizuki’s help, defers serving it when Mika spots the sleeping patient, and turns around when Chisato asks for privacy while changing (12.201–383). She can care firmly while making room for a specific refusal.

Mika brings Chisato bananas from Gotō, cooling sheets from Itō and vitamins from Kitamura. The box of jelly drinks is Yamadera’s. The three boxes of sweets from the writer Yoneoka are for Kurumi, who rescued his computer, a distinction Mika catches before handing them over (12.13, 12.393–420). The patient can also arrange something for her caregiver. A video spares Chisato the need to dress and greet the regulars, and puts Takina in their game night. Takina recognizes the second purpose. The customers’ affection feels good and raises a question about how visible a Lycoris should be. On her walk she gets lost, uses her phone and imagines where Chisato might want to go with her. Buying two large portions serves Takina’s own appetite too (12.428–518, 12.520–593). At the meal neither promptly proposes the second helping that both have left room for (12.598–657). In characterizing Chisato as someone it is rewarding to worry about, Takina still doubts that anyone would worry about her in quite the same way.

Takina returns to propose another walk and catches Chisato warming the thermometer. Chisato had recovered after the udon and a nap, then played with Kurumi into the morning. Takina suspects she had been sent home so that she would not stop the game. Chisato cites concern for her. The narration leaves *that* motive private, even as it identifies the later effort to feign illness as an attempt to gain another day’s sleep. Takina coughs in the middle of a lecture on discipline (12.662–724). The subsequent story establishes that she developed a fever. Who passed it to whom remains unestablished.

### 4 · One’s duties

DA’s ranks describe assignments as well as prestige. Thirds supply numbers across the country, Seconds matter especially in cities and Firsts can supervise without taking all of the everyday work (13.4–32). Fuki appreciates Sakura’s ready answer, recognizes how a partner’s manners affect an assignment and suspects reasons for the pairing that Kusunoki has never confirmed (13.46–67, 13.193–205). A high-five can mean something quite different under pressure (13.141–161). This episode of institutional life supplies the novel’s own account of how people are used. It supplies no numerical census of the anime organization.

When Sakura disappears, Fuki wants to go to her immediately but obeys an order to identify an adversary. Her inner argument lets her see reasons for obedience and still shows how hard she has to work to follow it (13.391–402). She can understand the gunman’s explanation of his pleasure without sharing it. Fuki distinguishes him from the rider and the hunter in her account of the encounter (13.515–533). Fatigue and relief attend the outcome of her own work. Her life in it began before she could choose it (13.513–545, 13.617–636). Later she moves off without her usual precaution in her haste to reach Sakura. Surprised at her own action, she infers how much she has been worrying about her (13.653–659). She also tries to infer a woman’s relationship to the dead gunman after the woman asks whether Fuki killed him. The woman’s grief and their supposed history together remain conjectures from Fuki’s position (13.662–695).

Sakura saves her. Fuki praises her accuracy and says she can distinguish her partner’s footfall. Sakura repeats the word “partner” and smiles. It matters who arrived first. The crew with Mika reaches the scene after this intervention (13.709–745). Fuki then invents a reason to go up to the helicopter. The narration supplies the simpler wish to see her teacher (13.745–748). Seeing Takina’s pallor does not let Fuki diagnose her. Chisato tells her Takina is unwell and wanted to come anyway (13.763–771).

Mika and Chisato actually offer the ride. Fuki refuses, and Chisato asks again, this time without her bravado. Fuki sees Chisato, the partner who left her, Takina, the partner she says she discarded, and Mika, the teacher who mattered so much to her. She feels she has no place in that vehicle. Sakura, beside her, would have liked a helicopter ride. Fuki’s metaphor and her reason for going up to the roof tell us different things about where she wants to be. She leaves with the present partner she addresses directly (13.781–829). Affection for Mika, pain about the former partnerships and a real attachment to Sakura have different evidence in this scene.

### 5 · Common occurrence

A memory gap starts Takina testing the world she is in. Chisato’s horror vocabulary seems an explanation until Takina notices how faithfully this version of her obeys genre convention. The threat changes shape according to what they expect. Missing Mizuki seems to have one part in the imagined bloodshed, then the fantastic quantity of Mika’s blood makes the assignment look different. Thoughts of killing a supposed survivor likewise belong to the dreamer (14.13–90, 14.119–178).

Dream Chisato announces that they are dating because she thinks that might let them live through a slasher plot. She asks Takina to say it with her. Takina asks whether the killer would listen and does not join the declaration. She reasons that this excessive attachment to rules sounds more like herself than the Chisato she knows. Kurumi’s rescue complicates the explanation, and Takina entertains a further possibility that a “couple” might only have to be two people working together. She never settles what caused them to survive (14.233–323).

What survives the cut for the reader is the sensation of a hand. The waking Takina remembers getting sick, being put to bed, an intervening deployment and the film Chisato played. She cannot recall the dream. The two futons meet at their heads and their hands overlap. Intent is expressly open (14.325–341). Mizuki says the get-well presents from the regulars are now for Takina, who recognizes something of the care she had thought exceptional in Chisato’s case. Mizuki also calls some of Chisato’s activity playing, and asks whether she managed a bath. Takina’s check of Chisato’s hair supplies the final joke (14.344–371). Receiving care does not require either girl to repeat a dream declaration awake.

### Two endings · そして、また and the unlisted continuation

The lights come on at the cinema. Chisato says that she stays through the credits for the music and the afterglow, sometimes spotting familiar names. A scene after the credits would be a pleasure, and this film does not have one (16.2–19). The book has given its own reader a reason to stay. Chisato explains that their tickets buy a free crane-game attempt each. **Chisato counts five people and five attempts.** Mizuki questions their ability to win and wants a drink, Kurumi wants something sweet, and Mika would like to try the spa. **Takina proposes that they go to the arcade together first and separate afterward**, since combined attempts give them better odds (16.20–40). Economics gives her a way to make a shared suggestion she actually wants. The S17 group illustration gives their machine a visible setting.

S18.25 confirms they all went to the arcade and then discussed the film over coffee before separating. Mizuki gets her beer, Mika goes to the spa, Kurumi goes for pancakes, and the partners eat together. The penguin won by the group is on Takina’s lap. Takina criticizes the excessive gore in the movie and finds a little to like in the killer’s design. When Chisato invites her to choose the next film, Takina realizes her criticism has somehow committed her to going out again. She feels maneuvered, takes pleasure in recalling the day, and takes up the invitation on a condition of her own. When Chisato leaves the next outing up to her, Takina says she wants to go again. A trailer she saw before the feature caught her interest. She will decide which film to choose after more information comes out and she has looked into it. Chisato asks her to let her know if it looks promising and suggests they go together. With half her face against the penguin, Takina agrees (18.25–40). The prize and shared time are accomplished facts. The proposed new screening is still ahead.
