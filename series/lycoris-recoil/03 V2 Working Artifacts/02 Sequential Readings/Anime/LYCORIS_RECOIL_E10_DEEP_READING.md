# 2. Executive thesis

Episode 10 is about **what remains morally one's own after institutions, benefactors, parents, enemies, and mortality all claim the right to define the meaning of a life**.

E09 established that Chisato's helping vocation emerged through a childhood reinterpretation of Alan's `使命` and `救世主` language. E10 places that self-authorship under retrospective assault. Mika finally admits that when he sought Yoshimatsu's artificial-heart intervention he did so, at least initially, as a commander pursuing institutional benefit, and that Yoshimatsu's price was a promise to raise Chisato as `最強の殺し屋`.

Chisato's answer is not that the origin was innocent. It is that **the life she actually lived was authored by her**:

> `私に決めさせてくれて ありがとう`
>
> `私の仕事も このお店を始めたのも 全部 私が決めたこと`

She constructs a counterfactual in which full disclosure of the killing mandate might have defeated her into resentful compliance. Mika's silence, however morally compromised, left open the space in which she could decide what the gun, the café, her work, and the time she had been given were for. Her gratitude to Mika and Yoshimatsu therefore survives the revelation—not because their intended purpose was legitimate, but because **her meaning was never reducible to their intent**.

Takina undergoes a parallel institutional transformation. Back inside DA, she does not return to the E03 grammar in which reinstatement itself proved value. She interrogates a captured intermediary for the route from Majima to Alan and Yoshimatsu, tells Majima directly `私は あなたに興味はない`, and uses the possibility of being expelled again as leverage because:

> `割と外の暮らしも楽しかったんで`
>
> `また クビになるのも いいかなあと…`

The outside life has become a credible fallback. DA still supplies authority, access, information, and force—but it no longer monopolizes the definition of where Takina can live meaningfully.

At the institutional level, E10 also forces DA's legitimacy claim into the open. Kusunoki tells Majima that DA predates the modern legal order and has cultivated the nation's security and morals across political regimes. Her ideal of peace is explicit:

> `真の平和とは 悪意の存在すら 感じない世界のことだ`

Majima correctly identifies the democratic/legitimacy problem in a secret pre-legal coercive institution. But his answer is not civic emancipation. He secretly distributes one thousand firearms, broadcasts hidden violence into public space, and tells civilians:

> `何をするかは お前らしだいだ`

DA withholds dangerous truth in the name of peace; Majima forces dangerous truth and lethal possibility onto people who did not choose his experiment. E10 therefore refuses to collapse anti-paternalism into Majima's libertarian violence.

The final hostage structure condenses the entire episode. DA orders Chisato toward Enkuboku because many lives are at risk. Robota threatens Yoshimatsu's life if she approaches. Chisato refuses to treat Yoshimatsu as expendable, but she also does not frame the situation as one life simply defeating many. She trusts Takina and Fuki to handle Enkuboku and chooses the unresolved person-specific task only she is positioned to pursue:

> `延空木は たきなやフキが守ってくれる`

The episode ends before the consequences are known. The source-native horizon must end there.

The governing E10 movement is:

`assigned purpose / compromised origin / hidden authority / imposed truth`

answered by

`authored responsibility / plural competence / gratitude without ownership / relationship without captivity`.

> `戸籍のないリコリスは うってつけってわけか`

This is an in-world inference, not independent documentary confirmation. Its importance is nonetheless substantial. E02 established that Chisato and Takina lack ordinary family-registration status sufficient to obtain passports. **E10-CLM-001 — STRONG:** Kurumi/Mizuki explicitly infer that the lack of ordinary family-registration status can make Lycoris useful for illicit human experimentation. > `このお店は 最後まで 楽しい場所じゃないとね`

She does not want the terminal phase of her own life to reorganize everyone around maintenance of her. She also refuses to let the social space become defined only by impending death.

# 26. Ethics and political theory

## 26.1 Self-authorship is not origin purity

E10's strongest ethical proposition is not “parents should hide difficult truths.” It is narrower and more demanding: a person's life cannot be reduced to the purpose for which others created an opportunity.

Chisato's artificial heart, gun training, café resources, and institutional background all have compromised origins or dependencies. Yet the purposes she authored with them remain morally and biographically hers.

## 26.2 Gratitude does not generate ownership

The Alan conflict is partly about a familiar moral mistake: converting rescue into debt and debt into purpose. E10 gives Chisato the clearest explicit rejection of that conversion while preserving gratitude.

## 26.3 DA and Alan converge structurally at the point of instrumentalization

Their stated missions differ, but both contain a logic in which exceptional persons are resources for a supra-individual end. Upper DA's `飼っていた` makes the structural comparison unusually difficult to dismiss.

## 26.4 Majima reveals a real legitimacy problem and responds illegitimately

His question—whether DA exists under law—is serious. His answer—placing hidden guns among civilians and forcing a live social experiment—is not a neutral restoration of agency. It is coercive exposure.

## 26.5 The episode rejects both epistemic paternalism and forced revelation as complete political ideals

DA assumes citizens should not even perceive malice. Majima assumes they should be violently forced to confront it. E10 does not endorse either as sufficient.

## 26.6 Distributed competence prevents false heroic dilemmas

The ending is not yet a solved moral dilemma. Chisato's trust in Takina/Fuki means her pursuit of Yoshimatsu is not simply equivalent to abandoning Enkuboku. E11+ outcome evidence remains inadmissible here.

# 31. Source-native synthesis before V1 comparison

At the E10 horizon, the series' ethical architecture is increasingly legible as a contest over **authorship without isolation**.

Chisato does not claim to have made herself from nothing. Mika named her, trained her, created a place for her, enabled her relationship with Takina, and searched for Yoshimatsu. Yoshimatsu supplied the life-saving technological possibility. DA supplied training, capability, access, and infrastructure. Chisato's position is instead that receipt does not equal ownership. What matters is whether a person may decide what those inherited capacities and relationships become.

Takina is independently arriving at the same structural freedom through a different personality. She does not abandon systems. She learns that systems can be tools rather than total identities. She can return to DA without making DA the final judge of whether her life is worthwhile because she now knows, from experience, that `外の暮らしも楽しかった`.

Mika's E10 confession reveals the cost of care emerging from compromised institutions. He was not originally a pure counter-institutional father; he participated in the bargain. Yet the fatherhood that followed included precisely the space in which Chisato was able to author a different life. E10 refuses both easy absolution and easy contamination-by-origin.

DA and Majima then scale the same problem to politics. DA gives citizens peace by withholding knowledge and coercive reality. Majima tries to restore agency by forcibly exposing reality and distributing lethal choice. One denies public authorship through concealment; the other violates it through imposed danger. The episode's ordinary-life ethic suggests a harder middle position: real agency requires information and choice, but also reciprocal constraint against treating other people as material for one's moral experiment.

This source-native interpretation is now fixed. V1 comparison may evaluate prior formulations against it but may not rewrite the prospective E10 reading or `CP3_POST_E10` predictions.
