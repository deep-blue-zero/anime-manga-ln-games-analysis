# 2. Executive thesis

Episode 05 turns the E04 conflict between **self-authored purpose** and **externally assigned purpose** into a deliberately engineered moral test.

Chisato's artificial heart is now explicit. Mika states that DA did not create it; the Alan Institute is implicated, and he describes the transaction as life accompanied by a world-facing mission. Kurumi asks what Chisato's mission is. Mika answers:

> `それは千束が決めることだ`

Later, a fabricated terminal client invokes the opposite logic. The speaker controlling “Matsushita” knows the Alan pendant, calls Chisato an Alan Child, and asks:

> `なんのために命をもらったんだ`

The demand is that Chisato convert the life she received into a killing obligation. Chisato refuses. Her answer is not merely “I dislike killing.” She defines her own vocation:

> `私はね 人の命は奪いたくないんだ`
>
> `私はリコリスだけど 誰かを助ける仕事をしたい`
>
> `これをくれた人みたいにね`

E05 therefore establishes two incompatible moral grammars of a life-saving gift:

`gift received -> debt -> externally specified purpose`

versus

`gift received -> gratitude/meaning -> recipient authors what that life is for`

The episode also subjects Chisato's E04 finite-time ethic to adversarial pressure. “Matsushita” claims terminal illness, murdered family, and no remaining time. The scenario attempts to convert another person's finite future into a warrant for revenge killing. Chisato does not accept that conversion. She first chooses evacuation and safety, then refuses to kill Jin even while believing the client's story. Her nonlethality is therefore not merely easy mercy under low stakes; it survives a constructed last-wish/revenge case designed to make lethal action emotionally legible.

At the same time, E05 complicates truth and deception. DA's public-order machinery continues to rewrite violence as accident. Lower-level police discover the lie, and one detective explicitly accepts concealment if it allows ordinary girls such as Chisato to live safely. The Alan-linked scheme uses a different kind of deception: a terminal patient's body, remote control, synthetic speech, fabricated biography, and staged assassin are used to shape one person's choice. Both systems manipulate epistemic conditions, but for different ends.

The episode closes on a smaller, relational answer to that problem. Chisato is hurt that the conversation and praise from “Matsushita” may all have been false. Takina does not offer abstract comfort. She separates the false identity from the valid local fact:

> `いいガイドだったのは ウソじゃないと思います`

Takina then returns to the artificial-heart question she initiated earlier. Chisato had objected only to checking her chest in public. Takina waits until nobody else is present and then checks. This is not merely increasing bodily proximity. It is evidence that Takina can probe a social boundary directly, receive a specific constraint, retain it, and later satisfy her curiosity inside the permitted condition.

E05's central developmental structure is therefore:

`assigned purpose and manufactured stories attempt to control action`

against

`self-authored purpose + bounded trust + evidence partitioning preserve agency`
