# 2. Executive thesis

Episode 04 turns E03's abstract permission to choose a life into the much harder practical question: **what does a person do when institutional usefulness has supplied most of the vocabulary by which she knows how to choose?**

Takina enters ordinary-life domains by repeatedly asking for an assignment, a specification, or a function. Underwear should be whatever is designated. Civilian clothes may also have a designated form. Underwear should be chosen for movement and combat. Even at the aquarium, she initially insists that being out of uniform does not stop them from being Lycoris.

But E04 does not present this as a fixed incapacity. Across a single day Takina begins generating preference statements and person-directed judgments that cannot be reduced to DA utility. She says she likes the dormitory karinto. She actively asks about Chisato's nonlethal ammunition and the reason she left DA. She remembers Chisato's `したいこと 最優先` principle and uses it to infer a missing motive. She asks about the Alan pendant and Chisato's talent. Most importantly for the relationship model, she directly tells Chisato not to hide an embarrassing garden-eel imitation and says it is very cute.

The developmental movement is therefore not:

`no preferences -> preferences`

but:

`institutional/function-first choice grammar -> concrete exposure -> comparison -> self-authored preference and evaluation becoming increasingly available`

Chisato supplies the complementary model. She treats roles as bounded rather than total. Takina says, in effect, "we are Lycoris even here"; Chisato answers that when they are not wearing the uniform they are not Lycoris. Later, when they encounter the aftermath of a major incident, Chisato physically stops Takina from reaching for her gun and reminds her that drawing it in civilian clothes would get her arrested. Capability does not create continuous duty.

That ordinary-life philosophy is not merely decorative. E04 gives Chisato her clearest source-native explanation yet for nonlethality: taking another person's time feels bad, and being forced by a bad person to feel that way is even worse, so she hits them hard enough not to die. In the same episode she argues that a human life contains only a finite number of meals and therefore each meal should be delicious, fun, and happy. The shared grammar is finite opportunity and experiential value. E04 supports that connection without yet establishing any biography-based mortality cause.

The episode then reveals the ideological antagonist to Chisato's self-authorship. Yoshimatsu says genius is a gift from God that must be delivered to the world and explicitly names Chisato's rare genius for killing. Alan support is therefore not neutral cultivation. At the E04 horizon, Yoshimatsu treats aptitude as obligation: what a person can do becomes what that person ought to be made to do.

Majima supplies a second antagonist. He experiences Japan's sanitized public order as a `healthy and unhealthy lie` and speaks of restoring balance. After the subway attack is publicly rewritten as an accident, he does not become reassured by concealment. He resolves to do something so large that it cannot be lied away. E04 therefore makes information control causally dangerous: the mechanism designed to preserve the myth of peace becomes one of the incentives for escalation against it.

# 11. Chisato's nonlethality: time, aversion, and punitive force

> `00-12-40.320` - （千束）そう 悪人に そんな気持ちにさせられるのは

> `00-12-47.620` - あれ 当たると めちゃくちゃ痛いのよ

The safest E04 formulation is therefore not "Chisato refuses violence." It is:

> **Chisato refuses to take away another person's remaining time, while retaining a strong willingness to inflict painful nonlethal punishment on people who force violence onto her.**

What E04 does *not* establish is that this philosophy is caused by Chisato's own mortality, medical history, or expected lifespan. Those biography-level explanations remain outside the current horizon.

**E04-CLM-010 - HIGH:** Chisato directly explains nonlethality through aversion to depriving another person of time, not through generic pacifism or a prohibition on painful force. `[JP][ETHICS]`

**E04-CLM-011 - STRONG:** E04 supports a finite-time value grammar spanning ordinary pleasure and nonlethality, but the causal link to Chisato's personal mortality remains OPEN. `[JP][ETHICS][BOUNDARY]`
