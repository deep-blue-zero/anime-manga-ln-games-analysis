# 2. Executive thesis


Episode 02 develops E01's concern with hidden systems of order into a broader argument about **controlled belief, provisional identities, and the ethical consequences of refusing to treat a role as the whole person**.


The episode repeatedly makes survival and power depend on what somebody else is made to believe:


- DA discovers that its gun-operation timing was false;  
- Walnut's pursuers are defeated not by killing Walnut's identity-holder but by making them believe Walnut is dead;  
- Robota hides ordinary vulnerability behind a hacker persona and sees killing as competitive cleanup;  
- Yoshimatsu's Alan affiliation retains a public language of anonymous beneficence while he privately commissions elimination;  
- Chisato and Takina protect the state while existing outside ordinary civic identity strongly enough that Chisato says they lack `戸籍` and cannot obtain passports;  
- Kurumi can enter LycoReco's ordinary social world only after the operational identity `ウォールナット` has been allowed to die.


Against those informational systems, Chisato supplies a different refusal of fixed classification. Her most important E02 line is:


> `あの人たちも 今回は敵だっただけだよ`


The armed men were **only enemies this time**.


This specifies E01's `命 大事に` rule. Chisato does not merely spare enemies because killing is personally distasteful. At the E02 horizon, she behaves as though **enemy status is contingent while personhood persists across the classification**.


The episode refuses to make that principle costless. Takina's critique is operationally serious: Chisato stops during an active extraction to treat a wounded attacker, separates from the team, and the remaining group reaches an ambush in which the protected client appears to be killed. The later fake-death reveal removes the apparent fatal outcome, but it does not erase Takina's causal argument that Chisato's policy can create mission risk.


At the same time, the wounded attacker Chisato treats voluntarily warns her about the ambush. The episode therefore produces a striking causal loop:


> Chisato refuses to collapse a person into `enemy` -> the enemy is temporarily restored to human reciprocity -> that person gives information that protects Chisato and Takina.


This does **not** retroactively make mercy correct because it was useful. Chisato helps him before any benefit is offered. The stronger point is epistemic and relational: E02 demonstrates why permanent enemy classification can be false even in the middle of violence.


Takina does not convert. She continues to frame the problem through authorized role and operational result:


> `私たちリコリスは 殺人が許可されています！`


and:


> `そういう話じゃないと思います`


The dyad therefore advances socially and tactically faster than it advances ethically. They share food, exchange small jabs, divide technical tasks, and operate as a more functional pair, while the central dispute over what another person's life can demand of a mission remains unresolved.
