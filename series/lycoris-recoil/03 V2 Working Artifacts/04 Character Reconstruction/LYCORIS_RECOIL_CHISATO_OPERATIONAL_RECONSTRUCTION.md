# Predicting Nishikigi Chisato’s next move

Chisato notices a person before she decides which job she is doing. What would make the next few minutes better for them? Who else is in a position to help? Who would lose time, a way out, or control over their own life if she said yes? Those questions explain why she can stay with an injured enemy, decline a purported dying man’s request, eat dessert while off duty, and want to rescue a benefactor whom she tells Mika she distrusts. They do not guarantee that she asks what the other person wants. She can move too fast, invent their reason for smiling, or deprive them of a choice because she thinks she is protecting their time. A plausible prediction needs both her first move and the conditions under which someone can make her change it.

Use this model to predict **a choice, an observable response and a possible revision**. Start with the episode state, who has told Chisato what, the addressee, and whether that person can decline. Specify an actual obligation or cost that could defeat her first preference. Then choose a form of address. Her cheerful delivery alone predicts little about whether she means to welcome, deflect, challenge an order or leave before somebody can stop her. The [CP5 Chisato profile](LYCORIS_RECOIL_CHISATO_BEHAVIORAL_RECONSTRUCTION.md) remains the frozen anime-only record. The current model adds conditions and alternatives. The supplementary portrayals below can be selected separately. None of the invented lines in this document counts as a source quotation or test result.

For near repetitions the title uses **R1**. **R2** interpolates between several attested choices, **R3** marks a consequential untested variable, and **R4** recognizes an open simulation. These are distances from evidence. Confidence can be high for the ordering of actions and low for a joke’s exact wording in exactly the same case. The generic method’s D0–D3 can additionally describe whether the occasion is attested, a close variant, a novel in-world case or a materially different setting. For a distant setting explicitly supply chronology, relationship, local role and resources. At R4, name the missing private fact, relationship history or assent that prevents a choice, and leave the outcome open.

## How the person in front of her changes the answer

**Takina.** Chisato can open first and name the partnership without ceremony; early Takina may hear an order. Once Takina elects to join a game, plans an outing herself or recognizes pleasure in a film she has watched, let her answers change the pace. Chisato’s delight at learning in Short 02 that Takina is excited becomes a laugh, an exclamation and a plan. It is not a verbatim echo of Takina’s admission. In Short 03 she hears the word イノベーション, asks for it again, recognizes *The Social Network*, and adapts it to herself. With a distressed Takina in E12 she thanks her and can still physically prevent something Takina wants intensely. With an absent Takina on the island she can leave the friend whose time she means to protect no say in her departure. Long familiarity supports shorthand, not mind-reading. [CHI-MB-E01-002, E03-003, E09-004; E12-CLM-021–024; SHORT02 C058–063/080, SHORT03 C022–035]

**Mika and Yoshimatsu.** Mika is 先生, a person before whom an exuberant closing day can give way to an admission of loneliness and an explicit request for recognition. Yoshimatsu remains ヨシさん even while she objects to his treatment of a life, refuses the future he assigns, tends his wound and returns his token. A generic model of “a father figure whose approval she needs” would produce the wrong answer for at least one of these men. With Mika she can seek a personal judgment. With Yoshimatsu in E12 her own knowledge of having been happy directly challenges his claim to define it. If either gives her a credible, entirely new fact, what the fact is and who told it remain part of the new premise. [E07-CLM-015–021, E10-CLM-019–030, E12-CLM-005–022, E13-CLM-031–037]

**Fuki, Kusunoki, Kurumi, Mizuki and the public.** With Fuki familiarity allows teasing about Mika. Fuki can still reserve permission for use of her own uniform. Chisato later accepts Mika’s assessment that Fuki and Takina can handle the Enkuboku defense. With Kusunoki Chisato can mock the official voice, set conditions or press a real case; agreement by the commander requires separate evidence. Kurumi gets an invitation to eat under her own name after Walnut’s apparent death, and can contribute knowledge Chisato lacks. Mizuki can bargain with her about the price of an ordinary mistake. With Majima a shared film conversation can suspend active hostility briefly. It does not entitle Majima to speak for Chisato. A new customer's “no” has its own context. A practiced café welcome can also be a comic response to someone who has come to rob the place. [E03 §14, 07:09–08:07, for Fuki; CHI-MB-E03-002 for pressing Kusunoki about Takina; CHI-MB-E02-006 for Kurumi; CHI-MB-E06-003; E08-CLM-013; E10 C0440–0442 for Mika’s assessment and Chisato’s assent; SHORT02 C035–041/065–080; SHORT04; SHORT05-CLM-003.]

**Voice, movement and evidence.** Start with what the utterance needs to do. A first meeting may license a stretched introduction and an immediate question. If a bit lands with Takina, Chisato may play back one of her words; an invented line need not imitate the actor's pitch or include a trademark phrase. Direct ありがとう, an unadorned 違う or a quietly specific request of her teacher can carry more in that scene than another joke. E12-AUD-012 and -013 refer to different speakers and propositions. E06 gives a loud game reaction to Takina; high activation need not mean graver attachment. Caption gaps time text, mixed-audio levels describe the mix, and attributed continuous listening supports whatever local feature its reviewers identified. An action benefiting from sightlines can fail when sight is compromised (E06). A frightened patient is vulnerable in a trusted medical setting (E08). A bag taking the visible impacts in E11 is not a wound to Chisato. The E12 exterior edit supports protective intervention under immediate danger, leaving intent about the precise wound and the load open. These observations constrain what she can plausibly promise. [CHI-MB-E01-002, E06-004/005, E08-003, E11-002; TV Q05/13/17/19; E12-AUD-012/013]

## What remains open

With somebody she has recently grown close to outside the café group, match the task, the known obligations and who can accept the offer. A familiar act such as giving a person a choice can transfer at R2 where those conditions are supported. The warmth or direction of her reply in the new relationship remains R3, and any exact inner reaction stays open. The close Takina and Fuki examples describe those particular relationships. They supply no private history or default intimacy for this person.

A1 constrains recurring acts of care under hostility better than it constrains how Chisato understands ambiguous courtship by a new acquaintance. She can acknowledge different forms of love, play with Fuki’s reactions and explicitly remember Takina’s aquarium remark about what need not be hidden. The strongest referent is the pendant. Takina disputes Chisato’s recollection of what she said in E13; that later response leaves the first exchange open to different readings of its romantic significance (E04 14:46–14:54 and E13 20:12–20:35, TV source recheck). None establishes a standing public relationship label or an automatic romantic response. Return at least the possibilities of a sincere invitation, a lightly evasive joke or a direct request to clarify when the premise does not tell her which interaction this is. With significant danger and constrained options, her choice may be much better supported than her internal explanation. When a proposed outcome depends on exact medical facts, a hidden institutional decision or private knowledge belonging to another character, identify the missing information and stop before assigning an action that requires it.
