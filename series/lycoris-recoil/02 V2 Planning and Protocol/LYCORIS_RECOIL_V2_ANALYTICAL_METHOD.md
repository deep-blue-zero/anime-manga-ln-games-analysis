# Lycoris Recoil V2 Analytical Method

## 1. Governing purpose

This is the canonical V2 deep-reading method for *Lycoris Recoil*.

It governs how source units are read, what evidence is preserved, how prospective interpretation is frozen and revised, how audiovisual evidence is escalated, how character and relationship behavior is extracted, and how later supplementary material is prevented from silently rewriting the anime-native model.

V2 is a materially restarted analytical generation. It inherits useful principles from the original Japanese-original audiovisual method, but it does not merely repeat that pass. The new method is designed for a richer corpus and a more demanding end state:

> **Interpret the work rigorously as narrative, audiovisual performance, ethical and institutional fiction, and ordinary-life character drama while simultaneously building auditable, state-sensitive character and relationship models that remain traceable to atomic source evidence.**

The method must support two different but connected questions:

1. **What is this source unit doing as a work?**
2. **What does this source unit establish about how these characters, relationships, institutions, and ethical positions behave under specific conditions?**

Neither question may consume the other.

---

# 2. Methodological inheritance and supersession

The prior document:

`Lycoris Recoil — Analytical Method and Episode Reading Protocol.md`

is preserved as historical methodological provenance.

Its strongest principles remain valid:

- Japanese-original evidence has priority for exact wording and register;
- written subtitles do not replace performed voice;
- screenshots do not replace motion;
- relationships must be read through speech, action, physical proximity, and routine;
- institutions must be treated as moral and epistemic actors rather than worldbuilding wallpaper;
- ordinary life is analytically meaningful;
- action choreography can reveal ethics;
- fact, inference, hypothesis, and speculation must remain distinct;
- later episodes may recontextualize earlier ones;
- *Lycoris Recoil* must not be reduced either to plot summary or solely to Chisato–Takina.

V2 adds controls that the original method did not formalize:

- one prospective truth horizon per sequential reading;
- frozen checkpoint predictions;
- explicit distinction between revelation and development;
- atomic microbehavior evidence extraction;
- conditional behavioral-policy modeling;
- relationship-conditioned character state;
- negative evidence and meaningful non-action;
- dedicated source-local versus integrated claim handling;
- source-authority classes;
- a base-rate firewall for derivative comedy;
- continuous-video escalation states;
- mandatory ledger/evidence routing before a source unit closes;
- a frozen anime-native character-model baseline before supplementary integration.

Where the two methods conflict, this V2 method governs.

During the prospective TV + shorts reread, the evidentiary order is:

Use the episode as recoverable through:

- original Japanese dialogue;

- dense timestamped clean frames;

- action continuity;
- facial and bodily acting;
- blocking;
- editing;
- framing;
- lighting and color;
- sound effects;
- music;
- silence;
- duration;
- targeted or full continuous-video escalation when required.

The episode itself remains authoritative over all derivatives.

### Tier B — Bundle metadata and technical provenance

Use for:

- timestamps;
- source identity;
- subtitle timing corrections;
- screenshot selection reasons;
- shot context;
- bundle QA;
- audio/subtitle linkage;
- extraction provenance.

Technical metadata helps locate evidence. It is not literary evidence by itself.

### Tier C — Earlier V2 source-unit readings and frozen ledgers

Earlier V2 findings form the current prospective model.

They may guide hypotheses for later episodes, but they are revisable only through explicit claim transitions.

### Tier D — V1 analytical corpus

V1 may be consulted for:

- claim inventory;
- comparison;
- revision auditing;
- locating previous interpretive questions.

During a prospective V2 source-unit read, V1 must **not** be allowed to supply facts or interpretations beyond the current V2 spoiler horizon.

If V1 knowledge is already mentally available, the method compensates by freezing the allowed evidentiary horizon in the artifact and treating later-series knowledge as inadmissible for prospective scoring.

### Tier E — Supplementary and outside material

Novels, *Recollect*, anthologies, manga adaptations, interviews, profiles, fandom interpretation, and later paratext are quarantined during the anime-native prospective phase.

They may not be used to explain, strengthen, or rescue an anime-native claim until the anime-native baseline is frozen.

# 4. Epistemic states

Every substantive claim should be recoverable as one of four ordinary epistemic states.

## `FACT`

Directly shown, spoken, narrated, or otherwise established by the admitted source evidence.

Examples:

- a character says a specific line;
- a character fires a weapon;
- a narrator explicitly states a thought;
- an institution issues an order.

A fact can still be limited by perspective. A character's confident statement is a fact **about what was said**, not automatically a fact about the world.

## `STRONG_INFERENCE`

Not explicitly stated, but strongly supported by converging evidence.

Typical support:

- repeated behavior;
- dialogue + performance + blocking;
- multiple independent scenes;
- an action that makes little sense under competing interpretations.

## `INTERPRETIVE_HYPOTHESIS`

A plausible thematic, psychological, relational, or institutional interpretation that remains meaningfully revisable.

## `SPECULATION`

A possibility with weak or incomplete source support.

Speculation is permitted when clearly marked. It must never be promoted merely because it is narratively attractive.

# 5. Prospective truth-horizon discipline

## 5.1 Governing rule

Each sequential anime reading has a frozen spoiler/evidence horizon.

For `E04`, the allowed sequential evidentiary horizon is:

> E01-E04 only.

For `SHORT03`, if the shorts are read after E13 as planned, the allowed baseline includes E01-E13 and only the shorts already read before SHORT03.

Later evidence may adjudicate an earlier claim, but it may not be smuggled backward into the original prospective reading.

## 5.2 Two separate states must remain recoverable

For important claims, preserve:

### Prospective state

What the evidence justified at the time.

### Mature state

What later evidence ultimately supports.

The claim-revision ledger connects them.

## 5.3 Revelation versus development

When later behavior differs from earlier behavior, do not automatically call it character growth.

Test at least four possibilities:

1. **development** — the character actually changed;
2. **relationship-state change** — the person behaves differently because the relationship permits different behavior;
3. **context/stakes change** — the underlying disposition stayed stable but the situation changed;
4. **revelation** — the capability or trait likely existed earlier but had not yet been observed.

This distinction is especially important for Takina.

# 6. Episode-bundle consumption protocol

## 6.1 Default bundle-first order

For each episode or short:

1. verify episode identity and bundle metadata;
2. inspect contact sheets for global visual progression;
3. inspect the dialogue index / corrected Japanese ASS;
4. establish dramatic movements and likely diagnostic scenes;
5. work sequentially through the episode evidence;
6. inspect the Japanese audio for performance evidence: use the lossless FLAC master for objective acoustic analysis and direct perceptual audition, when actually available, for load-bearing subjective performed-voice claims;
7. open full-resolution frames for visual/body/blocking claims;
8. inspect manifest/shot context where temporal placement matters;
9. escalate to continuous video only when the bundle cannot support a material claim;
10. record exact locators for load-bearing evidence.

Contact sheets are reconnaissance surfaces, not final fine-grained visual evidence.

The dialogue index is a routing surface, not a replacement for Japanese text or performed delivery.

## 6.2 Audio requirement

The preferred complete-audio analytical master is **lossless FLAC preserving the source sample rate/channel structure without normalization or speech enhancement**. MP3 remains a convenience/navigation derivative. Full-episode WAV should normally be generated only when a downstream tool requires PCM; bounded WAV clips may be derived from FLAC on demand.

Audio evidence must be classified before use:

1. `LINGUISTIC` — wording/register/source-encoded vocalization;
2. `ACOUSTIC_PERFORMANCE` — measurable pitch/range/intensity/timing/pause/activation/spectral or nonverbal-event evidence;
3. `PERCEPTUAL_PERFORMED_VOICE` — heard qualities such as deadpan, warmth, sarcasm, wryness, breathy intimacy, controlled irritation, or smiling-through-line effects.

Direct perceptual audition is mandatory **when a load-bearing claim depends on Layer 3**. If the execution surface does not provide trustworthy perceptual audition, the Layer-3 claim remains `OPEN / UNVERIFIED`; objective Layer-2 measurements may not be substituted as if they were subjective hearing.

Layer-2 acoustic analysis is appropriate when a claim materially depends on measurable:

- pitch contour/range;
- relative intensity;
- timing and pause structure;
- speech-rate change;
- onset/offset behavior;
- acoustic activation;
- encoded laughter/breath/sigh events;
- within-character deviation from an established baseline.

For Layer 2, prefer within-character and within-scene comparisons. Record score/background contamination or extraction-window sensitivity where material.

Do not infer performed affect solely from punctuation, subtitle wording, or acoustic metrics.

# 7. Continuous-video escalation

Use the corpus-wide `MANGA_ANIME_EPISODE_BUNDLE_SPECIFICATION.md`.

The allowed states are:

- `VIDEO_NOT_REQUIRED`
- `VIDEO_TARGETED_ESCALATION`
- `VIDEO_FULL_EPISODE_ESCALATION`

## 7.1 Escalate when the claim depends on

- gesture trajectory;
- bodily hesitation/acceleration;
- facial-expression transition;
- gaze movement over time;
- camera movement;
- exact edit rhythm;
- action geography;
- choreography;
- lip/gesture/speech synchronization;
- music-to-image synchronization;
- sound bridges or spatial sound;
- a visual event likely missed between extracted frames;
- a disputed load-bearing temporal claim.

## 7.2 Do not invent the path between frames

If frame A and frame B establish endpoints only, state the endpoints only.

Example:

Supported:

> Takina looks downward at A and toward Chisato at B.

Unsupported without continuous evidence:

> Takina slowly and hesitantly raises her gaze after a long internal struggle.

If the temporal path matters, escalate.

## 7.3 Escalation record

A deep reading should record:

```yaml
continuous_video_escalation:
  state: VIDEO_NOT_REQUIRED
  requests: []
```

or, where needed:

```yaml
continuous_video_escalation:
  state: VIDEO_TARGETED_ESCALATION
  requests:
    - interval: "00:12:41-00:14:08"
      reason: "Reaction timing and evolving facial/body performance are load-bearing."
      resolved: false
      supplied_object: null
```

An unresolved escalation constrains the claim. It does not block unrelated parts of the episode reading.

# 8. Sequential anime deep-reading passes

The V2 episode workflow uses **six integrated passes**.

These are analytical responsibilities, not six separate summaries.

---

## PASS 1 — Narrative and causal architecture

Establish what the episode does before deciding what it means.

Track:

- dramatic movements;
- location;
- participants;
- immediate goals;
- obstacles;
- causal links;
- revelations;
- reversals;
- scene transitions;
- emotional outcome;
- setup/payoff relations.

Ask:

> Why does this scene follow the previous one?

rather than:

> What happened next?

### Information distribution

Track separately:

- audience knowledge;
- Chisato knowledge;
- Takina knowledge;
- DA/Lycoris knowledge;
- antagonist knowledge;
- civilian/public knowledge;
- false beliefs;
- deliberate deception;
- institutional narrative management.

### Provisional episode thesis

End the pass with:

> **At this stage, the episode appears principally to be about ________.**

This is explicitly provisional.

---

## PASS 2 — Character state and microbehavior

This pass is central to the V2 restart.

For every materially diagnostic interaction, ask:

1. What stimulus or condition does the character perceive?
2. What appears to be their appraisal?
3. What do they want immediately?
4. What tactic do they choose?
5. What speech register do they choose?
6. What body behavior accompanies it?
7. How does the other person respond?
8. Does the character persist, escalate, soften, reframe, repair, or withdraw?
9. What outcome follows?
10. Is the behavior new, repeated, modified, or contradictory?

### Atomic observation format

Where analytically useful, route an observation through fields such as:

- evidence ID;
- source/scope;
- timestamp;
- scene/context;
- interlocutor;
- relationship state;
- stakes;
- character state;
- initiator;
- trigger;
- appraisal;
- immediate goal;
- tactic/behavior;
- speech act;
- register;
- humor mechanism;
- body behavior;
- partner response;
- recalibration;
- outcome;
- candidate policy;
- evidence stream;
- confidence.

Not every ordinary action needs an entry. Extract behavior when it helps explain a reusable conditional pattern, state transition, relationship rule, preference, boundary, or uncertainty.

### Meaningful non-action

Record absences when the episode presents a real opportunity for a response and the character notably does **not**:

- correct;
- retaliate;
- joke;
- disclose;
- pursue;
- interrupt;
- touch;
- obey;
- challenge;
- console;
- escalate.

Negative evidence must be opportunity-sensitive. The mere absence of an action is not evidence if there was no plausible occasion for it.

---

## PASS 3 — Japanese language, voice, and performed characterization

Treat voice evidence as **three separately typed layers**. Do not silently promote a lower layer into a higher one.

### Layer A — Linguistic evidence

Track where diagnostic:

- first-person forms;
- names/honorifics;
- polite/plain switching;
- sentence endings;
- ellipsis;
- contractions;
- slang;
- imperatives;
- requests;
- apologies;
- hedges;
- interjections;
- rhetorical questions;
- repetition;
- institutional vocabulary;
- semantic reframing;
- verbal teasing;
- deliberately exaggerated role language;
- source-encoded vocalizations.

The key question is:

> Why does this character speak this way to this person in this state?

### Layer B — Acoustic-performance evidence

Track where measurable and diagnostically useful:

- pitch contour and range;
- estimated F0 distributions;
- relative intensity/RMS;
- speech rate;
- pause structure;
- onset/offset behavior;
- overlap timing;
- acoustic activation;
- laughter/breath/sigh events when recoverable from the signal/source encoding;
- within-character deviations from the episode/relationship/state baseline.

Layer B can be produced computationally from a lossless FLAC/WAV without human-like perceptual audition. It is strongest when comparative: same character across states, same scene across interlocutors, or repeated policy-relevant contexts.

### Layer C — Perceptual performed-voice evidence

Track heard qualities only when trustworthy perceptual audition is actually available, including:

- deadpan quality;
- warmth;
- sarcasm/wryness;
- smiling-through-line effects;
- breathy intimacy;
- controlled irritation;
- panic/fear as perceived affect;
- false brightness;
- deliberately theatrical delivery;
- sudden simplicity/sincerity.

If direct perceptual audition is unavailable, record Layer C as `UNVERIFIED` rather than inferring it from Layer B. A narrow F0 range is not, by itself, “deadpan”; a broad range is not, by itself, “warm” or “excited.”

Do not reduce Chisato to “high-energy voice” or Takina to “flat voice.” The analytic object is **state-conditioned elasticity across linguistic, acoustic, and perceptual layers**.

---

## PASS 4 — Visual direction, embodiment, action, and sound

### Visual grammar

Use frames and continuity to track where significant:

- shot distance;
- eyeline;
- foreground/background hierarchy;
- centered/marginal placement;
- negative space;
- surveillance perspectives;
- doors/windows/screens;
- symmetry;
- repeated compositions;
- lighting/color shifts;
- visual isolation or incorporation.

### Blocking and bodies in space

Track:

- approach/retreat;
- distance;
- who leads movement;
- who follows;
- who protects whom;
- touch;
- tolerated proximity;
- gaze aversion;
- sitting/standing relationships;
- bodily orientation;
- who occupies or controls space.

### Ordinary embodiment

Give serious attention to:

- eating;
- dressing/changing;
- shopping;
- serving;
- housework;
- games;
- travel;
- resting;
- grooming;
- boredom;
- small competitions;
- embarrassment;
- trivial mistakes.

These are high-value reconstruction conditions, not filler by default.

### Action grammar

Track:

- line of sight;
- cover;
- range;
- movement;
- targeting;
- weapon handling;
- tactical efficiency;
- aggression;
- restraint;
- improvisation;
- teamwork;
- willingness to be hurt;
- reactions to injury;
- use of nonlethal means;
- willingness to inflict pain despite nonlethal commitments.

Ask:

> What does this person's way of fighting reveal about what violence is for?

### Sound/music

Record only when analytically meaningful:

- cue onset/withdrawal;
- silence;
- sound bridges;
- effects that alter moral tone;
- music supporting or counterpointing action;
- recurring motifs;
- synchronization with gesture/editing.

Escalate to continuous video when timing is load-bearing.

## PASS 5 — Relationships, institutions, ethics, and ordinary life

### Relationship state

For major relationships track:

- current baseline distance;
- initiative balance;
- permitted teasing;
- terms of address;
- physical proximity;
- care style;
- vulnerability permissions;
- information sharing;
- conflict;
- repair;
- trust;
- misreading;
- yielding;
- future orientation;
- tactical cooperation.

Do not equate increased conflict with relational regression or increased warmth with intimacy automatically.

### Chisato–Takina

Their dyad receives continuous tracking, but it must not swallow the rest of the work.

Distinguish:

- friendship evidence;
- partnership;
- domestic familiarity;
- dependency/interdependence;
- romantic coding;
- yuri coding;
- explicit canonical declaration.

Do not promote coding into declaration.

### Institutions

Treat DA, Lycoris, LilyBell, Alan, police/public-order systems, and Café LycoReco as institutions or counter-institutions when evidence warrants.

Track:

- stated purpose;
- actual practice;
- hierarchy;
- recruitment;
- information control;
- violence;
- discipline;
- legality;
- paternalism;
- concept of civilian protection;
- concept of subordinate autonomy;
- legitimacy claims.

### Ethics

Track behavior rather than attaching one-word moral labels.

Recurring questions include:

- killing;
- rescue;
- proportionality;
- obedience;
- conscience;
- secrecy;
- loyalty;
- punishment;
- sacrifice;
- autonomy;
- paternalism;
- enemy responsibility;
- self-sacrifice;
- gratitude;
- assigned purpose;
- self-authorship.

### Ordinary life

Ask not merely whether ordinary life exists, but:

> What concrete practices make it possible?

Track labor, money, food, transport, schedules, customers, maintenance, neighborhood trust, leisure, chosen work, and social routine.

## PASS 6 — Cumulative synthesis, policy candidates, and prospective freeze

Only after the prior passes:

### A. Revise the episode thesis

State what the episode now appears to be doing.

### B. Identify new or strengthened cumulative claims

For each:

- formulation;
- evidence state;
- source locator;
- whether it changes a prior V2 claim;
- whether it pressures a V1 claim.

### C. Generate candidate behavioral policies

A policy is a conditional rule inferred from repeated or highly diagnostic observations.

Example form:

```text
Policy ID: CHI-SOC-INIT-01
Condition:
Baseline response:
Common tactics:
Modifiers:
Failure/repair behavior:
Negative constraints:
Evidence:
Confidence:
```

Do not infer a stable policy from a single gag unless the observation is unusually diagnostic and clearly labeled provisional.

### D. Record unknowns

Unknown is a valid analytical result.

Example:

> Chisato's baseline interest in idol culture remains unestablished.

### E. Adjudicate prior predictions

Use:

- SUPPORTED
- PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED
- NOT_TESTED
- DISCONFIRMED
- DEVELOPMENTAL_INVALIDATION
- NEW_MODIFIER_REQUIRED

### F. Freeze new prospective predictions

Predictions should concern behavior or interpretation, not merely plot guesses.

# 9. Character reconstruction policy

The detailed reconstruction architecture is governed by the synthesis architecture and will receive its own companion method artifact. Until that file exists, this section is the minimum operational contract.

## 9.1 Stable traits versus strategies

Separate:

- temperament;
- values;
- behavioral strategies;
- defensive strategies;
- competence effects;
- relationship modifiers;
- state transitions.

Example principle:

> “Chisato is playful” is weaker than identifying when she uses play, what purpose it serves, and when she stops using it.

## 9.2 State + stimulus + relationship

A character response should be modeled as:

> **current state + stimulus + relationship state + stakes + constraints → probable action/register/recalibration**

not:

> trait label → generic response.

## 9.3 Relationship-conditioned models

Maintain distinct evidence for behavior toward:

- strangers;
- customers;
- coworkers;
- friends;
- intimates;
- authority;
- rivals;
- enemies;
- vulnerable people.

Major named relationships receive their own modifiers.

## 9.4 Reconstruction classes

Use:

- `R1_DIRECT_RECONSTRUCTION`
- `R2_CONSTRAINED_INTERPOLATION`
- `R3_WEAK_EXTRAPOLATION`
- `R4_OPEN_SIMULATION`

A generated crossover or hypothetical never becomes source evidence.

## 9.5 Negative constraints

Every mature model should eventually state what common simplifications must **not** be used.

Examples likely to require testing include:

### Chisato
Do not automatically model her as:
- generic pacifist preacher;
- generic anti-state dissident;
- endlessly manic;
- perfectly boundary-respecting;
- incapable of deception;
- celebrity-worshipping;
- consciously therapeutic in every joke.

### Takina
Do not automatically model her as:
- robotic;
- unintelligent because of literalism;
- emotionally flat;
- transformed into Chisato by character growth;
- stripped of lethal willingness.

These are provisional methodological safeguards until V2 evidence independently rebuilds the policies.

- `PRESERVE`
- `STRENGTHEN`
- `REVISE`
- `DOWNGRADE`
- `REJECT`

When an old formulation changes, preserve:

- old wording;
- old source;
- reason for transition;
- current wording;

# 11. Supplementary-source reading method

Supplementary sources begin only after the anime-native baseline is frozen unless the architecture explicitly changes the gate.

## 11.1 Source-native first pass

Do **not** open a novel or comic primarily to mine character facts.

First determine what the work itself is doing.

### Prose

Track:

- narrator;
- focalization;
- direct thought;
- free indirect/focalized narration;
- narrator assertion;
- dialogue;
- prose rhythm;
- story architecture;
- temporal placement;
- thematic purpose;
- ordinary-life construction;
- medium-specific characterization.

Distinguish:

- direct cognition;
- narrated interpretation;
- external action;
- analyst inference.

### Recollect

Track:

- comedy structure;
- recurring setup/payoff mechanisms;
- everyday-life focus;
- Abe Kanari's source-local characterization;
- physical/reaction comedy;
- genre amplification;
- recurrent relationship patterns;
- divergence from anime-native policies.

### Anthologies

Treat **creator + story** as the basic unit.

Track:

- contributor;
- story title;
- genre/tone;
- temporal placement if inferable;
- character realization;
- anime compatibility;
- caricature;
- independent convergence;
- source-specific interpretation.

Do not say “the anthology proves X” when only one contributor does X.

# 12. Multi-source integration firewall

The detailed authority integration procedure will receive its own companion method artifact. Until then, use the following minimum rules.

After source-native reading, compare observations to the frozen anime-native model using:

- `CONFIRM`
- `EXTEND`
- `SPECIFY`
- `PRESSURE`
- `CONTRADICT`
- `CARICATURE`
- `SOURCE_SPECIFIC`
- `OPEN`

## Base-rate firewall

A source may show that behavior X is **possible** without establishing that behavior X is **typical**.

Comedy and anthology evidence should expand the plausible envelope much more readily than it changes behavioral base rates.

## Asymmetric revision rule

No B1/B2/C1 derivative source may, by itself, `REVISE`, `DOWNGRADE`, or `REJECT` a high-confidence A1 anime-native behavioral rule.

It may pressure it, contradict it locally, or contribute to later revision if stronger evidence converges.

## Do not silently harmonize

If two sources disagree, preserve the disagreement.

An invented conditional explanation is not evidence.

This prevents isolated essays from accumulating without longitudinal integration.

# 17. Quality controls and failure modes

## Do not

- summarize instead of analyzing;
- infer delivery from subtitles without audio;
- infer motion from still endpoints;
- call every ordinary scene filler;
- call every behavioral difference development;
- convert a character belief into objective truth;
- convert romantic coding into declaration;
- call Chisato simply “pacifist” without testing the actual structure of her violence;
- call Takina simply “serious” or “robotic”;
- make DA a monolith without evidence;
- allow V1 to pre-answer the prospective reread;
- let later novels leak into the anime-native baseline;
- let a gag anthology alter behavior frequency by repetition alone;
- harmonize cross-source contradiction without evidence;
- make a source-unit essay “complete” before required ledger routing.

## Prefer

- conditional formulations;
- source locators;
- explicit unknowns;
- multiple converging evidence streams;
- preserved contradictions;
- prospectively frozen predictions;
- revision rather than defensive consistency;
- character models that become less confident as extrapolation distance grows.

# 18. Governing analytical questions

The project should remain open to source-discovered themes, but the following questions are structurally important:

- What gives a person authority over another person's finite life?
- What does Chisato's nonlethality actually mean in practice?
- What does Takina's development change, and what remains stable?
- What forms of ordinary life are treated as morally substantive?
- How do institutions manufacture legitimacy through secrecy and delegated violence?
- When does care become control?
- How does assigned purpose interact with gratitude and self-authorship?
- How do competence and moral authority diverge?
- What does the series imagine friendship/intimacy can change about ethical action?
- How do voice, blocking, humor, and bodily routine establish character beyond plot dialogue?
- What aspects of Chisato, Takina, Fuki, Kurumi, Mika, Mizuki, and others remain invariant across medium and creator?
- Where do later licensed interpretations reveal stable character affordances, and where do they deform them?

These are questions, not predetermined conclusions.
