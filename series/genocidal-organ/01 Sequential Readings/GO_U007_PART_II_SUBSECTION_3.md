## What the mother’s reassurance does

The opening presents burned bodies through culinary comparison. The narrator watches with curiosity and amusement; when his mother speaks, their exchange is intimate and playful rather than a change into panic. This makes the grotesque material available for an ordinary family conversation. The voice answers his corpse-as-thing exclamation by extending material existence to living people: the mother introduces the conditional claim that if a corpse is merely matter, living people are also merely things. His counterexample is practical: he cannot readily treat Williams as a mug. The exchange preserves a difference between recognizing material composition and knowing how to relate to a person (E001–02).

The mother next corrects his abstraction from matter to flesh. When he formulates being governed by flesh’s mechanisms, she answers that being flesh means no prison. The passage stages that disagreement rather than demonstrating a complete solution to determinism. Most clearly demonstrated is how he receives the answer: he assents because his mother was always right, and her assurance removes his worry. A familiar form of address immediately evokes her childhood corrections. Affection gives the philosophical proposition its practical force (E003; GO-CL003v3/028).

She then points out his arriving transport. The mother who reassures him also lets him depart; they wave goodbye. Williams beckons from the dream aircraft, which is distinct from the real friend already waiting in Prague. The narration makes the transition through paired sleep and waking in planes of the dead and living. The dead country recedes before the ordinary landing approaches (E004,E008). This strengthens the already admitted sleep/waking frame, while the narrator’s later language of his mother’s soul remains his way of describing the nightly address. Dream speech is not independent proof of the living mother’s philosophical doctrine.

The narrator reports more frequent visits since Alex’s death and says he has considered consulting a military counselor. He also says they have not impaired the mission so far, and therefore allows them to recur. Frequency, functional self-assessment and an actual consultation are different matters: the last has not occurred in this account. Increased recurrence qualifies the earlier picture of peaceful returns without establishing a diagnosis or the cause of any treatment’s success or failure (E005; GO-CL009v5).

## Attention learned at home, and a departure allowed

The waking explanation calls the mother-advises-son structure a copy of domestic life, apart from the strange scenery. The narrator attributes his early film and literary interests to her, and describes her raising him without remarriage after his father’s death. This gives the dream a remembered social form rather than treating its statements as disembodied doctrine (E006–07).

His account also gives his attentiveness a family history. He interprets her vigilance as fear that he might disappear as his father did. To spare her that fear, he says, he became careful about other people’s words and gestures, avoided trouble and concealed trouble when it happened. Care includes information management: he tries to maintain her security partly by keeping her from knowing. Her feared motive is his retrospective explanation, signaled as inference before he restates it more firmly; it is not independently accessed maternal thought.

The enlistment story complicates a simple account of maternal control. He says he volunteered for the new force because he tired of that careful self. His mother did not object and told him to do what he wanted. That reported permission is a material counterweight to the force of her authority in the dream. A child’s priority of reassuring a parent, a later decision to depart, and the parent’s assent coexist. No threat or explicit prohibition has been disclosed. The bitter reversal is that the mother, not the son on dangerous missions, disappears first. The analogy to his father concerns abrupt loss; it does not merge the already distinct accounts of their deaths (E006–07).

## Flesh that carries and accommodates him

The waking aircraft makes flesh a functional material. Its moving wing recalls the intrusion pod, and the narrator imagines stripping its surface and seeing its blood. This is an expressed desire, not an action he performs. At the same time, the system works for him: wing adjustment and changing seat material make the landing comfortable, while he resets his own sleep rhythm with a drug. The contraceptive comparison is the narrator’s analogy, not a medical instruction (E008; GO-CL028).

The commercial journey ends in his enjoyment of ordinary tourist travel. Flesh is therefore present as both a disturbing object of fascination and effective support. The dream’s claim that flesh need not be a prison is followed by embodied accommodation and self-regulation. The juxtaposition supports an inquiry into material agency without turning the mother’s reassurance into a proved metaphysical theorem or the technology into a uniformly hostile cage.
