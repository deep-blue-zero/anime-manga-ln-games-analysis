## Love supplies the boundary of the protected world

The opening is an argument about the ordinary moral intelligibility of violence, not an irrationality diagnosis of John. Clavis moves from kamikaze pilots, resistance and U-boats to a compressed history in which mass civic warfare and responsibility for a chosen war make patriotism a motive. The genealogy makes several sweeping historical claims, including about standing armies; these are the narrator's explanatory account, not independently verified military history. The ruby that pairs private military contractors with `ＰＭＦ` connects the supposedly modern contractor to that narrated older economy of hired violence. It does not establish every contractor's allegiance or identify the earlier attackers' ultimate employer. At N11 the account is explicitly attached to what John has said. The isolated first-person `わたし` in N12 continues reported John, not a sudden statement of Clavis's own new killing motive. (E001.)

The crucial proposition is precise: war **can** be fought through love. N10 calls this altruism with a restricted range; love and killing cease to be mutually exclusive adaptive dispositions once the loved recipients are bounded. That explains how they can cooperate without showing that their cooperation is right. The source has therefore answered U026's unnamed-beneficiary problem more specifically than a generic revelation that John “really cares.” He explicitly describes attachment to the consumer civilization in which he grew up, even while calling it decadent and ethically incomplete. The narrator's G9 formulation and his repeated `ぼくら` locate the protected world as the affluent in-group, not simply John's surviving relatives and not a newly demonstrated homogeneous national population. John's explicit N13 testimony now includes loss of both wife **and child** to a nuclear attack. It is stronger family testimony than U026's phrase about betrayal; it neither rewrites that earlier phrase nor turns the nuclear event into something Clavis personally witnessed. (E002–003.)

Lucia immediately objects that the man who says he will prevent grief has spread grief everywhere. His reply distinguishes suffering from suffering that protected people see. Clavis's clipped consumer catalogue—CNN clips, pizza, fifteen-minute previews and only partly followed product histories—participates in the critique rather than placing him outside it. This repeats the recent material supply-chain problem in a new register: partial access and stopping one's attention can coexist. ## A partition claimed as prevention, not justice

John now supplies the missing protective strategy. Hatred is to be directed inward before it reaches the protected world. Clavis summarizes the targets as countries close to recognizing a relation between their misery and “our freedom”; that is his rendering of John's plan, not an independent causal investigation of every country's poverty. The resulting separation is deliberately asymmetrical: one world kills itself while another remains peaceful. This is the explicit current reason offered for the journeys. No future theme or presumed motive is needed to infer the exclusion: the opposed `彼ら` and `われわれ` do the work in the admitted Japanese. (E003.)

The formal specificity matters. `翻訳` carries the ruby `コンパイル`: the deep grammatical structure must be adapted to local languages, and John claims that this confines effects to a language area and its surroundings. He explicitly connects avoiding English transmission to manageable scale. These are substantial new claims about linguistic deployment, not a displayed grammar, decoded instructions, measured boundary or independent demonstration of safe containment. They sharpen the fictional account while preserving the distinction between an asserted mechanism and a controlled causal test. His claimed official State Department statistics—terror increasing under biometrics, then reaching zero after his seeding while massacres increase—give his argument a public-data idiom. The actual tables, definitions, time window and comparison controls are not supplied. The account is coherent within the current scene, and Clavis later agrees that the advertised security cause is unrelated; that does not independently eliminate every causal confound. (E003/006.)

John's denial that he is claiming justice at N31 prevents a comforting misdescription of him as simply confusing explanation with justification. He says he protects what he wants to protect. Nor does she promise to punish or forgive his maternal decision. His thought that she is the only person who can punish him remains his dependence on her judgment. (E004.)

The proposed trial does not rest on an implausibly easy publicity solution. Clavis stresses secrecy and the apparent absurdity of a lone man behind worldwide killing. Clavis's short corpse-foundation sequence at N44–46 takes up that charge, and he actually agrees to bring John back. ## A named corpse resists the economy of things

Escape succeeds locally, without becoming a magical demonstration of camouflage or a clean rescue. Clavis attributes their avoiding the guards' inaccurate distant fire partly, and with a qualification, to adaptive camouflage; he shields the exposed John with his own covered body. Lucia's body cannot be brought from the confused firefight. He can articulate that reason while being unable to master grief, and realizes he is crying. No new physiological diagnosis or certainty about a suppression treatment's present state is required to read the conflict. (E007.)

The compact catalogue of dead girl, boy and burned women and children recalls the earlier body's-eye vocabulary of war. Those figures are remembered deaths, not newly present people in the jungle or a newly authenticated roster of particular victims. The contrast then becomes extraordinarily exact. Lucia's damaged brain displays the materiality through which Clavis has usually classified the dead as things; nevertheless this remains *Lucia Schkroup's corpse*. The proper name does not deny biological death or assert surviving consciousness. It makes bodily matter inseparable, for him, from a particular relation. (E007.)

Clavis's reply at N87 calls his own selective perception selfish. John's preceding question about the many bodies Clavis has seen is answered through Clavis's internal reflection and then his self-critical utterance. The text gives that objection force: grieving one's loved corpse does not automatically recognize all the strangers whose bodies one has reduced to things. Conversely, the presence of partiality does not make Clavis's grief unreal. The current insight is a rupture in his old habit, with a limit he and the scene acknowledge. Reading it as complete universal ethical conversion would exceed the scene, especially given the closing loss of felt reality. (E007/012.)

## Atonement without repentance, insight without a promised new policy

The jungle materializes John's dependence: civilian movement skills, an apparently sprained ankle and Clavis's pace constrain escape. He now says he will not return to seed the language again. He intends to explain the world publicly as Lucia asked, whether the outcome is execution or ridicule. He attributes involving her to a Prague visit for false ID and wanting to see her; that is his account of her involvement, not complete independent proof of everything she knew or did. His declaration that he has “killed” the woman he once loved is moral responsibility language here, not a retraction of Williams as the actual shooter. (E008.)

Clavis challenges an atonement focused only on betrayed wife and child and the beloved woman: what of the massacred dead? John says he has borne those dead since the first seeding. Yet when asked about regret for creating the killing conditions, he explicitly says no. Publicly accepting judgment, personal guilt over Lucia and continued endorsement of the original life-weighing choice coexist. There is no grammatical reason to smooth that conflict into total repentance. Nor does refusing regret establish that he feels no guilt or never intends disclosure: both contrary elements have just been spoken. His projected public choice remains a character's conditional account of what others would face, not an analyst forecast. (E008–009.)

Clavis recognizes his own convenient position while reprimanding John. He has wanted an answer for killing without choosing, an exemption that he calls responsibility evasion, and wanted punishment or forgiveness from Lucia. Her death does not deliver either. Alex's remembered inner-hell phrase now names being imprisoned in himself; it is remembered speech, not Alex's posthumous testimony. The question whether lifelong guilt itself is punishment remains a question. The thought that nobody can now judge or forgive him is the focal narrator's absolute experience of loss, not an objective metaphysical monopoly held by Lucia. (E009.)

Clavis's civilizational paragraph has an uneven but legible modality. `たぶん` and the repeated `だろう` qualify the claim of ethical progress; the rejection of a merely incommensurable sequence of value systems is emphatic. He presents conscience as struggling toward more other-directed conduct while insisting that “we” remain able to shut our eyes. It is substantial narratorial normative argument, not a neutral evolutionary mechanism or a real-world proof of moral progress. When John asks whether Clavis will again assassinate to protect the world, Clavis disowns that as his old motive: he says he had followed orders. About what comes next he honestly answers that he does not know, while hoping to see more clearly. Neither the spoken uncertainty nor that hope gives us a settled future decision policy. (E010.)
