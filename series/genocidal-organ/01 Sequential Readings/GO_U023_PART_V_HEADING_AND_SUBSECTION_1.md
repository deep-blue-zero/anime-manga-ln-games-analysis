# GO-U023 — counting bodies, inventing lives

The new Part begins with counting, then asks what a count or assembled record can recover of a person. Coffins receive logistical metadata; technicians put bodies together; consumers inspect the histories of goods; software composes lives. These arrangements can identify, transport and narrate. They cannot restore the dead person's experience or tell the survivor what his mother would have wanted. Alongside this problem, the narrator discovers that grief has not produced the anger he believes a soldier should feel. His imitation of Williams makes feeling another uncertain act of assembly.

## A birth that produces the dead

The separate **第五部** heading makes an actual formal division (E001). Its link attributes are preserved as markup, without navigating the destination. The first subsection then places **ひとつ。／ふたつ。** on separate lines, reveals **ぼくは棺を数える。**, and resumes the count amid conspicuous blank paragraphs (E002). The reader encounters units before learning what is being enumerated. Space and interruption prevent the count from becoming a smooth summary of casualties.

The arriving aircraft becomes a maternal sea creature whose opened womb releases eggs (E003). **死者の卵** and **死者が生まれてくる** deliberately join birth and death. This is the narrator's perceptual metaphor, not a new biological description of the transport. Its force comes from the literal contents that follow: collected and connected remains, **でっちあげられた骸**. The startling verb makes the apparently whole body a construction rather than an intact recovered person.

The military counts too. Coffin metadata announces arrival as a parcel network would; the FEDEX comparison resumes a vocabulary of cargo already applied to captives and supplies. Yet the text also gives the reason for assembling the dead: families must be able to see a body. The technicians' genetic markers and equipment tags perform real identification work. One need not reduce this care to fraudulent concealment to hear the violence in “making up” a corpse. Practical recognition and irrecoverable fragmentation coexist.

Williams is unequivocally alive: he and the narrator carry coffins. This new evidence closes the earlier frontier's uncertainty about his fate without changing U022's frozen ignorance. The opening count is not a reliable numerical casualty total. It names successive coffins in a staged scene and does not say that the sixth is the last.

## An anger assembled from another face

The narrator searches for the anger and hatred that he thinks should follow his comrades' deaths (E004). The repeated **はずだ** marks a felt obligation, not an emotion already present. He does feel grief: **悲しみはあったが、それがどうしても怒りに繫がってはくれなかった**. Calling him simply without feeling would erase the sentence that distinguishes his grief from the missing response.

Williams supplies an apparent model. The narrator reads anger, hatred, sorrow and murderous intent from his face, then imitates lips and eyes. After roughly three minutes, anger seems to appear: **あるような気がしてきた**. The phrase matters. It supports a change in felt possibility through bodily imitation, without certifying that an authentic stable hatred has been manufactured. Nor does it make Williams's interior directly available; the face is observed and interpreted by the narrator.

He wonders whether anger on another's behalf is a form of conscience. This is more troubling than a generic contrast between empathy and numbness, because the proposed care takes the form of wanting to kill an enemy. His inability to decide whom to hate—attackers, organizer or John—also has an epistemic basis. The absence cannot be assigned wholly to adjustment as though the subsection had isolated one cause. Grief, uncertain agency, unfamiliarity with this kind of defeat and his own sense of hollowness remain materially present alternatives.

The hollowness must be hidden from comrades, Rockwell and the counselor. The institution orders counseling to prevent PTSD and threatens court-martial for refusal (E005). It calls the soldiers expensive human resources requiring maintenance. This makes therapeutic care and coercion explicit institutional partners in this episode; it does not establish that the order has already produced another treatment session or that the narrator has a diagnosed disorder.

Williams's anger at the order is reported in speech. The narrator performs a comparable resistance to display morale, while privately demanding something different: **ぼくが必要としているのは罰だ**. The repetition of needing a punisher and wanting punishment for all his crimes strongly continues his earlier wish for owned, answerable guilt. It is a wish for a judging other, not an actual sentence, confession to these listeners or proof that every possible accusation would be correct.

## The comedy that has become a battle memory

Williams comes over with the familiar pizza, beer and film routine, calling it a substitute for counseling (E006). The narrator remembers the same arrangement after Alex and acknowledges its capacity to wrap a cold heavy feeling in vague idle time until it is forgotten. The habitual companionship is real even though his willingness is muted and he withholds his own condition.

The American beer does not taste like the Czech one. This short distinction returns Prague through consumption without restoring the earlier encounter. Williams's apparently ordinary laughter is accompanied by repeated glances that seem to ask whether the narrator finds the jokes funny too. The **まるで…かのように** preserves this as an interpretation of the glances, rather than an audible request or transparent diagnosis.

The narrator now states that Williams was unconscious at the rear throughout the fight, unshot and unable to witness the mutual mutilation (E006). His explanation that Williams is tormented by being absent carries **たぶん**. Survival and lack of direct experience are narrated facts here; shame as the exact inner cause is a strong, qualified reading of his friend.

The film's Black Knight loses limbs, denies pain and continues fighting (E007). The narrator explicitly connects the scene to both sides on the train. The joke therefore repeats an actual experienced structure without becoming evidence that the film explains the masking technology. A comedy familiar enough for banter has become a difficult shared viewing: one viewer saw the real analogue and the other did not. Williams's remarks about Gilliam's convincing servant face momentarily sustain the old conversational rhythm, while the narrator's attention moves toward dead comrades. Their differing histories of this battle interrupt the apparent sameness of the ritual.

## Histories for things, fictions for people

Remembering the unassembled remains leads the narrator back to engineered animal tissue (E008). Human flesh and the flesh inside aircraft, pods and cargo machinery share muscle, blood and pulses in his comparison. This is a specific material continuity, not proof that every human and machine capacity is identical or that the body fragments are literally interchangeable spare parts.

He wishes each cell possessed **物歴〔メタヒストリー〕** so that identification could be easier. Equipment tags and genetic markers already help, but the wish concerns a more exhaustive history of every fragment. The next object is pizza. The consumer's detailed ingredient histories, supply chains and ethical forums give the same records an ordinary domestic use. His ironic account of “smart” consumers renaming themselves “alpha” contains an attribution of embarrassment and sensitivity; it does not independently survey their motives. Their forums can influence products, yet the paragraph does not demonstrate that all recommended ethical improvements occur.

The analogy shifts again from commodities to lives (E009). Records of buying, moving and communicating, together with private albums and diaries, could supply software with material for a biography. **フリーハンドの権限さえあれば** conditions the operation on access. The narrator calls its editorial power almost violent: it can generate a story from an apparently ordinary life. Detail and force of presentation need not equal transparent truth about its subject.

The mother is the decisive limit. During the hospital summer he searched shared family areas and documents accessible through a guest account in her space and found no lifegraph there. The bounded search is newly disclosed; it is not proof that no lifegraph existed anywhere. Her participation in the reported seventy-percent practice remains a question. Earlier knowledge freezes about her wishes are preserved, with this added information dated here.

Could a lifegraph have changed his decision? The conditional imagines another profession and continuing hospital visits, not an alternative life that actually happened. More importantly, he himself calls the assembled metadata a **フィクション**. **生き延びたものは常に、想像することしか許されていない** culminates in the dead's rule through **経験不可能性**. The dead cannot be experienced from within or made to answer a survivor's question. This supports a substantial limit on substituting a constructed story for truth; it need not invalidate every factual inference or identification earlier in the subsection. His counterfactual still has emotional force precisely because the record might have affected him without certifying her desire.

## A culprit named by an apparatus

The later account identifies only five surviving team members: the narrator, Williams, Sean, Bob and Daniel (E010). The last three receive names and survival status, not invented personalities. It also reports the painstaking identification of the dead attackers as contractor soldiers previously missing, reported killed or allegedly executed. “Ghosts” now joins administrative death to actual bodies. This is retrospective information, not a hidden identity the reader possessed while U022's men were firing.

False IDs are inferred; the Prague network is only a possible source. Radioactive dust supports an inferred route through Pakistan, while the narrator says their precise origin and trail cannot be recovered. These limits survive the subsequent confidence about the organizer.

He states that E&K and its Senate-leader executive became nearly certain suspects because SNDGA supplied the answer (E011). **魔法**, **ご託宣**, Deep Thought and the Kevin Bacon game give the claim a sardonic voice. It would be too weak to describe the whole account merely as an unsupported possibility: the narrator reports a government investigation, a narrowed disclosure circle, a settlement and political retirement. It would be too strong to make the graph a displayed, independently reproducible proof. Its links, data and error rates are not shown.

The changing disclosure lists retrospectively give substance to John's earlier conditional argument about locating a leak. The narrator now interprets John as having anticipated his supporter's reckless rescue attempt. The earlier **仮に** remains a conditional in its original scene; later uptake is a new attributed finding rather than retroactive proof that the earlier words were a confession.

The government avoids a public hearing that would expose the DARPA relationship and NSA system. A private settlement lets the executive retire citing illness and concentrate on the war business. These are strong narrated institutional findings. They do not establish successful containment of the consequences: the narrator expressly says the eventual disruption exceeded Watergate and Iran-gate, despite the initial wish for secrecy. Its full course is not supplied here. The assertion that nobody in government wanted a scandal is broader than any individual access demonstrated here. The passage also distinguishes feared testimony from testimony actually given: no such public hearing is narrated.

The last accounting returns to the people the central pursuit displaced (E012). The locomotive's destruction and the curved track explain the unequal force on the cars in a retrospective reconstruction. The boy's reported five-hundred-foot trajectory has its own **だったそうだ**. The Hindu-India leaders never reach court or private prison: their bodies have been shot, each through the brow. This supports the narrator's inference that the rescuers prioritized John and disposed of the other captives, without supplying an individual shooter or an observed execution scene.

**残されているのはただひとつ** narrows the narrative toward John. It is a rhetorical concentration after many deaths, not proof that all other consequences are resolved. His current location and condition remain undisclosed. The next subsection is still outside the admitted boundary.
