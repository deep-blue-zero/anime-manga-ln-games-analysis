## Deep reading

### The question survives the person who could answer

The temporal jump returns a precise phrase before disclosing a death. Alex’s internal hell is remembered; his coffin is then seen at a Catholic funeral. The narrator explicitly reports self-killing. He did not learn what Alex’s hell contained, and the search finds no note. The rite supplies communal words around death, while the dead man’s private explanation remains unavailable. The narrator’s history/doctrine of funeral inclusion belongs to his account; its literary function is to distinguish acknowledged religious belonging from a recovered reason for dying (E001–02).

The father sequence initially offers a familiar hierarchy of loss: a father’s suicide should exceed a colleague’s. The narrator immediately calls that explanation **大嘘** (analyst’s gloss: “a complete lie”), because he had been too young to understand death when his father vanished. This self-correction withdraws the proposed comparison. It does not provide a reliable measure showing that Alex’s death leaves him untouched. His subsequent rejection of the magically perceptive child likewise refuses an adult’s retrospective gift of foreknowledge to his younger self (E003–04).

GO-CL007 can now be strengthened through explicit narrative evidence: the father’s death is called suicide and described as self-inflicted firearm death. The earlier preferred inference is corroborated by a later report, with the original U001 wording/freeze intact. Motive remains a different question. The narrator disputes **選ぶ** (analyst’s gloss: “choose”) as a description of the father’s perceived living options, while distinguishing selection among means. That is an argument about felt alternatives made by the surviving son. It is neither direct access to the father’s mind nor a universal medical explanation. The imagined contemporary recording system sharpens the distinction: a database could determine the second of a shot, while still leaving why beyond recovery (GO-RV010).

Repeated childhood questions encounter the mother’s stated not-knowing and terrible expression. The narrator calls such unexplained death a curse on survivors, specifying self-accusation and memory’s imperfect persistence. His later question about who cleaned the bodily traces is deliberately unrecovered. A remembered film image makes the possibility of his mother doing it newly dreadful; **もしや** keeps possibility and knowledge apart. The strongest counterreading is that “curse” overstates irreversibility through a grieving narrator’s rhetoric. That is a genuine limit, but it does not erase the concrete relation the passage builds: surviving people supply painful questions which the dead can no longer answer (E005; GO-CL020).

### Stress and sin are explanatory vocabularies, not recovered sessions

The counselor/priest sequence is conspicuously conditional. An imagined professional answer classifies occupational stress; the paired answer classifies occupational sin. The narrator then invents workplace-style advice about reassignment and holidays. No actual consultation or priestly failure has been witnessed. The repeated construction makes an explanatory substitution audible while keeping Alex’s actual reason inaccessible (E006).

Work nevertheless has material weight in the narrator’s account. The two-year interval contains repeated operations and a reported expansion of massacre. His accusation that Washington issued too many authorizations connects institutional demand to human capacity. It is serious causal suspicion, alongside Alex’s own earlier words, rather than a demonstrated single cause of suicide. His preferred possibility is that Alex killed himself to escape inner hell; the wording **だろう** and “possible” conclusion retain its inferential status. His own continuing peaceful dead-country experience further differentiates the men’s responses to death. GO-CL009 gains this divergence without turning a colleague’s suicide into a controlled test proving a treatment failed (E002,E007,E013; GO-RV011).

### Familiarity depends on who is listening

Williams’s account of Alex’s irreverent religious jokes is a material countercase to the narrator’s strict picture. The earlier Bible-as-entertainment anecdote already gives Alex wit; the newly reported jokes go beyond the one the narrator knows. Leland and Williams had received speech that this commanding colleague had not. The narrator admits the gap rather than silently absorbing the report into an always-complete portrait (E002,E012).

That does not require treating Alex’s devotion as a disguise. The narrator also remembers conversations in which each man retained his theological position without trying to recruit the other. Sincere belief, irreverent humour and different styles with different recipients can coexist. The source supports recipient-conditioned access more strongly than either a theory of secret unbelief or a simple pious caricature. Williams’s wish that Alex had consulted them likewise expresses grief and belated limitation; it is not evidence that he could have supplied a successful cure (GO-CL021).

The remembered base statement also revises disclosure order. Alex had spoken about internal hell before the Part I operation, rather than discovering that idea in front of the executions. This earlier scene is newly disclosed now. It gives recurrence to the image while preserving the earlier reading’s knowledge frontier. Even the narrator who had heard it more than once cannot now say what the particular hell was (E013).

### A plain name acquires a mythic information surround

The narrator separates responsibility from operational usefulness. He now says they have assigned too much responsibility to individuals, then states the instrumental predictions through which particular deaths are selected to reduce cohesion or facilitate peace. Calling killed first-layer targets possible martyrs is his provocative extension of that logic. The passage does not show that every selected killing achieves its projected effect (E008).

Within the stated two-year interval he reports two personal killings and five assassination operations; four orders include the same target, now named **ジョン・ポール** (analyst’s romanization: John Paul). These are bounded reports, not an invitation to invent a complete mission list. Each failed pursuit is followed, in his account, by a more detailed profile. The banal name and growing secrecy jointly produce a demonic/mythic aura. Legitimate compartmentalization could also explain piecemeal disclosure; the text gives the narrator’s frustration with its effect rather than an inspected institutional motive or a proved hidden agent (E009; GO-CL022).

His reply that the target is human has an explicit practical consequence: a human will eventually err and become capturable. Williams completes the projected sequence with killing. An ethical-humanization reading has some basis in the likeness to themselves, but the exchange assigns that likeness operational use. The humanity of a target remains compatible here with plans for his death. Williams’s theatrical “tourist” description is similarly an attributed label, while the currently supported pattern is repeated orders/failed capture and association with reported conflicts (E010).

### Private leisure continues to carry the war’s sound

The home scene makes the protected consumer vantage from Part I ordinary action. Williams orders pizza; both men drink and replay the violent opening of a war film because it is enjoyable and falls within the free preview. The narrator says they are both thirty and have not become adults within this consumer cycle. His **男やもめ** supports a man’s alone domestic situation; it does not establish that a wife died. Williams is explicitly described as having a wife, who remains an unseen ancillary mention (E011).

Gunfire now fills a living room through a movie stream. This is a changed source of sound, not the continuing battlefield shooting of U004. Its placement around difficult conversation and the narrator’s surprised recognition of Alex’s unknown humour joins consumption with incomplete mourning. The strongest competing emphasis is ordinary soldierly decompression: shared beer, familiar media and jokes may provide a way to keep company after loss. That is supported, alongside the narrator’s own criticism of their childish consumption. The reading need not choose between real companionship and the troubling pleasure of reproduced violence (E012–13; GO-RV012).

### Authentication becomes tangible in a pizza transaction

The courier scene actually enacts the thumb-and-database authentication earlier described by the narrator. The following dialogue distinguishes a system’s payer from its custodian: the military pays, while an Information Army contractor supplies private information-security storage. Williams’s lack of civilian adult experience helps explain why the cost had been invisible to him. The narrator describes secure retention and reliable access as work requiring payment (E014–15; GO-CL023).

The material comparison with cheap tags in Part I can therefore develop without collapsing the systems into sameness. Their technical assurance, expense and conditions of belonging differ. What recurs is identity made operational through an infrastructure that someone controls and maintains. Here that infrastructure delivers an ordinary good to privileged employed users; the division of payer, contracting authority and custodian matters for where dependence sits.

Williams then makes the recurring target’s movement newly puzzling through the transaction they have just completed. The narrator admits not considering this and reasons that authentication is payment, so someone with an account should not be able to avoid it. The account-holder condition is important. Their reasoning does not demonstrate that all movement by all routes is impossible, nor supply an actual workaround. It turns the mystery into a specific question of access rather than proof that the target is superhuman (E016).

The recall cuts across that ordinary life with almost no ceremony. Williams’s oily finger operates the domestic wall interface, the movie stops and the narrator’s own phone rings from headquarters. The source ends at Williams’s announcement. Purpose and new operational information belong beyond this boundary (E017).
