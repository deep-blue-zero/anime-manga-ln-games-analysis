The first-person report of roughly four sips and her probable abstention remain qualified recollection, not exact measurements of either person's attention (E001).

Her first reply says he acted rightly and should not carry this as guilt. Its knowledge is limited by his edited account. She does not receive his mission, history of killing or intention to kill her former lover. **職業倫理** here restrains disclosure: professional obligation preserves the secret while emotional pressure almost breaks it. ## What biological origin is asked to explain

ルツィア interprets his painful decision as undertaken for his mother's happiness, then argues that humans are predominantly made for good action rather than hell (E003). Her confidence exceeds what she can independently know of the mother's wish and his whole motive. The previous unit's painful uncertainty remains a material counterweight, even though the reassurance is sincerely offered as represented.

When he hears religious overtones, she explicitly shifts to evolution. He presents adaptation as individual survival and self-protection; she invokes social insects and an unspecified game-theory simulation to argue that stable groups and benefiting others can evolve (E004). These are forcefully represented arguments. The source neither names a reproducible experiment nor supplies its rules, population, payoffs or actual results. Her assertion that complexity tends toward altruism is not independently established by the conversation, and his simpler survival account is not installed as the analyst's biological premise either.

The significant distinction in her explanation is between conscience itself and its details. She gives the capacity an evolved origin while treating moral particulars as socially transmitted. His objection from different moralities therefore receives a specific answer, rather than being ignored. Her model also leaves questions: stable cooperation does not by itself demonstrate every actual helping act's moral worth, and explaining a tendency does not decide this individual medical choice.

He initially hears biological origin as reduction to a robot. His inner response insists that he chose his mother's death himself. Her later position does not require erasing that self-description. The argumentative issue becomes whether antecedent causes can serve as substitutes for the acting person in assigning guilt.

## The soul's usefulness and an unshared explanation of アレックス

Asked why he invokes a soul despite professed lack of faith, クラヴィス turns inward. A separable elevated center and an alternative afterlife could lessen the finality of the lives he has taken. He recognizes this as an instrumental, debased use of religion and declares that he is not the atheist he imagined himself to be (E005; GO-CL049).

The Japanese makes this a discovery he claims now, not a small possibility the analyst should soften away. Its target is his use of an imagined surviving self to evade consequences, rather than a demonstration that souls exist or that every believer evades guilt. His change in self-description also need not retroactively make every earlier profession deliberately deceitful.

He contrasts this use with アレックス's seriousness. First come qualified alternatives: perhaps アレックス did not escape, or could not. Then comes a categorical causal conclusion—this is why アレックス killed himself, he now understands. The increase in certainty is part of the narration. It does not supply independent access to the dead man's motives, and the thought is not spoken to ルツィア. A newly confident explanation must remain attributable to its thinker.

## Influence does not become an alibi in her argument

The dialogue returns to memes: conscience's details pass between generations, with some retained and others lost (E006). ルツィア rejects his proposal that people are simply ruled by these things. Memes, she says, depend on thinking and deciding hosts to circulate. She nevertheless acknowledges genetically conditioned thought and memetic influence while denying that either transfers responsibility for conscience or guilt away from the person.

That distinction is more precise than a claim that she denies all biological influence. It also does not settle every metaphysical question about freedom. Her categorical affirmation of choice faces his hypothetical cases of genetic sexual violence and childhood abuse producing a killer. Those are hypothetical arguments, not disclosures that he has such a gene, was abused, plans assault or is a serial killer. The exchange does not independently establish identical capacities under every antecedent condition.

Her formulation of freedom is the ability to choose to give up freedom: obligations, things one must or must not do, can be undertaken for oneself or another. This continues the earlier discussion of constraint and freedom at the scale of responsibility. Actual institutional pressure and unequal conditions remain visible elsewhere in the narrative; the speech need not abolish them to explain its effect on him.

He emphatically distinguishes that effect from approval or erased guilt. What rescues him is accepting that he chose to bear the acts' burden rather than having someone load it on him (E007; GO-CL045v2). His thanks is spoken and silently received. The represented effect of her words is strong evidence of changed self-understanding, while the truth of every scientific and philosophical premise remains a different question. She still does not know the full acts whose responsibility he silently applies her argument to.

## Keeping the cover while anticipating violence

A blank separates the philosophical exchange from the trip home. The narrator claims pervasive authentication deters planned crime but cannot prevent desperate, unplanned acts. The passage supplies his general account, not independently measured crime statistics or proof that the approaching attack belongs to that category. Its immediate practical consequence is that escorting a woman home remains customary (E008).

He detects surveillance on leaving transit, considers the previous followers and sends an emergency signal to ウィリアムズ. Sending occurs; receipt, arrival and help are not shown. His prediction of an assault gains immediate evidence when the follower follows their accelerated pace and a second man appears ahead. Two men are visible, but he repeatedly distinguishes that count from the possible total force (E009).

Cover changes the timing of force. He regards his own pistol as a last resort because displaying it would destroy the advertising-worker persona. That constraint can put him behind an attacker ready to shoot. This is neither total tactical freedom nor mere passive obedience. He chooses among risks while pulling ルツィア along despite her complaint; protecting her and withholding the reason coexist in the same physical action.

He reaches the front man before the rear runner arrives, seizes the pistol's slide, twists the hand and breaks the trigger finger. The narrated sequence credits real competence. It also reaches an unexpected limit: the captured weapon rejects his palm and locks when he tries to shoot the rear man (E010). Authentication functions as a material restriction here. It prevents his attempted firing, rather than supplying a simple guarantee of safety or a universally beneficial effect.

A registered attacker weapon makes him ask whether an intelligence service is involved. That remains his question, not a confirmed agency affiliation or proof of the earlier biometric mismatch's method. The front man is recognized as a previous follower; the rear man will be recognized as the youth he assaulted. Their cooperation is depicted through the attack, while their chain of command is not yet supplied.

## A body interrupts the argument about command

He has enough spare attention to imagine explaining his combat skill without revealing the current mission. The next sentence explicitly ends that margin. Three short paragraphs—flame, the fingers in sequence, electricity—break the fluent tactical account (E011). The enumeration makes the body insist before he can explain what has happened.

Pain is distributed through fingertips, toes, eyes and internal organs. The description claims an actual overwhelming present sensation. It is not an actual flame demonstrated inside the body; the later conditional comparison asks what burning from within might feel like. Like the earlier bodily attentiveness, the sensory detail makes embodiment specific, but here it defeats his capacity to act.

The rear man no longer runs: he advances calmly with a mobile terminal aimed at him. Together with the onset of pain and sudden change in tactical confidence, this strongly suggests device-associated incapacitation. It does not yet demonstrate a technical route, implant, signal protocol or particular neural mechanism. The narrator's near-collapse also limits what he can establish about unseen attackers.

ルツィア calls him by his cover names, touches his shoulder and appears close to panic. He tells her to flee; her eventual decision to leave feels intolerably slow to him under pain. This is not proof she betrayed him or had complete knowledge of the ambush. His narration explicitly relates the experienced duration to suffering.

Then a voice says she need not run. He identifies its speaker through a face repeatedly seen in operation documents and years of pursuit: ジョン・ポール (E012). Her frozen stillness is observed; its exact inner explanation is not given. The identification makes the pursued man physically present, and his timed intervention directly connects him to the confrontation. Who directed the attackers, their affiliation and the pain's specific mechanism remain unestablished.

The natural subsection ends with the narrator losing practical command. The conversation's relief did not erase guilt, and it did not immunize the choosing person against bodily coercion. That juxtaposition is formally powerful without becoming an experiment that proves or disproves all of ルツィア's account of freedom.
