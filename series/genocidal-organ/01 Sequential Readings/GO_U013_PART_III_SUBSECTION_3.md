## Privacy for a chosen disclosure

The conversation resumes with appraisals of ルーシャス. クラヴィス's French/Czech contrast remains a cultural judgment, not new evidence of nationality or occupation. ルツィア explains why a venue like this persists: older people remember less authentication, and some young people find the present order suffocating. She then distinguishes her own position from a wholesale rejection of information-managed safety. She fears terrorism too. What she wants is occasional respite from someone knowing her drinks, meals, partners in dancing and hours of attendance (E001).

These are her stated preferences, more explicit than her earlier glance at a demonstration. They do not authorize a complete political identity for her or every younger customer. The narrator expands them into a place without records or prying, where everything is permitted and she can truly be alone. That absolute formulation is his uptake, beyond the demonstrated technical scope of the club. Her invitation to him makes privacy relational: chosen company and chosen disclosure can belong to a private space, rather than contradicting it.

The scene's operational irony persists. He is a covert observer she has brought into that valued space; the source still supplies no proof of her mission awareness. His thanks may express real appreciation, but does not disclose the information asymmetry. The private setting changes the conditions of their encounter without proving complete transparency between them.

## Finding a listener outside the usual forms

She links the mood to John and says she has neither religion nor a liking for counselors. クラヴィス identifies with her. She also lacks a confessional priest and a diary habit; he offers fiction as another way of putting one's life into words. Her refusal on grounds of literary ability leaves him to offer himself as listener (E002).

This exchange proposes different forms of confession without enacting any institutional treatment. Her dislike is her preference, not evidence that counseling is generally useless. Nor does his suggestion of autobiographical fiction establish a theory that all novels simply sell private experience. The practical question is who can hear what she needs to say.

Her gaze turns toward the projected abyss. The narrator explicitly uses a comparison, as though she wanted to be absorbed into it. The floor remains a representation; the comparison does not demonstrate a present suicide intention. Similarly, the later lip-biting looks to him like self-punishment, without giving an independently verified motive for the gesture. Such qualifications matter because the account soon supplies forceful first-person descriptions of her actual remembered wishes. Those reports need not be replaced by his guesses.

## Pleasure remembered beside an irreversible loss

Her opening declaration joins the sexual encounter and Sarajevo's disappearance. She says John's wife and daughter were visiting an older sister in Sarajevo; the sentence does not explicitly identify whose sister. That detail supplements the earlier dossier's tourist-trip account through a different channel, without requiring an invented family tree or an immediate contradiction between visiting relatives and travel (E003).

Her account gives the absent family's time a remembered emotional value: freedom from worrying about the wife, enjoyment of Massachusetts together, happiness washing away guilt. This is her retrospective self-report. It supplies something the narrator's lists of purchases and locations could not contain. It does not make her a cause of the nuclear explosion or equate the affair's responsibility with responsibility for killing the family.

After the spacing break, the narrative presents long unquoted first-person blocks. Their speaker remains securely ルツィア: the introduction, the narrator's interruption and the explicit end of her story frame them. They must not be read as クラヴィス's autobiography. He reports her whisper becoming a level, almost storybook recital, as though she were reading about a woman named ルツィア・シュクロウプ. The variation belongs to his account of hearing, not an actual change of typeface or a different document (E004).

She recalls returning from the shower to find John fixed on a news clip. Links accumulate around it, yet he repeatedly watches the first report instead of pursuing them. That is a reported observable action, not direct access to his whole inner state or proof that a particular later program began at that instant. Her recollection gives a scene where the dossier supplied a trace.

The account then refuses a simple disappearance of love under guilt. She says she still wanted to meet him and be held, and wanted to obliterate the self that could continue wanting that. The latter is a strong remembered wish in her testimony; no attempt or clinical diagnosis is supplied. She felt unable to accompany him to check on the wife and child, and later feared encountering him because his presence had become synonymous with her guilt. The ethical conflict lies in incompatible desires she can still name.

Her reported loss of contact has a narrower vantage than the dossier's fuller chronology. She says he quietly returned, left the university and went abroad; she did not search. No exact interval here cancels the earlier account of a period of withdrawal. Her fear and avoidance are her explanation, not a newly proved account of every movement he made.

## What hearing adds

クラヴィス cannot find a helpful reply. He recognizes a likeness to his own burden. What had remained vague in the profile gains **血肉** through her words as sound; he explicitly says that understanding absent from the data took shape through her voice (E005; GO-CL044).

This is stronger than saying the conversation merely repeats facts. The narrator identifies a change in understanding and its immediate represented cause. It is also more bounded than a universal theorem about reading and hearing. His account of ears having no eyelids invokes an unnamed writer; the source does not supply a new attribution or independently admitted essay. The reader encounters the voice through written narration, which stages the very auditory force it describes. The novel's written representation does not make the scene's distinction meaningless, but keeps its medium visible.

The language of flesh also extends the material vocabulary of the preceding nightmare. Here words gain flesh as understanding, rather than actual muscle tearing through the city. The difference preserves the force of the correspondence without turning metaphor into a demonstrated biological mechanism.

Sound then acquires color: confession appears brown like dried blood, thickly laid on like a Rothko abstraction. These are narrator's sensory and artistic figures, not a colored source passage or an independently viewed painting. He names the heard story **懺悔**, situating it after her rejection of a priest. She has found a listener; whether he can provide absolution is another question (E006).

## The dead recipient and the living speaker

The final argument turns on the death of those toward whom guilt is directed. In the narrator's formulation, death removes the hope of hearing the injured person say that forgiveness is granted. He moves from this lack of a possible recipient to the particular horror of murder, then to the emphatic statement that the dead cannot forgive (E007).

These propositions are strongly asserted; treating them as tentative musings would flatten the Japanese. Their attribution remains consequential. The passage gives クラヴィス's argument, not a legal classification of ルツィア as a murderer, an authenticated statement of the victims' wishes or an authorial settlement of every theology of forgiveness. Temporal proximity between pleasure and death does not establish causal responsibility for the bombing. The likeness he finds concerns a structure of guilt and unavailable reply, not equal acts.

The following unnamed God-is-dead formulation transfers forgiveness to mortal human beings within that argument. It belongs beside both speakers' stated lack of religion and the missing priest, rather than authorizing a diagnosis of everyone without faith. The narrator's own nightmare had already revoked the desired maternal answer. Here he articulates why a living sufferer may be unable to obtain the answer from the person whose death matters most.

He explicitly identifies this likeness as the reason for his attraction to ルツィア: both are owners of guilt toward the dead, in his self-understanding. Her reciprocal romantic intention is not supplied. The final decision to tell his own guilt changes the direction of the conversation; a guest eliciting another's account now proposes to disclose something himself (E008; GO-CL031v4/045).

The retrospective final **だったのだろう** characterizes that decision as perhaps the pettiest form of confessing affection. Its conjectural retrospection does not erase his explicit present attraction, but it limits the last interpretation to the narrator looking back. Neither the actual content of his confession nor her response is admitted in this unit.
