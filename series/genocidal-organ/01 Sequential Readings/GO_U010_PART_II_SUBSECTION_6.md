## A professional welcome to danger

クラヴィス notices watchers immediately after leaving ルツィア's apartment. He reasons that they are following him rather than maintaining an earlier watch on her room, since his own opposite base had not noticed them. That is a strong local operational inference, not independent knowledge of their assignment. He restrains a grin, adjusts tension as adrenaline rises and registers the pavement with almost ticklish sensory sharpness. Readiness has a felt, partly pleasurable texture (E001).

The control shown here is specific. He avoids returning directly to the base and uses a head-scratch as an intended signal to ウィリアムズ; the text supplies no acknowledgment of its reception. He explains their staggered arrivals and his avoidance of real-time wireless transfer from worn sensors during visits. This new explicit restriction does not erase U009's monitored exchange, but it prevents inventing a live-broadcast mechanism through those sensors. An observed information channel and its unshown implementation remain distinguishable.

The immediate investigation is also a choice. He expects an organized adversary, without knowing whether it belongs to ジョン・ポール, and sets out to identify the tail. The prose does not yet depict an attack upon him. His eventual violence grows from tactical initiative and the desire for information, not a demonstrated need to repel a blow.

## The useful annotation and the cold labyrinth

The street's オルタナ overlays an excess of tourist advertising on Prague. The image is strikingly textual: reality acquires a mountain of annotations. The two ruby forms—副現実/オルタナ and 代替現実/オルタナテイヴ・リアリテイ—layer a technology's names onto the already layered city. Filtering is an operational act of attention: he removes advertising to seek information that can help him act (E002).

The interface remains bodily. A contact recognizes a keyboard drawing after three seconds, but the fingers do the work. His account of eye-input's decline privileges speed over the elimination of touch, while acknowledging that a drawn board gives no mechanical key response. This is narrated technological history surrounding an actual depicted use, not an independently checked account of real interface development.

His first search fails. He admits that preparation should have preceded the outing, posts a request and arranges forwarded replies. The network therefore does not provide effortless omniscience. Later someone supplies precisely the needed traffic map, attributed to transport-ministry open-source data and traffic-observation airships. The displayed reply has a practical consequence: he selects a nearby unusually quiet alley. The underlying measurement history is reported through that reply; no independent audit of every statistic is available (E004).

Meanwhile he tests the tail through reflections, a tram ride and repeated feints. The initial passengers are suspicious rather than instantly identified. Only later does he distinguish the group: the first young man, another man in a particular style and a woman in vintage sportswear. Their ages and clothing are observed; a group worshipping ジョン・ポール is his question. It must not become their established allegiance (E003).

Prague's preserved architecture and Kafka associations make the city a cold labyrinth for him. His contrast with Borges describes this perceiving imagination; it does not establish a secret literary code used by the pursuers. He lays pheromone markers in case he is abducted, imagining either rescue by a tracking dog or a scented grave marker. These are purposeful precautions and conditional futures, not an accomplished abduction, rescue or death. The inconveniences of repeated lesson visits help tip his thinking toward confronting the problem. A practical irritation sits beside imagined mortal danger.

## Lessons converted into coercion

Calling a follower a welcome lead, クラヴィス openly warms up for violence. The reader is given the aggressive intention before the ambush. The young man's confusion is the narrator's reading of his reaction. One unexpected blow drops him; the narrator's disappointment at how easily he falls, then the deliberate continuation of blows and kicks, make professional pleasure and asymmetry difficult to miss (E004–05).

He calibrates the beating to remove resistance while preserving consciousness. That ability is competence in coercion, with no need to treat it as ethical restraint. The practical limit later named is insufficient time and exposure in a city: in an appropriate setting he could inflict more pain. The victim is not shown striking or attempting to strike him. The result extends the earlier distinction between an operational explanation for violence and an actual self-defense event.

Three repetitions of **さて** organize the demand to know who the man is. The exchange is rendered as reported dialogue without the corner quotation marks that return in the later base conversation. Repetition makes each question a renewed procedural step, while bodily injury supplies its punctuation: swollen lips, a kidney kick, vomit (E006).

The third attempt applies a just-learned Czech lesson about placing the emphasized word first. The continuity with ルツィア's room is exact and disturbing. A language lesson acquired through sociable conversation becomes a tool in violent interrogation. The narrator's account of Czech emphasis remains a represented lesson, not a newly established universal grammar. The passage's formal force does not depend on validating that generalization: he consciously uses what he thinks he has learned.

The man moves from refusal to **俺は誰でもない** and a plea to be believed. クラヴィス first treats speech as something that will not disclose the required information. He abandons conversational communication and forces open the eyelids to photograph retinal vessels, then presses fingers onto a reader. The sequence replaces the person's claim with coerced bodily evidence. The body appears to promise an answer independent of testimony (E007).

His ensuing insistence that he is not a sadist is explicitly belated: **これだけやっておいて** acknowledges the preceding scene. The repeated **ぼくの仕事は** identifies the work with violence, death, pain, cries and vomit. Professional classification does not logically remove the pleasure, disappointment or domination already narrated. Conversely, the passage does not supply the basis for a clinical diagnosis. It gives a self-exculpatory distinction under pressure from the actions and affects surrounding it (GO-CL037).

## Nobody, within the database

After the spacing break, the result overturns the assumption that a body will deliver one name. Retina and fingerprints identify different people. The man's statement was true **少なくともデータベースに関するかぎり**. That restriction is load-bearing: he is neither literally without personhood nor shown to lack a lived history. The technical system cannot unify these identifiers into the identity the interrogator wanted (E008).

クラヴィス feels an urge to apologize and says that, if they meet again, he should do so. No meeting or apology occurs. ウィリアムズ calls him terrible with evident enjoyment; he agrees while they eat pizza and watch ルツィア having dinner. The juxtaposition makes brutality compatible with ordinary appetite, collegial banter and continuing surveillance. Remorse is not absent, but its conditional formulation produces no observed reparation.

He considers transplantation and ordinary database inconsistency, then discounts both through assertions about medical records, immunity and the resources devoted to critical personal-information management. The passage states those reasons clearly; their presence does not independently prove that every alternative is impossible. Nor does his dismissal of either identifier being the original person's identity become a complete forensic demonstration (E009).

The repeated nobody statement returns after this analysis. He explicitly attributes the tears to the injury he inflicted rather than a romantic predicament. The qualification refuses a sentimental account of the stranger's suffering, while also admitting its immediate cause. The later credibility granted to the statement exposes the earlier refusal to hear it.

## An authorized fiction and an unknown counterpart

The result prompts an illicit-editing hypothesis, followed by a comparison with クラヴィス's own identity. **ビショップ** is now explicitly an invented person supported by a separate, outsourced military identity-management system. The narrator describes advance approval by a Senate information-security committee chair and two others for such false identities in ID-managed developed states (E010).

This supplies a concrete institutional account where U009 had shown only successful cover authentication. It strengthens GO-CL023 without retrospectively changing what U009 knew. Institutional provision can make a fictional identity legible to working scanners; no scanner malfunction is necessary. The new account still does not expose the exact implementation, identify which record was altered in the young man's case or solve ジョン・ポール's disappearance.

The analogy leaves a government-agent possibility strongly remaining in the narrator's reasoning. He then asks whether another country's interest explains the urgency of the American killing order. ウィリアムズ accepts its possibility. Neither the affiliation nor that causal explanation has been confirmed. Even the idea that the watchers mistook him for a target associate remains a question. These are material competing possibilities rather than claims licensed by the mere presence of a tail.

His turn from speculation to **確たる情報** is therefore significant. He recalls ルツィア's account of prediction, survival, self and language, now using it to justify generating possibilities while resisting endless deliberation. This is uptake of her explanation, not demonstration of an evolved mechanism. And the nominally firm information to which he turns will contain further interpretation.

## The dossier's facts and the narrator's imagination

The profile becomes an almost-completed crossword, assembled through successive failed missions. クラヴィス condemns this piecemeal disclosure by analogy with piecemeal military commitment: information is ammunition and logistics. He and ウィリアムズ share the counterfactual that full disclosure at the start would have prevented the present trouble. Their professional grievance is explicit and well motivated by what they have been given; the source does not prove that outcome or the bureaucracy's hidden motive. He expressly says he does not know the latter (E011; GO-CL022v3).

The new biography reports that ジョン・ポール's wife and six-year-old daughter died while visiting Sarajevo. An ID trace places him at the student ルツィア's apartment at that time. These are dossier-mediated accounts, not a present target scene or independently inspected archive. The narrator describes the exceptional military/intelligence privilege that permits retrospective inspection of private life. The same infrastructure that made a cover fiction ordinary now lets an operator assemble an intimate past (E012).

クラヴィス distinguishes disinterest in the infidelity from training that enables vivid imagination of regret and pain. That is an account of his professional imaginative capacity, not direct access to ジョン・ポール's guilt. The text later says plainly that the man's feelings at the crater can only be imagined from records. The one-month interval before going to the camp and the subsequent three-week wait are retained; they are not collapsed into immediate arrival at the crater.

The earlier literary ruins acquire a terrible new relation to reported lived loss. His question asks what a person would feel when an attractive fictional landscape actually appeared. It is a powerful comparison, but its interrogative form leaves the target's answer unknown. The wife and daughter are recognized as distinct victims rather than reduced to evidence of an explanatory psychological law. Nothing here proves that bereavement mechanically produced a particular later program.

The six months after his return are reconstructed from missing transport/shop authentications and food orders. Those records support reported withdrawal and very little departure from home. They do not record every human interaction or mental event. Questions about despair, suicide attempts and sleepless nights are questions; no actual attempt or count of nights has been established. Likewise, the suggestion that connections and linguistic talent secured PR employment is marked as inference (E013).

## A confident accusation without an exposed technique

The PR account makes representation operational: receptions, selected media-friendly officials and press infrastructure gather attention, sympathy and expectations of American intervention. Recognition within that enterprise leads to advisory posts. The subsequent report that every assigned country fell into civil war and atrocities is categorical within the narrator's account, while the kinds of perpetrators vary (E014).

The narrator does more than note a correlation. **ぼくは断言できる** directly introduces his certainty that John could have brazenly remained at the company; the following **確かだ** directly marks indifference to office opinion. Then **なにせ、最初から…ために…のだから** asserts deliberate entry to prepare a path to massacre as the explanatory premise. It would misread the Japanese to dilute that purpose assertion into a mere tentative possibility voiced by him. Its grammatical force is strong even though the three formulations have different immediate scopes. His speculation about what colleagues could have imagined has a different modal status.

The evidentiary limit is elsewhere. The present reader receives this strong retrospective assertion from a narrator operating within the prior official causal account. The unit does not show ジョン・ポール admitting that aim, performing a new massacre-producing procedure or revealing how language might enact it. Attribution of certainty preserves both the force of the accusation and the unresolved technique (GO-CL038).

The final sequence contracts this thick biography into absence. Forced departure is followed by the cessation of authentication traces at a Prague shopping mall; later appearances survive in mission orders and reported sightings. The CIA officer's reported visual contact and the absence of an ID confirmation do not become a present on-page encounter with the target (E015).

**幽霊** names this operational pursuit of a person who repeatedly fails to coincide with the records and orders that promise him. The following **おそらく** keeps his figurative birth in the Sarajevo crater conjectural. The Godot allusion answers an earlier conversational association with the narrator's growing feeling of endless pursuit; it predicts neither perpetual failure nor a supernatural target. Part II ends with a confident accusation attached to an elusive person, after an interrogation showed how violently the demand for identity can misrecognize its answer.
