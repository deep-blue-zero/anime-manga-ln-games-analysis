# GO-U025 — choosing a path, hearing another person's future

The narrator crosses a landscape whose materials make his own mobility possible, then defines freedom through the trained capacity to choose a constrained path. He makes that definition bear on responsibility for killing. His movement brings him within hearing of Lucia and a soldier whose account of John's speeches imagines a better common life. Yet the narrator cannot hear Lucia's thoughts, cannot understand the technical notes he finds, and finally discovers John behind him with a gun. Skill, moral ownership and access to another mind remain different achievements.

## A freedom purchased through narrowed lives

**この湖が、ぼくらの生活を支えている** opens with an acknowledgment of dependence, now made from the shore actually reached in this subsection (E001). Wings, pods, prosthetic limbs, industrial machinery and porters form an inventory that connects an apparently remote lake to ordinary and military movement. The cetaceans' expected conversion into these objects joins the young people's restricted prospects: factory work, soldiering or sex work. These are the narrator's forceful descriptions of the represented economy, rather than a demographic survey proving an exhaustive list of every possible life.

He initially allows that ineffective law could be freedom **ある意味で**. The following **だが** changes the measure. The boys and girls have little they can become, while the users of artificial muscle obtain a **「自由な生活」**. The source expressly calls the former deprivation a transaction for the latter freedom. The quotation marks prevent consumer convenience from passing unexamined as a complete account of freedom. This continues the earlier recognition of material complicity, but now names the displaced choices of other people alongside his own enabled body.

## The difficult route as an ability to choose

He finishes removing the diving equipment and waits close to an hour; Williams and the others do not reach the meeting point (E002). This is actual non-arrival within the wait, not proof of their deaths or of the aircraft's destruction. The usual four-person plan must be discarded. The present text does not identify the unnamed other participants with the three newly named survivors of U023.

The ensuing route exposition distinguishes visible ease from safety. Open paths invite movement precisely because they reduce its immediate cost; their predictability makes them dangerous. The soldier's trained judgment therefore selects a harder route. Shore patrols and obstacles near the target partly remain expectations—**ことだろう**, unknown mines or sensors—rather than an observed inventory of every defense. His actual movement is nevertheless deliberate. The **「縛り」** turn apparent disorder into precise combinations of moves, figured through **詰め将棋〔チエス・プロブレム〕**. The source's ruby joins the Japanese game term to “chess problem”; it does not remove the constrained character of either image.

**その安全なルートを判別する能力こそが「自由」** makes the literary argument explicit (E003). Freedom here is a capacity acquired within limits, rather than permission to wander without consequence. Risk assessment and selection produce options that a merely arbitrary movement might destroy. This is the narrator's definition and its practical illustration, not a demonstrated account of every human freedom.

## Remembered speech and the correction from choice to non-choice

The argument widens from jungle routes to the histories that make a self. N24–25 explicitly recall Lucia's account: consciousness, language, conscience, guilt and punishment arise within adaptation, without an independently created soul; genes and memes still do not decide everything. Values, obligations and attachments can be weighed because alternatives remain. No date or location for this remembered statement is supplied here. It should not be silently installed as a newly recovered verbatim segment of the earlier Prague conversation.

N26's bird comparison and N27's definition continue in the narrator's present reasoning. The romantic phrase “free as a bird” becomes his foil for human selection, but his claim that the bird has only a genetically commanded option is an anthropocentric contrast in this argument, not established zoology. Its force is to give his **「わたし」** something it must own: choosing means abandoning other possibilities in one's own name.

That claim changes the sense of punishment. **だから、ぼくは罰せられるべきだ** follows from the stated possibility of choosing. Maternal responsibility supplies a measure for the responsibility he failed willingly to assume across prior choices. The dash then revises **すべての選択** into **選択しなかったこと**. This is a consequential correction: his target becomes the refusal of ownership under obedience, rather than a claim that every former decision was equally deliberate. The retrospective **引き受けてやれなかった** expresses failure and self-reproach; it neither retracts his earlier maternal acknowledgment nor proves that he literally lacked agency.

## A commanded assassination and a killing claimed as his own

N29 calls the current operation a Pentagon-ordered assassination; N30 then insists that he has come to kill John through **自分自身の意思**, rather than the will of the Pentagon or Special Operations Command (E004). Both statements matter. The narrator characterizes the institutional task while distinguishing the reason and ownership he now claims for undertaking it. Own will does not demonstrate that an institutional task has vanished.

This is a newly admitted characterization beside the earlier explicitly narrated arrest instruction. No new quoted directive or formal revocation of that earlier instruction is presented here. The current claim should therefore be recorded in its own date and voice, without rewriting the earlier capture frontier or inventing a missing institutional order.

His account of Alex sharpens the difference between being punished by an authority and taking responsibility oneself. He now calls Alex's response sincere and reproaches his own former exclusion of guilt from patriotic work. The sweeping **ぼくの主体的な決断も選択もなかった** remains his present retrospective assessment. Earlier tactical and moral decisions have not disappeared from the accepted record. The reading can recognize a serious change in what he owns without endorsing that sentence as an objective history of zero agency. His declared intention to kill John is a character plan; no killing has occurred at this boundary, and the intention is not an analyst's forecast.

## A state described as a game

The guesthouse becomes an observed target: a colonial-style two-story building, tended grounds and patrolling soldiers (E005). He advances slowly with camouflage that expressly cannot make him perfectly invisible. No dogs appear in his survey; this is narrower than proof that the site possesses none. When a guard later passes within a meter without noticing him, the camouflage has a concrete local success, rather than a universal guarantee.

The account of state formation interrupts that approach (E006). Developed countries desire a stable artificial-muscle supply and the convenience of dealing with one manager. The narrator presents wealthy coastal interests and European banking intervention as the political economy behind independence. Yet **仕組まれたゲームといった感** and bankers **あたり** retain the evaluative and approximate character of parts of his explanation. No banker is personally identified or shown giving the supposed advice.

His distinction between a modern army's necessary public justification and Africa's supposedly preserved “medieval” appetite for gain makes a much broader civilizational judgment. The betrayal-game account then describes a whole land's ethical code as reset and insufficiently complex. The earlier **かもしれない** qualifies a possibly different past; the reset itself is asserted within his analogy. These are his generalized causal and evaluative models. They are not an objective ethics score, a displayed simulation, or proof that John has caused a particular population-wide reset. The text gives them interpretive weight while leaving their speaker visible.

The analogy also complicates the freedom argument. He has just defended a historically formed self's ability to choose; he now describes other people's conduct through a developmental model that restricts what their society can do. The approaching dialogue supplies a material counterreading to the sufficiency of that model: an ordinary soldier can articulate hopes for other children's schooling and a shared future. His words do not falsify a fully specified mathematical model—the text supplies none—but they exceed an exhaustive portrait of people seeking only immediate private advantage.

## A recognized voice and a locally assigned marriage

He crosses into the courtyard and hides centrally, **「堂々と」**, using the camouflage (E007). A greeting first seems directed at him; he nearly responds with a knife before recognizing that the soldier addresses someone upstairs. His access to the scene depends on correcting that perception.

The answering voice is recognized through remembered discussions of the Holocaust, conscience and confessed betrayal. N50 names **ルツィア・シュクロウプ** directly. This is a stronger present identification than the earlier target report or his wish to find her. He hears her and later tracks her into a room; there is still no face-to-face reunion between them.

The soldier is greeted as **ムガベ**. That local named interlocutor is not identified with a historical politician, and no rank or personal biography follows from the name. His **マダム・ポール** and reference to **ご主人** establish a local address and assigned relationship. The narrator explicitly keeps two possibilities: Lucia is treated as John's wife here, or they have actually married. Legal marriage is not settled by the exchange, nor does the address erase her earlier identity.

## Hope heard through a soldier

Lucia says she has begun to adapt to the place while remaining distressed by the lives outside (E008). Her response to the soldier's promise that the visitors have come to improve those lives is cautious: **そうなるといいんだけど**. She reports John still working and sleeping very little, then suggests ordinary insomnia. These are her statements and a qualified explanation, not a clinical diagnosis or a proven consequence of linguistic work.

The soldier's longer speech supplies something the narrator's game model did not: an imagined civic future. He wants to become such a politician himself; radio speeches attributed to John make him feel that collective effort can overcome poverty and AIDS, restore fish to the lake and send girls to school instead of scavenging cetacean offal. Industrial export and recovered fishing coexist in that hope. The earlier objection to calling the manufactured lake a revival remains available; the promise has not been fulfilled merely because it is believed.

The repeated **気にさせられる**, **思える** and **信じさせてくれる** strongly support a felt persuasive effect as this soldier reports it. His final inference makes writing evidence of the writer's goodness. But we hear his account of the broadcasts, not their literal script, a demonstrated grammatical calculation, or an isolated causal test of genocide language. The language is already doing political work in his testimony; the specific hidden mechanism remains undemonstrated.

## The apology he cannot hear

Lucia does not immediately answer (E009). N62–63 then offer an apparently intimate apology in **わたし**: “my husband” has come to produce hatred and a future of human bones among the discarded cetacean bones. N64 decisively assigns these sentences to the narrator's imagination of her thoughts. They are neither an audible confession nor direct access to Lucia's consciousness.

The archaic declaration **反つて分争なり**, with its deliberately supplied readings, enters the narration between that imagined apology and his correction. It gives the peaceful promise a scriptural countervoice, rather than recording a new utterance by John or Lucia. The formal opposition is sharp: hopeful speech about a shared future, an imagined future of shared disposal, and a declaration of division. Their juxtaposition does not authenticate the imagined apology.

N66 then states his preferred estimate: it is more likely that Lucia still does not know what John is doing. This qualified judgment replaces the momentary imagined confession as his stated view, but it does not verify her actual knowledge. Her eventual courteous pleasure and goodnight can sustain the possibility of ordinary ignorance; her silence can also sustain discomfort or reservation. Neither licenses the analyst to select complicity or innocence as a demonstrated mental fact. The source has made the limit of the narrator's access explicit.

## The ghost, the paper and the gun behind him

He follows her route up the stairs after the soldier leaves (E010). Boots adapt to the contact surface and suppress his footsteps. This technical assistance changes his feeling of standing bodily on the ground: he feels like a ghost. The passage does not make him literally immaterial. Its sensory estrangement sits beside his insistence on acting in his own name; practical capacity and the felt ownership of an embodied act need not coincide automatically.

He enters with his gun ready, but Lucia is no longer visible in the room he saw her enter. The initially “unoccupied” room is his encountered appearance, not an established explanation for her disappearance or proof that nobody can be behind him. Moonlight, a desk and paper occupy his attention.

The draft seems intended for the Federation chairman; the desk suggests recent writing, and he infers that John apparently does not use a notebook terminal or mobile device for this work (E011). These qualified observations cannot establish a lifelong prohibition on electronic research or independently authenticate the author of every page. The notes seem to concern words' gender and case, logical conditions of sentences and particular patterns of arrangement. That partial recognition is real, but he immediately admits that understanding the specialized language is beyond him. A material trace of apparent linguistic work is stronger than an absent document; it is still not a reproduced formula or demonstrated causal mechanism.

The subsection began by making constraints intelligible as moves in a problem. It now lets him recognize arrangements while failing to understand their rule system. That formal recurrence links his tactical competence to a limit, without equating jungle routing with the grammar's actual mechanism.

The sound behind him changes the scene before interpretation can become mastery (E012). He hears a safety being released, is addressed as **殺し屋君**, turns and sees John pointing a handgun at him. **悲しそうな顔** reports a sad-looking face, not direct knowledge of John's motive. The man he has explicitly decided to kill is present and armed; the narrator is now the object of a weapon's direction. Lucia's location, his comrades' fate, the encounter's outcome and the causal status of the notes remain open at the natural ending.
