# GO-U022 — the battlefield that the body cannot leave

The return journey does not complete the mission's promised delivery. It carries an argument about responsibility into a new encounter with the soldier's own technology. John tells クラヴィス that the absence of a proper emotional response leaves him uncertain whether he owns his killing intent. The narrator explicitly recognizes the diagnosis. Later, an attack makes pain masking visible on both sides. He is frightened by that relation, relieved to remain outside it, then driven toward it by guilt. Those successive states make the previous operation's “perfect” function a bounded achievement rather than a permanent elimination of feeling.

## What can be heard beyond the forest

The train's simplicity promises durability (E001). It has survived nuclear war and continues to connect places. The opening outlines a return to Mumbai, the Hindu-India commanders' transfer to Hague prosecutors and their eventual confinement by Panopticon, with John expressly excepted. These are the intended arrangements that would finish the mission; the current subsection has not yet shown the deliveries completed.

The team has actually landed at a forward contractor base and stayed four days. Food provision has a geographical limit, unlike the apparently smooth universal circulation of supplies. The narrator infers that the catering company serving Mumbai has not come this far; his different ration experience does not itself inspect the contract.

Leland asks why an E&K soldier stares into the forest. The answer changes sight into listening: the soldier says he is waiting to hear mass death. The supposed column of combined voices is called **リゲティ**, with the name's origin and its film-composer connection transmitted through report. No actual forest massacre is directly witnessed in this exchange.

The soldiers want to know what the returning team has seen beyond a boundary their own force has not crossed since a reported failed offensive (E002). Kurtz and Willard furnish the narrator's analogy for this fascination. Stories of cannibalism, decorated nuclear weapons and wandering victims are explicitly folklore and ghost stories; their circulation is not confirmation of their content.

The question about why men near real death can still fear ghosts produces a substantial account of fiction's power. **フィクション──いや、この場合「妄想」と言ったほうが近いのかもしれない──** can threaten existence even on a materially dangerous battlefield. The correction from fiction to delusion keeps the category unsettled rather than establishing supernatural beings.

John belongs to this doubt. Capturing a person who identifies himself as John does not make a learned man's voice causing massacres intelligible to クラヴィス. His ironic **アカペラ** gives the alleged power the form of one unaccompanied voice, against the massed voices named Ligeti. The body is present; the supposed power is still difficult to assimilate. This distinction prepares the dialogue without turning it into a demonstration of that power.

## A transport that carries several kinds of confinement

The next movement is actual: an armored road journey to the station, repeated sedation of prisoners, their discomfort on waking, a kneeling guard arrangement and boarding (E003). A captive calls the conditions mistreatment. The narration supplies the bodily circumstances as well as the complaint; it need not accept his broader self-exoneration to recognize the coercion.

The train has three reserved forward cars around the prisoners' car, while ordinary passengers crowd the later cars and roofs. Their destination is inferred. The narrator considers several possible reasons to travel: flight from violence or poverty, and reliance on relatives. He then imagines the train merely adding inhabitants to Mumbai's shacks.

Calling poverty a camp without a fixed border and comparing the journey to Nazi deportation is his powerful analogy. It registers enclosure within apparent mobility, rather than proving that every passenger has the same destination, that none has useful kinship ties, or that this train is literally a deportation operation. His preceding questions preserve the alternatives his bleak conclusion threatens to flatten.

The train's rough durability is compared to a Kalashnikov. The same vehicle can be reliable infrastructure and a punishing place to sit. Even its roof is dangerous according to what he has heard. The apparatus that moves people is not their guarantee of safety.

## The prison that an old colonel has not imagined

An hour beyond the predicted danger line, クラヴィス visits the prisoners (E004). His expectation that an attack is unlikely is a current tactical judgment, not proof that this territory is secure. Its later failure should not enter an earlier forecast record that was never made.

The prisoners' faces receive varied interpretations, not one pooled mind. Their reduced resistance follows repeated knockout treatment in his account. An older man assumes the detention facility will be guarded by a weak national army. クラヴィス instead describes outsourced correctional confinement, contracted by the UN and New India to Panopticon and defended by private specialists.

**クリーンなアルカトラズ** is his compact image of this security. He predicts that escape or assault would be nearly impossible. The older man's former colonel rank comes from the instant ID performed at arrest; his ignorance and skepticism are the narrator's readings of his expression and historical position. Neither the conversation nor the broader claim about worldwide private prisons substitutes for an independently inspected institutional record.

The dispute matters because it concerns where coercive capacity now resides. The captive imagines the national army as its relevant holder. The narrator insists on a private provider's expertise and a contract. Their disagreement preserves the difference among state functions, delegated operation and a prisoner's actual knowledge of his destination.

## A slogan is not an experiment

John points to a passing sign and says he wrote its slogan (E005). クラヴィス sees heavy angular lettering, a soldier image and apparently forceful words; the slogan itself is not transcribed. We therefore cannot independently analyze the alleged grammar inside those words.

John says the effect does not depend on semantic content and can be embedded in ordinary conversation; **「濃度」** names the degree of embedding in a sentence. Propaganda supposedly tends toward concentrated embedding. Those are increasingly specific claims about the method. Specificity does not itself establish their truth through a shown linguistic sample or a measured outcome.

He then reverses a familiar causal story. **もしかしたら** introduces the possibility that extreme political ideas are requested as details that prepare massacre, rather than independently causing it. クラヴィス calls the reversal absurd; John replies that the original language claim is already absurd enough. This defense lowers the force of an incredulity objection, but supplies no independent causal test.

The narrator's attention moves through shafts of light imagined as a god drinking souls, with a Python cutout for that god, into a strange operational **無時間** (E006). Medication, emotional adjustment and masking are said to help sustain it. Rail pulses make the present feel indefinitely extended; another slogan returns his attention to the conversation. Images and bodily time mediate understanding before either speaker resolves the theory.

## The quantity of guilt and the absence of touch

John calls the propaganda's picture socialist realist and mocks a convergence of degraded aesthetic sense at political extremes (E006). That evaluative joke gives immediate context to クラヴィス's accusation of contempt. His accusation explicitly concerns John's use of other people, rather than simply repeating the accusation of spreading massacre (E007). Those who dirty their hands are, he says, more trustworthy. He assigns weight to direct participation.

John answers that the quantity of guilt he has chosen could never be accumulated through his own direct labor. He will complete it as the person responsible. This is an asserted undertaking, not an independently measured amount of guilt or demonstrated acceptance by a court or victim. It also does not imply that indirect responsibility requires less intention.

The custody distinction becomes audible to John. The other commanders are to be tried at The Hague; John asks **国防総省かね**, and クラヴィス confirms while saying he has not heard what will happen after that. A planned destination and an admitted knowledge gap coexist. No Pentagon delivery has yet occurred.

The following water request is met with a threat of unconsciousness as a substitute for thirst. The device's capacity to remove a complaint exposes how easily the custody relation can evade the person making it. The request does not establish that water is actually supplied, that another sticker is applied at this moment, or that the speaker's needs have disappeared.

## The accuser finds the right wound

The repeated Lucia question now becomes an explicit declaration: **ぼくが望むのはルツィアだ** (E008). John asks whether children may be killed for that purpose. The narrator hears hostility and feels both anger and relief at this first response he recognizes as emotional. The relief is not forgiveness; an antagonist has become affectively legible.

**仕事だから** is his deliberately dull defense. John attacks the formula through Nazi and East German examples, then makes a broad argument about labor, Protestant discipline, capitalism and religion. That universal account belongs to John. The dialogue does not prove that every kind of work exists solely to numb conscience.

Their dispute over direct and indirect killing then shifts from hands to **手応え**. John says he has none because he does not fire or burn. He asks whether the adjusted soldier possesses the expected relief, guilt and felt ownership when he kills. He also extends a claim to the soldier's comrades with **おそらく**, which should not become independent access to all their minds.

Here the narrator gives unusually strong ratification: **図星だった** and **真実をまくし立てるこの男**. John's account of his own felt incompleteness and anxiety about owning the intent hits its mark. It would be excessively hesitant to treat that correspondence as merely one remote possibility. His earlier private worry is now spoken by another person, and he hates the speaker for being accurate.

This does not validate every historical or neural proposition in John's surrounding speech. It corroborates the particular diagnosis through the narrator's direct recognition. The man seems strangely still within the rattling carriage, his background almost composited: the image expresses his disruptive presence, not an objectively separate plane of reality.

## Similar adjustment does not settle every moral distinction

The glove metaphor gives another account of mediation (E008–009). John says the soldiers allow a covering on the mind that permits insensitivity to children. His claim that this permission is in some sense crueler than killing is an evaluative argument with an identified speaker, not the analysis's default ranking.

He compares the grammar's effects with preoperation adjustment. クラヴィス replies that their treatment is defensive and enhances self-preservation, unlike ritual mutilation. John answers that both alter conscience by changing the balance among brain functions, while using different means.

クラヴィス calls the account **科学的に装飾した性善説**, with a religious smell like the counselor's explanation. John replies that the vocabulary of conscience creates the suspicion, then defines it as an achieved balance among many judgments and risk calculations. Suppressing a small area would destabilize that balance and prepare a society for massacre. This is a fuller claimed mechanism, including a limited-modulation comparison; it remains John's account rather than a displayed corpus, scan or controlled social experiment.

The analogy nevertheless has material purchase. The narrator has just enacted adjusted killing and recognizes the missing emotional response. He now restates the comparison between John's alleged grammar and the military's psychological treatment, then says he cannot answer it. This is current uptake, not neutral distance.

A functional likeness need not erase differences in purpose, scale, consent or method. The soldier's earlier acknowledgment of choosing the profession remains relevant, while unsuspecting people affected through everyday speech would occupy another relation if John's account is correct. Conversely, calling an intervention defensive does not refute the actual killings or automatically restore the emotional response. Both limits arise from admitted evidence; neither requires pretending the comparison has no force.

## A hypothesis about someone higher up

John's cheerfulness makes クラヴィス consider and reject an account of frightened overtalking, based on his reading of the man's manner (E010). He then accuses him of having a government insider. Repeated escapes, restricted operational knowledge and alleged continuing access to an NSA program form the argument.

The accusation is a strong inference made by the character. John's nod does not remove the next sentence's qualifications: he says he does not know whether the inference is right and proceeds **仮に情報漏れがあったとして**. His story about narrowing suspects, possible operations designed to locate a leak, and a supporter's impending loss remains conditional or conjectural. No insider is identified or independently confirmed.

The subsequent violence may make this discussion feel ominously relevant. It does not license the analyst to fill the unprovided command chain. The attacker identification must follow the actual evidence that comes next.

## A visible helicopter with no known owner

Williams reports a passenger's warning, and the two men actually move rearward to look (E011). At first they cannot see the aircraft. Combat glasses and the overlay then resolve a low-flying helicopter approaching quickly.

Williams calls it Chinese-made. The narrator immediately explains why that identifies no faction: Pakistan, Hindu-India, New India or E&K could all use such a machine. Manufacture and operational ownership are expressly separated. The market explanation for cheap equipment also belongs to his exposition, rather than establishing an independent global arms census.

His warning to Leland is interrupted by the train's abrupt deceleration and rotation (E012). He loses consciousness, with elapsed time unknown. When awareness returns, the world initially seems unrelated to him and the hospital summer comes to mind. That comparison is explicit; a clinical diagnosis or an equation of the two medical states is not.

Pain is known and located without being felt. The masking permits movement but complicates his own assessment of injury. A display lists fractures and bleeding; he calls the sensors unreliable because he feels little operational difficulty. The reader should preserve that judgment as his, rather than independently certifying false sensor readings or a body without significant damage.

## A calm voice inside catastrophic injury

The first medical overview is not a named casualty list: he says he has no time to inspect individual names (E012–013). Calls initially go unanswered. Survivors' cries form an actual chorus, and he wonders whether this is what the soldiers called Ligeti. A wounded passenger is then killed by a shot he identifies as intended for himself. The civilian cost has ceased to be merely a forest report.

Leland eventually answers. The locomotive's explosion is his **ようです** report, followed by his account of a heliborne attack and a trapped car. He does not know the captives' condition. The guards' deaths come through medical status, not his direct view of the neighboring car.

His own lost arm is spoken of in the same energetic voice (E013–014). クラヴィス briefly nearly laughs, while recognizing that it is not a joke. The imagined man who discovers his own missing head is the narrator's grotesque extension, not an additional actual report.

Leland confirms the sensory division in his own words: he recognizes pain but does not feel its quality. This second speaker and the catastrophic condition materially extend the earlier needle and cheek evidence. They support a local fictional mechanism more strongly than an isolated technical assertion would, without revealing every parameter of the intervention.

The sudden **バリー** cry and report that Nelson has been hit support a probable local linkage of the names. They do not warrant two separately modeled people or an invented complete biography. The later identifiable body gives Nelson a bounded recognition record; the exact instant of death is not separately narrated.

## The asymmetry disappears

クラヴィス attacks the surrounding force and sees severely injured men continuing to fire (E014–015). The ghostlike appearance is environmental camouflage; the zombie comparison concerns function after injury. Neither is a literal discovery of supernatural beings.

**痛覚マスキング。** names his conclusion. He explains it through the same knowledge-without-feeling distinction, and Leland independently agrees. Continued action despite injury, their equipment and this shared recognition strongly support the inference of comparable protection. An exact implant inspection and the attackers' employer are still absent.

The narrator contrasts this fight with the previous asymmetry of wealthy forces against poor ones. His sweeping statement about no prior G9 conflict is his history of the world; the immediate discovery is that both groups in front of him possess advanced means of disregarding wounds.

N153 also hardens his retrospective wording about the earlier children, now describing them as drugged. U021's actual argument had made their intoxication conditional. The present stronger description is recorded as a later narratorial assertion, without rewriting that frozen condition or treating it as an independent toxicology finding.

## Fear, relief, guilt, movement

The confession **ぼくはそこで怖気づいてしまった** is direct (E016). He expressly distinguishes the cause from ordinary fear of death. What arrests him is the meaning of sustained combat without painfulness: bodies can continue until mutually destroyed, and the relation becomes grotesque to him.

He has not yet reached Leland's car. He feels relief at being excluded, then calls that relief contemptible. **戦場** is redefined from a circle described by weapon range into a relationship among people able to keep firing through catastrophic injury—**脳のある状態が描き出す地図**. This is a forceful conceptual change in his narration, not a newly displayed literal neural cartography.

The same protected body can be unable to join that relation. Physiological masking has not guaranteed an identical emotional capacity in every circumstance. His fear and guilt are present while the sensory procedure still functions. There is no statement that the medication has simply worn off.

Guilt about nonparticipation finally drives a run that he says excludes professional practical judgment. This is neither the calm continuation of perfect work nor an uncomplicated moral recovery: he runs into lethal danger because remaining outside his companions' battlefield feels intolerable.

The account explicitly separates scene knowledge from later explanation. During the run he wonders why no bullet hits and experiences that as further exclusion. Only later does he understand that the attackers were withdrawing. His survival is not evidence of invulnerability or a providential rescue disclosed at the time.

## The leg that cannot answer the question

He reaches the car (E017). Some men still attempt to fire; no collective death total is established. Leland's condition is much worse than the earlier arm report revealed. The suit's strenuous effort to keep him alive is called futile by the narrator.

Looking for Leland's legs, クラヴィス picks up a leg near Nelson, then realizes he does not know whose it is. Offering it with **とりあえずこれで我慢してくれないかな** carries the habitual form of an inadequate practical solution into bodily catastrophe. Leland's weak smile supplies an answering human relation that cannot make the limb interchangeable or the action effective.

The body of Nelson is described as what had once been Nelson; death is strongly implied, while its precise event and timing remain unshown. Leland's end is more explicit: his voice and presence disappear, consciousness leaves, and the narrator answers **リーランドの骸**. Recording his death does not require reducing the passage to an indeterminate loss of consciousness.

His final question concerns the outside. クラヴィス answers **わからん** after the voice is gone. The narrative has not supplied him—or the reader—with the captives' current fate, Williams's later condition or the attackers' affiliation. The helicopter engine rises in pitch; gunfire gives way to the passengers' Ligeti. A name first carried by soldiers' stories now belongs to directly heard suffering, without authenticating the other stories told beyond the forest.
