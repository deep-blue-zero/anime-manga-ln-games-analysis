# GO-U014 — a decision made without the answer

The narrator returns to the hospital and home surrounding his mother's death. The withdrawal already disclosed in U002 gains a more difficult account of knowledge and motive. ## Freedom can strike a pedestrian

The initial question asks whether the mother suffers. The doctor's reply shifts attention to the subject who could suffer, before the intervening recollection supplies the circumstances of that question (E001/E009). The structure places the philosophical difficulty at the entrance rather than appending it as an abstract moral after the accident.

The narrator returns from a Central Asian scene of corpses three days after the accident. An old pink Cadillac, driven drunk and lacking contemporary safety devices, has struck his mother and three or four other pedestrians. Their deaths are not stated; the driver's death is. His formulation gives the machine the **自由** to carry an intoxicated brain onto the pavement. Beside the recent club discussion of freedom and security, this is a particularly sharp counterweight to treating freedom from an imposed safeguard as an uncomplicated good (E002).

The source depicts a consequential absence of constraint, without proving that every present safety system works or that this precise accident could certainly have been prevented. Responsibility still includes the drunk driver; the car's personified freedom does not turn it into an independently choosing subject. A second vehicle's collision ends both the Cadillac's motion and its driver's life through parallel stopping language.

The mother has, in his formulation, once died: spontaneous breathing and then the heart stop. Appropriate intervention restores heart activity. This sequence establishes narrated arrest and resuscitation, not a separately certified irreversible brain-death diagnosis followed by a miracle. Technologies familiar from sustaining battlefield function now maintain his mother's body. The military and domestic settings meet through a practical continuity of treatment, rather than a proved identity of all their ethical purposes.

He then attributes her survival and demanded decision to revenge for his dangerous career. The sentence is forceful, but it supplies no maternal directive, testimony or independently known wish. Later in this same account he repeatedly admits that he cannot imagine her wish. The attribution expresses the son's interpretation of being made responsible, and must not become evidence that the mother designed the situation.

His unhurried arrival likewise is not simple indifference. He explicitly describes sadness, while explaining that death is too familiar for surprise. The claim that the world is always sudden gives an unsettling coherence to his refusal to run: recurrence has made suddenness ordinary. Later sustained crying materially prevents this controlled approach from becoming a diagnosis of general emotional absence.

## A legible hospital and an unreadable subject

At reception, authentication and alternative guidance work. Having forgotten his contact device, he follows markers on the floor. The hospital turns complex space into readable functions; without that guidance he imagines a maze (E003). Technology here enables orientation and care. The missing personal device produces assistance, rather than the complete exclusion an undifferentiated surveillance thesis might predict.

The mother's shaved scalp becomes another surface of signs. The narrator compares medical reminders to a refrigerator door covered in notes and to an old phrenological diagram. These are his comparisons; the source does not establish that the doctors actually practice phrenology. The remembered notes at ウィリアムズ's house will soon contrast with his mother's completely bare refrigerator. A head marked to preserve knowledge and a home without preserved photographs make remembering materially conspicuous.

The doctor's full greeting names クラヴィス・シェパード and his rank. The doctor gives his own name, but the narration withholds it; an analyst cannot recover a name from the fact that one was spoken. The son initially reads a facial hesitation as loss of hope, then retrospectively corrects that reading: he now understands the difficulty as an expert's trouble explaining complex knowledge to a nonspecialist (E004). The narration asserts that reinterpretation strongly, while its retrospective perspective still does not provide independent access to the doctor's private thought.

The doctor can describe damaged and surviving modules. He cannot give an experiential answer to how many surviving functions amount to consciousness, or whether any remaining subject receives neural input as suffering (E005/E009). The text distinguishes mapped function from experience rather than making the doctor simply ignorant about the injury. The billiard-ball analogy explains the impact, while the narrator's marshmallow correction stresses the material's vulnerability.

These are medical claims within the fiction, not independently established neuroscience. Nor does inability to undergo the patient's experience prove that all inference about another person's suffering is impossible. The local force lies in the doctor's refusal to convert available anatomical knowledge into the certainty the son wants.

## A house that remembers movement

The return home begins with smell and an unreturned **ただいま**. His old room is maintained and nearly dustless; he moves through it like a detective or thief. He interprets the house as the mother's eye, searching for traces so that he will not disappear as the father did (E006). The explanation is his account of her care and fear, not direct testimony from her.

**追跡可能性**, given the literal witness ruby **トレーサビリテイ**, makes domestic observation explicit. Childhood evidence of snacks and secretly invited friends was read from the arrangement of the house. This is human attention to traces, not a newly proved camera network. The memory gives the wider vocabulary of tracking an intimate scale, where being seen can protect a relationship and become difficult to bear.

The narrator attributes enlistment and volunteering for special forces to that suffocation. The ensuing second-person self-address is harsh: he has obtained danger, corpses and loss, yet is still alive. Its reference to a comrade lost to suicide is not a secure new timestamp for the mother's hospitalization relative to アレックス; retrospective self-address and remembered time need not have identical knowledge limits. The text itself breaks off the thought because going further frightens him.

The later account supplies a material counterweight to calling the mother simply controlling. He explicitly says she was not overprotective, that he was brought up comparatively freely and did ordinary reckless things (E008). What differs is his persistent feeling of her gaze, even in her absence. The arrows he imagines through doors and corners describe that felt spatial organization. They do not demonstrate actual uninterrupted watching in each room. His final formulation makes reassurance the other face of suffocation; neither feeling cancels the other.

## Records desired in place of imagination

The kitchen's bare refrigerator reverses the hospital comparison. There are no notes, magnets or photographs. He wonders whether family images and a life graph reside in the mother's web space, but authentication is demanded. The account depicts neither a successful login nor an actual generated biography (E007).

His imagined software story is asked to disclose what his mother would want now. Yet he knows raw data would be unmanageable and questions how useful a software-narrated life could be. His desire for records survives that doubt. He explicitly revises its motive: he was afraid to admit that he could not even imagine her wish, rather than merely looking for evidence to support an existing imaginative answer.

The contrast with targeting is precise. He reads psychological charts and institutional logs to predict people he will kill; he cannot imagine the wish of the mother he loves. This does not prove that the military predictions work perfectly or that logs can never aid understanding. It exposes a local failure of proximity to guarantee knowledge, and of available professional habits to provide an answer in this different relationship (GO-CL046).

The phrase **それは間違いない** strongly affirms his love before the fear that some part of him resents her. The source presents feared ambivalence, not a confession that he never loved her. Nor does loving her give automatic access to her preference about continued treatment.

## No clear answer, but two available acts

He leaves for a motel because he cannot stay in the house. He tells the doctor that his mother was not the sort of person to prepare a will. The doctor then says her end-of-life treatment wishes are unknown and she has no religion, leaving the decision with him in this represented account. The passage supplies no exhaustive search proving that every possible directive is absent. This is the represented local allocation of surrogate responsibility, not an independently verified universal medical law (E009). The narrator's wish that someone already had settled the issue turns medical uncertainty into anger at medicine, then into a broad complaint against philosophy's neglect of technology (E010).

That complaint is his asserted diagnosis of intellectual failure, with its own **たぶん** in assigning philosophy the task. The doctor has provided a candid limitation, rather than the source proving deliberate professional neglect. A more charitable account of the same scene is unavoidable surrogate choice under uncertainty. That alternative does not remove his experienced cruelty of being required to decide.

His fear ends in prolonged crying and unsuccessful retching. The morning brings a decision, not the discovery of a missing fact. The isolated paragraph **二択でしかない** is given space between blank paragraphs (E011). Many dimensions of uncertainty have not disappeared; the available treatment action is binary. The formal compression makes decisiveness possible without pretending that complexity has been solved.

He barely reads the withdrawal consent, authenticates and stops support, then politely declines offered counseling (E012). The repeated institutional offer puts this death beside marital difficulty and pre-operation preparation. His irritation does not demonstrate counseling's general inefficacy, and his refusal is not a completed therapeutic encounter.

At the funeral he comes to think exhaustion made the decision possible. At the moment of authentication, he had believed he acted for his mother, who would not want an intermediate condition and must be suffering. He now explicitly recalls that the doctor did not know whether suffering was received at all (E013; GO-CL008v2). This does not prove the decision malicious. It changes the authority he can give his earlier justification.

The old home's felt pressure returns as another possible motive. By the funeral's end he can no longer find the loving basis for the choice, and the thought that he killed his mother begins to haunt him. The sequence establishes a powerful self-accusation, not a transparent account of his whole causal motive or a settled murder verdict. Fatigue may explain why an unavoidable decision became possible while love and ethical concern remained real. His inability to recover a pure motive is itself the burden the reader now understands more fully.
