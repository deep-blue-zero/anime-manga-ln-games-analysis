## History peeled back into muscle

The linked **第三部** heading is meaningful entering framing, separately read and rendered without following its navigation link (E001). Numbered subsection１ then begins with Prague apparently left behind by a riot. Cobbles and advertising layers have been torn away; artificial muscle pulses beneath the city's history. The conceit makes a literal surface of the overlays discussed in U010. What seemed a preserved architectural labyrinth now has a living-looking technological underside (E002).

The narrator tests the muscle with his shoe. Its firm elasticity joins sensation to the sight of exposed red tissue. Leaving the empty city, he first sees a meadow, then recognizes a field of stripped intrusion pods; a towering aircraft wing and his mother's flayed body share the same red material. Similar appearance does not prove that the mother is literally a machine, that the pods possess consciousness or that an actual riot has uncovered Prague's infrastructure. The waking transition and the narrator's later explicit account establish the scene as his nightmare.

The imagined empty city also develops an admitted contrast. U009 ended with peace in an imagined ruined Earth resembling his dead country. Here solitude becomes abandonment and fear. That contrast changes the current affective record without making the earlier comfort false. Nor does a distressing dream by itself prove incapacity to perform a mission (GO-CL009v6).

## An absolution offered and revoked

The mother's claim to have been burned by a nuclear bomb collides with クラヴィス's remembered Washington death. He insists that he killed her; her first answer separates the car that killed her, the doctor who ended it and the son who did not kill her. Their discussion then distinguishes a biological boundary from his actual question about responsibility. He wants to know what happened when he authenticated and said yes (E003–04).

The dream offers exactly the reassurance he seeks: a painful decision made for her, cessation of machines and nanomachine supply, a necessary act of care. Then the isolated **いいえ** revokes that comfort. Her next words identify the previous answer as what he wanted to hear and deny that anyone can supply the dead woman's actual answer. The formal reversal is unequivocal. It does not establish that his living mother opposed withdrawal, or turn the initial reassuring speech into independently authenticated consent. The scene dramatizes the desire for an answer whose source is unavailable.

The accusation then extends from maternal death to professional killing. Calling himself a gun or a policy instrument has supposedly allowed him to escape responsibility for choosing. The dream mother argues that the exceptional guilt attached to her death can become a way to seek absolution from all his other deaths. The comparison directly attacks a division in his own self-account; it does not provide a legal verdict that every medical decision and every military killing is identical (E005; GO-CL040).

Her assertion that he never seriously considered why he kills has the force of an accusation within this dream. It must not erase the actual earlier passages in which he questioned reasons or recognized surrendered judgment. Those passages make this conflict more specific: critical awareness has repeatedly coexisted with continued participation. The nightmare presses that coexistence until his familiar accounts of agency no longer comfort him. Institutional orders and pressure remain real contexts; the voice denies that those contexts eliminate his own decision.

His attempts to cover his ears and run fail. The words pursue him because they are not simply another person's speech in an external landscape. アレックス then points to his head and locates hell there. His material account of humans as cells, water, carbon compounds and DNA refuses the prospect of finding an ethical escape in something beyond matter. Muscle erupts through the city's historical layer and lifts the narrator toward an imagined place without guilt or hell (E006).

The ending of the nightmare gives that flight no established success. The next section wakes him sweating and crying. Later he explicitly describes mother and アレックス as shapes taken by **自分自身** in a dream. That retrospective statement strongly identifies the proximate source of these voices. It neither proves that the living アレックス held every materialist proposition nor turns the dream into the dead mother's independent moral judgment.

## A colleague's care and the limits he assigns to help

ウィリアムズ brings a cold towel and water and steadies him. This performed care is distinct from the cruelty and teasing already admitted in other contexts. クラヴィス acknowledges the dead-country experience and its increased frequency since アレックス's suicide. His partner unexpectedly reports dreams of アレックス too, but specifically calls them ordinary dreams: he remembers no content, only the unpleasant feeling on waking. Shared distress does not establish identical visions or a shared supernatural encounter (E007).

The counseling discussion also preserves different channels. クラヴィス considers help, citing counseling before operational killing of children. The Japanese places that earlier counseling before the killing; it is not a report of treatment afterward. He contrasts a possible priestly route for アレックス with his own lack of religion (E008).

ウィリアムズ supplies an ordinary-life case: he and his wife attended a military counselor during marital difficulty while their daughter stayed with a babysitter. He says it worked for that problem, then doubts the same man's ability to advise appropriately about アレックス's death. This is his bounded efficacy report and present judgment, not evidence that counseling generally fails for grief or that the narrator has already tried and failed. No new appointment or treatment refusal is enacted here.

クラヴィス says the burden involves more than アレックス, but cannot explain it well. The partner advises him to settle it himself, given that religious words cannot intoxicate him into release. The narrator's following recognition concerns the intolerability of hearing familiar self-knowledge from mother- and アレックス-shaped versions of himself; it need not certify every maximal accusation as a complete biography.

He offers to take the watch because he is awake and expects difficulty sleeping. ウィリアムズ complains about the sweaty sheets; the narrator understands the complaint as an attempt to distract him. That benevolent intention is the narrator's confident interpretation, while the exchange and offered shift are performed. Care takes the practical forms of towels, water, shared disclosure and familiar irritation. The nightmare leaves a visible aftermath without resolving professional or familial guilt (E009).

## An outing with an undisclosed experimental purpose

After another spacing break, the narrative arrives at a Jewish cemetery. The change is not assigned an invented same-day chronology. クラヴィス takes a small head covering from the office and receives ルツィア's explanation of the requirement. His description of unread Hebrew letters as artificial and alien characterizes his unfamiliar perception; it does not establish an objective property of the script or of its readers (E010).

The outing has an operational design. ウィリアムズ suggested meeting away from her home to distinguish whether the surveillance concerns クラヴィス alone, both of them or her possible complicity. These remain alternatives in a proposed discrimination. The unit does not give a positive or negative tail observation that resolves them. Asking for a guide in a world whose オルタナ supplies extensive guidance is, by the narrator's admission, a barely plausible excuse. Her hesitation and agreement are observed; her private reason for agreeing is not supplied.

He nevertheless participates in the cemetery's gestures with her, placing small stones for Kafka and his sisters. The situation extends the earlier coexistence of intellectual interest, attraction and elicitation. Real attention to a grave can occur inside an operation whose full purpose one participant has not disclosed.

## Which lives the record can recover

ルツィア's account of Ottla's marriage, divorce, departure and daughter is an embedded historical narrative. クラヴィス did not know it, and she attributes her broader Holocaust knowledge to repeated conversation with John. Her suggestion that it was his university research subject is qualified; the actual research contents remain unknown to her. These statements are newly heard testimony, not an independently admitted biography of Kafka's family or a fact-checked historical source (E011–12).

The passage then distinguishes the obscurity of individual deaths from the surviving precision of transport records. クラヴィス describes details of lives and deaths submerged in the large word Holocaust. ルツィア answers with the record of movement and immediately parallels it with their own repeated authentication. The connection concerns capacities to trace people, not an assertion that ordinary transit and extermination are the same event (E013).

クラヴィス gives a serious protective rationale: preventing terrorists' entry, tracing them after an attack and deterring action through the expectation of detection. These are his causal claims about the system; the conversation supplies no measurement of their effectiveness. ルツィア explicitly says she understands and refuses to make a generic Orwell/Big Brother complaint. Her counterpoint is more precise: classification, calculation and coordinated transport can serve organized persecution as well as administration.

Her account attributes a crucial logistical role to punch cards and IBM machinery. クラヴィス responds with a paraphrase about mass transport's dependence on that equipment and a broader computing/war association. Their exchange makes a strong literary argument about the use of technical capacities. It does not itself independently establish every historical claim or license an unbounded theorem that all computation produces atrocity. She distinguishes the classified research from the open transport tables John once showed her. Her report of seeing such a table remains mediated testimony; the table is not displayed to the present reader (GO-CL041).

## A metaphor received as a discovery

The conversation moves from records to a reported phrase: massacres have a distinctive **匂い**. ルツィア attributes this recurrence to John across several histories of intentional mass death. クラヴィス's unquoted narration then states that John reached that scent while investigating earlier massacres. He adopts the discovery as a definite part of his working account rather than keeping every part in tentative quotation (E014).

What the scent means is less settled. クラヴィス suggests it is not the smell of corpses; she agrees and offers a poetic expression as her interpretation, perhaps a way to speak about classified findings without revealing them. The qualifications belong to her explanation. The earlier nasal sensor does not make this a newly demonstrated chemical signature, and no causal linguistic technique is exposed here.

She also reports what appears to have been a small team while saying John seems to have done nearly everything himself. Whether nobody knew, and whether his wife knew, are her qualified beliefs. The new historical interests and phrase strengthen the research lead, while the boundaries of her actual knowledge remain visible (GO-CL036v2).

## Knowledge performed as ignorance

The mention of the wife prompts a deliberately acted surprise. クラヴィス says plainly how hard it is to appear shocked by something already known. ルツィア explicitly admits knowing that John had a child as well as a wife and calls herself a terrible woman. That is new attributed knowledge of her own past position; it does not establish that she knows the operation, approves massacres or shares his current purposes (E015).

Their reciprocal apologies briefly look like a mutual correction of conversational intrusion. His private narration exposes the asymmetry beneath them. He already knows restaurants, magazines, coffee stops and even the number of condoms John bought. The inventory makes a sharp echo of their discussion of traces: movement and purchases can be preserved with a precision unavailable for an inward feeling. They do not, by themselves, tell him what she wants now (E016).

He distinguishes the same response as **白々しい** for himself and **自然** for her. The distinction is his assessment of their unequal informational positions, not direct proof that she has no suspicion. His recognition of his own baseness continues U009's voyeuristic self-criticism without ending the practice. He can still look openly into her face.

Her request to go to one more place gives her an initiative within the outing; its destination and purpose remain unopened. Her eyes and smile are described as sad, not offered as transparent access to all private thought. The unit ends with his recognition of the brazenness needed to sustain ordinary interpersonal conduct while secretly possessing another person's intimate record. The dream's inescapable self-voice and the outing's performed innocence meet in that present self-recognition, without a final absolution or a forced forecast.
