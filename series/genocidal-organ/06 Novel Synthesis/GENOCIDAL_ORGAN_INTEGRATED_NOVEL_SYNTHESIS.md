# Useful accounts and the right to answer

Genocidal Organ repeatedly gives people enough knowledge to do something consequential before giving them enough knowledge to settle what that action means. Clavis Shepherd can imagine a target closely enough to kill him, authenticate a borrowed identity closely enough to pass a gate, and authorize his mother's death without knowing whether she still experiences suffering. John Paul can intervene through language without establishing every part of his theory of its origin. A biography can change Clavis's life without recovering Elisha Shepherd's consciousness. These are different kinds of action, with radically different moral stakes. Their recurrence makes a common difficulty visible: **a practically sufficient account of another life is not that person's answer, and claiming responsibility for acting on it does not confer authority to settle who must bear its costs.** The novel makes that distinction a problem of form as well as ethics. It first invites trust in an account, then discloses what the account left out, whom it reached, and what its speaker wanted the recipient to do.

This argument does not require a world in which every institution lies or every attachment is secretly domination. The more difficult world represented here contains effective medicine, functioning identification, useful records, competent teamwork, genuine love and a real alternative to concealed killing. Those positive cases explain how a person becomes dependent on an arrangement whose larger effects he does not know. They also prevent the final catastrophe from becoming the inevitable revelation of a wholly fraudulent civilization. Lucia Schkroup changes Clavis's chosen course; John changes his intended destination; public hearings actually expose secrecy. What fails is neither all knowledge nor all agency. The culminating failure is the attempt to make the admission “I chose” finish a judgment that other affected people have not been allowed to make. The epilogue gives Clavis unusually explicit authorship and then makes its insufficiency unmistakable. [GO-U002-E004–008; GO-U014-E009–013; GO-U027-E004/E008–009; GO-U028-E003–009.]

## A body can become usable before a life becomes known

The opening initially withholds the explanatory position from which an atrocity could be assigned to its causes. The girl seems to move into an adventure before the wound stops the imagined movement. The boy's parted mouth suggests words without supplying them. Tender attention to teeth and damaged bodies precedes a stable account of who killed these people or why. Clavis's mother then directs his looking and helps him recognize his own decay. The subsequent waking frame and remembered massacre make this experience more intelligible, but they do not recover the boy's message or make the deaths in the procession morally interchangeable. Recognition arrives before causal mastery, and causal clarification never makes every lost voice available. [GO-U001-E003–011; GO-U002-E006–009.]

Clavis's professional competence appears to solve a related difficulty by setting a different threshold. A target need not reveal his own account of himself; the operative needs a sufficiently vivid person-image to anticipate departures from the plan. Such imagination can include affection without obstructing murder. Its achievement is real, but its criterion of success is what the observer can do with it. The opening's unavailable speech therefore acquires a disturbing counterpart in the practiced construction of a person who can be understood operationally without being answered ethically. This is not an accusation that imagination itself is corrupt. Lucia's later testimony changes Clavis's understanding in another way. The question is what a particular act of imagining permits its subject to interrupt. [GO-U002-E004–005; GO-U005-E010; GO-U013-E003–005.]

The patrol encounter makes that practical threshold bodily. Clavis chooses to kill nonattacking men because their presence delays his team before dawn. The search and excision of the tag follow the killing; the tag's discovery precedes Williams's inferred suggestion and consultation with Alex and Leland; ingestion follows the consultation. Killing is not retrospectively explained as a plan to collect credentials. Once recovered, however, the bodily object supplies a new possibility. The next subsection shows the gate accepting the borrowed identification. A person whose last understanding is unavailable becomes a carrier of information that works for his killers. The system succeeds at the local recognition it performs, while its success cannot establish that the bearer is the person the credential names. [GO-U003-E007–010, especially L29–36; GO-U004-E007.]

The sequence also establishes agency before Clavis's later story of not choosing. Neither the camouflage nor the deadline decides to kill. He describes the nonattack, supplies the operational reason and identifies his decision. Williams's eyebrow and the teammates' gestures remain meanings Clavis assigns to visible conduct, rather than independently spoken consent. The count of three disembarking men and four reported deaths remains an unresolved relation between source statements; it supplies no warrant for an invented fourth biography. These limits leave the decision more precisely attributable, not less real. The narrative's first practical conversions already show how much can happen without the resulting account becoming complete.

## Partial competence makes a world run

The military and commercial institutions extend that conversion beyond a single body. Erica Sales's presentation divides war into staff, plans, approvals, delivery and reconstruction. Bodies return in Clavis's recollection while the presentation gives him the pleasure of manageable operations. He is neither an innocent recipient fooled by vocabulary nor an all-knowing critic standing outside it. He perceives the substitution and still wants what it enables. The organizational division has material force because people really can provide the named services. Its danger cannot be explained by calling every service imaginary. [GO-U006-E005–008.]

Successive assignments similarly distribute different kinds of knowledge and choice. Rockwell's initial instruction retains assassination as an objective while withholding permission to kill John immediately on discovery. The India assignment instead explicitly requires arrest and bars transfer to The Hague or the new Indian government. Clavis and his team capture him. Later, Clavis characterizes another mission as assassination while forming his own intention to kill; Lucia then obtains his agreement to arrest. Williams reports a dual-target lethal instruction that Clavis did not know. The final pickup supplies another lethal closure. A timeless order to kill would erase the changes that make the scene's choices and interruptions intelligible. [GO-U006-E020–021; GO-U018-E009–011; GO-U021-E011; GO-U025-E004; GO-U027-E004–006/E012.]

The qualifications belong to particular assertions. Rockwell's arrest imperative is direct. Williams directly reports being told to kill John and Lucia, while his explanation of higher intent is qualified by らしい. John's earlier inference about the order Clavis must have uses はず; it is not a command document. These distinctions prevent the institutions from acquiring a single private mind. They do not weaken the represented suppression into a merely fanciful suspicion. A network can act effectively through different participants who neither know nor intend every part of the result. What must be attributed is the actual contribution: access supplied, target assigned, order reported, discretion exercised, person killed.

The strongest defense of this arrangement is operational rather than cynical. Restricted knowledge and specialized tasks can protect an operation and coordinate rescue. Williams proposes alternatives during the hotel assault, accepts the rejection of smoke, contributes to designation and breach, and later takes manual control of the helicopter. Leland can challenge a tactical gamble. These are decisions within a technically elaborate system, not decorative gestures by powerless machines. Conversely, the actual coercion of prisoners' gait and the captive Clavis's exposure to remotely induced pain are decisive limits on an unrestricted chooser. The novel distinguishes these capacities rather than combining them into total technological control of a mind. [GO-U017-E006–007; GO-U021-E008–009/E012–014; GO-U022-E014–017.]

An institutional explanation is strongest, then, when it resists two consolations. One would make every operator innocent because the overview is elsewhere. The other would restore an overview by inventing an authority that planned every act. Both close a question that the narrative keeps distributed. Need-to-know can be practically justified in one operation and politically useful for excluding those who will bear another operation's consequences. The operator's local competence survives the planners' concealment; the planners' contribution survives the operator's discretion. No participant's limited view is a complete measure of the world produced between them.

## A record is not an answering person

Ordinary services reveal why legibility cannot simply be dismissed. A courier delivers pizza through authentication; gates admit authorized bodies; hospital floor markers guide Clavis to his mother's bedside. These systems make reliable encounters possible. A provisioned Bishop identity can also sustain a legitimate teaching contract while concealing the mission that brought Clavis to Lucia. Correctly recognizing a credential and adequately informing a person about a relationship are distinct achievements. The false premise does not make their shared discussion unreal, but the discussion's reality cannot provide retrospective consent to the concealed intrusion. [GO-U005-E014–016; GO-U006-E002–003; GO-U009-E008–012; GO-U014-E003.]

Lucia's chosen account of the affair demonstrates a different possibility for knowledge. Clavis already possesses a profile; listening lets him hear how she lived its events. The unquoted first-person blocks remain Lucia's testimony because the framing, interruptions and returning dialogue identify her. They are not a transfer of omniscience to him. Her happiness during the family's absence, guilt after the nuclear loss, continuing desire and fear of reunion remain conflicting experiences she describes. They change the listener's access without supplying John's private thoughts or a complete explanation for what he later does. A living speaker can give what a dossier could not, while still leaving much unknown. [GO-U013-E003–005; GO-U010-E012–014.]

Clavis's confession reverses that direction imperfectly. He removes his profession and operations from the maternal story, receives Lucia's reassurance, then privately measures her reply against the hidden killings. Her kindness overreaches what she knows of the patient and the son, but his withholding also determines the question she is permitted to answer. The withheld list is internal speech. It cannot be used either to give Lucia early knowledge of his acts or to establish that she forgave them. The scene matters because a real answer can be inadequate for a reason its recipient has arranged. [GO-U015-E001–003.]

The bedside supplies the strongest objection to treating incomplete knowledge as a moral fault in itself. Someone must decide even though the mother's wish and subjective suffering cannot be recovered. The attending doctor provides expertise and acknowledges its limit. His candor does not abandon Clavis; it refuses to turn medical competence into an answer medicine cannot warrant. Clavis wants the decision settled before it falls to him, yet in that same recollection admits that he has already decided many lives. Love does not suddenly create agency. It makes the known agency unbearable under a degree of personal uncertainty he could previously tolerate for others. The decision is not analytically identical to the patrol killing: surrogate care, unknown wishes, exhaustion and love make its moral situation different. The recurrence concerns the insufficiency of an available account, not equal guilt for unequal acts. [GO-U014-E005–013, especially N75–84.]

The eventual biography does not remove this limit. The earlier search of guest-accessible documents and the later disclosed failure to apply for exceptional access are different events and routes. Clavis's later admission revises the reader's estimate of what he could have tried; it does not prove what such a record would have revealed at the bedside. When he finally orders the biography, the program does something consequential: it puts his father overwhelmingly where he had expected himself to be. The mother who dominates his own story does not make him equally central in the generated account of hers. But the remembered exchanged gaze immediately survives the categorical conclusion that she had not looked at him. The absent record is one that he expected would prove affection. Missing proof is not recovered proof of no love. [GO-U014-E007; GO-U023-E009; GO-U028-E003/E005–006.]

The novel thus gives records two powers that must coexist. They can correct a person's self-centered picture; they can also acquire more authority than their selection warrants. The biography truly devastates Clavis without becoming Elisha's answer. Alex's different jokes to Williams and Leland likewise correct Clavis's portrait without revealing why Alex killed himself. Dream mother and dream Alex let him stage judgments against himself, but do not return either dead person's independent speech. The reader is repeatedly taught to accept a consequential correction while refusing the fantasy that a completed account has finally delivered a whole private mind. [GO-U005-E002–006/E012–013; GO-U011-E004–008; GO-U015-E005; GO-U028-E005–006.]

## Owning an act is necessary and insufficient

That discipline changes the meaning of Clavis's guilt. A reading in which he merely wants an alibi misses the counseling scene's most explicit denial: 罪から逃れたいのではない, he does not want to escape guilt. He fears that the adjusted self is too diluted to own its killing, and that the intensely felt survival for which he accepts guilt might therefore be false. His desire for someone to say お前が殺したのだ seeks an attribution made to him by another person. Guilt here is not only a penalty after an act; it is wanted evidence that a self was present in the act. This self-report is stronger evidence than an unsupported diagnosis that all his distress is pretense. It still cannot identify the complete motive for every mission. [GO-U019-E011–012, N79–89.]

The material condition of the anxiety is genuine. The needle probe differentiates knowing pain from feeling painfulness, and Leland later corroborates that local distinction while bleeding. It neither abolishes fear nor prevents Clavis's relief, shame, grief and guilty attempt at rescue. His earlier maternal crying and later grief over Lucia equally resist a universal numbness account. Real alteration matters, but no displayed intervention proves the authorship or nonauthorship of every intention. The available alternatives are not a wholly independent soul and a wholly inert mechanism. They are conditioned people whose particular acts, capacities and relations require separate assessment. [GO-U019-E013; GO-U021-E006–008; GO-U022-E014–017; GO-U027-E007.]

Lucia's response offers him a way to accept that condition. Her argument rejects genes and memes as an absolving warrant; it places freedom partly in choosing what one must or must not do for another. Clavis explicitly distinguishes the relief from approval and from erased guilt. This is an actual development. Yet the passage also names its risk: acquiring a way to bear a choice does not establish that the choice was right. Her categorical account of choosing under antecedent conditions is a philosophical position, not an externally demonstrated free-will theorem. The nearby ambush makes bodily incapacity conspicuous. Clavis urges her to flee, but U015 ends with Lucia frozen before John; her actual later U017 departure must remain separate. Neither powerful talk of choice nor the narrator's urgency can supply an action that was not shown. [GO-U015-E006–012; GO-U017-E009.]

In the jungle, Clavis defines freedom through the ability to select a viable route among constraints, then corrects his “choices” into “not choosing.” Accepted literally as a history, the correction would contradict both early decisions and the bedside acknowledgment. Read as an altered ethical self-account, it names his refusal to own the ultimate purposes for which he had exercised discretion. The distinction does not excuse that delegation. It explains why the earlier captain can exercise agency while his later self-account discovers an unbearable deficit in its felt ownership. His new intention initially still selects John's death. Lucia's demand, rather than the ownership claim alone, changes that course. [GO-U025-E003–004; GO-U027-E004.]

Williams supplies a crucial parallel without sharing Clavis's private problem. He can ask intelligent operational questions and accept the unit's moral classification before the profile is adequate. He can challenge a proprietary CIA attitude, question the evolutionary story, care for Clavis and perform skilled rescue. The towel and water after the nightmare, practical preparation and shared food do not become fraudulent because he finally kills Lucia. His lethal act is disturbing precisely because a real attachment has survived into a conflict over whom it should protect. The final appeal to cooperate tries to carry an old practical relationship across a choice that has destroyed Clavis's reason to continue it. [GO-U002-E010–012; GO-U006-E018; GO-U011-E007–009; GO-U020-E008–010; GO-U021-E002/E008–009/E014; GO-U027-E005–006.]

His stated reason connects personal care to the conditions of knowledge. Monita and the baby need not know that their world floats above hell. Lucia has just demanded that people know. Williams's words defend both loved people and their insulation. They are a reason for his killing, not proof of its necessity, his exclusive motive or every superior's knowledge. They also preserve a difference from merely obeying: his professional independence elsewhere prevents an alleged inability to challenge orders from doing all the explanatory work. The choice concerns what he is prepared to challenge for whom. [GO-U027-E004/E006.]

## Protection has a material geography

The lake economy prevents this argument from reducing politics to the troubled consciences of a few Americans. Artificial muscle materially supports mobility, work and military action elsewhere. The account of Lake Victoria distinguishes a productive restoration from recovery of the earlier ecology. Rockwell denies that the lake returned to what it was; its viability is recovered for industrial tissue. Williams directly refuses to call the result revival. A capacity really has been restored, but the criterion of success has moved toward the interests it serves. The living tissue is not shown to possess human consciousness, and Clavis's comparison with disassembled comrades cannot establish moral equivalence. It makes the shared material basis visible while leaving the lives and histories different. [GO-U023-E008; GO-U024-E004–006.]

The provenance system is particularly revealing because it supplies useful detail before reaching its limit. Consumers can inspect ingredients and production and press for improvements. Clavis follows the porter's components until the muscle's history ends at a factory. Its administrative origin is available; the animal and surrounding lives do not enter the retrieved story. His bounded failure to find a forum cannot prove a worldwide absence of criticism. Nor can his discovery make him an exempt observer: he admits that he too learned the origin only through Lucius. The problem involves both what is recorded and how far a person chooses to follow it. The rich-home placement and institutional-purchaser wording remain a local tension, not an occasion to invent an intermediary. [GO-U017-E005; GO-U023-E008–009; GO-U024-E005.]

At the shore, Clavis connects distant freedom to narrowed local occupations. His subsequent betrayal-game theory generalizes about a population whose options he has just described as materially constrained. Mugabe's actual speech provides a corrective within the novel. He hopes for less poverty and disease, girls going to school, fish returning and a shared future improved by effort. These are civic aspirations, not merely an appetite for private consumption. John's radio writing may be implicated in harm; neither its harmlessness nor the fulfillment of its promises is demonstrated. But possible induction cannot cancel what Mugabe expresses or make the narrator's selfishness-only picture exhaustive. A listener capable of hope remains more than a data point in either John's mechanism or Clavis's social theory. [GO-U025-E001/E006–009, especially N60.]

This countercase matters to John's final policy. His loved affluent world is not the only represented site of human attachment or collective aspiration. His 彼ら and われわれ divide people whose internal killing is to protect people like himself. The distinction is a chosen perimeter, not a discovery that one population alone contains ordinary life worth saving. The material chapters give that perimeter a history of dependence: what protects comfort abroad also makes distant labor and ecological change usable. Public ignorance is therefore neither a simple lack of data nor a universal refusal by inherently selfish consumers. It is sustained by records, economic boundaries, channels of attention and choices that the novel also shows people can question.

## The instrument works without finishing its explanation

John's confession joins those channels to an operative intervention. Academic research gains classified resources and intelligence access; a later PR phase gives him influence over speeches, broadcasts and copy. The later phase must not be silently treated as a continuing state commission. Network position, rather than formal rank alone, gives him routes into collective life. His distinction about the content of information concerns finding effective positions in a network; it does not prove that the grammar has no structure or that words never mean anything. Capacity is assembled through actual institutional affordances before any one person needs a complete view of it. [GO-U009-E013; GO-U016-E002/E006–009.]

The completed narrative gives strong reasons to accept the instrument's fictional operation. John's self-incriminating account is joined by the surviving notebook and address, a usable editor, deliberate composition, performance, distribution and the narrator's explicit attribution of disorder. Treating all this as an unrelated coincidence would require dismissing the cumulative structure without comparable positive evidence. Uncertainty about theory must not be used to demote the enacted intervention into mere rumor. Equally, Clavis's confident uptake is not an independently performed causal experiment, and the text withholds the algorithm, dosage, threshold and medium-specific parameters. Williams's individual-selection objection challenges an evolutionary story; it does not by itself falsify every linguistic effect. [GO-U016-E006–012; GO-U020-E008–010; GO-U026-E004–007; GO-U028-E004/E007–008.]

The biological explanation is also insufficient as a moral explanation of John. Clavis asserts choice and responsibility; John agrees. John includes care and sacrifice among evolved capacities and denies knowing whether the violent capacities are simply obsolete. The expected confrontation between a free moral subject and an antagonist who has explained responsibility away dissolves. John then specifies protective reasons and explicitly refuses to claim justice. He knows that choosing a beneficiary does not make his deed universally right, yet acts and claims its burden. The catastrophe consequently cannot be diagnosed only as a conceptual confusion corrected by saying “explanation is not justification.” John can accept that distinction and still decide other people's lives. [GO-U026-E003–010; GO-U027-E002–003/E009.]

Lucia's interpretation of his motive remains a serious challenge. Affair, bereavement and her intimate testimony give substance to the possibility that shame helps drive the need to prove human cruelty unavoidable. John's denials alone would not defeat it. But his repeated specification of beneficiaries, local-language compilation, policy and lack of regret gives protective intention more support than an exclusively nihilistic proof project. Mixed origins and unspoken self-exculpation remain possible. No reading needs complete access to sincerity in order to distinguish avowed protection from demonstrated benefit: the cited statistics are his account, and no independent counterfactual establishes that massacre was necessary to avert external terror. The editor's later use strengthens operation, not the efficacy or justice of his whole geopolitical bargain.

The contrast with ordinary medical and communicative usefulness is now decisive. The doctor acts responsibly by naming where expertise ends. John names the limit of a justification but makes his own capacity sufficient to act beyond it. Medicine's uncertainty and political uncertainty therefore do not automatically have the same ethical consequence. What differs is the purpose, available alternatives, exposure and authority claimed. The novel's recurring incomplete accounts become a discriminating structure only if these differences remain intact.

## An answer that changes action

Lucia's final intervention offers the strongest social alternative because it joins acknowledgment of uncertainty to an actual change of procedure. She forces disarmament, asks Bishop's real name, receives Clavis's identity and rank, then calls on that named person to arrest John and bring the account before others. 知る責任 makes knowing a responsibility. It is also a redistribution of who may judge: the operative is asked to preserve a defendant rather than settle the affair in an armed room. Clavis objects that the research and missions are secret and that nobody may believe the explanation. She allows impossibility and jury laughter. Her proposal does not depend on making all uncertainty disappear before the public can act. [GO-U027-E004, N32–47.]

This is more exact than an idealized opposition between violent men and a peaceful conscience. Lucia threatens with a gun; her earlier reassurance had exceeded her knowledge, and her account of John's complete continuing motive remains contestable. She supplies no finished safe-disclosure procedure for a dangerous instrument. Yet these limits cannot make her demand unreal. John lowers his weapon; Clavis accepts arrest in place of his own intended killing; John later intends to tell the world, accepting execution or ridicule. A materially opened route exists before Williams's shot and the pickup killing close it. The interruption's force depends on the path's reality. A narrative of inevitable control would have to erase the very decisions that make the suppression consequential. [GO-U026-E008–010; GO-U027-E004–009/E012.]

Publicity has other positive precedents. Somalia's represented improvement needs attention to make investment possible; accurate facts can fail when nobody attends. Authentication can sustain services; provenance can support criticism; Williams reports useful marital counseling; friends care under unequal knowledge. None guarantees general justice. Together they establish that the novel's constructive horizon is not an impossible demand for unmediated access to every person, nor retreat from institutions into pure intimacy. Its strongest alternative is the continued possibility that a partial account can meet another person's demand, be challenged, and alter what happens. Lucia's public procedure gives that possibility its most explicit political form. This is an interpretive conclusion from the contrary cases, not a claim that the novel supplies a complete institutional program. [GO-U006-E012–015; GO-U005-E014–016; GO-U011-E007–008; GO-U023-E008; GO-U025-E008; GO-U027-E004.]

John's response after her death tests the distinction. His willingness to tell changes his future conduct and accepts an audience he cannot guarantee will understand him. His stated atonement to Lucia is real redirection, yet he explicitly refuses regret for the original weighing of lives. It would be inaccurate either to turn this into complete repentance or to deny that anything changed. His self-attribution for involving Lucia also does not replace Williams as the actual shooter. Public answerability begins here as a possibility that exceeds the controller's private burden, while remaining unfinished and internally compromised. John's death prevents the promised appearance; the surviving resource will reach a different author. [GO-U027-E005/E008–009/E012; GO-U028-E004/E007.]

## The completed choice and the unfinished catastrophe

The epilogue initially gives disclosure an intelligible public value. Hearings expose assassination and scandal, and Clavis risks prosecution for classified testimony. Later disclosure of the editor changes the function of that same circulation. He has composed a manuscript as something like a score and performed it with a wish that Americans kill one another. His words are transcribed as well as circulated through audiovisual and hearing records. Sound and rhythm matter to his account, but cannot establish an exclusively acoustic sufficient cause. Truth-bearing testimony and intended coercive exposure occupy the same act. Access to information has occurred; Lucia's condition of informed responsibility has not thereby been fulfilled. [GO-U028-E004/E007.]

Retrospection has prepared the reader for precisely this double judgment. The biography corrected Clavis without delivering his mother's mind. A later conversation confirmed that a confession occurred without turning its preceding representation into a proved verbatim telling. Williams's final killing exposed a boundary in his loyalty without making every earlier kindness false. The final disclosure likewise reweights what telling does without proving that every earlier paragraph was calculated deception. The continuous first-person account, the performed manuscript and the hearings are related; they are not exhaustively identical transcripts. Nor does the source establish that the external Japanese novel literally contains the weapon. The ethical involvement of a reader who has trusted a voice is a formal relation, not evidence of an encoded hazard. [GO-U013-E008; GO-U014-E001; GO-U015-E001; GO-U027-E005–006; GO-U028-E002/E004/E007.]

The mother's biography intervenes in this ending without providing a single mechanical cause for it. Discourse moves from ordering her story to the notebook and public events, then returns to what the biography does to him. Clavis explicitly connects the intensified emptiness to the notebook's fit; the personifying alternative that the notebook found his emptiness is his figure. Neither the juxtaposition nor the explicit connection establishes a complete calendar of intention, a shared software mechanism, or a maternal discovery sufficient to explain every political act. More importantly, the connection puts two uses of authored accounts beside one another: he suffers a narrative that fails to confirm his imagined place in another life and also composes a narrative intended to change the conditions under which others can live. Suffering an inadequate answer does not confer a right to prescribe an audience's consequences. [GO-U028-E003–007.]

Clavis's final burden-taking inverts John's protective perimeter. America is to be plunged into disorder so that its outside can be saved. The repeated ことにした takes guilt, punishment and national destruction into the speaker's declared decisions. This is substantial ownership of agency; it is not a return to simple obedience. The strongest sympathetic reading sees a repudiation of the privilege that let distant war become background sound. It has real evidence in John's consumer-world defense and the senator's spectacle account. But it needs additional premises to become vindication: that this destruction was necessary, that those made to suffer authorized it, and that everyone outside America is actually saved. The account provides no such demonstration. Clavis's anguish may be real while those premises fail. [GO-U027-E002–003/E006; GO-U028-E004/E008–009.]

The temporal boundary makes the judgment sharper. Current deaths, riots, state forces firing on civilians, arsenal seizures and Clavis's rifle killing are narrated events. Civil war is a surviving network's projection. Mass killing at the scale Clavis would call 虐殺 has explicitly not yet occurred: まだ起こってはいなかった. This is the narrator's massacre-scale distinction, not an independently adjudicated legal classification. Future catastrophe and global benefit remain unfinished. The novel can nonetheless close its argument because the consequential decision, circulation and present harms are already there. The reader need not borrow a future outcome to judge what the self-described protector has chosen. [GO-U028-E004/E007–009, especially N29/N51–58.]

The pizza supplies the final test of that account's scale. Earlier pizza, beer and film gunfire made grief temporarily bearable in Williams's company; later comedy met friends who had witnessed different parts of the battle. The last gunfire is actual and Clavis eats alone. He has prepared a private stockpile and killed someone who came for it. The corpse is a disposal problem beside the named Lucia corpse he could not bear to abandon. His grief need not have been false for this difference to matter. Real attachment and recognition of selectivity have not produced equal regard. [GO-U005-E011–013; GO-U023-E006–007; GO-U027-E007; GO-U028-E008–009.]

The final quiet is something he imagines elsewhere. The account's protective inversion makes America's outside the dominant larger horizon, but the source does not map quiet everywhere or verify rescue. His softened feeling is an accomplished private consequence; the external peace that softens it is his thought. Thus the ending preserves, within destruction of the old protected exception, a small protected experience: enough food, a defended entrance, a sofa and a consoling account of what is happening beyond hearing. He has made his guilt explicit without surrendering the habit of selecting which consequences complete his story.

Genocidal Organ's whole movement is therefore more severe than a lesson that people should accept responsibility, and more discriminating than a revelation that they never choose. It places the possibility of answerability inside compromised bodies, services, language and institutions; there is no demonstrated outside from which perfect knowledge or pure innocence could arrive. But partial knowledge does not give every action the same warrant. The doctor can admit a limit, Lucia can make another person's demand change a plan, and a public can be asked to judge without a promised correct answer. John and finally Clavis instead make the ability to act and the willingness to bear guilt sufficient to proceed across other people's lives. The ending returns the opening's denied reply on a new scale: after so much intimate knowledge of the speaker, those exposed to his story still do not receive a comparable opportunity to answer it.
