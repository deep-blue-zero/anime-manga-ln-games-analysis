## Father

**Observation (HIGH):** The original phrase has 頭を as accusative body-part object and active transitive past 吹き飛ばした modifying 父さん. It names no expressed possessor/reflexive, weapon, scene, cause or motive. Context presents him among familiar dead beside the named childhood-cancer death.

**Preferred inference:** The active wording in this local death-list strongly invites a self-inflicted fatal head-injury reading. The current corpus should state that preferred inference, rather than leave agency as undifferentiated UNKNOWN.

**Limits:** Self-infliction does not by itself establish intent to die, a specific suicide manner/instrument, deliberation, coercion, circumstances or motive. The inference belongs to the narrator’s depiction; this unit independently authenticates no external biography.

**Strongest material counterreading:** The absent expressed possessor and agent prevent the active relative clause from being deductive proof of reflexivity; a head/body-part loss description can leave external causation unstated. The sentence lacks an explicit self-reference or narrated act. External injury therefore remains a real but less favored reading; it is not equiprobable merely because grammatically possible.

## Maternal address, scope and continuity

**Observation (HIGH):** p13 and p17 use お前 in look/inspect directives; p22’s denial/classification uses あなたが followed by わたしたちが暮らしてきた世界 and the concrete continuity phrase 地続きになっている. p21 explicitly recalls the childhood corrective gesture; p23 immediately reports assent, relief and tears.

**Preferred inference:** The expressed-subject scope change accompanies a movement from directing the narrator’s gaze to defining a wider world: あなたが is followed by わたしたちが, and 地続き figures continuity as contiguous ground rather than a wholly separate realm. A shared-life frame including the addressee is the natural local reading. This supplies exact formal support for the already evidenced maternal frame of assent; no change of actual audience is established.

**Limits:** Pronoun variation alone does not prove a psychological transformation, deliberate manipulative strategy, universal politeness hierarchy or independently isolatable causal effect on assent. わたしたち includes the speaker; inclusion of the addressee is the natural local reading rather than grammatically encoded certainty. Exact wider membership is unspecified, and a mother-plus-dead group alongside the separately named addressee remains possible.

**Strongest material counterreading:** The address forms may be ordinary local variation, and the remembered gesture, reassuring proposition and hand-leading could carry the affective effect without a distinct pronoun strategy. Japanese わたしたち does not encode inclusive versus exclusive membership, so this is expressed-subject scope expansion rather than a proven audience change. 地続き is figurative continuity, not independent proof of literal geography or afterlife ontology. These limits narrow intention/causality claims without weakening confidence that the formal shifts occur.
