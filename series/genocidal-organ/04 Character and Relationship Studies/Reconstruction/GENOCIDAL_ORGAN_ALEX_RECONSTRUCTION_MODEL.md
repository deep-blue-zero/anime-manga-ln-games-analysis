# Alex: professional attention and unequal access to a living voice

Alex supports a model of a small living repertoire: coordinated professional attention, checking an interlocutor and religious conversation that differs by recipient. It does not support predicting an unobserved suicidal decision. His death makes the distinction consequential: a remembered statement about inner hell can be genuine without revealing the contents of his private crisis.

## 1. Identity and temporal envelope

GO-C012 / アレックス is the living teammate in Part I and the earlier base conversations disclosed retrospectively in U005. **A1** covers U003–004's operation; **A2** is remembered living conversation with Clavis; **A3** is Williams's later report of Alex's living jokes with Williams/Leland. A2/A3 are disclosure labels, not newly observed post-funeral speech. Alex is reported dead at U005. Dream Alex in U011, the later recalled inner voice and Clavis's U015 explanation are representations by Clavis, excluded as independent Alex behavior.

## 2. Goals and self-model

Doing his team role, making sense of a colleague's state and maintaining serious theological conversation are supported actions. He locates an experienced hell internally and distinguishes it from an escapable battlefield. His answer about heaven admits not knowing (U004-E010). Clavis describes his faith/education, while Williams reports irreverent humor. No source authorizes a hidden goal of conversion, secret unbelief or an intention to die already operating through every earlier remark. These are alternative private stories the model refuses.

## 3. Constraints and resources

Alex is a subordinate in a four-person team with a stated role and shared constraints; his ability to communicate locally is observed, while full language and professional competence remain unmeasured. Clavis owns the senior decision. Consultation gestures are Clavis's interpretation, not verbatim commitments (U003-E003–004/E010). Alex can check Clavis and continue documentation when Clavis is disturbed (U004-E020). This model specifies communicative/role alternatives only, with no tactical, credential or killing instructions.

## 4. Attention and knowledge

He notices unfamiliar writing, asks questions, gives a local explanation in the checkpoint encounter, notices Clavis's changed state and suggests an intelligence error when told B is absent (U004-E002/E007/E020). The last is a possibility, not certified knowledge of B's departure. He has no narrator access to Clavis's internal panic or every command's purpose. The narrator admits not hearing jokes Williams/Leland knew, making recipient access a model input rather than a license to choose one supposedly authentic Alex.

## 6. Affect and regulation

The sad smile in the hell conversation and calm performance at the target-death record coexist (U004-E010/E020). They are source-described states, not proof of constant serenity or clinically established depression. The narration's later occupational stress/sin explanations include hypothetical counselor/priest scenes, not recovered consultations. No causal effect of emotional adjustment on his death is established. The model can preserve his avowed internal burden without specifying its content or converting it into an inevitable future act.

## 7. Directional relationships

With Clavis, Alex's speech often uses respectful questions and a shared but nonconverting theological debate. With Leland, his peer answer to the heaven question is more familiar. Williams and Leland reportedly receive a humor repertoire Clavis does not (U005-E012). This permits recipient sensitivity; it does not prove a deliberately maintained mask, deception or who knew Alex best. R006–009/017–018 remain distinct directions in the actor ledger.

## 8. Written Japanese voice

U004-E002/E020 uses respectful `です`/`ます` questions to Clavis; U005-E013's remembered address is `シェパード大尉`. U004-E010 shifts to peer `わからないよ` with Leland while preserving first-person uncertainty. The inner-hell wording uses the head/brain image, but repeating it cannot authenticate dream speech. Williams's reported irreverent jokes are a register contrast whose exact dialogue is unavailable. No novel joke, posthumous answer, acoustic timbre or canonical continuation is generated.

## 9. Development, contradictions and repair triggers

The source changes the survivors' knowledge of Alex more than it supplies an observed full development arc for Alex. Earlier base hell talk predates the Part I operation, though disclosed later. A model that says the battlefield first created that thought fails chronology. A model of uniform piety fails the humor countercase; one of hidden unbelief fails the respectful serious faith evidence. Repairs must preserve these coexisting forms. Additional mentions or a more confident narrator explanation cannot fill the missing crisis decision.

## 10. Uncertainty, negative constraints and use limits

**Model abstention is mandatory for suicide motive, preparation, crisis choice, hypothetical rescue outcome, private confession/counseling, complete home life and all posthumous dialogue.** The accepted report of self-killing and missing note establish death circumstances, not an authenticated intention. Clavis's later certainty in U015 is his changed interpretation. Literary treatment of that interpretation is appropriate; Alex simulation from it is not. The recognition of this gap does not negate A-R1–4's smaller living repertoire.
