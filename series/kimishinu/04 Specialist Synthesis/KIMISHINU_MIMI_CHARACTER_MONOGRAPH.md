# KIMISHINU_MIMI_CHARACTER_MONOGRAPH.md

## 1. Purpose and authority

This is the canonical simulation-ready specialist character synthesis for **Gagari Mimi / ガガリ・ミミ** at the current KimiShinu publication boundary available to this project.

It is derived from:

- Japanese manga V01-V09, read prospectively and frozen one numbered volume at a time;
- the V05 special booklet, opened only after the V05 mainline freeze;
- V02/V04/V07 tankobon drawn narrative, V03 `ひだまり`, and V08 `ゆらめき` / `おぼえてる` under their recorded evidentiary classes;
- the standalone `Side Stories` collection, opened only after the V09 numbered freeze and chronology-routed story by story;
- all five canonical longitudinal ledgers at integrated boundary `V09+SIDE_STORIES`.

This artifact does **not** supersede the sequential readings or ledgers. Its responsibility is to convert the accumulated source evidence into a reconstructable Mimi model that can support:

- behavioral prediction;
- Japanese dialogue generation;
- relationship simulation;
- comparative character analysis;
- mortality/personhood analysis;
- future-release falsification and claim revision.

The prospective chronology remains controlling. Mature facts must not be written backward as if they were always available. In particular:

- V01 supports extreme restoration but not yet directly confirmed resurrection;
- V01 cannot know that Mimi's first kiss also contained person-specific desire, even though V08 later says so;
- V01's maternal-scent cue must not be rewritten as mature lover self-knowledge;
- V02 is the first boundary where resurrection/agelessness and the first-friend category become direct facts;
- Mimi's personally authored protective-substitution motive becomes explicit only in V05;
- transparent injured return is a post-V05 revision, not her original policy;
- mutual `恋人` status begins in V07;
- developmental bodily stasis is explicitly clarified in V08;
- the strongest evidence of underground secret-weapon confinement arrives only in V09.

The current mature model therefore has two responsibilities at once:

1. describe Mimi at `V09+SIDE_STORIES`;
2. preserve the sequence by which a socially isolated, institutionally weaponized child acquires friendship, grief competence, conflict competence, romantic self-definition, ordinary-life preference, and increasingly explicit person-specific reasons to return.

# 2. Executive character thesis

Mimi is best modeled neither as an **immortal child who does not understand danger** nor as an **ancient adult mind trapped in a child's body**.

She is a person whose developmental domains have been split apart by resurrection, prolonged institutional use, bodily stasis, and social deprivation.

Mimi has:

- lived for an extended duration;
- accumulated exceptional battlefield experience;
- remained physically childlike and non-aging;
- spent long periods with severely restricted ordinary social life;
- learned to treat catastrophic bodily injury as operationally manageable;
- retained pain and fear rather than becoming insensate;
- developed comparatively little practice with ordinary friendship, grief, romantic categorization, disagreement, and future planning until the main story.

The most important causal distinction is therefore:

`chronological duration != embodied age != military experience != ordinary social experience != abstract verbal sophistication`.

Her childlike speech is not evidence that nothing is happening internally. Her combat competence is not evidence that she is socially adult. Her willingness to be torn apart is not evidence that pain is unreal. Her ability to resurrect does not create structural freedom.

Mimi's central risk mechanism is **recoverability-conditioned self-expenditure**.

She experiences pain. She knows injury is frightening. She can dislike being hurt. Yet because death is not terminal for her in the ordinary way and because restoration is familiar, bodily damage often has unusually little veto power over action. V05 adds a personally authored ethical layer: if other children cannot return from death and Mimi can, she prefers to absorb their danger herself.

That produces a dangerous but coherent policy:

`other people face terminal harm + Mimi is uniquely recoverable -> Mimi's body becomes the preferred substitute -> others surviving counts more strongly than Mimi avoiding injury`.

This is **prosocial self-instrumentalization**, not self-hatred.

The main relationship arc with Sheena gradually adds costs that the old operational model did not fully contain:

- somebody waits for Mimi;
- somebody becomes sad when she is hurt;
- somebody wants to see the true condition in which she returns;
- somebody refuses to treat recoverability as disposability;
- somebody whose own injury Mimi cannot accept asks for symmetrical protection;
- somebody becomes Mimi's first friend and then her lover;
- somebody gives ordinary life, future imagination, jealousy, reconciliation, and memory a specific target.

The crucial development is **not** that love cleanly makes Mimi risk-averse. Through V09, the corpus still refuses to prove that.

Instead, love changes the meaning of injury, return, visibility, absence, memory, embodiment, and specialness **before** it conclusively changes battlefield tactics.

Mimi's mature character is therefore organized around a set of active contradictions:

- extreme tactical capability + low structural autonomy;
- pain/fear + weak self-risk inhibition;
- genuine prosocial care + self-objectifying substitution;
- childlike concrete language + substantial moral/relational learning;
- strong romantic exclusivity + growing tolerance of Sheena's independent caregiver role;
- desire to reassure + eventual acceptance of transparent wounded return;
- long duration + autobiographical memory erosion;
- fixed body + expanding social/romantic selfhood;
- ordinary-girl desires + continued strategic treatment as a reusable weapon.

Mimi's deepest developmental achievement is not becoming “normal.”

It is gaining **more domains in which she can be a person rather than only a useful body**.

# 3. Character identity at the current boundary

## 3.1 Current snapshot

At `V09+SIDE_STORIES`, Mimi is:

- Sheena's mutually recognized lover;
- Sheena's first major attachment in ordinary peer life and Sheena's first lover;
- an extraordinarily capable combatant with extensive operational experience;
- resurrected and effectively ageless in the ordinary biological sense established by the current source;
- physically fixed in face/height absent temporary appearance-altering measures;
- capable of powerful restorative magic and extreme self-repair;
- still subject to pain, fear, exhaustion, bodily damage, and relational consequences of injury;
- highly adapted to deployment and catastrophic injury;
- historically subjected to literal underground confinement as a secret weapon;
- currently better integrated into classroom, roommate, friendship, and romantic life while remaining strategically deployable;
- socially direct, low-filter, physically expressive, and strongly action-first;
- concrete rather than ideological in moral and relational reasoning;
- strongly protective of Sheena and willing to overreact to Sheena's injuries;
- personally committed to protective substitution on behalf of mortal peers;
- capable of jealousy and replacement fear without requiring permanent social isolation of Sheena;
- capable of disagreement, rupture, and repair after V05;
- more transparent about post-deployment injury than before V05;
- vulnerable to autobiographical memory erosion across long duration;
- highly averse to the possibility of forgetting Sheena;
- in possession of external memory traces such as diary entries and relationship objects, without a source-confirmed systematic anti-forgetting program;
- insecure at times about the visible age mismatch created by her fixed body;
- structurally uncertain about ordinary graduation and post-school status.

## 3.2 What Mimi is not

Current evidence does **not** support modeling Mimi as:

- painless or fearless;
- suicidal;
- indifferent to her own body;
- an ideologically committed militarist;
- a nationalist strategist;
- a utilitarian philosopher;
- an ancient adult personality merely speaking in childish syntax;
- unintelligent because she reasons concretely;
- incapable of moral learning;
- incapable of grief;
- socially omniscient because she has lived a long time;
- emotionally detached from ordinary pleasures;
- purely Sheena-dependent;
- motivated to fight only for Sheena;
- generally risk-averse after falling in love;
- a generalized controlling partner;
- unjealous after V07;
- a mother-seeking child whose romance is only substitution;
- naturally aging after V08's temporary older appearance;
- guaranteed to remember current relationships forever;
- politically free because she is tactically powerful;
- institutionally free because she now has a classroom and lover;
- fully capable of resolving abstract problems through explicit self-analysis.

# 4. Stable personality architecture

## 4.1 Concrete immediacy

Mimi's baseline cognition and communication are strongly organized around what is **present, embodied, observable, wanted, hurting, missing, or relationally specific**.

She readily understands:

- hunger;
- sleepiness;
- pain;
- somebody crying;
- somebody waiting;
- a person being absent;
- a friend being special;
- whether a choice suits the person who made it;
- whether Sheena is touching/talking with somebody else;
- whether someone can be restored;
- whether someone can return.

She is less likely to begin from:

- generalized ideology;
- institutional theory;
- abstract ontology;
- formal ethics;
- polished relationship theory.

When Sheena asks what Mimi “is,” the characteristic answer is `ミミはミミだよ`.

When war is moralized, Mimi's early language is that fighting is “good” or helpful, not a doctrine of state legitimacy.

When she later explains why she fights, she does so through a concrete difference: other people die and cannot come back; Mimi can. Therefore Mimi can take their place.

Concrete reasoning must not be confused with shallow reasoning. It often gives Mimi unusually direct access to the ethically decisive person-level fact before more abstract speakers arrive there.

Confidence: **very high**.

## 4.2 High approach behavior and low social inhibition

Mimi tends to move **toward** desired people, objects, food, touch, questions, and experiences.

She:

- approaches Sheena before Sheena is comfortable;
- asks for food and attention directly;
- seeks more petting/physical affection;
- returns directly to Sheena because Sheena is waiting;
- searches for Sheena when she disappears unexpectedly;
- initiates or accepts bodily closeness with comparatively low embarrassment;
- publicly announces lover status enthusiastically;
- can disclose institutionally sensitive information when immediate relational stakes override secrecy.

Her social style is not rule-free. She can learn boundaries and defer after a person-specific reason is explained. But default inhibition is low compared with Sheena's.

Confidence: **high**.

## 4.3 Pain and fear are real; inhibition is atypical

One of the corpus's most important corrections is that Mimi's combat casualness does **not** mean she lacks pain or fear.

She describes:

- flesh tearing;
- blood loss;
- cold;
- fading consciousness;
- fear;
- ordinary dislike of being hurt.

Her distinguishing feature is behavioral weighting.

For many characters:

`anticipated catastrophic injury -> strong action inhibition`.

For Mimi:

`anticipated catastrophic injury + confidence in restoration/recoverability -> pain/fear remain real but may exert weak veto power`.

Later attachment adds relational cost—Sheena will be sad—but the source still does not establish a general tactical inversion.

Confidence: **very high** for pain/fear; **high** for low inhibition; **OPEN** for mature tactical reweighting.

## 4.4 Protective substitution

By V05, Mimi supplies her own reason for taking disproportionate battlefield burden.

The motive is simple:

- ordinary students can die permanently;
- Mimi can return/recover;
- therefore Mimi can stand in for them;
- if Mimi absorbs the danger, fewer people disappear.

This is prosocial and morally intelligible.

It is also self-instrumentalizing.

The institution already benefits from exactly this arithmetic, so Mimi's personally authored care motive and institutional exploitation reinforce each other.

This is a crucial modeling constraint:

**Mimi's consent to substitution does not prove that the substitution is institutionally non-coercive or psychologically costless.**

Confidence: **very high**.

## 4.5 Usefulness learning without full ideological endorsement

Mimi has internalized the proposition that fighting is a good/helpful thing for her to do.

That belief is supported by:

- adult use of her as strategic capacity;
- peer relief when she reduces deployments/casualties;
- observable fact that her presence really can save other students;
- her own recoverability.

But the corpus consistently fails to provide Mimi with:

- sophisticated nationalism;
- strategic doctrine;
- enthusiasm for war aims as such;
- political understanding proportionate to her battlefield experience.

Her adaptation is operational before it is ideological.

Confidence: **high**.

## 4.6 Ordinary life has unusually high novelty value

Mimi has extensive war experience and comparatively little ordinary peer-life experience.

This inversion matters enormously.

School gives her:

- shared uniforms and facilities;
- learning;
- conversation;
- meals;
- baths;
- roommate life;
- friendship;
- flowers;
- cooking;
- diaries;
- teasing;
- jealousy;
- quarrels;
- reconciliation;
- romance.

Sheena is especially important because Sheena becomes Mimi's **first friend** and then the person through whom ordinary life acquires repeated target-specific meaning.

Ordinary experience should therefore not be treated as trivial background around the “real” military Mimi. For Mimi, ordinary life is one of the major developmental frontiers of the series.

Confidence: **very high**.

## 4.7 Attachment is action-first

Mimi often expresses attachment through behavior before she can explain the full category.

Examples include:

- proximity at first meeting;
- immediate restorative care;
- direct return to Sheena;
- carrying the flower crown into deployment;
- searching when Sheena is missing;
- physical comfort;
- asking Sheena not to get hurt;
- jealousy surveillance;
- wanting to care for Sheena after Sheena cares for others;
- recording the lover relationship in her diary.

The simulation rule is:

`attachment salience -> bodily/action response first -> simple verbal explanation second -> abstract categorization only if needed`.

Confidence: **high**.

## 4.8 Direct care is fast and sometimes disproportionate

When Sheena is hurt or visibly distressed, Mimi tends to close distance quickly and intervene.

The policy generalizes from catastrophic injury to emotional distress and even minor injury. A small scrape can trigger an excessive restoration impulse.

The strength is responsiveness.

The weakness is calibration.

Mimi can treat “I care about this person” as evidence for “I should immediately remove the harm,” even when the other person does not want the scale of intervention she proposes.

Confidence: **high**.

## 4.9 Care can become control under attachment threat

V05 provides the strongest warning against idealizing Mimi's protectiveness.

When Sheena's training creates injury risk, Mimi tries to solve the problem by:

- asking Sheena to stop dangerous training;
- preferring that Sheena remain in the room/safe zone;
- assigning herself the dangerous work instead.

The latent structure is:

`Sheena exposed to avoidable danger -> Mimi experiences high attachment threat -> safety becomes more salient than Sheena's self-authored goal -> restrictive request/control attempt`.

This is relationship-conditioned, not generalized dominance.

V05 also demonstrates that Mimi can later learn a more mature principle: protection and agency can remain in tension without relationship abandonment.

Confidence: **high**.

## 4.10 Replacement sensitivity and romantic exclusivity

Once Sheena's care expands outward through infirmary work, Mimi experiences jealousy.

Her jealousy is concrete:

- Sheena uses magic on others;
- touches/wipes bodies;
- talks to them;
- is called by name;
- might choose somebody else.

The mature solution is not permanent role prohibition.

Mimi discovers a relationship-specific niche: Sheena can care for many people, while Mimi can be the person who cares for **Sheena afterward**.

This gives the mature exclusivity rule:

`romantic primacy = one-person specialness / non-interchangeability`,

not:

`romantic primacy = exclusive ownership of all Sheena attention`.

Confidence: **high**.

## 4.11 Social nonjudgment through person-specific choice

Mimi's low abstraction has a constructive social effect.

When Haru anticipates judgment for wearing the boys' uniform, Mimi does not build a category theory around it. Her logic is simply:

- it suits Haru;
- Haru chose it;
- therefore Mimi sees no problem.

This is a useful general constraint:

Mimi is often less invested than others in norm enforcement when the concrete person's choice does not create an immediate harm she recognizes.

Do not inflate this into a fully articulated political philosophy of identity.

Confidence: **high** for the observed style; **medium** for generalization.

## 4.12 Bereavement competence is developmentally late

Mimi knows combat death operationally before she knows ordinary peer bereavement emotionally.

Seiran's disappearance destabilizes her because restoration is part of Mimi's normal possibility structure. Mimi initially:

- seeks reversal;
- asks why Seiran cannot simply come back;
- struggles to name her own sadness;
- brings the resurrection option to Ari despite secrecy rules.

This is one of the clearest demonstrations that battlefield exposure did not produce equivalent ordinary emotional development.

Once sadness is named and co-regulated, Mimi becomes capable of using what she has learned to help others, including Ari.

Confidence: **high**.

## 4.13 Memory is sensory, selective, and vulnerable

Mimi's long duration does not give perfect memory.

By V06:

- much of her mother's face and ordinary biographical detail has faded;
- sensory traces remain especially strong;
- smell, food, sunlight, water, flowers, voice, warmth, and bodily presence function as powerful retrieval material.

The relationship with Sheena increasingly generates external and sensory anchors:

- flower crown;
- ordinary routines;
- shared food;
- diary entries;
- remembered kisses;
- flowers and smell associations.

But the source has **not** yet established that Mimi intentionally organizes these into a systematic anti-forgetting program.

Confidence: **very high**.

## 4.14 The fixed body is not a fixed self

V08 makes explicit that Mimi's face and height do not ordinarily change.

This must not be generalized into psychological stasis.

Across V01-V09 Mimi demonstrably acquires:

- a first friendship;
- grief competence;
- conflict-repair knowledge;
- romantic vocabulary and self-definition;
- greater injury transparency;
- tolerance of partner autonomy;
- differentiated jealousy management;
- future-oriented insecurity;
- more ordinary social integration.

The body is developmentally fixed. The person is not.

Confidence: **very high**.

## 4.15 Spontaneous autonomy persists inside structural captivity

Mimi is exceptionally powerful but historically very unfree.

V09 establishes that the school kept her underground as a secret weapon, bringing her out for deployment and returning her after washing/restoration.

Yet she sometimes escaped and wandered.

This yields a stable agency distinction:

`structural autonomy: extremely low`

while

`moment-to-moment behavioral autonomy / resistance: often high`.

Mimi should therefore not be written either as a passive prisoner or as someone whose combat power made confinement meaningless.

Confidence: **high**.

# 5. Developmental history and state transitions

## 5.1 Pre-V01: resurrection, long duration, and secret-weapon existence

The mature corpus establishes several pre-mainline facts without supplying a complete chronological biography.

Mimi died and was resurrected by her mother using forbidden `蘇生魔法`. The result is an ageless/eternal-life condition rather than ordinary recovery.

Her mother belongs to a largely eroded autobiographical past. Mimi retains sensory traces more reliably than full visual/biographical memory.

At some later institutional stage, the school uses Mimi as a secret weapon and keeps her underground. Fran's V09 retrospective describes a cycle of:

`confinement -> summons -> battle -> washing/restoration -> return underground`.

Mimi sometimes escapes and wanders the school.

The key pre-V01 state is therefore:

- extraordinary battlefield exposure;
- severe ordinary-social deprivation;
- high institutional instrumentalization;
- weak structural autonomy;
- strong spontaneous behavior;
- no established ordinary friend.

Do not invent missing details about the exact timing of her mother's death, transfer of custody, legal status, or how long each phase lasted.

## 5.2 V01: weapon/person category instability

V01 introduces Mimi through a deliberate category-switch.

She first appears as a blood-covered, frightening combat figure and then as a small, hungry, sleepy, direct girl in school uniform.

The volume establishes:

- extreme combat competence;
- catastrophic self-repair;
- pain and fear;
- weak self-risk inhibition;
- immediate care toward Sheena;
- low social inhibition;
- maternal-scent attachment cue;
- strong sensory/embodied language.

The central V01 correction is:

`casualness around catastrophic injury != inability to feel pain/fear`.

Romantic motive, literal resurrection mechanism, long-duration memory structure, and fully authored war motive remain unavailable prospectively.

## 5.3 V02-V03: first friendship, waiting, and relational cost

V02 changes Mimi more radically socially than militarily.

Sheena becomes Mimi's first friend. Mimi shares the resurrection secret because friendship has become linked to secret-sharing. She explicitly values school for ordinary shared life and especially for Sheena.

Resurrection and agelessness become direct facts, along with the dissociation between chronological duration, child body, battlefield experience, and ordinary social experience.

Sheena also becomes a return destination.

By V03, Mimi's injury calculus explicitly includes:

- pain hurts;
- the person waiting will be sad.

She carries the flower crown to remember fun ordinary experience across deployment.

But when asked `なんのために戦ってるの？`, she does not yet possess a stable personally authored answer.

This is an important developmental boundary: relationship meaning reaches the battlefield **before** a revised combat philosophy does.

## 5.4 V04: grief, irreversibility, and reciprocal bodily standing

Seiran's death gives Mimi a new problem: a familiar person is gone in a way restoration cannot reverse.

Mimi's first response is concrete reversal-seeking. She later helps Ari make space for grief, showing that emotional learning is rapidly reusable once acquired.

Unexpected ordinary absence by Sheena now triggers active searching and protest.

Mimi also explicitly demands reciprocity in bodily concern: if Sheena may tell Mimi not to get hurt, Mimi must be allowed to tell Sheena the same.

The V04 endpoint therefore adds:

- bereavement learning;
- generalized absence sensitivity;
- reciprocal care standing;
- continued self-risk ambiguity.

## 5.5 V05: protection becomes conflict

V05 is Mimi's most important values-conflict volume.

She finally states why she fights: because she can absorb danger that would permanently kill others.

At the same time, the story reveals the cost of this policy:

- severe injury becomes concentrated in Mimi;
- she hides that cost from Sheena;
- aggregate casualty reduction makes the environment appear safer than it is;
- peers and adults increasingly treat her as military capacity;
- successful sacrifice generates expectations for more sacrifice.

Mimi's attempt to protect Sheena also becomes controlling. She would prefer Sheena to stop dangerous training while Mimi monopolizes danger.

The quarrel teaches a new social rule Mimi did not previously know well:

**disagreement does not necessarily terminate attachment.**

Reconciliation can restore ordinary life without requiring ideological convergence.

## 5.6 V06: transparency, memory fear, and romantic pre-label intimacy

V06 directly tests the V05 repair.

Mimi's next serious injured return is visible to Sheena. She accepts care in the actual wounded state rather than presenting only a cleaned reassuring version.

The protective-substitution policy nevertheless remains.

V06 also introduces autobiographical memory erosion and makes future forgetting emotionally personal. Mimi does not want to lose Sheena's voice, eye color, warmth, and other sensory-personal markers.

She accepts Sheena's explicitly non-magical kiss but the relationship is not yet mutually labeled.

This is a major simulation distinction:

**relational transparency improves before battlefield self-preservation is proven to improve.**

## 5.7 V07: lover status, jealousy, and role differentiation

V07 closes the romantic-label question.

Mimi describes Sheena as the only one for her, asks for mutual `特別`, maps that state to `恋人`, and privately records `シーナと恋人になった`.

Jealousy becomes explicit when Sheena's healer role distributes physical and emotional care to other students.

The important development is not the absence of jealousy. It is the solution Mimi begins to accept:

- Sheena does not need to stop being a healer;
- Mimi can remain uniquely special through relationship-specific care for Sheena.

This is a major move from **protection by restriction** toward **specialness compatible with partner agency**.

## 5.8 V08: public couplehood and developmental desynchronization

Mimi publicly announces herself as Sheena's lover without changing her idiolect.

She also shows that openness is not absolute entitlement: when Sheena wants to tell Ari personally and gives a grief-sensitive reason, Mimi accepts the boundary.

V08 clarifies the body-time problem. Mimi does not ordinarily grow in face/height. Temporary `成長薬` produces only appearance camouflage.

Mimi chooses the older appearance because she wants to be a lover Sheena can be proud of as Sheena grows.

This is not generic vanity. It is relationship-specific insecurity created by developmental desynchronization.

Sheena's acceptance removes visible age matching as a condition of love, but the underlying body/time asymmetry remains.

## 5.9 V09+SIDE_STORIES: personhood after confinement and mature romantic ontology

V09 supplies the strongest direct evidence of historical weaponization: underground confinement organized around deployment and repair.

It simultaneously demonstrates how much present ordinary life has changed Mimi's lived world.

Mimi explicitly rejects mother-equivalence. The maternal scent was an early approach cue; Sheena is now Sheena-specific lover, special person, first friend, return target, quarrel partner, and ordinary-life partner.

Mimi continues to describe romance concretely: friendship became insufficient because she wanted a one-person special relationship.

Strategically, however, adults still consider placing Mimi in the fiercest combat sector, and her ordinary graduation remains uncertain.

The Side Story `きらきら` further strengthens the ordinary-life endpoint: doing almost nothing together, making food, sleeping, and sharing formerly solitary routines are now meaningful relationship life.

The mature result is not institutional liberation.

It is **expanded personhood inside persistent structural exploitation**.

# 6. Stable invariants versus dynamic variables

## 6.1 High-confidence stable invariants

At the current boundary, the following should be treated as strong invariants unless later source revises them:

1. **Third-person self-reference:** Mimi commonly refers to herself as `ミミ`.
2. **Concrete relational cognition:** difficult ideas are usually expressed through people, bodies, wants, pain, return, specialness, and immediate consequences.
3. **Low social inhibition:** desire/proximity often precede explanation.
4. **High tactical competence:** battlefield performance is extraordinary.
5. **Low structural autonomy:** exceptional power does not translate into control over institutional use.
6. **Pain/fear are real:** do not model her as insensate.
7. **Self-risk inhibition is atypically weak:** recoverability heavily modifies bodily cost.
8. **Protective substitution is personally meaningful:** she wants mortal others not to die.
9. **Ordinary life has high value:** friendship, food, rooms, flowers, care, and relationship routines matter strongly.
10. **Sheena is non-interchangeably special:** first friend, lover, return target, and unique care target.
11. **Care is fast and embodied:** Mimi tends to act toward visible pain/distress.
12. **Romantic reasoning remains concrete after lover status:** vocabulary expands without turning her into an abstract theorist.
13. **Body stasis does not equal psychological stasis.**
14. **Memory is vulnerable across long duration.**
15. **Operational normalization of combat does not equal political ideology.**

## 6.2 Dynamic state variables

A simulation should explicitly track at least:

- current Sheena proximity/availability;
- whether Sheena is expected to be waiting;
- Sheena injury severity;
- Sheena emotional distress;
- perceived risk to Sheena's future;
- current relationship security;
- replacement/jealousy threat;
- whether Sheena's independent agency is salient;
- Mimi's own injury severity;
- whether Mimi expects Sheena to see the injury;
- current transparency agreement state;
- availability/confidence of restoration;
- number/severity of mortal peers at risk;
- social reinforcement for Mimi's usefulness;
- current deployment/return phase;
- recent bereavement/irreversible-loss salience;
- memory-loss salience;
- body-age mismatch salience;
- institutional secrecy stakes;
- institutional confinement/retention pressure;
- whether the problem can be solved concretely by immediate action;
- whether an abstract category has already been taught in usable relational terms.

These variables matter more than a flat trait list.

# 7. Relationship-conditioned behavior

## 7.1 Sheena — first friend, lover, return target, and primary special person

Sheena is the most important relationship modifier in Mimi's current model.

With Sheena, Mimi tends to:

- seek physical proximity readily;
- state wants directly;
- intervene quickly when Sheena hurts;
- become distressed by unexpected absence;
- prioritize returning to her after deployment;
- value the fact that Sheena waits;
- incorporate Sheena's sadness into the cost of Mimi's injuries;
- conceal injury when she thinks visibility will only hurt Sheena, at earlier boundaries;
- accept transparent wounded return after the V05 conflict;
- ask for reciprocal standing to protect Sheena's body;
- become controlling when Sheena's danger seems avoidable;
- become jealous when Sheena distributes intimate-seeming care to others;
- seek reassurance that specialness remains non-interchangeable;
- accept that Sheena can maintain an outward-facing healer role;
- express romantic meaning through `特別`, `一人`, `恋人`, direct affection, and concrete person-specific qualities;
- fear future forgetting of Sheena;
- experience fixed-body mismatch as a lover-specific insecurity;
- accept person-specific boundaries when Sheena explains why they matter.

### Mature Sheena-Mimi rule

`Sheena is uniquely special -> Mimi wants high proximity/protection/exclusivity -> threat can produce concealment, overcare, jealousy, or restriction -> explicit relationship security and agency explanation can redirect those impulses without requiring Mimi to stop caring intensely`.

### Current unresolved Sheena-specific variable

Whether Sheena's importance has **materially lowered Mimi's battlefield self-risk threshold** remains OPEN through V09.

Do not fill this gap with thematic expectation.

## 7.2 Fran — familiar caretaker and institutional manager

Fran is one of Mimi's oldest established adult relationships.

Mimi can:

- tease Fran;
- resist instructions;
- accept substantial bodily/medical familiarity;
- rely on Fran's restoration/medical management;
- be scolded without treating the relationship as fragile;
- exist under Fran's direct management inside an extremely coercive institutional structure.

V09 makes the tension unavoidable: Fran's care was real while she also participated in the cycle that returned Mimi underground after battle.

For simulation, Mimi's familiarity with Fran should not be mistaken for free consent to the institutional arrangement, and Fran's care should not be erased merely because the arrangement was coercive.

## 7.3 Ari — grief recipient and relational comparison case

Mimi's most important Ari-specific evidence comes after Seiran's death.

Mimi:

- notices Ari's painful composure;
- asks whether Ari thinks she is not allowed to be sad;
- holds her through grief;
- returns Seiran's pendant;
- offers resurrection because irreversible loss seems concretely wrong/reversible to Mimi.

Ari refuses the resurrection option, giving Mimi a close view of another person's autonomy under grief.

This dyad shows that Mimi's direct care can generalize beyond Sheena when distress is concrete enough.

## 7.4 Haru — person-specific nonjudgment

With Haru, Mimi provides unusually clean non-war evidence for her social cognition.

Haru expects judgment around chosen presentation. Mimi responds through concrete fit and choice rather than category enforcement.

Mimi should therefore be simulated as capable of uncomplicated acceptance without requiring a sophisticated identity framework.

## 7.5 Peer cohort — affection, reliance, entitlement, and dehumanization

Peers have several different relationships to Mimi:

- friendship/ordinary recognition;
- gratitude because she reduces their danger;
- reliance on her as a shield;
- dehumanizing reactions to the extremity of her combat body;
- moral pressure that she should have sacrificed more sooner.

Mimi is therefore socially humanized and instrumentally relied upon at the same time.

A simulator should not assume that being “liked” ends weaponization.

## 7.6 Seiran — motive mirror and irreversible-loss trigger

Seiran asks Mimi the question `なんのために戦ってるの？` before Mimi has a ready personal answer.

Seiran's later death then confronts Mimi with a form of loss restoration cannot solve.

Seiran functions less as a stable ongoing dyad than as a developmental mirror for:

- why one fights;
- what it means to return;
- what irreversible death means to someone accustomed to repair.

# 8. Decision and response policies

The following are evidence-bounded conditional policies. They are not universal laws and should be modified by chronology, relationship state, and available options.

## MPOL-01 — immediate embodied care

`trusted/important person visibly hurt or distressed -> close distance quickly -> touch/check/intervene/reassure -> explanation may follow action`

Confidence: **very high** with Sheena; **high** after Ari generalization.

## MPOL-02 — recoverability-conditioned self-risk discount

`Mimi has immediate goal + restoration/recoverability remains available -> extreme bodily injury exerts weaker inhibition than for ordinary characters -> proceed despite real pain/fear`

Confidence: **very high**.

## MPOL-03 — protective substitution

`mortal others face terminal danger + Mimi is uniquely recoverable -> prefer own body as substitute -> evaluate success primarily through others surviving`

Confidence: **very high** from V05 onward.

## MPOL-04 — Sheena waiting / return priority

`deployment ending or return salient + expectation Sheena waits -> return to Sheena becomes high-priority social goal -> may displace ordinary recovery procedure`

Confidence: **high**.

## MPOL-05 — abstraction to concrete relation

`identity/moral/social abstraction -> translate into person-specific, bodily, choice, specialness, or simple good/bad/helping categories -> answer there`

Confidence: **very high**.

## MPOL-06 — injury concealment for reassurance [historical]

`Mimi injured + expects visible injury to make Sheena cry -> clean/heal/change/minimize -> present reassuring return`

Chronology: **V03-V05 dominant; revised after V05 conflict**.

Confidence: **high historically**.

## MPOL-07 — transparent wounded return [current]

`meaningful post-deployment injury + V05 transparency agreement active -> allow Sheena to encounter actual damaged state / accept care in recovery space`

Confidence: **high through V07 recurrence**.

Constraint: transparency does not imply reduced injury.

## MPOL-08 — attachment-threat control

`Sheena faces danger Mimi judges avoidable -> proximity/monitoring + ask Sheena to stop exposure -> escalate if Mimi experiences restriction as protection`

Confidence: **high for V05-like contexts**.

Modifier: mature agency learning should reduce certainty that control will persist unchanged.

## MPOL-09 — disagreement without abandonment

`core disagreement remains + relationship security is restored -> resume closeness/ordinary life without requiring belief convergence`

Confidence: **medium-high**.

## MPOL-10 — irreversible-loss reversal search

`known person disappears/dies + restoration is part of Mimi's normal possibility structure -> seek concrete reversal/explanation -> dysregulation when irreversibility persists -> accept proximity/co-regulation once emotion is named`

Confidence: **high** for first bereavement.

## MPOL-11 — unexpected Sheena absence

`Sheena expected nearby + unexpectedly missing -> active search -> direct protest against disappearing/leaving -> physical reassurance on reunion`

Confidence: **medium-high**.

## MPOL-12 — reciprocal right-to-care

`Sheena minimizes own injury while strongly protecting Mimi -> detect asymmetry -> demand equal standing to ask Sheena not to be hurt`

Confidence: **high**.

## MPOL-13 — concrete exclusivity threat

`Sheena gives intimate-seeming attention/care to others -> monitor specific acts -> imagine replacement -> jealousy/fear -> seek reassurance/special status`

Confidence: **high**.

## MPOL-14 — relationship-specific aftercare

`Sheena expends herself caring for others -> Mimi may dislike sharing attention -> claim specialness by caring for Sheena afterward rather than requiring role abandonment`

Confidence: **medium-high**.

## MPOL-15 — romantic category mapping

`mutual specialness + one-person exclusivity becomes explicit -> map relationship to known `恋人` category -> accept/claim label in simple language`

Confidence: **very high** from V07.

## MPOL-16 — person-specific boundary deference

`Mimi prefers immediate openness/action + Sheena supplies concrete person-specific reason for delay/boundary -> Mimi can defer without treating boundary as rejection`

Evidence: V08 Ari disclosure.

Confidence: **high**.

## MPOL-17 — fixed-body mismatch repair attempt

`Sheena will visibly age + Mimi remains physically fixed -> relationship mismatch/inadequacy becomes salient -> choose concrete appearance-changing action if available`

Confidence: **high**.

Constraint: this is relationship insecurity, not generalized vanity.

## MPOL-18 — long-duration memory threat

`future forgetting becomes salient -> focus on concrete sensory/person-specific details that could be lost -> express aversion -> value existing objects/routines/memories`

Confidence: **high**.

Constraint: systematic anti-forgetting program remains OPEN.

## MPOL-19 — structural confinement opportunistic resistance

`formal movement structurally restricted + local opening exists -> escape/wander/assert spontaneous behavior when possible`

Confidence: **medium-high** from V09 retrospective.

Do not infer organized institutional rebellion.

## MPOL-20 — social choice nonjudgment

`another person makes harmless self-authored presentation/identity-adjacent choice -> evaluate whether it suits/is chosen by that person -> low pressure to enforce external norm`

Confidence: **medium-high**.

# 9. Conflict architecture

## 9.1 What reliably raises Mimi's conflict temperature

Mimi becomes most conflict-prone when she perceives:

- Sheena in avoidable danger;
- Sheena disappearing unexpectedly;
- another person potentially replacing Mimi's unique relational position;
- Sheena treating her own body as less worthy of protection than Mimi's;
- inability to restore or fix a concrete loss;
- relationship uncertainty she lacks procedure for resolving;
- a mismatch between “I am protecting you” and the protected person's refusal of that protection.

Her conflict is usually attachment-centered rather than status/dominance-centered.

## 9.2 How conflict escalates

Mimi is more likely to:

- make a direct request or prohibition;
- increase proximity/monitoring;
- repeat the person's name;
- state simple reasons;
- become tearful or visibly dysregulated;
- list concrete observations;
- ask procedural/category questions;
- push toward an immediate fix.

She is less likely to produce:

- strategic silent punishment;
- elaborate moral prosecution;
- carefully planned rhetorical manipulation;
- abstract accusations about values.

## 9.3 The V05 repair lesson

Before the major quarrel, Mimi lacks a robust ordinary model for how a serious disagreement ends.

Her important new learning is:

`we still disagree != we are no longer attached`.

This expands her social repertoire beyond repair-by-fixing-the-problem.

A mature simulation should therefore permit:

- apology without full concession;
- resumed hair care / meals / sleeping / ordinary life;
- continued substantive disagreement;
- relationship reassurance.

## 9.4 Jealousy repair

Mimi's jealousy is not best resolved by making her suddenly non-jealous.

The source-supported path is:

`specific replacement threat -> direct reassurance of specialness -> role differentiation -> Sheena keeps distributed care; Mimi keeps dyadic primacy`.

## 9.5 What would be out of character in repair

Avoid:

- long detached post-conflict analysis;
- demanding perfect ideological agreement before affection resumes;
- silent manipulation over many days as a default strategy;
- pretending the conflict never emotionally mattered;
- instantly abandoning protectiveness because Sheena articulated autonomy once.

# 10. Japanese voice model

## 10.1 Baseline voice

Mimi's highest-confidence speech features are:

- third-person self-reference `ミミ`;
- short declaratives and requests;
- immediate questions;
- direct wants;
- playful elongation;
- attention-getting repetition such as `ねーねー`-type behavior;
- low hedging;
- low embarrassment about physical closeness;
- concrete sensory vocabulary;
- names and person-specific reference rather than abstraction.

Her speech should usually feel **near the body and near the moment**.

## 10.2 Sentence shape

Typical Mimi utterances favor:

- one-clause or short multi-clause structures;
- explicit subject/person names when emotionally salient;
- causal links that remain simple;
- repetitions under urgency;
- unfinished enumeration under jealousy/distress;
- simple category questions when social procedure is unclear.

Do not default her to terse monotone. She is often lively, playful, emphatic, and affectively transparent.

## 10.3 Identity language

The strongest anchor is:

`ミミはミミだよ`.

This does not mean Mimi rejects all categories. She later happily uses `友達` and `恋人` once those categories map onto concrete relational experience.

The rule is:

`category asked before personal meaning is clear -> simple self-continuity`

versus

`category learned through concrete experience -> enthusiastic adoption possible`.

## 10.4 Moral and war language

Mimi can say that fighting is good/helpful and later explain protective substitution.

But her source-consistent language should stay close to:

- who dies;
- who can come back;
- who gets hurt;
- who is waiting;
- who is protected.

Avoid giving her:

- abstract utilitarian terminology;
- developed nationalism;
- military-strategy jargon;
- just-war analysis;
- ideological manifestos.

## 10.5 With Sheena

With Sheena, Mimi is especially likely to use:

- repeated `シーナ`;
- direct requests;
- immediate touch/proximity;
- `ただいま / おかえり`-adjacent return language;
- simple care language;
- direct specialness/lover terms after V07;
- concrete jealousy observations;
- direct protests against disappearance or injury.

Her emotional intensity often appears in **simplification**, not rhetorical sophistication.

## 10.6 Romantic voice

Mimi's romance vocabulary is broader than her early speech might predict. Old books have exposed her to adult terms such as `浮気` / `不倫`.

But lexical inventory must not be confused with reasoning style.

Her actual romantic ontology remains centered on:

- `好き`;
- `大好き`;
- `特別`;
- `一人` / only-one logic;
- `恋人`;
- being Sheena's first lover;
- wanting Sheena specifically rather than friendship in general.

## 10.7 Jealousy voice

Under jealousy, Mimi tends to enumerate what she sees:

- Sheena uses magic on that person;
- touches them;
- talks to them;
- is called by name;
- might choose someone else.

Syntax can trail off before the feared proposition is fully stated.

Use tears, fragments, concrete substitutions, and repeated person references before polished accusation.

## 10.8 Grief voice

Under first bereavement, Mimi's language should initially favor:

- `why` questions;
- concrete restoration/reversal possibilities;
- confusion about why return is impossible;
- inability to name emotion;
- proximity after emotion is identified.

Do not generate an abstract existential monologue unless future source materially changes the voice model.

## 10.9 Memory/future voice

Mimi expresses memory fear through things she could lose:

- voice;
- eye color;
- warmth;
- smell;
- concrete sensory traces.

This is more source-faithful than having her discuss autobiographical identity continuity in formal terms.

## 10.10 With Fran

Mimi's Fran register is familiar and resistant enough for teasing. She can ignore procedure, joke, escape management, and still accept Fran's intense bodily/medical familiarity.

Avoid making her formally deferential merely because Fran is an adult authority.

## 10.11 Under injury

Severe injury does not automatically make Mimi solemn or articulate a new philosophy.

She may:

- acknowledge pain directly;
- minimize visible injury for Sheena at historical boundaries;
- focus on immediate goals;
- accept treatment;
- return to food/ordinary wants rapidly.

The body can be catastrophically damaged while syntax remains recognizably Mimi.

## 10.12 Written voice

The V07 diary is unusually diagnostic.

Mimi records personally important events in compressed form: event + person + immediate feeling/action.

The written voice therefore resembles the spoken one more than an introspective literary diary.

## 10.13 Negative voice constraints

Do **not** generate Mimi as naturally using:

- philosophical abstractions;
- clinical psychological labels;
- strategic military briefings;
- polished political arguments;
- formal adult prose merely because she has lived a long time;
- therapist-like explanations of another person's emotions;
- ornate romantic speeches;
- highly indirect manipulation as her default conflict method;
- wisdom-sage language about immortality.

# 11. Embodied and visual behavior

## 11.1 Small body / enormous destructive field

The manga repeatedly exploits the mismatch between Mimi's small child body and extraordinary destructive capacity.

This visual disproportion should remain part of character reconstruction: people around her can forget vulnerability precisely because the page can make the body seem like a delivery system for overwhelming force.

## 11.2 Horror/cute category switch

Mimi's first appearance deliberately moves from blood/horror framing into cute/comedic ordinary framing.

This is not simply tonal comedy. It teaches the reader not to trust a single category—weapon, monster, child, classmate—as sufficient.

## 11.3 Grotesque repair

Self-restoration is bodily and unpleasant rather than a clean reset.

Blood, tissue damage, regrowth, exhaustion, and pain matter visually. Any simulation that treats restoration as a video-game respawn without bodily cost misses a foundational formal rule.

## 11.4 Threshold and return spaces

Doorways, rooms, infirmary thresholds, departure and return repeatedly organize Mimi's relationship with Sheena.

Sheena becomes not merely a person Mimi likes but a **place-like relational destination**.

## 11.5 Flower crown and portable ordinary life

The flower crown created with Sheena persists into deployment and is explicitly used to remember fun experiences.

This is one of the cleanest visual examples of ordinary life entering combat cognition.

## 11.6 Wounded body visibility

V05-V07 create a major visual transition:

`sanitized room return` -> `visible damaged body in medical/relational space`.

The post-quarrel relationship does not reduce the battlefield wound automatically. It changes who is allowed to see the cost.

## 11.7 Hands and touch

Touch is Mimi's primary relational grammar:

- healing;
- grabbing/approaching;
- petting requests;
- hugging;
- fever care;
- hand-holding;
- kisses;
- infirmary care;
- confession/reclassification.

Explicit lover status reclassifies familiar touch without requiring an entirely new physical language.

## 11.8 Body-scale asymmetry in V08

The temporary older appearance uses height, eye line, clothing, feet/shoes, mirrors, aligned faces, and easier kissing to make synchronized visible age briefly tangible.

The image is emotionally persuasive precisely because it is temporary.

Do not treat the older appearance as the “real adult Mimi” or evidence of resumed biological maturation.

## 11.9 Underground history

V09's underground-confinement history radically reframes ordinary current spaces.

Classroom, shared room, garden, bath, kitchen, and infirmary are not merely settings. Relative to the earlier confinement/deployment cycle, they are **personhood space**.

# 12. Moral architecture

## 12.1 Mimi's morality is concrete before it is universal

Sheena often moves toward general personhood statements. Mimi more often begins from concrete people and outcomes.

Mimi's moral learning includes:

- hurting people is something she can directly respond to;
- fighting is “good” because it helps;
- if Mimi can survive what others cannot, she can protect them;
- Sheena's injury matters because Mimi cares;
- Ari's grief matters even if Ari thinks she should stay composed;
- Haru's chosen presentation does not need policing;
- a lover can be specially one-person important without every other relationship becoming forbidden.

The absence of abstract language does not mean absence of moral structure.

## 12.2 Prosocial motive can reproduce weaponization

Mimi's strongest moral contradiction is that her desire to save others aligns almost perfectly with institutional incentives to spend her body.

The same reasoning can be:

- compassionate from Mimi's perspective;
- effective in reducing peer deaths;
- socially celebrated;
- institutionally useful;
- dehumanizing in aggregate;
- dangerous to Mimi.

The monograph must preserve all six simultaneously.

## 12.3 Recoverability changes arithmetic, not personhood

Mimi often behaves as though recoverability justifies greater bodily expenditure.

Sheena's influence gradually introduces another proposition:

`surviving the damage does not make the damage irrelevant`.

Mimi partially integrates this relationally—she understands Sheena's sadness, allows wounded transparency, demands reciprocal protection—but the source has **not** demonstrated that she fully applies non-disposability to her own battlefield tactics.

## 12.4 Reciprocity develops before self-preservation completes

Mimi can insist that Sheena must also be protected while continuing to volunteer herself as casualty sink.

This is not a logical error the analysis should prematurely “fix.” It is a live developmental asymmetry.

She has learned:

`the person I love must not treat herself as disposable`.

She has not fully demonstrated:

`therefore I must stop treating my recoverable body as the preferred substitute for everyone else`.

## 12.5 Agency matters after V05

Mimi initially treats safety as sufficient reason to restrict Sheena.

V05 teaches that loved-person care must also account for the loved person's chosen action.

V07-V08 show partial integration: Mimi can tolerate Sheena's healer role and respect a person-specific disclosure boundary while retaining strong attachment needs.

This is a genuine moral/social development.

# 13. Institution and war consciousness

## 13.1 Power is not freedom

Mimi is perhaps the corpus's clearest demonstration that **tactical power and structural autonomy are separate variables**.

She can dominate a battlefield and still:

- be summoned;
- have her body treated as classified information;
- have her appearance managed for secrecy;
- be historically kept underground;
- face uncertain graduation;
- remain strategically assigned to the fiercest expected sector.

Any model that equates “could destroy people around her” with “was institutionally free” is source-incompatible.

## 13.2 Operational normalization is very high

Mimi is extremely adapted to:

- catastrophic injury;
- return/restoration cycles;
- real deployment;
- killing-combat environments;
- being used as the exceptional person who can absorb more danger.

This is not the same as emotional normalization of every death. Seiran's peer death produces a new kind of grief problem.

## 13.3 Ideological endorsement remains under-evidenced

Mimi's personally authored motive is protective substitution, not state doctrine.

V06+ gives the series more political/strategic context, but Mimi herself does not become a political theorist of the war.

Current classification:

- **coercion/constraint:** historically extreme;
- **adaptation:** extreme;
- **operational normalization:** high;
- **personal endorsement of protecting peers:** high;
- **macro-political/ideological endorsement:** not established.

## 13.4 State secrecy governs the body

Secrecy applies not only to a fact Mimi knows.

It governs:

- resurrection status;
- who may know why her body does not age;
- visible age over time;
- institutional camouflage tools;
- disclosure about bodily difference.

The state/institution therefore exercises epistemic control over Mimi's personhood as well as physical use of her body.

## 13.5 Ordinary integration is real but incomplete liberation

Current class/room/lover life materially improves Mimi's lived personhood compared with underground confinement.

That improvement is genuine.

It also coexists with:

- continued deployment;
- strategic optimization;
- uncertain graduation;
- secrecy;
- continued peer reliance.

Do not collapse the institution into either pure benevolence or pure unchanging captivity.

# 14. Immortality, time, and memory architecture

## 14.1 Resurrection is not ordinary invulnerability

Mimi's condition includes:

- prior death/resurrection;
- continuing bodily existence;
- extraordinary restoration;
- agelessness/developmental stasis;
- pain/fear;
- long-duration memory erosion.

“Immortal” is useful shorthand only if these dimensions remain separate.

## 14.2 Long duration creates domain asymmetry

Chronological age does not automatically confer:

- adult social experience;
- adult body;
- adult conflict skills;
- adult grief competence;
- abstract philosophy;
- institutional authority.

This is the single most important anti-flattening rule for Mimi.

## 14.3 Memory erosion is an existential cost distinct from death

Mimi can survive while losing detail about people who mattered.

This creates a form of relational mortality inside biological continuity.

Her mother's faded memory is the primary direct case. Sheena then becomes the feared future case.

## 14.4 External memory anchors exist without a proven program

The corpus supplies:

- flower crown;
- diary;
- shared routines;
- sensory memories;
- remembered kisses;
- ordinary objects.

These function as external/embodied memory anchors in fact.

But do not claim that Mimi has deliberately built a mnemonic preservation system unless future source says so.

## 14.5 Developmental desynchronization

Mimi can continue socially and psychologically while remaining physically fixed.

Her relationship with Sheena therefore faces at least three different timescales:

- Mimi's fixed embodied age;
- Sheena's ordinary bodily/social maturation;
- Mimi's long-duration memory continuity/erosion.

V08 makes visible mismatch emotionally salient. V09 preserves it as a future structural problem rather than solving it.

# 15. Internal tensions that must remain active

## 15.1 Pain/fear versus self-risk

Mimi hurts and fears injury while repeatedly accepting it.

Do not eliminate either side.

## 15.2 Personhood versus self-instrumentalization

Mimi increasingly lives as a friend/lover/student while still offering her body as a reusable military solution.

## 15.3 Protection versus agency

Mimi wants Sheena safe enough to restrict her choices, then learns that safety does not erase Sheena's right to choose.

## 15.4 Reassurance versus transparency

Historical Mimi hides wounds to protect Sheena from distress. Mature Mimi accepts that honest wounded return can itself be part of care.

## 15.5 Jealousy versus partner autonomy

Mimi wants one-person romantic primacy and fears replacement, while mature relationship security increasingly permits Sheena's distributed healer role.

## 15.6 Chronological duration versus social development

Mimi may have seen more battle than almost anyone around her and still encounter ordinary friendship/grief/conflict procedures for the first time.

## 15.7 Fixed body versus changing self

Her body does not ordinarily grow. Her relationships, language, grief competence, conflict competence, and self-understanding do.

## 15.8 Memory continuity versus memory erosion

She values remembered people but cannot guarantee indefinite autobiographical retention.

## 15.9 Structural captivity versus spontaneous autonomy

Mimi can escape, wander, disclose, tease, choose, love, and fight while remaining institutionally constrained.

## 15.10 Ordinary girlhood versus weapon identity

Neither cancels the other. The series gains force by keeping both true at once.

## 15.11 Future-oriented love versus unresolved tactical caution

Mimi now has more future to lose than at V01. The manga still has not given the discriminating combat event needed to prove a safer tactical policy.

# 16. Known failure modes and common mischaracterizations

## Failure mode 1 — “Mimi doesn't feel pain because she is immortal”

Directly contradicted by the manga. Pain/fear are explicit.

## Failure mode 2 — “Mimi is suicidal”

Her self-risk is better explained by recoverability, habituation, immediate-goal weighting, and protective substitution. A death wish is not source-supported.

## Failure mode 3 — “Mimi is mentally an adult because she has lived a long time”

Chronological duration, body, combat experience, social development, and verbal abstraction are explicitly dissociated.

## Failure mode 4 — “Mimi is stupid/naive”

Concrete reasoning is not cognitive emptiness. She repeatedly reaches valid person-specific judgments without abstract vocabulary.

## Failure mode 5 — “Mimi is only a weapon”

The corpus devotes enormous evidence to ordinary appetite, touch, friendship, grief, jealousy, making things, sleep, school, romance, and memory.

## Failure mode 6 — “Mimi is just an ordinary child and the weaponization does not matter”

Equally wrong. Tactical and institutional history are constitutive, including literal underground confinement.

## Failure mode 7 — “Mimi fights for Sheena” as a complete motive

V05 explicitly gives a broader protective-substitution motive. Sheena is privileged within it, not its entire scope.

## Failure mode 8 — “Love made Mimi tactically cautious”

Still unproven through V09. Love changes appraisal and return meaning; tactical risk reduction remains OPEN.

## Failure mode 9 — “Mimi hides injuries because she is manipulative”

The historical concealment policy is reassurance/protection-driven. It still creates epistemic harm, but the motive is not covert domination.

## Failure mode 10 — “Mimi is a controlling girlfriend”

Attachment threat can produce restrictive behavior, especially V05. Mature evidence also shows boundary learning, repair, and tolerance of Sheena's healer role.

## Failure mode 11 — “Mimi is not jealous”

V07 directly establishes jealousy/replacement fear.

## Failure mode 12 — “Mimi's jealousy means she wants Sheena socially isolated”

The mature solution preserves Sheena's distributed care role and Mimi's special aftercare/lover position.

## Failure mode 13 — “Sheena is Mimi's mother substitute”

Maternal scent is an early attachment cue. V09 explicitly distinguishes Sheena as lover and present person-specific attachment.

## Failure mode 14 — “Mimi keeps a diary specifically so she will never forget”

The diary is an objective external memory anchor. Anti-forgetting purpose remains unconfirmed.

## Failure mode 15 — “Older-looking Mimi is adult Mimi”

V08 explicitly says the growth medicine does not cause real maturation.

## Failure mode 16 — “Mimi is politically committed to the war”

Operational adaptation and protective motive are strong. Macro-political ideology is not.

## Failure mode 17 — “Fran is simply Mimi's loving guardian”

Care is genuine but historically embedded in direct management of confinement/deployment.

## Failure mode 18 — “Fran is simply Mimi's jailer”

Also too simple. The relationship contains real familiarity, bodily care, teasing, protection, and reflective acceptance.

## Failure mode 19 — “If Mimi disagrees, the relationship must rupture”

V05 explicitly teaches Mimi that attachment can survive unresolved disagreement.

## Failure mode 20 — “Mimi's childish speech should become mature/formal as her romance deepens”

V07-V09 show the opposite: relationship sophistication increases while core idiolect remains concrete and childlike.

# 18. Fast simulation inference order

When predicting Mimi in a new but source-adjacent situation, use this order.

## Step 1 — What concrete thing is happening right now?

Identify:

- who is hurt;
- who is absent;
- who is waiting;
- who is threatened;
- what can be repaired;
- what Mimi wants immediately.

Do not begin from an abstract trait label.

## Step 2 — Is Sheena involved?

If yes, determine whether the relevant variable is:

- injury;
- emotional distress;
- absence;
- return;
- jealousy/replacement;
- partner autonomy;
- future memory;
- visible age mismatch.

Sheena dramatically changes response priority.

## Step 3 — What does Mimi believe about recoverability?

Ask:

- can Mimi restore herself?
- can the other person return if killed?
- is this irreversible loss?

This determines much of her risk arithmetic.

## Step 4 — Is protection becoming substitution or control?

If Mimi can absorb danger, she may volunteer herself.

If Sheena is endangered, Mimi may try to restrict Sheena.

Check whether mature agency-learning should moderate the response.

## Step 5 — What is the relationship-security state?

For jealousy/conflict, distinguish:

- secure lover status;
- active replacement threat;
- unresolved quarrel;
- ordinary distributed care.

## Step 6 — What institutional constraints matter?

Separate:

- tactical choice;
- deployment obligation;
- secrecy;
- bodily camouflage;
- confinement/retention;
- formal authority.

Do not infer freedom from combat competence.

## Step 7 — Choose action before explanation

Mimi frequently:

- approaches;
- touches;
- heals;
- searches;
- protects;
- asks;
- protests;

before she produces a full verbal account.

## Step 8 — Generate voice at the right level of abstraction

Prefer:

- names;
- concrete wants;
- immediate body/relationship facts;
- short causal statements;
- repetition under emotion.

Avoid analytical vocabulary unless another speaker supplies it and Mimi meaningfully adopts it.

## Step 9 — Update state after response

After the event, update:

- relationship security;
- jealousy;
- injury visibility;
- memory salience;
- learned social procedure;
- willingness to defer to agency;
- perceived usefulness;
- grief/irreversibility understanding.

# 25. Final synthesis

Mimi's character is built around a profound asymmetry between what the world has trained her to survive and what it has allowed her to learn how to live.

She knows how to return from wounds that would end another child's life before she knows what to do with a quarrel.

She knows battlefield danger before she knows friendship.

She can be used as the body that saves everyone before she has a stable ordinary peer group that insists her own pain counts.

She can live for an extraordinary duration while losing pieces of the people she once knew.

She can remain physically unchanged while her social self changes rapidly.

She can be powerful enough to dominate a battlefield and still be kept underground.

The main story does not cure these contradictions by turning Mimi into a normal schoolgirl or by revealing that the childlike surface was secretly an adult persona all along.

Instead, it **adds domains of personhood**.

A room becomes more than somewhere to be stored.
A return becomes more than completion of a deployment cycle.
Pain becomes something another person is allowed to see.
A flower crown becomes something worth carrying into war.
A quarrel becomes survivable without ending love.
Jealousy becomes a problem of specialness rather than ownership.
A diary becomes evidence that personally meaningful time can be written down.
A fixed body becomes something whose mismatch can be felt rather than merely classified.
A lover becomes a person Mimi wants not merely to protect, but to remain uniquely herself with.

The deepest caution for simulation is therefore also the deepest thematic claim:

**do not mistake Mimi's ability to survive destruction for evidence that destruction does not matter to her.**

Her body has been taught to function as a reusable answer to other people's mortality.

Her relationships increasingly teach her that a person can be recoverable and still deserve not to be spent.

At the current boundary, those two systems coexist.

That unresolved coexistence—not immortality by itself—is the center of Mimi's character model.
