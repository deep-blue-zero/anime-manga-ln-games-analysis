# KIMISHINU_ADULT_INSTITUTIONAL_MEDIATION_SPECIALIST_SYNTHESIS.md

## 1. Purpose and authority

This is the canonical specialist synthesis for **adult institutional mediation** in *Kimi ga Shinu Made Koi wo Shitai* at the current analytical boundary `V09+SIDE_STORIES`.

Its analytical object is not “the teachers” as a loose cast category and not the institution as an abstract villain. It asks how adult actors make a militarized orphanage/school **humanly actionable**: how they train children, treat their bodies, interpret ability, preserve secrets, discuss risk, provide opportunities, limit information, maintain schedules through grief, and sometimes defend local self-authorship inside a structure whose larger terms the children do not control.

The principal adult evidence divides into three layers:

1. **Fran** — the strongest longitudinal case: caretaker, medical manager, secret custodian, mentor, realist, identity interlocutor, and historically direct participant in Mimi's confinement/deployment/recovery cycle.
2. **Omi-sensei** — a narrower but diagnostically important contrast: care expressed through preparation, realistic training design, procedural responsibility, and willingness to be less liked if that seems necessary for student competence or safety.
3. **Unnamed adult strategic/administrative actors** — especially V09 CH41: people who interpret enemy strength, discuss force allocation, manage combat readiness, control strategic information, and debate continued concentration of danger in Mimi.

This artifact does **not** supersede `KIMISHINU_INSTITUTION_WAR_CONSCIOUSNESS_AND_AGENCY_LEDGER.md`. The ledger remains the chronological institutional-state authority. This synthesis instead integrates the adult-facing pattern that would be flattened if the eventual full-series synthesis treated the school as either a faceless machine or a collection of individually benevolent teachers.

The governing rule is:

> **Do not infer structural freedom from interpersonal care, and do not infer interpersonal indifference from structural complicity.**

The current numbered prospective boundary remains V09. Standalone Side Stories are supplemental/retrospective evidence and refine the mature model without rewriting what earlier prospective readings could know.

# 2. Executive thesis — mediated coercion through caring adults

KimiShinu's adult system is best understood as **mediated coercion**.

The school is plainly coercive in structural terms. It houses orphaned children, trains their innate magic for lethal use, measures their capacities, advances them through increasingly realistic combat preparation, deploys them to war, absorbs casualties into routine, and controls strategic information. Mimi's history hardens the strongest case: she was literally kept underground as a secret weapon, retrieved for summons, washed/restored on return, and sent back underground. By V09, adults still discuss whether her recoverability makes her the appropriate body for the fiercest expected sector.

Yet the adults through whom this structure becomes lived are not presented as one psychologically uniform class of abusers.

Fran notices pain. She feeds, washes, treats, teaches, warns, teases, listens, identifies aptitude, takes Sheena's healer goal seriously, tells Haru that nobody else may decide who Haru is, recognizes the reality of Sheena and Mimi's love, and worries about the grief their temporal asymmetry may cause. Omi worries about preparation and student outcomes even when she does not seek affection or popularity. Unnamed adults can say that students should not be wasted pointlessly while still speaking about readiness throughput and force distribution.

The series therefore refuses two simplifying equations:

`care -> innocence`

and

`complicity -> absence of care`.

The more difficult formulation is:

`person-specific care + professional duty + limited local discretion + institutional normalization + strategic necessity claims + unequal structural power -> adults can protect children in some dimensions while continuing the machinery that endangers them in others`.

That is not a rhetorical compromise between “good teachers” and “bad system.” It is a causal model.

The adult layer matters because institutions usually reach children through persons, not abstractions. A student experiences “the system” through:

- who tells her to train;
- who evaluates her magic;
- who decides what she is allowed to know;
- who treats her after combat;
- who explains why another child is sent in her place;
- who recognizes that grief exists but continues tomorrow's exercise;
- who offers an opportunity;
- who says the choice is hers;
- who determines whether that choice is actually available;
- who turns a rare body into a classified problem;
- who may personally love or care about the child whose body is being managed.

KimiShinu's adult ethics are therefore not located in a simple opposition between individual virtue and institutional evil. They are located in the **friction between local personhood recognition and continued participation in a system organized around child military utility**.

Fran is the clearest embodiment of that friction.

She can look at Mimi and see a hungry, unruly, lonely child who deserves ordinary classroom life. She can also be the person who retrieves Mimi from confinement when the summons comes, restores the body after battle, and returns that body to confinement. She can teach Sheena to identify displaced harm rather than imagining that “Mimi should stop fighting” is a costless solution. She can later help Sheena build a healing vocation that is self-authored rather than combat-centered. She can defend Haru's right not to have identity decided by others. She can manage Mimi's visible aging problem as classified information. She can tell Sheena that “always” does not erase death and still accept that choosing Mimi now can be worthwhile.

Fran's mature adult rule is not reducible to kindness or obedience. It is closer to:

> **Within conditions I may not control, keep bodies alive, keep choices psychologically honest, keep secrets the system requires, and help children act inside reality rather than comforting them with false costlessness.**

That rule can be humane and still reproduce coercion.

Omi clarifies the point from another direction. Her care is less bodily and less emotionally demonstrative. It is procedural. She values preparation, realistic training, and responsibility over popularity. The Side Story `くらくら` makes clear that she and Fran know Class 14 is destabilized by Laura's death, yet the mock-battle schedule proceeds. This is not evidence that Omi cannot see grief. It is evidence that **seeing grief is not enough to suspend the institution's temporal logic**.

By V09, the strategic layer makes the structural problem explicit. Adult decision-makers discuss enemy force trends, supply-line hypotheses, allocation among bases, qualification throughput, future summons, information concealment, and Mimi's assignment to intense combat. Their language can include sincere desire not to waste student lives. But the children remain resources inside the calculation.

The mature thesis is therefore:

> **KimiShinu's adults humanize the system without necessarily liberating the children from it. Their care makes the institution more survivable, more intelligible, and sometimes more autonomy-compatible at the local level; it does not by itself make the institution non-coercive.**

# 4. The adult layer is not the institution itself

A major analytical error would be to collapse three levels into one:

1. **institutional structure**;
2. **adult role/function**;
3. **adult personal psychology**.

They interact but are not identical.

The school can be structurally coercive even if a particular teacher is kind.

A teacher can carry out a coercive function without personally desiring the child's suffering.

An adult can sincerely defend a child's local choice while lacking authority over the larger menu of available choices.

Conversely, the fact that an adult “has orders” or works inside a system does not erase moral agency. The source does not provide enough evidence to excuse every action as bureaucratic helplessness. Fran's participation in Mimi's confinement cycle is a real action history, not a metaphor. The correct model preserves uncertainty over her available alternatives rather than converting uncertainty into innocence.

This creates four distinct questions whenever evaluating an adult act:

### 4.1 What does the adult personally perceive or value?

Examples:

- Fran recognizes Mimi as a child who needs care;
- Fran worries about Sheena's exhaustion;
- Omi cares about student preparation;
- adults recognize Class 14's grief after Laura's death;
- Fran validates Haru's right to self-definition.

### 4.2 What professional role is the adult performing?

Examples:

- medical treatment;
- teacher preparation;
- magical aptitude assessment;
- secrecy enforcement;
- readiness evaluation;
- strategic allocation.

### 4.3 What structural power does the adult actually possess?

Often OPEN.

The source does not establish that Fran can cancel Mimi's deployment, abolish confinement policy, release classified information, or decide national strategy. It also does not establish that she had no meaningful discretion whatsoever.

### 4.4 What system does the act reproduce regardless of motive?

Examples:

- restoring wounded children enables them to remain within a recurrent deployment system;
- testing combat proficiency creates deployable readiness;
- age camouflage protects Mimi from exposure but also preserves state secrecy over her body;
- withholding strategic uncertainty reduces student knowledge while maintaining orderly participation.

The adult synthesis must keep all four questions active.

# 5. Fran — the central mediator

Fran is the strongest adult case because her role spans the entire ethical range of the institution: care, medical practice, secrecy, training, realism, autonomy language, bodily management, and direct historical participation in coercion.

## 5.1 V01 — bodily manager, boundary enforcer, and first human face of Mimi's exceptional status

From V01, Fran is already associated with the fact that Mimi's body requires unusual adult management.

Mimi returns from battle blood-covered and does not report to the infirmary as expected. Fran's reaction treats this as a recurring behavioral problem rather than a one-time shock. She knows Mimi well enough to be exasperated by her habits and to physically/socially manage her in a familiar register.

Fran also says Mimi has been told not to involve herself with students. At the V01 boundary, the reason is not yet fully known. Retrospectively, the restriction belongs to the larger secrecy and exceptional-asset architecture.

The important adult pattern is already present:

`Mimi difficult to contain + body strategically exceptional -> Fran performs person-facing management`.

Fran is neither merely a nurse nor merely a guard. The source initially withholds how deeply those functions overlap.

## 5.2 V02 — custodian of classified bodily truth

V02 makes Fran a gatekeeper of Mimi's ontological and strategic condition.

She warns Mimi not to disclose the secret and later explains to Sheena that Mimi was resurrected by forbidden magic, no longer ages normally, and is strategically valuable because terminal loss does not function normally for her.

The secrecy rule is especially revealing. Fran treats information about Mimi's own body as **not simply Mimi's private property** because foreign knowledge could have strategic consequences.

That produces a concrete conflict between bodily personhood and state-class information control:

`Mimi's body is Mimi's lived body`

but

`knowledge of Mimi's body is treated as a strategic asset`.

Mimi can violate the rule interpersonally by telling Sheena. She does not thereby gain recognized institutional authority over the classification regime.

Fran therefore mediates a system in which person and intelligence object occupy the same body.

## 5.3 V02 — consequentialist substitution realism

Fran's most important early ethical intervention comes when Sheena wants Mimi not to fight.

Fran does not dismiss the concern as childish sentiment. She forces Sheena to confront the displaced cost: under current conditions, if Mimi stops, another child may have to take the same danger. Peace is framed through eventual victory rather than through one girl's withdrawal.

This is adult realism in its strongest and most dangerous form.

The reasoning is not obviously sadistic:

`war continues + someone must face danger + Mimi is recoverable -> concentrating exposure in Mimi can reduce terminal losses elsewhere`.

The moral problem is that a numerically defensible harm-reduction policy can normalize **repeated catastrophic injury to one child**.

Fran's intervention changes Sheena for the better epistemically. Sheena stops assuming that her first compassionate impulse is automatically costless. She learns to ask who bears the displaced harm.

But Fran's argument also exemplifies how a caring adult can stabilize an exploitative equilibrium. By making the substitution logic intelligible, the adult can help a child think more rigorously while also making the system's continuation psychologically coherent.

The correct synthesis therefore preserves two truths:

- Fran teaches real moral complexity.
- The complexity does not dissolve Mimi's instrumentalization.

## 5.4 V03 — medical infrastructure turns war into recoverable routine

V03 makes the adult care system materially visible through the overloaded infirmary/recovery pipeline.

Wounded students arrive in large numbers. “Recovery” language sits beside grievously damaged bodies. Medicine becomes insufficient. Staff triage, transport, treat, and continue functioning under casualty pressure.

Fran is part of the health infrastructure that receives what the battlefield returns.

This produces a crucial institutional duality:

> **Medical care opposes bodily destruction at the level of each patient while enabling the institution to survive repeated destruction at the systemic level.**

That is not an accusation against medicine. It is a structural fact about sustainment.

The infirmary is simultaneously:

- refuge;
- treatment space;
- site where suffering becomes visible;
- route back into ordinary school life;
- infrastructure that makes recurrent deployment administratively possible.

Later Sheena will enter this same space voluntarily as a healer. The adult mediation model therefore matters for understanding how a humane vocation can exist inside a coercive system without becoming morally identical to that system.

## 5.5 V04 — talent recognition without immediate appropriation

After Seiran's death, Sheena approaches Fran because she wants to know what she can do.

Fran first notices exhaustion and tells her to rest. When Sheena persists with a clear goal, Fran tests the magic rather than simply praising determination. She identifies Sheena's `治癒魔法`, distinguishes it from restoration magic, explains the connection to Mimi's mother's attribute without making shared ability into shared destiny, and begins turning vague aspiration into observable capability.

This is one of the corpus's strongest examples of constructive adult mediation:

`child expresses self-authored goal -> adult checks bodily condition -> adult tests capacity -> adult supplies technical language -> adult does not dictate identity from capability -> child gains bounded path of competence`.

Importantly, V04 does **not** show the institution immediately claiming Sheena as a military medical asset.

The institution/agency ledger therefore leaves instrumentalization OPEN rather than assuming that every recognized talent is automatically seized.

This distinction is vital. KimiShinu's institution is coercive, but the source still allows meaningful domains of authorship inside it.

## 5.6 V05 — adult autonomy language exposes the system's internal contradiction

V05 contains a high-value adult principle: adults can provide opportunities, but the choice belongs to the children.

Whether treated as a general adult norm or a statement by a particular adult speaker, its presence matters because it is not empty on its face. The series repeatedly gives children real local choices:

- Sheena chooses healing rather than self-substitution;
- Haru later chooses how to present and define the self;
- Ari refuses resurrection;
- Ari later chooses the combat proficiency exam;
- Mimi makes interpersonal disclosures despite secrecy rules.

But the menu of choices is structurally constrained.

A child may be allowed to choose among paths inside a school she did not meaningfully choose to need.

A child may choose combat after growing up in a system where competence, gratitude, peer protection, and survival are deeply tied to usefulness.

Mimi may endorse protective substitution while her body has historically been confined and strategically exploited.

Thus:

> **local choice can be real without making the surrounding structure freely chosen.**

The adult autonomy statement should neither be dismissed as hypocrisy nor used to certify the institution as non-coercive.

## 5.7 V06 — quantified bodies and bounded knowledge

V06 expands the adult-mediated system in two directions.

First, semiannual examination routinely quantifies bodily and magical capacities. For Sheena this measurement validates chosen growth. Institutionally, the same measurement regime tracks bodies whose capacities have military relevance.

Second, students finally learn a more accurate account of the protected grimoire and why external forces seek it, but their knowledge remains partial. Prior contradictory cover stories show that ignorance is not merely accidental.

An adult/teacher can also invoke national protection and victory rhetoric while telling students that hiding behind someone else will not let them survive the battlefield.

This matters because adult mediation is epistemic as well as bodily.

The school does not require children to know as much as adults know in order to require them to act.

The emerging structure is:

`adult strategic knowledge > student strategic knowledge`

while

`student tactical obligation remains high`.

By V09 this becomes explicit policy rather than inference.

## 5.8 V07 — mentorship becomes clinical apprenticeship

V07 hardens Fran's constructive role in Sheena's development.

Sheena no longer only practices privately. Under Fran/medical supervision, she assists with actual patients: ordinary treatment, cleaning, bandaging, pain response, observation, conversation, and selective magic use.

Fran tests what Sheena's healing actually does instead of permitting a grandiose “healer” self-image to remain unexamined. Sheena learns that magic is not a universal substitute for medicine and that patient conversation can be part of treatment.

This is adult mediation at its best-supported autonomy-compatible form:

`aptitude -> supervised practice -> procedural competence -> bodily limits remain visible -> social usefulness grows without requiring identity replacement`.

The same scenes also preserve the structural ambiguity of medical work. The infirmary is where injured children are made more survivable inside a recurrent war cycle.

Sheena's role is morally different from sending them to war, but the institution can accommodate her humane vocation precisely because care is necessary to sustain the population being deployed.

No current evidence shows Fran weaponizing Sheena's vocation against her wishes. No current evidence proves such appropriation can never happen.

## 5.9 V08 — secrecy reaches the visible body

Fran explains that Mimi's face and height do not ordinarily change and introduces the `成長薬` as temporary appearance management.

The institution's secrecy regime has therefore moved beyond classified information in the abstract.

Mimi's visible body is itself an information leak.

The solution is cosmetic governance:

`non-aging body -> ordinary class progression makes anomaly visible -> strategic secret at risk -> appearance-management technology becomes available`.

Two distinctions must be preserved.

First, the drug does **not** actually make Mimi grow. It produces age camouflage.

Second, V08 does not establish that Mimi is forcibly dosed. Mimi later takes it for a personal romantic reason. Institutional rationale, available technology, and individual use are separate variables.

This is a recurring adult-layer rule:

> **the presence of a structural control technology does not tell us, by itself, how much coercion occurs in every individual use.**

## 5.10 V08 — Fran articulates self-authorship more cleanly than the institution embodies it

`ゆらめき` gives Fran perhaps her clearest explicit autonomy statement.

Haru is distressed by pressure to identify what kind of person Haru is, what Haru likes, and what future relational category will fit. Fran refuses to solve the problem from outside.

Her response:

`納得できるまで 悩み続けな`

and then:

`他人事よ`

`だって 他人が決めていいことじゃないもの`

The ethical structure is direct:

`outside observer may advise / observe / support -> authorship remains with the person whose identity is at stake`.

Fran also reminds Haru that friends already relate to the present Haru rather than to a completed label.

This is strong evidence that an adult inside the school can hold a robust self-authorship norm.

It also makes the institution's contradictions sharper rather than softer.

The same adult culture that can say “other people do not get to decide who you are” exists inside a system that:

- trains children for killing;
- classifies bodily information;
- historically confined Mimi;
- channels progression toward combat readiness;
- withholds strategic uncertainty.

KimiShinu therefore distinguishes **identity authorship** from **structural sovereignty**. A child may have meaningful authority over self-description while having little authority over military conditions.

## 5.11 V09 — the confinement history destroys any uncomplicated caretaker reading

CH40 is the decisive retrospective reclassification of Fran.

Fran remembers a repeated historical cycle:

1. summons;
2. retrieve Mimi from underground confinement;
3. Mimi fights;
4. wash and restore Mimi on return;
5. send Mimi back underground;
6. repeat.

Mimi sometimes escaped and wandered the school, and Fran was reprimanded when others found her.

This is not merely evidence that “the institution” confined Mimi. Fran's own care was physically embedded in the cycle.

She restored the body and returned the child to the place of confinement.

The mature model cannot therefore say:

`Fran cared, therefore she opposed what happened to Mimi`.

Nor can it say:

`Fran participated, therefore every act of care was cynical management`.

The source instead makes the contradiction constitutive:

> **Fran's hands are both caregiving hands and system-maintenance hands.**

The same act of washing/restoring can be compassionate toward Mimi and functional for the asset cycle.

This is the clearest adult-level form of KimiShinu's broader personhood problem: a person can be sincerely cared for and simultaneously treated as reusable.

## 5.12 V09 — Fran's present recognizes the expansion of Mimi's personhood

The confinement retrospective also makes present ordinary life more meaningful.

Mimi no longer has to return to the underground room. She has classmates, a roommate, a lover, ordinary meals, study obligations, friendships, jokes, baths, and a future problem that extends beyond the next summons.

Fran can recognize this change.

That recognition matters because the institution is not entirely static. Mimi's social personhood has expanded.

But the expansion is incomplete. CH41 still asks whether her body should absorb the fiercest expected fighting. CH42 leaves open whether she can graduate with the others.

Thus:

`social integration ↑`

while

`strategic disposability incentive persists`

and

`release autonomy remains OPEN`.

An adult can celebrate the first without having solved the other two.

## 5.13 V09 — mortality realism without relationship prohibition

Fran's CH42 conversation with Sheena is another example of adult mediation that resists comforting falsehood.

Sheena wants a shared future with Mimi but is frightened by unequal aging, death, separation, and eventual forgetting. Fran distinguishes sincere “always” language from literal endless coexistence. She raises the possibility of bodily synchronization only to expose its sacrificial cost. She admits that seeing Sheena hurt makes her want to tell Sheena to stop before the pain deepens.

Yet Fran does not ban or delegitimize the relationship.

Her role is closer to:

`young person's desired future -> expose irreversible costs/asymmetries -> refuse false guarantee -> preserve young person's final choice`.

Sheena then chooses continued love despite foreseeable pain.

This is structurally similar to Fran's earlier intervention about Mimi's deployment. In both cases, Fran resists cost-free fantasy.

But the domain matters:

- in V02, realism can stabilize a coercive distribution of military harm;
- in V09, realism helps Sheena make a more informed relational choice.

The same epistemic style is not morally identical across contexts.

# 6. Omi-sensei — care through structure

Omi's evidence is much narrower than Fran's, which is precisely why a separate Omi monograph is not justified at the current boundary. But `SS03 くらくら` gives enough information to establish a distinct adult-care mode.

## 6.1 Procedural responsibility rather than popularity

Omi is reserved, serious, and more concerned with teaching quality and student outcomes than with whether students like her.

Fran teases her about popularity, but the better Omi model is:

`teacher responsibility -> preparation / realistic training / procedure -> student welfare`.

This is not emotional absence.

The negative constraint is important:

> **reserved affect must not be generated as indifference.**

Omi's care is embedded in what she builds and prepares rather than in Fran-style bodily familiarity or teasing warmth.

## 6.2 Grief recognition without schedule suspension

`くらくら` is placed after Laura's death and around Mimi's integration / the impending mock battle.

The adults know Class 14 is unsettled.

The training schedule continues.

This scene eliminates an easy explanation for institutional harshness. The school does not continue merely because adults fail to notice that children are grieving.

They can notice and continue anyway.

That creates a sharper model:

`student distress recognized -> adult concern real -> institutional task still judged necessary -> preparation continues`.

Omi is especially useful here because her care is procedural. If she believes realistic training is necessary for survival, improving the training can itself feel like care—even when the timing imposes further burden on grieving children.

This is a recurring KimiShinu ethical structure: protection against one danger can reproduce another harm.

## 6.3 Omi and Mimi — integration as both management and care

Fran and Omi discuss Mimi's new classroom placement and the mock battle. Fran effectively asks Omi to look after Mimi.

That request personalizes what could otherwise be modeled as asset reassignment.

Mimi is certainly a staff-management problem: classified, difficult to contain, exceptionally powerful, socially underintegrated.

She is also a child one adult asks another adult to care for.

The same transfer can therefore be:

- administrative placement;
- risk-management decision;
- social integration;
- an act of person-specific care.

The categories overlap.

## 6.4 Fran–Omi differentiation prevents a monolithic adult voice

Their interpersonal register reinforces the distinction.

Fran is teasing, bodily/socially close, and willing to provoke embarrassment. Omi is more restrained.

The evidence supports an established, comfortable professional relationship with asymmetrical teasing. It does not establish a romantic or sexual relationship.

More importantly for institutional synthesis, the pair demonstrate:

`same institution != same emotional style != same care technique`.

A simulator or full-series synthesis should not make every adult speak in Fran's tone, Omi's tone, or generic bureaucratic language.

# 7. The unnamed adult strategic layer — where children become force variables

Fran and Omi show the institution at person-facing scale. V09 CH41 finally exposes a more strategic adult layer.

Adults discuss:

- declining enemy deployed strength;
- whether the cause is supply difficulty or strategic change;
- uncertainty about enemy intention;
- possible redistribution of friendly force among bases;
- the danger of reacting mechanically to numbers;
- student proficiency-exam throughput;
- readiness for the next summons;
- further testing;
- not wanting to waste student lives pointlessly;
- concealment of strategic uncertainty from students;
- whether Mimi should remain in the region expected to face the fiercest combat.

This scene matters because it makes the institutional calculation explicit without supplying a cartoon villain.

## 7.1 “We do not intend to waste lives” is compatible with resource management

An adult can sincerely oppose pointless death while still treating students as military resources.

Those are not contradictory if the operating ethic is consequentialist:

`children will be used -> use them efficiently -> avoid unnecessary loss -> maintain enough readiness -> allocate exceptional assets where expected payoff is highest`.

The moral controversy lies one level earlier:

**why are children inside the deployable resource pool at all, and how much power do they have over that condition?**

The adults' desire to avoid waste may reduce harm inside the system while leaving the system's coercive premise untouched.

## 7.2 Combat readiness is explicitly a production pipeline

By V09, higher-class progression, increasingly realistic training, proficiency tests, and upcoming summons are discussed together.

Education and force generation are not merely adjacent functions.

They are coupled:

`instruction -> evaluation -> qualification -> deployability`.

The language of school progression therefore cannot be modeled as politically neutral education with a separate wartime add-on.

For many students, advancement increases the institution's capacity to use them militarily.

## 7.3 Strategic secrecy is deliberate epistemic control

Adults explicitly prefer that students not notice the uncertainty surrounding enemy behavior.

This is a major agency finding.

Children are expected to become capable of:

- surviving combat;
- executing trained roles;
- qualifying for summons;

faster than they are permitted to become capable of evaluating the strategic situation that makes those roles necessary.

The structure is:

`adult strategic interpretation -> restricted circulation`

while

`student tactical responsibility -> expanded`.

This is **structured epistemic deprivation**.

It matters because “choice” under such conditions is never purely a matter of physical compulsion. The child's decision environment is shaped by what adults allow the child to know.

## 7.4 Mimi remains an optimization problem

The adults' discussion of assigning Mimi to the fiercest expected region shows that the reusable-child logic survives ordinary integration and romantic development.

Mimi can have friends, a lover, a room, schoolwork, and improved social standing while the adult strategic layer still sees a body that can absorb risk unusually well.

This is one of the synthesis's most important non-substitution rules:

> **personhood gain does not automatically eliminate utility-based risk concentration.**

The source also does not establish that every adult in the meeting agrees on Mimi's placement or that one speaker has final authority. Preserve the debate structure rather than inventing a single command voice.

# 8. The autonomy contradiction — real choice inside an unchosen field

Adult mediation repeatedly produces meaningful local agency.

That agency should not be dismissed simply because the surrounding structure is coercive.

But it should not be inflated into structural freedom.

## 8.1 Ari — narrow but morally decisive agency

Ari cannot undo Seiran's deployment or death. She can refuse resurrection.

That refusal is enormously meaningful because it protects Seiran's posthumous ownership of her own life.

This is a model of **constrained but real agency**.

## 8.2 Sheena — self-authored role differentiation

Sheena cannot end the war or simply leave the institution.

She can reject passive waiting as her only contribution, discover healing aptitude, accept technical instruction, develop clinical competence, and eventually imagine adult medical work.

Fran's mentorship expands the feasible set without dictating the choice.

This is one of the strongest examples of adult support functioning non-coercively at the local level.

## 8.3 Haru — identity authorship

Haru cannot choose a healthy body or erase the institution's expectations.

Haru can choose presentation, resist externally imposed identity categories, accept help that expands viable options, and keep unresolved questions unresolved until personally settled.

Fran explicitly defends this authorship.

## 8.4 Mimi — where local agency and structural captivity collide most sharply

Mimi can:

An adult can respect one domain while constraining another.

# 9. Care infrastructure and the reproduction of war

One of the most uncomfortable adult-level findings is that **care itself can become sustainment infrastructure**.

This does not mean care is morally bad.

It means a system capable of repeatedly injuring children requires systems capable of repeatedly repairing, stabilizing, feeding, educating, and psychologically reintegrating them.

The infirmary is the clearest case.

## 9.1 The protective function is real

Students survive because adults treat them.

Pain is reduced.

Bodies are repaired.

Sheena learns to listen to patients and tailor care.

Without that work, the institution would be even more lethal.

## 9.2 The sustainment function is also real

Recovered students return to school rhythm and remain liable to future training/deployment.

Mimi's historical cycle makes the mechanism extreme: restoration directly participates in reusable-asset continuity.

Thus:

`care -> survival`

and

`survival -> renewed availability to the institution`

can coexist.

The adult synthesis must not respond by condemning healing. The analytically useful question is **who controls what follows healing**.

Sheena's chosen vocation is oriented toward persons. The military system can still benefit from that work.

This distinction will be important in full-series synthesis because KimiShinu repeatedly asks whether usefulness to a system can coexist with personhood without becoming identical to personhood.

# 10. Grief, continuity, and the adult schedule

The adults do not appear wholly numb to student death.

Side Story evidence specifically shows recognition of Class 14's destabilization after Laura's death.

Yet training continues.

After later deaths, ordinary schedules repeatedly resume.

The institutional temporal rule is:

`loss acknowledged -> local mourning occurs -> operational continuity resumes`.

This differs from:

`loss not perceived`.

The distinction matters morally and psychologically.

A system can become normalized not because nobody cares but because **care is required to fit inside continuity rather than continuity being reorganized around care**.

This helps explain why children perform so much grief labor for one another.

Ari, Sheena, Mimi, Esta, Rika, Haru, and others build person-specific practices of waiting, remembering, sitting nearby, finding someone, returning objects, talking, or not talking.

Adult care exists, but the source does not show a large therapeutic apparatus that suspends military demands until grief is resolved.

The school remains capable of noticing pain and moving on schedule.

The institution does not produce one standardized psychological outcome.

## 11.1 Mimi — care inside exceptional-asset capture

Fran's technical mentorship then gives Sheena a way to act without becoming the kind of fighter she fears becoming.

Adult mediation helps transform:

`helpless moral distress`

into

That is a genuine developmental achievement.

## 11.3 Haru — adult refusal to seize authorship

**the answer is Haru's.**

This supports identity exploration without making the adult the classifier.

## 11.4 Laura — institutional usefulness can be internalized without explicit adult cruelty

That choice occurs inside a system that produces combat readiness, but the motive cannot be reduced to adult indoctrination.

A mature institutional synthesis must leave room for children to make genuinely authored choices that are still shaped by their environment.

# 12. Adult behavioral policies

The following policies are current mature modeling aids, not claims that every adult follows one rule.

## AIPOL-01 — Fran: cost-displacement realism

`child proposes compassionate simplification -> identify who bears displaced cost -> refuse false costlessness -> return choice/problem to child at higher complexity`.

Strong examples:
- Mimi should stop fighting -> someone else must face the danger;
- “always” with Mimi -> mortality/asymmetry must still be faced.

**Risk:** realism can stabilize an unjust status quo if alternatives outside the current system are excluded from consideration.

## AIPOL-02 — Fran: bodily care before abstraction

`visible injury/illness/exhaustion -> assess body -> treat/rest/restore -> then discuss larger problem`.

Fran's adult presence is repeatedly grounded in bodies rather than ideology.

## AIPOL-03 — Fran: aptitude -> test -> differentiate -> supervise

`child identifies possible capacity -> empirical test -> name limits/mechanism -> supervise practice -> avoid equating ability with destiny`.

Best demonstrated in Sheena's healer development.

## AIPOL-04 — Fran: self-authorship boundary

`identity/value question belongs to child -> adult may observe/advise -> refuse to author final answer -> validate current person before final classification`.

Best demonstrated with Haru.

## AIPOL-05 — Fran: secrecy management

`strategically sensitive body/information -> restrict disclosure / manage legibility -> preserve institutional secret`.

This can conflict directly with bodily autonomy.

## AIPOL-06 — Fran: care inside assigned function

`system assigns/manages child -> Fran performs personal care within role -> attachment may become genuine -> role participation does not disappear`.

This is the core Mimi policy.

## AIPOL-07 — Omi: procedural care

`student welfare problem -> improve preparation / realism / teaching structure -> accept reduced popularity if necessary`.

Do not generate high-affect reassurance merely because care is present.

## AIPOL-08 — adult continuity under grief

`student loss/distress recognized -> acknowledge concern -> required training/administrative schedule persists unless source establishes exception`.

## AIPOL-09 — strategic staff: readiness allocation

`enemy/mission uncertainty -> interpret available data -> estimate force need -> manage qualification/readiness -> allocate bodies/assets -> minimize avoidable loss within system`.

This policy is descriptive, not moral endorsement.

## AIPOL-10 — strategic staff: epistemic containment

`strategic uncertainty judged disruptive/sensitive -> restrict student awareness -> maintain tactical participation despite incomplete political knowledge`.

## AIPOL-11 — choice inside constrained menu

`adult provides option -> child may genuinely choose -> structural conditions shaping available options remain analytically separate`.

Never infer “fully free” from one observed choice.

## AIPOL-12 — care does not prove opposition

`adult expresses affection / protects child locally -> do not infer opposition to institution without direct evidence`.

## AIPOL-13 — complicity does not prove sadism

`adult performs coercive/system-sustaining role -> do not infer pleasure, indifference, or absence of attachment without direct evidence`.

# 18. Implications for the eventual full-series synthesis

This specialist synthesis closes a major architecture gap because it prevents several later errors.

The full-series synthesis should not describe KimiShinu's institution as an impersonal machine while ignoring the adults who humanize and reproduce it.

It should not treat Fran as proof that the school “really cares,” because the confinement history makes that untenable.

It should not treat Fran as a simple villain, because the source repeatedly shows genuine care, technical mentorship, and respect for local self-authorship.

It should not use Omi's reserve as shorthand for institutional coldness.

It should not treat “choice” as one binary variable.

And it should not treat care as external to militarization. The care system is one of the places where KimiShinu most clearly shows that person-preserving practices can be morally meaningful while also functioning inside a structure organized around continued exposure to violence.

The strongest full-series formulation available from the adult layer is:

> **The institution survives ethically not by eliminating personhood but by allowing and sometimes cultivating islands of personhood inside functions that still consume children's bodies, time, knowledge, and futures.**

That formulation remains a synthesis claim, not proof that every adult consciously understands the system in those terms.

# 19. Final synthesis

KimiShinu's adults matter because coercive systems rarely appear to children only as rules on paper.

They appear as the person who washes the blood away.

The teacher who prepares the next exercise.

The adult who says the class is grieving and then continues the schedule.

The nurse who tells you that your compassionate solution would simply move the danger to somebody else.

The mentor who discovers that the magic you thought was useless can heal.

The woman who tells you nobody else gets to decide who you are.

The staff member who hides the truth because the institution believes you should fight without knowing everything it knows.

The meeting that calls your proficiency a readiness resource.

The adult who cares whether you come back alive and still participates in deciding where your body can be used.

Fran contains nearly all of these contradictions in one person.

That is why she is analytically more important as an **institutional mediator** than as an isolated character monograph at the current boundary.

Her care does not acquit the structure.

Her complicity does not erase her care.

Omi confirms that adult participation does not produce one personality. V09's strategic layer confirms that even sincere harm-minimization can operate through a framework in which children remain allocable resources.

The mature adult-institutional model is therefore:

`children live inside constrained military structure`

`-> adults translate structure into training, care, secrecy, opportunity, evaluation, and allocation`

`-> some adult actions expand local agency and personhood`

`-> the same adults/infrastructure can reproduce strategic control and recurrent exposure`

`-> children develop different forms of authored agency inside those constraints`

`-> interpersonal humanity changes how the system is lived`

`-> interpersonal humanity does not by itself abolish the system's coercive logic`.

The deepest adult-level thesis is:

> **KimiShinu does not ask whether caring adults can exist inside a coercive institution. It shows that they can, then asks what care means when the caring person is also one of the hands through which the institution continues to work.**
