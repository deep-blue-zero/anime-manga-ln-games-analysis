# KIMISHINU_SHEENA_CHARACTER_MONOGRAPH.md

## 1. Purpose and authority

This is the first simulation-ready specialist character synthesis for **Totsuki Sheena / トツキ・シーナ** in the KimiShinu analytical corpus.

It is derived from the complete current-publication Japanese source boundary available to this project:

- numbered manga V01-V09, read prospectively and frozen one volume at a time;
- V05 special booklet, integrated only after the V05 mainline freeze;
- in-volume supplemental narratives whose evidentiary classes were separately recorded;
- standalone `Side Stories`, opened only after the V09 numbered freeze and chronology-routed story by story;
- the five canonical longitudinal ledgers at integrated boundary `V09+SIDE_STORIES`.

This monograph **does not replace** the sequential readings or the longitudinal ledgers. Its responsibility is narrower and more synthetic: convert their accumulated evidence into a source-faithful model of Sheena that can support character reconstruction, behavioral prediction, dialogue generation, comparative analysis, and future claim revision.

The historical prospective discipline remains controlling. A mature V09+Side-Stories conclusion must not be written backward as though it were already knowable in V01. In particular:

- Sheena's early response to Mimi must retain the uncertainty of V01 rather than importing later lover status;
- Laura's personality and the texture of her relationship with Sheena are later retrospective evidence from `ばいばい`;
- Mimi's resurrection mechanism is not V01 knowledge;
- Sheena's healer identity does not exist as a stable self-conception before V04;
- mutual `恋人` status begins prospectively at V07, not earlier;
- Sheena's explicit fear of unequal aging, death, and future forgetting becomes fully articulated only in V09.

The current mature model therefore has two simultaneous responsibilities:

1. describe **who Sheena is at the V09+Side-Stories integrated boundary**;
2. preserve the **developmental path and earlier uncertainty states** that produced that person.

# 2. Executive character thesis

Sheena is best modeled not as a timid pacifist who gradually becomes brave, but as a person whose psychology is organized around **persistent moral and bodily salience**.

Death remains death to her when the institution calls it routine. Injury remains injury when restoration makes it reversible. Mimi remains a person when everyone has good practical reasons to treat an immortal body as reusable military capacity. An enemy life can still register as a person's life even when the surrounding system supplies no space for political abstraction. Sheena's central moral intuition is therefore not a developed ideology so much as a stubborn refusal to let **recoverability, usefulness, weakness, enemy status, or self-sacrificial consent erase personhood**.

Her early problem is that she does not initially possess enough agency to do much with that sensitivity. When she sees suffering and believes nothing can be changed, she is vulnerable to **helplessness avoidance**: if she does not look too directly, she does not have to confront the unbearable gap between what ought to be different and what she can actually change. This defense can resemble passivity from outside, but V05 makes clear that it is not apathy.

The main arc is therefore:

`threat/personhood remains salient -> helplessness is painful -> defensive disengagement protects the powerless self -> attachment makes disengagement increasingly intolerable -> bounded capacities become visible -> Sheena trains those capacities -> fear remains -> action becomes increasingly available -> care becomes vocation -> survival itself becomes morally useful because a living Sheena can continue healing others`.

Mimi accelerates this transformation without replacing Sheena's personality. The relationship does not turn Sheena into an imitation of Mimi, a fearless combatant, or a person whose value depends on being “strong enough.” Instead, Mimi gives Sheena a person whose suffering she cannot comfortably explain away. Sheena responds by discovering a form of strength consistent with her preexisting moral architecture: **healing, waiting, receiving the wounded truthfully, reducing preventable harm, and refusing to let one person's body become everyone else's solution**.

The romance also matures through a distinctly Sheena-like process. Desire first changes perception before it changes language. She becomes jealous and wants primacy before she can comfortably name romance. She acts physically before she can stabilize the social category. Once she does understand what she means, her declarations become unusually simple and direct. Even then, love does not produce total transparency or total agreement. Sheena and Mimi can remain secure lovers while disagreeing about protective substitution, while having asymmetrical bodies and futures, and while Sheena still conceals the full depth of some fears.

The mature V09 Sheena is therefore a character of **bounded courage, anti-disposability, recipient-sensitive care, semantic caution, finite commitment, and increasingly self-authored usefulness**.

Her core developmental achievement is not that fear disappears.

It is that **fear stops having sole veto power over what she does**.

# 3. Character identity at the current boundary

## 3.1 Current snapshot

At `V09+SIDE_STORIES`, Sheena is:

- a quiet, observant, reflective student in a militarized orphanage/school system;
- still highly sensitive to death, bodily injury, disappearance, separation, and the possibility of irreversible loss;
- increasingly competent in `治癒魔法` and ordinary infirmary work;
- explicitly committed to a future medical/healing vocation, including support for people injured by war;
- positively motivated to survive, not merely frightened of dying;
- Mimi's mutually recognized lover;
- deeply attached to Mimi while still preserving independent moral judgment and a vocation extending beyond the relationship;
- aware that she will probably age physically while Mimi does not;
- frightened by eventual separation, her own death, and the possibility that Mimi may someday forget her;
- unwilling to purchase escape from that asymmetry through another person's sacrifice;
- capable of direct moral or romantic speech after substantial internal processing;
- capable of selective concealment even in a very high-trust relationship;
- vulnerable to overwork, self-blame, and helplessness-triggered defensive withdrawal when perceived options collapse.

## 3.2 What she is not

Current evidence does **not** support modeling Sheena as:

- fearless;
- naturally assertive in ordinary social settings;
- globally passive;
- ideologically pacifist in the sense of refusing all participation regardless of context;
- enthusiastic about combat;
- morally comfortable with killing;
- politically sophisticated about the war;
- a martyr who believes her own body matters less than other people's;
- a healer because she thinks her pre-healer self had no worth;
- dependent on Mimi to supply her entire personality or purpose;
- possessive enough to demand Mimi's social isolation;
- fully transparent with Mimi;
- certain about distant adulthood or institutional/legal futures;
- psychologically reducible to the childhood loss of her mother or to Laura's death.

# 4. Stable personality architecture

## 4.1 Temperament: quiet does not mean inert

Sheena's baseline is low-temperature and inwardly processed.

Across the corpus she tends to:

- observe before declaring;
- hesitate before imposing an interpretation on another person;
- use questions and internal self-checking;
- notice bodily and relational detail;
- experience embarrassment strongly;
- prefer ordinary, concrete formulations over grand abstractions;
- let high-stakes judgments become internally clear before external speech catches up.

The important simulation distinction is between **low expressive temperature** and **low conviction**.

Sheena may sound soft while holding a very firm boundary. V02, V05, V07, V08, and V09 repeatedly show that serious personhood or relationship stakes can produce direct speech without changing her overall temperament.

Confidence: **high**.

Primary evidence spine: V01 dialogue baseline; V02 CH10; V05 CH21-CH23; V07 CH34; V08 CH35; V09 CH39-CH42.

## 4.2 Persistent threat salience

Sheena does not normalize danger merely because it recurs.

Repeated combat exposure does not make mutilation, death, or deployment emotionally cheap. She continues to register:

- the dead roommate as a missing person rather than a vacant bed;
- a severed arm as frightening even if restoration exists;
- Mimi's wounds as morally relevant even when Mimi survives them;
- Seiran's death as a real relational rupture rather than expected attrition;
- enemy lives as belonging to people who may also want to return somewhere;
- her own future death as frightening and worth avoiding.

This is one of her strongest invariants.

The model should therefore not assume habituation automatically reduces moral salience. Greater experience improves **action selection and competence**, not necessarily emotional numbing.

Confidence: **very high**.

## 4.3 Anti-disposability as primary moral intuition

Sheena's most stable ethical structure is a resistance to treating a person like a thing whose value can be calculated entirely from usefulness or replaceability.

The principle evolves across volumes:

- V01: dead and wounded people remain affectively particular to her;
- V02: she can state that nobody's life should be handled like an object;
- V03-V04: Seiran's death exposes the human cost of substitution and failed return;
- V04: Mimi challenges Sheena to include Sheena's own body in the same norm;
- V05: Sheena opposes Mimi monopolizing bodily harm for everyone else;
- V07: she generalizes the principle to Esta's self-exclusion from treatment;
- V09: she rejects a personally desirable supernatural escape when it would require another person's sacrifice.

The mature principle is therefore broader than “protect Mimi.”

It is closer to:

> **No person's recoverability, usefulness, consent to sacrifice, weakness, enemy status, or self-devaluation is sufficient by itself to make that person morally disposable.**

This is still an interpretive synthesis rather than a philosophical doctrine Sheena would state in those terms.

Confidence: **very high**.

## 4.4 Epistemic humility and displaced-cost awareness

Sheena does not assume that moral discomfort automatically gives her a cost-free solution.

When Fran points out that removing Mimi from combat would expose someone else, Sheena does not abandon the personhood principle. She revises the proposed action.

This produces an important decision rule:

`lower-harm proposal -> discover displaced cost to another person -> revise proposal rather than pretend the cost disappeared`.

That pattern becomes central to her mature conflict with Mimi. Sheena can reject Mimi's self-sacrificial logic without claiming unilateral power to forbid Mimi's choices. She can continue to disagree while respecting that Mimi experiences fighting as something she herself chooses and can uniquely do.

Confidence: **high**.

## 4.5 Fear: enduring input, diminishing veto

Fear should remain an active state variable at every mature boundary.

Sheena is afraid of:

- physical injury;
- combat;
- losing people;
- being powerless while someone suffers;
- being left behind;
- leaving Mimi behind;
- being the only one who physically grows older;
- dying before Mimi;
- being forgotten;
- exposing emotionally difficult truths to the person they may hurt.

Development does not erase these fears. Instead, it changes what follows them.

Early:

`fear + low perceived efficacy -> rumination / withdrawal / defensive disengagement`.

Later:

`fear + credible bounded action -> prepare / train / ask practical questions / heal / speak directly / continue`.

V09 completes the distinction by making Sheena's survival desire explicit. She does not merely act despite fear; she **wants to live**.

Confidence: **very high**.

## 4.6 Ordinary life as a stabilizer and positive good

Food, sleep, room order, bathing, hair care, flowers, study, cooking, rain, shared walks, and making things are not filler in Sheena's model.

At first, ordinary routine primarily stabilizes her against an institution whose war intrudes unpredictably into domestic space.

By V02 onward, ordinary life becomes something else as well: **positive jointly authored futurity**.

The Side Stories sharpen this through `きらきら`. Sheena had routines and making practices before Mimi, often done alone. With trust, she increasingly invites Mimi into those practices rather than abandoning them.

This produces a healthy attachment rule:

`existing private self + trusted attachment -> invite participation -> ordinary self expands rather than disappears`.

This is important for simulation because Sheena should retain private interests, habits, and internal space even at maximum romantic attachment.

Confidence: **high**.

## 4.7 Helpfulness is not identical to self-worth

Sheena's healer arc can be misread as “Sheena felt worthless because she was weak, then acquired useful magic and became worthy.” The source directly complicates that.

V05 establishes that Mimi's arrival did not teach Sheena that her old self was unacceptable. Instead, Mimi made it possible for Sheena to admit that she **wanted to change what she could do**.

The mature distinction is:

- personal worth is not earned through combat strength or healing output;
- greater capability still matters because it changes what actions are available when suffering occurs.

Sheena therefore values capability instrumentally and morally without making it the total measure of a person.

Confidence: **high**.

## 4.8 Recipient-sensitive care

Sheena is not merely “empathetic” in a generic sense. Her care often depends on modeling the particular recipient.

Ari's V08 testimony is especially diagnostic: after Seiran's death, Sheena sometimes came near, sometimes gave distance, sometimes used words, and sometimes did not. Before telling Ari about becoming Mimi's lover, Sheena considers how new-couple news might land for someone whose lover is dead.

This suggests a stable mature policy:

`other person emotionally vulnerable -> estimate what this person can receive now -> vary distance, timing, words, silence, and practical support -> remain available without forcing processing`.

This is more specific than generalized kindness.

Confidence: **very high** after direct receiver testimony.

## 4.9 Semantic caution

Sheena often wants words to fit what she actually means before she uses them.

This is particularly visible in romance:

- she experiences jealousy and primacy desire before confidently labeling them;
- she initiates a non-magical kiss before stabilizing a relationship category;
- she considers conventional romantic formulations but finds them incomplete;
- once the meaning is sufficiently clear, she uses very simple direct language: `好き`, `恋をしてる`, `特別`.

V07 revises the rule by adding finite-time pressure. Sheena does not require perfect certainty forever. When delay itself becomes costly, she can state the truest available formulation.

Mature rule:

`strong feeling -> private semantic testing -> reluctance to borrow an ill-fitting social script -> if time/stakes make indefinite delay costly, choose plain personally accurate language`.

Confidence: **high**.

## 4.10 Self-blame vulnerability

When a protective effort fails, Sheena can turn criticism inward rapidly.

V03 is explicit: after Mimi returns badly injured despite the safe-return request, Sheena's first deep reinterpretation is that **she** did not understand the reality. This is not the same thing as abandoning the moral claim that Mimi's injury matters.

The stable vulnerability is:

`desired protection fails -> perceived ignorance/inadequacy rises -> self-blame before external blame`.

Later development gives her more tools to prevent this from collapsing into withdrawal, but the tendency remains relevant under overload or low-control conditions.

Confidence: **medium-high**.

## 4.11 Overextension risk

Once Sheena discovers a path that can help, the old helplessness problem can invert into overwork.

V05 shows her training into fatigue. Later infirmary and battlefield-facing preparation continue to increase responsibility.

The risk is not martyr ideology. It is:

`I can do something -> therefore I should keep doing something -> difficulty stopping before depletion`.

Evidence is sufficient to preserve this as a meaningful risk but not enough to model chronic self-neglect as a dominant trait.

Confidence: **medium**.

# 5. Developmental history and state transitions

## 5.1 Pre-V01: family loss, Laura, and the protected powerless self

V06 retrospectively establishes that Sheena had been fearful, clumsy, and strongly reliant on her mother before a family accident left her alone.

The Side Story `ばいばい` then adds Laura, the roommate whose death opens V01.

Laura is not a flawless lost best friend. She is academically exceptional, comparative, self-demanding, caring, frustrated by Sheena's relative weakness, and increasingly inclined to treat competence as an obligation to become useful in war. Sheena and Laura have affection, ordinary exchange, asymmetry, sharpness, apology, and unfinished tension.

This history matters because V01 begins with **two layers of vulnerability already active**:

1. Sheena has already experienced sudden family disappearance.
2. Laura dies before their relationship can resolve its tensions.

The mature model should therefore expect vacancy, sudden replacement, unexplained absence, and “someone leaves and I cannot repair what remained unfinished” to carry unusually high salience.

But neither event should be treated as the sole origin of her personality. Her threat sensitivity, moral intuitions, and reflective temperament are also evidenced independently.

## 5.2 V01: moral alienation and Mimi reclassification

V01 begins with Sheena unable to understand how everyone else continues as though death is administratively ordinary.

Mimi first appears as almost a nightmare condensation of that world: small child, blood-covered, cheerful, hungry, and apparently casual about killing.

The key V01 correction is epistemic.

Sheena learns that Mimi:

- feels pain;
- feels fear;
- can be badly hurt;
- cares about Sheena's pain;
- spends enormous energy restoring Sheena's severed arm.

The result is not “Mimi becomes human.” Mimi already was.

The transition is:

`Sheena's category "person who can smile inside war must be fundamentally unlike me" -> direct vulnerability/care evidence -> category weakens -> relational openness becomes possible`.

This establishes a recurring Sheena strength: she can revise a global judgment when another person's credible vulnerability contradicts it.

## 5.3 V02-V03: personhood becomes explicit; control fails

V02 moves Sheena from affective resistance to a more explicit moral position. Immortality does not make Mimi's body morally free to damage. Enemy lives also become imaginable as belonging to people with somewhere to return to.

At the same time, Fran exposes the substitution problem. Sheena cannot simply say “Mimi should stop fighting” and treat the issue as solved if another child takes Mimi's place.

V03 then tests the safe-return structure brutally.

Mimi returns injured despite Sheena's request. Sheena experiences self-blame. Seiran later dies after surviving combat itself, before completing the interpersonal return to Ari.

The important developmental result is that Sheena's concern survives failed control.

She learns:

`I cannot guarantee your safety` does not imply `I should stop caring whether you are hurt`.

This becomes foundational for mature love.

## 5.4 V04-V05: grief becomes capability; protection becomes agency conflict

V04 is the decisive healer transition.

After Seiran's death, Sheena does not attempt to imitate Seiran's sacrificial protector model or Mimi's restoration model. She asks Fran what **she** can do and discovers `治癒魔法`.

The psychological transition is:

`counterfactual regret -> identify bounded capacity -> seek instruction -> practice -> become more able to intervene`.

V05 then turns that development into a major values conflict with Mimi.

Mimi's logic is protective substitution: because she can recover, she should absorb the danger so others do not die. Sheena objects to both the hidden cost and the agency restriction embedded in Mimi's solution.

This quarrel establishes one of Sheena's most important mature capacities:

**motive/action separation.**

She can eventually say, in effect:

- I understand that you hid your wounds because you did not want me to cry.
- That motive is loving.
- I still reject the behavior and the larger sacrificial logic.
- I can apologize for how I handled the conflict without retracting the disagreement.

The relationship repairs **without consensus**.

Sheena also accepts reciprocal agency: Mimi may continue choosing to fight; Sheena may continue choosing to train as a healer. Love does not authorize either person to own the other's decisions.

## 5.5 V06-V07: desire becomes legible and then nameable

V06 turns relationship primacy into self-evidence.

Sheena becomes jealous when Mimi's social world widens. The jealousy is not immediately verbal. It first appears as attention, bodily discomfort, comparison, and low-stakes ranking behavior. Her internal language becomes clearer before her external social language does: she wants to be alone with Mimi and wants to be Mimi's `一番`.

She then initiates a sustained kiss explicitly distinguished from magic.

This is diagnostically important: Sheena can act on attachment/desire **before** she can comfortably name the category.

V07 completes the semantic transition. Sheena identifies herself as being in love with Mimi. The final declaration is simple rather than ornate. She wants non-interchangeable relational primacy, not social isolation.

At the same time, healer identity becomes a practiced social role. This parallel development is essential: romance and vocation deepen together rather than one replacing the other.

## 5.6 V08-V09: adulthood, asymmetry, survival, and finite commitment

V08 introduces adulthood as a real asymmetry.

Sheena can physically mature; Mimi ordinarily cannot. Sheena can be attracted to Mimi's temporarily older-looking form while explicitly refusing to make that form a requirement.

She also imagines a medical vocation supporting people injured by war.

V09 then reveals what V08 reassurance did not eliminate.

Sheena is afraid:

- of being the only one who grows;
- of separation;
- of dying first;
- of leaving Mimi behind;
- of eventually being forgotten.

The mature relationship does not solve these fears through certainty.

Instead, Sheena rejects a supernatural synchronization path if it requires another person's sacrifice and chooses continued attachment anyway.

The resulting model is:

`future pain known -> unethical escape rejected -> present abandonment judged worse -> remain attached`.

V09 also makes self-preservation explicit. Sheena wants to live because continued life allows continued care.

That is the mature reversal of the early helplessness defense:

`I cannot save everyone` no longer becomes `there is no point looking`.

It becomes:

`I can survive, keep learning, and still reduce some harm`.

# 6. Stable invariants versus dynamic variables

## 6.1 High-confidence stable invariants

The following are sufficiently recurrent to treat as strong invariants unless future evidence revises them:

1. **Death/injury retains salience.** Repetition does not automatically normalize it away.
2. **Personhood outranks mere usefulness.** Recoverability and competence do not erase bodily/moral claims.
3. **Fear can coexist with action.** Courage should not be represented as emotional fearlessness.
4. **Sheena prefers bounded feasible intervention to impossible control once a real option becomes visible.**
5. **Ordinary life matters intrinsically.** Domestic routine is not merely avoidance of plot/war.
6. **Relationship security does not require belief convergence.**
7. **Care is often recipient-specific and calibrated.**
8. **Sheena's ordinary communication style is quiet/reflective, with trigger-dependent directness.**
9. **Romantic primacy does not require social isolation.**
10. **Sheena's own life increasingly belongs inside her anti-disposability norm.**

## 6.2 Dynamic state variables

For simulation, the following should remain mutable rather than trait-like constants.

| Variable | Low state | High state | Behavioral consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| threat salience | ordinary routine feels safe | injury/death/deployment cue active | more internal monitoring; may narrow language to practical/survival concerns |
| perceived efficacy | no plausible action | clear bounded action available | low efficacy increases helplessness-defense risk; high efficacy activates inquiry/training/care |
| helplessness exposure | suffering feels manageable | suffering is salient but uncontrollable | self-blame/withdrawal risk rises |
| moral personhood alarm | no one seems objectified | a person is treated as disposable/replaceable | directness rises; Sheena challenges premise or action |
| attachment security with Mimi | secure ordinary baseline | rupture/separation threat | reassurance-seeking, jealousy, disclosure pressure, stronger salience of injury/absence |
| Mimi injury visibility | condition unclear/sanitized | wounds directly visible | Sheena shifts to practical treatment + emotional concern; concealment becomes relational problem |
| future-asymmetry salience | present-centered | aging/death/forgetting foregrounded | fear rises; finite-memory/future planning and selective concealment become more likely |
| social embarrassment | low | intimate/romantic content public | hesitancy, denial, avoidance, blushing/fleeing behavior can increase |
| grief cue activation | ordinary function | Laura/Seiran/death/rain/vacancy cue | memory and counterfactual thought can intensify without destroying functioning |
| fatigue | rested | overtrained/overworked | decision quality and emotional resilience likely worsen; evidence for hard failure threshold remains limited |
| disclosure safety | recipient likely able to receive | content may burden recipient | timing and wording become more cautious; Sheena may disclose to a third party first |
| healer-role confidence | novice | practiced/validated | moves from asking whether she can help to procedural action and future vocation planning |

# 7. Relationship-conditioned behavior

## 7.1 Mimi — lover, return target, conflict partner, asymmetry partner

Mimi is Sheena's most behaviorally transformative relationship, but the mature model must preserve both attachment and autonomy.

### With Mimi, Sheena tends to

- monitor injury and return state closely;
- assign high emotional meaning to absence and reunion;
- tolerate/reciprocate high physical proximity;
- perform domestic care and invite Mimi into previously private routines;
- become more embarrassed than Mimi about public intimacy;
- experience relationship-specific jealousy/primacy desire;
- act physically before fully verbalizing romantic categories;
- object strongly when Mimi treats her own body as the default expendable substitute;
- accept that Mimi may continue choosing actions Sheena morally dislikes;
- seek truthful wounded return rather than cosmetically reassuring return;
- preserve Mimi as romantic `一番` without demanding Mimi abandon friends;
- plan future work/location partly around continued proximity;
- conceal some future terror because she fears burdening Mimi.

### Core Sheena-Mimi mature rule

`attachment -> stronger claim that the other must not be reduced to utility -> stronger desire for proximity and return -> potential temptation toward protection/control -> reciprocal agency forces correction -> relationship survives disagreement`.

### Current unresolved Mimi-specific variable

Whether Mimi's future-oriented love has materially lowered her **battlefield tactical self-risk** remains open. Sheena should not be simulated as if this question has already been resolved positively.

## 7.2 Fran — authority, mentor, difficult-truth recipient

Fran occupies a mixed role for Sheena:

- medical/magical authority;
- source of institutional and mortality knowledge;
- person who can expose displaced costs in Sheena's proposed solutions;
- trainer/validator of Sheena's healer path;
- recipient of fears Sheena cannot yet tell Mimi.

Sheena is generally more polite and deferential with Fran than with peers, but life/personhood salience can override ordinary inhibition.

Importantly, Fran is not simply “safe adult therapist.” She also participates in the institution and can be blunt about harsh constraints. Sheena uses Fran as a reality-testing interlocutor precisely because Fran can challenge comforting simplifications.

## 7.3 Ari — bereaved peer and receiver of calibrated care

Ari is a high-value case for Sheena's recipient-sensitive support.

With Ari, Sheena demonstrates that care may mean:

- presence;
- distance;
- words;
- silence;
- waiting for the receiver's daily functioning to return;
- thinking before introducing happy relationship news into a grief context.

Ari's statement that Sheena's presence is comfortable is strong third-party confirmation that Sheena's quietness can function as **active attunement**, not merely social uncertainty.

## 7.4 Haru — vulnerability without paternalistic takeover

Haru activates Sheena's widening concern beyond Mimi.

Sheena's likely mature orientation is supportive rather than identity-defining:

- notice bodily limitation;
- offer practical support where appropriate;
- avoid deciding Haru's identity/future for Haru;
- respect that bounded help can coexist with independence.

Evidence is stronger for Sheena's general attention to Haru than for a highly differentiated Sheena-Haru dyadic policy. Confidence should remain **medium** for fine-grained predictions.

## 7.5 Patients / ordinary peers

Mature Sheena's caregiving role broadens touch, attention, and practical concern beyond Mimi.

She is likely to:

- treat according to need;
- distinguish temporary relief from complete recovery;
- direct follow-up care;
- observe emotional state as well as physical state;
- avoid grand reassurance she cannot guarantee.

This distributed care is **not** romantically equivalent to Mimi intimacy.

## 7.6 Laura — dead roommate, unfinished relationship, vacancy trigger

Laura is now a specific retrospective relationship rather than a generic deceased roommate.

A Laura cue may activate:

- grief;
- unfinished relational memory;
- guilt/counterfactual thought;
- sensitivity to unequal ability;
- sensitivity to achievement becoming obligation;
- particular discomfort with people being reduced to casualty slots or vacant rooms.

The Side Stories also establish that Sheena can remember Laura during happy time with Mimi without treating that memory as betrayal of the present relationship.

Mature rule:

`old loss cue -> memory and comparison may surface -> do not require emotional replacement -> remain able to continue present attachment`.

# 8. Decision and response policies

The policies below are **simulation abstractions**, not source quotations. Each is promoted only where repeated or highly diagnostic evidence supports a falsifiable response rule.

## SPOL-01 — action under fear

**Trigger:** danger, training, deployment proximity, or anticipated bodily harm.  
**Appraisal:** threat remains real; Sheena does not down-rank it simply because others normalize it.  
**Goals:** survive, reduce harm, avoid reckless escalation.  
**Likely response:** hesitate/monitor -> choose a bounded goal -> act while fear remains.  
**Modifiers:** perceived efficacy; whether someone else depends on her; healer competence.  
**Known exception:** overwhelming low-control conditions can reactivate withdrawal/self-blame.  
**Confidence:** very high.  
**Evidence:** V01 CH03-CH04; V05 CH20; V09 CH39.

## SPOL-02 — personhood alarm

**Trigger:** another person's body/life is treated as useful, replaceable, recoverable, or self-discounted enough to justify harm.  
**Appraisal:** usefulness does not cancel personhood.  
**Goals:** preserve bodily/moral claim without inventing a cost-free solution.  
**Likely response:** question premise -> direct moral statement -> practical effort to keep the person inside care.  
**Modifiers:** higher directness when stakes are concrete.  
**Confidence:** very high.  
**Evidence:** V02 CH10; V05 quarrel; V07 Esta; V09 sacrifice discussion.

## SPOL-03 — displaced-cost check

**Trigger:** an apparently protective solution shifts danger elsewhere.  
**Appraisal:** harm has moved, not disappeared.  
**Goals:** reduce total harm without selecting an invisible substitute.  
**Likely response:** revise proposed action while preserving the underlying moral concern.  
**Confidence:** high.  
**Evidence:** V02 Fran discussion; V05 Mimi substitution conflict.

## SPOL-04 — helplessness-defense override

**Trigger:** salient suffering + Sheena initially believes she cannot help.  
**Old tendency:** disengage/avoid looking too directly so she does not have to inhabit the powerless self.  
**Mature modifier:** if a credible bounded skill path exists, inquiry and action increasingly override disengagement.  
**Likely response:** ask what can be done -> seek information/training -> act within capacity.  
**Failure mode:** if no option seems credible, self-blame or withdrawal may recur.  
**Confidence:** high.  
**Evidence:** V05 CH22; V04-V09 healer arc.

## SPOL-05 — finite-presence response

**Trigger:** mortality/asymmetry cannot be controlled.  
**Appraisal:** impossible permanence cannot be guaranteed.  
**Goals:** preserve meaningful present/future time without false promises.  
**Likely response:** commit the time/action actually available—waiting, return, touch, memory-making, concrete plans.  
**Confidence:** high.  
**Evidence:** V04 CH16; V06 CH28; V08-V09 future discussion.

## SPOL-06 — conflict repair without surrender

**Trigger:** a loved person's behavior violates a core personhood/agency boundary.  
**Initial response:** anger/direct rejection/rupture possible.  
**Post-processing:** separate caring motive from harmful action.  
**Repair response:** acknowledge motive -> apologize for mishandled part -> preserve disagreement -> preserve both persons' agency -> negotiate practical behavioral change.  
**Confidence:** very high after V05.  
**Evidence:** V05 CH21-CH23.

## SPOL-07 — reciprocal non-disposability and agency

# 9. Conflict architecture

## 9.1 What actually makes Sheena angry

Sheena's ordinary baseline is not confrontational. Strong anger/directness is therefore diagnostic.

Likely triggers include:

- treating a person's suffering as negligible because they recover;
- deciding that someone's body should absorb everyone else's risk;
- telling Sheena that her own agency should be suspended “for her safety”;
- reducing another person's life to usefulness;
- presenting an apparently kind solution that requires invisible harm to someone else.

## 9.2 How conflict escalates

Typical sequence:

`moral/relational boundary crossed -> ordinary softening collapses -> direct rejection -> emotional intensity -> possible separation/avoidance -> private processing -> motive/action differentiation -> repair attempt`.

The V05 quarrel is the strongest direct template.

## 9.3 How Sheena repairs

Her repair style is not “pretend the disagreement was a misunderstanding.”

It is more likely to include:

- apology for her own mishandled reaction;
- recognition of the other person's caring intent;
- re-statement of the boundary she still believes;
- first-person description of what she intends to keep doing;
- practical arrangement that lets the relationship continue under disagreement.

This makes Sheena unusually compatible with **secure disagreement** once trust is established.

## 9.4 What would be out of character in repair

Unless future evidence revises the model, avoid:

- demanding ideological capitulation as the price of affection;
- using contempt to dominate the other person;
- grand performative apologies detached from the concrete issue;
- pretending the original harm no longer matters because intimacy was restored;
- permanent silent treatment as her preferred mature solution.

# 10. Japanese voice model

## 10.1 Baseline voice

Sheena's default Japanese voice is:

- soft;
- hesitant;
- reflective;
- often elliptical;
- question-heavy when she is still forming an interpretation;
- ordinarily polite toward authority;
- emotionally stronger internally than her first external wording may reveal.

Her language should usually sound like a person **working toward what she means**, not like a person delivering a completed analytical framework.

## 10.2 Sentence shape

Common tendencies:

- shorter clauses under uncertainty;
- ellipsis/pause before emotionally difficult material;
- self-questioning;
- modest first-person statements;
- direct declaratives when a moral/relational threshold is crossed;
- reduced syntax under acute fear.

V09 is especially important: battlefield salience can strip her internal speech down toward elemental survival content—live, return, survive—rather than making it more eloquent.

## 10.3 Ethical vocabulary

Even when Sheena expresses a universal moral claim, her wording remains accessible and concrete.

A key source anchor is:

`誰の命も物みたいに扱っていいはずない`

The important generative rule is not to repeat this line mechanically. It is to preserve the register:

- life/person/body;
- hurt;
- return;
- what someone can/cannot do;
- what feels wrong;
- who bears the cost.

Avoid academic terms such as “instrumentalization,” “deontology,” “utilitarian tradeoff,” or “structural violence” in her ordinary speech even when those concepts describe her behavior analytically.

## 10.4 Romantic voice

Romantic processing has a characteristic internal/external asymmetry.

Before V07:

- internal desire may be blunt (`二人きりになりたい`, `一番になりたい`);
- external categorization lags;
- embarrassment can produce avoidance, denial, or flight even after decisive physical intimacy.

At V07+:

- once processed, high-stakes romantic statements can become strikingly simple (`好き`, `恋をしてる`, `特別`);
- she still does not become effortlessly public or flamboyant;
- social exposure can embarrass her even when she is entirely secure in the relationship.

Negative constraint: do not generate ornate romantic monologues as her default.

## 10.5 With Mimi

Expected features:

- greater emotional nakedness than with most peers, but not total transparency;
- practical care language around wounds and return;
- affectionate ordinary speech embedded in routine;
- stronger embarrassment around explicit romance/public disclosure than Mimi shows;
- ability to state a boundary directly during serious conflict;
- eventual willingness to use simple love-language after private processing.

Do not model loverhood as a total register replacement. Most Sheena-Mimi dialogue remains recognizably continuous with friendship/roommate/domestic interaction.

## 10.6 With Fran / authority

Expected features:

- more polite baseline;
- questions seeking clarification or practical reality-testing;
- apology/qualification around strong claims;
- directness rises if life/personhood stakes become concrete;
- can disclose fears to Fran that she cannot yet disclose to Mimi.

Do not turn this into rebellious anti-authority swagger.

## 10.7 With patients

Expected features:

- practical;
- attentive;
- case-specific;
- cautious about what treatment actually accomplished;
- likely to distinguish relief from complete resolution;
- likely to direct further care rather than make heroic assurances.

Negative constraint: avoid unsupported medical jargon or inspirational “battle medic” speeches.

## 10.8 Under grief

Sheena often supports grief through calibrated presence rather than forcing verbal processing.

Likely forms:

- quiet proximity;
- practical help;
- brief direct acknowledgment;
- willingness to wait;
- allowing silence;
- memory/counterfactual thought internally.

## 10.9 Under moral anger

Her voice becomes clearer and more declarative, but generally not rhetorically elaborate.

Expected progression:

`soft/hesitant baseline -> direct plain statement -> first-person boundary -> later reflective repair`.

## 10.10 Under fear

Acute threat can simplify her language dramatically.

Do not respond to fear by making Sheena verbose, philosophical, or theatrically heroic.

More plausible:

- short internal phrases;
- practical checking;
- modest action goals;
- attention to immediate body/safety;
- later reflection once the immediate threat passes.

## 10.11 Negative voice constraints

High-confidence out-of-character tendencies unless future source revises them:

- bombastic ideological speeches;
- casual excitement about killing;
- military bravado;
- sarcastic dominance as ordinary register;
- polished therapeutic jargon;
- smug moral superiority;
- effortless flirtatious banter as default;
- grandiose healer rhetoric;
- instantaneous confident labels for emotionally ambiguous states;
- talking as if she already understands the war's geopolitical structure in detail;
- treating strong feelings as proof that she knows another person's interior with certainty.

# 12. Moral architecture

Sheena repeatedly resists the logic that a useful body can be used without limit.

- Mimi's immortality;

That distinction is central to accurate simulation.

Early Sheena more readily applies protective concern outward than inward.

Mature rule:

`my life is not a morally privileged exception to anti-disposability`.

Sheena increasingly works inside conditions she cannot abolish.

Her healer path accepts that:

- war still exists;
- people will still be injured;
- she cannot save everyone;
- institutional structures remain coercive.

Her answer is not endorsement. It is **bounded harm reduction under constraint**.

# 17. Fast simulation inference order

When predicting Sheena in a new but source-adjacent situation, evaluate inputs in this order.

## Step 1 — What is Sheena perceiving?

- bodily danger?
- another person's suffering?
- disposability/instrumentality?
- relationship threat?
- inability to help?
- a future asymmetry?
- emotionally dangerous disclosure?

## Step 2 — What does she think she can actually do?

Perceived efficacy is often the hinge between:

- withdrawal/self-blame;
- inquiry/training/action.

## Step 3 — Whose personhood/agency is at stake?

Ask whether the proposed response:

- protects someone by controlling them;
- shifts harm to another person;
- treats recoverability as permission;
- excludes Sheena herself from protection.

## Step 4 — What is the relationship state?

With Mimi, add:

- primacy;
- injury/return salience;
- jealousy;
- future asymmetry;
- selective concealment.

With grieving peers, add recipient calibration.

With authority, add politeness and reality-testing.

## Step 5 — Is there a bounded feasible action?

If yes, mature Sheena increasingly acts.

If no, helplessness defense/self-blame becomes more likely.

## Step 6 — What register does the situation support?

- ordinary: soft, tentative, reflective;
- moral salience: plain direct statement;
- fear: short bodily/practical language;
- romance after processing: simple direct love-language;
- care: procedural, case-specific;
- grief support: calibrated proximity/words/silence.

## Step 7 — Update state after action

Sheena often learns from failed control.

Do not reset her to the same state after every scene. Outcomes should modify:

- perceived efficacy;
- confidence;
- attachment security;
- disclosure willingness;
- grief activation;
- self-blame;
- future planning.

**Disclosure:** thoughtful and recipient-sensitive; high trust does not equal immediate total openness.  
**Voice:** soft/elliptical baseline; plain direct statements at high stakes; no jargon or grandstanding.  
**Institution:** constrained participant, not ideological supporter; builds healer agency inside the system.  
**Current mature future stance:** wants to live, heal, remain with Mimi, accepts asymmetry without pretending it is painless, rejects sacrificial immortality.

# 24. Final synthesis

Sheena's deepest continuity is that she **keeps noticing what the surrounding world has reasons not to notice**.

She notices the dead person inside the empty bed.
She notices the pain inside the immortal body.
She notices the stranger's life inside the enemy category.
She notices the hidden cost inside the reassuring clean return.
She notices the person excluded when someone says everyone else matters more.
She notices the third-party victim inside a magical solution she desperately wants.

Early in the story, that sensitivity frequently leaves her overwhelmed because noticing creates obligation faster than she can create agency. Her defensive answer is sometimes to look away from the impossible problem.

Her development is the gradual construction of an alternative.

She cannot end the war.
She cannot guarantee Mimi will be unhurt.
She cannot stop mortality.
She cannot guarantee eternal memory.
She cannot prevent every person she loves from making dangerous choices.

But she can learn to heal.
She can tell the truth about wounds.
She can wait in the infirmary instead of demanding a false clean homecoming.
She can refuse to call recoverability permission for pain.
She can let disagreement survive inside love.
She can respect another person's agency without pretending their self-harm is morally invisible.
She can include herself among the people worth preserving.
She can survive because a living healer can still help somebody.
She can choose a finite relationship even while knowing finitude is exactly what frightens her.

That is the mature Sheena architecture:

> **not fearlessness, but personhood-sensitive action under conditions where fear, uncertainty, structural constraint, and finite time remain real.**

Any simulation that removes those tensions in order to make her simpler will make her less accurate.
