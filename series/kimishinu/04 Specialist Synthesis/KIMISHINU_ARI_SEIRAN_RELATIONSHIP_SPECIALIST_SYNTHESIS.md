# KIMISHINU_ARI_SEIRAN_RELATIONSHIP_SPECIALIST_SYNTHESIS.md

## 1. Purpose, authority, and temporal rule

This is the canonical specialist synthesis for the relationship between **Ari / アリ** and **Rizzy Seiran / リジィ・セイラン** at the current KimiShinu analytical boundary.

Its responsibility is not to restate every Ari or Seiran character-state entry. It treats **the relationship itself as a changing causal system** across two fundamentally different temporal conditions:

1. a living reciprocal relationship whose last mainline living-dyad state is V03; and
2. a posthumous relationship that continues to organize Ari's grief, ethics, identity, memory, social behavior, and later agency through V09.

Later supplemental evidence, especially `SS06 ぴかぴか`, can deepen the mature reconstruction of the pre-death relationship. It must not be projected backward into what the prospective V01-V02 reader could already know.

The current integrated boundary is `V09+SIDE_STORIES`. The numbered prospective freeze remains V09.

### Hard simulation rule

> **Do not simulate post-V03 Seiran as though she survived.**

Living-dyad simulation is permitted only at source-consistent pre-death states or securely placed retrospective pre-death scenes. From V04 onward, the relationship remains analytically active through Ari and through source-confirmed memories, objects, testimony, and retrospective facts. It does not generate new Seiran choices, speech, or reactions unless later canon explicitly supplies them.

This distinction is not administrative trivia. It is part of the relationship's ontology. Ari–Seiran changes from a reciprocal dyad into a one-survivor relationship whose second participant remains particular and ethically consequential without remaining behaviorally available.

# 2. Executive dyadic thesis

The Ari–Seiran relationship is best understood as **mutual seeing that becomes protective asymmetry under war, followed by posthumous love that gradually learns not to become ownership**.

The relationship does not begin with sacrifice.

Its quiet substrate is attention.

Seiran watches Ari closely enough to distinguish socially managed brightness from more spontaneous ease. She notices ordinary bodily and behavioral details: hair, skin, hands, posture, eating, movement, expression. Ari does not merely tolerate this gaze. She actively wants it. In `ぴかぴか`, she asks Seiran to look at her more and wants Seiran specifically to think she is cute.

The early dyadic structure is therefore:

`Seiran notices Ari in fine detail <-> Ari wants to be especially seen and valued by Seiran`.

Ari reciprocates in a different medium. She notices the private person inside Seiran's serious public role. She facilitates the things Seiran quietly wants: adornment, flowers, appearance play, shared sleep, kisses, teasing, aesthetic pleasure, ordinary private life. Where Seiran's attention says **I see how you actually are**, Ari's care says **you may be more than the role you perform**.

This would already be a meaningful relationship architecture in an ordinary setting. KimiShinu places it inside an institution where children are trained, evaluated, deployed, injured, and killed.

That environment transforms Seiran's person-specific vigilance into a dangerous protective rule:

`Ari becomes precious -> Ari bleeding in war becomes intolerable -> Seiran prefers herself as the substitute -> Seiran seeks greater combat competence and accepts greater bodily risk`.

The crucial point is that Seiran is not merely ordered into this logic. She sincerely wants to become useful. She experiences the school as having rescued her from precarity and likely lonely death, and she wants to repay it. Yet her deepest martial motive becomes relational: she cannot bear the image of Ari bleeding.

Love therefore does not simply make Seiran safer.

It **militarizes her self-conception**.

Ari's corresponding problem is almost the inverse. She respects Seiran's chosen strength, supports her, tells her she is proud, gives her a portable safe-return object, and repairs her moral self-conception after the first kill. Ari is not passive. She actively helps Seiran remain recognizably human inside combat.

But Ari's respect creates its own danger. Her strongest attachment-protective preference remains largely unvoiced while Seiran is alive. After Seiran's death, Ari admits the wish she had denied herself:

`行かないで` — don't go.

The relationship's living tragedy is therefore not that either girl failed to care.

They care intensely.

The problem is that their care policies do not fully confront one another before war makes confrontation impossible.

- Seiran translates love into **self-substitution**.
- Ari translates love into **support of Seiran's chosen identity**, while suppressing her veto.
- Ari can repair Seiran's shame after killing.
- Ari can ask for safe return.
- Ari can make ordinary life available.
- But Ari never receives the later opportunity Sheena and Mimi receive in V05: a sustained quarrel in which protection and agency can be made explicit, contested, and renegotiated.

Seiran's final return attempt is the formal proof that attachment has real causal force without magical sovereignty.

Under catastrophic injury, Ari becomes `帰らなきゃ` — the person/place Seiran must return to. Seiran survives enemy contact, helps another wounded student, keeps moving, joins the return march, and still dies before reaching Ari.

Thus:

> **love motivates return; love does not guarantee reunion.**

After death, the relationship changes form rather than becoming analytically inert.

Ari initially treats composure as loyalty to Seiran's agency. If Seiran knowingly chose danger, Ari feels pressure to prove that she understood and therefore should not collapse. Mimi interrupts that false equivalence: respecting Seiran's choice does not mean Ari is forbidden to be sad.

Then resurrection turns grief into an ownership test.

Ari wants Seiran back and still refuses to impose resurrection because Seiran's life belongs to Seiran. This is one of the series' clearest formulations of posthumous autonomy:

> **the intensity of the survivor's desire does not create ownership of the dead person's existence.**

Ari later develops a second posthumous policy. She will continue living as **the self Seiran loved**. This does not mean preserving every habit or obeying every presumed preference. It means refusing to let grief erase the person who was loved.

V09 completes the current arc by testing whether posthumous love has become a permanent command. Ari chooses to take the combat proficiency exam despite believing Seiran would be hurt by that decision. She knows Seiran is no longer waiting in that world. She also admits that some part of her had been waiting to be called.

The mature relationship therefore arrives at a difficult but coherent endpoint:

`love preserves particularity -> memory remains active -> grief remains legitimate -> Seiran's values continue to matter -> Ari's future remains Ari's`.

The deepest relationship thesis is:

> **Ari's fidelity to Seiran ultimately becomes fidelity to the fact that they were two persons, not one will.**

That principle is already latent while Seiran lives and becomes explicit only after death. Ari refuses to rewrite Seiran's life through resurrection, and eventually refuses to let her own life be frozen by what she believes Seiran would have wanted.

# 4. The relationship's three regimes

The cleanest way to model Ari–Seiran is not as one continuous state but as three regimes.

## 4.1 Regime I — perceptual and ordinary-life formation

Before the terminal military arc, the relationship is built through **fine-grained mutual recognition**.

Seiran's characteristic contribution is sustained observation.

Ari's characteristic contribution is making room for Seiran's less public self.

This produces a reciprocal structure:

`Seiran sees beneath Ari's socially usable brightness`

and

`Ari sees beneath Seiran's serious usefulness-oriented presentation`.

The two are not psychologically identical, but each provides the other with a mode of personhood that is less reducible to school function.

## 4.2 Regime II — living protection under war

War turns the relationship's attunement into action.

For Seiran:

`Ari vulnerability -> self-substitution -> seek strength -> accept pain/risk -> battlefield exposure`.

For Ari:

`Seiran chosen role -> support and affirm -> protect moral self after violence -> ask for return -> suppress stronger veto`.

The system is affectionate and mutually supportive, but it contains an unresolved asymmetry: Seiran's protection increasingly uses her own body as the expendable variable, while Ari's respect for Seiran's agency makes direct prohibition difficult.

## 4.3 Regime III — posthumous ethical continuity

Once Seiran dies, the relationship cannot continue through reciprocal new behavior.

It continues through:

- grief;
- memory;
- objects;
- remembered speech;
- routines;
- retrospective clarification;
- Ari's ethical decisions;
- Ari's identity;
- Ari's interpretation of what Seiran would think;
- Ari's eventual willingness to diverge from those presumed wishes.

This regime must be modeled as **Ari-with-Seiran-as-particular-dead-beloved**, not as a two-agent live system.

# 5. Chronological relationship architecture

## 5.1 V01 — close dyad, high familiarity, prospective romantic caution

V01 establishes Ari and Seiran as highly familiar, physically close, socially coordinated, and comfortable with intimate restorative contact.

Because the world uses kiss-like contact for healing, the prospective reading correctly avoids converting every intimate gesture into independent proof of romance.

At the V01 boundary, the correct classification is:

`close/intimate dyad; romance plausible; explicit status open`.

This uncertainty is historical, not an error to erase after V08.

## 5.2 V02 — love, death, and resurrection become thinkable together

V02 strengthens romantic plausibility through discussion of resurrecting a beloved person. The dyad begins to function as an early thematic site where love and mortality are considered together.

The later importance is striking: V02 entertains resurrection as a counterfactual around love; V04 forces Ari to make an actual decision about resurrection after Seiran dies.

The mature arc therefore turns an abstract relational question into an autonomy test.

## 5.3 `SS06 ぴかぴか` — the pre-sacrifice substrate: seeing and being seen

`ぴかぴか` is supplemental, publication-late evidence securely placed before Seiran's V03 death, likely in the V01-V02 developmental range. Exact chapter placement remains unresolved.

Its value is not to prove romance, because V08 already confirms the dating relationship. Its value is to reconstruct **how their specialness operates before war dominates the frame**.

Seiran observes Ari with unusual precision. She tracks small changes in appearance, bodily state, expression, and behavior. Most importantly, she notices that Ari's frequent smiles are not all psychologically identical.

Ari's brightness can function as:

- social smoothing;
- accessible friendliness;
- reassurance;
- habit;
- genuine amusement;
- intimate dearmoring.

Seiran is unusually sensitive to the distinction.

This corrects a flat Ari model. Ari is socially fluent, but fluency is not perfect transparency.

The relationship becomes reciprocal when Ari explicitly asks for Seiran's gaze:

`私のこと いっぱい見て`

and

`可愛いって あなたにもっと 思ってほしいから`.

Ari wants to be looked at, and she wants this particular person's valuation.

Seiran's response establishes that she has been looking from the beginning.

The mature dyadic grammar is therefore:

`Seiran sustained attention -> recognizes subtle Ari state`

`Ari experiences Seiran's attention as desired intimacy -> solicits more`

`mutual specialness deepens through perceptual particularity`.

This is not decorative romance material. It becomes causal context for Seiran's later protection. The battlefield rule `Ari vulnerable -> absorb burden` grows from a quieter preexisting habit: **Ari's state matters enough to Seiran that small changes are already highly salient**.

## 5.4 V03 CH12 — institutional debt and relational motive converge

Seiran wants the combat evaluation. Her agency should not be erased.

She understands the school as having rescued her from severe precarity and likely lonely death. It gave her shelter, training, capability, present identity, and the social world in which she met Ari. She therefore wants to repay the institution by becoming useful with the capacities it gave her.

This creates a genuine authored motive.

It also creates a dependency-shaped motive.

The relationship matters because Ari is inside the life the institution made possible. Gratitude to the institution and attachment to Ari are not separable variables.

The causal stack is:

`past precarity -> school experienced as rescuer -> gratitude/debt -> usefulness ethic -> Ari becomes central good within school life -> protecting Ari becomes deeper martial motive`.

Seiran is therefore neither a coerced puppet nor a freely choosing adult outside structural pressure.

## 5.5 V03 CH13 — love becomes self-substitution

Seiran states the deeper motive directly:

`アリが 戦争で血を流す姿なんて 絶対に見たくないから`.

She cannot bear the image of Ari bleeding in war.

Her solution is not to reject war. It is to become stronger so that **she** can take Ari's share.

This produces one of KimiShinu's clearest demonstrations that love can increase self-risk:

`Ari more valuable -> Ari injury more intolerable -> Seiran body becomes preferred substitute -> Seiran seeks more battlefield competence/exposure`.

The important comparison is not “Seiran loves too much.”

The important mechanism is **protective concentration**. She treats harm as something that can be morally improved by relocating it onto herself.

Because Seiran is mortal, this cannot be explained by Mimi-style recoverability. It is a more general relationship-protective self-expenditure pattern.

## 5.6 The departure kiss — romantic evidence becomes prospective, not only retrospective

Before actual deployment, Ari stops Seiran and kisses her in a non-healing context.

This removes the earlier healing-contact confound. By V03, reciprocal romantic/intimate classification is strongly supported even though the explicit `つき合ってる` retrospective confirmation does not arrive until V08.

The departure ritual also shows Ari's characteristic compromise:

- she does not stop Seiran;
- she marks the separation intimately;
- she sends attachment with Seiran into the danger zone.

## 5.7 First kill — Seiran's martial self-model breaks

Seiran's first actual kill creates a discontinuity between chosen training and embodied violence.

She acts as trained when threatened. The enemy dies violently. Seiran shakes, becomes frightened, and is stunned by how easily a human body can be destroyed.

The deeper injury is interpretive.

Seiran treats her own horror as evidence of weakness and uselessness because she cannot function as smoothly as more habituated peers.

The chain is:

`kill -> fear/horror -> impaired performance -> compare against normalized combat ideal -> shame -> self-condemnation`.

This is why Ari's response is a major relationship function rather than generic comfort.

## 5.8 Ari's moral repair — protecting Seiran from the institution inside Seiran

Ari rejects Seiran's equation of humane hesitation with weakness.

She reframes the event around survival and relation:

`帰ってきてくれて ありがとう`.

Thank you for coming back.

This changes the success criterion.

The institutionalized criterion available to Seiran is approximately:

`kill effectively -> remain functional -> be useful`.

Ari offers:

`you were frightened -> you still acted -> you returned -> the person you wanted to protect is here`.

Ari is therefore protecting something Seiran's armor cannot protect: **Seiran's moral interpretation of herself**.

The relationship does not remove Seiran from combat. It provides a counter-standard inside it.

## 5.9 Portable Ari — the pendant/letter as mobile relationship infrastructure

Ari creates a magical letter stored in a pendant and asks Seiran to carry it.

Mainline establishes the object's interpersonal role. Paratext reproduces fuller letter content and must remain labeled as paratext rather than silently promoted into mainline evidence.

The pendant does several things at once:

- carries Ari's presence across separation;
- expresses pride in Seiran;
- encodes a safe-return wish;
- provides an ordinary personal object inside military space;
- later becomes material proof that Ari remained salient during Seiran's final return attempt.

The relationship begins to externalize memory and voice into objects before death makes those objects unbearable.

## 5.10 Seiran's private ordinary self — `ひだまり`

The V03 in-volume extra `ひだまり` sharply complicates Seiran's public presentation.

In private with Ari, Seiran likes beautiful stones, clothes, flowers, manicures, piercings, appearance play, shared sleep, kisses, teasing, and being told she looks beautiful. She thanks Ari for repeatedly doing with her the things she wanted to try.

This matters for the dyad in two directions.

First, Seiran is not “the soldier” plus a hidden romantic exception. She has an ordinary aesthetic self whose public opportunities are constrained.

Second, Ari actively helps make that self livable.

Ari's function is:

`Seiran private desire appears -> Ari notices/permits/facilitates -> ordinary non-military identity receives shared space -> intimacy strengthens`.

Thus Ari does not merely wait to be protected.

She is one of the people through whom Seiran gets to be **less weapon-like**.

## 5.11 The support-versus-veto contradiction

The same extra gives Ari's decisive grief counterfactual:

`セイラン あなたが`

`あなたがもっと弱虫なら`

`私もわがままを言えたのに`

`行かないでって`.

Ari wishes Seiran had been weaker or more cowardly so Ari might have permitted herself the selfish request: do not go.

The contradiction is not:

`Ari supported Seiran, therefore Ari secretly opposed her all along`.

It is:

`Ari respects Seiran's chosen protective identity`

and

`Ari wants Seiran alive and near her`.

While Seiran lives, Ari gives priority to support.

After death, Ari discovers the emotional price of that priority.

The unresolved question—could stronger opposition have changed anything?—has no canonical answer and should remain open.

## 5.12 Battlefield rescue — Seiran's substitution generalizes beyond Ari

V04 testimony reveals that Seiran gave all her medicine to a gravely wounded senior she saved. The senior believes retaining it might have allowed her to survive.

This is crucial because it shows that Seiran's self-expenditure is not reducible to romantic tunnel vision.

The relationship-specific rule has generalized into a broader prosocial rule:

`another person in immediate danger -> Seiran prioritizes their survival -> spends own reserve`.

The rescue is morally real. The saved life is real.

The fatal cost is also real.

The correct synthesis is not “Seiran's sacrifice was meaningless” or “therefore it was right.” It is that **a valuable rescue can still expose a dangerous policy of putting oneself last**.

## 5.13 `帰らなきゃ` — Ari as survival command

Under catastrophic injury, Seiran's representation of Ari becomes a direct continuation signal:

`……帰らなきゃ`.

She must return.

This demonstrates real relational causation under acute threat.

Ari is not merely sentimental background. She changes Seiran's behavior while the body is failing.

Seiran:

- survives the immediate fight;
- helps another wounded person;
- continues despite severe injury;
- joins the return movement;
- walks toward the place where Ari waits.

Then the body fails.

## 5.14 Failed reunion — the limit of return grammar

Seiran dies on the return march.

The visual sequence progressively dissolves her into white absence while the pendant falls and remains.

Immediately beforehand, ordinary return talk continues around terminal injury; Mimi talks about dinner while Seiran leaves a heavy blood trail.

This is the relationship's formal hinge.

The manga distinguishes:

`survived combat`

from

`returned to the person waiting`.

Seiran achieves the first and fails the second.

The relationship therefore proves both sides of attachment causality:

> **Ari motivates survival effort. Ari cannot make Seiran immortal.**

## 5.15 V04 — grief initially appears as loyalty-performance

After Seiran's death, Ari helps clear Seiran's room.

She smiles and presents herself as prepared because she understood Seiran's choice and knew the risks.

This is not low attachment.

Ari is treating emotional composure as a form of retrospective respect:

`Seiran chose knowingly -> I respected that choice -> therefore visible collapse may feel like betrayal of what I claimed to understand`.

Mimi exposes the mistake by asking whether Ari thinks she is not allowed to be sad.

Ari's grief becomes expressible without requiring the conclusion that Seiran's agency was false.

The new rule is:

`respect for the dead person's choice != prohibition on survivor grief`.

## 5.16 The pendant returns; personhood cannot be replaced by relic

Mimi returns Ari's pendant and reports that Seiran had carried it happily during the return.

The object completes a directional reversal:

`Ari's words travel outward with Seiran`

then

`Seiran fails to return; the object travels back to Ari`.

The pendant matters because it preserves evidence of relational orientation.

It does **not** substitute for Seiran.

That distinction becomes important for V05 memory work.

## 5.17 Resurrection refusal — love as non-ownership

When Mimi offers resurrection, Ari refuses.

The governing idea is not “death should never be reversed.” It is **ownership and consent**.

Seiran's life belongs to Seiran.

Ari's desire does not give Ari the right to impose an eternal or altered existence on her dead partner, especially where resurrection itself carries additional cost and uncertainty.

This is the relationship's strongest posthumous ethical act.

While Seiran lived, Ari's respect for agency contributed to a suppressed veto.

After Seiran dies, that same respect becomes a clear boundary against appropriation.

The relationship therefore develops a principle that survives the loss of reciprocity:

> **Love does not confer title over the beloved's existence.**

## 5.18 V05 — memory moves from objects to enacted ordinary life

Ari resumes study and routine while remaining vulnerable to Seiran-linked cues. Rain and accidental assumptions that Seiran is still present can reopen grief immediately.

When Mimi dislikes the idea that Seiran's things will disappear from the room, Ari explains that memory persists in repeated ordinary practices:

`私たちが覚えてる限り 何も変わらない`.

The statement should not be read literally as “nothing changes.” Seiran is dead; enormous things have changed.

Its relational function is to reject a museum model of love.

Memory can live in:

- warm milk;
- sitting together;
- windows;
- habits;
- remembered speech;
- the way another person has already altered one's ordinary life.

The pendant remains meaningful, but the relationship is not reducible to preserving objects unchanged.

## 5.19 V05 — the unknowable support question remains open

Ari explicitly does not settle into a simple regret doctrine.

Her pain is that she cannot ask whether her encouragement helped Seiran.

Esta offers an analogy from her own relationship: having a loved person nearby during fear can itself provide strength.

That is a consoling hypothesis.

It is **not direct evidence of Seiran's inaccessible inner state**.

The synthesis must preserve both possibilities:

- Ari's support may have helped Seiran endure fear and retain a return orientation;
- Ari's affirmation may also have made it easier for Seiran to continue inhabiting a dangerous self-sacrificial role.

The corpus does not provide a counterfactual experiment.

## 5.20 V06 — grief integration through identity continuity

Ari eventually recognizes that she has been withdrawing and judging herself for grief.

Her central formulation is:

`あの子が好きになってくれた 私のままで生きることが 私にとっては大切なんです`.

Living as the self Seiran loved matters to her.

This is a sophisticated posthumous relationship policy.

It is neither:

`forget Seiran so life can continue`

nor

`freeze life so Seiran remains present`.

It is:

`Seiran loved a living Ari -> grief threatens to erase that Ari through withdrawal/self-devaluation -> preserve the self that participated in the relationship -> re-enter ordinary life without requiring grief to disappear`.

Ari's continued life becomes part of how the relationship is honored.

## 5.21 V06 — Ari becomes a relational interpreter for Sheena

Ari later helps Sheena think through jealousy and whether “friendship” still adequately names Sheena's bond with Mimi.

This is a secondary but meaningful posthumous effect.

Ari's relationship history makes her capable of recognizing relational ambiguity in others without turning her own loss into ownership of romance as a category.

She does not demand that Sheena reproduce Ari–Seiran.

She helps Sheena articulate a different relationship.

This is evidence that grief integration has become socially generative rather than socially enclosing.

## 5.22 V07 — preservation rather than forced progression

V07 contains no sufficiently diagnostic Ari–Seiran material to justify a major state revision.

This absence matters methodologically. The relationship should not be “developed” in every volume merely because a synthesis needs continuity.

The V06 posthumous model remains current until new evidence arrives.

## 5.23 V08 — retrospective dating status closes the historical classification

Ari tells Sheena:

`私とセイランは 結局あなたに言わなかったわよね つき合ってるって`.

Current mature authority therefore closes the historical classification: Ari and Seiran were dating before Seiran's death.

This does **not** make the earlier prospective caution wrong.

The correct claim architecture is:

`V01-V02: close/intimate; romance increasingly plausible`

`V03: reciprocal romance strongly supported behaviorally`

`V08: explicit retrospective confirmation that they had been dating`.

The history of knowledge matters because this project is preserving how the text reveals relationships, not merely the final encyclopedia fact.

## 5.24 V08 — new love does not insult old love

When Sheena tells Ari that she and Mimi are dating, Ari congratulates her and can discuss her own relationship with Seiran.

Ari does not treat Sheena and Mimi's happiness as competition with Seiran's memory.

This matters because it demonstrates a mature non-zero-sum grief model:

`Seiran remains loved -> new relationships can exist around Ari -> other people's romance can be welcomed -> Seiran is not displaced by their happiness`.

Ari can preserve particularity without demanding exclusivity from the whole social world.

## 5.25 V09 — posthumous love stops short of becoming a permanent command

Ari chooses to take the combat proficiency exam.

She believes Seiran would be hurt by that decision. She also knows Seiran is no longer present in the battlefield world and admits that some part of her had been waiting to be called.

The motive remains mixed/open.

This is not sufficient evidence for:

- war endorsement;
- suicidal intent;
- desire to imitate Seiran;
- rejection of Seiran;
- proof that grief is over.

What it does establish is a major agency principle:

> **Ari can continue loving Seiran without treating Seiran's presumed preference as a permanent veto over Ari's own future choices.**

This completes the current posthumous development from **respecting Seiran's agency** to also **reclaiming Ari's agency**.

# 6. Reciprocal seeing and being seen

The most important new contribution from Side Stories is that Ari–Seiran's relationship should be modeled perceptually, not only ethically.

## 6.1 Seiran's attention is relational knowledge

Seiran's gaze is not generic attraction.

She notices differences that require repeated exposure and person-specific interest. Ari's smiles are not interchangeable. Seiran can distinguish social function from more spontaneous ease.

This creates a high-confidence policy:

`Ari enters salient context -> Seiran monitors subtle bodily/emotional change -> signs of genuine ease are especially rewarding -> signs of vulnerability lower Seiran's protective threshold`.

The later protector logic is therefore not a discontinuous personality switch.

It is an escalation of attention under militarized stakes.

## 6.2 Ari wants interpretation by this person

Ari actively solicits Seiran's gaze.

That matters because being watched is not automatically intimacy. It becomes intimacy because Ari wants **Seiran** to keep looking and to find her cute.

The relational rule is:

`Seiran's attention is experienced as chosen valuation rather than surveillance -> Ari requests more -> reciprocal specialness becomes explicit`.

## 6.3 Ari's smile is not transparent state output

Ari is socially smooth enough that visible cheer cannot be used as a direct emotional meter.

This remains true after Seiran's death, when Ari initially smiles during mourning labor.

The pre-death Side Story and post-death V04 material therefore converge on a broader rule:

> **Ari can use socially accessible affect while carrying a different internal state.**

Seiran is unusually good at noticing the difference while alive.

Mimi, after Seiran's death, independently notices the contradiction strongly enough to challenge Ari's grief-smile.

# 7. Seiran's protective self-instrumentalization

## 7.1 Love does not produce one universal risk response

Ari–Seiran is a crucial anti-simplification case for the corpus.

Attachment can make a character:

- avoid risk;
- seek risk;
- conceal risk;
- redistribute risk;
- demand reciprocal preservation;
- or remain tactically unchanged while injury acquires new meaning.

Seiran's response is specifically:

`loved person's injury becomes intolerable -> self becomes substitute`.

## 7.2 The policy is prosocial, not evidence of self-hatred

Nothing requires the conclusion that Seiran hates herself.

She has tastes, desires, affection, embarrassment, aesthetic pleasure, gratitude, and ordinary-life wants.

Her self-expenditure is better modeled as a **priority rule** than a global negative self-concept:

`when another person's immediate survival/welfare conflicts with preserving own reserve -> other person can receive priority`.

The problem is that repeated use of this rule can make Seiran functionally expendable even if she does not believe she is worthless.

## 7.3 Institutional gratitude amplifies the rule

Seiran's school debt/usefulness ethic means the environment rewards exactly the behavior her relationship-protective policy tends to produce.

The institution needs people willing to absorb danger.

Seiran wants to be useful and to keep Ari safe.

Those motivations align rather than cancel.

This is why individual consent does not settle the ethical question.

## 7.4 First-kill moral shock reveals a boundary the self-model denied

Seiran's self-image assumes that serious resolve should make fear manageable.

Actual killing disproves that assumption.

Ari's moral repair is necessary because Seiran has internalized a criterion under which normal horror appears as failure.

Thus the relationship partially resists the institution **inside Seiran's own evaluative framework**, even while it does not remove her from the institution.

# 8. Ari's support-versus-veto contradiction

## 8.1 Ari is not a passive enabler

Ari gives Seiran:

- emotional recognition;
- ordinary private identity;
- physical intimacy;
- moral repair;
- safe-return wishes;
- a portable letter/pendant;
- pride;
- a person/place to return to.

Calling this “enabling” alone erases the actual care work.

## 8.2 Ari also does not fully oppose Seiran's risk policy

Ari supports Seiran's chosen strength.

She knows Seiran's role matters to her.

She suppresses the stronger attachment-protective veto because it feels selfish relative to Seiran's agency and self-conception.

The tragedy is that Ari never learns whether a more direct conflict would have changed the outcome.

## 8.3 The counterfactual is structurally unresolvable

After death, Ari can ask:

- Did my pride help her endure?
- Did my support make it easier to keep going?
- Would saying `行かないで` have mattered?
- Would it have violated the person Seiran was trying to be?

The source provides no final answer.

A correct synthesis preserves the uncertainty rather than rewarding the reader with retrospective omniscience.

## 8.4 Contrast with Sheena–Mimi V05

Sheena and Mimi eventually get something Ari and Seiran do not: time for the protection/agency contradiction to become an explicit quarrel.

Ari–Seiran therefore should not be used to prove what Sheena and Mimi “must” do. It demonstrates what can happen when a protective system remains partly implicit until irreversible loss intervenes.

# 9. Return, waiting, and failed reunion

Ari–Seiran and Sheena–Mimi share a return grammar but produce different outcomes.

## 9.1 Return as a relationship variable

A person waiting changes the meaning of survival.

For Seiran, Ari becomes an acute survival representation under injury.

For Ari, Seiran's return is valued not merely as mission completion but as preservation of the person.

## 9.2 Ari's `ありがとう` redefines victory

After the first kill, Ari's “thank you for coming back” shifts successful action away from killing competence toward continued relationship.

This is a relational value system operating inside, not outside, the war institution.

## 9.3 The final return fails after behavior succeeds

Seiran's motivation works behaviorally.

She continues moving.

The body still fails.

This is why the relationship should never be summarized as “love could not save her” without qualification.

Love **did** alter action.

It did not abolish mortality, injury severity, medical scarcity, or accumulated self-expenditure.

# 10. Objects, memory, and material residue

## 10.1 Pendant as outbound voice

While Seiran lives, the pendant lets Ari's care cross deployment distance.

## 10.2 Pendant as inbound absence

After Seiran dies, the pendant returns without her.

The same object changes semantic direction:

`Ari -> Seiran presence`

becomes

`Seiran -> Ari absence evidence`.

## 10.3 Objects are evidence, not replacements

The pendant's importance is enormous precisely because it cannot restore the person.

V05 then prevents a relic-centered grief model by locating memory in enacted ordinary life.

## 10.4 Memory is behavioral incorporation

Warm milk, rooms, windows, weather, habits, food, and remembered language show that relationships persist by altering future perception and routine.

The mature rule is:

`relationship history -> ordinary practices acquire person-specific meaning -> cue can reactivate grief/continuity even when object or person is absent`.

# 11. Grief development: from loyalty-performance to self-continuity

## 11.1 V04 — grief permission

Ari initially behaves as though understanding Seiran's agency creates an obligation to grieve correctly.

Mimi breaks that rule.

Sadness is not retroactive disrespect.

## 11.2 V05 — grief can coexist with ordinary function

Ari can study, keep schedules, talk to others, and still be destabilized by rain or accidental assumptions of Seiran's presence.

This is not inconsistency.

It is a cue-sensitive grief model.

## 11.3 V06 — the self Seiran loved

Ari's identity-continuity formulation is the current strongest grief policy:

`grief threatens withdrawal/self-erasure -> remember that Seiran loved this living person -> preserve that person -> participate again`.

The relationship becomes a reason **not** to disappear.

## 11.4 V08 — generosity toward new love

Ari can welcome Sheena and Mimi's romance and speak about Seiran.

Grief is no longer organized as a scarcity system in which another couple's happiness competes with the dead relationship.

## 11.5 V09 — self-authorship beyond presumed Seiran preference

The combat exam is the crucial mature test.

Ari does not stop caring what Seiran would think.

She does not make that imagined judgment sovereign.

The relationship therefore changes from:

`How do I remain loyal to Seiran?`

toward:

`How do I remain a person whose relationship with Seiran matters without surrendering authorship of my own remaining life?`.

# 12. Resurrection, consent, and posthumous autonomy

Ari's refusal of resurrection is one of the series' strongest anti-possession claims.

## 12.1 Wanting restoration is not sufficient authority

The fact that Ari desperately misses Seiran increases the emotional force of the choice. It does not increase her legitimate ownership.

## 12.2 Resurrection is not framed as a neutral undo button

The source presents altered conditions and cost. Ari is not refusing a simple reversible medical procedure whose preferences are already known.

## 12.3 The dead beloved remains a subject, not a resource for survivor repair

Ari's grief cannot turn Seiran into an instrument for fixing Ari's grief.

This is the inversion of Seiran's wartime self-instrumentalization:

- while alive, Seiran risks turning **herself** into the tool that protects others;
- after death, Ari refuses to turn **Seiran** into the tool that repairs the survivor.

## 12.4 Respect has to apply in both directions

The mature relationship ethic becomes:

`Seiran's life was Seiran's`

and eventually

`Ari's remaining life is Ari's`.

That symmetry does not emerge at once. V09 makes it visible.

# 13. Relationship effects on each individual

## 13.1 What Ari gives Seiran

Ari provides:

- desired gaze and reciprocal valuation;
- ordinary private space;
- access to aesthetic/self-expressive desire;
- moral repair after violence;
- a return target;
- portable relational presence;
- social recognition of the person behind the martial role.

## 13.2 What Seiran gives Ari

Seiran provides:

- particularized attention;
- someone who notices differences beneath Ari's social brightness;
- desired romantic gaze;
- private intimacy;
- a relationship in which Ari can be explicitly wanted;
- later, a remembered valuation that becomes part of Ari's self-continuity.

## 13.3 What the relationship risks doing to Seiran

It can intensify:

- self-substitution;
- willingness to absorb pain;
- performance pressure;
- protective concentration;
- acceptance of dangerous roles.

## 13.4 What the relationship risks doing to Ari

It can intensify:

- suppression of protective veto;
- retrospective responsibility uncertainty;
- grief self-policing;
- withdrawal;
- overreliance on imagined Seiran judgment.

## 13.5 Mature posthumous correction

Ari progressively corrects the last two without erasing the relationship.

She learns:

- grief is allowed;
- memory need not become a museum;
- continued life is not betrayal;
- other people's love is not replacement;
- Seiran's presumed wishes matter but are not sovereign over Ari's future.

# 15. Joint conflict and regulation model

The living dyad has surprisingly little overt interpersonal rupture before death.

That does not mean it is conflict-free.

Its central conflict is **underexpressed**.

## 15.1 Seiran's side

Seiran makes her protective logic explicit enough to others:

`Ari must not bleed -> I will fight instead`.

But she does not appear to frame this as a relational negotiation requiring Ari's agreement.

## 15.2 Ari's side

Ari supports Seiran's identity and handles the emotional cost privately.

The withheld `行かないで` is therefore a missing negotiation.

## 15.3 Why this is not simple communication failure

Ari's silence is value-laden.

She is not merely afraid to speak.

She treats Seiran's self-authored strength as something she should not selfishly override.

Thus the relationship's unresolved tension is:

`protect the beloved by stopping her`

versus

`respect the beloved by allowing her to choose`.

## 15.4 War terminates the negotiation window

Seiran's death makes the living conflict permanently counterfactual.

The posthumous relationship then develops the agency principle Ari could not fully enact while Seiran lived.

---

# 16. Comparison with Sheena–Mimi without reducing either dyad

Ari–Seiran is analytically valuable partly because it shows that similar relationship variables can produce different systems.

## 16.1 Shared structures

Both dyads contain:

- bodily care;
- waiting/return;
- portable relationship objects/memory;
- protection disagreements;
- person-specific safe-return wishes;
- tension between love and self-risk;
- ordinary private life inside militarized institutional space.

## 16.2 Critical difference: mortality does not explain everything

Mimi's recoverability helps explain why she can discount bodily damage.

Seiran is mortal and still chooses concentrated self-risk.

Therefore protective self-instrumentalization is not merely an immortality artifact.

## 16.3 Critical difference: Ari–Seiran loses the repair window

Sheena and Mimi reach an explicit V05 conflict over unilateral protection and can reconcile without solving the disagreement completely.

Ari and Seiran do not receive that developmental opportunity.

Ari's strongest veto appears retrospectively after Seiran is already dead.

## 16.4 Critical difference: posthumous ethics become central

Ari must answer questions Sheena has not yet had to answer at the current boundary:

- may I bring the beloved back if I can?
- does honoring the dead mean suppressing grief?
- does preserving love require preserving objects unchanged?
- does continuing life betray the relationship?
- do the dead partner's presumed wishes govern later choices?

## 16.5 Do not treat Ari–Seiran as merely a warning label

Their relationship is not valuable only because Seiran dies.

It contains positive relational achievements:

- mutual seeing;
- ordinary-life facilitation;
- moral repair;
- safe-return meaning;
- posthumous autonomy respect;
- grief-compatible self-continuity.

The tragedy changes the system. It does not exhaust its meaning.

# 17. Japanese voice and dialogue constraints

## 17.1 Seiran public/goal-directed register

Seiran is comparatively firm and declarative in public or duty-oriented contexts.

She can insist on choice, responsibility, usefulness, and strength.

Do not translate this into emotional simplicity.

## 17.2 Seiran after moral shock

After the first kill, the register destabilizes:

- fragments;
- repetition;
- bodily description;
- fear;
- shame;
- loss of confident declarative control.

A correct reconstruction must permit the public serious register to collapse under actual violence.

## 17.3 Seiran private with Ari

Trusted private context permits:

- embarrassment;
- teasing;
- aesthetic vocabulary;
- gratitude;
- softness;
- physical affection;
- pleasure in ordinary things.

Do not generate Seiran as permanently stern or military.

## 17.4 Ari living-dyad register

Ari is socially fluid and relatively physically confident.

With Seiran she can be:

- playful;
- teasing;
- appearance-focused;
- direct about affection;
- perceptive under distress;
- morally clear without becoming abstractly ideological.

Her post-kill response is a good example: she reframes Seiran's self-judgment through concrete relational facts rather than a speech about just-war theory.

## 17.5 Ari grief register

Grief can compress her language into sparse counterfactual structure:

`if only...`

`don't go`.

Later posthumous Ari becomes more capable of reflective self-definition while retaining relational grounding.

## 17.6 Key lexical anchors

The following lines are especially diagnostic and should constrain paraphrastic simulation rather than be copied mechanically:

- Seiran: `アリが 戦争で血を流す姿なんて 絶対に見たくないから`
- Seiran to Mimi: `カガリさんって… なんのために戦ってるの？`
- Ari to Seiran: `帰ってきてくれて ありがとう`
- Seiran under fatal injury: `……帰らなきゃ`
- Ari's suppressed veto: `行かないでって`
- Ari on enacted memory: `私たちが覚えてる限り 何も変わらない`
- Ari on identity continuity: `あの子が好きになってくれた 私のままで生きることが 私にとっては大切なんです`
- Ari's retrospective dating confirmation: `私とセイランは 結局あなたに言わなかったわよね つき合ってるって`
- Ari in `ぴかぴか`: `私のこと いっぱい見て`

The relationship generally expresses difficult psychology in concrete relational language before abstract theory.

# 18. Visual-form relationship grammar

## 18.1 Sustained gaze

`SS06` repeatedly organizes Ari through Seiran's looking.

The accumulation matters more than any single close-up. Hair, hands, posture, eating, smiles, and spontaneous laughter turn visual observation into relationship knowledge.

Ari then verbally converts the manga's gaze grammar into explicit intimacy by asking Seiran to keep looking.

## 18.2 Ordinary intimacy revealed after loss

`ひだまり` is formally cruel because it gives the reader the density of Seiran's private ordinary life after mainline death has made that life unrecoverable.

The effect is not “flashback makes death sad.”

It reclassifies what has been lost:

not a generic soldier,

but a person with aesthetic preferences, teasing routines, kisses, sleep, adornment, and future ordinary desires.

## 18.3 Pendant persistence

The pendant visually survives where Seiran does not.

This creates a material contrast between durable object and non-durable body.

## 18.4 Progressive white dissolution

Seiran's disappearance is shown as gradual visual subtraction.

The body becomes less present until the relationship object remains.

The formal sequence makes “absence” something the reader watches happen rather than only learns afterward.

## 18.5 Blood trail against dinner/return talk

Ordinary conversation continues while Seiran's body is visibly failing.

This juxtaposition prevents the institution's daily-life surface from cleanly separating itself from lethal cost.

## 18.6 Room clearing

V04 converts Seiran's former room into administrative labor for survivors.

The visual/social space remains, but the person has been converted into belongings to sort.

Ari's grief makes the difference between **vacancy** and **replacement-resistant particularity** explicit.

## 18.7 Rain as grief retrieval

Rain becomes a repeated Ari-Seiran memory cue.

Its later use changes from immediate death association toward a context in which Ari can decide how grief will shape continued selfhood.

# 19. Ethical and agency synthesis

## 19.1 Seiran's consent is real and structurally conditioned

Seiran wants to fight and become useful.

The source does not support erasing that agency.

It also does not support treating the choice as context-free.

Her motivations include institutional rescue/debt, normalized usefulness, relationship protection, and a world where children are trained for war.

## 19.2 Love can reproduce institutional expendability without becoming false love

Seiran's desire to keep Ari safe is genuine.

The institution's need for bodies is also genuine.

When Seiran solves the first problem by offering more of her own body, personal love and institutional demand become aligned.

That alignment is ethically dangerous precisely because no villain has to falsify her motive.

## 19.3 Ari's support can be good and still leave an unresolved cost

Ari's support gives Seiran dignity, moral repair, ordinary identity, and a return target.

It may also have reduced friction against Seiran's continued dangerous role.

The correct ethical posture is not retrospective blame. It is uncertainty.

## 19.4 The rescued senior prevents purity narratives

Seiran's medicine sacrifice saves a real person.

This means the corpus does not allow easy moral cleanliness:

- preserving Seiran's life would have mattered;
- saving the senior's life mattered;
- Seiran's policy of spending herself was dangerous;
- the beneficiary is not morally equivalent to a thief of Seiran's life.

## 19.5 Ari's resurrection refusal is an agency correction after death

Ari cannot renegotiate Seiran's wartime choices.

She can refuse to make a new unilateral choice **for** Seiran after death.

This gives posthumous agency a negative form: restraint.

## 19.6 V09 extends the same principle back to Ari

If Seiran's life was Seiran's, Ari's remaining life cannot belong wholly to the dead partner either.

Ari's combat-exam choice may be dangerous. It may later be revised. But the current ethical significance is that posthumous attachment does not abolish the survivor's authorship.

# 21. Failure modes and common misreadings

## 21.1 “Seiran died because Ari encouraged her”

Too strong.

Ari's support is part of the relational environment. Seiran also possesses institutional gratitude, a self-authored usefulness ethic, a protector policy, and an independent willingness to fight. The source never supplies the counterfactual outcome under Ari's opposition.

## 21.2 “Ari should have stopped her”

This converts a genuine agency dilemma into hindsight certainty.

Ari herself experiences the counterfactual pain. The text does not prove that direct prohibition would have succeeded or been ethically uncomplicated.

## 21.3 “Seiran was suicidal”

Unsupported.

Seiran wants to return, has ordinary desires, values intimacy, and under fatal injury actively tries to survive toward Ari. Her self-expenditure is not equivalent to a desire for death.

## 21.4 “Seiran's sacrifice was noble, therefore not a problem”

Too simple.

The rescue is valuable and the self-spending pattern is dangerous. Both can be true.

## 21.5 “Ari refused resurrection because she accepted death”

Incomplete.

Her decision is grounded in Seiran's ownership of Seiran's life and the limits of survivor authority. Grief remains active.

## 21.6 “Ari moves on from Seiran”

Poor model.

Ari integrates grief, preserves Seiran's role in identity, re-enters social life, and later diverges from presumed Seiran wishes. None requires forgetting.

## 21.7 “Ari is always cheerful”

Contradicted by `SS06` and grief evidence.

Ari's smile can be socially adaptive and is not a transparent internal-state indicator.

## 21.8 “Seiran is just the serious one”

Contradicted by `ひだまり` and `SS06`.

Private Ari context reveals aesthetics, teasing, embarrassment, affection, and ordinary desire.

## 21.9 “They are only important as a Sheena–Mimi mirror”

Incorrect.

The dyad independently develops mutual gaze, protector self-instrumentalization, first-kill moral repair, failed return, posthumous autonomy, enacted memory, grief-compatible identity continuity, and later survivor self-authorship.

# 23. Mature claim transitions

## PRESERVE

- Seiran sincerely wants greater usefulness and combat competence.
- Seiran's love for Ari materially increases willingness to take battlefield burden.
- Ari is a genuine survival/return target.
- Ari's support and pride are real rather than retrospectively false.
- Seiran's rescue of the senior has genuine moral value.
- Ari remains attached after Seiran's death.
- Earlier prospective romantic uncertainty remains historically authoritative at its original boundaries.

## STRENGTHEN

- Ari–Seiran is a mutually romantic/dating relationship.
- Seiran's protectiveness grows from longstanding person-specific attention, not only battlefield ideology.
- Ari actively creates space for Seiran's non-military self.

# 25. Final synthesis

Ari and Seiran matter because their relationship demonstrates that intimacy does not operate outside institutions, bodies, or mortality.

It begins with looking.

Seiran notices Ari closely enough that smiles have texture. Ari wants to be seen by Seiran specifically. Ari, in turn, makes room for the person beneath Seiran's serious utility-oriented surface. They build private life from touch, appearance, small desires, shared space, teasing, ordinary pleasure, and mutual valuation.

War does not erase that intimacy.

It weaponizes one of its mechanisms.

Seiran already attends to Ari's vulnerability. Under military conditions, that attention becomes a policy: if Ari might bleed, Seiran will become the body that takes the danger instead. Institutional gratitude and relationship protection align. Seiran's agency is real, and so is the structural environment that makes self-expenditure useful.

Ari's care pushes in another direction. When killing breaks Seiran's self-image, Ari refuses the institutionally normalized conclusion that horror is weakness. She thanks Seiran for returning. She gives Seiran ordinary life and a reason/place to come back to.

But Ari also respects Seiran's chosen strength enough to withhold the request she most wants to make.

`行かないで` arrives only after death.

The relationship therefore never receives a living resolution to its deepest contradiction.

Seiran's final `帰らなきゃ` proves that love has causal force. She keeps moving toward Ari. The return still fails.

What follows is not the disappearance of the relationship but a change in its mode of existence.

Ari first has to learn that respecting Seiran does not require performing painless acceptance. Then she has to decide whether wanting Seiran back gives her the right to remake Seiran's existence. She says no. Then she has to decide whether continuing to live turns Seiran into the past in the sense of irrelevance. She says no again: she will preserve the self Seiran loved.

Finally V09 asks the hardest posthumous question: if the dead beloved remains important, must the survivor obey her forever?

Ari's answer, provisionally, is also no.

She can believe Seiran would be hurt by her choice and still choose.

The arc therefore arrives at a form of fidelity more demanding than obedience:

> **Seiran remains particular enough to matter, but not so appropriated that Ari may rewrite Seiran's life; Ari remains connected enough to remember, but not so appropriated by memory that Seiran's presumed wishes may rewrite Ari's.**

That symmetry is the mature achievement of a relationship that can no longer be symmetrical in presence.

The final model is:

`mutual seeing -> ordinary selfhood -> love-protective self-substitution -> moral repair -> safe-return orientation -> failed reunion -> grief permission -> refusal of posthumous ownership -> enacted memory -> identity continuity -> survivor self-authorship without forgetting`.

Ari and Seiran do not demonstrate that love defeats death.

They demonstrate that **death does not automatically reduce love to either possession or paralysis**.
