# KIMISHINU_SHEENA_MIMI_RELATIONSHIP_SPECIALIST_SYNTHESIS.md

## 1. Purpose and authority

This is the canonical specialist synthesis for the relationship between **Totsuki Sheena / トツキ・シーナ** and **Gagari Mimi / ガガリ・ミミ** at the current KimiShinu source boundary available to this project.

Its responsibility is deliberately different from either individual character monograph.

`KIMISHINU_SHEENA_CHARACTER_MONOGRAPH.md` reconstructs Sheena as an individual agent.

`KIMISHINU_MIMI_CHARACTER_MONOGRAPH.md` reconstructs Mimi as an individual agent.

`KIMISHINU_RELATIONSHIP_AND_INTIMACY_LEDGER.md` preserves the sequential relationship-state record.

This artifact instead treats **the relationship itself as a dynamic system**. It asks what happens when two distinct people, with different bodies, risk models, care styles, developmental histories, institutional positions, and temporal horizons repeatedly act on one another.

The synthesis is intended to support:

- relationship-state reconstruction;
- behavioral prediction under changing dyadic conditions;
- joint scene simulation;
- conflict and reconciliation modeling;
- Japanese dialogue generation constrained by relationship state;
- comparative relationship analysis;
- future-release falsification and claim revision;
- analysis of how intimacy interacts with mortality, institutional violence, personhood, agency, memory, and ordinary life.

It does **not** supersede the sequential readings, individual monographs, or ledgers. The chronology remains controlling. Later evidence can revise the mature interpretation of earlier events without changing what was knowable prospectively at the earlier boundary.

The central methodological rule is therefore:

> **Do not model Sheena + Mimi by adding two static personalities together. Model the relationship as a history-dependent system in which each person's state changes the other's available meanings, costs, and likely actions.**

The current integrated boundary is `V09+SIDE_STORIES`. The numbered prospective freeze remains V09. Standalone Side Stories are supplemental/retrospective evidence and must not be projected backward into earlier prospective knowledge states.

# 2. Executive dyadic thesis

The Sheena–Mimi relationship is best understood as the progressive construction of **mutual non-disposability under irreducible asymmetry**.

It begins in a system that already makes persons replaceable.

Sheena's previous roommate has died. The institution fills the vacancy. Mimi enters the room partly because another child is gone. Mimi herself has historically been treated as an unusually reusable military body: deployed because she can return, repaired because she can be used again, and—V09 reveals—formerly kept underground as a secret weapon between summons.

The relationship therefore does not begin in an ordinary social environment and later encounter mortality. **Replacement, bodily utility, death, and institutional substitution are present at formation.**

Against that background, the pair gradually produce a counter-logic.

Sheena learns that Mimi is not equivalent to the institution's description of her. Recoverability does not make pain irrelevant. Immortality does not make injury costless. Usefulness does not make a body public property. A person who consents to taking danger does not thereby lose the right to be cared for.

Mimi learns a parallel but non-identical lesson. Sheena is not a passive object to be protected by keeping her weak, safe, or waiting. Sheena's fear does not cancel her agency. Her body matters too. Her chosen healing work matters. Her care for other people does not make Mimi replaceable. Her eventual mortality does not make present attachment pointless.

The relationship's mature achievement is therefore **not symmetry**.

They do not become alike.

- Mimi remains far more willing to expose her own body to catastrophic damage.
- Sheena remains far more sensitive to injury, death, and irreversibility.
- Mimi remains physically non-aging under current rules.
- Sheena expects to grow older and die normally.
- Mimi's autobiographical memory can erode over long duration.
- Sheena's life is finite but presently continuous in a way Mimi's memory may not be.
- Mimi's primary protective role remains interception/substitution.
- Sheena's primary chosen role becomes healing, reception, harm reduction, and future medical support.
- Mimi tends to act before abstractly explaining.
- Sheena tends to interpret before naming and often names only after meaning has stabilized.

What changes is the **policy for living with those differences**.

The mature dyadic rule can be stated as:

`difference/asymmetry becomes salient -> neither person is allowed to disappear into utility, protection, or fear -> each retains standing to care about the other's body -> each retains agency even when the other dislikes the choice -> truthful return is preferred to sanitized reassurance -> primacy is protected without social isolation -> future pain is acknowledged without preventive abandonment -> finite presence is chosen without pretending to create eternity`.

This is why the V05 quarrel is the central structural hinge of the relationship.

Before V05, care repeatedly intensifies intimacy. In V05, **care itself becomes dangerous** because both girls try to convert love into unilateral control.

Mimi says, in effect: if Sheena is vulnerable, Sheena should remain safe while Mimi takes the danger.

Sheena says, in effect: if Mimi is being destroyed to protect everyone, Mimi should stop accepting that role.

Both positions are loving. Both positions can erase the other's authorship.

The pair mature when reconciliation no longer requires solving the underlying disagreement. They learn:

> `I understand why you choose this` does not require `I think your choice is right`.

and:

> `I love you` does not grant `I may decide for you`.

From there, romance becomes less a destination than a new layer added to an already functioning relationship system.

V06 makes primacy conscious.

V07 names the relationship `恋人` and gives exclusivity a mutual, one-person meaning.

V08 asks whether couplehood can survive unequal bodily development.

V09 asks whether it remains worth choosing when unequal aging, death, institutional uncertainty, and eventual forgetting are no longer hypothetical background problems.

The answer at the current boundary is yes—but not because the problems are solved.

The answer is:

> **future loss is real; symmetry may be impossible; an unethical escape is rejected; letting go now would itself be a loss; therefore the relationship is chosen as finite, ongoing presence rather than guaranteed permanence.**

The deepest relationship thesis is consequently:

> **Sheena and Mimi do not overcome mortality by becoming permanent. They resist disposability by continuing to make one another particular.**

### What the relationship is not

Current evidence does **not** support modeling the pair as:

- a fusion in which individual goals disappear;
- a relationship in which love cures Mimi's self-endangerment;
- a relationship in which Sheena becomes fearless;
- a relationship in which Mimi becomes broadly non-jealous;

- a simple protector/protected pairing;
- a simple healer/warrior complement where each role is harmless to the person occupying it;
- an example of love defeating the institution at the structural level.

The pair have gained **relational agency**, not sovereignty.

# 4. Why the dyad must be modeled as a dynamic system

A static relationship summary would say:

- Sheena is cautious and caring.
- Mimi is direct and protective.
- They love each other.
- They fight because each worries about the other.

All four statements are broadly true and analytically insufficient.

The relationship changes what each trait means.

## 4.1 Sheena changes the meaning of Mimi's injury

Before attachment, Mimi's body is primarily processed through recoverability, use, restoration, and operational continuation.

Once Sheena becomes a waiting person, injury gains a new cost:

`injury -> pain + repair requirement + Sheena's sadness + possible failure to return visibly/intact`.

This does not erase Mimi's recoverability arithmetic. It adds a relational term to it.

That is why V03 can support both:

- Mimi remains willing to suffer severe damage;
- Mimi says she dislikes injury partly because the person waiting will be sad.

A flat model would mistake those as contradictory. The dynamic model treats them as simultaneous weights that have not yet reached the point where the new weight dominates tactical choice.

## 4.2 Mimi changes the meaning of Sheena's fear

Sheena's fear initially tends to coexist with low perceived efficacy. That combination can produce disengagement or self-blame.

Mimi makes inaction harder because the threatened body is now particular. Sheena cannot comfortably tell herself that repeated violence is merely normal when the person returning covered in blood is someone whose hair she combs, whose food she shares, and whose hand she holds.

The relationship therefore turns moral discomfort into a search for bounded capability.

It does **not** turn Sheena into Mimi.

Sheena's development is:

`fear + attachment + visible harm -> need for a non-fantasy way to help -> healing competence -> infirmary work -> portable healing experimentation -> adult medical vocation`.

Mimi catalyzes the question. Sheena authors the answer.

## 4.3 The pair create problems neither individual produces alone

Several major states are genuinely dyadic.

**Waiting** exists because one person leaves and another expects return.

**Transparent wounded return** exists because Mimi's old reassurance strategy collides with Sheena's right to know what her protection costs.

**Jealousy over distributed care** exists because Sheena's public healer role makes intimate-seeming actions available to many people while Mimi wants one-person primacy.

**Age-matching insecurity** exists because Mimi's fixed body becomes meaningful relative to Sheena's future development.

**Fear of being forgotten** exists because one person's finite life intersects another person's unusually long and unstable autobiographical memory horizon.

**Finite "always"** exists because both want permanence while their bodies and temporal conditions make literal symmetry unavailable.

These cannot be reconstructed by reading either monograph alone.

# 5. Chronological relationship architecture

## 5.1 Precondition: the relationship begins in a vacancy created by death

The Side Story `ばいばい` retrospectively makes the V01 beginning harsher and more specific.

Sheena's previous roommate Laura is not an empty narrative placeholder. Laura was a real peer: capable, self-demanding, caring, sometimes sharp, increasingly pressured by usefulness, and part of an imperfect unfinished relationship with Sheena.

Laura dies.

The school continues.

The room becomes available.

Mimi is assigned into it.

This establishes one of the relationship's deepest background rules:

> **The institution can replace occupants faster than persons can be emotionally replaced.**

Sheena's early aversion toward Mimi must therefore not be read as mere prudishness around an eccentric new girl. Mimi enters a physical and emotional space whose emptiness was produced by casualty processing.

At the same time, V09 retrospectively reveals Mimi's own prehistory of institutional non-personhood: underground confinement, retrieval for battle, washing/restoration, and return to confinement.

Thus both girls approach V01 from opposite sides of the same system.

Sheena has just experienced a person becoming an institutional vacancy.

Mimi has historically been treated as a strategic body whose ordinary social place barely existed.

Their relationship grows partly by undoing those classifications at the interpersonal level.

## 5.2 V01: fear, bodily care, and reclassification

The first attachment is profoundly asymmetric.

Mimi approaches.

Sheena recoils.

Mimi is already comfortable with blood, injury, physical proximity, direct questions, and bodily intervention in ways Sheena is not. Their earliest physical contact therefore cannot be treated as evidence of symmetrical social meaning.

The first healing kiss is especially important as a methodological example. It occurs in a restorative-magic context. The correct V01 reading was not "obviously romantic kiss" but **bodily trust/boundary crossing under ambiguous motive**.

Later V08 evidence revises Mimi's mature motive: Mimi says she personally wanted to kiss Sheena even though the act also served magic. That later testimony strengthens the mature relationship history without changing V01 knowability.

The decisive V01 reclassification occurs around vulnerability.

Sheena initially experiences Mimi as almost category-incompatible with ordinary human fear because Mimi can smile, fight, restore herself, and inhabit violence with extraordinary ease.

Then Sheena learns that Mimi feels pain and fear.

The weapon-like body becomes a vulnerable body.

The uncanny child becomes someone Sheena can recognize as sharing a bodily condition even if the consequences of injury are radically different.

Meanwhile Mimi restores Sheena's catastrophically injured arm at significant personal cost.

By the volume endpoint, forced cohabitation has become desired ordinary company.

The V01 dyadic transition is therefore:

`vacancy/replacement -> asymmetric approach -> fear/category instability -> costly bodily care -> vulnerability recognition -> ordinary companionship becomes possible`.

Romantic self-conception is not yet required.

## 5.3 V02: first friendship, secret trust, and the invention of return

V02 supplies the first explicit relational category: **friendship**.

For Mimi this matters disproportionately because Sheena is her first friend.

The category immediately changes disclosure. Mimi shares the institutionally restricted truth of resurrection because friendship has become associated with secret-sharing. This is not only intimacy; it is local interpersonal agency against a secrecy regime governing Mimi's body.

V02 also establishes the relationship's most durable spatial and behavioral grammar.

They build ordinary reciprocity through:

- room care;
- food;
- laundry;
- books;
- flowers;
- physical affection;
- shared plans;
- routine school life.

Then deployment produces the complementary structure:

`Sheena waits -> Mimi returns directly to Sheena -> ただいま / おかえり -> battle residue is received in domestic space`.

This is a major transformation because Mimi now has a **person-specific destination** after combat.

The flower crown intensifies the mechanism. An object made in ordinary leisure follows Mimi into battlefield cognition and later becomes explicit memory technology. Ordinary life is no longer merely what occurs between missions. It becomes something portable enough to modify what returning means.

Sheena's `ケガしないで 帰ってきて` is equally important.

It creates a request Mimi cannot reliably fulfill.

The relationship therefore acquires its first central contradiction:

`the loved person wants safe return` versus `Mimi's operational body is built around surviving damage rather than avoiding it`.

V02 does not solve the contradiction. It makes it relationally legible.

## 5.4 V03: the safe-return promise fails, but care does not

V03 is the first real test of whether relationship expectations can survive material failure.

Mimi returns badly injured.

She tries to heal, clean, and minimize the damage before Sheena sees her. She apologizes for failing the no-injury request.

Sheena's first major reaction is not rage at Mimi. It is self-blame: the request was unrealistic because Sheena did not understand the conditions well enough.

Mimi then supplies a crucial correction. The request still mattered because it made her happy.

The result is one of the pair's foundational mature principles:

> **A care request can matter even when reality makes perfect compliance impossible.**

This prevents the relationship from collapsing into two bad alternatives:

- if Mimi cannot guarantee safety, Sheena's concern is meaningless;
- if Sheena cares, Mimi must guarantee the impossible.

Instead, care changes appraisal without guaranteeing outcome.

V03 also makes emotional care fully bidirectional. Sheena holds the injured Mimi; Mimi responds to Sheena's crying and asks whether Sheena hurts. The pair are no longer easily divided into caregiver and receiver.

The flower crown becomes explicit memory technology: Mimi carries it to remember fun things. Ordinary life is now intentionally used to resist the psychological totalization of deployment.

Most importantly, Mimi states that injury is bad not only because it hurts but because the person waiting will be sad.

Sheena is now **inside Mimi's injury calculus**.

But the prospective architecture correctly keeps tactical risk reduction OPEN.

### Seiran as the first major comparison case

V03 simultaneously presents Seiran–Ari as a different attachment-to-war transformation.

Seiran loves Ari and therefore wants to take Ari's share of battlefield danger.

Ari loves Seiran and validates her moral humanity after killing, while privately wishing she could ask her not to go.

Seiran dies trying to return.

The comparison prevents a false universal rule such as "love makes people safer."

The source instead establishes:

- attachment can make a person want to survive;
- attachment can make a person take more danger as substitute;
- attachment can make a waiting person suppress a veto to respect the loved person's chosen identity;
- return desire can be behaviorally real and still fail.

This comparison becomes essential to Sheena–Mimi's later agency conflict.

## 5.5 V04: grief, finite home, and reciprocal non-disposability

Seiran's death changes the main pair without turning the story into a simple lesson that they should avoid all danger.

Mimi experiences irreversibility in a new way. She understands extreme injury and restoration far better than she understands a familiar person becoming permanently absent. Her first response is concrete reversal-seeking. She needs Sheena to help name the feeling as sadness/loneliness.

Sheena responds by making a finite commitment.

She cannot promise that she will never die.

She cannot promise that Mimi will never lose anyone again.

She can promise that while she is alive she can be a place to return to, embrace Mimi, and say `おかえり`.

This is the first mature formulation of **finite home**.

The relationship's answer to immortality is not immortality.

It is available presence.

V04 then generalizes absence threat beyond deployment. When Sheena is unexpectedly missing, Mimi actively searches, protests against being left, and seeks bodily reassurance. Seiran's failed return likely increases salience, but the source does not require a global separation-anxiety diagnosis.

The central V04 transition arrives when Mimi reacts strongly to a small injury on Sheena.

Sheena has repeatedly asked Mimi not to be hurt while minimizing her own harm.

Mimi asks why that rule should be one-way.

This creates **reciprocal non-disposability**.

The dyadic rule becomes:

`your body matters to me -> my body is not exempt from your concern -> neither person's recoverability, weakness, or role cancels the other's standing to care`.

This is more important than simple reciprocity of affection. It creates a shared moral jurisdiction over concern while stopping short of giving either person control over the other's choices.

V05 will expose that missing second half.

## 5.6 V05: the agency crisis

V05 is the relationship's most important conflict volume because it reveals that **non-disposability without agency can become coercive care**.

### Mimi's protective logic

Mimi reasons that:

- ordinary students can die permanently;
- she can return;
- therefore it makes sense for her body to take their place;
- if she absorbs danger, fewer people disappear.

This is not self-hatred. It is a prosocial protective-substitution ethic operating through extreme recoverability.

The institution benefits from exactly the same arithmetic.

The important distinction is that Mimi has now personally authored a motive that aligns with the system's incentive to concentrate violence in her body.

Love does not automatically oppose institutional weaponization. A person's sincere care can reproduce the institution's preferred allocation of harm.

### Sheena's protective logic

Sheena sees Mimi's hidden injuries and rejects the idea that aggregate safety makes the concentrated bodily cost morally irrelevant.

She also discovers that Mimi has been cleaning, healing, and changing before reunion so Sheena will not have to see the damage.

The concealment exists **because Sheena matters**.

That makes it relationally more complicated, not less.

Mimi is performing reassurance through information control:

`Sheena will be sad if she sees the wounds -> remove visible evidence -> present safe return`.

Sheena's objection is therefore not merely "stop being injured."

It becomes:

> **Do not make your suffering invisible to make my feelings easier.**

### Symmetrical control

The quarrel escalates because each girl tries to solve danger by limiting the other's authorship.

Mimi wants Sheena to stop dangerous training and remain safer while Mimi carries combat.

Sheena wants Mimi to stop using her body as the preferred casualty sink.

The formal symmetry is:

`I love you -> your danger is intolerable -> therefore I should determine what danger you are permitted to choose`.

This is why neither girl's position can simply be declared the relationship's final truth.

### Repair without convergence

The crucial V05 breakthrough is that Sheena separates **motive** from **method**.

She can recognize that Mimi's concealment and substitution contain real kindness while still believing the method is wrong.

Mimi can continue to believe she should fight while accepting that Sheena will continue training and healing.

The relationship gains a new rule:

> **Agreement is not the admission price for continued attachment.**

They can repair by recognizing why the other chooses something, apologizing for the part that caused unnecessary hurt, preserving the unresolved belief, and negotiating behavior around the disagreement.

This is the origin of their mature **secure disagreement**.

### Transparent reception

Sheena then revises waiting.

Earlier:

`wait -> receive the returning Mimi`.

After V05:

`wait -> receive the actual Mimi in the condition she really returns -> do not require injury to be erased before reunion`.

Waiting becomes epistemic as well as physical.

Sheena wants the truth of the body.

This is a major relational answer to expendability: if suffering remains visible to someone who treats it as significant, it is harder for the protection system to disappear entirely into statistics.

### Mutual transformation without self-rejection

V05 also prevents a common romantic-development error.

Mimi worries that her presence has made Sheena unable to remain herself.

Sheena answers that meeting Mimi made it possible to admit that **she wants to change what she can do**.

Mimi says that meeting Sheena meant no longer being alone.

Neither formulation says:

`the old self was worthless`.

The relationship produces development through expansion, not replacement.

The V05 special booklet then demonstrates repair robustness: grooming, bathing, food, studying, shared sleep, and flower-viewing plans resume without the values dispute being solved.

The pair have learned that conflict does not require prolonged relational coldness.

## 5.7 V06: truth, jealousy, memory, and pre-label romantic primacy

V06 directly tests the transparent-return agreement. Mimi again returns badly injured, but Sheena is now present in the recovery space while the cost is visible. The relationship has moved from **sanitized reunion** to **contact-zone intimacy**.

This does not make Mimi safer.

It makes her suffering more shareable as information and care.

### Sheena's jealousy

Mimi's social world begins to expand.

Sheena experiences competitive discomfort and wonders whether she is upset because she is no longer Mimi's only friend.

The response is diagnostic. Sheena does not demand that Mimi lose the new relationships. She turns inward, compares, becomes embarrassed, and eventually asks Ari for help interpreting the feeling.

Thus Sheena's emerging primacy desire is not initially ownership.

It is a category problem:

`many friends can matter -> I want a position that is not interchangeable with ordinary friendship -> what is that position?`

### Maternal memory and relationship differentiation

V06 clarifies that Mimi's first attraction to Sheena's scent connects to degraded memory of Mimi's mother.

This increases the need for a careful causal model rather than supplying a reductionist one.

The correct structure is:

`maternal sensory trace -> initial approach salience`.

It does not imply:

`entire mature attachment = mother substitution`.

Six volumes of person-specific trust, conflict, ordinary routine, bodily care, jealousy, first-friend status, and direct desire now exist independently of the cue.

V09 later closes the reduction explicitly when Mimi says Sheena is not her mother but her lover.

### Memory asymmetry

V06 also changes mortality architecture.

Mimi fears forgetting Sheena's voice, eye color, warmth, and other sensory particulars. Sheena privately does not want to be forgotten.

This creates a distinct relationship threat:

`Sheena may die` is not identical to `Mimi may later forget Sheena`.

The finite partner can be lost twice:

1. bodily absence;
2. erosion of autobiographical particularity in the surviving partner.

The pair respond through finite memory-making rather than a guarantee of permanent recall.

### The non-magical kiss and conscious primacy

Sheena initiates a mouth-to-mouth kiss explicitly identified as not magic: `…これも 魔法じゃないよ`.

The importance is not that physical intimacy begins from zero. They have already touched, embraced, bathed, groomed, healed, and kissed in magical contexts.

The new fact is **category disambiguation**.

A familiar bodily grammar acquires an explicitly non-instrumental intimate meaning.

By CH29, Sheena can state internally that she wants to be alone with Mimi and become Mimi's `一番`.

Yet when Haru introduces `恋人`, Sheena still reaches the boundary of social categorization rather than immediately claiming it.

V06 therefore ends with meaning ahead of label.

## 5.8 V07: lover status, mutual exclusivity, and differentiated care

V07 does not replace the relationship. It **reclassifies** it.

Sheena works through conventional formulations such as wanting to date or become lovers, but worries those phrases do not fully capture what she means. Her deeper desire is spatial and relational:

- nearest place;
- hand she can keep holding;
- non-interchangeable primacy;
- a position she does not want to yield to someone else.

When finite-time pressure makes endless semantic delay costly, Sheena's spoken language becomes much simpler than her internal analysis: `好き`, `恋をしてる`, `特別`.

Mimi reciprocates in equally characteristic terms.

Her romantic ontology is concrete:

- Sheena has long been the only one;
- Mimi wants to be special to Sheena too;
- friends can be many;
- a lover is one person.

The relationship becomes explicitly mutual `恋人`.

The drawn bonus `だいすき` then independently confirms Mimi's private recognition through diary language such as `シーナと恋人になった` and `シーナ だいすき`.

### Jealousy under distributed care

V07 also provides one of the most important post-confession tests.

Sheena's healer role expands. She touches, wipes, talks to, and treats other students. From Mimi's perspective, behaviors that are highly intimate in the dyad now appear in a public care context.

Mimi watches from the infirmary doorway and imagines replacement.

The easy but wrong solution would be:

`Mimi is the lover -> Sheena must reduce distributed care`.

The actual solution is more mature.

Sheena keeps the healer role.

Mimi claims a relationship-specific niche: **she can be the person who cares for Sheena after Sheena spends herself caring for others.**

This produces the pair's mature primacy rule:

> **Romantic exclusivity means non-interchangeable one-person specialness, not exclusive ownership of all care, touch, friendship, or social attention.**

The rule remains stress-sensitive. Higher-threat jealousy could still reactivate control attempts. But V07 proves that jealousy is not structurally required to erase Sheena's public agency.

### Privacy after couple formation

Mimi's diary remains private.

Sheena wants to read it.

Mimi says no.

This small scene is analytically valuable because it prevents couplehood from becoming informational fusion.

Mutual lovers can still possess private internal/archive space.

## 5.9 V08: the couple enters public time

V08 is the first volume in which the pair begin already knowing they are lovers.

The result is continuity rather than personality replacement.

Mimi proudly announces the relationship. Sheena is embarrassed by the public performance, not ashamed of the bond.

When Mimi wants Ari to know, Sheena asks to handle the disclosure carefully because Ari's lover is dead. Mimi accepts the delay once Sheena gives the person-specific reason.

This demonstrates another agency-compatible relationship policy:

`one partner wants immediate disclosure -> the other identifies a recipient-specific emotional reason for timing -> disclosure authority can be delegated without interpreting delay as rejection`.

### Dating and marriage language

Peers introduce social scripts such as `デート` and `結婚`.

The pair experiment with picnic/date behaviors, food sharing, kissing, resting together, and future talk without suddenly becoming a conventional adult couple in miniature.

Social vocabulary becomes material to test, not an automatic template.

### Developmental desynchronization

The major V08 transition is bodily time.

Sheena will ordinarily grow.

Mimi's face and height do not.

A temporary `成長薬` can make Mimi look older but does not cause real growth.

The chapter deliberately makes temporary symmetry sensorially attractive. Eye line changes. Height changes. Clothing scale changes. Kissing becomes physically easier. Sheena admits the older-looking Mimi makes her heart race.

This matters because Sheena's eventual reassurance is not based on denying attraction to synchronized appearance.

She says, in effect:

`I like this too` and `you do not have to become this for me`.

Mimi's motive for taking the drug is equally important. She wants to become a `かっこいい恋人` Sheena can be proud of.

Her insecurity is not generalized vanity. It is a concrete attempt to solve relationship asymmetry through visible self-modification.

The proposed policy is:

`Sheena keeps aging -> Mimi looks increasingly mismatched -> repeatedly use age camouflage -> manufacture visible synchrony`.

Sheena rejects appearance matching as the condition of attachment.

The closing emphasis on `私だけが / 大人になっても` does not solve the problem. It moves the relationship into a new state:

**commitment despite acknowledged developmental desynchronization**.

### Joint history and first-kiss classification

The V08 bonus `おぼえてる` shows the couple negotiating its own history.

They remember the same earlier kiss but initially classify it differently. Sheena categorizes by function; Mimi adds her person-specific motive.

The important relational point is not which person "wins."

The pair co-author a shared history through discussion.

A mature relationship includes **negotiated memory**, not only shared facts.

## 5.10 V09: finite love under known future cost

V09 strips away the possibility that V08 reassurance solved the temporal problem emotionally.

### Mother substitution closes

Mimi explicitly tells Fran that Sheena is not her mother. The maternal smell was an early cue, but the present relationship is Sheena-specific loverhood built through accumulated history.

This closes the reductionist model.

### Institutional history deepens the stakes of ordinary relationship life

# 6. Core dyadic mechanisms

## 6.1 Reciprocal non-disposability

The strongest moral relationship mechanism is not generic kindness. It is the progressive extension of bodily and personal standing in both directions.

### Sheena toward Mimi

Sheena rejects:

- immortality as evidence injury does not matter;
- recoverability as permission for concentrated harm;
- successful protection as proof that the protector's body is cheap;
- concealment as a way to make the cost socially disappear.

### Mimi toward Sheena

Mimi rejects:

- Sheena minimizing her own injuries while making Mimi's injuries morally important;
- the idea that only the immortal partner deserves protective concern;
- Sheena's attempt to remain exclusively on the caring side of the relation.

The mature norm is:

> **Both bodies remain claimable by care even though the consequences of bodily damage are radically unequal.**

This is not equal-risk arithmetic.

Mimi really can recover from things Sheena cannot.

The relationship does not deny that fact.

It denies the inference that asymmetric recoverability creates asymmetric personhood.

## 6.2 Protection versus agency

Reciprocal concern creates a second-order problem: who gets to decide what the loved person may risk?

V05 supplies the answer.

Protection is incomplete if it preserves the body by deleting the person as an author.

This produces the relationship's mature correction:

`care about your safety + recognize your authorship + preserve my right to disagree + negotiate behavior where possible`.

The policy is difficult because both girls have strong reasons to regress under threat.

Mimi's likely regression:

`Sheena danger -> restrict Sheena / take the danger herself`.

Sheena's likely regression:

`Mimi extreme self-expenditure -> try to stop/override the substitution because the body cost is intolerable`.

The stable relationship does not remove these impulses. It gives them a repair pathway.

## 6.3 Waiting, return, and reception

Waiting is one of the relationship's most distinctive inventions.

It begins as a domestic/deployment ritual and develops through several stages.

### Stage 1 — destination

Sheena is somebody Mimi returns to.

### Stage 2 — safety wish

Sheena asks Mimi to return unhurt.

### Stage 3 — failed safety

Mimi cannot reliably fulfill the request.

### Stage 4 — sanitized reassurance

Mimi hides damage so the waiting person does not suffer emotionally.

### Stage 5 — transparency conflict

Sheena discovers that the clean return presentation is false as an account of cost.

### Stage 6 — transparent reception

Sheena asks to receive Mimi in the condition she really returns.

### Mature meaning

`return` no longer means only physical survival.

It means **relational legibility**.

The waiting person has standing to know what happened to the returning body.

The relationship therefore turns visibility into an anti-expendability practice.

## 6.4 Truth versus reassurance

Mimi's early concealment is not simple dishonesty.

It is care optimized for emotional outcome:

`Sheena sees less pain -> Sheena suffers less`.

Sheena's mature response introduces another good:

`if I cannot see the cost -> I cannot actually care for you or understand what your protection requires`.

The pair therefore move from **affective reassurance** toward **shared difficult information**.

The same tension appears later in reverse around Sheena's future terror.

Sheena reassures Mimi about continued love while not fully disclosing how frightened she is about aging, death, and forgetting.

This prevents a triumphalist interpretation in which V05 permanently solves transparency.

The durable rule is more modest:

> **High trust creates pressure toward difficult truth, but each person may still lag or selectively conceal when they believe disclosure will hurt the other or destabilize themselves.**

## 6.5 Primacy without social enclosure

Both girls develop jealousy.

Their jealousy is asymmetrical in form.

### Sheena's jealousy

Triggered by Mimi's expanding friendships and the possibility that first-friend uniqueness is no longer enough.

Typical Sheena path:

`comparison -> inward confusion -> embarrassment -> third-party consultation -> category clarification`.

She does not initially solve jealousy through commands.

### Mimi's jealousy

Triggered by Sheena's distributed healer care: touch, wiping, magic, attention, name use, conversation.

Typical Mimi path:

`observe concrete acts -> imagine replacement -> distress/monitoring -> direct specialness need -> seek complementary role/reassurance`.

The mature common solution is:

`many social relations remain legitimate + romantic position remains one-person and non-interchangeable`.

This is stronger than saying the pair are "not possessive." They do experience possessive pressure.

What matters is that current evidence shows **agency-compatible regulation** rather than mandatory social isolation.

## 6.6 Healer/protector role differentiation

The pair become complementary in a way that must not be romanticized into perfect balance.

### Mimi: interception / substitution / immediate protection

Mimi's dominant protective logic is:

- go toward danger;
- use extraordinary restoration/recoverability;
- absorb cost others cannot;
- act quickly when Sheena is hurt;
- prefer direct bodily solution.

### Sheena: reception / healing / reduction / continuity

Sheena's dominant chosen care logic becomes:

- keep injury morally visible;
- reduce pain/damage;
- build healing competence;
- support many people according to need;
- receive Mimi after danger;
- preserve ordinary life and future capacity;
- survive so care can continue.

### Why complementarity is not resolution

Mimi's role can reinforce her self-instrumentalization.

Sheena's role can reinforce overextension and the temptation to become endlessly available.

The relationship is healthiest when each role also **checks the other's pathological edge**.

Sheena checks:

`protection -> body as disposable infrastructure`.

Mimi checks:

`care -> Sheena treats her own body/time as exempt from reciprocal concern`.

The V07 aftercare solution is the most mature form of differentiation currently available:

Sheena can care outward.

Mimi can remain special by caring for **the caregiver**, not by abolishing the caregiving role.

## 6.7 Ordinary life as irreplaceability production

One of the corpus's strongest relationship claims is that ordinary life is not secondary evidence.

Food, laundry, flowers, baths, grooming, sleep, notes, schoolwork, fever care, cookies, walks, making things, diaries, and shared idleness produce person-specific knowledge.

- casualty;
- healer;
- secret weapon;

Ordinary relationship life performs the opposite operation.

It creates innumerable low-stakes distinctions:

- how this person eats;
- how this person reacts when embarrassed;

- how this person touches;
- what this person remembers;
- how this person cares for plants;
- what this person writes in a diary;
- what this person's injured body looks like before it is cleaned for social re-entry.

That accumulation is what makes "special" more than a verbal romantic category.

The pair become irreplaceable partly because they acquire **too much particular knowledge** to remain abstractly substitutable.

## 6.8 Body, time, mortality, and memory asymmetry

The relationship contains several distinct temporal asymmetries that should never be collapsed into a generic immortal/mortal binary.

### Bodily development

Sheena ordinarily ages.

Mimi ordinarily does not show corresponding physical maturation.

### Terminal mortality

Sheena can die permanently under ordinary rules.

Mimi can resurrect/recover under extraordinary rules established by the current source.

### Pain

Both can experience pain and fear.

Recoverability changes consequence, not phenomenology into nothingness.

Thus bodily persistence is not identical to memory persistence.

### Social/institutional time

Sheena can imagine graduation, vocation, work, and adult social progression.

Mimi's graduation/release remains institutionally uncertain.

### Romantic time

The pair want continuing one-person specialness even though the relationship may not map cleanly onto ordinary equal-age adult scripts.

### The mature solution

The current source supplies no final metaphysical solution.

Instead it produces policies:

- do not require cosmetic age matching;
- do not buy synchronization through another person's sacrifice;
- make concrete future/location plans where agency exists;
- preserve memories without claiming guaranteed remembrance;
- treat "always" as continued chosen presence rather than literal eternal co-duration;

## 6.9 Privacy and selective disclosure

Couple formation does not abolish boundaries.

The relationship is not closed around two people.

They interlace after conflict.

They become newly hot after relationship reclassification.

They support in sickness.

They stage the final V09 decision not to let go.

Hands fit the series' ethics unusually well because they represent **finite contact** rather than metaphysical possession.

A hand can be held now.

It cannot guarantee eternity.

# 14. Japanese dialogue and joint voice constraints

This section governs relationship-conditioned dialogue generation. It does not replace the individual voice models.

## 14.1 Sheena speaking to Mimi

Baseline characteristics:

- quieter than Mimi;
- more hesitation/ellipsis around socially embarrassing topics;
- ordinary vocabulary;
- semantic caution before labels;
- increased directness under personhood/agency salience;
- first-person future/action language when she has reached a decision.

With Mimi, Sheena can move between:

- soft domestic instruction;
- practical treatment language;
- embarrassed romantic denial/avoidance;
- direct `好き` / `特別`-type declarations after internal processing;
- unusually firm moral rejection when Mimi's body is treated as disposable;
- simple survival/return language under extreme threat.

Do **not** give Sheena polished relationship-theory speeches merely because the synthesis can formalize her reasoning.

She may think complexly and speak simply.

## 14.2 Mimi speaking to Sheena

Baseline characteristics:

- third-person self-reference `ミミ`;
- short wants and declaratives;
- low hedging;
- direct questions;
- concrete sensory/person-specific language;
- repetition under distress;
- low embarrassment around public affection;
- immediate category mapping once a useful category becomes concrete.

With Sheena, Mimi commonly expresses:

- wanting proximity;
- direct protection requests;
- simple jealousy observations;
- `特別` / `恋人` / one-person exclusivity;
- concrete memory fears such as voice, eyes, warmth;
- immediate care language;
- questions about procedures she does not understand (`when does a quarrel end?`, etc.).

Do **not** translate Mimi's underlying ethics into philosophical vocabulary in her mouth.

## 14.3 Couple disagreement language

The pair should not sound like debate-club partners.

A source-faithful serious disagreement is more likely to involve:

- concrete examples;
- repeated names;
- bodily stakes;
- simple accusations such as "that's wrong";
- direct questions about why a choice is acceptable;
- first-person statements of what each intends to do;
- pauses/withdrawal followed by later clarification.

## 14.4 Couple reassurance language

Reassurance is usually more concrete than cosmic.

High-confidence forms include:

- being here;
- waiting;
- returning;
- not disappearing;
- staying near;
- holding hands;
- doing ordinary activities later;
- explicit simple love/specialness.

Literal eternal guarantees should be avoided at V09+ unless the scene is specifically dramatizing their tension with finitude.

## 14.5 Jealousy language

### Sheena

Jealousy often appears before naming through:

- comparison;
- wanting preference rankings;
- bodily discomfort;
- awkward questioning;
- internal thoughts about `一番`.

### Mimi

Jealousy is more externally concrete:

- who touched Sheena;
- who Sheena talked to;
- who called her name;
- what Sheena did for them;
- fear that somebody else might become special.

Neither should default to sophisticated accusations of betrayal.

# 16. Comparison cases that clarify the central pair

## 16.1 Ari–Seiran: love can intensify self-substitution

Ari–Seiran is the most important foil because it prevents a simplistic "love makes you value your own safety" model.

Seiran's love for Ari motivates more risk. She chooses to become stronger and absorb danger partly so Ari does not have to bleed.

Ari supports Seiran's chosen identity while privately wishing she could ask her not to go.

Seiran dies trying to return.

The central pair learn in a narrative world where this tragedy already demonstrates that respect for a loved person's agency can coexist with catastrophic consequences.

Sheena–Mimi's mature difference is not that they discover the perfect alternative. It is that they become more explicit about **keeping both agency and the survivor's veto-like concern inside the relationship at the same time**.

## 16.2 Ari after death: love without ownership

Ari refuses resurrection for Seiran because grief does not give her the right to author Seiran's existence.

This establishes a series-wide relationship ethic important to Sheena–Mimi:

> **wanting someone to remain does not automatically authorize control over the form of their life.**

V05 applies the living-person version of that principle.

## 16.3 Esta–Rika: reciprocal protector claims without Mimi's immortality

The Side Story `ずきずき` shows that competing protector impulses are not unique to supernatural recoverability.

Rika refuses to let Esta remove herself from reciprocal care and emphasizes that she can find Esta when hurt.

This strengthens the wider corpus norm that the person who protects others remains available to be protected.

## 16.4 Sheena–Laura: present attachment does not erase unfinished prior bonds

Laura's retrospective history prevents Mimi from becoming a replacement-object in the analytical model.

Sheena can remember Laura while building happy life with Mimi.

The relationships are different rather than rank-ordered copies.

This is important for modeling Sheena's grief and primacy simultaneously.

## 16.5 Fran–Mimi: care can exist inside coercive management

Fran's history with Mimi is a warning against assuming interpersonal warmth proves structural freedom.

Fran genuinely cares and also participates in the retrieve/restore/return-underground system.

The main pair's own care must therefore be analyzed separately from the institution's structure. Love can create personhood space without automatically dismantling coercion.

# 17. Ethical synthesis

## 17.1 Love as refusal of fungibility

The relationship repeatedly rejects the idea that functional equivalence is personal equivalence.

A dead roommate can be replaced administratively, not relationally.

A recoverable fighter can replace mortal fighters tactically, not morally.

A healer can care for many patients without making a lover interchangeable.

A temporary older body can imitate age symmetry without becoming the person's true temporal development.

A diary can preserve traces without guaranteeing a living memory.

The relationship's ethical force lies in maintaining these distinctions.

## 17.2 Love as expanded moral perception

Sheena makes Mimi's hidden bodily cost visible.

Mimi makes Sheena include herself inside the category of people worth protecting.

Each therefore expands the other's moral field in a relationship-specific direction.

## 17.3 Love does not cancel arithmetic

Mimi is correct about an important practical fact: if an immortal/recoverable body takes danger, other mortal bodies may survive.

Sheena is correct about an important moral fact: successful substitution can still create concentrated dehumanization and escalating obligation.

The relationship does not resolve this by pretending one side's factual premise disappears.

It keeps the arithmetic and personhood problem in tension.

## 17.4 Consent is necessary and insufficient

Mimi's willingness to fight matters.

It means she is not simply a passive victim with no authored motive.

But willingness does not prove:

- the institution is non-coercive;
- the social expectation is fair;
- the bodily cost is morally irrelevant;
- peers are entitled to unlimited substitution;
- love should stop questioning the arrangement.

The relationship becomes strongest when it can recognize Mimi's agency without converting that agency into blanket moral permission for everyone else to spend her body.

## 17.5 Finite attachment as an ethical achievement

The mature pair do not require eternal certainty before loving.

That matters in a supernatural setting where the temptation to solve finitude literally exists.

V09's achievement is not "love conquers death."

It is:

> **love remains worth choosing even after death, unequal aging, memory erosion, and institutional uncertainty are admitted as real.**

That is a more limited claim and a stronger one.

# 18. Visual/formal relationship grammar

## 18.1 Category switch: weapon body to ordinary body

Early Mimi framing places a small child body against blood, grotesque repair, enormous magical force, and dark combat fields.

Domestic scenes then repeatedly bring the same body into baths, beds, food, grooming, flowers, and touch.

The formal point is not that ordinary life reveals the "real" Mimi while combat is fake.

Both are real.

The visual system forces the reader to hold incompatible social categories around one body.

Sheena's relationship is one of the main mechanisms that keeps the ordinary vulnerable category from disappearing.

## 18.2 Clean versus dirty return

V05's sanitized room return and hidden recovery-space damage create a formal split between what attachment sees and what the protection system hides.

V06 collapses that split by putting Sheena directly into the recovery image.

This is one of the clearest visual representations of relational transparency in the corpus.

## 18.3 Doorway jealousy

Mimi at the infirmary door visualizes a central romantic question:

`if Sheena's care moves outward, where is Mimi's special place?`

The eventual answer is not to close the door.

## 18.4 Body geometry in V08

Temporary age camouflage changes eye line, mirror image, clothing fit, head position, and kissing geometry.

The manga therefore makes synchronized-looking adulthood emotionally persuasive before denying that it is actual maturation.

This visual experience is necessary to understand why Mimi's fix is tempting and why Sheena's acceptance matters.

## 18.5 Dark fields and memory/death fear

Sheena's early alienation and V09's death/forgetting nightmare use emptiness/darkness to reduce environmental continuity.

Ordinary spaces then return.

The formal alternation suggests that personhood is sustained not by erasing horror but by repeatedly re-entering materially particular life.

# 19. Common misreadings and failure modes

## Failure mode 1 — “Mimi learns to value her life because Sheena loves her”

Too strong.

Mimi increasingly values relational consequences of injury and return, but the source still does not prove a general tactical inversion. Protective substitution remains active through V09.

## Failure mode 2 — “Sheena saves Mimi from being a weapon”

Too strong structurally.

Sheena creates a powerful interpersonal counter-norm and expands Mimi's personhood space. Mimi remains a strategic allocation problem under the institution.

## Failure mode 3 — “They solve V05 by compromising halfway”

Wrong mechanism.

The durable achievement is not midpoint policy convergence. It is **recognition of motive + preserved disagreement + reciprocal agency + practical behavioral revision**.

## Failure mode 4 — “Jealousy proves the relationship is controlling”

Incomplete.

Control risk is real. But V07 specifically demonstrates a non-isolating solution to Mimi's jealousy, and V06 shows Sheena's jealousy turning inward rather than immediately becoming prohibition.

## Failure mode 5 — “Their love is maternal substitution”

Rejected by mature source evidence.

Maternal scent is an early approach cue. V09 explicitly distinguishes Sheena as lover rather than mother.

## Failure mode 6 — “Mimi is the protector and Sheena is the protected”

Obsolete after V04-V05.

Both claim standing to protect and be protected. Their roles differentiate by mechanism, not by one-way value.

## Failure mode 7 — “Sheena's healer role is just a way to follow Mimi”

Too reductive.

Staying near Mimi is one motive. Supporting many war-injured people is independently established and personhood-consistent.

## Failure mode 8 — “Mimi's immortality makes the age difference irrelevant”

Opposite of V08-V09.

Bodily stasis, adult appearance, graduation, mortality, and memory asymmetry become increasingly central precisely because the relationship is serious.

## Failure mode 9 — “High trust means no secrets”

Rejected.

Mimi keeps diary privacy. Sheena retains future-terror she has not fully disclosed. Disclosure is high and incomplete.

## Failure mode 10 — “Becoming lovers is the climax that resolves the relationship arc”

Too shallow.

V07 supplies a category. V08-V09 immediately show that explicit romance creates new problems: public disclosure, role distribution, age mismatch, adult future, institutional uncertainty, and finite commitment.

## Failure mode 11 — “Ordinary domestic scenes are filler around the war plot”

Analytically backwards.

Ordinary scenes are where person-specific knowledge, irreplaceability, memory anchors, and self-authored future are built.

## Failure mode 12 — “Their relationship is perfectly equal because concern is reciprocal”

No.

Reciprocal standing does not create equal bodies, equal risk, equal institutional power, equal time, or equal memory conditions.

The achievement is reciprocity **inside inequality**, not elimination of inequality.

## Failure mode 13 — “Sheena's V09 choice means she is no longer afraid”

Wrong.

V09 explicitly reveals that she remains deeply afraid. The choice matters because fear is present.

## Failure mode 14 — “Mimi's diary proves she has solved future forgetting”

Unsupported.

The diary is an external memory anchor. Deliberate systematic anti-forgetting remains OPEN.

When generating or evaluating a Sheena–Mimi interaction at the current boundary, use this order.

Is the scene:

- V03 waiting/return period?

## Step 2 — identify the active asymmetry

Which matters most now?

- combat recoverability;
- fear;
- injury visibility;
- social primacy;
- bodily age;
- future mortality;
- memory;
- institutional power;
- disclosure timing;
- competence/care role.

## Step 3 — identify threat salience for each partner

Sheena threat candidates:

- Mimi injured;
- Mimi self-disposable;
- helplessness;
- replacement;
- aging/death/forgetting;
- burdening a recipient.

Mimi threat candidates:

- Sheena hurt;
- Sheena disappearing;
- Sheena being replaced as special person or choosing another special person;
- inability to fix loss;
- being visibly mismatched/inadequate as lover;
- institutional confinement/control.

## Step 4 — preserve individual policy before coupling

Generate each girl's first impulse according to her individual model.

Then apply relationship history to modify it.

Do not skip straight to a harmonious couple answer.

## Step 5 — check the V05 agency rule

If one impulse protects by controlling, ask whether mature relationship learning should redirect it.

## Step 6 — check the primacy rule

Does the scene confuse distributed care/friendship with romantic interchangeability?

If yes, model jealousy or reassurance without automatically erasing the outside relationship.

## Step 7 — check truth/visibility

Is somebody hiding cost to protect the other's feelings?

If Mimi injury: current norm favors visible return.

If Sheena future terror: incomplete disclosure remains plausible.

## Step 8 — resolve at the correct scale

The pair usually solve **behavioral next steps**, not the entire metaphysical problem.

Good resolutions include:

- wait here;
- let me see the injury;
- I will keep training;
- you can keep helping them;
- I will take care of you afterward;
- tell Ari later;
- work nearby if graduation fails;
- stay with me now.

Bad source-inflated resolution:

- complete philosophical consensus;
- eternal certainty;
- global institutional reform from one conversation.

## Step 9 — restore ordinary life if the conflict permits

Post-repair relationship security is often shown through mundane continuity rather than a second grand speech.

# 25. Final synthesis

Sheena and Mimi's relationship is not most interesting because an immortal child and a mortal girl fall in love.

That description is true and too coarse.

The relationship becomes distinctive because the manga repeatedly asks what love should **do** when the two people do not share the same risk, body, institutional power, developmental time, or mortality horizon.

The early answer is care.

Care turns fear into curiosity.

Care turns a weapon into somebody whose pain matters.

Care turns a roommate into a first friend.

Care creates a person worth returning to.

Then the manga makes care fail as a sufficient answer.

Mimi hides injury because she cares.

Mimi tries to restrict Sheena because she cares.

Sheena tries to stop Mimi's self-substitution because she cares.

The V05 quarrel reveals that love can become coercive precisely when its motive is sincere.

The relationship survives by adding **agency** to non-disposability.

From that point onward, their strongest achievements are not perfect agreements. They are durable policies for remaining attached without collapsing difference.

Sheena can say:

`I know why you do this. I still think the cost matters. I will not pretend your wounds did not happen. I will keep choosing my own way to help.`

Mimi can say, in her much simpler relational grammar:

`Sheena is special. Sheena's body matters too. Sheena can help other people and still be Mimi's one person. Mimi will come back to her.`

Romance then makes the system more explicit rather than simpler.

They become lovers.

Jealousy appears.

Public care expands.

Privacy remains.

The future acquires bodies.

Sheena will grow; Mimi will not in the same way.

Mimi may survive far longer; Sheena may die first.

Mimi's body can remain while memory erodes.

The institution may keep using Mimi after the peers around her move forward.

No confession solves these facts.

The mature relationship therefore becomes a practice of **finite holding**.

They make plans where plans are possible.

They reject solutions whose cost requires making someone else disposable.

They preserve one-person primacy without claiming ownership of every friendship, patient, diary, thought, or future choice.

They receive the actual wounded body rather than a reassuring performance.

They keep ordinary life alive beside war rather than waiting for war to end before personhood can begin.

And when literal eternity becomes impossible to promise honestly, the relationship does not collapse into meaninglessness.

It contracts to something more concrete:

`I am here.`

`I will wait.`

`Come back.`

`Let me see you as you are.`

`You can still choose.`

`I can still disagree.`

`You are still special.`

`I will keep holding your hand while I can.`

That is the current canonical Sheena–Mimi relationship model.

Its governing proposition is:

> **Love does not make them symmetrical. Love makes asymmetry morally and emotionally impossible to ignore, then teaches them to remain persons to one another inside it.**
