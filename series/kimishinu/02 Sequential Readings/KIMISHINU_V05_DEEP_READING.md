## 2. Volume-level thesis - protection becomes an argument about agency, not simply safety

V04 ended by establishing **reciprocal non-disposability**: Sheena has long insisted that Mimi's body matters even if it can be restored, and Mimi finally claims the same right to care whether Sheena is injured. V05 immediately reveals that this ethical symmetry does not mean the pair agrees on what protection should look like.

The volume's central conflict is between two internally coherent but incompatible protective policies.

**Mimi's policy** becomes explicit. Because she alone can resurrect and survive the kind of damage that kills ordinary students, she sees substitution as obvious harm minimization: if she takes everyone else's place, fewer mortal students will be injured or die. She wants Sheena to remain in the room, avoid dangerous training, bake cookies, gather flowers, and wait for her while Mimi absorbs the battlefield cost.

**Sheena's policy** rejects that bargain. She accepts the substitution arithmetic intellectually - V02 already taught her that risk displaced from Mimi may land on someone else - but V05 shows that she cannot accept a solution that saves everyone else by rendering Mimi's suffering morally irrelevant. She also refuses Mimi's second premise: that weakness means one should do nothing. Sheena's healing training is not an attempt to imitate Mimi or justify her existence through soldiering. It is a rejection of her old defense against helplessness: if she can learn a bounded way to reduce suffering, she no longer wants to protect herself from failure by declaring in advance that nothing can be done.

The argument matters because **both girls are trying to protect the other**. Mimi conceals wounds to spare Sheena distress and tries to monopolize danger so Sheena can remain ordinary. Sheena trains because she wants to help Mimi and others, and she insists on seeing the cost Mimi has been hiding. Each experiences the other's protective action as a threat to the other's personhood:

- Mimi hears Sheena's training as evidence that Sheena cannot remain safely herself because of Mimi.
- Sheena hears Mimi's substitution as a demand that Mimi become an endlessly damaged tool while everyone else calls the outcome successful.

V05's most important relational achievement is therefore not agreement. It is **reconciliation without belief convergence**.

Sheena apologizes for rejecting the kindness inside Mimi's concealment, but explicitly says she still cannot agree with Mimi's way of fighting. Mimi continues to say that taking the burden is something only she can do. Sheena answers by preserving her own choice too: she will not stop healing practice. She will wait for Mimi, but now also at the infirmary, and she asks Mimi not to erase or disguise the condition in which she returns.

The new relational rule is:

`I can recognize why you chose this -> I can love the care inside the choice -> I still do not have to endorse the choice -> disagreement does not authorize me to erase your agency -> our relationship can continue while the conflict remains open`.

That is a major simulation-model hardening. A weaker model would predict that intimacy forces moral convergence. V05 shows the opposite: their bond becomes stronger because they learn how to remain attached **through unresolved value conflict**.

The volume also makes three broader advances:

1. **Mimi's personal reason for fighting is no longer wholly OPEN.** She gives a directly authored protective-substitution reason: if she takes everyone's place, people who cannot resurrect do not have to die. The motive is concrete and prosocial, not ideological; Sheena is especially protected inside it, but V05 does not reduce the motive to romance alone.
2. **Sheena's early passivity is reclassified.** It was not proof that she believed weakness was good or that she did not care. It was a defense against the pain of confronting powerlessness: if she decided nothing could be done, she did not have to experience herself failing to act.
3. **The institution and peer culture reward Mimi's self-instrumentalization.** Adults call her part of military strength; peers celebrate lower casualty rates while describing her blood-soaked body as no longer human. The system can produce objectively safer outcomes for others while deepening the dehumanization of the person carrying the cost.

The V05 governing transition is therefore:

`reciprocal non-disposability -> apparently safer deployments -> hidden concentration of injury in Mimi -> substitution logic made explicit -> Sheena/Mimi protection conflict -> Sheena's helplessness defense exposed -> each recognizes the care inside the other's behavior -> reconciliation without consensus -> equal right to self-directed protective action -> ordinary intimacy resumes while structural disagreement remains`.

## 3. CH20 - `第20話 だいじょうぶ`

### 3.1 Sheena returns to practical testing with fear intact

Class 14 enters another practical examination (`V05 / CH20 / p_0005-p_0014`). Sheena remembers the earlier simulated injury in which her arm was severed. Her fear is explicit. The important transition is not desensitization; she tells herself that today she will do better than before.

This preserves the established policy:

`danger cue -> fear remains -> action is scaled into a manageable task rather than replaced by bravado`.

V05 therefore continues to reject a conventional soldiering arc in which repeated exposure makes fear disappear. Sheena is becoming more agentic **while remaining afraid**.

The surrounding peer discussion also embeds Seiran's death into the practical logic of the school. Students recognize that they will eventually become upperclass students, that deployment is unavoidable, and that more people may fail to return. Practical training is justified by a future danger everyone already knows to be real.

### 3.2 Mimi's reciprocal non-disposability becomes anticipatory control

Mimi reacts badly to Sheena needing to train. She asks why Sheena has to push herself and imagines the possibility of Sheena ending up damaged like the upperclass students they have seen (`p_0015-p_0019`).

This is the first V05 extension of V04's reciprocal non-disposability. Mimi is no longer merely entitled to object after Sheena is hurt; she wants to prevent the exposure in advance.

That protective impulse is understandable and relationally meaningful, but V05 will show its control risk. If Mimi's answer to caring about Sheena is "Sheena should not do dangerous things," reciprocal care can slide into asymmetric agency denial even though the motive is love.

### 3.3 Ordinary life remains the desired destination

After the examination the pair returns to familiar low-stakes intimacy. They eat, sleep in the same bed, talk about cookies, and Mimi says she wants the next day's classes to be ones they can take together (`p_0020-p_0023`). She also tells Sheena that she watched her throughout the day.

These details strengthen two long-running models:

- Mimi's ordinary-life appetite is active, not decorative relief between battles.
- post-Seiran attachment sensitivity is visible as monitoring, proximity, and preference for shared schedule.

### 3.4 The promise not to train dangerously while Mimi is away

When another deployment summons Mimi, she asks Sheena to promise not to perform dangerous practice in her absence (`p_0024-p_0026`). Sheena agrees. Mimi tells her to wait.

This is not generic jealousy or possessiveness. It is specifically injury/absence-conditioned control after a peer death has made separation costly.

### 3.5 Clean returns create a false causal model

Mimi returns cheerfully. On successive deployments the reported casualty situation appears much better. Mimi looks clean, reports that nobody was hurt, and asks whether Sheena is happy that she returned healthy (`p_0027-p_0033`). Sheena says of course and feels reassured.

The visual and informational structure is crucial. Sheena sees **outcomes** but not the hidden cost allocation producing them. Fewer casualties + clean Mimi appearance naturally generates the inference that the deployments themselves have become safer.

That inference is wrong.

V05 is therefore constructing an epistemic trap:

`visible casualties fall + Mimi conceals injury -> observers infer violence has decreased -> concentrated harm disappears from ordinary social perception`.

The chapter tail continues to show severely damaged students passing through recovery/medical space (`p_0034-p_0036`), preventing the reader from fully sharing Sheena's reassurance even before CH21 exposes the mechanism.

## 4. CH21 - `第21話 無垢`

### 4.1 Battlefield perspective reveals what Sheena cannot see

CH21 begins away from Sheena, following a black-haired student later identified as Esta-senpai through combat (`V05 / CH21 / p_0039-p_0045`). Mimi appears as an overwhelming rescue/combat presence. From the battlefield perspective, her intervention is genuinely life-preserving.

That point matters. The analysis must not turn Mimi's substitution into an empty or delusional gesture. She is actually preventing injuries and deaths.

The ethical problem is **who pays for that prevention and how the payment becomes socially invisible**.

### 4.2 Institutional language converts Mimi into military capacity

Adult dialogue makes the instrumental logic unusually explicit (`p_0046-p_0047`). The enemy incursion has been contained with minimal casualties. Mimi is credited as the reason, but one speaker immediately reframes her as part of the available force:

`「アレも戦力の一部だ」`

The pronoun/wording is dehumanizing: Mimi is "that" which forms part of military strength. The institution's interest is not necessarily sadistic. It is outcome-oriented. If one functionally immortal child can absorb damage that would otherwise kill many children, the military arithmetic rewards using her more.

This is exactly why V05's personhood question is difficult. The system can reduce aggregate mortality **and** instrumentalize the person who makes that reduction possible.

### 4.3 Peer praise repeats the same dehumanization from below

Students likewise celebrate the result: light injuries, fewer casualties, Mimi becoming even stronger. But the praise curdles into language about how blood-covered and extreme she now looks, ending with:

`「なんかもう 人間じゃないよね」`

This is one of the clearest peer-normalization data points in the series. The children are not administrators calculating force structure; nevertheless, they reproduce the same conceptual movement:

`Mimi saves us -> Mimi can absorb more -> Mimi looks less like one of us -> her exceptionality becomes evidence of non-personhood`.

The institution does not need to impose every dehumanizing idea from above. Successful instrumentalization creates cultural feedback that students themselves can reproduce.

### 4.4 Sheena sees the hidden return state

Sheena reaches the infirmary/recovery space and sees Mimi in the condition concealed by the clean-room returns: blood-covered, injured, physically spent (`p_0049-p_0052`). Her shock is specifically epistemic:

`「ミミが今でもこんな姿で帰ってきてるって 全然知らなかった」`

She has been beside Mimi every day and still did not know that Mimi continued returning like this.

This is not evidence that Sheena failed to care. It demonstrates the effectiveness of Mimi's relationship-conditioned concealment policy established in V03 and V04.

### 4.5 Mimi confirms concealment as care

Asked why she hid it, Mimi explains that she properly showered and changed into clean clothes before returning because if Sheena saw her injured and dirty, Sheena might cry again (`p_0051-p_0053`).

That motivation matters later during reconciliation. Mimi was not lying because Sheena's concern was meaningless. She concealed **because Sheena's concern mattered**.

The harmful behavior and the caring motive must remain analytically separable.

### 4.6 Mimi finally authors a reason for fighting

Mimi then states the logic V04 left unresolved (`p_0053-p_0055`). If she does not do this, other people end up damaged. Those other people cannot resurrect. If Mimi becomes everyone's substitute, then nobody else has to die.

This is the strongest direct answer yet to the recurring question of what Mimi fights for.

Current formulation:

`others are mortal + Mimi is uniquely recoverable -> Mimi treats her own body as preferred substitute -> absorb combat burden -> reduce mortal casualties`.

The motive is **protective and concrete**. It is not:

- an abstract patriotic ideology;
- a death wish;
- proof that Mimi feels no pain;
- proof that Mimi thinks her body has zero value;
- an explicit romantic confession.

Sheena is a privileged beneficiary inside this policy, but Mimi says "everyone" and reasons from others' inability to resurrect. The correct V05 transition is therefore **personally authored protective substitution**, with relationship-specific intensity around Sheena.

### 4.7 Sheena offers another model of usefulness

Sheena explains why she has been training (`p_0055-p_0056`). Her healing attribute can relieve pain and fear if developed. She wants to become stronger enough to help Mimi and others by another method.

This is the clearest contrast yet between two forms of usefulness:

- **Mimi:** concentrate damage in the recoverable body.
- **Sheena:** increase capacity to heal/support damaged bodies.

Neither is currently powerful enough to solve the war. The difference is what each policy does to the actor's own body and agency.

### 4.8 Mimi converts protection into permission to remain weak

Mimi rejects Sheena's premise. If Sheena is weak, she says, Sheena can simply do nothing (`p_0057-p_0058`). She does not need to train. Mimi will fight enough for everyone; Sheena can wait, bake cookies, gather flowers, and do enjoyable things with her.

This is emotionally revealing. Mimi does not despise Sheena's ordinary competencies. She values them so much that she wants to preserve Sheena inside them.

But the protective fantasy is asymmetrical:

`Sheena remains ordinary and safe -> Mimi monopolizes violent usefulness`.

Mimi's love/care thus risks freezing both people into roles: Sheena as waiting home, Mimi as endlessly repairable shield.

### 4.9 Sheena rejects the bargain without denying its results

Sheena says the way of thinking is wrong (`p_0058-p_0060`). Her objection is not "Mimi failed to save people." She explicitly recognizes that deaths and injuries have decreased. The objection is that aggregate success cannot make Mimi's injuries morally unmentionable.

Her position is approximately:

`even if everyone else is safe -> if Mimi is being hurt -> I still need to be allowed to hate that outcome`.

This strengthens reciprocal non-disposability into a resistance against utilitarian erasure of the concentrated victim.

### 4.10 The quarrel reveals symmetrical control attempts

The conflict escalates because each girl tries to control the other's protective behavior.

- Mimi: stop dangerous practice; wait in the room; let me do this.
- Sheena: stop fighting in this self-destructive way; do not conceal the cost; let me help.

Mimi finally rejects Sheena's requests too and leaves (`p_0060-p_0064`). They sleep turned away from one another.

The quarrel is therefore not a temporary misunderstanding that can be solved by "explaining feelings." Their feelings are already understandable. The conflict is about incompatible principles of agency and burden.

## 5. CH22 - `第22話 迷子の子供`

### 5.1 Sheena's passivity is reclassified as defense against helplessness

The argument's most damaging phrase for Sheena is Mimi's claim that if she is weak, she can simply do nothing. CH22 uses that phrase to expose the mechanism underneath Sheena's early-series passivity (`V05 / CH22 / p_0069-p_0076`).

Sheena did not believe weakness was admirable. Rather:

`if I look directly at suffering -> I may discover I cannot change it -> that discovery forces me to confront my own powerlessness -> therefore it is emotionally easier to declare that nothing can be done and remain detached`.

This is a major character-model revision because it prevents a simulator from representing early Sheena as naturally passive or low-empathy. Her withdrawal was partly **avoidance of helplessness shame/pain**.

Mimi has made that strategy impossible to sustain. Once Sheena knows the specific person absorbing the cost, "nothing can be done" no longer provides moral anesthesia.

### 5.2 Ari teaches Mimi that memory is enacted, not merely stored in objects

During the quarrel Mimi spends time with Ari (`p_0075-p_0082`). The absence of Sheena's normal grooming is immediately visible: Ari notices that Mimi's hair has not been combed in the usual way.

The sequence revisits Ari clearing Seiran's belongings. Mimi dislikes the idea that Seiran's things will disappear from the room. Ari explains that memory persists in repeated ordinary acts - warm milk, sitting together, looking through the window, and the habits through which a relationship has already entered one's life.

Her governing idea is:

`「私たちが覚えてる限り 何も変わらない」`

This does not say objects are irrelevant. V03-V04 established the pendant's enormous importance. It says that relationship memory cannot be reduced to preserving a room as a museum.

For Mimi, who is likely to outlive ordinary partners, this is also indirect education in how mortal attachment can persist after material/social routines change.

### 5.3 Concentrated protection produces retrospective blame

Back in school, students observe that fewer people are now being summoned because Mimi can effectively cover the work of many fighters (`p_0082-p_0087`). Some then ask why, if Mimi could do this, she did not do it earlier when classmates died.

This is an ugly but analytically important feedback loop:

`Mimi absorbs more danger -> others become safer -> safety becomes the new baseline -> previous deaths are reinterpreted as preventable -> Mimi is blamed for not having self-instrumentalized enough sooner`.

Successful sacrifice does not necessarily earn personhood. It can increase expectations for more sacrifice.

### 5.4 Sheena recognizes the kindness she accidentally rejected

Sheena continues training alone despite fatigue and eventually falls asleep at her desk (`p_0084-p_0089`). She remains anxious about the quarrel but does not abandon the healing identity merely to restore immediate harmony.

She then arrives at a crucial correction. Mimi hid her wounds out of kindness. In condemning the concealment and the underlying substitution policy together, Sheena also rejected that kindness:

`「それを隠したのはミミの優しさで / 私はその優しさも一緒に否定してしまったんだ」`

This is exactly the kind of motive/action separation the simulation architecture was designed to preserve.

Sheena's revised appraisal becomes:

`Mimi's behavior is harmful/unacceptable to me` **and** `Mimi did it partly because she loves/cares about me`.

That dual representation makes reconciliation possible without requiring Sheena to endorse the behavior.

### 5.5 Esta turns abstract rescue into a named beneficiary

Sheena meets Esta-senpai, the student Mimi saved in CH21 (`p_0088-p_0094`). Esta has been looking for Mimi in order to thank her and gives Sheena a small food/candy gift to share.

This prevents another analytic simplification. Mimi's substitution does not merely serve an anonymous institution. It saves identifiable people who can feel gratitude and continue living.

The ethical problem remains precisely because the saved lives are real.

### 5.6 Mimi asks how long a quarrel lasts

Mimi insists to Ari that she was not wrong. Ari does not force a verdict. She observes that both girls acted because they cared about each other, making the fight sad precisely because the motives are protective.

Mimi then asks:

`「ケンカっていつ終わるの?」`

Ari's response is structurally important:

`「ミミちゃんがここにいる限りは ずっとこのままかもね」`

This can be read as more than "relationships have arguments." The underlying contradiction - immortal substitute versus mortal partner who refuses her disposability - may recur for as long as Mimi inhabits this social world.

V05 therefore prepares reconciliation as **conflict management**, not conflict elimination.

## 6. CH23 - `第23話 わたしのままで`

### 6.1 Adults notice that conflict itself marks Mimi's humanization

Staff discussion reframes the quarrel from another angle (`V05 / CH23 / p_0097-p_0103`). Mimi once went to war with little visible doubt or relational friction. Now she has attachments strong enough to generate competing desires, hurt, anger, and a need to reconcile.

One adult describes her as becoming:

`「ただの女の子」`

This is not a claim that combat power or immortality disappeared. It is a claim about expanding ordinary subjectivity. A weapon does not need to negotiate incompatible care with a roommate. A person does.

### 6.2 Mimi wants repair but lacks the procedure

Sheena finds Mimi crying alone on the stairs (`p_0103-p_0107`). Mimi says she wants to make up but does not know how.

This is highly useful simulation evidence. Mimi's low-filter directness does not imply high social competence in conflict repair. She can identify the desired relational endpoint before knowing the conversational steps that reach it.

### 6.3 Sheena apologizes for rejecting motive, not for having a moral boundary

Sheena apologizes first. She now understands that Mimi cleaned herself and hid injuries because she did not want Sheena to worry or cry. She is sorry she ignored that motivation and responded only with anger (`p_0106-p_0110`).

Then she does something more important:

**she preserves disagreement.**

Sheena says she still cannot agree with Mimi's method.

Mimi initially treats that as incompatible with reconciliation. This exposes a simple conflict model: if the belief conflict remains, perhaps the relationship conflict must remain too.

Sheena teaches another possibility.

### 6.4 Equal agency replaces forced consensus

Sheena asks whether Mimi still intends to fight to her limit. Mimi says yes; this is something only she can do. Sheena answers in effect: understood - and then asserts that she likewise will not stop her training (`p_0110-p_0115`).

This is the decisive V05 relationship transition:

`recognize your agency without endorsing your policy -> preserve my own agency without demanding your surrender -> continue relationship`.

The symmetry is important. Sheena does not claim moral authority to control Mimi while refusing control from Mimi. Each is allowed a choice the other fears.

### 6.5 Sheena changes what waiting means

Sheena does not abandon the return promise. Instead, she changes its epistemic conditions. She will wait for Mimi at the infirmary too. Mimi does not need to clean herself into a reassuring fiction first. Sheena asks:

`「どんな姿でも もう隠さなくて大丈夫だから / なかったことにしないでね」`

The return grammar therefore changes from:

`deployment -> hidden repair/cleaning -> safe domestic reunion`

to the desired:

`deployment -> reveal actual return condition -> receive care while injured -> domestic life can follow without erasing the cost`.

This is a major anti-instrumental practice. Suffering becomes harder to convert into an invisible input cost.

### 6.6 "Is being weak bad?" - Mimi challenges Sheena's self-worth model

Mimi then asks whether weakness is a bad thing (`p_0115-p_0121`). Her reasoning is unexpectedly sophisticated despite simple language. Sheena already does many things Mimi values:

- performs well academically;
- bakes sweets;
- cleans and maintains ordinary space;
- shares sleep and comfort;
- creates a place Mimi wants to return to.

Why do those things not count as enough?

Mimi's most important question is approximately:

`「ミミのせいで シーナは 今のままじゃいれないの?」`

This is not merely "Mimi wants Sheena helpless." It reveals fear that Mimi's presence is forcing Sheena to become someone she does not want to be.

### 6.7 Sheena separates growth from self-rejection

Sheena's answer is crucial:

`「そうじゃないよ… 私 ミミと出会えたから」`

Because she met Mimi, Sheena can finally say that she **wants** to change. Weakness frustrates her not because weakness makes her worthless, but because she now has concrete people and situations in which she wants the capacity to act. Mimi's presence did not prove the old Sheena invalid; it made desire and agency possible.

This revises the character model from:

`I must become useful or I am worthless`

toward:

`there are things I care about enough that I want greater capacity, while my ordinary self remains valuable`.

The distinction will matter enormously for later healer-role modeling. Training driven by self-hatred would predict very different overextension behavior than training driven by chosen care.

### 6.8 Mutual attachment is explicitly transformative

Mimi answers that meeting Sheena meant she was no longer alone. Sheena says she knows Mimi has always cherished her and asks to be allowed to protect Mimi too (`p_0120-p_0126`). Their hands interlace.

Mutual separation fear is explicit:

`「いなくならないで」`

and Sheena answers:

`「ここにいるよ」`

The scene remains stronger than a generic friendship repair and continues to intensify the romantic/intimate trajectory, but V05 still does not give the pair a mutually adopted formal romantic label.

### 6.9 Adult autonomy philosophy

The chapter coda turns the local conflict into an institutional ethical proposition (`p_0125-p_0128`). Adults discuss the painful fact that immortal Mimi will eventually lose attachments. One position wonders whether avoiding attachment might therefore have been kinder.

The counterposition rejects that preventative deprivation. Loss is part of living; new people and possibilities arise in the time that follows. Adults can provide opportunities, but choice belongs to the children themselves:

`「与えるのは大人の仕事だけど 選ぶのはアイツら自身でしょ?」`

This is one of the series' clearest explicit autonomy philosophies.

It does **not** erase the institution's coercive military structure. The same institution still trains children to kill and treats Mimi as force capacity. The contradiction should be preserved: individual adults can articulate autonomy-respecting care inside a system that materially constrains children's choices.

## 7. CH24 - `第24話 会いたい`

### 7.1 External observers now perceive romantic possibility

CH24 uses Esta as a social mirror (`V05 / CH24 / p_0131-p_0138`). She asks Ari whether Sheena and Mimi might be dating:

`「シーナちゃんとミミちゃんって もしかしてつき合ってるの?」`

Ari's answer is cautious rather than affirmative:

`「今はそういう風には…」`

This is new evidence, but its evidentiary class must be kept precise. It shows that the pair's changed atmosphere is legible enough to peers to trigger romantic classification. It is **not** a mutual self-label by Sheena and Mimi.

Earlier observers had read some of their dynamic as parent/child-like; now the atmosphere has shifted. The corpus should therefore track a social-perception transition without declaring the relationship formally resolved.

### 7.2 Ari resumes ordinary life without grief disappearing

Ari studies, manages assignments, and can decline an invitation to eat because she still has work to finish (`p_0138-p_0143`). She recognizes that friends are being considerate and accepts the possibility of another time.

This strongly supports the V04 prospective model:

`grief remains active + ordinary function resumes`.

The two are not mutually exclusive states.

### 7.3 Accidental reference reveals the persistence of absence

Esta casually asks whether "Sei" is waiting in Ari's room, not realizing Seiran died. Ari has to say that Seiran is no longer there (`p_0142-p_0147`).

The mistake cuts through the stabilized routine. Ari reflects that she had told herself nothing essential would change even if the room stopped physically being Seiran's room, but actual separation has scattered feelings and words in ways she did not anticipate.

Her V03/V04 model is therefore not rejected, but refined. Memory can persist without objects, yet persistence of memory does not mean lived experience stays unchanged.

### 7.4 Esta supplies a comparison case: fighting beside the person you love

In the rain/memorial sequence, Esta apologizes and reveals that she also lost a girl she loved (`p_0147-p_0158`). Her loved person remained outwardly strong in battle and in private; Esta repeatedly told her that she could cry, but never saw the break she expected.

Ari asks:

`「好きな人と 一緒に戦うのは どんな気持ちですか?」`

Esta answers simply:

`「嬉しい」`

Knowing her beloved was nearby gave Esta emotional support and helped her continue when she was terrified.

Ari then explains the asymmetry in her own situation. She could not fight beside Seiran, but she wanted at least to remain close to Seiran's feelings. When Seiran chose the examination/deployment path, Ari smiled, encouraged her, and saw her off.

### 7.5 Support after loss remains epistemically unresolved

Ari wonders whether not stopping Seiran was wrong. Esta asks whether she regrets not holding Seiran back. Ari says no - but she can never now ask Seiran whether Ari's support really helped (`p_0157-p_0162`).

Esta offers an analogy from her own relationship: because her beloved never broke, Esta could keep moving through fear; perhaps Seiran likewise needed Ari's words at moments when she could not stand alone.

That is a useful **grief-repair inference**, not direct proof of Seiran's unspoken V03 interiority. The ledger should preserve the source status:

- Ari's uncertainty: direct.
- Esta's analogy/hypothesis: direct as Esta's belief.
- claim that Seiran definitely needed Ari in precisely that way: not established.

The chapter ends with rain as memory trigger. Ari remembers that the day of Seiran's death was rainy (`p_0161-p_0163`). Grief remains stateful and cue-sensitive even while ordinary life continues.

## 8. Sheena - V05 character-model transition

### 8.1 Fear remains compatible with chosen action
V05 **PRESERVES/STRENGTHENS** Sheena's established model: fear does not disappear, but it increasingly fails to veto action.

### 8.2 Helplessness avoidance is now explicit
The largest retrospective revision is the mechanism beneath old passivity:

`perceived inability to help -> anticipated confrontation with powerless self -> emotional withdrawal / "nothing can be done" -> temporary protection from helplessness`.

This should replace any simplistic simulation rule such as "Sheena is naturally passive."

### 8.3 Healing identity is chosen growth, not self-erasure

## 13. Mortality / expendability / personhood state

V05 adds several durable distinctions.

1. **Aggregate harm reduction can coexist with concentrated exploitation.** Fewer children are injured because Mimi absorbs the violence.
2. **Recoverability creates substitution pressure.** Mimi's resurrection/restoration capacity becomes a reason for her to prefer her own body as the casualty sink.
3. **Hidden injury is itself a personhood problem.** If the cost must be cleaned away before ordinary reunion, others can consume the benefit while losing access to the suffering that produced it.
4. **Weakness is not equivalent to worthlessness.** Mimi explicitly values Sheena's ordinary competencies even while Sheena seeks additional capacity.
5. **Protecting another person includes respecting self-authored growth.** Keeping Sheena physically safe by forbidding her chosen practice is not a complete personhood solution.
6. **The immortal person's body remains morally claimable by others as loved body.** Sheena refuses the idea that Mimi's injuries stop mattering merely because they are repairable.
7. **The mortal person's agency remains morally claimable by herself.** Sheena refuses to be frozen into the safe waiting role.
