## 2. Collection thesis — relational negative space becomes evidence

The numbered manga is built around absence. A dead roommate leaves a bed and belongings. A dead lover leaves a pendant. A deployed child comes back carrying wounds that may or may not be visible. People become militarily legible through strength, test scores, survivability, and deployment readiness while their ordinary life accumulates in kitchens, rooms, hands, flowers, meals, jokes, clothes, homework, and habits.

The Side Stories reconstruct what might be called the **relational negative space** around those absences.

They repeatedly ask a question that the mainline's mortality ethic depends upon:

> **What made this person irreplaceable before the system reduced them to a vacancy, casualty, combatant, patient, or asset?**

The collection's answer is rarely grand declaration. Irreplaceability accumulates through repeated observation and mundane participation:

- making food with somebody rather than merely sharing a room;
- noticing how a smile changes depending on whether it is socially performed or genuinely spontaneous;
- knowing where a friend hides when skipping class;
- remembering who finds whom after battle;
- learning who takes achievement as proof of usefulness;
- letting another person choose their clothes, address, and future without deciding their identity for them;
- seeing a dead roommate not as a flawless lost ideal but as a difficult, caring, frustrated person with whom unfinished tensions remained.

That makes the Side Stories unusually important to the mature character-simulation project. They do not primarily add plot. They add **conditional history**: how particular habits, defensive strategies, care responses, self-conceptions, and relational expectations formed before the mainline needed them.

# 3. SS01 — `きらきら`

## 3.1 Placement and evidentiary role

**Source:** `SS01 p_0003-p_0028`.  
**Best placement:** established Sheena–Mimi domestic period, securely after their earliest forced-roommate formation and plausibly V02+; exact slot unresolved.  
**Evidence class:** SUPPLEMENTAL / INTERSTITIAL.  
**Chronology confidence:** medium.

Nothing in the story requires post-V07 lover status. Conversely, the degree of bodily ease and shared domestic habit is stronger than their earliest V01 estrangement. The correct routing is therefore a broad established-attachment window rather than an invented chapter number.

## 3.2 “Doing nothing” is positive life, not absence of plot

The story begins with Sheena and Mimi imagining a day without obligations: waking when they want, spending time as they want, staying up late, and allowing ordinary desire rather than institutional schedule to govern the day.

For these characters, “nothing” is not empty time.

The school has repeatedly made time externally owned:

`class -> exercise -> summons -> treatment -> return -> class again`.

A day that can be organized by appetite, sleepiness, food, touch, and mutual preference is therefore a small form of lived autonomy.

This strengthens the series' recurring distinction between **survival** and **having a life**. Mimi's immortality guarantees neither ordinary control of time nor ordinary companionship. The very possibility of wasting a day with Sheena is valuable because it is non-instrumental.

## 3.3 Shared making changes the meaning of domesticity

The central activity becomes making strawberry jam together. The important fact is not merely that Sheena can cook. The kitchen staff note that Sheena used to come and make things **alone**. Sheena herself then thinks of Laura and recognizes that although Laura had been her roommate, the two of them had never done this together (`SS01`, especially around `p_0024`).

The comparison must not be read as a ranking in which Mimi proves Laura was unimportant.

It instead sharpens three different relational states:

1. **Laura mattered enough that Sheena still spontaneously thinks of her.**
2. **Room-sharing alone did not automatically produce the same form of domestic participation.**
3. **With Mimi, Sheena increasingly converts solitary stabilizing routines into shared activity.**

This strengthens the V02+ model that ordinary life is no longer merely Sheena's private defense against war. It is something she **co-authors with another person**.

## 3.4 Laura memory and Mimi attachment are not zero-sum

A weaker character model might assume that deepening attachment to Mimi requires the old roommate to recede into irrelevance, or that remembering Laura threatens Mimi's specialness.

The story supports neither.

Sheena can remember Laura inside a happy activity with Mimi without the memory destroying the present moment. The dead person remains particular; the living relationship remains distinct. This is consistent with the larger corpus's grief ethic: continuity does not require replacement, forgetting, or emotional exclusivity toward the dead.

For simulation, this gives Sheena a useful mature rule:

`ordinary present echoes a lost relationship -> memory may surface -> compare without collapsing people into substitutes -> remain able to enjoy the present relationship`.

## 3.5 Low-stakes intimacy remains voice-consistent

Mimi is physically direct, eager, sensory, and interested in immediate shared pleasure. Sheena is more organizing and practical but increasingly allows and reciprocates the closeness. The story ends in shared-room intimacy and affectionate goodnight contact rather than a large declaration.

This preserves the relationship's central grammar: intimacy is enacted through **routine before rhetoric**.

### SS01 claim transitions

- **STRENGTHEN:** shared ordinary life as positive jointly made futurity.
- **STRENGTHEN:** Sheena's solitary routine can become relational rather than disappearing.
- **STRENGTHEN:** Laura remains a living memory without functioning as a rival/substitute category.
- **PRESERVE:** do not force an exact numbered-volume chronology or lover label absent direct markers.

# 4. SS02 — `ばいばい`

## 4.1 Placement: a genuine pre-V01 bridge

**Source:** `SS02 p_0029-p_0060`.  
**Placement:** **PRE-V01**, ending directly at the V01 CH01 classroom death announcement.  
**Evidence class:** SUPPLEMENTAL / PREQUEL.  
**Chronology confidence:** very high.

This is the collection's most important chronology result. The story gives a face, personality, conflict structure, and institutional trajectory to **Laura**, the dead roommate whose absence organizes V01's opening.

The final pages reproduce the school announcement that the previous night's attack produced one casualty from the class. That casualty is Laura. The story therefore does not merely occur “sometime before V01”; it **hands directly into V01's opening state**.

## 4.2 Laura is not an idealized dead-roommate abstraction

V01 allowed the reader to know Sheena was disturbed by the death, the emptied room, and the system's rapid conversion of vacancy into a new roommate slot. It did not show what living with Laura had actually been like.

`ばいばい` materially revises the mature model.

Laura is:

- academically exceptional;
- highly sensitive to comparative competence;
- increasingly oriented toward combat qualification;
- capable of care and aesthetic attention;
- lonely and self-pressuring;
- frustrated by the asymmetry between her own expectations and Sheena's relative weakness;
- not always gentle with Sheena;
- still genuinely attached to her.

Sheena's grief is therefore grief for a **particular, imperfect relationship**, not nostalgia for a saintly dead friend.

That matters because unresolved friction often makes bereavement more complex rather than less real. Sheena loses not only somebody she loved or valued, but also the possibility of future repair, mutual maturation, and unfinished ordinary time.

## 4.3 Achievement becomes a route into weapon usefulness

The opening foregrounds Laura's high academic performance. But within this institution, achievement does not remain a harmless school metric. Laura increasingly interprets being “good” as needing to become useful both in the classroom and on the battlefield.

As combat progression becomes available, she internalizes the proposition that the school exists to prepare them for war and that her competence ought to convert into military value.

The important transition is not simply:

`good student -> good soldier`.

It is:

`high achievement -> comparative identity -> usefulness expectation -> combat-readiness pressure -> self-worth increasingly tied to being capable where the institution needs capability`.

This makes Laura one of the corpus's clearest examples of **internalized institutional utility without simple coercive enthusiasm**.

She is not merely ordered to care about usefulness. She has learned to use usefulness as a measure of herself.

## 4.4 Sheena's weakness has an ambivalent social function

Laura's frustration with Sheena is particularly valuable for reconstructing the pre-V01 Sheena.

Sheena's lower combat/academic/magical capability can make her seem comparatively protected from some performance expectations. Laura's `いいよね シーナちゃんは`-type frustration is not a neutral statement of difference. It exposes resentment toward what appears, from Laura's position, to be the privilege of being unable to shoulder the same expectations.

That does not mean Sheena actually experiences weakness as privilege. The mainline later establishes her helplessness avoidance and painful concern about being unable to act.

The mature synthesis is therefore asymmetric:

- **to Laura**, Sheena can look exempt from burdens Laura feels compelled to carry;
- **to Sheena**, weakness can feel like inability to prevent suffering;
- both interpretations arise from the same institution's unequal conversion of capacity into obligation.

This is useful evidence against simplistic “strong characters have agency / weak characters lack value” modeling.

## 4.5 Care persists through relational friction

The story also supplies flower-making, food/ordinary gestures, concern, and Laura's continued attention to Sheena. Their conflict does not invalidate their care.

That makes Laura a pre-series antecedent to a major later KimiShinu relationship principle:

**disagreement or asymmetry does not automatically cancel attachment.**

The difference is tragic: Laura and Sheena do not get enough time to metabolize the tension. Laura's death freezes the relationship before it can become something else.

## 4.6 The V01 vacancy is now historically denser

The last transition into the death announcement makes V01's room replacement much more severe in mature hindsight.

When Mimi is assigned to the vacant roommate position, Sheena is not simply being asked to accept “a new roommate after someone died.” She is being asked to continue inside a physical arrangement whose previous relationship ended with unresolved affection, resentment, competence asymmetry, and sudden death.

This **strengthens** the current mature explanation for Sheena's early aversion while preserving the V01 prospective artifact. A V01 reader could not yet know Laura's internal pressures or the texture of the relationship.

### SS02 claim transitions

- **STRENGTHEN:** Sheena's V01 death non-normalization is grounded in particular relational history.
- **REVISE current mature Laura model:** from absent roommate/casualty to high-achieving, usefulness-oriented, caring but conflicted peer.
- **STRENGTHEN:** institutional achievement can become self-authored military obligation.
- **STRENGTHEN:** unequal capability creates relational resentment as well as protection.
- **PRESERVE:** V01 remains correct about what was knowable before this later prequel evidence.

# 5. SS03 — `くらくら`

## 5.1 Placement and function

**Source:** `SS03 p_0061-p_0084`.  
**Placement:** early V01 interstitial: after Laura's death and Mimi's arrival in Class 14, before/around preparation for the V01 mock battle.  
**Evidence class:** SUPPLEMENTAL / INTERSTITIAL ADULT PERSPECTIVE.  
**Chronology confidence:** high.

The story shifts the camera away from the children and gives institutional life an adult relational layer through **Omi-sensei** and **Fran**.

## 5.2 Omi-sensei's care is procedural rather than popularity-seeking

Omi is reserved, serious, and concerned with teaching quality and student outcomes more than whether students like her. Fran teases her about student popularity, but Omi's own orientation is closer to:

`teacher responsibility -> structure / preparation / realistic training -> student welfare`.

This is an important differentiation inside the adult staff.

KimiShinu's institution cannot be modeled as one psychological actor. Individuals may participate in the same harmful system while relating to students through different mixtures of affection, duty, pragmatism, normalization, and concern.

Omi's seriousness should therefore not be generated as emotional indifference. Her care is simply less demonstrative and more embedded in pedagogy/procedure.

## 5.3 Adult staff know the class is carrying grief

The story situates the impending mock battle against the immediate aftermath of Laura's death. That reinforces an institutional pattern already visible from the student side: grief and training overlap rather than receiving cleanly separated time.

The adult perspective does not reveal a hidden therapeutic infrastructure that the mainline had somehow omitted. Instead, staff continue planning the next training demand while recognizing that the class is unsettled.

The system's violence therefore remains ordinary not because adults literally cannot perceive distress, but because **perceiving distress does not stop the schedule**.

## 5.4 Mimi is a staff-management problem and a child somebody asks another teacher to care for

Fran and Omi discuss Mimi's new classroom integration and the mock battle. Fran's ending request — effectively asking Omi to look after Mimi — matters because it personalizes what could otherwise be read purely as asset transfer.

This strengthens the V09 Fran model:

**personal care and institutional management can coexist in the same adult.**

Fran has participated in a system that used and confined Mimi, yet she also forms individual protective attachments and wants other adults to recognize Mimi as somebody who needs looking after.

Those facts are morally uncomfortable precisely because neither cancels the other.

## 5.5 Fran–Omi interpersonal register

Fran repeatedly teases Omi with bodily/social closeness and enjoys provoking visible embarrassment or irritation. Omi is more restrained.

This is strong interaction evidence for an established, comfortable adult dyad. It is **not** sufficient to classify them as romantic or sexual partners.

Current relationship state:

`familiar professional relationship -> asymmetrical teasing/provocation -> bodily/social comfort sufficient for embarrassment play -> exact romantic classification OPEN`.

### SS03 claim transitions

- **NEW:** Omi-sensei as reserved, procedure-oriented adult caretaker/teacher.
- **STRENGTHEN:** staff are differentiated people inside the institution, not a single bureaucratic voice.
- **STRENGTHEN:** grief recognition can coexist with uninterrupted militarized scheduling.
- **STRENGTHEN:** Fran's personal care for Mimi exists inside, not outside, institutional management.
- **OPEN:** Fran–Omi romantic/sexual interpretation.

# 6. SS04 — `ずきずき`

## 6.1 Placement

**Source:** `SS04 p_0085-p_0098`.  
**Best placement:** governing present post-V07 Esta intervention, with earlier/recalled Esta–Rika protector history embedded within it.  
**Evidence class:** SUPPLEMENTAL / LATER INTERSTITIAL WITH RETROSPECTIVE RELATIONSHIP MATERIAL.  
**Chronology confidence:** high for governing present.

The story is valuable because it prevents Esta's V07 grief-driven self-deprioritization from being modeled as social isolation.

## 6.2 Rika knows where to find Esta

Rika catches Esta avoiding/skipping class and does so with the confidence of somebody who knows her habits. Their dialogue establishes an ordinary baseline of academic teasing, practical monitoring, and familiarity.

Esta may be naturally capable of securing adequate test results with relatively little preparation, while Rika remembers earlier failure/low-score patterns. Their friendship therefore includes a long enough history for competence to be discussed without ceremony.

## 6.3 Their protector logic is reciprocal but not identical to Mimi's

The battlefield conversation matters more than the school teasing.

Rika tells Esta that if she is hurt, she should let Rika find her; Rika presents herself as reliable. Esta resists relying on Rika because Rika is also repeatedly exposed and badly hurt. Esta, in turn, wants to be the shield for Rika.

This gives the corpus another dyadic self-risk mechanism:

`I care about you -> I want to carry danger for you -> your reciprocal wish to carry danger for me prevents clean one-way substitution`.

Unlike the Sheena–Mimi V05 conflict, neither participant here is an immortal recoverable body. The dangerous logic is therefore not justified through resurrection asymmetry. It emerges from ordinary attachment and reciprocal over-protection.

Rika's corrective is especially important: **do not simply become less important because you want to protect me; let me find you too.**

## 6.4 Post-loss care refuses compulsory forgetting

The later/present portion supplies one of the collection's cleanest grief-care formulations. Rika does not demand that Esta forget the person she lost. She does not demand that Esta “recover” on somebody else's emotional timetable. Her core request is much narrower:

`ただ / しぶとく 生きててくれたら / それでいいよ`

She wants Esta to keep living stubbornly.

This is an important personhood countermodel to grief-driven self-disposability.

The care policy is:

`bereaved person does not value own future highly -> do not erase the lost relationship -> do not require cheerful recovery -> continue asserting that the bereaved person's ongoing life still matters`.

This aligns with, but is distinct from, Sheena's V07 insistence that Esta still has a claim to care.

## 6.5 Esta remains socially held

The reference to promises involving Sheena and Mimi indicates that Esta's V07 intervention has not vanished after the chapter where it occurred. Her social environment now contains several people who reject her attempt to remove herself from the category of lives worth protecting.

The mature Esta model should therefore not be:

`loss -> self-deprioritization -> isolated withdrawal`.

It is closer to:

`loss -> own future loses weight -> self-deprioritizing choices become tempting -> peers repeatedly insist on reciprocal survival claims -> grief persists inside a network that refuses to let her self-erasure become uncontested`.

## 6.6 Rika is not the deceased girl

This story is also a useful identity constraint. Rika is alive and actively supporting Esta in the governing present. Therefore the girl Esta had previously lost is **not Rika**.

Do not collapse Esta's relational history into one person simply because both relationships involve battlefield care.

### SS04 claim transitions

- **STRENGTHEN:** Esta's grief-driven self-deprioritization occurs inside ongoing peer attachment rather than social emptiness.
- **NEW:** Rika as a persistent, searching, survival-oriented friend.
- **NEW:** mutual-protector dynamics among mortal peers.
- **STRENGTHEN:** healthy grief care can reject forced forgetting and forced “moving on.”
- **REJECT:** Rika = Esta's deceased loved girl.

# 7. SS05 — `めろめろ`

## 7.1 Placement

**Source:** `SS05 p_0099-p_0112`.  
**Placement:** post-V04 Haru introduction; exact relation to V08 `ゆらめき` unresolved.  
**Evidence class:** SUPPLEMENTAL / HARU SOCIAL-PERCEPTION STUDY.  
**Chronology confidence:** high for post-V04 lower bound, medium-low for exact slot.

The story is told largely through **Claire's** attraction to Haru and is especially useful because it supplies the kind of external projection that V08 later warns the reader not to confuse with Haru's self-definition.

## 7.2 Claire's attraction is clear; Haru's reciprocal attraction is not

Claire notices Haru's face, tracks Haru's interactions, becomes jealous of Sheena's recurring gifts of cookies/onigiri, and wonders how Sheena became close to Haru.

This supports:

**Claire → Haru romantic attraction: high confidence.**

It does not support:

**Haru → Claire reciprocal romantic attraction: established.**

Haru notices Claire's activation and interacts with her, but the story does not require a romantic interpretation of Haru's side.

This distinction should remain explicit in relationship simulation.

## 7.3 External observers project gender meaning onto presentation

Claire sees Haru's trousers/masculine presentation and wonders whether a masculine form of address, `ハルくん`, would make Haru more comfortable. Claire then asks whether she can use it.

The significant point is that **Claire generated the hypothesis**.

Haru's response is essentially:

`クレアの好きにすれば？`

and:

`ボクは どうでもいい`.

In this context, Haru does not reveal that `くん` is a deeply required self-label. Nor does Haru object to it.

That fits the mature V08 model extremely well:

**self-authorship is not the same thing as rigid control over every external sign.**

Haru may resist when another person tries to decide what Haru *is*, while remaining low-investment about a particular interpersonal form of address.

## 7.4 The story strengthens the anti-labeling constraint rather than solving Haru

Nothing here establishes a fixed modern gender identity or sexual-orientation label for Haru.

The evidentiary layers remain separate:

- `ボク`: source fact about self-reference;
- boys' uniform/trousers: source fact about presentation choice;
- tolerance of `ハルくん`: source fact about this interpersonal address;
- V08 uncertainty about future attraction: source fact;
- self-chosen identity category: **not supplied here**.

The correct model is therefore **more specific behavior with continued categorical openness**.

## 7.5 Sheena–Haru friendship receives quiet corroboration

Claire's jealousy depends on the ordinary fact that Sheena regularly gives Haru food and has become meaningfully close to Haru. That strengthens the V04–V09 model of Sheena's widening field of care without requiring the story to become a Sheena episode.

### SS05 claim transitions

- **STRENGTHEN:** Haru self-authorship ≠ blanket refusal of externally chosen wording/help.
- **STRENGTHEN:** external observers may project identity meaning onto Haru's presentation.
- **PRESERVE:** Haru's categorical identity/attraction remains self-authored and unresolved.
- **NEW:** Claire→Haru attraction, high confidence.
- **OPEN:** Haru→Claire reciprocal romance.
- **STRENGTHEN:** Sheena–Haru ordinary friendship/caretaking.

# 8. SS06 — `ぴかぴか`

## 8.1 Placement

**Source:** `SS06 p_0113-p_0145`.  
**Placement:** before V03 CH14 Seiran death, after Ari/Seiran are already within Sheena's school-peer orbit; likely V01–V02-era relational development. Exact chapter slot unresolved.  
**Evidence class:** SUPPLEMENTAL / ARI–SEIRAN RELATIONSHIP HISTORY.  
**Chronology confidence:** high for pre-V03 upper bound; medium for exact placement.

This is the collection's richest relationship-history story.

V08 already established as current mature fact that Ari and Seiran had been dating before Seiran's death. `ぴかぴか` does not need to “prove” romance anew. Its value is developmental: **what did Seiran notice about Ari, and how did mutual specialness become behaviorally legible before the relationship's terminal arc?**

## 8.2 Seiran watches Ari closely enough to distinguish smiles

The story is organized around Seiran's observation of Ari's body and behavior: hair, skin, eyes, hands, eating, posture, homework, movement, and especially facial expression.

Seiran notices that Ari smiles frequently but suspects that not every smile is the same. Some of Ari's brightness appears adaptive: a way of moving through social life, smoothing interaction, and continuing day to day.

This materially revises any simple “Ari = naturally cheerful extrovert” simulation.

A better model is:

`social situation -> Ari often deploys accessible smile / brightness -> internal state may or may not match outward ease -> trusted relational context can produce more spontaneous affect`.

## 8.3 Seiran's protectiveness originates in sustained attention, not only duty

Later mainline volumes make Seiran's protector logic dramatic: she wants to become stronger so Ari will be less exposed, eventually gives away survival resources, and dies trying to return.

`ぴかぴか` shows the quieter substrate underneath that behavior.

Seiran is already habitually monitoring Ari's condition. Her attention is not solely tactical. She notices ordinary pleasure, discomfort, style, expressions, and changes over time.

That means the later policy:

`Ari vulnerable -> Seiran absorbs burden`

should not be modeled as a sudden battlefield ideology. It grows from a more general disposition:

`Ari salient -> observe closely -> detect small state changes -> value signs of genuine ease/happiness -> orient action toward preserving them`.

## 8.4 Ari actively wants Seiran's gaze

The relationship is not one-way observation.

As the story approaches its intimate endpoint, Ari explicitly asks Seiran to keep looking at her and says she wants Seiran to think she is cute even more:

`私のこと いっぱい見て`

`可愛いって あなたにもっと 思ってほしいから`.

This is powerful reciprocal evidence because Ari is not merely accepting Seiran's care. She **wants to occupy Seiran's attention** and actively communicates that desire.

Seiran's answer is equally revealing: she has been looking all along, from the first time she saw Ari.

The dyadic model therefore becomes:

`Seiran sustained observation / protective attention <-> Ari desire to be seen / valued by Seiran`.

That gives the later romance a mutual perceptual structure, not merely mutual affection.

## 8.5 Spontaneous laughter is a relationship-state signal

Ari laughing at Seiran in a messy/dirty/soaked state becomes important because Seiran experiences the expression as qualitatively different — more natural, more disarmed, more “cute” precisely because it is less managed.

For simulation, Ari's smile cannot be treated as a binary emotional-output channel. Different smiles may carry different pragmatic functions:

- social smoothing;
- reassurance;
- habit;
- genuine amusement;
- intimate dearmoring.

Seiran is unusually good at detecting the difference.

## 8.6 Romantic intimacy is strong without rewriting prospective history

Handholding, explicit desire to be seen, reciprocal “I have always watched you” language, and close bodily framing make the story strongly romantic/intimate.

Current mature authority already knows they dated. `ぴかぴか` therefore **STRENGTHENS the developmental model**.

It does not authorize rewriting V01/V02 deep readings into “the prospective reader definitely knew they were dating.” Those earlier artifacts remain historically correct because the direct dating confirmation arrived only later.

## 8.7 Sheena's presence establishes social embedding

The ending returns Ari and Seiran to ordinary school proximity with Sheena. Their romance is not sealed in a private symbolic world. It develops inside the same peer environment in which meals, class, homework, teasing, combat preparation, and eventually grief will occur.

That continuity reinforces a central corpus rule:

**ordinary social life and lethal institutional life are not separate narrative layers. The people who will later become casualties first accumulate personhood inside ordinary shared space.**

### SS06 claim transitions

- **STRENGTHEN:** current mature Ari–Seiran romantic/dating history.
- **NEW:** Ari's frequent smiling includes an adaptive/socially managed component.
- **NEW/STRENGTHEN:** Seiran is a high-attention observer of Ari's subtle state changes.
- **STRENGTHEN:** Seiran's later protectiveness grows from ordinary relational vigilance, not only military duty.
- **STRENGTHEN:** Ari actively seeks Seiran's gaze/valuation; reciprocity is explicit.
- **PRESERVE:** earlier prospective dating uncertainty remains historically authoritative.

# 9. Cross-story character reconstruction

## 9.1 Sheena — grief before Mimi was already relationally complicated

The Side Stories significantly improve Sheena's reconstruction without changing her numbered-volume arc.

Before V01:

- she had already experienced roommate intimacy without the same level of shared domestic participation she later develops with Mimi;
- she had already lived inside an ability asymmetry where a more capable peer could resent the apparent shelter created by Sheena's weakness;
- Laura's death cut off a relationship containing both care and unresolved friction;
- later memories of Laura can coexist with happiness beside Mimi.

This makes Sheena's sensitivity to vacancy, replacement, loss, and “being unable to do enough” more historically grounded.

But the evidence should **not** be collapsed into “Laura trauma caused everything.” Sheena retains stable temperament, ethical dispositions, family-loss history, and later independent development.

## 9.2 Laura — achievement under militarized usefulness

Laura is now sufficiently evidenced for a compact conditional model even if not yet a monograph candidate.

### Stable/provisional traits
- intelligent and high-achieving;
- comparative and self-demanding;
- caring but capable of sharpness under pressure;
- institutionally legible as promising;
- increasingly equates being good with being useful.

### High-value policy
`high capability + institution signals combat usefulness -> raise own obligation -> compare self/others -> frustration toward those who appear exempt -> continue striving despite relational strain`.

### Failure mode
Do not simulate Laura as a pure militarist. Her behavior is better understood through achievement, obligation, comparative pressure, and a need to justify usefulness than through abstract nationalism.

## 9.3 Omi-sensei — care through structure

Omi provides a useful adult contrast to Fran.

- Fran's care is bodily, teasing, familiar, and historically entangled with Mimi management.
- Omi's care is restrained, pedagogical, and structured around preparation rather than popularity.

This is another reason the institution should not be assigned a single “adult voice.”

## 9.4 Esta and Rika — survival without forced recovery

Esta's V07 self-deprioritization now sits inside a relationship where another person persistently knows how to find her, asks for reciprocal survival, and refuses to equate healing with forgetting.

Rika's best current policy is:

`Esta withdraws / devalues own survival -> locate her -> do not demand forgetting or emotional performance -> insist only on continued living and reciprocal findability`.

## 9.5 Haru — external projection does not become internal identity

Claire's story strengthens rather than resolves Haru's self-definition arc.

Haru can tolerate a masculine address because another girl prefers it without thereby converting the address into an identity declaration. This is important generatively: Haru's autonomy is not constant argumentative defensiveness. The trigger is **authorship loss**, not every external variation.

## 9.6 Ari and Seiran — seeing and being seen

`ぴかぴか` makes their mature relationship model significantly more precise.

Seiran does not merely want to “protect Ari.” She has a long habit of watching Ari and distinguishing outward social function from subtler internal state. Ari, in turn, wants Seiran's gaze and wants to be judged beautiful/cute by this particular person.

Their later protector tragedy therefore rests on a prior intimate structure:

`I watch you closely because you matter -> you know my gaze matters and want it -> your welfare becomes increasingly behaviorally privileged`.

That is stronger and more character-specific than generic devotion.

# 10. Cross-story relationship and ethics synthesis

## 10.1 Ordinary life is how irreplaceability is manufactured

The collection repeatedly spends pages on activities that would be disposable in a plot synopsis:

- making jam;
- studying;
- skipping class;
- cooking;
- deciding what somebody should call you;
- looking through a window;
- teasing a teacher;
- holding hands;
- noticing a smile.

But those are precisely the scenes that make the manga's mortality ethic work.

The institution can count:

- students;
- test scores;
- qualified combatants;
- casualties;
- force allocation;
- magical capacity.

Relationships count something else:

- **this** person knows where I hide;
- **this** person made something with me;
- **this** person looks at me in a way I want;
- **this** person remembers how I smiled;
- **this** person annoyed me and cared for me before dying.

The ethical collision between those accounting systems is one of KimiShinu's strongest recurring structures.

## 10.2 Grief does not require idealization

Laura is important because the manga does not retroactively make her perfect in order to justify Sheena's grief.

Likewise, Rika does not ask Esta to sanitize the lost relationship into a motivational lesson. Ari does not eventually stop loving Seiran in order to continue living. Sheena does not need Laura to disappear from memory in order to love Mimi.

The collection therefore strengthens a general grief rule:

**continued life does not require simplifying, replacing, or forgetting the dead.**

## 10.3 Care should preserve rather than author the other person

Several stories independently support the same ethical distinction:

- Rika wants Esta alive but does not prescribe how she must grieve.
- Claire may guess what form of address Haru wants, but Haru retains authority over whether the guess matters.
- Ari wants Seiran to see her, but asks rather than forcing an interpretive identity onto her.
- Sheena and Mimi turn formerly solitary practices into shared ones without erasing Sheena's prior self.

This aligns strongly with V05–V09's relationship architecture:

`care -> increase another person's viable life / legibility / support`

is ethically different from:

`care -> decide who the other person must be`.

**OPEN / ACTIVE STRATEGIC-WAR TRACKING — PRESERVE.**

# 12. Visual and dialogue findings

## 12.1 `ばいばい` creates a literal formal bridge into V01

The most important formal operation in the collection is the transition from Laura's living perspective into the exact class-death announcement that begins the main series.

The effect is not merely continuity trivia. It changes the reader's mature experience of the V01 opening:

`abstract announcement -> known person's extinguished perspective`.

The institution's standardized announcement remains the same; what changes is the amount of particular life the reader now knows it compresses.

## 12.2 `ぴかぴか` uses sustained looking as relationship grammar

Seiran's gaze is not one isolated close-up. The story repeatedly structures intimacy around noticing fine bodily and facial difference. Ari's eventual request to be looked at converts the visual grammar itself into explicit relationship content.

For later retrieval, this is strong evidence that **gaze/observation** belongs in the Ari–Seiran relationship model, not merely in generic romance staging.

## 12.3 Dialogue keeps character identity local and concrete

The collection continues the mainline's resistance to abstract explanatory speech.

- Rika does not give Esta a therapeutic philosophy; she wants her to keep living stubbornly.
- Haru does not turn `ハルくん` into an identity manifesto; the response is local and low-investment.
- Fran's care for Mimi surfaces through teasing and a simple request to another teacher.
- Laura's institutionalization surfaces through competence/usefulness pressure rather than political slogans.

This strengthens the generative negative constraint against writing these characters as if they naturally verbalize their psychology in analytical terminology.
