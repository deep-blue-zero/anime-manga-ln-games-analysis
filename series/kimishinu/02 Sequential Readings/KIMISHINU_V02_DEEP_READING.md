## 2. Volume-level thesis

V01 established the problem of emotional non-normalization: Sheena lives in an institution that treats child death, killing, and deployment as ordinary, while she cannot make those facts feel ordinary. V02 turns that discomfort into a more explicit ethical and relational structure.

The volume's most important development is not merely the confirmation that Mimi is immortal. It is the revelation of what that immortality means socially. Mimi's mother broke an old taboo and used resurrection magic on her. Mimi no longer ages normally and is treated by the institution as a uniquely reusable military asset. Her exceptional survivability is therefore not liberation from death so much as a condition that makes indefinite instrumental use possible.

That revelation creates a serious test for Sheena. The simple response would be to decide that Mimi should stop fighting. Sheena initially moves toward exactly that wish and even searches for a way to undo resurrection magic. But V02 refuses to let her moral intuition remain cost-free. Fran points out that if Mimi stops, another child must take her place. Sheena is forced to recognize both her own protected position and the substitution problem built into the institution.

Sheena does not respond by abandoning her ethical objection. Instead she articulates it more carefully. Her objection is no longer only that *Mimi* should not be used because Mimi is her friend. In the bath conversation she extends the claim to enemy lives as well: rapid healing or immortality cannot justify treating anyone's life like an object. Her emerging ethic is therefore universalizing rather than purely possessive.

At the same time, Sheena's practical agency remains extremely constrained. She cannot end the war, change national policy, or presently remove Mimi from military use. The volume therefore ends by converting the abstract wish for a world without war into a relational act that is small enough to perform but not morally trivial: she waits for Mimi, tells her to come home without getting hurt, embraces her before departure, and sends her off with `いってらっしゃい`.

The tankobon bonus then carries that request directly into combat. Mimi fights while preserving the flower-crown motif from their ordinary day together, and Sheena's `ケガしないで 帰ってきて` remains present in the battlefield sequence. V02 thus supplies the first clear evidence that relationship state is entering Mimi's combat cognition. Sheena is becoming not merely someone Mimi likes at school, but a person whose waiting gives return a specific interpersonal destination.

The V02 state can be summarized as:

`immortality confirmed -> strategic instrumentality exposed -> first friendship named -> ordinary life acquires positive value -> Sheena universalizes personhood beyond Mimi -> institutional constraints complicate but do not erase the ethic -> waiting/return becomes relationship-conditioned survival meaning`.

## 3. CH06 - friends

### 3.1 One month of ordinary cohabitation

CH06 deliberately opens by restating the institution before showing what has changed inside it. The school takes children without families, educates them, and sends them into war; it is both orphanage and national magical-weapon training institution. Against that structure, the chapter marks approximately one month of Sheena and Mimi sharing a room (`V02 / CH06 / p_0001-p_0007`).

The change from V01 is immediately behavioral. Sheena now wakes Mimi, manages room order, handles laundry, thinks about meals, and negotiates ordinary school routine with her. The domestic labor is not background fluff. In V01, routine was primarily Sheena's attempt to restore normality after disruption. In V02, routine becomes something jointly inhabited. Mimi is no longer merely contaminating Sheena's normal day; she is becoming part of what an ordinary day means.

Mimi remains recognizably herself. She is hungry, sleepy, physically casual, socially direct, and low in embarrassment. V02 does not domesticate her by rewriting her into Sheena's temperament. The relationship develops by creating a shared life between unlike regulatory styles.

### 3.2 Fran, secrecy, and the limits of ordinary integration

Sheena notices that teachers, especially Fran, know Mimi unusually well. In a private exchange, Fran warns Mimi not to talk about her secret and emphasizes that it is not merely Mimi's personal matter (`p_0010-p_0015`). This is the first V02 sign that ordinary student integration sits on top of an unresolved state-security layer.

The contrast matters. Mimi can now share a room, meals, lessons, laundry, and class conversation, but there remains information about her body that the institution does not regard as hers alone to disclose. Her personhood is therefore split administratively: socially she is being admitted into ordinary childhood; strategically information about her body remains managed as a national-interest security matter.

### 3.3 Friendship has to be named because Mimi has not had it

Fran casually observes that Sheena and Mimi already look like friends. Mimi's response reveals that the category is not socially obvious to her. The ensuing conversation establishes friendship through concrete practices: talking, spending time together, helping one another, and sharing secrets (`p_0017-p_0026`).

Mimi then identifies Sheena as her first friend. Sheena's surprise is diagnostically important. V01 had already suggested isolation, but V02 gives it social form: this extremely capable child has existed without an ordinary peer friendship.

Sheena does not retreat from the weight of being first. She is pleased. Her engagement with Mimi's ordinary vulnerability therefore directly supports the V01 model that credible vulnerability/care weakens avoidance and increases relational openness.

The scene also develops Mimi's character without forcing abstract introspection. She learns a relationship category through examples and immediately values it. This is consistent with the V01 voice model: concrete relational concepts are easier for Mimi to inhabit than formal abstractions.

### 3.4 Secret-sharing as trust, then the first direct immortality claim

Because friends share secrets, Mimi entrusts Sheena with the fact that explains the school's restrictions. Her wording is characteristically simple and startling:

`ミミが死なないのはねえ もう死んでるからなんだよ`

At the V01 boundary, claims that Mimi had returned from death were peer rumor. CH06 changes the epistemic status: Mimi herself says her inability to die is connected to already having died (`p_0029-p_0033`). The exact mechanism is still not yet stable at the end of CH06, so the chapter should not be read in isolation as metaphysical closure. CH07 supplies the institutional explanation.

Relationally, however, the transition is already complete: a state secret becomes a friendship secret. Mimi's first ordinary friendship immediately intersects the very information the institution has ordered her to withhold.

## 4. CH07 - accomplice

### 4.1 `Mimi is Mimi`: identity before ontology

Sheena initially struggles to categorize what she has heard. If Mimi has already died, is she a ghost? Mimi's response is not a technical explanation but an identity claim:

`ミミはミミだよ`

The line is important for later character synthesis. Mimi does not initially define herself through the institution's weapon category, a supernatural category, or a philosophical category of posthuman existence. She answers categorization by asserting continuity of self (`V02 / CH07 / p_0035-p_0040`).

This does not prove that Mimi has a fully developed theory of personal identity. It does establish a negative constraint: external labels do not automatically replace her immediate self-conception.

### 4.2 Adult war talk remains institutional rather than geopolitical

The chapter moves into adult discussion of front-line pressure, student deployment, and the usefulness of exceptional magical assets (`p_0041-p_0048`). V02 therefore adds more war-consciousness than V01, but the scale remains operational. We learn about strain, casualties, and personnel needs, not a developed account of enemy politics, war aims, diplomacy, ideology, or state formation.

This confirms the architectural decision not to create a dedicated macro-political ledger yet. The source is becoming more explicit about *why this institution uses Mimi*, not yet about why the war exists in the LOGH/86 sense.

### 4.3 Resurrection magic is confirmed and historically taboo

Once Fran learns that Mimi has told Sheena, she provides the first authoritative explanation (`p_0050-p_0055`). Resurrection magic - `蘇生魔法` - is known from history, was forbidden centuries earlier, and can return the dead to life. The resurrected receive what Fran describes as `永遠の命`, an eternal life.

Most importantly:

`ミミはね 母親が禁忌を破って 蘇生魔法で蘇らせた子供なの`

Mimi's mother broke the taboo and resurrected her.

This produces a major claim transition from V01:

- V01: literal resurrection after completed death = unverified peer rumor.
- V02: resurrection = directly confirmed by Fran as Mimi's origin condition.

V01 should not be retroactively rewritten as though this was known then. The correct transition is REVISE/STRENGTHEN at V02.

### 4.4 Immortality is also agelessness

Fran further explains that Mimi has remained physically the same for years and does not grow normally (`p_0053-p_0056`). Knowledge and experience can accumulate, but the body is effectively fixed. This changes the meaning of the child body on the page.

In V01, Mimi's smallness contrasted visually with battlefield power. V02 makes that smallness temporally structural. She is not merely a young prodigy who may grow out of the role; her resurrected state threatens to trap her in an unaging child body while the institution continues to accumulate years of military use around her.

That is a much darker form of weaponized personhood than simple exceptional talent.

### 4.5 Why Mimi keeps fighting: survivability as strategic utility

Fran explains that Mimi's repeated deployment is not only because she is strong. Her inability to die makes her uniquely useful. Her presence can reduce the number of other children who must be exposed, and adults therefore treat continued use of Mimi as a grim but rational substitution (`p_0056-p_0061`).

This is the first explicit institutional defense of her instrumentalization. It is not cartoon cruelty. The logic is consequentialist and structurally coercive:

`use the child who returns -> reduce deaths among children who may not`.

Fran also makes the temporal asymmetry explicit: the ordinary people now around Mimi will die, while Mimi will continue. The institution's problem is therefore not only how to use an immortal child now but how a permanently reusable child is imagined across successive mortal cohorts.

### 4.6 State secrecy and ownership of bodily knowledge

Mimi's condition is treated as highly restricted information because foreign knowledge of it could matter strategically. Fran's earlier statement that the secret is not merely Mimi's own now becomes legible: the state/institution claims an interest in controlling information about Mimi's body (`p_0056-p_0062`).

This is a key agency distinction. Mimi can choose to tell Sheena in an interpersonal sense, but institutionally she is not recognized as having full authority to decide who knows what she is.

### 4.7 Sheena cannot simply negate Mimi from a protected position

Sheena asks whether Mimi will simply keep going to war when ordered. Mimi answers with a learned moral calibration:

`だって ミミが戦うのって いいことなんでしょ?`

For Mimi, fighting has been taught as good/helpful behavior. This is not yet ideology in the abstract. It is closer to a concrete social rule: Mimi fights, others are helped, therefore fighting is good.

Sheena's first impulse is to reject the arrangement. But she checks herself. She has not gone to the front. She has been protected partly because Mimi has. She therefore resists the easier moral posture of simply declaring Mimi's life wrong from outside it (`p_0060-p_0064`).

This is a major Sheena development. Her moral non-normalization survives, but it gains epistemic humility. She begins separating:

- `this system feels wrong` from
- `I therefore already know what Mimi should choose`.

That distinction will matter for any mature simulation model of Sheena. She is not merely rule-driven anti-war opposition; she is capable of revising action proposals when confronted with the costs borne by others.

## 5. CH08 - continuation of the fairy tale

### 5.1 Immortality becomes a problem of time, not merely survival

After Fran's explanation, Sheena ruminates on the image of Mimi continuing after everyone else is gone (`V02 / CH08 / p_0065-p_0070`). Classroom discussion of old resurrection magic now carries personal weight. The forbidden magic is no longer folklore detached from the present; it explains the roommate sleeping beside her.

V02 therefore shifts immortality from spectacle to temporal asymmetry. The central question becomes not only "can Mimi die?" but "what does an endless child life look like when every ordinary attachment is mortal?"

### 5.2 Ari and Seiran provide a relational counterfactual

Ari and Seiran discuss resurrection through the hypothetical of reviving someone one loves (`p_0069-p_0076`). The conversation tests an intuitive romantic fantasy - giving everything to bring a beloved person back - against the harder question of whether resurrection is actually what the loved person would want.

The scene is analytically useful in two ways.

First, it prevents resurrection from remaining a purely technical magical rule. The manga frames it as a relational ethics problem: love can motivate restoration, but love does not automatically settle consent, consequence, or the meaning of continued life.

Second, Ari/Seiran continue to function as a parallel intimacy structure. Their physical closeness and the content of the conversation strengthen the reading of them as an unusually intimate dyad beyond the V01 healing-contact ambiguity. Explicit mutual romantic self-label remains open at this boundary, but romantic plausibility is strengthened.

### 5.3 Mimi's pre-school life was not ordinary childhood

On a day off, Sheena and Mimi go into a flower-filled outdoor space. Sheena asks how old Mimi really is and how long she has lived. Mimi has not kept count in an ordinary age-based way (`p_0077-p_0082`).

She describes a past before the present school integration, when she was largely alone. Leaving was associated with deployment, not leisure. Detours were forbidden. Ordinary institutions of childhood - peers, matching clothes, cafeteria life, baths, lessons, casual conversation - are things she has acquired comparatively recently.

The characterization consequence is substantial. Mimi's childlike directness should not be modeled as simple chronological naivety. She may have lived far longer than her body suggests while remaining socially deprived of ordinary peer development. Chronological duration, embodied age, social experience, and military experience are therefore four different variables.

### 5.4 School has value because it offers ordinary social life

Asked what she likes now, Mimi answers in concrete terms: learning things she did not know, talking to people, wearing the same clothes, eating and bathing in shared spaces, and being with Sheena (`p_0081-p_0087`).

This is the strongest evidence yet that institutional integration has a double character. The same organization that weaponizes Mimi also supplies the social environment in which she can finally experience peer life. A simplistic "institution bad / outside freedom good" model would therefore lose important character truth. Mimi has genuine reasons to value parts of school life even while her deployment is structurally coercive.

Sheena reciprocates by saying she also enjoys being with Mimi. This makes the friendship symmetrical at the level of stated preference, not merely Mimi seeking and Sheena tolerating proximity.

### 5.5 Flower crowns and ordinary future expansion

The girls make and exchange flower crowns (`p_0084-p_0089`). They also talk about things Mimi has not yet experienced and ordinary activities they can do together, including baking cookies and the coming school event.

This is the first strong V02 evidence of relationship future orientation. It remains near-term and ordinary rather than grandly romantic: "there are more things we can do together." That scale is important. For a character whose institutional life is organized around repeated deployment, ordinary plans become evidence of an expanding imagined future.

The flower crown is not merely decorative. The drawn bonus later carries it into battle, turning this ordinary-life object into a cross-context relationship anchor.

## 6. CH09 - someday you will understand

### 6.1 `Henrei no hi`: death administered through reuse

CH09 centers on a school-wide cleanup/return event, `返礼の日`, during which old spaces and belongings are sorted and redistributed (`V02 / CH09 / p_0093-p_0104`). The school does not merely announce deaths; it metabolizes them materially.

Clothes and objects left by students who are gone circulate to those still living. A senior's inability to wear matching clothing with its original partner is carried inside the practical act of handing that clothing down. The institution turns absence into resource circulation.

This extends V01's "absence as death grammar." V01 emphasized empty rooms and memorial objects. V02 adds reuse: death becomes embedded in the economy of ordinary school possessions.

### 6.2 Sheena searches for a technical exit from Mimi's condition

While sorting old books, Sheena becomes interested in historical material about resurrection magic and wonders whether the spell can be undone (`p_0098-p_0108`). Her reasoning is understandable: if Mimi became mortal again, perhaps the institution could no longer justify endless use of her as an immortal weapon.

At this stage, however, the idea is still under-modeled. Sheena has not yet fully confronted who would replace Mimi. CH10 will force that complication.

Characterologically, the search shows that Sheena's moral distress is becoming active inquiry. She does not remain only in rumination. When she perceives a possible mechanism for reducing harm, she investigates it.

### 6.3 Affection becomes explicitly sought and reciprocated

A minor physical incident gives a useful low-stakes behavior sample. Sheena checks whether Mimi is hurt and strokes/pats her head. Mimi immediately asks for more - `もっとして もっと` - and leans into the attention (`p_0108-p_0114`).

This matters because it is not healing magic, combat rescue, or forced cohabitation. It is ordinary affection. Mimi's proximity-seeking is therefore increasingly relationship-specific and positively reinforcing. Sheena, for her part, no longer treats that closeness primarily as a boundary violation.

Peers notice the clinginess. The relationship has become socially legible as unusually close even before the text requires a mature romantic self-definition.

### 6.4 `It is still alive`: Sheena's life-preservation reflex in miniature

Sheena finds a neglected plant that others treat as effectively finished. She takes it because:

`まだ生きてるもの`

It is still alive (`p_0117-p_0122`).

The plant should not be promoted into a one-to-one allegory. It is nevertheless diagnostic formal evidence. The small action condenses a pattern already visible in Sheena: the threshold between "still living" and "already disposable" matters to her. She resists treating weak or inconvenient life as functionally equivalent to dead matter.

The plant's continued recovery in their room therefore belongs in the visual/personhood ledger as a motif supporting, rather than proving, Sheena's emerging ethic.

## 7. CH10 - endless night

### 7.1 The question changes from "what is wrong?" to "what do you want?"

CH10 opens by asking Sheena what she wants (`V02 / CH10 / p_0127-p_0129`). This is the correct developmental question after V01 and the first four V02 chapters. Sheena already knows that the system disturbs her. The problem is whether moral discomfort can become a desire or action that survives contact with structural reality.

### 7.2 Mimi comes home to Sheena before procedure

Mimi returns from deployment and exchanges `ただいま` / `おかえり` with Sheena (`p_0129-p_0134`). The language matters because it domesticates return. Sheena is now not merely a roommate present in the same building; she is someone Mimi expects to be waiting.

Mimi has come directly back rather than first completing ordinary medical procedure. Her explanation is explicit:

`だってシーナが待っててくれてると思ったから`

She expected Sheena to be waiting (`p_0131-p_0135`).

This is strong support for a relationship-conditioned policy: when deployment ends, Sheena's waiting can override at least some procedural sequencing. The evidence does not show that Mimi will always disregard safety procedures for Sheena, but the target-specific priority is now stronger than in V01.

### 7.3 The bath makes war contamination literally wash into domestic intimacy

Sheena helps Mimi bathe. Blood and dirt from battle are washed from a small child's hair and body while the scene remains physically close, playful, and domestic (`p_0134-p_0140`).

The visual effect is one of V02's strongest formal statements. War does not stay outside the room. It enters as smell, blood, grime, and the need to wash another person's body. Yet the bath is also an act of care and ordinary shared life. Intimacy does not remove war; it becomes one way of metabolizing its residue.

Sheena is relieved that Mimi is not injured. Her relief now has a different structure than V01. Mimi's survival is no longer enough. Sheena cares whether Mimi returns *unhurt*, even when death is supposedly impossible.

### 7.4 Sheena universalizes the personhood problem to the enemy

Sheena raises the people Mimi fights. Those enemies may also have somewhere they want to return and people they want to see. She is careful not to frame this as a denunciation of Mimi. She explicitly distinguishes criticism of war and instrumentalization from rejection of the child who has been made to fight (`p_0138-p_0143`).

The strongest formulation is:

`すぐ治るからって 死なないからって 誰の命も物みたいに扱っていいはずない`

The operative word is `誰の命も` - anyone's life. Sheena's objection now crosses three boundaries:

1. immortal versus mortal;
2. friend versus stranger;
3. allied child versus enemy combatant.

This is the first stable basis for describing a universalizing personhood ethic in Sheena rather than merely personal anxiety about Mimi.

### 7.5 Moral salience changes Sheena's speech policy

V01 established a hesitant, inward, self-questioning speaker. That remains true. In this scene Sheena apologizes, qualifies, and worries that what she is saying is strange or difficult. But she says it anyway.

This is important simulation hardening. The correct voice rule is no longer simply "Sheena avoids direct statements." A better conditional policy is:

`ordinary conflict -> hesitation/softening/internalization`

but

`perceived threat to personhood or serious harm -> hesitation remains, yet direct moral statement becomes more likely`.

The transition is not a personality reversal. It is a context-conditioned exception becoming visible.

### 7.6 Mimi answers moral abstraction through concrete relational language

Mimi does not suddenly produce a theory of just war. She responds in the same basic linguistic architecture established in V01:

`おかしくなんかないよ! ミミ ちゃんと大切にできるよ`

She reassures Sheena that what she is saying is not strange and answers the personhood concern through `大切にする` - treating/holding something or someone as precious/important (`p_0141-p_0144`).

This strongly supports V01 prediction 5. Under an emotionally difficult moral conversation, Mimi remains concrete, relational, and childlike rather than shifting into institutional ideology.

It also shows receptivity. Mimi's learned rule that fighting is "good" is not presented as dogmatic resistance to Sheena. She can take in a new relational/moral frame even if V02 does not yet prove how deeply it changes battlefield decision-making.

### 7.7 Mimi intervenes directly when Sheena is distressed

As Sheena becomes visibly upset, Mimi closes distance, reassures her, hugs/touches her, and checks whether her physical state is also affected by the hot bath (`p_0142-p_0145`).

This directly tests V01 prediction 3. The original policy was derived from physical injury. V02 shows the pattern generalizing to emotional distress: Mimi does not respond with procedural distance or prolonged verbal analysis; she moves toward immediate embodied reassurance.

The policy therefore strengthens from "pain response" toward a broader target-specific care rule, though evidence remains strongest for Sheena.

### 7.8 Fran forces the substitution problem

Sheena asks Fran whether resurrection magic can be undone. Her hope is not primarily metaphysical curiosity. If Mimi became mortal, perhaps the institution could no longer exploit immortality as a reason for endless deployment (`p_0146-p_0153`).

Fran challenges the premise. If Mimi does not fight, someone else must. Someone else will be hurt. Wars that have persisted do not vanish because one individual withdraws. Within the institution's logic, peace is imagined through victory rather than unilateral moral refusal.

Sheena responds that she wishes war would disappear quickly - `戦争なんて早くなくなればいいのに` - and insists that she does not want *anyone* hurt.

Fran's answer is not a geopolitical theory. It is institutional realism: there is no available arrangement in which the present conflict ends without somebody bearing harm. She tells Sheena to face that reality and then think again about what she wants:

`その上で もう一度考えてごらん 何を望むか`

This is one of V02's most important agency scenes. Sheena's moral belief survives, but her first proposed solution is downgraded as insufficient because it externalizes the cost onto unnamed others.

### 7.9 Sheena's V02 answer: not a program, but a return claim

Mimi is summoned again shortly after returning (`p_0153-p_0159`). She says she will come back and asks Sheena to wait. Sheena initially participates in the ordinary departure formula, then calls Mimi back, embraces her, and says:

`ケガしないで 帰ってきて`

Come back without getting hurt.

This is a modest statement, but V02 has spent the entire volume explaining why it is not trivial. Mimi's immortality has taught the institution that injury is acceptable because permanent loss is unlikely. Sheena explicitly rejects that premise. "Return" is not enough if the body is treated as infinitely repairable material.

Her final `いってらっしゃい` does not mean she now endorses war. Nor does it mean she has solved the structural problem. It is an act of relational agency inside constraint: I cannot presently prevent your deployment, but I will not normalize your suffering, I will wait, and I will ask you to orient toward return without injury.

## 8. Tankobon drawn bonus - ordinary intimacy enters the battlefield

`p_0160-p_0163` are narrative and must not be collapsed into paratext.

The almost wordless sequence shows Mimi in combat among hooded child soldiers. Crucially, the flower-crown motif from CH08 survives into the war space. Sheena's request - `ケガしないで 帰ってきて` - is recalled/present within the sequence.

This is the clearest V02 evidence that Sheena has entered Mimi's combat cognition. The ordinary relationship is no longer confined to school scenes before and after deployment. A token of shared leisure and the language of safe return accompany Mimi into the military environment.

The correct behavioral inference is deliberately limited:

- **Supported:** Sheena's waiting/request matters to Mimi during deployment; relationship state supplies a salient return/safety cue.
- **Not yet supported:** Mimi has changed tactics, become generally risk-averse, or will now avoid all injury.

The bonus therefore strengthens a new policy candidate without prematurely scoring V01 prediction 4 as supported.

## 9. Sheena - V02 character-state transition

### 9.1 PRESERVE/STRENGTHEN: death and violence remain non-normal

V02 strongly supports the V01 prediction that further exposure would not produce casual enthusiasm. Sheena continues to ruminate on deployment, enemy death, Mimi's body, and the fact that others accept repeated war as normal.

### 9.2 STRENGTHEN/REVISE: from affective discomfort to universalizing personhood ethic

V01 Sheena primarily experienced wrongness. V02 Sheena can state a principle: neither immortality nor rapid healing permits anyone's life to be treated as an object. By explicitly including enemy lives, the principle cannot be reduced to possessiveness toward Mimi.

### 9.3 STRENGTHEN: epistemic humility and anti-self-exemption

Sheena recognizes that she benefits from Mimi's fighting and that she has not personally experienced the front. She therefore hesitates to condemn Mimi's own way of living from a protected position.

This complicates, rather than weakens, the moral model. Sheena is increasingly unwilling both to accept instrumentalization and to make morally easy demands whose costs fall on others.

### 9.4 STRENGTHEN: ordinary routine becomes positive future-building

Food, laundry, flowers, cookies, baths, books, plants, and room life now do more than regulate distress. They create positive content for a life worth returning to. Ordinary life becomes an alternative valuation system to military utility.

### 9.5 NEW: moral salience can override Sheena's default speech inhibition

Sheena remains hesitant and apologetic, but the bath conversation demonstrates that she can become directly declarative when personhood and serious harm are at stake. This is a conditional voice/behavior rule, not a global increase in assertiveness.

### 9.6 NEW: constrained agency becomes waiting/protection rather than withdrawal

Fran's substitution argument prevents Sheena from treating "make Mimi stop" as a complete solution. The V02 endpoint is a more local form of agency: seek knowledge, speak the moral objection, make return matter, and refuse to normalize injury even when departure cannot presently be stopped.

## 10. Mimi - V02 character-state transition

### 10.1 REVISE/STRENGTHEN: immortality mechanism is now direct evidence

Mimi was resurrected by her mother using forbidden magic. She is ageless/undying according to Fran and has remained physically unchanged for years. V01's resurrection rumor is therefore upgraded, while V01's finding that pain and fear are real remains preserved.

### 10.2 STRENGTHEN: social directness remains distinct from chronological experience

Mimi's body is childlike, her speech is childlike, and her peer social experience is strikingly limited despite an unknown but clearly extended lifespan. Simulation must not map one "age" value onto every domain. Military experience, chronological duration, embodied development, and ordinary social learning are dissociated.

### 10.3 NEW/STRENGTHEN: first friendship is a major motivational event

Sheena is explicitly Mimi's first friend. Shared school life is valuable because it gives Mimi experiences she previously lacked. Mimi's attachment should therefore be modeled not simply as attraction to one person but as access to a whole category of ordinary human life mediated through that person.

### 10.4 STRENGTHEN: direct Sheena-care policy generalizes to emotional distress

V01 showed costly bodily healing. V02 shows immediate reassurance and physical closeness when Sheena is emotionally distressed. The policy becomes broader:

`Sheena visibly distressed -> close distance -> concrete reassurance/care -> low procedural or interpersonal delay`.

### 10.5 NEW: Sheena becomes a return destination

Mimi goes directly back to Sheena after deployment because she expects Sheena to be waiting. Sheena's safe-return request then persists into the battlefield bonus. This supports a new relationship-conditioned appraisal variable: "Sheena is waiting for me" can become salient during/after deployment.

### 10.6 PRESERVE: no clean new test of self-risk discount

V02 contains more deployment, but it does not provide a sufficiently clean new scene in which Mimi knowingly chooses major injury over safety while both options are visible. The V01 self-risk model therefore remains plausible and preserved, but V01 prediction 4 is scored INDETERMINATE rather than artificially confirmed.

### 10.7 STRENGTHEN: concrete relational moral language

When challenged with difficult ethical content, Mimi answers through concrete relational categories rather than abstraction. The learned statement "Mimi fighting is good, right?" and her later promise to treat lives as important show a mind that currently processes moral structure through socially learned, person-centered rules more readily than formal ideology.

## 11. Sheena-Mimi relationship - V02 transition

The relationship undergoes several distinct state changes rather than one generic increase in closeness.

### 11.1 Explicit friendship

They name themselves friends; Sheena is Mimi's first. This is a categorical transition from V01's emerging companionship.

### 11.2 Trust through restricted knowledge

Mimi entrusts Sheena with a state secret. Friendship therefore becomes linked to disclosure and shared risk. Sheena keeps the secret rather than converting intimacy into peer gossip.

### 11.3 Ordinary-life reciprocity

The volume accumulates low-stakes reciprocal life: room care, meals, laundry, flowers, gift-making, future activities, book sorting, head-patting, clothing, bathing, waiting. These scenes are analytically essential because they reveal behavior outside crisis.

### 11.4 Care becomes bidirectional and non-medical

V01 care was especially strong Mimi->Sheena through healing. V02 makes Sheena->Mimi care clearer: checking injuries, washing battlefield blood, physical affection, protecting the neglected plant, researching the immortality problem, waiting for return.

### 11.5 Future orientation appears

The girls imagine more things to do together. The scale is deliberately ordinary. Shared future is not yet an abstract lifelong promise, but it exists as a growing sequence of anticipated next experiences.

### 11.6 Return grammar becomes relational infrastructure

`ただいま / おかえり`, `待ってて`, `帰ってきて`, and `いってらっしゃい` collectively create a domestic grammar of departure and return. This is one of the strongest V02 relationship structures.

### 11.7 Romantic status remains prospectively disciplined

V02 materially intensifies intimacy: physical affection, flower-crown exchange, bath care, secrecy, exclusivity-like first-friend importance, and explicit mutual enjoyment. The source also places Ari/Seiran's resurrection-love discussion nearby as a thematic counterpoint.

Nevertheless, the V02 boundary does not require a claim of mature mutual romantic self-conception. The correct status is **romantic/intimate trajectory strengthened; explicit mature mutual self-definition still OPEN**. Later volumes may make the category explicit; V02 should not borrow that knowledge.

## 12. Ari-Seiran - V02 development

Ari and Seiran continue as a close relational mirror. Their resurrection discussion is especially important because it moves beyond the V01 ambiguity in which intimate physical contact could be explained partly by healing mechanics.

Their hypothetical asks what love permits one to do to a dead beloved and whether devotion makes resurrection ethically obvious. Physical and conversational intimacy therefore strengthen romantic plausibility, although the V02 prospective boundary still does not require an explicit mutual-romance label.

For simulation purposes, evidence remains insufficient for rich individual policies. Their primary current analytical value is relational and thematic comparison.

## 13. Fran - V02 development

Fran becomes more complicated than the V01 "exasperated protective caretaker" model.

She still cares personally for Mimi, but she is also a keeper of institutional secrets and an articulate defender of the substitution logic that keeps Mimi deployed. Those roles are not mutually exclusive. V02 should therefore resist two flattenings:

- Fran is not simply the cruel handler exploiting Mimi.
- Fran is not simply the benevolent adult secretly opposed to the system.

Her observed position is closer to protective institutional realism. She sees the costs, knows Mimi intimately, enforces secrecy, and believes that removing one extraordinary asset does not remove the need for someone to absorb battlefield harm.

Her challenge to Sheena - face reality, then decide what you want - also gives Sheena agency rather than merely shutting down the moral objection. That makes Fran both a system representative and a developmental interlocutor.

## 14. Institution and war-consciousness - V02 development

V02 substantially deepens the institutional layer while still not becoming a macro-political war narrative.

### 14.1 Strategic secrecy

Mimi's resurrected state is controlled information. Her body is not only militarily used; knowledge about it is securitized.

### 14.2 Immortality creates reusable-child logic

The institution sees Mimi as unusually safe to expose because she returns. This reduces some mortality cost to others while concentrating potentially endless violence and injury in one unaging child.

### 14.3 Ordinary integration is both humane and administratively useful

Letting Mimi attend class and live with peers improves her life. It may also make the weapon more socially functional. V02 does not allow those interpretations to collapse into one another; both can be true.

### 14.4 Death is administered materially

`返礼の日` extends the system of death normalization through redistribution of the dead/absent students' possessions. Institutional life continues by literally passing objects down to survivors.

### 14.5 Fran articulates the substitution constraint

If Mimi stops, another child fights. Sheena's objection therefore cannot be evaluated only at the level of Mimi's individual freedom. The institution's violence is systemic: reducing harm to one person can transfer it to another.

### 14.6 Macro-politics remains OPEN, not absent

V02 supplies pressure, attrition, secrecy, and an institutional theory of winning/peace. It still does not provide enough recurring evidence about governments, enemy ideology, war origins, diplomacy, or state factions to justify a dedicated political-war ledger.

## 15. Mortality, expendability, and personhood - V02 development

### 15.1 Immortality is no longer rumor

Mimi was resurrected. Her continued existence is the consequence of forbidden magic used by her mother.

### 15.2 Immortality does not erase injury

V01's pain/fear evidence remains controlling. V02 adds no reason to rewrite immortality as invulnerability. Indeed, Sheena's request not to get hurt becomes more meaningful precisely because the institution's logic tends to discount nonterminal injury.

### 15.3 Agelessness creates a unique temporal vulnerability

Mimi can accumulate experience without ordinary bodily development. Everyone around her may age and die while her child body persists. This creates a form of relational mortality asymmetry distinct from battlefield risk.

### 15.4 Personhood versus utility becomes explicit

The institution values Mimi partly because her body is endlessly reusable. Sheena's response is not to deny usefulness but to deny that usefulness exhausts moral status.

### 15.5 Enemy personhood enters the frame

Sheena imagines enemies as people with homes and desired reunions. This is the first explicit extension of the project's personhood problem across enemy lines.

### 15.6 The plant and flower crown form a preservation constellation

The living plant, ordinary flower crown, old belongings, and safe-return request repeatedly distinguish "can be used/replaced" from "is still living/remembered/awaited." These are formal motifs supporting the volume's personhood structure, not simple symbols with fixed one-to-one meanings.

## 16. Japanese dialogue and simulation-relevant voice findings

### 16.1 Sheena

V01 baseline is preserved: hesitant, self-questioning, often internalizing, polite with authority, inclined to soften strong statements.

V02 adds a conditional transformation. Under high moral salience, Sheena can sustain direct declarative content while retaining hedges/apology around it. A simulation should therefore avoid both extremes:

- not a blunt slogan-speaker in ordinary interaction;
- not so conflict-avoidant that she cannot directly defend personhood when stakes become intolerable.

Her ethical vocabulary remains ordinary rather than philosophical-jargon heavy. `誰の命も物みたいに扱っていいはずない` is conceptually strong but linguistically accessible.

### 16.2 Mimi

V01 voice is strongly preserved:

- third-person `ミミ` self-reference;
- concrete wants and social categories;
- direct questions and requests;
- low hedging;
- childlike morphology/intonational feel;
- little interest in abstract ideological explanation.

V02's morally difficult scenes strengthen the negative constraint against suddenly generating sophisticated political rhetoric for Mimi. Even when talking about immortality, war, and caring for lives, she speaks through simple statements of identity, learned goodness, and `大切にする`.

### 16.3 Fran

Fran's directive, familiar register remains. V02 adds the ability to shift from teasing/management into sober institutional explanation without becoming ceremonially formal. She can state brutal structural facts directly to Sheena.

## 17. Visual-form findings

### 17.1 Black/empty fields continue to mark existential isolation

V01 used dark or emptied fields around Sheena's alienation. V02 continues the strategy around the immortality secret, endless time, and morally difficult war reflection.

### 17.2 Domestic return grammar is spatial as well as verbal

Doors, room entry, bath space, bed/room waiting, and departure thresholds repeatedly frame Mimi's movement between war and Sheena. The relationship becomes spatially organized around leaving and coming back.

### 17.3 Blood in bathwater

The bath scene visually converts battlefield residue into domestic cleanup. The effect is not purification in a simple moral sense; the blood is evidence that intimacy must physically handle what war deposits on the body.

### 17.4 Flower crown crosses the institutional boundary

The flower crown originates in leisure and mutual gift exchange. Its persistence into the drawn combat bonus is a strong cross-context motif: ordinary relationship meaning accompanies the weaponized body into battle.

### 17.5 Hand-me-downs make absence wearable

Old clothes are not abstract memorials. They are put back into circulation on living bodies. The school visually folds dead/absent students into the continuing material life of the cohort.

### 17.6 Plant recovery as low-volume visual insistence

The neglected plant occupies little narrative space, but Sheena's insistence that it remains alive and its continued presence in the room reinforce the volume's refusal to equate damaged/weak with disposable.
