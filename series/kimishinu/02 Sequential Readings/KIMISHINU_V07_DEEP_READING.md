## 2. Volume-level thesis — specialness becomes a mutual relationship choice

V06 ended at a deliberately unresolved threshold. Sheena had already crossed the friendship-only boundary internally: jealousy, the desire to become Mimi's `一番`, and a kiss explicitly distinguished from magic made her romantic desire directly evident. But she had not yet decided what she wanted the relationship itself to be, and Mimi had not supplied a symmetrical romantic category.

V07 does not solve that problem by simply attaching the word `恋人` to an unchanged relationship. It spends the entire volume clarifying **what the label must mean to these particular girls**.

Five movements lead to the answer.

1. **Sheena refuses to use a category she has not made her own.** Haru's questions force her to separate same-sex attraction, friendship, helpfulness, exclusivity, and the desire to become lovers. Sheena knows she loves Mimi before she knows what promise she wants to make.
2. **Care becomes plural without becoming impersonal.** Sheena develops a real infirmary role and begins helping many students. Mimi becomes jealous, but instead of successfully forcing Sheena back into passivity she discovers a complementary role: Sheena may care for others, while Mimi wants to be the person who cares for Sheena afterward.
3. **Personhood ethics generalize beyond the central couple.** Esta's grief-driven self-neglect gives Sheena a new version of the same problem previously seen in Mimi and Seiran: a person can sincerely want others to receive scarce safety while underweighting her own life. Friends answer by insisting that concern is reciprocal.
4. **Finite time makes indefinite deferral morally costly.** Watching lives end, seeing Esta's post-loss collapse, and recognizing that time is not equal for everyone makes Sheena realize that waiting for a philosophically perfect answer can itself become a way of failing to speak while there is still time.
5. **The confession defines a relationship through reciprocal primacy.** Sheena says directly that she is in love with Mimi. What she wants is not merely the social label `lover`; she wants the nearest place, the hand she can hold under any condition, and a position she does not want to yield to anyone else. Mimi responds in her own concrete idiom: Sheena has always been the only one for her, Sheena may be Mimi's `special`, and if they are each other's special person then they can be lovers.

The V07 governing transition is therefore:

`romantic category pressure -> self-examination without forced label -> healer role expands beyond dyad -> Mimi jealousy tests agency -> complementary care replaces sole-possession solution -> Esta exposes grief-driven self-disposability -> finite-time urgency -> direct romantic confession -> reciprocal specialness -> mutually accepted 恋人 relationship -> diary records the new state`.

This is a major character-simulation milestone. At V07, Sheena-Mimi is no longer merely a relationship with strong romantic evidence. The source now establishes a **mutually recognized romantic relationship**. Just as importantly, the way they arrive there constrains how the relationship should be simulated:

- Sheena requires semantic/moral fit before using a relational label.
- Mimi reasons through concrete exclusivity, specialness, touch, waiting, and observed behavior rather than abstract romance theory.
- Becoming lovers does not erase friendship, room-sharing, cookies, infirmary work, disagreement about risk, or each girl's separate role.
- Romantic commitment is an added layer of mutual primacy, not a personality replacement.

## 3. CH30 — `またたき`: knowing she loves Mimi is not yet knowing what to call it

### 3.1 Haru removes the wrong obstacle

CH30 begins from the `恋人` question that closed V06. Haru does not treat the problem as whether two girls are *allowed* to love each other. She explicitly stops organizing the issue around gender and instead asks Sheena to think about what she wants from Mimi.

This matters because it prevents a weak model in which Sheena's hesitation is read as internalized prohibition. The source instead locates the uncertainty in **relationship definition**.

Haru also introduces a concrete exclusivity test: if the person Haru loved loved somebody else, Haru would feel lonely. That lands because it maps onto feelings Sheena has already experienced but has not yet integrated into a category.

### 3.2 Sheena separates role facts from relationship ontology

Sheena already possesses several privileged relational facts:

- she is Mimi's roommate;
- she is Mimi's first friend;
- she knows the resurrection secret;
- Mimi waits for and returns to her;
- they share unusually intimate routines and touch.

None of those facts alone answers whether Sheena wants to call Mimi her lover. CH30 therefore resists a common retrospective error: because V07 eventually establishes `恋人`, earlier forms of closeness do not retroactively become the same relationship state.

Sheena's position is:

`I already know this person is uniquely important -> I recognize feelings friendship alone no longer explains -> I still need to decide what I mean when I ask for a different relationship`.

### 3.3 Fever creates a low-agency intimacy test

Sheena falls ill and Mimi immediately reorganizes herself around nursing her. Mimi takes notes, brings water/medicine, feeds her, helps with sweat-soaked clothes, stays physically near, and offers to obtain whatever Sheena wants.

The care is characteristically Mimi-like: concrete, bodily, enthusiastic, and low on procedural distance. It strongly supports V06 test #2. Mimi has not suddenly become verbally sophisticated about romance; she knows how to **do things for Sheena**.

Mimi's desire to handle everything for Sheena also preserves a familiar control risk. The line is affectionate in the immediate illness context, but it belongs to the same behavioral family as V05's wish to keep Sheena safe by monopolizing danger. The difference is that nursing an acute fever does not presently erase a chosen Sheena role.

### 3.4 `好きなんだよね？` — self-recognition becomes explicit

While Mimi sleeps beside her, Sheena internally reaches a much cleaner formulation: she likes/loves Mimi. The remaining uncertainty is not whether the attraction exists.

Sheena then asks a particularly diagnostic question of the sleeping Mimi:

`ねえ ミミは 私じゃなくても キスした？`

Would Mimi have kissed somebody even if that person were not Sheena?

This is the strongest exclusivity probe so far because it tests what Sheena now needs to know about Mimi's side of the relationship: are their intimate acts merely products of Mimi's low boundaries/curiosity, or are they person-specific?

Mimi cannot answer in the moment. That deferral is analytically useful because it prevents CH30 from resolving the relationship before Sheena has articulated her own desire.

### 3.5 Sheena asks Haru for time rather than for permission

By the chapter endpoint, Sheena still says she has not found the answer and asks Haru to wait a little longer. Haru accepts the delay and also protects Sheena's confidence when another student approaches.

This establishes Haru as a trusted relational sounding board and reinforces an important Sheena policy:

`emotion becomes morally/relationally significant -> do not force a premature declaration merely to end discomfort -> continue internal differentiation until the chosen statement feels true`.

That policy will be revised, but not rejected, by CH33's finite-time pressure.

## 4. CH31 — `希望`: healing becomes a practiced social role rather than private aspiration

### 4.1 Recovery does not reset the V06 romantic problem

After Sheena recovers from the fever, the girls return to ordinary school rhythm. The unresolved romantic question remains present underneath the routine rather than dominating every scene. This is important simulation evidence: Sheena can carry a high-salience unresolved internal problem while still attending exams, talking to friends, helping people, and interacting normally with Mimi.

### 4.2 Haru becomes a confidentiality test

Sheena thanks Haru for giving her a reason to think and says she is glad she could talk to Haru. When another classmate approaches, Haru covers the conversation with a mundane explanation rather than exposing Sheena's romantic uncertainty.

This strengthens Haru's direct-social diagnostic role without making Haru omniscient. Haru is good at naming avoided categories, but can also respect the other person's timing once the question has been asked.

The V06 comparison/inferiority and illness-help policies are **not materially re-tested here**. V07 Haru evidence is primarily relational-advisory.

### 4.3 Sheena turns aptitude into procedure

The chapter returns to Sheena's developing magic and infirmary interest. Fran tests what Sheena's magic actually does rather than allowing `治癒魔法` to remain a broad fantasy label.

The source continues distinguishing it from Mimi's restoration. Sheena does not simply close severe tissue damage by reproducing a lost physical structure. Her magic acts more subtly on living recovery, discomfort, sensation, and emotional/bodily state. Practical trials show that it can make pain and distress more bearable even when ordinary medicine has limitations.

This is important for simulation because Sheena's role is becoming **procedural and patient-facing**:

- assess what is wrong;
- use ordinary treatment where appropriate;
- add magic when it helps;
- observe response;
- learn from the individual case;
- avoid treating magical power as a universal substitute for medicine.

### 4.4 The plant test keeps the magic tied to life rather than war

Sheena and Fran also test the effect on living plants. As in the V05 booklet, the magic can encourage living recovery/growth, but the scene does not turn it into Mimi-style restoration of an exact prior physical state.

The repeated plant evidence matters because it keeps Sheena's power conceptually attached to **supporting life processes**, not merely making soldiers usable again.

### 4.5 `頼ってもらえるように` — usefulness becomes chosen competence

Sheena's aspiration is increasingly clear: she wants to become someone people can rely on. That is a significant revision of early-series passivity but not a rejection of the person she was.

V05 established that saying “nothing can be done” had partly protected Sheena from confronting helplessness. V07 shows the mature alternative:

`limited capability acknowledged -> practice concrete skill -> accept that results are partial -> become more reliable through repetition`.

Sheena still becomes tired. The source does not reward commitment by eliminating bodily limits.

## 5. CH32 — `ここにあるもの`: transparent return and complementary care

### 5.1 Transparent injured return persists

CH32 gives the clean V07 test of the V05→V06 transparency transition. Mimi returns from deployment visibly dirty, bloodied, and physically depleted. Sheena greets and receives the real body rather than a cleaned-up performance.

This supports prospective test #3's transparency half.

It does **not** show Mimi has adopted tactical self-preservation. Her body is still carrying substantial combat cost. The correct longitudinal transition remains:

`injury concealment ↓`  
while  
`protective self-risk remains high`.

Those variables must remain separate in any simulator.

### 5.2 Sheena is now inside the recovery system

Sheena increasingly assists Fran in the infirmary. The work includes ordinary bodily care, medicine, cleaning, bandaging, monitoring, conversation, and selective magic use. She is still subordinate to instruction and still inexperienced, but she is no longer merely training in anticipation of some future usefulness.

She has entered a real social function:

`battlefield injury arrives -> Sheena helps receive/treat person -> learns individual limits -> develops skill through repeated exposure`.

This directly supports V07 test #4.

### 5.3 Conversation is part of treatment

A particularly valuable modeling insight is Sheena's growing recognition that talking to patients is not ornamental. Different people calm down, focus elsewhere, tolerate care, or reveal needs through different forms of interaction.

Her healer role therefore fits her existing personality unusually well. She does not become a commanding battlefield medic persona. Her attentiveness, quiet observation, and person-specific concern become clinically useful traits.

### 5.4 Mimi's jealousy does not erase Sheena's role

Mimi notices that Sheena touches, hugs, talks with, and is called by many people in the infirmary. She becomes clingy and jealous.

The key change from V05 is what follows.

Mimi does **not** successfully translate jealousy into “stop helping them.” Instead she increasingly claims a complementary dyadic role: when Sheena spends herself helping other people and comes back exhausted, Mimi can be the one who hugs, rests, and cares for her.

This is a major relationship-policy hardening:

`Sheena care distributed outward -> Mimi exclusivity alarm -> monitor / seek reassurance / claim special aftercare role -> preserve Sheena's chosen work rather than abolish it`.

The control risk remains available under stronger threat, but V07 demonstrates a more agency-compatible solution.

### 5.5 War remains cyclical rather than politically elaborated

The infirmary repeatedly receives the output of renewed fighting. V07 describes war as something that pauses, breathes, resumes, attacks, answers, wounds, recovers, and starts again.

This deepens **war consciousness** but does not meaningfully expand V06's causal political layer. The grimoire, governments, enemy decision-makers, diplomacy, and strategic policy are not developed enough here to move the macro-politics gate beyond **AJAR / ACTIVE WATCH**.

## 6. CH33 — `日々の果て`: grief can make a survivor treat her own life as leftover

### 6.1 Esta becomes a high-value comparison character

V05 introduced Esta as an older combatant who had also lost a girl she loved. V07 finally gives that history enough shape to model its behavioral consequences.

Esta had loved a long-haired girl and oriented substantial effort—study, training, fighting—toward being seen by or being worthy in relation to that person. The manga includes physical intimacy in the remembered relationship, but Esta's own retrospective classification is crucial: she describes it as a love that was only hers.

Therefore the corpus should not overwrite Esta's direct self-understanding with a stronger reciprocal-romance claim merely because the images are intimate.

### 6.2 Purpose collapse is not the same as explicit suicidal intent

After the loved person's death, Esta's motivational structure collapses. Her most diagnostic formulation is that she is tired and no longer cares very much about what happens to herself.

That produces a mortality state distinct from both Mimi and Seiran.

- **Mimi:** self-risk is normalized by recoverability and protective substitution.
- **Seiran:** self-risk rises because protecting a living Ari is worth absorbing danger.
- **Esta:** self-preservation is deprioritized because the relationship that organized future effort is gone.

The correct category is **grief-driven self-deprioritization / self-disposability**, not automatic death wish.

### 6.3 Scarcity makes self-undervaluation look altruistic

Esta also argues that treatment/survival should go to people who still have dreams, futures, or responsibilities. That is morally intelligible under scarcity, which is exactly why it is dangerous.

Her policy becomes:

`scarce care + own life feels less future-bearing -> rank others above self -> conceal/minimize wound or leave treatment early -> worsening injury becomes more likely`.

This is another instance of the series' core warning: a person can sincerely care about others while using that care to rationalize her own expendability.

### 6.4 Peer reciprocity generalizes the central couple's ethic

Esta's friends reject her ranking. Their counterclaim is simple: if Esta is allowed to worry about them, they are allowed to worry about Esta.

That is the Sheena-Mimi reciprocal non-disposability principle generalized into peer ethics:

`my care for you does not license me to exclude myself from the set of people whose survival matters to you`.

Sheena participates directly by insisting that Esta receive treatment and threatening continued irritation/pressure if Esta keeps disappearing from care. Sheena's personhood ethic is therefore no longer confined to Mimi.

### 6.5 Esta is a future-loss mirror for Sheena

Esta also becomes a terrifying possible image of the future Sheena has been avoiding: the person left alive after the relationship that organized ordinary meaning is gone.

Sheena has already learned that Mimi may live long after her. V07 reverses the viewpoint and shows what losing the loved person can do to the survivor who remains.

This does not make Sheena promise immortality. Instead it intensifies the urgency of truthful finite commitment.

### 6.6 `時間は平等じゃない` — waiting for certainty also spends time

Near the chapter endpoint Sheena reflects that time is not equal and that she has already been taught this repeatedly. The day ends while she still has not found the “best” answer.

Then the decision changes:

`それでも 伝えなくちゃ ミミに`

Even so, she has to tell Mimi.

This is a critical modification of the CH30 policy. Careful semantic self-examination remains valuable, but **indefinite deferral is no longer neutral** when mortality is real.

Updated policy:

`important relationship category uncertain -> investigate rather than force answer -> if finite time makes endless delay itself costly -> communicate the truest available formulation without requiring perfect certainty`.

## 7. CH34 — `二人のかたち`: lovers as mutually chosen primacy, not a borrowed script

### 7.1 Sheena considers the conventional formulation and rejects it as insufficient

Sheena explicitly thinks of simply saying:

`つき合ってほしい`  
`ミミの恋人になりたい`

She suspects Mimi would probably not reject her. Yet that is not enough. Her actual concern is whether this is truly what she wants to **tell** Mimi.

This is one of the clearest demonstrations of Sheena's semantic/ethical care in the entire series. She does not want the socially recognizable label to replace the underlying meaning.

### 7.2 Ordinary flowers make space for difficult speech

The confession develops during an ordinary shared flower-gathering/wrapping sequence rather than a spectacular battlefield crisis. This is consistent with the manga's established relationship grammar: major attachment states are often articulated through low-temperature domestic/sensory activity.

The flowers are not a generic romance decoration. They connect:

- V01-V02 ordinary-life stabilization;
- V05 booklet shared plant/flower care;
- V06 maternal sensory memory;
- V07 finite-time confession.

### 7.3 Mimi names why Sheena's infirmary role makes her jealous

Mimi finally gives the full concrete content of her discomfort. She watches Sheena through the infirmary doorway because she worries. She sees Sheena use magic on other people, wipe bodies, answer when called by name, talk with patients, and appear happy doing it.

Her fear is not expressed as a theory of monogamy. She imagines other people becoming absorbed in Sheena's magic/cookies, wanting the kind of closeness Mimi has, and then imagines Sheena perhaps choosing someone who is not Mimi.

The sentence breaks before completion.

This is exactly the kind of V07 evidence prospective test #2 was designed to catch:

`distributed Sheena care -> concrete observation -> person-specific exclusivity threat -> monitoring -> unfinished fear -> tears`.

### 7.4 Sheena's confession is direct

Sheena first answers Mimi's fear with a simple rejection of the imagined replacement: she does not want that.

Then she says:

`好き`  
`好きなの`

and, crucially:

`私はね ミミに 恋をしてるの`

Sheena directly identifies her state as being **in love with Mimi**.

She then differentiates this `好き` from generic friendship/affection: it is special.

At this point the V06 OPEN question concerning **Sheena's own romantic self-label** is closed at high confidence.

### 7.5 What Sheena actually wants: nearest place, hand-holding, non-yielded primacy

Sheena's internal clarification is more important than the label itself. What she wants to convey is approximately:

- she wants to be close enough to hold Mimi's hand whatever happens;
- she wants the nearest place;
- she does not want to yield that place to anyone else;
- this desire exists because she loves Mimi—deeply and specifically.

The model should therefore define Sheena's romantic commitment as **relational primacy + continuing presence + non-interchangeability**, not merely sexual/physical attraction or social-status acquisition.

### 7.6 Mimi's answer is symmetrical in content but distinct in language

Mimi responds first through the language she already knows best: Sheena has been the only one for her for a long time. She also wants `special` status.

When Sheena's hand-holding now feels newly hot, Mimi asks for reciprocal specialness:

`シーナを ミミの特別にしても いいよね`

This is not a sophisticated doctrinal account of romance. It is a concrete mapping:

`Sheena says Mimi is uniquely special -> Mimi knows Sheena has long been uniquely special to her -> reciprocal specialness is allowed -> relationship category can follow`.

### 7.7 `恋人` becomes mutual rather than merely suggested

The decisive category transition occurs when Mimi says that if both of them are special to each other, then they can become lovers.

Sheena checks whether Mimi actually understands the term. The result is comic but analytically useful: Mimi has encountered romance vocabulary in old infirmary books and even knows terms such as `浮気` and `不倫`. Yet her *reasoning* about their relationship remains simple and concrete: friends can be many, but a lover is one person; Sheena is that person for Mimi.

Mimi further identifies herself as Sheena's first lover.

Sheena does not reject that classification. The chapter then stages another explicitly non-magical kiss and an exuberant Mimi declaration that she loves Sheena.

Therefore, at the V07 boundary:

**Sheena-Mimi formal mutual romantic relationship / lover status: ESTABLISHED.**

This is a genuine transition from V06, not a retrospective correction to V01-V05.

### 7.8 The relationship does not erase prior identities

CH34 also contains a quieter but equally important compatibility test around Sheena's infirmary role.

Mimi admits that Sheena helping others can make her jealous, but also says she enjoys what the new role gives *her*: after Sheena works herself limp, Mimi can care for her. When Sheena suggests Mimi might be suited to general infirmary work, Mimi immediately says she only wants to do that kind of care **because it is Sheena**.

This is a powerful negative constraint for simulation:

- Do not generalize Mimi into a universally nurturing caregiver because she nurses Sheena intensely.
- Do not interpret romance as requiring Sheena to stop helping other people.
- Do not interpret Sheena's public healer identity as reducing Mimi's special status.

Their V07 solution is role complementarity under exclusivity:

`Sheena may care for many people -> Mimi may feel jealous -> Mimi remains Sheena-specific caregiver/return-home partner -> relationship primacy is protected without demanding social isolation`.

## 8. Drawn bonus `だいすき` — the first explicit externalized memory record of the relationship

The drawn bonus `だいすき` is post-CH34 narrative evidence.

### 8.1 Mimi keeps a diary

Sheena discovers Mimi writing and learns that Mimi keeps a diary. Mimi does not write a detailed daily chronicle; she writes only occasionally because ordinary days can feel repetitive and writing takes time. Some entries are extremely short.

This is important after V06's memory revelation. A diary is, materially, an **external memory object**.

However, the source does **not** explicitly say Mimi began the diary because she fears long-term forgetting. The correct inference is therefore limited:

- direct fact: Mimi records selected personally meaningful events in writing;
- strong structural relevance: this creates a durable memory anchor outside biological memory;
- not established: deliberate anti-forgetting strategy motivated by immortality.

### 8.2 Sheena is a privileged diary subject

Mimi tells Sheena that she writes a lot about her. Sheena becomes intensely curious, but Mimi refuses to let her read it because it is a diary/private record.

This is a small but useful agency boundary. Becoming lovers does not mean total informational access.

### 8.3 The relationship transition is recorded in Mimi's own words

The bonus reveals simple entries such as:

`シーナと恋人になった`  
`シーナ だいすき`  
`今日は 夜ふかししよ`

The first line is especially important because it independently confirms the CH34 classification from Mimi's side. She does not merely repeat Sheena's confession or tolerate a label in dialogue. She privately records that **she became lovers with Sheena**.

This closes the remaining ambiguity about symmetrical relationship recognition.

### 8.4 Memory, romance, and ordinary time converge

The diary juxtaposes the extraordinary category transition—becoming lovers—with extremely ordinary plans such as staying up late.

That is the series' relationship grammar in miniature:

`mortality/forgetting threat -> finite day -> ordinary shared act -> selected memory -> relationship identity preserved in routine as well as declaration`.

The diary is therefore one of V07's strongest memory/personhood objects even though its origin motive remains unspoken.

## 10. Behavioral-policy updates

`relationship feeling becomes clear -> conventional label considered -> test whether label captures intended promise -> if perfect answer remains unavailable while finite time is salient -> communicate the truest available meaning rather than defer indefinitely`  
Confidence: high.

**S-POL-12 — distributed care with dyadic primacy**
