## 2. Volume-level thesis — love does not abolish war; it changes what return, fear, and loss mean

V02 ended by establishing a relationship-conditioned return grammar: Sheena waits, asks Mimi to come back without injury, and becomes a specific interpersonal destination on the far side of deployment. V03 immediately tests that model against actual wounds and then constructs a devastating comparison case through Ari and Seiran.

The volume's central development is that **attachment can push behavior in opposite directions without becoming ethically simple**.

For Sheena and Mimi, being valued as someone whose pain matters creates pressure toward return, care, and the refusal to treat an immortal body as disposable. Mimi does not suddenly become tactically cautious; instead, she tries to satisfy Sheena's request by hiding/repairing the evidence of injury before being seen. Sheena, confronted with the gap between her wish and war's reality, momentarily condemns her own request as naïve. Mimi refuses that interpretation: being told not to get hurt made her happy because it meant someone cared whether she suffered.

For Seiran and Ari, love initially produces almost the reverse policy. Seiran wants to become stronger, fight more, and accept pain because she cannot bear the thought of Ari bleeding in war. She therefore converts attachment into **protective self-instrumentalization**: if somebody must absorb battlefield risk, she wants it to be herself. Her first actual kill then reveals the psychological cost that training and declared resolve could not erase. She trembles, is horrified by how easily a human body comes apart, and interprets her own moral shock as shameful weakness. Ari repairs that interpretation by telling her that reluctance to kill is not weakness and that returning alive is itself meaningful.

CH14 gives Seiran a second battlefield experience. This time she experiences something new: she successfully helps a wounded senior. She can now locate value in combat through rescue rather than killing. When she herself is catastrophically wounded, Ari becomes an explicit survival target — `帰らなきゃ`, "I have to get back." She does return from the combat zone. She does not return to Ari. She dies on the march back, body disappearing in front of Mimi, leaving Ari's pendant behind.

The extra story `ひだまり` then refuses to reduce Seiran to "the serious girl who wanted to fight." It reconstructs the private life that military seriousness concealed: Seiran likes pretty stones, new clothes, flowers, manicures, piercings, teasing domesticity, shared sleep, kissing Ari, and being told she looks beautiful. Ari's retrospective grief exposes the unresolved asymmetry inside their love. Because Seiran was strong and resolute, Ari supported her. After Seiran's death, Ari admits the selfish request she had suppressed: if Seiran had been more of a coward, perhaps Ari could have said `行かないで` — don't go.

V03 therefore transforms several earlier concepts:

- "return" is not guaranteed by love; it becomes a **motive whose failure is narratively legible**;
- "fear" is not proof of unfitness; it can be an intact moral response to killing and mortality;
- "strength" can become a social trap when loved ones feel they must respect it;
- "protecting someone" can produce both life-preserving restraint and self-sacrificial militarization;
- the institution's replacement/substitution logic is now embodied in a named relationship rather than remaining abstract;
- ordinary beauty and domestic intimacy become not decorative relief from war but evidence of the lives war consumes.

The V03 state can be summarized as:

`safe-return request tested by real injury -> being cared about becomes valuable in itself -> protective love militarizes Seiran -> first-kill moral shock -> Ari redefines weakness/return -> ordinary attachments become survival anchors -> Seiran dies after the battle while trying to return -> Ari's retrospective ordinary life reveals the private future foreclosed by institutional war`.

## 3. CH11 — おかえり

### 3.1 V02's safe-return request meets the infirmary

CH11 deliberately reactivates Sheena's V02 language before offering any reassurance. The operation ends and Sheena chooses to meet Mimi rather than remain at ordinary distance. The infirmary is crowded with wounded students, and Fran directs Sheena into a back area while treatment continues (`V03 / CH11 / p_0001-p_0008`).

The chapter then converts the institution's abstract attrition into literal recovery logistics. A hooded student arrives with the familiar tone of routine work — `いつも通り回収してきたからな` — and Sheena discovers grievously damaged bodies among what has been "recovered" (`p_0008-p_0012`). The visual violence is important because it places institutional language and bodily reality on the same page. "Recovery" is not a metaphor; children return as bloodied material requiring sorting, transport, and treatment.

This strengthens V01-V02's bottom-up institutional thesis. The school does not merely tell children that death is normal. It has infrastructure for receiving bodies after battle.

### 3.2 Mimi tries to make the request true after the fact

Mimi finally appears badly injured but conscious and actively restoring. Her first emotional response on seeing Sheena is delight that Sheena waited (`p_0012-p_0017`). She visibly experiences pain, then immediately attempts to retract what Sheena has perceived:

- she insists the pain was a lie;
- claims she is not injured;
- explains that she heals quickly;
- says she thought it might be all right if she cleaned herself up before returning;
- apologizes: `シーナのお願い 守れなくてごめんね`.

This is the first major V03 modeling correction.

V02 established that Sheena's safe-return request was salient inside Mimi's deployment cognition. V03 confirms that salience but reveals an unexpected behavioral pathway:

`Sheena asks Mimi not to get hurt -> Mimi is injured anyway -> Mimi tries to repair/conceal the visible injury before reunion -> Mimi apologizes for failing the relational request`.

That is **not tactical risk aversion**. It is relationship-conditioned impression management/reassurance. Mimi wants Sheena not to be hurt by seeing her hurt.

For simulation purposes, this becomes a distinct policy candidate from self-protection. A model that simply lowers Mimi's combat-risk parameter because Sheena cares about her would be wrong at this boundary.

### 3.3 Sheena's moral wish collides with structural reality — and she overcorrects against herself

Sheena does not respond to Mimi's wounds with anger. She hugs her despite the blood (`p_0017-p_0022`). She then begins accusing herself of naïveté: asking Mimi to value herself and not get hurt now feels to Sheena like childish insistence against a reality she does not understand.

Her internal chain is significant:

`I asked Mimi to treasure herself / not get hurt -> war makes that impossible -> therefore my request may have been selfish ignorance -> I should apologize`.

This is a failure mode in Sheena's emerging personhood ethic. Her epistemic humility can become **self-erasure** if structural limits are interpreted as proof that moral concern itself was illegitimate.

Mimi interrupts that collapse.

### 3.4 Mimi distinguishes impossible compliance from unwanted care

Mimi's response is emphatic and emotionally simple:

`謝っちゃヤダ！`
`ミミは嬉しかったよ`

She does not argue that Sheena understands war correctly. She says that Sheena's concern made her happy (`p_0020-p_0024`).

This matters because it separates two propositions Sheena had fused:

1. "I can ensure Mimi returns uninjured."
2. "It matters to me whether Mimi is hurt."

The first is beyond Sheena's control. The second is not meaningless merely because the first fails.

Mimi then notices Sheena's tears and again shifts immediately into concrete care: asking whether her cheek hurts, telling her not to cry, repeatedly calling her name. This further strengthens the V01/V02 `M-POL-01` model: Sheena's visible distress pulls Mimi toward proximity and direct reassurance.

### 3.5 The flower crown becomes portable memory, not armor

After cleaning up, Sheena and Mimi walk together rather than immediately restore full classroom routine. Mimi has carried the V02 flower crown into battle. Asked why, she says keeping it in her pocket lets her remember many fun things (`p_0025-p_0031`).

This is the clearest evidence yet for the flower crown's function. It is not a magical protective charm. It is a **retrieval object for ordinary affect**.

Sheena makes another crown. Her internal wish is no longer the impossible demand that nothing bad occur. She wishes, in effect, that even when wounded Mimi will be able to smile again, and that the living thing before her can remain alive and well as long as possible (`p_0030-p_0034`).

The chapter closes with Sheena beginning to formulate `私が ここで生きる意味は` — the meaning of her living here. V03 does not yet warrant a final life-purpose thesis, but it strongly suggests that Sheena is moving from "I cannot accept this system" toward "within what I cannot presently change, what form of life-preserving relation can I enact?"

That is a more simulation-useful development than generic optimism. Sheena's agency remains structurally small, but she is beginning to attach meaning to the quality of return, repair, and ordinary life.

## 4. CH12 — 理由

### 4.1 A combat exam turns friendship into a test variable

Seiran and Mimi are summoned for a special combat evaluation (`V03 / CH12 / p_0035-p_0042`). The ordinary custom is for a student to duel an upperclass student so ability can be assessed. Because of availability and capability, Mimi is assigned as Seiran's opponent and instructed not to hold back.

Mimi initially asks for the situation in concrete terms rather than displaying abstract understanding of the evaluation's institutional purpose. This supports the V02 prediction that difficult institutional questions remain filtered through simple practical categories.

The deeper point becomes clear when classmates discuss the pairing. Mimi is no longer a distant secret weapon. She is a classmate. The exam may therefore test whether Seiran can suppress relational inhibition and concentrate on eliminating an opponent as though the opponent were an enemy (`p_0042-p_0050`).

The institution is not only teaching attack technique. It is testing whether ordinary social recognition can be overridden by combat function.

### 4.2 Seiran is not coerced in the simple sense

When Sheena reacts with alarm, Seiran insists:

`私は 望んでこの試験を受けるんだ！`

This is a crucial agency datum. Seiran wants the examination. Her participation cannot be represented as simple external compulsion.

But "voluntary" is not the end of the analysis.

Ari explains that Seiran is serious, direct, and kind; wanting to fight does not mean she loves war or killing (`p_0043-p_0049`). Seiran's own account then supplies the causal structure.

### 4.3 Gratitude produces institutional loyalty

Seiran says she used to envy Mimi's strength and wanted to become useful on the battlefield. The reason is biographical. She understands the school as the institution that rescued her from a life in which she expected to die powerless and alone. School gave her shelter, training, present identity, and the relationship with Ari. She wants to repay that debt with the abilities it taught her (`p_0049-p_0056`).

Her endorsement is therefore best modeled as:

`prior precarity + institutional rescue -> gratitude/debt -> desire for usefulness -> voluntary martial commitment`.

This is neither simple brainwashing nor fully unconstrained autonomous preference. The institution is the source of both genuine goods and the obligation she feels to risk herself.

That complexity validates the architecture's insistence on separating coercion, adaptation, normalization, and endorsement.

### 4.4 Ari as the deeper reason

Seiran's most important motive emerges later: she does not want Ari to bleed in war. She wants to become strong enough to bear the battlefield burden in Ari's place.

That motive is not fully disclosed until CH13, but CH12 already establishes the dyadic architecture. Ari offers to practice with her; Seiran's seriousness exists inside a highly attached relationship rather than outside ordinary intimacy.

### 4.5 Sheena's response validates the V02 pragmatic-harm-reduction model

The night before the test, Mimi clings physically to Sheena. Sheena notes that Mimi likes being attached to her (`p_0057-p_0061`). She does not try to abolish the exam. Instead, she checks a concrete harm condition: the duel will use practice magic rather than ordinary lethal magic.

Once that lower-harm condition is confirmed, she supports Mimi's effort.

This is a clean V03 example of the V02 prediction that Sheena's personhood objection would survive while her action policy became more pragmatic:

`dangerous institutional demand -> identify whether preventable harm can be reduced -> verify safety constraint -> support person rather than pretend the institution can be wished away`.

Sheena has not normalized combat. She has become more capable of acting without confusing moral objection with impossible total control.

### 4.6 Seiran discovers that formal willingness is not the same as killing a person

Mimi dominates the duel's tempo, and adults observe the large ability gap. Seiran nevertheless performs seriously and notices that Mimi is not using everything she could (`p_0060-p_0064`). The test ends without catastrophic injury.

At this stage, Seiran still believes she can convert resolve into battlefield competence. V03 will shortly show that simulated target-elimination and actual killing are psychologically discontinuous.

## 5. CH13 — 涙のあと

### 5.1 Mimi now says why injury matters

After the exam, Mimi apologizes for hurting Seiran. Seiran rejects the apology because she herself chose to fight seriously and treats injury as the consequence of her own insufficient strength (`V03 / CH13 / p_0065-p_0071`).

Mimi's response materially advances the V02 safety model:

`だって ミミはケガするのやだもん`
`痛いの嫌いだし`
`それに…待っててくれる人が悲しむから`

Two motives are now explicit:

1. Mimi personally dislikes pain/injury.
2. Injury matters because it would sadden the person waiting for her.

This is stronger evidence than V02's flower-crown motif alone. Sheena has entered Mimi's **valuation of injury**.

The correct transition remains cautious. V03 confirms a changed appraisal variable; it does not yet demonstrate a general tactical policy of risk avoidance under combat pressure.

### 5.2 Seiran converts love into self-instrumentalization

Mimi asks whether Seiran dislikes pain and whether she dislikes making Ari sad. Seiran says yes — and then explains why she accepts both.

She wants to become stronger, go to the battlefield repeatedly, and win. School gratitude matters, but her deeper motive is explicit:

`アリが 戦争で血を流す姿なんて 絶対に見たくないから`

She will fight in Ari's place. Therefore she believes she cannot afford to be frightened by injury or indulge the wish not to hurt others.

This is one of the series' strongest early character-model contrasts:

- Sheena's attachment to Mimi: **your pain remains morally important; return matters; don't let immortality erase personhood.**
- Seiran's attachment to Ari: **because your pain is intolerable to me, I will make myself the body that absorbs war instead.**

Both emerge from love. Their behavioral consequences oppose each other.

V03 therefore warns against simplistic claims such as "love humanizes people away from violence." Love can also **militarize the self** when protection is interpreted through substitution.

### 5.3 Mimi recognizes incompatible relational ethics without abstract synthesis

Mimi observes that Seiran says something entirely different from Sheena. She does not resolve the difference with a political theory. The conversation instead turns to what each person has that they want to protect.

Seiran then asks Mimi the question V02 had left structurally open:

`カガリさんって… なんのために戦ってるの？`

Mimi is visibly caught off guard (`p_0071-p_0077`).

This is a major character-model opening. Until now Mimi has possessed a learned rule — fighting is good/helpful — and enormous operational competence. V03 asks for **personal motive** rather than socially supplied usefulness. The volume does not force an answer before the question has had time to work.

That restraint should be preserved longitudinally.

### 5.4 Two departure rituals

Mimi and Seiran are called for an actual deployment. The manga constructs parallel departures.

Mimi tells Sheena she is going; Sheena tells her to be careful.

Seiran tells Ari she is going. Ari stops her and kisses her before departure (`p_0077-p_0083`).

At V01-V02 the Ari–Seiran relationship had been kept conservatively at close/intimate with increasing romantic plausibility because healing-contact could confound kiss evidence. CH13 removes that ambiguity. The kiss is not a healing procedure. It is an affectionate departure act inside an already highly intimate dyad.

The relationship can therefore be promoted at V03 to **reciprocal romantic/intimate relationship**, while leaving open whether the girls use an explicit verbal label such as `恋人`.

### 5.5 The first kill breaks Seiran's self-model

The deployment is small enough that the group returns quickly. Seiran withdraws socially after return, and in private she collapses against Ari (`p_0084-p_0092`).

Her account is direct:

`人を殺した`

She killed one person. When an enemy aimed a wand at her, she fired back as trained. The other person's body burst apart. She shakes, says she was scared, and is stunned by how easily a human being dies.

The most important psychological layer comes next. Seiran does not only experience fear and horror. She experiences **shame about experiencing them**.

She says she became unable to move properly afterward, could not function like everyone else, and feels useless. She believed she understood war and had prepared herself to fight. The fact that killing still destabilized her becomes evidence, in her own mind, that she is weak.

This creates a two-stage injury:

`actual kill -> moral/bodily shock -> institutionally conditioned self-judgment that shock = weakness`.

The series thereby distinguishes behavioral willingness from emotional normalization. Seiran had voluntarily sought combat and sincerely endorsed service. Neither fact made the first kill emotionally ordinary.

### 5.6 Ari performs moral repair

Ari refuses Seiran's shame model.

She tells her that hesitating to take a life is not weakness and that she should not be ashamed. Seiran fought while trembling, survived, and fulfilled her role. Ari then grounds the abstract "what you protected" in her own embodied presence: the person Seiran wanted to protect is here.

Most importantly, she tells Seiran:

`帰ってきてくれて ありがとう`

Thank you for coming back.

Ari thereby redefines successful soldiering away from efficient killing and toward **return, survival, and preserved attachment**.

This is not necessarily anti-war resistance on Ari's part. She still supports Seiran's chosen role. But it is an alternative evaluative framework inside the institution:

`you felt horror -> that does not make you weak`
`you survived and came back -> that matters`.

For character modeling, Ari emerges as someone who can respond to another person's self-condemnation by reframing the standard rather than merely soothing the feeling.

## 6. CH14 — もう怖くないよ

### 6.1 Ari turns care into an object Seiran can carry

Ari prepares a magical "letter" whose words are stored inside a pendant/amulet and asks Seiran to carry it (`V03 / CH14 / p_0101-p_0105`).

The object parallels Sheena's flower crown. Both are small pieces of ordinary relationship that can cross the spatial boundary into war:

- flower crown -> Sheena/Mimi -> fun memories, waiting, return;
- magical letter/pendant -> Ari/Seiran -> care, pride, safe-return wish.

The objects are not protective artifacts in the mechanical sense established by V03. Their function is affective and mnemonic.

### 6.2 Seiran's private aesthetic self becomes visible before battle

Mimi asks about the pendant. Seiran says Ari gave it to her and then talks about liking beautiful things: sparkling stones, new clothes, and similar objects (`p_0105-p_0108`). She notes that she had not really said this aloud, but Ari somehow understood.

This is essential characterization because it complicates the "serious martial protector" persona before the extra chapter expands it further. Seiran has a quiet aesthetic appetite that her role and presentation do not advertise.

Mimi answers by showing the flowers Sheena gave her and says she keeps them in her pocket. She wants to get home soon.

Again, V03 constructs two relationship-conditioned return objects before exposing both girls to battle.

### 6.3 Seiran finds a new form of battlefield usefulness: helping

During combat, Seiran is attacked and then encounters a wounded senior/student. She applies hemostatic medicine and coordinates carrying the injured person back (`p_0109-p_0115`).

Her internal reaction is diagnostically important:

she has, for the first time on a battlefield, **helped a person**.

After CH13's first-kill crisis, this supplies a new route through her service motive. "Being useful" need not mean only eliminating enemies. Rescue produces a positive self-appraisal that killing did not.

This may have become a healthier integration pathway had it had time to develop. V03 does not give Seiran that time.

### 6.4 Ari becomes a survival command

Seiran is severely wounded in a subsequent attack (`p_0115-p_0119`). Pain, weakness, and loss of motor control are explicit. As she struggles to move, the image/memory of Ari appears and Seiran thinks:

`……帰らなきゃ`

She has to return.

This is the strongest evidence in V03 that attachment can function as a survival-motivation variable under acute bodily crisis. The pattern now exists in both central/parallel dyads:

`someone waits -> return has a specific face/place -> survival effort becomes relationally organized`.

But V03 immediately refuses to romanticize this into guaranteed rescue.

### 6.5 The institution is overwhelmed by casualties

The return/recovery zone is overloaded. A report indicates four from Class 17 and five from Class 16 have "disappeared," while staff say medicine is already insufficient and some wounded require the health officer (`p_0120`).

The language `消えた` is consistent with the established body-disappearance system surrounding death. V03 therefore makes attrition numerically concrete while still keeping macro-politics almost entirely offstage.

War appears as bodies, missing classmates, medical shortage, and exhausted staff — still the bottom-up institutional perspective.

### 6.6 Seiran survives the battle and dies on the return

Seiran makes it into the returning group. Mimi notices her condition. Seiran is still bleeding heavily but minimizes it as being "a little cold" (`p_0121-p_0124`).

The understatement can be read in more than one way and should not be over-fixed:
- she may not recognize the terminal severity;
- she may be suppressing it to keep moving;
- she may be habituated to minimizing bodily need;
- some combination may apply.

What the page does establish is that the body is failing while ordinary return behavior continues around her. Mimi casually wonders about dinner. Seiran is walking toward the place where Ari waits.

Then Seiran stops.

Her body begins to disappear (`p_0125-p_0128`).

Mimi watches as the person she had just been discussing injury, waiting, and reasons for fighting with dissolves in front of her. The magical pendant falls to the ground. Seiran's body is gone; the object carrying Ari's words remains.

This is V03's cruelest formal realization of the series' absence grammar.

In V01, a dead roommate left belongings and an empty room.
In V02, dead students' belongings became material circulation.
In V03, the reader sees the conversion occur in real time:

`person -> disappearance -> object`.

### 6.7 The "return" grammar breaks one step before completion

Seiran did nearly everything attachment was supposed to motivate:
- she survived immediate enemy contact;
- she helped another wounded person;
- she kept moving after severe injury;
- she oriented herself toward Ari;
- she made the return march.

She still dies before reaching Ari.

This does not invalidate attachment as a motive. It establishes its limit.

The manga's return grammar is therefore not:
`love -> survival`.

It is:
`love -> a reason to try to return`, under conditions where the institution cannot guarantee that return.

That distinction is fundamental for later simulation and thematic synthesis.

## 7. 番外編 ひだまり — ordinary intimacy after death has made it irrecoverable

### 7.1 Evidence-class note

`ひだまり` is an in-volume narrative extra published after CH14. Its scenes largely depict Ari and Seiran's ordinary/private life before Seiran's death, while its framing voice clearly belongs to Ari's grief after the loss.

For prospective discipline:
- these scenes become **available evidence at the V03 boundary**;
- they can revise our understanding of the pre-death relationship;
- they must not be retroactively treated as information the V01/V02 reader already possessed.

### 7.2 The private relationship is substantially more intimate than the public evidence showed

The extra depicts Ari and Seiran sharing room/private space with easy physical familiarity, sleep/waking routines, teasing, kisses, and domestic planning (`V03 / EXTRA / p_0133-p_0144`).

This removes the remaining substantive ambiguity about the dyad's romantic/intimate nature. Their relationship is not merely a close friendship onto which readers project romance. The manga directly depicts reciprocal kissing and couple-like private intimacy.

The absence of a spoken label such as `恋人` remains a lexical fact, but it is no longer a reason to leave romantic classification merely "plausible."

### 7.3 Seiran's hidden ordinary desires

The extra gives Seiran a much richer baseline outside war.

She likes:
- pretty stones;
- new clothes;
- flowers;
- manicures;
- piercings;
- being adorned;
- private teasing and affection;
- shared domestic space.

She explicitly thanks Ari for repeatedly doing with her the things she had wanted to try (`p_0145-p_0158`).

This revises a potentially misleading V01-V02 impression. Seiran's seriousness is real, but it is not the whole person. The martial role is superimposed on an ordinary aesthetic and intimate self that has not been given much room publicly.

For simulation, this means Seiran cannot be generated as permanently stern, utilitarian, or battle-focused. Trusted private context reveals embarrassment, pleasure, aesthetic preference, reciprocal teasing, and softness.

### 7.4 Ari as facilitator of Seiran's non-military self

Ari does more than receive Seiran's protection. She helps Seiran inhabit a life not organized around usefulness:
- arranging flowers;
- experimenting with appearance;
- painting nails;
- making private space comfortable;
- teasing;
- kissing;
- affirming beauty.

Seiran's gratitude makes the function explicit: Ari repeatedly joins her in things she wanted to experience.

This gives Ari a second major relational role alongside CH13's moral repair:
**Ari creates conditions in which Seiran can be a person with tastes and pleasures rather than primarily a weapon/protector.**

### 7.5 Present intimacy versus future risk

One internal line in the extra describes the present moment of having the other's smile to oneself as more important than any future one could imagine (`p_0159`). The retrospective placement makes that sentiment painfully double-edged.

The pair has intense present-tense intimacy but does not secure a shared future against the institution's demands. Seiran channels love into more fighting; Ari respects that strength even while wanting a life of ordinary closeness.

Their romance therefore becomes a counterexample to any assumption that emotional truth automatically produces protective decision-making.

### 7.6 Ari's suppressed veto

The extra's grief captions are the decisive Ari evidence:

`セイラン あなたが`
`あなたがもっと弱虫なら`
`私もわがままを言えたのに`
`行かないでって`

Ari wishes, after the fact, that Seiran had been weaker/more cowardly so Ari might have permitted herself the selfish demand: don't go (`p_0160-p_0161`).

This is not proof that Ari should have or could have stopped Seiran. It reveals the internal conflict she suppressed while Seiran was alive:

`respect Seiran's chosen strength and protective commitment`
versus
`want Seiran to stay because losing her is unbearable`.

Ari chose support. Grief exposes the cost of that choice without making the alternative simple.

### 7.7 The pendant/letter survives where Seiran does not

The extra closes around the writing apparatus and returned pendant (`p_0161-p_0163`). Ari is left with objects and words after the person who carried them has disappeared.

This mirrors the series' earlier memorial materiality but is more intimate:
- the object was designed to carry Ari's voice toward Seiran;
- after death, it carries Seiran's absence back toward Ari.

### 7.8 PARATEXT — Ari's reproduced letter

The physical-edition back-cover reproduction at `p_0165` contains the full letter placed in the pendant. Ari wishes:
- that Seiran not experience pain;
- that someone help her if she is hurt;
- that Seiran not worry because Ari will not cry alone;
- that Seiran know Ari is proud of the strength with which she goes to the battlefield;
- that she return safely.

It is signed, in effect, "to my beloved/dear Seiran, from your Ari."

Because this is reproduced cover/back-cover material, it remains **PARATEXT**. It nevertheless corroborates the relational contradiction already established in the main narrative and extra: Ari simultaneously blesses Seiran's strength and begs for safe return.

The line about not crying alone becomes especially painful after the extra reveals that Ari had suppressed `行かないで`.

## 8. V03 character-state transitions

### 8.1 Sheena — moral concern survives the failure of control

**STRENGTHEN:** Sheena's personhood ethic remains intact.

CH11 briefly exposes a failure mode: when her safe-return request cannot be fulfilled, Sheena begins treating her own concern as naïve ignorance. Mimi's reaction prevents that from becoming a full retreat. Sheena learns that caring whether Mimi hurts has interpersonal value even when she cannot prevent war.

**STRENGTHEN / REVISE:** ordinary-life agency becomes purpose-bearing.

V02 already made ordinary routine positive shared futurity. V03 strengthens this into a candidate meaning structure: Sheena may not control deployment, but she can make return lead back to a world containing food, flowers, touch, waiting, repair, and somebody who refuses to regard wounds as trivial.

**STRENGTHEN:** pragmatic harm-reduction policy.

**OPEN:** response to Seiran's death.

### 8.2 Mimi — relationship state now changes injury appraisal

**STRENGTHEN:** Sheena as waiting/return destination.

V03 provides repeated direct evidence:
- joy that Sheena waited;
- apology for failing the no-injury request;
- flower crown carried for happy memories;
- explicit statement that injury would make the waiting person sad;
- desire to return home quickly.

Mimi's first demonstrated adaptation to Sheena's request is concealment/repair before reunion. The correct model is therefore:

`Sheena cares about injury -> Mimi values avoiding Sheena's distress -> may attempt to return looking/being okay`.

Whether that reduces combat risk-taking remains OPEN.

**STRENGTHEN:** direct emotional care.

When Sheena cries, Mimi immediately rejects the apology, names her own happiness, checks Sheena's pain, and asks her not to cry.

**OPEN:** personal reason for fighting.

Seiran directly asks Mimi why she fights. Mimi's surprise establishes that "fighting is good" may not yet be a personally authored motive. Do not invent the answer.

**NEW:** exposure to mortal return failure.

Mimi witnesses Seiran die after the battle while carrying Ari's return object. This is likely to be highly diagnostic for an immortal child whose own return has been treated differently, but V03 ends before her processing is shown. The state change is **exposure**, not a guessed conclusion.

### 8.3 Seiran — from grateful soldier to morally injured protector

V03 upgrades Seiran from a relatively thin serious/practical peer into a substantial model.

**Core motive architecture:**
`institutional rescue/debt + desire for usefulness + love for Ari -> become strong -> take more battlefield burden -> keep Ari from bleeding`.

**REVISE:** willingness to fight ≠ emotional normalization of killing.

Her first kill produces fear, shaking, functional disruption, and shame.

**REVISE:** shame is not simply fear of death.

Seiran is ashamed that she could not behave like the experienced norm after killing. Her self-criticism is therefore tied to an internalized performance standard.

**STRENGTHEN:** attachment as survival motive.

Under fatal injury, Ari becomes `帰らなきゃ`.

**NEW:** rescue can satisfy the usefulness motive without killing.

Helping a wounded peer produces a positive internal response that may have offered an alternative combat identity.

**NEW / retroactive V03 evidence:** private aesthetic/affectionate self.

`ひだまり` establishes strong interests and behaviors that public seriousness obscures: beauty, adornment, playful embarrassment, domestic intimacy, kissing, shared appearance experimentation.

**Terminal V03 state:** deceased.

The character model freezes at death; later retrospective evidence may expand understanding but cannot create later behavioral state unless the series supplies supernatural return evidence specific to Seiran.

### 8.4 Ari — affection, moral repair, and suppressed possessiveness

**STRENGTHEN:** perceptive relational caretaker.

Ari understands Seiran's tastes, provides objects/activities aligned with them, and creates ordinary private space.

**NEW:** moral-reframing policy.

`Seiran self-condemns after first kill -> Ari rejects weakness framing -> validates moral hesitation -> redirects success toward survival/protection/return`.

**STRENGTHEN:** romantic attachment.

Departure kiss + `ひだまり` reciprocal kisses/domestic intimacy make romantic classification strong.

**NEW:** support can conceal a competing veto.

Ari supports Seiran's strength and writes that she is proud, but post-death grief reveals she wanted to say don't go. Her behavior cannot be modeled as simple endorsement. She suppresses possessive/protective desire in order not to invalidate Seiran's chosen identity.

**Terminal V03 dyadic transition:** bereavement.

Ari survives with returned objects and the private knowledge of a request she did not make.

## 10. Mortality, expendability, and personhood transitions

### 10.1 Death is no longer only announced after the fact

V01 began with a death notice and absence.
V03 makes Mimi and the reader witness Seiran's disappearance in real time.

The institution's body-disappearance rule is therefore no longer abstract administrative lore. The visual sequence directly shows a wounded living student become absence, leaving a personal object behind.

### 10.2 Return and survival separate

Seiran demonstrates that "survived combat" and "returned to the person waiting" are not the same state.

A simulation or ledger must therefore distinguish:
- combat-zone survival;
- medical stability;
- return transit;
- reunion completion.

Attachment can affect all four without guaranteeing any.

### 10.3 Fear becomes morally diagnostic

Seiran's first kill shows that fear/hesitation can persist inside voluntary martial participation. Ari explicitly treats reluctance to kill as compatible with strength.

This strengthens the project's resistance to collapsing:
`coercion -> adaptation -> normalization -> endorsement`.

Seiran is partly endorsing participation and still not emotionally normalized to killing.

### 10.4 Protective love can reproduce expendability

Seiran's wish to keep Ari safe leads her to treat her own body as the substitute.

That is not institutional coercion in a narrow sense, but it dovetails perfectly with the institution's substitution logic:
`someone must bear risk -> I choose myself because I love the other`.

V03 therefore shows how structural expendability can be reproduced from below through sincere attachment.

## 11. Institution / war-consciousness / agency transitions

### 11.1 The school tests relational suppression

The combat exam appears designed partly to test whether Seiran can treat a familiar person as an enemy-object. This is a more psychologically invasive form of military training than ordinary skill measurement.

### 11.2 Institutional gratitude complicates consent

Seiran sincerely wants to repay the school that rescued her. Her choice is real. Its motivational environment is also structured by dependency and debt.

The correct classification is neither:
- "forced child soldier with no agency" nor
- "fully free adult-like volunteer."

It is **constrained, socially produced endorsement**.

### 11.3 Medical infrastructure is visibly insufficient

CH11 and CH14 together show:
- mass wound recovery;
- grievously injured children;
- finite medicine;
- multiple class losses;
- return transit that fails to catch Seiran's terminal condition.

The institution is organized for attrition but is not capable of making that attrition safe.

### 11.4 Macro-politics gate

**Status: remains CLOSED.**

V03 adds important military-institutional material:
- combat examinations;
- target-elimination conditioning;
- casualty counts;
- medical shortage;
- individual motives for service;
- substitution through love and gratitude.

It still does not provide enough recurring evidence about:
- enemy political identity;
- war origins/casus belli;
- state factions;
- diplomacy;
- propaganda/public politics;
- strategic war aims beyond generalized victory.

The existing bottom-up institution/war-consciousness ledger remains the correct home.

## 12. Visual and dialogue grammar — V03

### 12.1 Objects cross the battlefield boundary

Two objects now structure attachment:
- Sheena's flower crown;
- Ari's magical-letter pendant.

Both compress ordinary intimacy into something portable. The key difference is outcome:
- Mimi returns carrying/remembering the flower crown;

The paired motif makes the relationship comparison formal, not merely thematic.

### 12.2 Person -> absence -> object

Seiran's death sequence uses progressive white dissolution. The body becomes less visually present across successive panels until only the pendant drops.

The formal grammar directly enacts the series' institutional ontology of death:
**the person vanishes; the relationship object remains retrievable.**

### 12.3 Blood trail against ordinary conversation

Just before Seiran collapses, Mimi talks about dinner while Seiran's legs leave a heavy blood trail. This juxtaposition is classic KimiShinu normalcy/horror contamination: ordinary return talk and terminal injury occupy the same movement through space.

### 12.4 Mimi voice hardening

- pain is bad;
- someone waiting will be sad;
- Sheena and Seiran say different things;

She does not suddenly develop abstract war theory.

### 12.5 Sheena voice hardening

Sheena remains more internalized than Mimi. Under moral failure/self-doubt she turns blame inward and apologizes. CH11 therefore adds a negative simulation warning:
**direct moral concern does not imply stable confidence.** When reality defeats her proposed action, she may retreat into self-criticism before rebuilding a workable position.

### 12.6 Seiran voice layers

Public/goal-directed Seiran:
- firm declaratives;

Post-kill Seiran:

Private Seiran in `ひだまり`:

A simulation must condition Seiran's voice heavily on context rather than generate one uniformly serious register.

Ari is socially more fluid:

## 17. V03 freeze

V03 is now frozen at the V03 prospective boundary.

The most important model change is not merely "Seiran dies." It is that the volume constructs a complete causal chain around that death:

`institutional rescue/debt -> voluntary martial identity -> love turns self into substitute -> first kill reveals intact moral fear -> Ari repairs shame -> attachment supplies a return motive -> Seiran helps rather than kills -> catastrophic injury -> return attempt -> death before reunion -> object returns without person -> grief reveals the veto Ari never voiced`.

Against that chain, Sheena and Mimi's safer-return relationship gains new meaning without being sentimentalized. Being loved can make injury matter differently. It cannot guarantee survival. The question moving into V04 is how an immortal child and a death-sensitive classmate metabolize seeing that difference become personal.
