## 2. Volume-level thesis — after return fails, love becomes a reciprocal limit on disposability

V03 ended by proving that attachment can motivate return without guaranteeing it. Seiran survives combat, attempts to return to Ari, and dies before reunion. V04 asks what the survivors do after that failure of return becomes irreversible.

The volume gives three sharply different answers.

**Ari** is offered the most literal rejection of irreversibility: resurrection. She refuses it. Her refusal is not a rejection of love and not a sudden ideological conversion against the institution. It is an autonomy judgment. Seiran's life belongs to Seiran; Ari's grief does not give Ari ownership over Seiran's soul, body, or future. Eternal life cannot be imposed merely because the survivor cannot bear loss. V04 therefore turns resurrection from a metaphysical curiosity into a personhood test.

**Mimi**, by contrast, initially cannot make irreversibility emotionally coherent. She has been resurrected herself, can reconstruct catastrophic bodily damage, and has lived inside a world where extreme injury is often reversible. Seiran's disappearance creates a category failure. Mimi asks why Seiran cannot simply be made as she was, why yesterday's ordinary classmate is suddenly the one person for whom continuation has ended. She cannot initially name the feeling. Sheena names it with her: sadness/loneliness. Mimi's grief remains characteristically concrete and relational rather than abstract or political.

**Sheena** does not answer mortality by seeking immortality or by copying Seiran's sacrificial protector logic. She confronts the fact that Mimi may continue losing people long after Sheena herself dies and chooses a finite commitment precisely because she is finite: for as long as she is alive, she will be a place Mimi can return to, embrace her, and say `おかえり`. Then she converts grief into skill-building. She asks Fran what her own magic can do and discovers an aptitude for `治癒魔法`, healing magic. Her response to regret becomes: if there is something she can do in time, she does not want to fail to act.

That distinction is crucial. Seiran converted love into **substitution** — "let my body bear the risk instead of Ari's." Sheena increasingly converts love into **support** — "let me develop the capacity to preserve life without treating either person's body as expendable." CH19 completes the contrast when Mimi rejects the remaining asymmetry in Sheena's safe-return ethic. Sheena has repeatedly asked Mimi not to get hurt; Mimi now asks why she is not allowed to demand the same of Sheena. Sheena accepts the claim.

The main-pair relationship therefore crosses an important V04 threshold:

`Sheena asks the immortal weapon to return safely -> Mimi learns that injury saddens a waiting person -> Seiran's failed return makes absence concrete -> Sheena chooses finite presence and healing -> Mimi's post-loss attachment sensitivity intensifies -> minor Sheena injury triggers disproportionate care -> Mimi demands equal standing as someone allowed to fear Sheena's injury -> reciprocal non-disposability`.

V04 is thus not a story in which love abolishes war, death, or asymmetry. It is a story in which love increasingly functions as a **mutual constraint against instrumentalizing either person's body**.

## 3. CH15 — `ただいま`

### 3.1 Morning continues; death remains institutionally routine

CH15 opens by preserving the series' most disturbing normalcy grammar. A classmate has died, the death is announced, and the surviving students are expected to continue training. Morning comes anyway. The school's procedural language does not collapse simply because the dead girl now has a name the central cast knows intimately (`V04 / CH15 / p_0001-p_0008`).

This is an important V03→V04 institutional transition. Seiran's death does not trigger visible institutional rupture. The school is structurally capable of incorporating her death into ordinary operation. That does **not** mean the people closest to her can do the same.

Mimi tells Sheena what happened with characteristic semantic simplicity: Seiran "disappeared." The wording remains close to the literal visual event Mimi witnessed in V03 rather than becoming a theory of death or war. The V03 prediction that Mimi would process the event concretely and relationally is therefore immediately supported.

### 3.2 `ただいま` / `おかえり` after a failed return

The chapter title is not incidental. Sheena and Mimi still possess the return exchange that Seiran and Ari have just lost. The ordinary greeting pair — "I'm home" / "welcome home" — now carries mortality information.

This is a visual/dialogue reweighting rather than a new vocabulary item. The words remain domestic and simple. Their significance changes because the reader has just seen a person survive battle and fail to complete the interpersonal return those words presuppose.

For simulation, this means return language should not be generated as generic affection. By V04 it is a relationship-state marker with accumulated death history behind it.

### 3.3 Clearing Seiran's room — administrative absence versus relational particularity

Sheena and Mimi help Ari clear Seiran's room and belongings (`p_0009-p_0020`). This extends the institutional material grammar already established around earlier deaths: a person's social space becomes an administrative problem for survivors to sort.

Ari initially performs composure. She says she understood Seiran's wish to participate, knew the danger, and had prepared herself for the possibility of this outcome. Her smile is therefore not evidence that she feels little. It is an enacted loyalty to the version of Seiran she believes she is obliged to respect.

Mimi does not accept the performance at face value. She asks why Ari is smiling when she is hurting and, more sharply, whether Ari thinks she is **not allowed to be sad**. The intervention matters because Mimi does not rebut Ari with political theory or an argument about military institutions. She sees a person in pain behaving as though the pain is forbidden and challenges that immediate contradiction.

Ari's composure breaks. Mimi holds her and reassures her, in simple relational terms, that Seiran would not be angry at her for grieving.

This is Mimi's first major post-Seiran care expansion. Her direct-care policy is no longer only Sheena-specific bodily rescue. She can identify and respond to another person's emotional suppression when the distress is concrete enough to perceive.

### 3.4 The pendant returns; the person does not

At the memorial/prayer sequence, Mimi returns Ari's pendant. She explains that Seiran had been holding it on the return march and had shown it happily. The pendant therefore completes the object grammar established in V03: relationship objects can cross the boundary that people fail to cross.

The object is not a substitute for Seiran. Its significance lies in proving what Seiran was oriented toward before death. Ari was not an interpretive invention imposed by survivors; Ari was materially present in Seiran's final return effort.

### 3.5 The senior's testimony revises the causal account of Seiran's death

An upperclass student approaches and explains that Seiran saved him after he was gravely wounded and surrounded. She gave him **all of her medicine** and remained with him. He believes that if she had kept the medicine, she might have survived (`p_0023-p_0029`).

This materially strengthens the V03 model of protective self-instrumentalization.

The terminal causal chain is now more specific:

`desire to be useful/protect others -> battlefield rescue opportunity -> gives away all medicine -> wounded self continues without reserve -> survives immediate combat -> attempts return -> dies before reunion`.

The important analytical restraint is that the story does not frame the rescued senior as having "stolen" Seiran's life. He expresses guilt; the others can still recognize that Seiran genuinely saved him. V04 therefore preserves the moral value of the rescue while exposing its personal cost. The choice can be generous and still participate in a self-sacrificial pattern that kills the chooser.

### 3.6 Resurrection becomes an actual decision rather than background lore

Near the chapter's end, Mimi tells Ari that she knows of magic that can bring Seiran back. This is the first time resurrection is introduced not as Mimi's origin story but as a live option facing a bereaved survivor.

V02 asked what immortality means. V04 now asks whether love gives the survivor the right to choose immortality for the dead.

## 4. CH16 — `選択`

### 4.1 Ari refuses resurrection without denying grief

Ari rejects resurrection decisively (`V04 / CH16 / p_0037-p_0048`). Her reasoning is the strongest personhood/autonomy statement the series has offered outside Sheena's anti-instrumental ethic.

The core of Ari's position is that **Seiran's life is Seiran's**. Ari's desire to see her again does not make Seiran's existence a resource Ari may manipulate. Eternal life is not an obviously benevolent gift simply because death is painful to the survivor. The scene also indicates that resurrection involves an exchange/cost involving life, further undermining any fantasy of costless restoration.

Ari's refusal should not be misread as "Ari has moved on" or "Ari now approves Seiran's death." She thanks Mimi for thinking of Seiran. Her grief remains intact. The choice is precisely that grief does not override Seiran's autonomy.

This is an important revision to the V03 support-over-veto conflict. Ari's difficulty saying `行かないで` while Seiran lived does not imply passivity in every high-stakes relational choice. Once the choice becomes **what survivors may do to Seiran after death**, Ari becomes extremely clear.

### 4.2 Mimi breaks secrecy because grief outranks the abstraction

Afterward, Sheena reminds Mimi that resurrection is connected to a state secret. Mimi's response is revealing: Seiran died.

This does not establish general political rebellion. It establishes a priority ordering:

`abstract/institutional confidentiality -> normally obeyed or accepted`

but

`concrete dead friend + possible restoration -> secrecy loses motivational priority`.

Mimi's cognition remains person-first and immediate. She does not formulate a doctrine that state secrecy is illegitimate. The death in front of her simply matters more than the rule in that moment.

### 4.3 Mimi's first explicit grief episode

Mimi becomes dysregulated because Seiran's irreversible absence does not fit her ordinary repair model. She asks why Seiran has to stay dead, why she cannot simply be returned to the way she was, and why a person who was just eating and attending class can now be "over" while everyone else continues (`p_0049-p_0058`).

Her internal state is not initially labeled. She describes confusion/badness inside herself rather than producing an abstract grief concept. Sheena supplies the word and shared frame: this is sadness/loneliness.

This is one of the strongest V04 simulation data points because it distinguishes:

- **Mimi knows death operationally** — she has fought for years, has herself died/resurrected, and has seen battlefield bodies;
- from **Mimi now experiences particular bereavement** — the irreversible absence of someone integrated into ordinary peer life.

Immortality has not made her emotionally immune to mortality. Ordinary attachment has instead made mortality newly costly.

### 4.4 Sheena sees the future asymmetry of immortal attachment

Watching Mimi grieve, Sheena realizes the temporal problem implied by V02's immortality revelation: Mimi may continue encountering deaths long after the current cohort is gone, including after Sheena herself dies.

This is not merely "Sheena fears dying." It is a relationship model of **future bereavement asymmetry**. Sheena understands that she cannot promise permanent presence to an ageless person.

### 4.5 Finite presence rather than eternal possession

Sheena's response is the volume's central relational commitment. She cannot abolish death and does not seek resurrection for herself. Instead, she chooses a bounded promise: **for as long as she is alive**, she will be someone Mimi can return to; she will receive her, embrace her, and say `おかえり`.

This is analytically distinct from both Ari's suppressed veto and Seiran's substitution.

- Seiran: "I will take the danger instead of you."
- Ari after death: "My love does not give me authority to decide your eternal existence."
- Sheena: "I cannot control mortality, but I can decide how I will be present while I am alive."

The promise is powerful precisely because it acknowledges its own limit.

### 4.6 Grief returns to ordinary appetite without erasure

The chapter lets Mimi and Sheena return to hunger/food and ordinary life. That transition should not be classified as emotional reset. KimiShinu repeatedly treats ordinary routines as the medium through which life continues after violence. The grief remains part of the state while eating becomes possible again.

### 4.7 `私にできることを知りたい` — inquiry becomes vocation

CH16 closes with Sheena approaching Fran and asking her to examine Sheena's magic because she wants to know what she can do.

This is a strengthened form of the inquiry policy first visible in V02. Sheena is no longer investigating only an external solution to Mimi's condition. She is asking what **her own capacity** can become.

## 5. CH17 — `わたしの魔法`

### 5.1 Fran recognizes a change in Sheena before identifying the magic

Fran initially notices Sheena's exhaustion and tells her to rest. Sheena persists, but the persistence is not framed as reckless self-erasure. She can state a goal: she wants to understand her magic and develop something useful (`V04 / CH17 / p_0067-p_0076`).

Fran's reaction marks a character transition. The girl who often looked down, froze, or apologized for wanting things can now raise her head and articulate a chosen direction.

### 5.2 `治癒魔法` versus `修復魔法`

Sheena explains that she is poor at ordinary magic but can make plants become more vigorous. Under Fran's instruction she concentrates and produces an explosive growth response. Fran identifies the attribute as **`治癒魔法` — healing magic** (`p_0075-p_0083`).

The text distinguishes this from **`修復魔法` — restoration/reconstruction magic**, the kind of bodily repair already associated with repairing severe damage. The exact magical taxonomy should not be expanded beyond the source, but the functional distinction is analytically meaningful:

- restoration reconstructs what has been damaged;
- healing supports or accelerates living recovery/growth.

Sheena's emerging role is therefore not "become another Mimi." Her aptitude is unusually congruent with her established moral/behavioral direction: preserve life, help living processes continue, and act before regret becomes irreversible.

### 5.3 Same attribute as Mimi's mother — without collapsing identity

Fran reveals that Mimi's mother possessed the same magic attribute, although at a vastly greater level and with the power that culminated in forbidden resurrection. Fran also preserves the taboo/criminal boundary: possessing the same attribute does not morally equate Sheena with Mimi's mother, nor does healing aptitude make resurrection Sheena's destination.

This is a major anti-deterministic constraint for later modeling. Shared capability is not shared policy.

### 5.4 The possibility that Sheena's touch has magical effects remains only partial explanation

Sheena wonders whether the calming or bodily effects associated with embracing Mimi could involve unconscious healing magic. Fran treats magical compatibility as plausible. This should be retained as a mechanism hypothesis, not allowed to erase relational causality.

The series has already established that Mimi seeks Sheena for ordinary social reasons, memories, waiting, food, room life, reassurance, and friendship. Magic may participate in bodily regulation; it does not explain away attachment.

### 5.5 Counterfactual regret becomes non-substitutionary agency

Seiran's death remains central to Sheena's motivation. She remembers having been supported when she was afraid and feels the pain of not having done enough in return. The risk is that Sheena could respond by reproducing Seiran's logic: become stronger so her own body can replace someone else's.

She does not.

Her new formulation is closer to:

`someone was vulnerable + I may have had an unrealized capacity to help -> I do not want future preventable regret -> build skill now`.

She acknowledges that she may not yet be able to save many people. But "I cannot save everyone" no longer collapses into "there is nothing I can do."

This is a highly diagnostic simulation transition. Sheena is developing **bounded efficacy**: action calibrated to real capacity, without requiring total control before action feels meaningful.

### 5.6 Mimi panics at Sheena's unexplained absence

The chapter then turns the same death experience into a relationship test. Mimi wakes and cannot find Sheena. She searches for her and protests that Sheena must not disappear without telling her or leave her behind (`p_0087-p_0096`).

This materially strengthens the V03 prediction that Seiran's failed return would make absence more psychologically consequential. But again, the effect is not primarily tactical. It is attachment regulation:

`recent witnessed irreversible disappearance -> expected close person unexpectedly absent -> rapid search + protest + proximity-seeking on reunion`.

Sheena replies that she also hates the idea of being left by Mimi. The absence threat is explicitly mutual.

### 5.7 `私 もう逃げないよ`

The chapter closes with Sheena's commitment not to keep fleeing from what she can do. The line should not be inflated into a global personality inversion. Sheena remains cautious, reflective, and vulnerable to fear. What has changed is the relationship between fear and agency: fear no longer automatically has veto power over chosen care.

## 6. Drawn bonus — `ゆめ`

The eight-page `ゆめ` is narrative evidence rather than decorative bonus material (`V04 / BONUS / p_0099-p_0106`).

It revisits Seiran's unresolved question to Mimi: **what does Mimi fight for?** The question appears in dream/memory form after Seiran's death.

The important result is negative evidence. Mimi does **not** give a clear verbal answer. She wakes beside Sheena and moves into physical closeness. The juxtaposition strongly keeps Sheena, ordinary intimacy, and the unresolved motive adjacent to one another, but adjacency is not enough to promote the claim:

> "Mimi fights for Sheena."

That formulation remains **OPEN** at V04. The architecture's hardening rule matters here: a simulator should preserve uncertainty instead of filling a thematically obvious blank with a neat motive statement the source has not yet supplied.

The V03 prediction that Mimi might produce a more personally authored answer is therefore **INDETERMINATE**, not supported.

## 7. CH18 — `まだ知らない`

### 7.1 Ordinary life resumes without restoring the missing person

CH18 returns to lunches, classes, leisure, and bright ordinary space. The narration explicitly frames the class as re-entering routine without Seiran being restored (`V04 / CH18 / p_0107-p_0114`).

This is not forgetting. The formal move is one of KimiShinu's central techniques: continuity of life and permanence of loss are represented simultaneously.

For Sheena, ordinary life now contains a new chosen practice. She studies and trains healing magic. She notes that magic practice, once associated primarily with fear and failure, can now be enjoyable because it reveals what she is capable of doing.

This is a strong state update:

`training = imposed preparation for violence`

is no longer the only available interpretation. A self-chosen healing track lets Sheena experience magic as competence directed toward preservation.

### 7.2 Mimi's post-loss clinginess and Sheena's first boundary negotiation

Mimi becomes more physically clingy and tries to insert herself into Sheena's practice. Sheena interprets the increased proximity sympathetically after Seiran's death but also needs time and attention for her chosen work.

This is important because healthy relationship simulation cannot model intimacy as limitless access. V04 supplies the first meaningful **care-versus-autonomy boundary** for the main pair:

`Mimi seeks reassurance/proximity -> Sheena understands the need -> Sheena still preserves a separate task/goal`.

The relationship is strengthening without erasing individual projects.

### 7.3 Haru — bodily limitation and compensatory self-demand

CH18 introduces **Haru** as a classmate frequently absent or in the infirmary because of recurring illness/fever/cough. She cannot participate in practical training consistently and studies intensely to avoid falling behind (`p_0115-p_0128`).

Haru adds a third model of usefulness under institutional vulnerability:

- **Seiran:** vulnerability/debt -> seek more battlefield burden;
- **Sheena:** grief/vulnerability -> build healing capacity;
- **Haru:** bodily limitation -> compensate through academic self-demand.

At V04, this is an initial character model only. It would be premature to diagnose the long-term emotional meaning of Haru's overwork or willingness to receive help.

### 7.4 Sheena's field of concern widens

The chapter ends with the image/narration of an unknown flower budding inside familiar daily life. Literally, Haru is becoming a newly noticed person in Sheena's ordinary world. Structurally, the flower language also marks a widening of Sheena's attention.

Her healing project can no longer be understood solely as "learn how to help Mimi." She is becoming attentive to vulnerability as a broader social field.

## 8. CH19 — `忘れない日`

### 8.1 Domestic routine after bereavement

CH19 opens with room cleaning, laundry, hair, and ordinary bodily/domestic care (`V04 / CH19 / p_0133-p_0140`). Mimi enjoys having Sheena available rather than occupied by practice. The domestic material is doing the same work it has done since V01, but with greater relational density: ordinary routine is now a shared life that both characters actively value.

### 8.2 Haru's uniform and Mimi's concrete social nonjudgment

When Haru anticipates being judged for wearing the boys' uniform, Mimi answers in her established direct style: it suits Haru; Haru chose it; therefore Mimi sees nothing strange about it (`p_0140-p_0147`).

The source does **not** yet justify assigning a modern identity label to Haru from clothing preference alone. What it clearly supports is:

- Haru expects social judgment;
- the uniform is personally chosen/meaningful;
- Mimi evaluates the choice through person-specific fit and preference rather than an abstract gender rule.

Sheena explicitly notices the broader trait: Mimi sees things directly and says what she thinks in her own words. Around Mimi, concerns structured by anticipated social judgment can lose some of their force.

This is unusually useful voice-model evidence. Mimi's simplicity is not merely cognitive limitation. In some social contexts it acts as a **de-normativizing strength** because she does not automatically import the category rules other people expect her to enforce.

### 8.3 The hidden archive does not yet reopen the macro-politics gate

The group discovers older teacher records/notebooks and a concealed/dark archive space (`p_0148-p_0155`). This is potentially important future evidence infrastructure.

At the V04 boundary, however, discovery is not content. The volume does not yet provide recurring, stable information about war origins, governments, diplomacy, enemy ideology, political factions, or strategic national aims from these records.

**Macro-politics gate: remains CLOSED.**

A later volume may reopen it if the archive actually yields sufficient causal political content.

### 8.4 Minor injury, major Mimi response

Inside the archive Sheena suffers only a small scrape. Mimi immediately tries to keep her from standing and moves toward using restoration magic (`p_0154-p_0159`). Sheena has to stop the response because the injury does not warrant that level of intervention.

This is a clean re-test of `M-POL-01` after Seiran's death:

`Sheena visibly hurt -> rapid proximity/intervention -> low calibration delay -> potentially disproportionate care`.

Seiran's death has increased injury/absence salience but has **not** turned Mimi into a careful risk-calibration engine. Her first move remains embodied and immediate.

### 8.5 Mimi rejects the one-way safe-return ethic

The chapter's most important relationship exchange follows. Sheena explains that she is trying to become useful/supportive and does not want to make Mimi anxious through unnecessary injury. Mimi identifies an asymmetry Sheena has not fully confronted:

Sheena repeatedly asks Mimi not to get hurt. Why should Mimi not be allowed to ask the same of Sheena?

`ミミはシーナに言えないの？`

The question changes the dyadic moral structure.

Up to this point, Sheena's concern has often been framed against Mimi's exceptional body: Mimi is immortal, highly repairable, heavily deployed, and therefore at special risk of being treated as disposable. V04 now forces the inverse recognition. **Mimi's immortality does not make Sheena's mortal body a permissible expendable substitute either.**

Sheena accepts the reciprocity.

The current relationship policy becomes:

`I do not want you hurt because you matter to me <-> you have equal standing to say my hurt matters to you`.

This is the clearest main-pair anti-substitution principle yet.

### 8.6 Healing and embrace — capability joins intimacy without replacing it

Sheena is able to use her emerging healing capacity around the minor injury/discomfort, and the scene closes in physical closeness. The magic should be modeled as **one mechanism of care**, not the explanation for the relationship. The emotional reciprocity precedes and exceeds the technical effect.

## 9. Ari–Seiran after death — autonomy, grief, and the cost of support

V04 does not simplify the Ari–Seiran story into a moral lesson that Ari should have forbidden Seiran to fight.

Three claims coexist:

1. **Seiran chose service for reasons that were psychologically real to her.** Institutional rescue/debt and protecting Ari both mattered.
2. **Her protective self-instrumentalization materially contributed to her death.** V04 reveals that she gave away all medicine to save another student.
3. **Ari's love does not become ownership after death.** She refuses resurrection even though she wants Seiran back.

This produces a mature autonomy conflict:

`respect chosen agency` does not equal `approve every consequence`, and

`grieve/regret` does not equal `gain retrospective ownership over the dead person's life`.

Ari's V03 suppressed-veto conflict is therefore **STRENGTHENED**, not replaced by a new anti-war identity.

## 10. Sheena's emerging vocation — support without substitution

V04 is the point at which Sheena acquires the beginnings of an affirmative role.

Earlier volumes defined her largely through what she could not normalize: death, killing, instrumental use, and the idea that repairability makes harm irrelevant. V02 let her articulate personhood; V03 showed the limitations of care when return fails. V04 gives her an answer to the question "what can I do that does not simply reproduce the system's logic?"

The answer is not total escape. It is **healing**.

This should remain calibrated:

- Sheena is still a child inside the institution.
- Healing does not give her power over deployment policy.
- She cannot save everyone.
- Her new skill may itself become institutionally useful in ways she does not control.

But psychologically the shift is substantial:

`fear + moral concern -> paralysis/rumination` is no longer the only pathway.

A new pathway is visible:

`loss/vulnerability -> counterfactual regret -> identify bounded capability -> train -> intervene where possible -> preserve non-disposability`.

This is one of the strongest simulation-hardening transitions in the corpus so far.

## 11. Mimi after first bereavement — repair logic meets irreversibility

V04 also forces a significant correction to any model that treats Mimi as emotionally "older than her body" simply because she has years of combat experience.

Her military competence does not supply ordinary bereavement competence. When Seiran dies, Mimi initially approaches loss through the logic she knows best:

- damaged things can be repaired;
- she herself was resurrected;
- therefore why must this person's state remain irreversible?

The failure of that logic produces confusion rather than philosophical acceptance.

Once the feeling is named, her behavior moves toward proximity, searching, hugging, and greater sensitivity to absence. The consequence is not a sudden adult grief discourse. It is a childlike attachment system under newly understood threat.

For future simulation, this is a major negative constraint:

> Do not generate Mimi as a detached immortal who has long ago mastered bereavement merely because she has existed for many years.

V02 already separated chronological duration, child body, military experience, and ordinary social experience. V04 validates that separation strongly.

## 12. Haru — initial state only

Haru enters too late in the volume for a mature behavioral model, but enough evidence exists to establish a cautious initial state:

- recurrent physical illness/fever/cough;
- frequent practical-training absence / infirmary presence;
- strong compensatory studying to remain academically aligned with peers;
- anticipatory defensiveness around a personally chosen boys' uniform;
- responsiveness to Mimi's straightforward nonjudgment;
- current relationship with Sheena/Mimi: newly opening rather than established intimacy.

The key open question is whether Haru's self-demand functions primarily as pride, fear of exclusion, fear of dependency, desire for ordinary belonging, or some combination. V04 does not settle it.

## 13. Institutional / war-consciousness findings

### 13.1 Death procedure remains stronger than individualized institutional mourning

Seiran's death is announced and routine continues. Peers perform the emotionally particular work: clearing belongings, memorializing, crying, returning objects. The institution remains organized for continuity through attrition.

### 13.2 Resurrection is both taboo and information-controlled

Mimi's origin remains a state-relevant secret. V04 demonstrates that grief can override Mimi's motivation to preserve secrecy, but it does not establish that secrecy has been institutionally revoked.

### 13.3 Agency differentiates by available domain

V04 gives three highly diagnostic forms of agency:

- **Ari:** refuses resurrection; exercises autonomy over what *she* will not do to Seiran after death.
- **Sheena:** chooses healing practice; expands capacity inside the institution rather than controlling the institution.
- **Mimi:** violates secrecy norms relationally; claims reciprocal care rights, but remains structurally deployed/managed.

The girls' agency is real but domain-limited.

### 13.4 Healing may become a new institutional utility — OPEN

Sheena's rare aptitude is potentially valuable to the school. V04 does not yet show how authorities will classify or deploy that value. This should remain an explicit prospective risk: an anti-disposability skill can itself become instrumentalized.

### 13.5 Hidden archive — evidence gate, not conclusion

Old records/teacher notebooks are found. No macro-political conclusion follows yet.

**Macro-politics gate at V04: CLOSED / insufficient density.**

## 14. Mortality / expendability / personhood findings

### 14.1 Love does not imply resurrection entitlement

Ari's refusal produces the strongest source-grounded formulation to date that grief and love do not confer ownership over another person's existence.

### 14.2 Immortality increases exposure to bereavement rather than canceling mortality

Mimi cannot die normally, but everyone around her can. V04 foregrounds immortality as accumulated-loss risk.

### 14.3 Self-sacrifice remains morally mixed

Seiran's medicine transfer saves a student. It also likely removes the resource she needed to survive. The correct analytical state is neither "meaningless sacrifice" nor "heroism proves the choice was right." It is a life-saving act embedded in a self-instrumentalizing policy with lethal cost.

### 14.4 Finite care becomes an alternative to eternal control

Sheena does not promise forever. She promises presence during the time she actually possesses. The limitation is part of the ethic, not a defect in it.

### 14.5 Reciprocal non-disposability

CH19 makes the main pair's anti-instrumental ethic symmetrical. Mimi can demand that Sheena protect her own body too. This is a major advance over a model in which only the unusually weaponized/immortal partner receives protection.

### 14.6 Magic categories must remain distinct

At V04:

- `修復魔法` — restoration/reconstruction of damage;
- `治癒魔法` — healing/living recovery attribute associated with Sheena and Mimi's mother;

Do not collapse these into a generic "healing magic" bucket in later retrieval or simulation.

## 20. V04 freeze

V04 is frozen at the V04 prospective boundary.

The most important transition is not simply that Sheena discovers healing magic. It is a reorganization of what protection means across the cast:

`Seiran dies after giving away her own survival resource -> Ari refuses to reclaim her through ownership of the dead -> Mimi discovers that irreversibility hurts in a way repair logic cannot solve -> Sheena accepts the finite horizon of her own life -> healing becomes a chosen way to act before regret -> absence becomes a mutual attachment threat -> Haru widens vulnerability beyond the central dyad -> Mimi rejects one-way bodily concern -> Sheena and Mimi establish reciprocal non-disposability`.

The V05 question is therefore no longer only "can Sheena protect Mimi?" It is whether Sheena's new healing capacity can remain person-preserving when it meets people whose bodies, preferences, and available choices differ from Mimi's — and whether Mimi's increasingly costly attachment to Sheena changes behavior without being simplified into generic caution, possessiveness, or romance-script inevitability.
