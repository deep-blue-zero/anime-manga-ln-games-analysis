## 2. Volume-level thesis — the future acquires a body

V07 solved the relationship-label problem. Sheena and Mimi mutually chose `恋人`, and the source made clear that becoming lovers adds explicit primacy without replacing friendship, roommate life, ordinary school rhythm, or either girl's established personality.

V08 asks the harder question that naturally follows: **what does a future together mean when the two people do not stand in the same relation to growth, adulthood, injury, time, or memory?**

The answer is not given through one tragic revelation. V08 builds it through ordinary future-language until the physical asymmetry becomes impossible to ignore.

1. **The new couple becomes socially real.** Mimi announces the relationship with characteristic exuberance. Sheena is embarrassed by the publicity but not ashamed of the relationship. She carefully tells Ari because Ari's own lover is dead, and Mimi accepts Sheena's judgment about timing and disclosure.
2. **The relationship enters future vocabulary.** Classmates introduce scripts such as dating and marriage. Sheena and Mimi test those concepts through a picnic, kissing, sleeping beside one another, and playful speculation. The borrowed adult vocabulary gradually becomes their own question about whether they can continue together.
3. **Adulthood becomes a general character problem.** Haru, whose weak body and comparative self-ranking had remained only partly tested, says she wants to become able to live by her own strength and eventually reaches the simpler desire `大人になってみたい`. Sheena names a concrete adult vocation: work in medical facilities, receive people wounded by war, and support as many as she can.
4. **Mimi's bodily future is explicitly asymmetric.** Fran states that Mimi's facial features and height do not change. A `成長薬` can temporarily alter her appearance to look older, but it does not make her actually grow; once the drug leaves her system she returns to her original form.
5. **The couple refuses to make matching bodies a condition of continuity.** Mimi initially tries to solve the asymmetry cosmetically because she wants to become a lover Sheena can proudly show off and later imagines repeatedly using the drug as Sheena advances through higher classes. Sheena answers at the deeper level: Mimi does not need to keep manufacturing age-matched appearance for Sheena to remain beside her.
6. **Self-definition becomes a parallel form of adulthood.** `ゆらめき` turns Haru's `ボク`, masculine uniform, childhood clothing conflict, uncertain future attraction, and frustration with imposed categories into explicit character evidence. Haru does not yet know whether a future loved person would be a boy or girl and does not yet possess a settled identity label. The story's rule is that other people do not get to decide it for Haru.
7. **Memory reclassifies the past without erasing its original ambiguity.** In `おぼえてる`, Sheena and Mimi disagree about which kiss counts as their first. Mimi remembers the transfer-day healing kiss as something she herself wanted to give Sheena even though she also intended it as magic. This changes the mature interpretation of Mimi's personal motive while preserving the fact that an early reader could not yet know that motive.

The governing V08 transition is therefore:

`mutual lover status -> social/public couple -> disclosure with grief sensitivity -> borrowed adult relationship scripts -> concrete future desires -> Haru chooses adulthood/self-support -> Sheena chooses medical service -> Mimi's physical non-aging becomes explicit -> cosmetic growth fails to solve temporal asymmetry -> togetherness detached from matching bodily age -> Haru claims self-definition under uncertainty -> remembered past gains new motive without retroactive knowledge erasure`.

V08 is thus not simply a “dating volume.” Its central object is **futurehood**: who can imagine one, what kind of adult one can become, how institutions manage bodies that do not age normally, what it means to choose oneself before knowing the final answer, and whether a relationship can remain real when time will visibly mark only one partner.

## 3. CH35 — `これから`: a private relationship enters a social world

### 3.1 Mimi's first post-confession mode is exuberant continuity, not personality replacement

The first ordinary-school test of V07's new lover status is almost deliberately anti-transformative. Mimi does not become smoother, more mature, or more socially restrained because she now has a recognized romantic label. She becomes *more obviously Mimi*.

She announces:

`今日からはっ シーナの恋人のミミなの！`

The third-person `ミミ`, the immediate excitement, the desire to make the new status visible, and the lack of strategic concern about embarrassment all preserve her established speech and behavior grammar (`V08 / CH35 / p_0005-p_0009`).

Haru responds with familiar dry bluntness. Sheena blushes and is embarrassed. No one has been replaced by a generic “dating version” of herself.

This is strong support for prospective test #1.

### 3.2 Publicity embarrassment is not relationship shame

Sheena's discomfort needs precise classification. She does **not** regret becoming Mimi's lover and does not ask Mimi to hide the relationship because same-sex romance is forbidden or because she is ashamed of Mimi. The problem is Mimi's unilateral conversion of a deeply processed private transition into morning-classroom news.

Sheena's later handling of Ari makes the distinction clearer. She wants to tell Ari herself and asks Mimi not to announce it first. The reason is relational:

`もう アリの隣には セイランがいないから`

Ari no longer has Seiran beside her. Sheena does not know how hearing about a new happy couple will land inside that bereavement, so she wants to choose timing and wording carefully (`p_0010-p_0013`).

This creates a useful Sheena policy:

`important personal truth -> consider the recipient's emotional position -> disclose honestly but not mechanically -> preserve the other person's right to receive it at an appropriate distance`.

That is consistent with V07's semantic caution. Sheena does not treat truth as less important; she treats communication as person-specific.

### 3.3 Mimi accepts disclosure boundaries even when she prefers openness

Mimi asks whether Ari must be kept in the dark. She herself wants Ari to know. But after Sheena explains why she wants to handle the disclosure, Mimi says in effect that she will leave it to Sheena and will not tell Ari first.

This is a valuable post-V07 exclusivity/agency test. Mimi's status as lover does not produce a claim that she controls the couple's disclosure policy. She can have a preference, hear Sheena's reason, and relinquish immediate control.

Prospective test #2 therefore receives positive evidence before any stronger jealousy crisis even appears.

### 3.4 Ari already knows — and gives the couple social legitimacy without collapsing grief

When Sheena formally tells Ari:

`実は私ずっと… ミミのことが好きで それで昨日からミミと おつき合いすることになったの`

Ari laughs with relief. She says she had already noticed from watching them and congratulates Sheena (`p_0019-p_0021`). She is glad they told her.

The scene avoids two weak trajectories:

- Ari does not become a jealous guardian of Seiran's memory who resents new love.
- Ari also does not respond with emotionally empty “moving on” cheerfulness.

Her grief remains part of why Sheena approached carefully; her capacity to welcome the relationship coexists with that grief.

### 3.5 Major retrospective clarification: Ari and Seiran were actually dating

V08 supplies a relationship fact that earlier prospective readings could not securely establish.

Ari tells Sheena:

`私とセイランは 結局あなたに言わなかったわよね つき合ってるって`

Sheena says she had noticed anyway (`p_0022`). Ari explains that she and Seiran had discussed when to tell Sheena, but because Sheena did not pry into their relationship and allowed the three-person friendship to remain comfortable, they kept postponing the explicit disclosure.

**Current mature claim:** Ari and Seiran were a romantic couple / were dating before Seiran's death.

**Prospective-history rule:** do **not** rewrite V01-V07 readings as though this fact had already been textually available. Earlier analyses correctly treated romantic classification with increasing confidence but incomplete explicit mutual status. V08 retrospectively closes that uncertainty.

This is exactly the kind of later-volume clarification the project's prospective method is designed to preserve rather than flatten.

### 3.6 Ari identifies Sheena's grief-care style

Ari also provides unusually clean third-person evidence for Sheena's interpersonal baseline. After Seiran died, Sheena did not force Ari to talk or decide how grief should proceed. Ari says:

`私が私の日常に戻れるまでは 何も言わずに待っていてくれた`

and describes Sheena as sometimes coming close, sometimes giving distance, sometimes using words and sometimes not. She concludes:

`シーナのそばは 居心地がいい`

(`p_0023-p_0024`).

This is strong character-model evidence because it describes Sheena's support style from the receiver's perspective:

`distress cue -> assess rather than flood -> stay available -> vary proximity/words to person and moment -> wait for self-directed re-entry`.

It helps explain why Sheena's infirmary role fits her personality so well. She is not only magically useful; she can titrate social presence.

### 3.7 The adult conversation returns ordinary romance to militarized context

The chapter closes by moving from student relationship talk to adult concern about reduced deployment frequency and approaching progression/promotion. A quieter combat period allows students to relax into ordinary adolescence, but adults also worry about complacency.

This is classic KimiShinu institutional simultaneity:

`less immediate combat pressure -> more ordinary school/romance life -> adults recognize children relaxing -> institution still prepares for future military demand`.

No new macro-political cause is supplied. War remains the structural horizon around ordinary life.

## 4. CH36 — `約束`: borrowed adult scripts become a finite-future promise

### 4.1 Peer culture supplies `date` and `marriage` before the couple fully owns those concepts

Once the class knows Sheena and Mimi are lovers, peers immediately supply stereotyped couple scripts: dates, kissing, marriage, what lovers are “supposed” to do. The energy is teasing rather than coercive, but the distinction matters.

V07 established that Sheena does not like using a relational category until she understands what it means to her. V08 repeats that mechanism at the next scale. She can now say `恋人`, but `結婚` initially arrives from outside as an adult-life word attached to romance.

The chapter's work is to move that word from peer script toward a concrete question about shared continuation.

### 4.2 The picnic is not a separate “romantic mode”; it is ordinary life intensified

Sheena and Mimi's date consists of food, a blanket, flowers, tree shade, play, touch, kisses, and talking. This is not a new high-glamour romance grammar. It is an extension of the domestic/school ordinary-life grammar that has always carried the relationship.

Mimi's comic insistence that she has been “Sheena-only” much longer than Sheena has understood her own feelings remains characteristically concrete. Sheena wonders whether Mimi had been carrying this kind of feeling earlier than she herself could recognize it (`p_0055`).

The relationship therefore keeps retrospective curiosity separate from historical certainty.

### 4.3 Shared feeling matters more than synchronized interpretation

At the visual center of the picnic, Mimi lifts or pulls Sheena into close physical play while petals/empty space isolate them from the environment. Sheena thinks:

`いま ミミと 同じ気持ちならいいな`

(`p_0056`).

That line is an important correction to over-intellectualizing Sheena. She does not always need a perfectly symmetrical verbal theory. At high trust, she can accept the simpler hope that their present affect points in the same direction.

### 4.4 `結婚` creates the adulthood problem

Back in bed, Sheena's thoughts move through “lover-like things,” dates, and marriage. The shift becomes serious when she reaches:

`まずは 大人にならなきゃね`

Then the mortality/time asymmetry enters:

`もう 何をしても ミミは 大人になれないのかな`

and the unfinished wish:

`二人で 一緒に……`

Sheena calls herself persistent / unable to give up (`……諦めが悪いなぁ 私`) (`p_0057-p_0058`).

At this point V08 has not yet explained the body's rule in detail, so the chapter should be read as a question rather than a settled fact. But the question changes the meaning of `結婚`: it is not simply a cute fantasy of an adult ceremony. It becomes a test of whether two people can enter the same social future when only one may physically mature.

### 4.5 The chapter ends in present intimacy, not an impossible answer

Sheena and Mimi kiss and go to sleep. V08 does not solve the future by promising supernatural equality. It returns them to the present relationship while leaving the adulthood asymmetry open for the next chapters.

That is consistent with the series' finite-presence ethic:

`future problem cannot yet be solved -> do not treat present relationship as meaningless -> continue ordinary intimacy while keeping the problem visible`.

## 5. CH37 — `夢をみる`: adulthood becomes an authored desire

### 5.1 Haru's chronic weakness becomes behaviorally relevant

The V06-V07 Haru model had two partially separated mechanisms:

- comparison/inferiority sensitivity;
- chronic-illness help/autonomy.

CH37 finally activates them in a future-oriented context. Haru is not merely “the sickly student.” She asks what kind of life is materially available to a person whose body is weak and whose abilities do not feel exceptional.

She describes the problem without self-pitying melodrama:

`体も弱いし 優秀な人間でもないから`

and concludes that the range of work she could plausibly do feels limited (`p_0070`).

The comparative frame remains present: bodily and academic differences become evidence in Haru's internal ranking of viable futures.

### 5.2 Haru's autonomy goal is concrete self-maintenance, not social isolation

Haru says she wants:

`一人で 生きていけるように なりたい`

(`p_0071`).

The phrase can sound like a global rejection of dependence if abstracted from context. The surrounding dialogue is more precise. Haru is talking about being able to obtain the means of living, perform a role, maintain her body and material needs, and not have her future wholly decided by physical weakness or another person's protection.

Friends suggest possibilities rather than receiving a categorical rejection. Mimi, for example, points out that some work can be done indoors or from home. Haru jokes back rather than treating the suggestion itself as an insult.

The updated model is therefore:

`weak body narrows perceived options -> comparison/self-ranking rises -> seek a role that permits self-support -> accept information/help if it expands choice -> resist having another person define the entire future`.

This is not “Haru refuses help.” It is **autonomy as authorship**.

### 5.3 `大人になってみたい` — adulthood itself becomes the dream

When pressed about what she actually wants, Haru reaches a simpler and more emotionally revealing formulation:

`大人に なってみたい`

(`p_0074`).

The modesty of the line is precisely why it matters. In a school where children are trained for lethal deployment and not all of them will live to graduate, “I want to try becoming an adult” is not banal developmental language.

For Haru specifically, adulthood contains:

- surviving long enough to reach it;
- inhabiting a body whose weakness does not automatically end possibility;
- finding work she can actually perform;
- choosing how she lives;
- learning who she is rather than accepting somebody else's classification.

The later side story will make the final point explicit.

### 5.4 Sheena now has a concrete adult vocation

Haru's question turns toward Sheena. Unlike earlier volumes, Sheena can answer with more than “be useful somehow.” She says:

`私 将来は 各地の医療施設で 働きたいなって`

She wants to work in medical facilities in different places, receive people injured by war, and become support for as many people as she can (`p_0077`).

This is a major Sheena character-state transition.

Her healer identity has now moved through four levels:

`ethical aversion to harm -> bounded skill practice -> actual infirmary usefulness -> self-authored adult vocation`.

The vocation remains recognizably Sheena. She is not imagining herself becoming the strongest combatant or commanding others. She wants to meet injured people on the receiving side of violence and reduce what can still be reduced.

### 5.5 The vocation also protects relational proximity without reducing to it

Mimi recognizes another implication. Sheena says that if she can work across medical facilities:

`そうすれば ミミがどこで戦ってても そばにいられるから`

(`p_0078`).

This is important because the motive is dual rather than pure.

Sheena wants to support many wounded people **and** remain near Mimi wherever Mimi is sent. Love does not erase public service; public service does not require pretending Mimi is irrelevant.

A good simulation must permit mixed motives without forcing a cynical hierarchy between them.

### 5.6 The institution prepares a cosmetic solution to Mimi's non-aging

Fran introduces a so-called `成長薬`. The wording initially suggests real maturation, but her explanation is precise.

Mimi's face and height otherwise show no change as she advances through classes. That creates a secrecy problem because Mimi carries a state-class secret. The potion is therefore a concealment technology: it temporarily changes facial impression and body size so that she can **look** as if she is growing.

Fran explicitly distinguishes appearance from actual growth:

`つまり「成長」に見えるように 外見を変化させるだけ…`

and:

`実際に成長するわけじゃないし 薬が抜ければ すぐ元の姿に戻るわ`

(`p_0082-p_0084`).

This closes an important immortality rule that V06-V07 had left underdetermined.

**FACT at V08:** Mimi does not naturally show ordinary facial/height maturation; the potion creates temporary age camouflage rather than biological growth.

### 5.7 State secrecy reaches into the visible body

The potion also deepens institutional analysis. The state-secret regime is not limited to hiding that Mimi resurrects. If she remains in ordinary school progression while visibly not aging, the institution anticipates suspicion and alters her appearance to keep the secret manageable.

This is a stronger form of bodily governance:

`anomalous child body -> classified strategic information -> normal social progression risks exposing secret -> institution prepares cosmetic body management`.

It deepens secrecy/policy infrastructure, but it still does not provide enough recurring macro-political material to justify a separate political-warfare ledger.

## 6. CH38 — `届かないの？`: age camouflage cannot solve temporal asymmetry

### 6.1 Mimi takes the growth drug outside the intended plan

CH38 immediately turns Fran's institutional tool into a relationship problem. Mimi takes the potion and appears physically older/taller. The transformation is visually convincing enough to startle Sheena.

The key distinction remains explicit: this is not a new adult Mimi generated by actual time. It is the same Mimi under temporary exterior alteration.

The chapter uses Sheena's attraction to that appearance without making attraction itself the moral conclusion.

### 6.2 Sheena is genuinely attracted to older-looking Mimi

Sheena becomes visibly flustered by the transformed appearance. Height, face-to-face distance, clothing, the mirror, and bodily proportion all make a familiar person feel newly perceptible.

Later she admits:

`私も 成長したミミに ずっとドキドキしてたよ`

(`p_0114`).

This is useful because Sheena does not need to deny the pleasure of seeing a possible older-looking Mimi in order to reject the idea that Mimi must keep changing her appearance to deserve the relationship.

Attraction and acceptance are not opposites.

### 6.3 Visual difference creates a private couple grammar

The temporary height change repeatedly alters familiar interactions:

- who can look down at whose face;
- whether their mouths align naturally;
- whether Sheena can see the crown of Mimi's head;
- how they fit in a hug;
- how “adult” clothing sits on Mimi;
- what they look like in the mirror.

Sheena notices small changes that others do not immediately recognize. The chapter frames this as a specifically intimate mode of perception: relationship familiarity makes tiny bodily differences legible.

When their faces align more closely, Sheena says in embarrassment that their faces are close; Mimi discovers:

`キス しやすいね`

and Sheena agrees it is easier than usual (`p_0111-p_0112`).

The visual gag is tender rather than solutionist. Easier kissing does not mean the temporary body is the “correct” body.

### 6.4 Mimi's motive is not simply vanity

When Sheena asks why Mimi took the medicine, Mimi answers:

`シーナが 自慢したくなるくらい かっこいい恋人に なりたんだもん`

(`p_0113`).

This reveals a new insecurity inside the explicit lover relationship. Mimi wants to be a lover Sheena can feel proud of. She has taken surrounding adult-romance imagery, the idea of becoming older, and the social fact that Sheena will continue to mature, and converted them into a concrete self-improvement problem:

`Sheena will grow / lovers have adult-looking futures -> Mimi's body will not -> change appearance -> become visibly worthy / impressive lover`.

The reasoning is characteristically Mimi-like: concrete, relational, action-oriented, and somewhat impulsive.

### 6.5 Sheena separates “I like this” from “you must become this”

Sheena admits that the older appearance makes her heart race, but she does not make that reaction a requirement. Her reassurance culminates in:

`大好きだよ ミミ`

(`p_0115`).

This is a meaningful post-V07 relationship development. Sheena's love is no longer only a claim about occupying the special relational position; it must now survive visible divergence in age presentation.

### 6.6 Mimi imagines repeated camouflage as Sheena advances

The deepest temporal anxiety appears after they lie together. Mimi asks whether, when Sheena reaches higher classes, Mimi can simply keep taking the medicine:

`シーナが 15クラスになっても 16クラスになっても ミミが あのお薬 いっぱい飲めば それで だいじょうぶなんだよね？`

(`p_0117`).

This is not only about height. Mimi is trying to manufacture synchronized visible time.

The question reveals that couple formation has changed the immortality problem. V06 focused on memory: Mimi may outlive and eventually forget details of finite people. V08 adds **developmental desynchronization**: Sheena's body and social age will move through ordinary time while Mimi's body remains fixed unless artificially disguised.

### 6.7 Sheena rejects age matching as the condition of staying

Sheena does not tell Mimi that the problem is imaginary. She does not claim their futures will be physically symmetrical. Instead she removes matching appearance from the condition of attachment:

`でも苦いの いやだったら 飲まなくても いいんだよ`

`なにがあっても 私がついてる`

and notes that Fran is there too (`p_0118`).

The closing narration is sparse and visually separated:

`一緒にいる`

`私が`

`私だけが`

`大人になっても`

The scene therefore lands on a much stronger proposition than “the magic potion can make marriage possible.”

It says:

`asynchronous bodily aging -> anxiety is real -> cosmetic synchronization is available but false/temporary -> relationship commitment need not depend on identical visible development`.

That does not solve death, memory, legality, deployment, or lifespan. It establishes a present relationship policy under one newly explicit asymmetry.

## 7. Side story `ゆらめき` — Haru and the right not to be prematurely defined

### 7.1 Evidence-class note

`ゆらめき` is a substantial in-volume side story, not decorative paratext. It is Haru-centered and materially expands character reconstruction. Because it is separately indexed as `番外編`, claims drawn from it are marked supplemental rather than silently merged into numbered-mainline chronology.

### 7.2 Childhood gender policing is explicit

The story opens on a remembered demand that Haru behave more “like a girl.” Childhood clothing was often determined by what others handed down or considered appropriate. Haru remembers wanting alternatives and being corrected for crossing the imposed line.

The central wound is not reducible to disliking one particular garment. Haru says the painful thing is being sorted by other people through an imposed category:

`性別を 勝手に 決めつけられて 仕分けされること`

and:

`それが ずっと悔しいんだ`

(`ゆらめき p_0119-p_0124`).

### 7.3 `自分で選べるんだ` — presentation becomes self-authorship

Haru cuts/chooses appearance and clothing with the realization:

`自分で 選べるんだ`

The masculine school uniform, short hair, and `ボク` are therefore not random stylistic quirks for simulation. They belong to a durable preference that decisions about presentation should be Haru's own.

This does **not** by itself establish a modern identity label. The source is unusually explicit that Haru has not resolved that question.

### 7.4 Haru does not know the gender of a future romantic partner

Watching Sheena and Mimi after their relationship becomes public prompts Haru to imagine having a romantic relationship personally. The thought reaches an unresolved fork:

`相手は 男子？ 女子？`

`想像 つかないな`

(`p_0125-p_0128`).

This is direct source evidence against prematurely assigning a settled attraction category at V08.

Supported state:

**romantic/sexual target unknown to Haru; active curiosity exists.**

Not supported:

- fixed heterosexual label;
- fixed lesbian label;
- fixed bisexual label;
- fixed asexual/aromantic label;
- any other modern category the text has not supplied.

### 7.5 Haru asks Fran rather than treating uncertainty as shameful silence

Haru eventually asks Fran whether Fran has someone she likes and whether she likes men or women. The conversation is awkward and blunt, consistent with Haru's established diagnostic style.

The important development is that Haru can externalize uncertainty to an adult without surrendering the right to decide.

### 7.6 `自分が何者なのか` remains an open question

Haru's internal language becomes exceptionally explicit:

`自分の成長が 一番優先だ`

but even while prioritizing growth, Haru does not yet know:

- `自分が何者なのか`
- what Haru likes;
- what future relational identity will feel true.

The uncertainty creates impatience:

`自分の正体が わかるんだろう`

`早く 見つけないと`

and Haru recognizes being in a hurry to find an answer (`p_0131-p_0132`).

The story treats uncertainty itself as psychologically costly without presenting premature certainty as the cure.

### 7.7 Fran supplies the key autonomy rule

Fran's response is perhaps the most important line in the side story:

`納得できるまで 悩み続けな`

Haru complains that this sounds like an outsider's answer. Fran agrees:

`他人事よ`

`だって 他人が決めていいことじゃないもの`

(`p_0133`).

This is a direct thematic articulation of Haru's character problem. Other people can observe, advise, offer language, accept, tease, or support. They cannot author the answer for Haru.

Fran then points out that Haru's friends became friends with **the Haru who already exists now** — foul-mouthed, competitive, physically fragile, wearing the boys' uniform, and not yet reducible to a final explanatory category (`p_0133-p_0134`).

### 7.8 Current selfhood does not have to wait for final classification

The story ends by returning from abstraction to ordinary peer contact. Someone notices Haru's boys' uniform and invites Haru along. The repeated governing language is:

`自分で選ぶ`

and the implied/explicit commitment to find the answer personally.

This gives Haru a high-value policy:

`external category imposed -> react strongly against loss of authorship -> inspect own preferences -> tolerate temporary uncertainty badly but increasingly seek self-chosen evidence -> accept friends who relate to current self without requiring final label first`.

### 7.9 Relationship to CH37 adulthood

`ゆらめき` also clarifies why `大人になってみたい` mattered. Haru is not seeking adulthood only as economic self-sufficiency. Adulthood represents enough future time and autonomy to discover a self that has not yet been fully determined by illness, school, family expectations, or gendered sorting.

Survival, employment, presentation, and relational identity therefore converge on one Haru question:

**Can I live long enough and freely enough to find out who I am?**

## 8. Drawn bonus `おぼえてる` — later knowledge can reclassify motive without rewriting prior knowability

### 8.1 Evidence-class note

`おぼえてる` is a post-relationship drawn bonus and directly concerns Sheena-Mimi kiss history. It is high-value supplemental evidence because it explicitly revisits an event whose meaning was intentionally held open in V01.

### 8.2 A peer asks for the first non-magical kiss — and the couple disagrees

A classmate asks:

`魔法じゃない キスって いつしたの？`

Sheena and Mimi answer simultaneously but give different moments.

- Mimi: at night, in Sheena's bed.
- Sheena: in the morning, in an empty classroom.

The disagreement is not memory failure. It is **category disagreement**.

Sheena's answer corresponds to the later kiss she already understood as non-magical/romantic. Mimi reaches back to the transfer-day bedside kiss.

### 8.3 V01's healing kiss gains new evidence about Mimi's motive

Sheena objects that the transfer-day kiss was meant to treat her stomach pain with restorative magic. Mimi agrees that she intended magic, but adds the missing motive:

`ミミが シーナに したくてした キスだもん`

She wanted to give **Sheena** that kiss.

This is a major but carefully bounded retrospective clarification.

At V01, the correct prospective classification was:

`kiss occurs in explicit healing-magic context -> romantic motive OPEN`.

At V08, the mature classification becomes:

`the act had a healing/magical intention AND Mimi now states that she personally wanted to kiss Sheena`.

What changes is knowledge of **Mimi's motive**, not the historical evidence available to the V01 reader.

### 8.4 The method invariant therefore survives in a stronger form

The project must not now rewrite V01 as “obviously a romantic kiss all along.” V08 supplies new testimony that did not exist at the V01 boundary.

Correct claim transition:

**REVISE mature interpretation / PRESERVE frozen V01 classification.**

This is exactly what prospective reading is for.

### 8.5 `ミミはぜーんぶ思い出せるもん` — remembered relationship history becomes explicit

Mimi insists:

`ミミは ぜーんぶ 思い出せるもん`

Sheena answers:

`私だって 忘れてないよ`

(`p_0143`).

They then settle on the transfer-day event as “our first” and connect it to the fact that neither slept normally afterward (`p_0144-p_0145`).

Given V06's long-term autobiographical memory erosion, the title `おぼえてる` and this exchange are structurally significant. Yet the evidence still does **not** support the stronger claim that Mimi has consciously designed diaries, objects, or repeated recall as an anti-forgetting system.

The correct V08 update is:

`relationship history -> actively remembered and jointly narrated in present -> external/internal memory anchors remain salient -> anti-forgetting strategy motive still OPEN`.

## 12. Mortality, expendability, personhood, and time

### 12.1 Immortality now includes developmental stasis

V06 established mnemonic erosion. V08 adds bodily developmental stasis.

Mimi's immortal/reconstructed condition therefore currently carries at least these distinct temporal consequences:

1. catastrophic bodily injury can be repaired / death is nonterminal in unusual ways;
2. ordinary autobiographical memory can erode over long duration;
3. facial/height maturation does not proceed normally;
4. social progression can continue despite fixed body, requiring concealment;
5. relationships with ordinary mortals will produce visible age desynchronization.

Do not collapse these into the vague label “immortal.” They generate different behaviors.

### 12.2 Cosmetic adulthood is not actual adulthood

The growth medicine is an institutional disguise, not a metaphysical solution. It can change how Mimi looks to others and change embodied experience temporarily, but it does not give her an ordinary developmental trajectory.

That distinction protects the project from a false resolution in which V08 simply “fixes” the age problem through magic.

### 12.3 Sheena's future now risks becoming visually solitary even while relationally shared

The closing CH38 image is conceptually sharp: the relationship may continue while only Sheena's body records ordinary adulthood. V08 does not yet show the distant outcome, but it makes the asymmetry an explicit object of consciousness.

### 12.4 Haru's wish for adulthood is mortality evidence

`大人になってみたい` should also be routed here. In a lethal child-military institution, adulthood is not only a developmental stage; it is a survival horizon. Haru's chronic weakness adds another layer: reaching adulthood means outliving both war risk and personal bodily limitation long enough to possess a self-authored life.

### 12.5 Memory is jointly narrated, not merely stored

`おぼえてる` shows memory functioning interpersonally. Sheena and Mimi do not simply possess individual memory traces; they compare them, disagree about categories, negotiate meaning, and settle on a shared relationship history.

This adds **joint narration** to the memory-anchor taxonomy without proving deliberate anti-forgetting strategy.
