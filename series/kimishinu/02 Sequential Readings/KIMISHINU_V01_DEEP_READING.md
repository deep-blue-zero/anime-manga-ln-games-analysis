## 2. Volume-level thesis

Volume 1 does not begin by asking the reader to understand the war. It begins by asking what it means to **remain emotionally responsive inside an institution where war, killing, mutilation, and student death are administratively ordinary**.

The volume's primary contrast is initially Sheena versus the institutional norm. Totsuki Sheena is disturbed by a roommate's death, wonders whether everyone else is really fine, finds the school's normalization of killing emotionally alien, and wants an ordinary day to remain ordinary. The school, by contrast, has routinized death notices, training, deployment, memorialization, replacement roommates, and even the disappearance of bodies after death.

Gagari Mimi first appears to Sheena as the most frightening possible embodiment of that norm: a small child returns from battle covered in blood, asks for food with almost no social transition, is rumored to be an unbeatable secret weapon, and subsequently destroys mock enemies with overwhelming ease. Sheena initially reads Mimi's smile, combat competence, and apparent casualness as evidence of a mentality she does not want to understand.

V01's central correction is that this reading is wrong in a precise way. Mimi is **not** established as pain-blind, fear-blind, or emotionally inhuman. She knows injury hurts. She describes real battlefield trauma in concrete bodily terms. She agrees that it is frightening. She experiences hunger, sleepiness, pleasure, curiosity, attachment, and a childish desire to get back to warm food. Her extraordinary risk tolerance and combat effectiveness therefore cannot be modeled as simple indifference to her own body or to death.

The first movement of the Sheena–Mimi relationship is consequently not “normal girl teaches weapon to become human.” It is **Sheena revising a category error**. She begins by seeing Mimi as an uncanny weapon who smiles while going to kill; she ends by recognizing that Mimi is also a child whose body hurts, who can be scared, who wants comfort, and who responds to Sheena with immediate care. Mimi, meanwhile, attaches to Sheena before Sheena has decided what to make of her, apparently drawn by a “mama-like” smell, food, and then sustained proximity.

The volume therefore establishes a strong initial model:

`institutional normalization of killing` + `Sheena's refusal/inability to normalize` + `Mimi's extreme operational adaptation without proven emotional indifference` + `forced proximity becoming reciprocal care`.

## 3. CH01 — はじめまして

### 3.1 Death enters an ordinary morning

The opening pages deliberately refuse a heroic war entrance. Sheena is first shown in mundane sensory life: breakfast, warm food, untidy hair, flowers, classroom movement. That normality is immediately interrupted by the announcement that a student died in a western district bombardment. The visual and tonal transition matters: death is inserted into a school morning as an administrative fact rather than staged as a climactic battlefield event (`V01 / CH01 / p_0001–p_0005`).

The school response is formulaic. The surviving students are told to honor the dead student's efforts and continue training so they can improve their abilities. Sheena's reaction is already diagnostically different from the surrounding norm. The text does not show her grandstanding or moralizing; it shows quiet incomprehension and grief.

### 3.2 The school defines itself without euphemism

V01 quickly establishes the institution's function. It is simultaneously an orphanage and a training organization for magical weapons used in national war. Students roughly ten to seventeen live in age/group cohorts. Most importantly, the narration explains that the school takes the magic everyone is born with and trains it **so that students can kill people with it** (`V01 / CH01 / p_0005–p_0009`).

This is not yet macro-political analysis. V01 gives no developed war aim, ideology, enemy political system, or state history sufficient to explain why the war exists. The institution is analytically available primarily from the bottom up: what the children are trained to do and how the routine is normalized.

### 3.3 Sheena's first stable state

Sheena's deceased roommate leaves an empty practical and emotional space. She is excused from afternoon lessons and asked to clear the room. Handling the roommate's belongings, especially a stuffed toy, produces delayed grief. Sheena hugs it, cries, gets hungry, eats alone, and tries to restore ordinary routine (`V01 / CH01 / p_0011–p_0020`).

Her internal question is more important than an abstract anti-war statement: **are the others really fine?** She cannot make the transition from “a person was here yesterday” to “the seat/room is now simply available” as quickly as the institution requires.

### 3.4 Mimi's entrance: horror, appetite, proximity

A hooded figure sits beside Sheena. The reveal is deliberately delayed through white space, close framing, and Sheena's gaze: the girl underneath is covered in blood (`V01 / CH01 / p_0021–p_0024`). Rather than explain herself, she asks for Sheena's food—`ちょーだい`—and eats with childlike immediacy (`p_0024–p_0027`).

Sheena initially assumes the blood implies injury. Mimi says she is not injured; the blood is not her own. This forces Sheena to confront the more disturbing possibility that Mimi has just returned from killing others. Fran, the school health figure, then arrives and treats Mimi's failure to report to the infirmary as a recurring behavioral problem (`p_0028–p_0034`).

Fran also states that Mimi has been told not to involve herself with students. Asked why she approached Sheena, Mimi says Sheena smelled like her mother—explicitly distinguishing that attraction from the rice ball itself (`p_0033–p_0035`). The detail matters because Mimi's first attachment cue is not purely instrumental food-seeking.

### 3.5 Visual contamination of ordinary life

After the encounter, Sheena tries to return to her room and routine, but the blood smell remains psychologically present. The manga uses black fields, environmental silence, and the contrast between an empty domestic room and distant bombardment to make the war persist after the scene has ended (`p_0035–p_0037`).

The next day, the class is introduced to a new student. The boots and body language identify her before the face is fully recontextualized: the blood-covered combatant is now wearing the same school uniform. She smiles and introduces herself simply: `ミミですっ よろしく！` (`p_0038–p_0040`).

This is V01's first major ontological collision: **the frightening battlefield object and the cute new classmate are the same child**.

## 4. CH02 — キス

### 4.1 Gagari Mimi becomes a peer by administrative assignment

The school formally identifies her as **ガガリ・ミミ / Gagari Mimi**, age ten. She had only just entered school and had previously remained unaffiliated with an ordinary class. She is now assigned to Class 14 and, because Sheena's room has become vacant, becomes Sheena's roommate (`V01 / CH02 / p_0043–p_0046`).

The reassignment is institutionally mundane but psychologically violent for Sheena: the vacancy created by one girl's death is immediately filled by the blood-covered girl she is already struggling to comprehend.

Mimi recognizes Sheena publicly as `おにぎりの人`, collapsing Sheena's desired anonymity and establishing Mimi's socially direct, low-filter behavior.

### 4.2 Memorial without a body

On the way to the prayer/memorial space, Mimi repeatedly seeks Sheena's attention and physical proximity. Sheena carries the deceased roommate's stuffed toy. The school memorial contains flowers and personal objects rather than a corpse.

V01 explicitly states that students' lives are managed so that their bodies disappear promptly when they die (`p_0048–p_0051`). Whatever the later mechanism or political rationale may be, the immediate institutional effect is clear: death leaves an administratively empty body-space. The remaining students mourn through belongings and ritual.

Sheena places the stuffed toy among the memorial objects. Mimi participates in the goodbye despite never having known the deceased (`p_0051–p_0052`). This is the first small piece of evidence against a model of Mimi as socially indifferent.

### 4.3 Rumor versus evidence

Ari and Seiran emerge as important classmates. Their conversation surfaces the school's rumor structure: there is supposedly a special, overwhelmingly strong student named Mimi, perhaps the school's secret weapon, with stories extending as far as survival after death or ghost-like status (`p_0055–p_0062`).

The deep reading does **not** promote these rumors into facts. At V01's prospective boundary they establish what peers believe or speculate, not the metaphysics of Mimi.

Sheena's reaction to the rumor is revealing. She does not become fascinated by the school's strongest fighter. Her internal resistance is specifically moral/affective: she does not want to understand the feeling of going to kill people with a smile (`p_0063`).

### 4.4 Healing magic and the first kiss

The chapter establishes that restorative magic can involve directly pouring magic into another person. Ari and Seiran are shown in physically intimate healing behavior, and Fran is treated as someone who ordinarily performs restorative treatment (`p_0067–p_0069`). This matters for reading the Sheena–Mimi kiss: a kiss can be a mechanism of magic transfer in this world and therefore cannot, by itself, prove romantic intent.

That does not make the scene relationally neutral. When Sheena is unable to sleep because of stomach pain, Mimi notices, asks whether she is hurting, offers to heal her, and kisses her (`p_0071–p_0074`). The act combines bodily care, extreme boundary directness, and a mechanism the reader has just been taught to associate with healing.

Sheena's closing recognition is that her wish for an ordinary, unchanged routine is already failing. Mimi's presence makes “normal” unstable.

## 5. CH03 — わたしはわたし

### 5.1 Sheena cannot simply metabolize the kiss

Sheena remains self-conscious after the kiss while Mimi appears comparatively untroubled (`V01 / CH03 / p_0077–p_0084`). The asymmetry is important for simulation: Mimi's physical directness is not yet shown to carry the same social or romantic self-consciousness that Sheena experiences.

The chapter also continues classroom instruction about magic and the nation's history, but V01 does not provide enough political substance here to justify a macro-political model. The war remains an institutional given from the children's perspective.

### 5.2 Preparing for the mock battle

The class receives an abrupt mock-battle exercise. Approximately one hundred targets will be prepared, with an expected target of three per student (`p_0083–p_0091`). Students are warned not to become careless merely because it is practice.

Sheena's anxiety is not abstract. She has performed poorly before and has to tell herself to reach even the minimal three-target goal. Mimi treats the exercise as straightforward practice and says she is not frightened in the same anticipatory way.

The dialogue exposes a key distinction that later modeling must preserve:

- Sheena's fear is activated by anticipated violence and bodily danger before combat begins.
- Mimi can acknowledge pain/fear yet does not display the same anticipatory inhibition.

These are different regulatory patterns, not necessarily different capacities to feel.

### 5.3 Combat competence and institutional valuation

Mimi rapidly destroys mock enemies at a scale that astonishes classmates (`p_0091–p_0096`). The visual grammar enlarges her movement, uses dense black enemy forms around a very small body, and makes her speed/efficiency perceptually dominant.

Other students immediately translate this into institutional value: if Mimi is this strong, perhaps others will not have to fight as much. This is one of V01's clearest weaponized-personhood signals. Mimi's personhood and her utility are being discussed at the same time, but the group conversation naturally gravitates toward what her existence could save them from having to do.

Sheena's response is subtler. She wonders whether Mimi has always lived while being wanted in this way—wanted because of what she can do (`p_0095–p_0096`). This is the first evidence that Sheena's concern is shifting from “Mimi frightens me” toward “what has Mimi's life been like as the object of this demand?”

### 5.4 “I am me” as self-positioning

Sheena does not become Mimi. Faced with Mimi's overwhelming competence, she reorients toward her own modest requirement: she has to be herself and do her three (`p_0097–p_0100`). The chapter title therefore works less as a generic self-esteem slogan than as refusal of an impossible comparison.

V01 does not establish Sheena as courageous in the conventional martial sense. It establishes that she can act while afraid and while rejecting the surrounding emotional norm.

## 6. CH04 — ちゃんと痛いよ

### 6.1 The mock battle becomes bodily real

The warning that practice is dangerous is realized immediately. Sheena suffers catastrophic injury, including loss/severance of an arm, and experiences terror rather than heroic composure (`V01 / CH04 / p_0103–p_0108`).

The scene is analytically important because it prevents the training environment from being treated as symbolic schooling. Even simulation can dismember a child. The school's institutional distinction between “practice” and “war” is therefore much thinner at the bodily level than the word suggests.

### 6.2 Mimi responds to Sheena's injury with immediate care

Mimi stays near the injured Sheena and, when dissatisfied with waiting, uses restorative magic directly on her (`p_0107–p_0115`). She kisses Sheena and reconstructs the missing arm. Fran's reaction indicates that Mimi's competence with this restoration is not simply an ordinary lesson Fran expected her to possess.

Mimi then collapses/asleeps from the exertion. This matters for a behavioral model: her response to another person's injury is not passive sympathy. She acts immediately, physically, and at substantial energetic cost.

At V01, this supports a **provisional relationship-conditioned care policy**, not yet a universal rule: when Sheena is visibly hurt and help is available, Mimi strongly favors direct intervention over waiting for ordinary procedure.

### 6.3 Sheena's internal fear is explicit

The chapter finally gives direct language to what Sheena has been carrying. She is scared. She does not want to go to war. She does not want to be injured or die. She cannot understand why everyone seems able to treat this as normal (`p_0115–p_0117`).

This is more precise than calling her “anti-war.” V01 establishes a moral-emotional non-normalization: Sheena experiences the institution's baseline expectations as something her body and mind resist.

### 6.4 Mimi is not pain-blind

In the room afterward, Mimi and Sheena discuss injury. Mimi describes real enemies as using magic stronger than the mock targets. She describes flesh tearing, blood loss, cold, and vision fading (`p_0123–p_0129`). Asked whether that is painful or frightening, Mimi does not deny it. Sheena realizes that Mimi experiences these things too.

This forces the major V01 revision:

**REJECT / REVISE:** “Mimi fights casually because pain and fear do not matter to her.”  
**Current model:** Mimi is capable of pain and fear but has an extraordinarily adapted, compartmentalized, or differently weighted relationship to self-risk.

V01 does not yet tell us why.

### 6.5 Humanization is epistemic, not sentimental

Sheena's change is not that Mimi suddenly becomes nice. Mimi was already friendly, hungry, playful, and caring. The change is that Sheena acquires evidence that lets her interpret those behaviors differently. Mimi stops being an impossible contradiction—cute child plus blood-soaked killer—and becomes a child who can contain both vulnerability and terrifying capability.

## 7. CH05 — 死なない女の子

### 7.1 Sheena admits the original basis of her aversion

Ari and Seiran push Sheena to articulate why she has been uncomfortable with Mimi (`V01 / CH05 / p_0133–p_0141`). Sheena's answer confirms the model built in earlier chapters: when she first met Mimi, the sight of a smiling, blood-covered child returning from war made Mimi feel uncanny and morally frightening.

Sheena had assumed that someone who could go to war smiling must be fundamentally unlike her—someone for whom injury, killing, and death were simply fine. CH04 has now contradicted that assumption. Sheena explicitly recognizes that Mimi is “the same” in the important sense that pain hurts and fear exists.

This does not erase the difference in behavior. It relocates the difference from **capacity to feel** to **how those feelings govern action**.

### 7.2 Deployment attrition remains routine

The school announces the return of a night-combat group. Fourteen had been called; only twelve return (`p_0140–p_0143`). The next day's prayer time follows naturally from that fact.

V01 therefore closes the loop introduced in CH01. Student death is not an exceptional backstory event. It is an ongoing operating condition.

### 7.3 Mimi's grievous return and self-restoration

Mimi returns from battle catastrophically injured. Fran receives her and refuses to perform the recovery for her, telling Mimi to heal herself (`p_0145–p_0153`). Mimi reconstructs her own badly damaged body through restoration magic.

This is the strongest V01 evidence behind the “girl who does not die” title, but the epistemic boundary must remain strict:

- V01 **confirms extraordinary self-restoration from grievous injury**.
- V01 **does not yet directly confirm resurrection after an observed completed death**.
- Peer rumors that Mimi can die and return remain rumors unless later source evidence verifies them.

Mimi's regeneration is depicted as grotesque and bodily rather than clean superhero invulnerability. Tissue, blood, and restoration are visually emphasized. The body can be repaired, but the violence done to it remains perceptually real.

### 7.4 Recklessness has a childlike motive

Fran criticizes Mimi for returning in such a ruined state. Mimi's answer is revealing: she wanted to get back quickly to warm food. This is not sophisticated fatalism or heroic martyrdom. A ten-year-old immediate desire coexists with extreme tolerance for bodily destruction (`p_0153`).

This is a major simulation warning. Mimi's risk behavior should not automatically be generated from ideological sacrifice, self-hatred, or absence of fear. V01 supports a model in which **immediate desire + confidence in restoration + operational habituation can heavily discount bodily cost**.

### 7.5 Sheena's changed behavior

After Mimi returns, Sheena's interaction with her is noticeably softer. Mimi notices the change. Sheena thanks Mimi for healing her arm and begins to engage without the earlier recoil (`p_0154–p_0160`).

The volume does not close on a declaration of love. It closes on a much more foundational transition: Sheena can now look at Mimi as a person she wants to feed, speak with, and understand rather than as an incomprehensible weapon she should avoid.

## 8. Character-state reconstruction

### 8.1 Totsuki Sheena

**Baseline temperament:** quiet, observant, cautious, socially functional but inwardly ruminative. She is not presented as confrontational.  
**Moral/emotional baseline:** unusually resistant to institutional normalization of death and killing.  
**Fear profile:** anticipatory; danger is experienced before action as bodily anxiety and dread.  
**Coping:** seeks routine, food, sleep, room order, and ordinary school continuity; ruminates when those fail.  
**Agency:** externally constrained but internally differentiates her own values from institutional norms.  
**Conflict behavior:** no evidence yet of aggressive interpersonal confrontation; tends toward hesitation, internal processing, and modest self-command.  
**Attachment behavior:** initially avoidant toward Mimi because Mimi is categorized as morally/ontologically threatening; rapidly softens when vulnerability and reciprocity become legible.  
**Self-conception:** does not imagine herself as the strong fighter; “I am me” becomes a way to act without adopting Mimi as the comparison standard.  
**Current V01 state:** fearful of war and death, no longer treating Mimi as fundamentally inhuman, increasingly curious/protective/affectionate toward her.

### 8.2 Gagari Mimi

**Baseline presentation:** exuberant, physically direct, hungry, sleepy, curious, low-filter, childlike.  
**Speech/behavior:** self-reference as `ミミ`; simple direct wants; rapid shifts between appetite, play, sleep, and action.  
**Combat state:** extremely capable and operationally unhesitating.  
**Pain/fear:** explicitly present. V01 rejects a pain-insensitive/fearless model.  
**Risk weighting:** bodily destruction appears heavily discounted relative to immediate goals, likely in part because extraordinary restoration is available; mechanism of that weighting remains open.  
**Care behavior:** notices Sheena's pain, offers help, uses direct restorative magic, and expends herself to reconstruct Sheena's arm.  
**Boundary behavior:** repeatedly ignores ordinary social and institutional boundaries—approaching students when told not to, physical closeness, direct kissing as healing, rushing/returning in poor condition.  
**Attachment cue:** initially says Sheena smells like her mother; food and proximity then reinforce contact.  
**Current V01 state:** newly integrated into ordinary class/room life while still functioning as an extraordinary combat asset.

### 8.3 Fran

Fran functions as a health/caretaker authority with unusually intimate knowledge of Mimi. Fran's register is coarse, teasing, directive, and highly familiar (`アンタ`, commands, sharp reprimands). Mimi's joking categorization of Fran as an `おっさん` should not be converted into an identity claim beyond what V01 establishes.

Fran is simultaneously exasperated and protective. Fran knows Mimi's repeated failure to follow medical procedure, tries to keep her separate from ordinary students, treats her restoration capacity as something that permits but does not excuse recklessness, and trusts/forces Mimi to perform self-repair by CH05.

### 8.4 Ari and Seiran

V01 establishes Ari and Seiran as a close dyad and socially important peers. They provide Sheena with rumor/context, normalize discussion of combat competence, and challenge her to articulate her feelings about Mimi. Their physical intimacy overlaps with restorative-magic mechanics, so V01 alone should not use every kiss/proximity cue as independent proof of romance. Their relationship is nevertheless clearly close and mutually familiar.

## 9. Sheena–Mimi relationship state

### Initial state

`stranger / uncanny threat / involuntary proximity`

Sheena first sees Mimi as blood-soaked evidence of a mentality she cannot understand. Mimi, by contrast, approaches Sheena immediately and positively.

### Transition 1 — forced cohabitation

Mimi becomes Sheena's classmate and replacement roommate. Institutionally forced proximity makes avoidance impossible.

### Transition 2 — care without shared interpretation

Mimi notices Sheena's stomach pain and heals through a kiss. Sheena experiences the act as socially/emotionally consequential; Mimi behaves as though direct physical care is uncomplicated.

### Transition 3 — Sheena sees Mimi as institutionally used

During the mock battle, Sheena notices that classmates value Mimi because her strength might reduce their own burden. She begins asking what it means to be wanted for combat usefulness.

### Transition 4 — reciprocal bodily vulnerability

Mimi reconstructs Sheena's arm. Sheena then learns that Mimi also feels pain and fear. This is the decisive V01 reclassification.

### V01 endpoint

`asymmetric attachment → reciprocal care / growing trust / Sheena's aversion substantially reduced / romantic meaning not yet fully established at this boundary`

The relationship is already unusually intimate, but V01's strongest defensible claim is not mature romance. It is that Mimi's immediate attachment and care have become reciprocated enough for Sheena to seek ordinary closeness rather than distance.

## 10. Mortality, expendability, and personhood

### 10.1 Death as administrative routine

Death notices, room reassignment, memorial ritual, and replacement continue without institutional rupture. Student bodies disappear after death under the school's/state's life-management system. The institution has therefore reduced some logistical signs of death while leaving personal belongings and social absence behind.

### 10.2 Sheena refuses emotional fungibility

The empty bed and stuffed toy matter because Sheena does not experience the deceased roommate as a replaceable slot. Her grief establishes irreplaceability against administrative replacement.

### 10.3 Mimi is valued as a solution

The classmates' reaction to Mimi's combat performance immediately raises the possibility that her strength can spare others. This is understandable from the students' perspective and simultaneously instrumentalizing: Mimi's body becomes a resource for redistributing collective danger.

### 10.4 Restoration complicates but does not erase harm

Both Sheena's restored arm and Mimi's self-reconstruction demonstrate that severe bodily injury is medically/magically reversible to an extraordinary extent. The manga nevertheless refuses to make that reversibility clean. Pain, blood, severance, exhaustion, and fear remain real.

At V01, the correct distinction is:

`repairable body ≠ consequence-free body`.

## 11. Institution, war consciousness, and agency

### Established facts at V01

- The school is both orphanage and magical-weapons training institution.
- Children approximately ten to seventeen are trained to use innate magic for killing.
- Students are summoned for real combat while still living ordinary school routines.
- Death is frequent enough to have standardized notices and prayer/memorial procedure.
- A night deployment can lose multiple students without changing the basic institution rhythm.
- Students' bodies disappear upon death through a managed life-system mechanism.
- Mock training itself can cause catastrophic bodily injury.
- Exceptional students such as Mimi may be socially understood primarily in terms of battlefield utility.

### Not established at V01

- the political origin of the war;
- the enemy state's identity or political ideology;
- the protagonists' state's governmental structure;
- war aims, diplomacy, or strategic history;
- whether the institution's practices are contested politically outside the school.

No LOGH/86-style macro-political inference should be manufactured from the absence of this information.

### Agency model

Sheena has very low structural autonomy but meaningful moral and relational agency. She cannot simply leave the system in V01, but she can resist its emotional categories, choose how to treat Mimi, care for the dead, and define her own response to fear.

Mimi has enormous tactical capability but unclear structural autonomy. Being powerful does not mean she chooses the war. V01 shows her repeatedly summoned, medically managed, and treated as an exceptional asset. Her apparent freedom of movement therefore should not be confused with freedom from institutional use.

## 12. Japanese dialogue and generative voice findings

### 12.1 Sheena

V01 supports a **soft, hesitant, internally elaborative** speech model.

Observed tendencies:
- frequent pauses and ellipsis;
- tentative questions rather than aggressive assertions;
- polite/standard school register with teachers;
- internal self-questioning and comparison with others;
- emotionally important thoughts are often carried in narration/internal monologue rather than spoken directly;
- under anxiety, language narrows toward immediate bodily concern and modest tasks.

Simulation implication: semantic correctness alone will not make Sheena sound right. A highly blunt, declarative, emotionally exhibitionistic Sheena would be low-fidelity unless a later state change supports it.

### 12.2 Mimi

Mimi's V01 voice is already highly diagnostic.

Observed tendencies:
- third-person self-reference as `ミミ`;
- short, direct requests: `ちょーだい`;
- attention-getting repetition such as `ねーねー`;
- elongated, playful delivery and energetic punctuation;
- simple desire statements about food, sleep, curiosity, and immediate action;
- low embarrassment around bodily proximity;
- quick transitions from serious injury context to ordinary child concerns;
- direct emotional and sensory framing rather than extended abstraction.

Negative constraint, V01 provisional: Mimi is unlikely to express her combat relationship in elaborate ideological language. Her risk-taking is currently narrated through concrete wants and habituated action, not political doctrine.

### 12.3 Fran

Fran's speech is highly familiar and directive, mixing care with scolding and teasing. Commands, rough address, and exasperated familiarity dominate. This creates a useful contrast with the formal school instructor and Sheena's cautious register.

### 12.4 Ari / Seiran

Enough evidence exists to distinguish them socially, but not yet enough for high-confidence generative voice specifications. Keep their speech models OPEN pending V02+ recurrence.

## 13. Manga visual grammar

### 13.1 Ordinary life interrupted rather than replaced

Food, beds, classrooms, hair, flowers, and corridors recur around death and deployment. The manga does not visually move from “peace mode” into a separate “war mode.” It makes war contaminate ordinary objects.

### 13.2 Empty space as death evidence

The empty room, empty bed, absent body, and memorial objects repeatedly visualize loss through what is missing. This formal grammar complements the world's body-disappearance mechanism.

### 13.3 Mimi's first reveal

The hood → eye → blood-covered face sequence makes Sheena's first perception of Mimi a controlled horror reveal. Later uniform scenes reuse the same face in cute/comedic framing, forcing the reader through the same category revision Sheena undergoes.

### 13.4 Small body / large destructive field

Mock-battle spreads repeatedly contrast Mimi's small body with large fields of black enemies and explosive magical effect. This creates the “weapon” reading visually before the later chapters complicate it through pain and care.

### 13.5 Restoration is deliberately grotesque

Self-restoration in CH05 is not represented as a clean glow or reset. The page emphasizes blood, missing/damaged tissue, regrowth, and the discomfort of seeing a body rebuild itself. The visual form therefore resists an invulnerability reading even as the plot demonstrates extraordinary recovery.

### 13.6 Black fields and internal alienation

Sheena's deepest war/mortality thoughts frequently sit against black or near-empty fields. The simplification removes school detail precisely when her internal distance from the school's “ordinary” becomes most acute.

## 14. Simulation-model hardening at the V01 boundary

V01 is sufficient to initialize conditional hypotheses but **not** sufficient to promote a large number of stable behavioral policies. The following are evidence-bounded candidates.

### Sheena policy candidate S-POL-01 — death/violence non-normalization

**Trigger/context:** death, anticipated deployment, severe injury, casual discussion of killing.  
**Perceived state:** danger remains morally and bodily salient even when peers treat it as routine.  
**Likely response:** anxiety, rumination, search for ordinary continuity, cautious action rather than enthusiastic participation.  
**Modifier:** direct need to help or survive can override hesitation.  
**Confidence:** medium-high after recurrence across CH01, CH03, CH04, CH05.  
**Open test:** whether repeated exposure produces normalization, stronger refusal, or a different coping strategy.

### Sheena policy candidate S-POL-02 — reclassification through vulnerability

**Trigger/context:** a person initially perceived as threatening displays pain, fear, or care.  
**Likely response:** Sheena revises global negative judgment and becomes more relationally open.  
**Evidence:** Mimi's pain/fear disclosure and healing behavior materially soften Sheena's stance.  
**Confidence:** medium; only one major target relationship so far.

### Mimi policy candidate M-POL-01 — immediate direct care

**Trigger/context:** Sheena is visibly in pain/injured.
