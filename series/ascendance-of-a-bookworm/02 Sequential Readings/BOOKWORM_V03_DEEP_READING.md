## 3. Volume-level analytical synthesis

V03 closes Part 1 by converting several questions that began as private bodily, household, or craft problems into explicit conflicts over **institutional membership, contractual jurisdiction, class access, political scarcity, and self-authored life**.

The production arc reaches a decisive transition. Plant paper no longer depends on Myne and Lutz personally making every sheet. The children teach and specify; adult artisans redesign tools; a workshop is located around water, storage, drying, and adult bodies; Mark coordinates procurement; Benno manages institutional risk; guild and association conflict determines which markets can legally be entered. Innovation has therefore crossed from experiment into **organization**.

The same shift occurs around `身食い`. V02 strongly established the local diagnosis. V03 explains substantially more of the mechanism: the internal heat is mana running out of control, magical tools can absorb that mana, and noble society possesses the tools and training that ordinary commoners do not. This resolves the earlier apparent contradiction between “magic is for nobles” and commoner `身食い`: the observed exclusivity is social and institutional more than biological.

But greater explanatory clarity makes the ethical and agency problem sharper. A commoner with `身食い` can survive by entering a noble relationship that provides magical tools. Frieda's wealth allows her grandfather to negotiate unusually favorable terms, but the arrangement is still a future transfer into noble control. Myne understands the choice and rejects survival if the price is losing her family and the ability to live according to her own purposes.

Her formulation is not best summarized as “choosing death.” She explicitly distinguishes the two: she chooses **being with her family**, while continuing to seek any route that might let her live without surrendering herself to a noble contract.

The temple creates exactly such a route—but only because its own political and mana shortages give Myne bargaining leverage. Her admission as a blue-robed shrine maiden, home commute, health accommodations, library work, and continued workshop are not gifts of class equality. They are negotiated exceptions made possible because an institution that initially attempts to seize a commoner child discovers that it needs what her body contains.

Part 1 therefore closes on a durable principle:

> **Practical freedom is not the same as formal status. It emerges from the interaction of resources, information, leverage, jurisdiction, relationships, and the ability to make refusal costly to the institution demanding compliance.**

## 4. Prologue and emergency intervention: Frieda as parallel and rival

The prologue resolves V02's cliff-edge without retrospective smoothing. The Guild Master urgently summons Frieda after Myne collapses. Frieda opens her strongbox and uses one of the remaining functions of a deteriorating magical tool that had been retained for her own future crises.

This is direct evidence of care: she is spending scarce survival capacity on another child. It is also direct evidence of calculation. Frieda understands debt, commercial value, and her grandfather's desire to pull Myne into the Othmar network. The two motives coexist.

The price information strengthens this interpretation. Frieda knows the tool's actual value to be much lower than the figure her grandfather presents to Benno. Inflating the debt would make repayment harder and increase the chance of economically capturing Myne. The Guild Master's household should therefore not be reduced either to predatory antagonists or altruistic rescuers. V03 repeatedly shows genuine affection operating through commercial habits that can become coercive.

## 5. `身食い`: mechanism, recurrence, and class-mediated survival

Frieda gives Myne the clearest system explanation so far.

The magical tool does not remove the condition. It receives accumulated mana until its own capacity is exhausted. The interval before dangerous accumulation returns is approximate rather than exact; the emergency intervention buys time.

Frieda's case also separates two bodily questions that earlier volumes could not cleanly distinguish. When her mana is controlled, Frieda is not generally fragile in the way Myne is. Myne's chronic weakness therefore cannot responsibly be collapsed into `身食い` alone. The corpus should continue tracking ordinary frailty, deconditioning/illness, and mana crisis as related but non-identical bodily dimensions.

Most commoners with `身食い` lack access to magical tools. Noble contracts become the ordinary survival route because nobles possess both the devices and the social infrastructure required to use them. Wealth does not abolish that hierarchy, but it can improve bargaining position.

Frieda's grandfather has negotiated a comparatively favorable future for her: she remains with her family until adulthood, will enter a noble relationship later, is expected to receive a shop in the noble quarter, and will surrender a portion of profit to the patron. Frieda prepares aggressively for this future because she expects to enter a world where her present family cannot simply accompany her as an equal support network.

Her acquisitiveness and planning are therefore partly adaptive armor.

## 6. Myne's survival choice: family, self-authorship, and refusal

Myne's V03 choice is one of Part 1's clearest value tests.

She has already moved beyond V01's relatively weak attachment to continued life. She now likes her present life, has relationships she values, has made paper, has economic projects, and still wants books. Her rejection of the ordinary noble-contract route is therefore not indifference to living.

When she says that she did not choose death but chose her family, V03 gives the distinction behavioral weight. She informs her family of the condition and options. She seeks alternatives. She negotiates. She accepts the temple only after a route emerges that preserves family residence and a meaningful degree of self-direction.

The strongest current formulation is:

**Myne wants to live, but survival is not lexically superior to belonging and self-authorship.**

Her prior death also reduces the terror of mortality relative to someone who has never died, but V03 does not support the stronger claim that death has become emotionally trivial to her.

## 7. Books versus family: genuine development from Urano

The temple library produces a direct conflict between the series' most stable desire and Myne's developed attachments.

The chained collection is protected by a magical barrier and is accessible through temple affiliation. Myne initially reacts with the intensity expected from Urano's established book fixation and seeks shrine-maiden status primarily to gain library access.

The family objects for concrete reasons: commoner temple service carries stigma, gray-robed labor suggests severe status loss, and the parents fear separation. Tuuli's question forces Myne to test her promise to remain with the family against the library she desperately wants.

The resulting answer is a genuine developmental revision. Myne recognizes that the family of this life has become as important to her as books. In her prior life she believes she would have chosen the books without hesitation. Here, she gives up the immediate library route rather than break the family bond.

The loss is not emotionally cheap. Suppressing the desire destabilizes the internal mana for roughly two days. Development does not mean the old value vanished; it means another value has become capable of constraining it.

## 8. Lutz's merchant path becomes unmistakably self-authored

V02 established family opposition. V03 establishes authorship.

Lutz directly rejects the idea that his merchant goal is merely Myne's influence. He describes it as the path he finally opened for himself. Even if Myne withdrew, became too ill, or told him to stop, he would still want to become a merchant.

His mother, Carla, finally listens to the actual history of that desire. She learns that the earlier dream of becoming a traveling merchant was serious and that the present city-merchant route is an informed reframing rather than a whim invented by Myne.

Lutz can also demonstrate material preparation: savings, willingness to live away from home if necessary, work experience, and a realistic transition plan. He explains why household life matters to the aspiration: elder brothers dominate shared goods and results, and merchant work offers the possibility of achievement he can identify as his own.

Carla becomes an explicit ally even while expecting the father to remain opposed. Her support produces an immediate household consequence when she protects Lutz's food from his brothers.

The durable revision is not simply “family opposition decreases.” It is:

**Lutz's merchant identity has crossed from dependent aspiration to self-authored occupational project, and one parent now recognizes that authorship.**

## 9. Lutz's apprentice life: occupational-class acculturation

The appended Lutz viewpoint is analytically important because success at entering the merchant world reveals new deficits rather than ending the problem.

Merchant-family children arrive with years of tacit exposure to schedules, hygiene expectations, posture, speech, customer interaction, document forms, purchasing behavior, dress, and business conventions. Lutz must learn these explicitly.

This lets him reinterpret Myne's earlier difficulty with local “common sense.” His own body and speech now mark him as an outsider to a social environment that other children absorb at home.

Prior instruction from Myne gives him meaningful advantages in literacy, arithmetic, and study habits. Benno and Mark provide the institutional apprenticeship that Myne cannot. His unique role around Myne remains valuable, but V03 no longer supports explaining his competence solely as “the person who helps Myne.”

Lutz is becoming an independent merchant subject while retaining the relationship as one of his most important specializations.

## 10. Myne ↔ Lutz: from complementary partnership to chosen dyadic responsibility

The relationship now clearly exceeds a collection of useful exchanges.

Across V01-V03 it spans:

- physical pacing and care;
- paper experimentation and production;
- commercial entry;
- shared earnings and rights;
- career support;
- privileged identity disclosure;
- survival intervention;
- body-state recognition;
- protection of each other's projects;
- a deliberate commitment to continue making things together.

V03 tests the relationship under prospective separation. Lutz is unhappy that temple service may mean they cannot simply enter the same ordinary shop life. He clarifies that he wanted to make books **with Myne**, not merely sell products she happens to invent with other people.

Myne answers by identifying Lutz as the first collaborator who remained when the value of her ideas was still unknown. She promises to consult him first about new creations; Lutz promises that he will make the things she conceives.

The new magic contract giving Lutz the right to sell Myne Workshop products adds an institutional layer to the relationship. Their bond is now simultaneously personal, epistemic, productive, commercial, and legally/magically structured.

This is sufficient evidence for an independent future relationship synthesis. It is not sufficient evidence, by itself, to canonize a romantic interpretation. The volume contains playful social framing and an appended narrator's speculative ambiguity, but the current source boundary does not require romance as the explanatory model.

## 11. Myne ↔ Benno: protection, extraction, education, and strategic infrastructure

Benno's mixed role becomes more mature rather than simpler.

He protects Myne from the Guild Master's debt strategy by ensuring enough money is available to repay the emergency intervention. He hides the children's identities during trade conflict. He pays for magical contracts. He trains Myne and Lutz in pricing, bargaining, procedure, and risk. He builds or commissions infrastructure around their products.

He also wants first access to Myne's knowledge and economic upside. His protection is not disinterested charity.

The key analytical correction is to stop treating those motives as mutually exclusive. Benno is increasingly willing to spend resources protecting Myne and Lutz because he values them personally **and** because their knowledge, growth, and continued freedom matter to his own enterprise.

Mark's viewpoint adds important longitudinal context. The children appear to reactivate an older expansive ambition in Benno that had been suppressed by family and business responsibility. Myne and Lutz do not merely supply products; they change what he believes his own commercial future can contain.

## 12. Frieda as comparison case: adaptation under anticipated constraint

Frieda is not simply a foil showing what Myne could become.

She shares `身食い`, but her bodily state, family resources, temperament, and negotiated future differ. She can therefore distinguish mechanism from personality and class circumstance.

Her intense commercial preparation becomes more legible once her future is stated. She expects eventual entry into noble society under an arrangement negotiated around her mana and wealth. The shop promised to her offers continued economic identity; the noble relationship constrains her personal autonomy; her present household cannot straightforwardly follow her into that social world.

Her attempts to acquire Myne's products, information, and friendship can therefore be read as both ordinary merchant competition and an effort to build durable assets before a constrained transition.

V03 provides enough evidence to keep Frieda under active monograph consideration, but not enough multi-state depth yet to require a dedicated character artifact immediately.

## 13. Knowledge transfer: abstraction helps, but tacit expertise remains decisive

The cake episode gives the knowledge-transfer model a useful refinement.

Myne chooses a cake whose ingredient relation is based on equal-weight proportions because a ratio can survive differences between Japanese and local measurement systems more easily than a recipe dependent on exact remembered units.

This is a real cognitive advantage: **abstraction can improve portability**.

It is not sufficient implementation. Ilse supplies professional execution—mixing, whisking, heat management, oven knowledge, testing, and practical adjustment. The source again refuses the fantasy that remembered modern information is a complete technology package.

The later recipe sale strengthens the point. Ilse can reconstruct and extend the recipe because she is a skilled professional. Benno cannot simply convert money into the same result without developing culinary capability and supply access. Knowledge diffusion therefore creates demand for adjacent expertise rather than eliminating expertise.

## 14. Information withholding and externality reasoning

Myne's commercial behavior is no longer simple enthusiasm for diffusion.

She deliberately withholds parue byproduct commercialization because increasing the value of the resource could raise its price and hurt poor households that currently depend on it. This is one of the clearest source-grounded examples so far of Myne reasoning beyond direct personal profit toward second-order distributional effects.

She also withholds some cooking knowledge as a potential future defense in noble-facing environments.

These choices complicate any timeless label such as “Myne wants knowledge to be free.” Her actual policy is conditional:

- she strongly favors diffusion of book-making and useful production;
- she accepts time-bounded exclusivity when it finances goals or protects collaborators;
- she withholds information when diffusion threatens vulnerable users or her own bargaining position.

## 15. Myne Workshop: bodily limitation converted into organizational design

Because ordinary shop apprenticeship is incompatible with Myne's health and would distort workplace fairness, Benno helps create a different structure: `マイン工房`.

The workshop converts the very mismatch that once made Myne call herself useless into an organizational model in which her highest-value work is ideation, planning, specification, teaching, rights allocation, and coordination rather than sustained manual labor.

This is not escape from dependence. The model requires Lutz, Benno, Mark, artisans, household labor, guild permission, and market access. It is instead evidence that institutions can be redesigned around bodily reality rather than treating one standard labor form as the measure of usefulness.

## 16. Scaling paper: from prototype to production system

V03 strongly confirms the production thesis that the body of the inventor is not the system.

The small equipment used by Myne and Lutz cannot simply be enlarged mechanically. Adult workers need larger and differently proportioned vats, screens, and tools. The workshop needs water, drying and storage space, transport, raw material handling, weather planning, and repeatable instruction.

Lutz becomes the process carrier because he has physically executed the prototype steps. Mark becomes the coordinator who can turn needs into artisan orders. Adult craftspeople embody expertise in tool construction and workflow. Benno manages capital and commercial exposure.

By early summer the new shop is capable of successful production.

The project has therefore moved through three distinct technological states:

1. **concept** — plant-fiber paper should be possible;
2. **prototype** — the children can make a viable sheet;
3. **production system** — an organization other than Myne's own hands can reproduce the output.

This distinction should remain longitudinally important.

## 17. Parchment association conflict and product segmentation

The parchment association conflict is the first clear case in which Myne's innovation threatens an incumbent trade structure at institutional scale.

Benno deliberately hides the children while negotiating. Myne can identify the core economic conflict: new writing material can substitute for an incumbent product, changing demand and threatening livelihoods.

The settlement does not simply “defeat” parchment. It differentiates product uses. Plant paper and parchment receive different market roles, and the unusually fire-resistant Trombe paper becomes a premium/security-relevant product rather than an ordinary substitute.

This supports a broader current model:

**Successful diffusion frequently requires institutional segmentation and negotiated coexistence, not merely technical superiority or lower price.**

## 18. Contract magic: jurisdiction and uninformed third-party harm

V03 materially changes the contract-magic model.

Myne learns that a magical contract may punish a person who violates protected rights even if that person never signed the agreement and does not know it exists. Her response is immediate moral alarm: consent and knowledge are not guaranteed by enforcement.

Benno supplies jurisdictional constraints. Ordinary city contracts operate within the city's magical boundary. The outer wall is therefore not only physical architecture but a legal-magical boundary. Merchant status and special tools control access to this infrastructure. Contracts capable of affecting nonsigners require reporting to the lord.

Benno had attempted to register the Plant Paper Association through the proper route. The Guild Master delayed the process through administrative discretion, contributing to the incumbent conflict.

This creates an important institutional distinction:

- a rule can be formally neutral;
- an official can still use control of timing, registration, or information as leverage;
- magical enforcement can make those administrative choices physically consequential.

The duplicate ordinary parchment retained alongside the magical contract is also analytically important. Human-readable records remain necessary because magical enforcement has jurisdictional limits and may not leave accessible terms after activation.

## 19. Merchant institutions and occupational socialization

V03 strengthens the model of commerce as a social institution rather than a set of clever bargains.

Apprenticeship requires clothing, hygiene, tools, ink, slate, abacus, parchment, contracts, and role-specific behavior. Families normally provide years of tacit merchant socialization. Firms and kin networks transmit skills and customer expectations.

Lutz can partially substitute earnings, explicit teaching, and careful observation for inherited capital, but substitution is costly. His ability to cross class/occupational boundaries depends on money, sponsors, literacy, memory, and willingness to be visibly inexperienced.

This is practical agency produced by a network, not pure individual merit.

## 20. Temple discovery: class inference precedes doctrine

Myne's first meaningful temple encounter is shaped by mistaken status inference.

Her baptism clothing looks unusually fine because the family has collaboratively reused and improved limited materials. Temple personnel infer wealth from appearance. When Myne offers a large donation and presents herself as commercially connected, her access improves dramatically and procedural exceptions become imaginable.

This matters before any later overt hostility: the institution is already sorting her by perceived class.

The library itself is protected by magic and accessible only to authorized affiliates. Books are therefore not merely expensive objects; access is also organizational and status-mediated.

## 21. Baptism, civic registration, and religious doctrine

The baptism ceremony further confirms the link between temple ritual and civic status. Children use blood on a medal associated with registration, and the ceremony is not separable from becoming a formally recognized working city person.

V03 also provides much more religious narrative: the gods of Darkness and Light and the seasonal divine family are taught and enacted through institutional ritual.

At the V03 boundary these are **direct evidence of temple doctrine and civic-religious practice**, not independent proof of the metaphysical history the doctrine narrates.

The corpus should preserve this distinction even as magical phenomena make supernatural claims more plausible in general.

## 22. Temple political economy: mana shortage after a noble purge

The Priest Chief's explanations reconstruct why Myne suddenly possesses leverage.

Blue-robed clergy are nobles or noble children. Gray-robed temple workers are drawn from the orphanage and occupy a subordinated labor position. A central political purge sharply reduced the noble population. In the local territory, stronger noble children who had previously been sent to the temple were recalled into noble society, leaving fewer blue priests and less mana.

The temple's sacred implements are magical tools. Blue clergy fill them with mana. The spring prayer festival uses that mana in a process believed/observed to affect agriculture; insufficient mana threatens yields.

The temple therefore has an institutional resource crisis.

This is the first strong evidence that mana is simultaneously:

- an embodied biological capacity;
- a medical risk when uncontrolled;
- a class-stratified resource;
- an input into civic/religious infrastructure;
- a bargaining asset.

Myne's body is politically valuable because of what it can supply.

## 23. Resolving the noble-only magic contradiction

V02 preserved an apparent contradiction: ordinary explanations treated magic as a noble power, while `身食い` evidence showed commoner mana.

V03 supplies the missing distinction.

Commoners can possess mana. `身食い` is precisely the dangerous case in which a child has mana but lacks the ordinary noble channels for managing it. Nobles possess the tools, training, status, and institutional connections that make mana survivable and useful.

Thus:

- **biological mana possession is not exclusively noble**;
- **safe management and institutional use of mana are heavily monopolized by noble structures**.

This resolves V02-C14 without requiring the earlier speakers to have been simply lying. “Magic belongs to nobles” can function as a socially accurate commoner doctrine while remaining biologically incomplete.

## 24. Temple Head confrontation: class power becomes open coercion

The Temple Head's behavior tests the limits of negotiated status.

While he believes Myne is a wealthy merchant child, he treats her with deference and imagines exceptions. When her parents appear and are identified as a soldier and dyer, his behavior changes sharply. He denies them ordinary courtesy, treats their claim to their daughter as negligible, and frames taking Myne as charity.

When they refuse, he orders the parents seized and Myne confined. Resistance is threatened with criminal/death consequences.

The scene demonstrates how quickly nominal opportunity can collapse when class classification changes. It also provides direct evidence that family bonds among poor commoners are institutionally discounted by someone accustomed to temple children who have been relinquished.

## 25. Myne's `威圧`: mana as dangerous agency

Myne's anger at threats to her parents produces the strongest mana event yet observed.

The pressure she directs at the Temple Head can immobilize and physically injure adults. The Priest Chief later explains it as emotion-driven mana overflow directed against a target. Children are particularly prone to losing control; effect strength varies with mana quantity.

Myne's increasing ability to exert pressure indicates that her mana amount is itself increasing.

This is agency in a narrow physical sense, but the scene also shows why power is not equivalent to freedom. Myne can nearly kill the official threatening her family; doing so would turn the family into relatives of someone who killed a noble and expose them to catastrophic punishment.

The Priest Chief stops her not by physically overpowering the mana first, but by correctly identifying the consequence that matters to her.

## 26. Priest Chief: opportunism, regulation, and a different institutional model

The Priest Chief should not be flattened into either rescuer or neutral bureaucrat.

He admits that he allowed the initial attempt to pressure Myne because it would have been administratively easiest if a mana-rich child entered without conditions. He underestimated the parents because temple experience has taught him that children there—whether orphaned or noble—are commonly children their parents did not keep.

Myne's family therefore violates an institutional expectation embedded in his worldview.

At the same time, he is capable of updating. He restrains Myne by understanding her priorities, recognizes that the temple needs her mana, and becomes the principal negotiator of a workable arrangement.

His own envy of the parents' willingness to risk themselves for Myne is important counterevidence against reading him as purely instrumental.

V03 establishes him as a likely future regulator/mentor/institutional gatekeeper, but his mature relationship with Myne lies outside the current source boundary.

## 27. Negotiated temple entry: exception, not equality

The final settlement includes unusually favorable conditions:

- blue robes rather than ordinary gray commoner service;
- treatment closer to a noble child in task assignment;
- home commute rather than family separation;
- exemption from work when ill;
- library access/work;
- continued workshop activity subject to further economic arrangement;
- responsibility for mana-related temple functions;
- attendants expected as part of blue-robed status.

The family experiences this as a victory, and relative to the Temple Head's attempted seizure it is.

But the analytical model should remain precise. The concession is not evidence that the temple suddenly recognizes commoner equality. Myne receives an exception because:

1. her mana is unusually valuable;
2. the temple is unusually short of mana;
3. her family demonstrates willingness to resist;
4. she has commercial resources;
5. the Priest Chief can see that coercion risks losing the resource or causing disaster.

Practical agency is leverage-sensitive.

## 28. Gunther and Otto: institutional position as protective intelligence

The epilogue re-evaluates Gunther's work.

Gunther initially feels that being a low-status soldier offers little protection against nobles. Otto reframes the gate as an information chokepoint. Because contractual and political jurisdiction is geographically bounded, monitoring outside nobles and other entrants can give the family warning that more powerful actors do not automatically control.

Gunther converts this insight into a protection strategy. His statement that he will protect his family together with the city is not merely paternal sentiment; it ties family defense to his actual institutional role and information access.

Otto's own life is also moving. Corinna's pregnancy and the Gilberta Company's growth make his eventual transition from guard work into merchant-family work increasingly plausible. V03 does not complete that transition.

## 29. Tuuli: seeing the merchant-facing Myne

Tuuli's appended viewpoint exposes recipient-conditioned behavior that the household normally misses.

She sees Myne bargaining and navigating commercial adults with an assurance very different from the frail younger sister at home. This widens the family model without granting Tuuli Urano knowledge.

Tuuli's own occupational development is also materially shaped by presentation. Following Myne's hygiene advice improves how adults evaluate her for customer-facing work. She recognizes that craft skill alone is insufficient; observing wealthy clients, fashion, nature, and material variation is part of the information work of becoming better at sewing.

The sisters therefore influence each other's professional development in different directions.

## 30. Ilse: professional competence and patronage barriers

Ilse's story deepens the production model beyond household craft.

She has real culinary expertise and can reconstruct Myne's recipes. Yet advancement in elite kitchens depends not only on skill but lineage, patronage, and connection. Professional competence therefore does not dissolve status structure.

When Ilse questions Myne's adult-like knowledge, Myne uses “dream” as a protective explanation. This is evidence of strategic disclosure, not evidence that dream transmission is the objective reincarnation mechanism.

Myne also withholds soup knowledge as possible future noble-facing leverage, reinforcing the increasingly conditional information policy noted above.

## 31. Benno, Mark, and the creation of adjacent capability

The tasting event teaches Benno a marketing method as much as a recipe. He recognizes controlled sampling as a way to create desire before sale and sees a market extending beyond the narrow initial target.

Rather than accept permanent dependence on competitors with better culinary infrastructure, he decides to develop missing capability himself.

Mark turns the ambition into an operational question: staff, supply, association conflict, contracts, training, and financing.

This is an important systemic consequence of imported knowledge. Innovation does not simply add products; it can induce organizations to build entirely new capabilities.

Mark's retrospective perspective also shows that Benno's ambitions are changing. The possibility of spreading plant-paper production to other towns and operating beyond the existing Gilberta business revives a larger commercial dream.

## 32. Gustav / Guild Master: competing institutional rationalities

Gustav's viewpoint materially complicates the commercial conflict.

He sees the Commercial Guild as responsible for continuity and balance across merchants and political changes. He interprets intervention in Benno's family and later businesses as protective/systemic. Yet his earlier marriage and support proposals were timed and framed so poorly that Benno experienced them as attempts to capture vulnerable people and assets.

The conflict is therefore not reducible to one party possessing benevolent motives and the other greed. They operate from different governance models:

- Gustav thinks in terms of citywide commercial coordination and institutional channeling;
- Benno thinks in terms of protecting a firm/family from a powerful official whose “help” has historically threatened autonomy.

Both models can generate harmful actions even when the actor believes them justified.

Gustav's final response to the temple investigation also shows how rapidly Myne's value now crosses institutional boundaries. He supplies favorable information but advises Frieda to keep some distance because noble/temple interest changes the risk profile.

## 33. Mana, communication, and layered jurisdiction

The appended material includes urgent magical correspondence in the form of a white magic bird, further confirming that mana technologies are part of elite administrative communication.

Across V03, at least four institutional layers now interact:

- household/kin authority;
- merchant/guild/association governance;
- temple religious and mana infrastructure;
- lord/noble political jurisdiction.

They overlap without collapsing into one hierarchy.
