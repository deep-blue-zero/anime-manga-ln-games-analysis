# Ascendance of a Bookworm — Part 1 boundary synthesis

## 1. Responsibility

This document owns the **V01-V03 Part 1 checkpoint** for `第一部「兵士の娘」`.

Its responsibilities are narrower than a full-series synthesis and broader than a single-volume reading:

- reconcile the model reached across the three frozen Part 1 volumes;
- adjudicate the major prospective claims that survived or changed across those boundaries;
- identify what has become stable enough to support independent character, relationship, or specialist work;
- identify what remains unresolved;
- record whether the analytical architecture needs to split, extend, or remain as-is before Part 2 begins.

It does **not** replace `BOOKWORM_V01_DEEP_READING.md`, `BOOKWORM_V02_DEEP_READING.md`, or `BOOKWORM_V03_DEEP_READING.md`. Those remain the historical records of what each prospective source boundary supported.

No V04 or later numbered-volume knowledge is used here.

## 2. Part 1 macrostructure

Part 1 moves through three progressively larger scales of agency:

1. **V01 — survival, household adaptation, and concept formation.**  
   Urano/Myne must learn the material world, local body, family, literacy environment, and limits of remembered knowledge. The paper goal becomes the first project that requires a durable collaborator and external commercial gate.

2. **V02 — prototype, identity recognition, commercial law, and class-mediated illness.**  
   Paper becomes real; Myne/Lutz becomes an identity-bearing partnership; `身食い` becomes a strongly corroborated local condition; contracts, guild registration, commercial capture, and magical infrastructure become visible.

3. **V03 — organization, institutional conflict, political mana scarcity, and negotiated membership.**  
   Paper scales into a workshop and association; Lutz's career becomes independently authored; `身食い` is materially explained as uncontrolled mana; the temple and noble resource system expose how survival, class, public infrastructure, and coercion interact; Myne enters a new institutional phase through negotiated exception.

The Part 1 arc is therefore not merely “a girl wants books and becomes a shrine maiden.” It is the construction of an increasingly complex **agency network** around a physically limited child whose imported knowledge, relationships, products, and mana progressively make her valuable to institutions that can both enable and constrain her.

## 3. Identity: Urano, Myne, and social continuity

### 3.1 What Part 1 establishes

The source strongly establishes first-person continuity from Urano into the current consciousness using Myne's body and memories. It also establishes that the local cast observes a meaningful behavioral discontinuity.

Lutz is the first person to confront that discontinuity directly. After receiving the prior-life disclosure, he chooses the current person as “his Myne.” His decision is relational rather than metaphysical: the person with whom he has actually cooked, made hairpins, pursued paper, argued, worked, and cared is the current consciousness.

Effa's recollection of the earlier child's fever dreams complicates a clean instantaneous-replacement model. The household also notices that Myne changed but interprets the shift through recovery, maturation, and family resemblance rather than supernatural replacement.

### 3.2 What Part 1 does not establish

Part 1 does not determine whether the ontology is:

- replacement;
- merger;
- memory/personality emergence;
- continuity through a prior hidden state;
- some other mechanism.

Current analytical authority should therefore separate two questions:

- **social/personal continuity:** increasingly strong;
- **metaphysical mechanism:** OPEN.

## 4. Myne's value development

Part 1 contains a genuine change in what continued life means to Myne.

At V01, books dominate the future and attachment to this life is comparatively weak. Relationships and obligations begin to create reasons not to disappear.

At V02, Myne explicitly says she does not want to die. Enjoyment, usefulness, paper progress, family, and Lutz have become positive reasons to remain alive.

At V03, the value hierarchy is tested rather than merely stated. Myne rejects the normal `身食い` survival route when it would require surrendering family belonging and practical self-authorship. She also gives up immediate access to the temple library when it appears incompatible with the family promise.

The mature Part 1 formulation is therefore:

> **Books remain a stable core desire, but family belonging and self-authored life have become co-central enough to constrain both book-seeking and survival choices.**

This is development, not evidence that the book drive weakened into irrelevance. The distress of abandoning the library temporarily destabilizes her mana, showing how powerful the original desire remains.

## 5. Bodily state: frailty, `身食い`, mana, and control

Part 1 begins with a weak child whose fevers and stamina limits are only partly legible.

V01 separates some deconditioning from a stranger internal heat responsive to emotion and will.

V02 strongly corroborates `身食い` as the local diagnosis and shows the crisis accelerating.

V03 explains much more:

- `身食い` is uncontrolled/accumulating `魔力`;
- magical tools can absorb or transfer excess mana;
- the intervention is temporary rather than curative;
- mana quantity can grow as the child grows;
- Myne's mana appears unusually high and increasing;
- emotion can direct overflowing mana as `威圧` against another person;
- strong mana without tools normally kills commoner children earlier.

However, Frieda provides an important counterexample to over-unification. When her mana is managed, she is not chronically frail in the same way as Myne. The master model should therefore continue distinguishing:

1. ordinary physical weakness / illness / deconditioning;
2. mana accumulation and `身食い` crisis;
3. emotional control of mana;
4. the social ability to access tools and training.

## 6. Mana and class: resolving the Part 1 contradiction

V02 preserved a conflict between ordinary “magic belongs to nobles” doctrine and evidence that commoners can possess mana.

V03 resolves the contradiction materially.

The strongest current model is not biological noble exclusivity. It is **institutional noble monopoly**:

- commoners can be born with mana;
- commoner mana without tools appears as dangerous `身食い`;
- nobles possess the tools, education, patronage structures, and social expectations that make mana manageable;
- wealth and connections can sometimes buy access, but commonly through dependent contracts;
- mana quantity itself is stratified, and noble families may send low-mana or surplus children to the temple.

Thus a commoner may possess the same category of embodied resource while lacking the institutions that make it survivable.

This distinction should govern future class/magic analysis unless later evidence revises it.

## 7. `身食い` survival as an autonomy problem

Frieda and Myne establish two different responses to the same structural condition.

Frieda's family uses wealth and connections to negotiate a comparatively favorable noble arrangement. She prepares economically and socially for a future in which her survival is tied to a patron.

Myne rejects that route because she interprets life under comprehensive external control as incompatible with meaningful living. Her refusal is not abstract anti-nobility ideology. It is grounded in specific valued relationships and projects she expects the arrangement to sever or subordinate.

The temple then offers a third path because the institution itself needs mana. This path is still hierarchical, but Myne can negotiate:

- residence with family;
- illness accommodations;
- library work/access;
- blue-robed status;
- continued workshop activity;
- a role supplying mana.

The result demonstrates that the choice set is not fixed by doctrine alone. Scarcity and leverage can alter it.

## 8. Myne ↔ Lutz: the first fully promotion-worthy relationship

Across V01-V03 the relationship develops through several independent dimensions:

### Production

Myne supplies concepts, literacy, experimental framing, and design. Lutz supplies physical execution, local materials knowledge, observation, memory, transport, and embodied process continuity.

### Care

Lutz learns Myne's bodily warning signs, adjusts pace, carries or assists her, and participates in emergency response.

### Identity

Lutz recognizes the discontinuity others normalize, receives the Urano disclosure, and deliberately chooses the current person.

### Career

Myne opens merchant routes and teaches foundational skills; Lutz becomes increasingly independent and explicitly refuses to let his occupational goal be reduced to Myne's influence.

### Institutional/commercial linkage

A magical contract ties Lutz to the sales rights of Myne Workshop products.

### Mutual future commitment

When temple service threatens ordinary shared work, both explicitly preserve a joint creation relationship: Myne will bring ideas to Lutz; Lutz will make what she conceives.

The dyad now has independent state and downstream importance. A future `Myne ↔ Lutz` synthesis is warranted.

Part 1 does **not** require a romantic reading. Affection, possessiveness, social teasing, and narrator speculation remain compatible with multiple developmental interpretations.

## 9. Lutz as an independent character trajectory

Part 1 also establishes that Lutz is no longer analytically reducible to “Myne's helper.”

His merchant project is self-authored. It predates full family approval and persists independently of Myne's instructions. V03 gives him:

- explicit articulation of the goal;
- conflict over household expectations;
- savings and willingness to leave home if necessary;
- one parent's active support;
- paid work and tools;
- formal apprenticeship;
- cross-class acculturation;
- recognition of inherited merchant cultural capital he lacks;
- a distinct relationship with Benno/Mark as teachers.

His continued specialization around Myne is therefore a chosen role inside a broader merchant identity rather than the whole identity.

This is sufficient for a future Lutz character monograph.

## 10. Myne's practical competence and remaining risk blindness

Part 1 repeatedly shows Myne undervaluing herself when comparing her body to ordinary child labor. External actors instead value:

- literacy;
- calculation;
- planning;
- recipe/product knowledge;
- negotiation;
- rapid learning;
- experimental comparison;
- information design.

By V03 these skills can alter guild negotiations and workshop economics.

Yet Part 1 should not invert the error and treat her as an omniscient adult strategist. She repeatedly misses local risk assumptions:

- unfamiliar material hazards;
- commercial capture;
- institutional status signals;
- magical contract implications;
- noble/temple power;
- the political meaning of valuable mana.

Her strongest mature Part 1 competence is not “knowing the modern answer.” It is **iterative problem framing plus unusually strong literacy/cognition**, embedded in a network of adults and peers who know the local system better.

## 11. Knowledge transfer: the Part 1 causal model

The three volumes produce a consistent chain:

```text
remembered concept
      ↓
local hypothesis
      ↓
material / procedural experiment
      ↓
collaboration with local embodied expertise
      ↓
prototype
      ↓
organizational scaling
      ↓
market / guild / institutional consequences
```

Examples include:

- failed writing-media attempts before plant paper;
- shampoo success followed by failed “improved” replication that removes useful particles;
- plant paper requiring wood species, binder, steaming, beating, water, tools, season, measurement, and Lutz's body;
- pound cake using a portable ratio but still requiring Ilse's professional technique;
- adult paper production requiring redesigned equipment and workshop geography;
- Benno deciding to develop missing culinary capability once a product reveals a market gap.

The key Part 1 conclusion is:

> **Imported knowledge does not enter the world as finished technology. It enters as a hypothesis whose value must be rediscovered through local materials, bodies, skills, institutions, and incentives.**

## 12. Diffusion policy is conditional, not absolute

Myne strongly favors spreading useful production, especially where it supports books. But Part 1 supplies several limits:

- she accepts commercial exclusivity for some products;
- she shares rights with collaborators rather than insisting on personal monopoly;
- she withholds parue-related commercialization to avoid harming poor users through price effects;
- she withholds some recipes/techniques as future bargaining protection;
- Benno strategically limits disclosure to prevent capture or imitation.

A mature model should therefore distinguish **desire for diffusion** from **indifference to timing, distributional harm, ownership, or personal risk**.

## 13. From craft network to organization

The economic system around Myne grows in stages:

### Household contribution

Food, hygiene, baskets, hairpins, and sewing connect remembered knowledge to family labor.

### Dyadic prototype

Myne/Lutz create paper through complementary competence.

### Merchant-backed project

Benno provides capital, pricing, tools, contracts, and market access; Mark organizes execution.

### Distributed production

Households make ornaments for wages; adult artisans build equipment; professional cooks reconstruct recipes.

### Institutionalized enterprise

Myne Workshop, Plant Paper Association, guild registration, product rights, market segmentation, and planned geographic expansion appear.

This progression is analytically more important than any one product. Part 1 turns knowledge into **organizations capable of persisting beyond the inventor's body**.

## 14. Merchant governance: guilds, associations, firms, and discretion

Commercial institutions are not a unified market mechanism.

Part 1 now distinguishes:

- individual firms/shops;
- the Commercial Guild;
- craft/product associations;
- merchant families and apprenticeship networks;
- the lord's political jurisdiction;
- magical contract systems.

The parchment conflict demonstrates that new production can collide with incumbent rights. The Plant Paper Association cannot simply exist because the product works; registration and negotiated scope matter.

Gustav and Benno embody rival institutional rationalities. Gustav thinks in citywide coordination and continuity. Benno interprets concentrated guild power through a history in which institutional “help” threatened his family's autonomy.

Neither model should be reduced to neutral public governance versus selfish private enterprise. Each has self-interest and legitimate system concerns.

## 15. Contract magic: law stronger than consent

Contract magic is one of Part 1's most consequential institutional discoveries.

V02 establishes strong magical enforcement. V03 shows that the effect can reach uninformed nonsigners and is geographically/jurisdictionally bounded.

This produces several durable distinctions:

- enforcement power ≠ informed consent;
- magical validity ≠ accessible human-readable record;
- jurisdiction can be spatially bounded;
- merchant eligibility controls access to contract infrastructure;
- contracts affecting outsiders may require higher political reporting;
- administrative delay can create physical/legal exposure.

Ordinary parchment duplicates and literacy remain protective technologies even in a magical system.

## 16. Temple as religious, civic, class, and resource institution

Part 1 ends by forcing the analysis to abandon a simple “church” category.

The temple participates in:

- baptism and civic registration;
- religious doctrine and ritual;
- libraries and literacy resources;
- orphan/gray-robed labor organization;
- noble-family surplus/status management;
- mana storage and sacred tools;
- agriculture-linked public ritual;
- political consequences of noble purges;
- class sorting and patronage.

Myne's entry is therefore simultaneously religious affiliation, labor assignment, mana-resource allocation, class exception, library access, and survival infrastructure.

Part 2 should preserve those responsibilities separately rather than treat “temple” as one homogeneous cause.

## 17. Temple power and negotiated exception

The Temple Head and Priest Chief show different uses of institutional power.

The Temple Head's treatment changes abruptly when Myne's family class becomes visible. His willingness to seize the child, dismiss parental claims, and invoke criminal punishment shows coercive class hierarchy directly.

The Priest Chief also begins from institutional self-interest. He admits that an unconditional entry would have been easiest and that he allowed pressure to develop. But he updates after seeing the family's attachment and Myne's mana danger. He can translate the conflict into negotiable terms because he understands both the temple's needs and the consequences Myne cares about.

The final settlement is best understood as a **negotiated exception under reciprocal dependence**.

It should not be generalized into evidence that commoners normally receive blue-robed equality.

## 18. Family as an agency institution

Part 1 progressively revises the family from survival dependency into a major source of practical agency.

Effa, Gunther, and Tuuli provide:

- food, shelter, care, materials, labor, and local knowledge;
- occupational networks;
- emotional belonging;
- production capacity;
- class identity and social constraints;
- willingness to resist institutional seizure;
- information and protection through Gunther's gate role.

The family can constrain Myne's choices, but those constraints are not reducible to oppression. By V03 Myne herself treats family membership as part of what makes life worth living.

Gunther's gate position is especially instructive. Low formal rank does not mean zero agency. Once matched to the actual threat model, gate information becomes a strategically useful protective resource.

## 19. Ordinary life as causal evidence

Part 1 repeatedly makes “small” material details structurally important:

- ash, cloth, fuel, food, and animal products have competing household uses;
- walking practice changes stamina without curing all illness;
- hygiene affects occupational presentation;
- merchant children possess cultural capital from household exposure;
- Lutz's brothers taking food/possessions affects his desire for independent achievement and storage;
- baptism clothing alters class perception;
- tools must fit adult bodies and local workflow;
- books and writing materials are scarce enough to change memory strategy;
- professional cook advancement depends on patronage as well as technique.

The corpus should continue retaining mundane evidence when it explains actual option sets.

## 20. Information asymmetry at the Part 1 boundary

Important knowledge is now distributed unevenly:

### Lutz uniquely knows

- Myne's explicit Urano prior-life disclosure.

### Family knows

- Myne is changed and unusual;
- `身食い`/mana threatens her life;
- temple service is connected to survival;
- they do not receive the Urano disclosure in Part 1.

### Benno/Mark know

- unusually much about Myne's products, market value, contracts, risk, and workshop structure;
- they still lack her full prior-life identity.

### Frieda/Gustav know

- `身食い`, noble-access economics, and high-level guild networks;
- they possess partial but commercially interested models of Myne.

### Temple officials know

- Myne has unusually strong mana and commercial resources;
- they begin investigating her network and finances;
- they do not possess an omniscient account of her origin.

### Reader knows

- multiple private viewpoints that no in-world person combines.

Future analysis must resist turning this distributed knowledge into one omniscient character model.

## 24. Transition state into Part 2

Part 1 ends with Myne entering a new social phase while preserving several old networks.

She is now simultaneously:

- a commoner child and registered city resident;
- physically fragile;
- mana-rich;
- a workshop owner/knowledge producer;
- commercially linked to Benno and Lutz;
- bound for blue-robed temple service;
- embedded in a family she consciously refuses to surrender;
- increasingly visible to guild, temple, and noble-adjacent actors.

That combination creates the key Part 2 analytical problem: **how does a person retain or revise self-authored projects when multiple institutions now possess legitimate, coercive, economic, or bodily claims on her time and mana?**

This is a question for V04+, not an answer imported from later volumes.

## 25. Part 1 abstentions

At the V03 boundary, do not claim that:

- Urano/Myne metaphysics are resolved;
- Myne's chronic frailty is wholly caused by mana;
- temple blue-robed status makes her socially equal to nobles;
- all noble contracts are identical to Frieda's negotiated arrangement;
- all commoners with mana necessarily receive the same `身食い` trajectory;
- temple doctrine is independently proven metaphysical history;
- the Priest Chief is already a trusted mentor rather than an opportunistic but adaptive institutional actor;
- Myne/Lutz is canonically romantic;
- Benno or Gustav is simply benevolent or predatory across contexts;
- merchant institutions are neutral or uniformly corrupt;
- innovation will inevitably improve welfare for every class;
- Part 2 outcomes are knowable from the Part 1 transition.
