# Authorship, fiction, observation and the person who exceeds an account

This canonical administrative derivative has passed independent review at the R09_6 local literary scope. The [ensemble and thematic integration review](../08%20Audits%20and%20Manifests/TOMOZAKI_ENSEMBLE_AND_THEMATIC_INTEGRATION_REVIEW.md) binds the accepted argument, exact corrections, actual read/source scopes and canonical render. Preserved authoring-history references describe their earlier stage. Cross-specialist convergence, final synthesis, literary release, distinct models and repository publication retain separate gates.

Kikuchi's writing repeatedly gives another person something direct reassurance could not deliver. It also makes people feel exposed, misdescribed or used. The series does not resolve that conflict by choosing accurate observation over invention, or benevolent invention over accuracy. It makes the writer revise what observation is for while preserving a limit she cannot remove: another person's life includes a standpoint she does not own. Nagisa's unavailable final feeling makes that limit decisive. The story can help Aoi discover what she wants to say; it cannot make the dead girl certify the answer.

The central interpretation is that **fiction becomes a consequential offer when the author includes herself among the people whose interests matter, lets encountered particulars resist the preferred conclusion, and owns the remaining wish as her own. None of those achievements converts representation into testimony, recipient benefit into permission, or an owned writing reason into a universal right to expose.** This is an INTERPRETIVE HYPOTHESIS about the admitted novels. Its ethical evaluation is explicit and conditional; it neither authenticates every fictional likeness nor declares all representation impermissible. [AO-E01–03]

This domain owns the relation among worlds and people, observation and self-inclusion, fictionalization and exposure, possible publication and the use of another person's pain. The game/form theme separately asks how acquired technique becomes inhabited practice. Their overlap matters: a story is a made form, but the fiction problem includes absent models, private material, translation, an author's choice to omit, readers who appropriate differently and a person who cannot answer at all. Those responsibilities require a separate sustained argument.

## 1. Evidence, access and four different authorities

The source boundary is Japanese main V01–V12 plus V06.5 and V08.5, fourteen admitted witnesses. Source-facing readings remain frozen at their knowledge horizons; current owners record later revisions. V06.5's diary and formation stories do not make early Fumiya know Kikuchi's private writing. V08.5's New Year's Eve precedes V08; its publication position does not put the thought after V09's online writing or V11's crisis. V05's earlier appended bonus, V07's Special Edition/art distinction and the parallel karaoke/adapted VR segregations remain intact. No adaptation, future book or extratextual author interview supplies a missing permission or outcome.

DIRECT evidence can be a spoken proposition, represented private thought, act, surviving document within the outer fiction or actual depicted reading. Those are not the same kind of access. Kikuchi's thought about Alcia is direct evidence of her thought; Alcia's speech is authored speech. Aoi's first-person chapter is new direct reader access without becoming an interview the ensemble received. Andi's manuscript marks are directly represented as marks, while a reconstruction of why he made them remains attributed interpretation. An emotionally persuasive sequence cannot silently collapse these layers.

The novel makes at least four authorities distinguishable:

| Standing | What may be chosen in the represented case | What does not follow automatically |
|---|---|---|
| Author over her work | draft, revise, show a particular chapter, withhold, finish, name her purpose | consent from every modeled person, an editor's future decision or a right to circulate private source papers |
| Participant/contributor | tell a bounded account, agree to a role-play attempt or share particular material | equal authorship of every later use, knowledge of all source material or authority for an absent subject |
| Modeled person | choose disclosure, object to a likeness, refuse an interpretation or receive it differently | power to make every external interpretation factually false, or prior consent inferred from later benefit |
| Reader/recipient | resist, identify, continue, stop, reread, appropriate a meaning and describe an effect | ownership of the author's motive, testimony from fictional persons or a universal verdict on the method |

This is an analytical map of depicted permissions, not a legal theory of copyright or a demand for a comprehensive contract before every conversation. Its value is precision. Kikuchi can permit Fumiya to share a chapter without authorizing the private Hinami facts incorporated in it. Mizusawa can accept a particular sharing without granting Hinami's rights. Haruka can request an interpretation while not knowing its likely force. Aoi can take the manuscript after pressure without turning the preceding refusal into advance assent. Each movement has a different chooser. [AO-R01–03, AO-R08–10, AO-R16]

## 2. Readers have lives before becoming material for the central relation

Kikuchi's literary life precedes the romance and cannot be reconstructed as an aptitude Fumiya awakens. The V06.5 middle-school retrospective shows animated retelling in a meaningful field and Goda Sayaka's unusual route to connection: covers and design interest first, then acquired reading of a book Kikuchi values. The teacher's aesthetic attention is real although it is not initially knowledge of the contents. That difference is already a small epistemic lesson. Sharing an object does not establish sharing its whole meaning; an adult can revise her approach instead of requiring the younger reader to become universally sociable. [AO-R04]

The diary images, admitted and actually inspected in the accepted KF-P04 transaction, place writing and observations of Fumiya within the V01 period. This draft inherits that accepted historical image evidence; it has not newly re-viewed or OCRed the images and supplies no new exact diary quotation. Private dates and an analyst's later access do not become contemporary knowledge for Fumiya. The chronology matters because it preserves a reader/writer with initiative and ongoing interests beyond the role of a heroine discovered by the protagonist. [AO-E04; AO-R04]

Fumiya initially tries to approach her through a false shared-author premise. The honest reset and later genuine reading are more significant than successful imitation of a literary identity. A learned opportunity becomes a real shared world because there is now an object he can answer about, not because it has always been pure at origin. Silent library time, ordinary book discussion and continued contact retain their own value. The later couple's fictional language has a history in chosen reading rather than functioning as a code that automatically produces the correct romantic outcome. [AO-R05]

Her family time is another independent good. In the interstitial New Year's Eve narration, the annotated script is cherished as a record of shared work; familiar speech, food and the family's different responses do not merely prove readiness for a future author crisis. A father who wants to read when work permits is neither the professional editor nor the intimate partner. Riku's call from the kitchen returns her to a warm household while an unsettled responsibility question remains. Such continuity prevents the V12 fear of having no value without writing from becoming an omniscient finding that she has never had another connection to the world. [AO-R06]

The relevant interpretive revision is consequently not that books imprison an otherwise empty person. Literary attention is a practiced and enjoyed relation to worlds; it can support selected sociability and still become a way of arranging the world so that the author disappears from it. The same capacity has different uses, and the ordinary reader's life remains evidence against a crisis-only account.

## 3. The author perspective has ethical reach and can omit its own author

V07 gives Kikuchi a vocabulary Fumiya's game model cannot simply absorb. She says she tends to consider how the world should come together beautifully before what she herself wants. When he calls that a player perspective, she gently rejects his term. In a game the character's opposite may be the player; in a novel, she proposes, it may be the author. The refusal matters because the series allows two serious modes of distance from a lived world. Her compositional responsibility is not exhausted by an operator's calculation of advantage. [AO-E05; AO-R07]

That account has ethical content. Kikuchi does not want to select an ending merely because it gratifies her. Other characters and the created world's coherence matter to her. Treating this only as timidity would miss a capacity for considering more than the speaker's immediate gain. Yet the author who protects the whole world can quietly remove herself from the people whose happiness the arrangement counts. Voluntarily trying to become a universally sociable Popol then becomes self-exclusion, not simply growth chosen by a shy person. Her particular habitat and desired ties have been judged defective by a generalized world ideal. [AO-R07–08]

The correction is therefore self-inclusion within a widened account, not abolition of concern for others. Selected affinity, the writer-aspirant identification and Chris's craft make positive paths possible without requiring that every setting fit. It is equally important not to freeze the habitat metaphor into an essence. Her middle-school failed translation explains why it is protective; later genuine contact and collaboration show expandable range. A metaphor can preserve a valid constraint and still narrow what its user imagines possible. [CRI-KIK-001; CS §8.6]

The public play and private novel separate unresolved goods. The public Libra–Alcia arrangement belongs to one authored world-facing judgment; the private Chris–Libra ending gives desire a different authored space. Neither is a transparent forecast of the real triangle. Fumiya's initial reading of her apparent refusal must be corrected through actual approach and clarification, with Mimimi's costly assistance rather than a private novel magically choosing for its model. The direct romantic decision has force because these people act after the proxy has proved insufficient. [AO-E06; AO-R08]

This is the first major formal lesson for the authorship domain. A represented character may be an exacting object of the writer's responsibility without becoming a literal witness about a living model. Conversely, a fictional ending can reveal something about the writer's desire without being reducible to an encoded confession. Public work, private work, direct speech and an actual relationship remain distinct evidence objects. Their crossings matter because the distinctions survive them.

## 4. Earlier conscience and later vocation intensify the problem together

Kikuchi's conscience does not first appear when Haruka cries in V11. In the earlier year-end unit she directly asks herself whether it was right to tell Hinami about Alcia's interior and to investigate her circumstances. She recognizes that a thing once put into circulation cannot simply be taken back and that crossing into another person's affairs brings responsibility. She also says the person's real response is known to that person. This is not a completed safeguard, but it prevents a later conversion story in which an innocent observer only then discovers that writing has effects. [AO-E07; AO-R06]

The sequence becomes more difficult as the work gains an audience. V09's online Alcia/Libra fiction is actual public-facing activity at that scale; it is not the V12 private manuscript relay and not a demonstrated commercial book. Kikuchi asks Fumiya questions whose answers worsen her jealousy because characterization remains part of the work she has chosen. The writer's commitment has real rigor and real cost. It can expose her own vulnerability while also turning another person's disclosed experience into material. Courage to endure the first does not authorize the second. [AO-R09]

V11's conditional editorial interest makes the stakes more concrete. Interest in continuation and a possible publication route supply external recognition of craft; they do not establish a signed contract, publication, income, a career or negotiated permissions from the models. The problem is not resolved by sorting writer and exploitative observer into different people. Kikuchi can enjoy writing, want a prize, have talent and ask whether she has an adequate reason to prioritize writing when it hurts someone. The later question concerns those variables together, rather than proving the earlier enjoyment unreal. [AO-R10]

Haruka's participation requires equal precision. She asks for Kikuchi's view and stops Fumiya's attempted interruption before the tears. The inquiry is not represented as persistence against an explicit Haruka demand to stop. That positive agency does not mean she anticipates the full impact, possesses all the relevant family information or grants Hinami's permission. Kikuchi later acknowledges creative absorption and harm. The exact cause of every tear is not externally certified; the distinction between invited speech, foreseeable care and authorial exposure remains. [AO-E08; AO-R10]

The V11 encounter with Fumiya makes recipient failure explicit in another register. Direct reassurance does not reach him; the call ends without an identified terminating actor. Fiction then allows him to see an account he could not receive as advice. He apologizes and thanks Kikuchi before asking her to help with a further story. His inward momentary thought that this might have been the reason for dating her is evidence of instrumental temptation in that moment, not proof of the actual historical origin of their relationship. Her own reason remains unfound when she accepts his initial rescue reason provisionally. [AO-R11; accepted R07]

The important new question is thus not whether narrative has power. It plainly does. It is whether a writer can distinguish what she wants to make, what she is able to make another person feel, what she has grounds to claim, and what she is entitled to do with the people involved. The volume leaves these apart rather than granting success in one domain authority in all the others.

## 5. Collective material can still be collected toward a conclusion chosen in advance

The V12 sewing-room coalition is actual: Fumiya, Kikuchi, Mimimi and Mizusawa meet and contribute. It revises the V11 proposal state. It is not evidence that Hinami has agreed, that the four know the same private history or that each contributor controls every later use. The new project has more perspectives without yet having the subject's perspective in the form of direct participation. [AO-R12]

The individual contributions resist being treated as interchangeable testimony. Mimimi trusts the Tama tie but fears losing her own self-regard during physical separation; the “next Tama” possibility is a conditional self-question rather than a prediction of an exclusive replacement. She reports having refused weakened Fumiya opportunism and can like the self who chose that conduct while continuing to love him. This is not her first ever self-regard: V09 already contains an affirmative partial self-account. Nor is it a universal future stopping policy. The writer receives a particular person's account, not a solved specimen of independence. [AO-E09; current Mimimi §17; CRI §18]

Mizusawa's contribution is differently bounded. He has read a likeness of himself, accepted one particular sharing with an ambiguity Fumiya still notices, and disclosed that access to Hinami was also a motive for contact. Friendship now has a directly received response, not simply an origin purified by good effects. When Kikuchi proposes unscripted acting he resists, looks to Fumiya for help and agrees to one attempt under pressure. His skill does not prove an acting preference or unlimited availability. His performed Sharif is neither a new real romantic confession nor a renewed answer from the actual Hinami. [AO-R12–13]

The actual interviews and role-play initially support the independent-star remedy. The later objection comes from Kikuchi's authored Alcia accusing Chris of finding only the answer desired in advance. That is a different layer from the modeled peers objecting, and a different layer again from the actual absent Aoi speaking. The fiction can nonetheless be a real instrument of self-criticism: Kikuchi recognizes her listening and composition in the charge and suspends the meeting rather than continue confidently toward a result she now fears may harm. [AO-E10; AO-R13]

The methodological defect is not insufficiently rich data. A sufficiently determined author can make many observations confirm an end by selecting what the observations are allowed to mean. Nor would replacing the author's own wish with a vote remove the problem. The witnesses retain uneven access, and the modeled person remains absent. The revision required is a willingness to let evidence change the question, together with recognition that no amount of evidence makes every choice cease to be authored.

## 6. Ordinary affection and chosen travel do not cure the author into availability

Kikuchi's exhaustion is not evidence that she needs the right romantic reassurance to become a flawless writer. Fumiya's opening insistence that helping Hinami is his own wish is sincere and knowingly persuasive: his narration directly recognizes that it secures Kikuchi's acceptance and is unfair. Her acceptance and generosity remain actual; so does the asymmetry he can exploit. The couple's earlier trust is not a standing waiver of fatigue, jealousy or a reason of her own. [AO-R12; CRI §21]

She chooses a solo inquiry to Nagi Island and refuses his company because she expects to borrow his reason again. The trip does not automatically deliver an independent answer. Isolation and block continue. The later telephone agreement is a new event: he says he wants to see her, asks for her answer and waits; she accepts. The changed reason matters without turning the earlier no into an error or proving he has discovered a generally effective remedy for refusals. [AO-E11; AO-R14]

Their ordinary island company has value independent of solving the manuscript. Poetic description becomes playful competition; Fumiya names something about this particular Kikuchi he likes. Affection can answer her fear that she matters only as a writer more concretely than an abstract assurance did. These are local received acts, not a sexual encounter inferred from closeness, a permanent cure of the block or proof that every boundary has disappeared. Her subsequent renewed difficulty is evidence that the trip is not a single therapeutic conversion. [AO-R14]

The ordinary evidence also changes how “experience” should be understood. She does not become qualified to write another person's death simply by traveling, feeling love or acquiring a new activity. A larger lived world gives different things to notice and reasons to choose; it cannot supply an absent person's final standpoint. Treating experience as an automatically authorizing credential would repeat the independent-star remedy's mistake in a more attractive form.

## 7. Mitsu's work and the material world refuse an omniscient observer

Mitsu is a person with ongoing work, bodily limits, standards and selective decisions. Her full name is positively attested at V12 p0016 P634 and P653, not supplied in full by the earlier innkeeper's Mitsu-only route. Custody of Andi's place and papers does not prove she is his spouse. The widow reported in the festival history is neither identified as Mitsu nor proved to be someone else; marital status remains OPEN. Those are limits on biography, not reasons to reduce her to an anonymous wise elder. [AO-R15; accepted Mitsu dossier]

The couple first encounters a refusal and work it cannot freely redesign. Other tasks are delegated within scope. Kikuchi misclassifies cloth colors, compares them under changed conditions and improves through practice. Names matter, but a name alone does not exhaust what can be seen beside another shade, under a different light or on a wearer. The method requires concrete correction rather than a universal exhortation to observe more sympathetically. It also distinguishes wider perception from an obligation to turn every dislike into liking. [AO-E12; AO-R15]

The costume sequence must not be narrated as a harmonious joint opening followed by wise approval. Kikuchi touches the cord without pulling; Fumiya proposes opening and opens after her assent. He later says the proposal was his. The written blue color name is correct and the scarlet sample mismatches it; possible swapping mechanisms and a no-blame interpretation are Fumiya's conjecture, not established fault. Their later proposal has genuine usefulness and receives partial expert correction from Mitsu. That correction is not blanket prior permission for the first opening. Fumiya supplies pins at P779–780 and Kikuchi pins the proposal at P780; the following movement/carrying sequence is not a second source for that supply. [AO-R15; CRI §21; root R07 qualification]

The force of this material case extends beyond a comforting metaphor for writing. Novices can contribute, an expert can revise, and improvement remains dependent on things no one has fully controlled. It is not true that the author can dissolve resistant particulars by changing her interpretation. Nor does material success retrospectively make every access decision authorized. The author learns a practice with external corrections while still having to answer for the choice to use it.

## 8. Plural ordinary witnesses give Andi a personhood no single theory can exhaust

The island does not authenticate a complete sage. Residents give partial and sometimes awkward accounts of Andi's fishing/fish relation, observation, jokes, drinking, translation and social encounters. Those reports are direct evidence that particular people report these things; their explanations are not an omniscient authorial manifesto. The actual maintained place, notes and working objects give material weight to observational labor. They do not reveal every private motive, diagnose a compulsion or guarantee that the observer understood everyone better than they understood themselves. [AO-E13; AO-R16]

This matters because Kikuchi and Fumiya are attracted to a godlike-observer model. A writer apparently trying to see every standpoint without imposing himself seems to offer a way beyond their prescribed remedy. Their psychological account is a serious hypothesis about the represented documents and local reports. It is still theirs. Treating its elegance as authenticated Andi interior would reproduce the very substitution of model for person the story is testing. His legal identity, complete age/biography and last intention are not recovered by the literary argument.

Mitsu also challenges a total origin story. She reports practices that predate Andi's novel and different subsequent appropriations of the fiction. Her account is testimony rather than an exhaustive externally audited history, but it suffices to defeat the couple's assumption that one author created the community's whole meaningful practice. People can find something in a story without becoming its derivative characters. Mitsu's own reception of a flawed model and slight self-liking is particular evidence, not proof that any accurate resemblance will help any recipient. [AO-R17]

The storm-damaged birds make this plural practice visible. Damage remains damage. Children can name what has happened and find their own uses or meanings while new birds are also made. Kikuchi's questioning becomes receptive rather than steering every answer toward a preselected thesis. The continuation does not prove the storm was necessary, secretly good or justified by what followed. Nor do the children all become one collective voice. Different responses survive inside the shared festival. [AO-E14; AO-R17]

The case revises a seductive authorship fantasy: that an exceptionally observant novel can own the source of every practice it illuminates. Here the world precedes the fiction, materially exceeds it and changes what people do with it. The author has made something consequential inside that world. That is a stronger person-level account than either an omnipotent creator or an irrelevant recorder.

## 9. Observation cannot eliminate the choice to include or omit

Kikuchi resumes writing after more attentive observation and then stops again at Nagisa's unavailable voice. The renewed block matters. It prevents craft training and island pleasure from becoming a turnkey method. The actual girl is dead; Fumiya never met her; the available reports cannot supply what she last felt. Distress and calf-deep movement into water are represented, but an explicit suicidal intention or attempt is not given. The epistemic problem should not be converted into a diagnosis to make the narrative neater. [AO-R18]

Omitting the reunion does not solve authorship by declining to invent. It remains a selected outcome with implications for what the story can offer. Kikuchi recognizes that gathering others' material has also deferred asking what she herself wants to do. The remaining decision is not made correct by becoming personal. It becomes identifiable as hers, available for responsibility instead of hidden behind an imagined complete record. Self-inclusion here is different from assigning the subject an outcome the author wants: it names who is making the offer and preserves the subject's unavailable or resisting position. [AO-E15; AO-R18]

Mitsu's loan of the private continuation has conditions, including next-morning return. The couple reads through phone translation and returns the manuscript by placement behind the counter; no hand-to-hand receipt or acknowledgment should be invented. Access to a custodied private paper is not automatic editorial or publication authority. The manuscript is also not a final testament available to answer any question about its author. Its private/unpublished state makes the assumed-rights problem sharper rather than disappearing because these readers admire him. [AO-R19]

The represented revision is exact: **believe → tell → record → wish → pray**, with the first four crossed out and the final word circled. These are documentary marks DIRECT within the outer novel. The explanation of what each deletion means, and the story of a godlike observer finding a remaining human wish, are the couple's interpretive account. The marks are neither the mistaken alternative sequence of verbs nor independent posthumous psychological testimony. Documentary specificity constrains interpretation without making the interpretation unnecessary. [AO-E16; AO-R19]

Kikuchi adopts observation plus prayer as her own practice. The movement is not from facts to anything goes. She has just learned to let observation resist a predetermined form; she cannot now responsibly falsify what is known because a wish is benevolent. Nor is the movement toward an observation so pure that no author remains. A small owned wish can survive accurate attention and acknowledged uncertainty. Its sincerity makes the author answerable for it; sincerity does not make it universally true, benign or permitted. The V12 concluding denial that tears prove rightness keeps this risk internal to the resolution rather than leaving it to an external critic. [AO-R19–20]

## 10. Aoi reads a likeness, resists it and finds an address

The completed story's delivery crosses several different thresholds. Yuzu, Nakamura and Takei appear while Aoi speculates about apology or Fumiya's strategy; those motives remain her hypotheses. She refuses contact. Takei's voice crosses the curtains. Haruka leaves the bundle, later reads independently, cries, names their shared sister and urges Aoi to take it. Aoi can imagine refusing and takes it. The sequence contains actual agency within pressure. Neither “fully consensual delivery” nor “no choice occurred” describes it adequately. [AO-E17; AO-R21]

Haruka is especially important as an incidental reader with her own grief and admiration. She is not merely the successful access mechanism of the couple's plan. Her response precedes and affects her sister's reading while remaining limited by what she knows. Her tears cannot authorize Aoi's representation, and Aoi's eventual benefit cannot retroactively settle the cost of involving a younger sister in secret channels. Sustained initiative and uneven access coexist.

Aoi's reading is initially invasive and painful. A changed name does not make a likeness anonymous to its subject. She objects to being defined and notices resemblance to the maternal mechanism of an appealing explanation entering a weakened person. These objections are not erased when something in the story reaches her. The revised authorial method and owned wish do not make the offered story wholly innocent in contrast to an entirely manipulative family story. Yoko's affection and grief are real; her meaning-making can be harmful; the practice remains unnamed rather than assigned a religion or diagnosis. Different effects and authorities must be specified. [AO-R21–22]

The manuscript also exposes models inside the reader. Aoi's earlier picture of Mimimi as a weak copy does not become the analyst's current truth. Independently evidenced Mimimi has acted, reflected and named partial self-regard; the fiction's Nana makes Aoi confront a discrepancy in her own account. A perceptive author does not automatically know the whole subject, and a newly available subject interior is not automatically correct about everyone else. The reader can be changed partly by having her own representation of another person disturbed. [AO-E18; AO-R12/AO-R22]

The ellipse offers another correction with an evidentiary limit. Recalcitrant observations are retained while an assumed perfect circle changes. Aoi receives the image as a possible way to judge a life containing different achievements, borrowed light, belief and doubt. The mathematical object does not prove a universal psychology, turn a focus into another physical star or make every desirable story true. Her lived sensory memories differentiate where a single correctness category had flattened them. This is direct evidence of a changed uptake in her own narration, not proof that her prior life lacked content. [AO-R22]

Nagisa's last step remains explicitly unresolved in the same chapter. Accident, intentional entry, physical failure and despair remain alternatives after more than five years; the V10/V11 grade conflict is not harmonized. The reunion is a thirty-second possibility within a story. Semantic ruby crosses Alcia/私, 私/Alcia and 渚/ナギサ; later 魔法 is read as ものがたり. Both written base and reading matter. The crossing stages imaginative identification without introducing a deceased witness. No illustration becomes a photograph or resurrection by following the prose's emotional immediacy. This draft inherits the established source/ruby/image-role evidence and makes no new pixel claim. [AO-E19; AO-R22–23]

What Aoi finds is an address she wants to make: apology, love, admiration and thanks. It differs from recovering the cause she imagined asking about. Her gratitude that Nagisa was herself to the end is an affirmation inside that encounter, not verified access to the historical girl's final consciousness; her apology is not proof of causal culpability or preventability. The story's power lies partly in making this distinction livable without pretending to have removed it. An effect can be real when its content is not a newly discovered historical fact.

## 11. The writer's owned reason and the recipient's gain do not settle rightness

Aoi later says she likes herself a little. Fumiya allows that this may be temporary. Actual public participation follows: she attends the school event in uniform, sits among peers and accepts an exhibition. These are changes in contact and chosen activity. They do not establish resumed presidency, regular attendance, coaching, completed family repair or permanent self-belief. The exhibition match and winner remain unshown. The response should not be dismissed as unreal because it is fiction-mediated, or inflated into comprehensive recovery because it is emotionally satisfying. [AO-R24]

Fumiya's replacement-Nagisa offer is immediately retracted by him. The available support becomes the shared story beside doubt, not an accepted office of surrogate sister. V11 already explicitly named reassurance without grounds, so the later distinction is not the discovery that assurance lacks proof. It changes the offered medium and what Aoi is asked to accept. His confidence that the story can remain usable is an owned promise with no longitudinal guarantee. A physical reminder can be available without one living person becoming exclusive keeper of another's worth. [AO-R24; game/form theme GF-R16]

Kikuchi supplies the decisive internal qualification: Aoi's tears do not prove the story correct, and the fictional Nagisa's feeling is not authenticated as the actual girl's. This is not a rhetorical flourish cancelling all care. It keeps true the actual reception while refusing to let effect certify historical truth or the prior method. A writer who had to manufacture the dead person's approval of her work would evade precisely the unavailable standpoint the volume has made central. [AO-E20; AO-R20]

Her new stated reason is to write something with which people unable to believe alone can believe. The aim is now hers, after the borrowed starting reason, revised observation and owned prayer. It does not establish a contract, publication date, successful career or general fictionalization permission. At P167–173 she asks Fumiya what he wants to show through professional play. His later answer at P260–279 is inward; it is not an immediate café reply she hears. Their mutual influence remains real without distributing every private conclusion to both actors. [AO-R20/AO-R24]

Nor does the finished manuscript remove ordinary competing responsibilities. Fumiya and Mimimi's absorbed work displaces actual event preparation; Ashigaru supplies a professional guest contribution in that setting. The source does not establish improvisation or quantify a career loss. The concrete cost matters because the story has been made inside other lives with ongoing work. Literary success can exceed its initial motive and still owe an account of what it displaced. “Prayer” is not a way to exempt art from those recipients.

## 12. Rivals that the argument must survive

| Rival | Serious evidence for it | Required disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Accurate fiction is a superior hidden testimony | Alcia is perceptive; subsequent history corroborates part; recipients recognize themselves | REJECT testimony substitution. Independent speech/actions are corroboration; fiction cannot corroborate itself or authenticate Nagisa's intent. |
| The ethical author must remove herself completely | imposed remedies and selective observation can injure | REJECT purity. Omission and selection remain choices; self-exclusion can harm the author. Retain disciplined observation and declared authorship. |
| Owning a prayer licenses invention about anyone | Kikuchi needs to choose and gives something meaningful | REJECT general permission. Her wish remains hers; affected persons, private sources, circulation and future publication have separate standing. |
| If the recipient benefits, the method was justified | Aoi's small self-liking and return actually occur | REJECT retrospective authorization. Earlier refusal, pressure, painful exposure and missing permission remain; benefit is not denied. |
| Fiction is too invasive to offer anything ethically valuable | renamed likeness wounds; family meaning has harmed | REJECT total nullification. Actual recipient agency, differentiated uptake, authorial correction and owned address matter without guaranteeing innocence. |
