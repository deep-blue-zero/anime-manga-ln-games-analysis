# What a result can prove, and what a story can offer

This canonical administrative derivative has passed independent review at the R09_4 local literary scope. The [ensemble and thematic integration review](../08%20Audits%20and%20Manifests/TOMOZAKI_ENSEMBLE_AND_THEMATIC_INTEGRATION_REVIEW.md) binds the accepted argument, exact corrections, actual read/source scopes and canonical render. Preserved authoring-history references describe their earlier stage. Cross-specialist convergence, final synthesis, literary release, distinct models and repository publication retain separate gates.

## 1. The problem is the passage from explanation to authority

Tomozaki repeatedly shows that action can change a life. Training, attention, strategy, observation, material work and another person's help have real effects. The series does not require us to abandon causal inquiry in favor of a comforting story. Its harder question is what an explanation, a result or an emotionally effective account permits someone to conclude. A correct prediction may say little about the actor's desired end. An owned reason does not settle the permissibility of a method. A later meaning does not recover an inaccessible earlier intention. A story that helps a reader need not become a true account of the person it models. [TC-E01–03]

The sustained argument here is that the narrative **preserves causal competence while interrupting transfers among result, cause, reason, meaning, worth and authority**. Some transfers are first-person projects: Aoi tries to make controllable achievement answer whether her life is valuable. Others are interventions: Fumiya treats shared mastery or understanding as standing to demand trust; a collective tries to produce a particular reader's response; Kikuchi wants the evidence to choose an answer she will not have to own. These are related problems without being identical personalities or one universal error. Their effects can be beneficial, harmful and unfinished at once. [TC-E04–07,12–16]

The ending allows a more limited practice. One can keep looking carefully, acknowledge what the records do not decide, choose what one wants to offer, and receive something without making it an exhaustive explanation. This possibility has positive content: ordinary company, particular care, play, craft judgment and a reader's slight self-liking become available. It does not establish a new law guaranteeing recovery. Aoi's unresolved questions about her sister, the material storm damage, the withdrawn surrogate offer and the unplayed final match remain boundaries, not blanks that the thematic argument may fill. [TC-E10–17]

## 2. Cause, reason, result and meaning have different objects

These are working distinctions, not an assertion that the novel employs every Japanese word consistently as a technical term. The source uses `原因`, `理由`, `意味`, `祈り`, `正しさ` and related forms in particular speakers, times and tasks. JVL owns those local attestations. [TC-E01; JVL §13.5]

| Question | What would answer it here | What does not answer it by itself |
|---|---|---|
| What happened? | An action, result or record at an identified narrative level. | A later expectation, metaphor, private hypothesis or planned unwritten episode. |
| What helped cause it? | Relevant observation and a warranted relation among conditions and effects. | Mere sequence, an effective retelling or one person's confidence. |
| Why did this actor act? | Attributed avowal, represented interiority or a bounded inference tied to access. | Another person's preferred interpretation treated as complete motive. |
| What could have prevented it? | Evidence sufficient for that counterfactual under stated conditions. | Present guilt, an imagined better action or the wish that control had been possible. |
| What does it mean to someone now? | A present act of interpretation, valuation or use. | Proof that the earlier event was necessary, deserved or intended to carry that meaning. |
| Is the account or intervention permissible? | Separate attention to standing, scope, permission, consequences and alternatives. | Its causal accuracy, sincerity, beauty, popularity or emotional benefit alone. |
| What makes a person worth caring about? | A value judgment and particular relation that must remain visible as such. | Rank, a single result or an exhaustive causal explanation. |

A scene can supply several kinds of evidence. The same sentence can state a reason and attempt to make someone comply. A represented tear establishes an effect, not all its causes or the correctness of the words that elicited it. This is why the literary argument has to track voice, access and time as closely as plot. [TC-E02–03]

## 3. Early success earns confidence within a domain

Fumiya's initial mastery supplies real grounds for confidence. Repetition and correction have changed his play. Hinami can therefore challenge the exemption he has made for life: he treats one world as learnable while dismissing the other before testing it. Her practical teaching then produces observable changes in his available conduct and encounters. The novel gives those effects room to count. Social method is not discredited merely because another person taught it, and genuine pleasure does not have to precede every useful experiment. [TC-E04; TC-P01]

The limits begin when success in a task becomes proof about a whole domain of value. A social technique can make a conversation possible without deciding whom Fumiya should love. A victory can resolve an agreed match without proving the winner's ethical philosophy. A route that makes Yuzu's learning useful to Nakamura does not by itself prove a theory of emotions as independent ends, and showing that emotion can be calculated does not make the emotion unreal. V03 and V04 thus concern both the reach of causal explanation and the standing to select a life. [TC-E04–05; TC-P02]

This is also a critique of a tempting binary: if desire has causes, it is fake; if it is genuine, it is beyond explanation. Learned forms can produce opportunities for curiosity and later owned liking. Influence can be real without exhausting the resulting relationship. The account of friendship in V12 makes this explicit: Aoi was one initial motive for Mizusawa's approach, while later attachment to Fumiya is also real. One discovered cause is not the whole current relationship. [TC-E05; TC-P02,11; EFF §§23/80]

## 4. Knowing that chance exists is not the same as living with it

Aoi's private middle-school account already recognizes luck and events outside her control. It also explicitly includes emotion within reasoning. A later description in which she first discovers unpredictability or feeling through Fumiya would therefore be false. The consequential question is how she accommodates what cannot be made dependable by correct action—especially when a result has become the proposed answer to her own worth. Conceptual knowledge of a limit need not make its emotional consequences bearable. [TC-E06; TC-P03]

The V06 game supplies a bounded example rather than a complete psychic mechanism. Fumiya observes her taking certain damage and models it as preferring a calculable loss to uncertain risk. His inference is situated within this play. It is not Hinami's private universal admission, not a diagnosis and not proof that she never takes risks. Their rivalry also produces real excitement, attachment and changing local results. A causal interpretation may illuminate a decision without exhausting the chooser. [TC-E06; TC-P04]

V08 gives a different discrimination. Fumiya's early risky combo succeeds, while a later effort to avoid a small mistake costs him an opportunity. Ashigaru's model concerns the relation between practiced reliability, pressure and a single format. Neither a successful first choice nor a lost series alone settles the quality of every decision. His aggregate forecast is not an aggregate experiment. The narrative makes actual result, predicted frequency and interpretation disagree productively; none is entitled to impersonate the others. [TC-E07; TC-P05]

## 5. Formation explains without erasing ordinary life

The retrospective family material changes what the reader can understand about Aoi's project. The first mathematical result matters as a hopeful discovery of agency: something she did could improve a life. Later competition offers fields in which effort, analysis and individual responsibility seem capable of producing a reliable proof. Yet formation is not a master key that makes all her subsequent action merely a symptom of one original event. Enjoying games with sisters, wanting an inhabited summit, valuing Tama, feeling attraction, taking pleasure in a rival and receiving birthday care remain actual affective possibilities. A result-regime account is weaker, not stronger, when it declares all such experiences unreal. [TC-E08; TC-P03,06]

The V08.5 team story is important here. The team shares a declared national goal and exerts itself, but the meaning of failure is not identical for Aoi and her teammates. Their regret, commitment and answerability have their own force. They cannot be reduced to inefficient supporting material for her proof. The shift toward individually accountable ranked play changes the conditions of control available to her; it does not establish that a team could or should have guaranteed her value. [TC-E08; EFF §53]

The supplemental and retrospective layers have different access rules. Precontact aspiration is reader knowledge at that story clock, not a desire nanashi already received. Rena's age-seventeen retrospective account, adult year-end narrative and parallel/VR material also cannot be collapsed into one contemporaneous clinical formation story. This study uses the all-fourteen responsibility map without promoting an alternate continuity or later private reflection into earlier public evidence. [TC-E00,08; EFF §§52–55/current source controls]

## 6. Two temporal answers to an irreversible loss

Yoko's attributed meaning-making and Aoi's attempt at antecedent control differ in direction. In the focalized historical account, the mother can make a past loss part of a story that allows continuing life. Aoi seeks conditions under which correct conduct can secure a good result before it happens. Both can sustain action; each can also press a whole life toward a single answer. Their similarity is a literary comparison of practices, not an identification of identical motives, beliefs or causal responsibility. [TC-E09; EFF §72; TC-P07]

Yoko must remain a person with affectionate care as well as injurious meaning-making. Current warmth and the birthday video matter. In V12 the visual trigger is the room/scroll context encountered through the video, not merely seeing her mother's face. This distinction prevents a falsely generic account in which any maternal presence automatically causes collapse. The private trigger, its remembered meaning and the mother's current behavior occupy different evidentiary fields. No current Yoko interior, complete reconciliation, malicious intention or sole causal responsibility is supplied. [TC-E09; TC-P07–08]

No named religion or diagnosis follows. A church and doctrine inside Kikuchi's fictional world can be discussed as fictional structures; they do not identify the actual family's practice. Nor can clinical vocabulary silently replace a literary description of focalized history. The source supplies love, harm, explanation, grief and competing responses without granting an exhaustive family biography. [TC-E09; current Yoko/Nagisa dossiers and CRI-EPI controls]

Nagisa's death presents the decisive counterfactual limit. Aoi can fear that she failed to prevent it and can doubt what happened; a present narrator or reader cannot conclude from that guilt that prevention was available or that death was intended. Reported moral action, targeting, ordinary sister/game life and attributed distress remain important evidence about a lived person. They do not establish the final private thought. The school-year tension also remains unresolved; an exact harmonization cannot be manufactured to smooth the retrospective sequence. [TC-E10; TC-P07–08]

This is more precise than saying the text knows nothing about Nagisa. It knows and represents particular actions and relationships, through particular reports and memories. The limit concerns which proposition those materials warrant. Aoi's imagined reunion can answer her need to address a loved sister without answering the historical question of what that sister thought at the end. The absence of that answer is an active ethical and formal constraint on the living characters' accounts. [TC-E10,15]

## 7. Beneficial causes do not confer a right to rule

Hinami's V05 intervention works in a substantial causal sense. It changes class pressure and protects someone she values. The later V06 exchange identifies revenge and attachment rather than leaving the action a purely affectless algorithm. These facts complicate, rather than dissolve, the ethical problem. The method recruits pressure, exposure and punishment beyond what causal efficacy can justify. Tama's own stop and Fumiya's partial objection belong to different positions within that field. A beneficiary is not merely evidence that the helper has calculated correctly. [TC-E11; TC-P04,09]

The correction to the apology record is load-bearing. Akiyama and other group members actually apologize after Konno leaves. The source does not require a choice between total absence of acknowledgment and completed justice. Those acts have force without providing full restitution, Konno's own apology, universal forgiveness or an institutional safeguard. Later useful Konno conduct can likewise be real without serving as retrospective authorization of the earlier punishment or substituting for her own repair. [TC-E11; TC-P09; CS §20/SOC §18/CRI §19]

Fumiya's care repeats the transfer problem in another form. His insight into Hinami and his explicit awareness of overstepping make his action understandable. They do not create a right to pass on all her life, recruit a remedy or demand her chosen end. V10's heard ride questions are real; V11's further disclosure is real; V12's private chapter gives the reader still another layer. These must not become one fully shared transcript. Exact overlap includes the earlier V10 ride, but that does not assign the whole later recollection to one scene. [TC-E12; TC-P07–08,10–11; CRI §20]

The resulting standard is not that no intervention is possible without exhaustive advance agreement. Ordinary care often begins under uncertainty, and some useful action precedes a fully formed plan. It is that understanding, sincerity and effect cannot erase the need to identify what was agreed, what was merely guessed, what remains open to refusal and who bears the cost. A causal model can guide an offer; it cannot make another person's answer unnecessary. [TC-E11–12]

## 8. V11 already contains groundless confidence

The comparison between V11 and V12 is easily flattened into proof versus faith. V11 already has Fumiya offering groundless assurance and inviting reliance beyond demonstrated reasons. At the same time he invokes the unbeaten competitive standing that gives him special credibility with Hinami. The actual defeat rejects that named credential. The contradiction is productive: groundless confidence can coexist with a claim to authority grounded in a record. Merely removing an explicit argument from a later reassurance does not explain the whole change. [TC-E13; TC-P10]

The result does not decide whether his wish to help is real, whether all his acquired skills are false, whether Aoi has no need for anyone or whether her general account of life is true. It defeats a particular warrant within an actual contest. Treating it as an ontological verdict would repeat the very transfer the study is examining. Nor should Aoi's own win be minimized to rescue Fumiya's self-image; it happened and changed their relation. [TC-E07,13]

V12 permits him to act from a want that remains after unique usefulness fails, and to receive particular partner care instead of having to prove irreplaceability. The later encounter changes the offered support and its standing. His brief proposal to become a Nagisa substitute is immediately withdrawn. The story is proposed as something available beyond one exclusive living rescuer. His assurance of enduring support remains an avowed commitment, not evidence that future grief or another failure has already been survived. [TC-E13,16; TC-P11,16]

## 9. The collective correction is real and incomplete

The four-person writing party becomes an actual plural inquiry, but its initial interviews and role-play support the proposed independent-star remedy. Mimimi's account separates being someone's first from liking her own chosen conduct. Mizusawa contributes disclosures and reluctantly agrees to one attempt at unscripted acting; Kikuchi plays Alcia within that attempt. The counterargument comes later from the written manuscript's Alcia, who accuses Chris of listening only to the desired answer. This authored self-criticism can reveal a weakness in the writers' hypothesis without becoming actual Hinami testimony or the initial outcome of the party's performance. [TC-E14; TC-P11]

The remedy initially tries to decide what the reader needs and organize observation toward it. Kikuchi recognizes the danger and changes her work. This is a positive methodological correction. It does not make every earlier inquiry harmless, remove the pressure on contributors or retroactively supply Hinami's consent. Nor does plurality automatically destroy all authority to write: the contributors reasonably defend fiction's ability to carry a desired effect. The problem is the claim that a preselected effect certifies the model and settles the recipient's life. [TC-E14; EFF §80/REL §17/SOC §17]

Kikuchi's own action must remain irreducible. She refuses the initial solo-trip companion; later she agrees to a changed invitation. Fumiya's particular request to circulate drafts receives permission, but author permission is not modeled-subject permission. His appeal to help Hinami is also explicitly conscious of securing Kikuchi's acceptance and unfair to her in that form. A changed method requires noticing these relations; it cannot be described only as the hero discovering the correct theory. [TC-E12,14; TC-P11]

## 10. Observation is material, active and corrigible

The island makes the corrected method work on things that can resist it. A neighboring cloth shade changes what a color looks like. Light, angle, wearer and movement matter, while exact names and trained discrimination still matter too. Context therefore does not imply that any description is equally good. Mitsu can correct the teenagers because she knows how to see and make something they have not yet mastered. Her refusal of some help and acceptance of a limited task through a trusted local establish recipient-specific access, not a lesson that all resistance should be overcome. [TC-E14–15; TC-P12]

The source precision at the package is necessary. The written blue-color name is correct; the attached scarlet sample mismatches it. Fumiya proposes and first opens the package with Kikuchi's assent, then later owns the proposal. His speculation about how the mismatch occurred does not identify a proven culprit. Kikuchi's later costume trial and Mitsu's skilled correction produce an effect, but none of those facts supplies permission for the original opening. Accurate observation can expose an error while leaving the investigator's standing incomplete. [TC-E12,14; TC-P12; CRI §21]

The broken birds supply a different resistance. Children can identify forms and tell their own accounts; damaged and new birds can be combined into sufficient material. Observation and interpretation make action possible without reversing the storm or making its loss necessary. There are still material limits and practical labors. The festival becomes a common occasion despite multiple meanings, not because everyone accepts the same story of cause and purpose. [TC-E15; TC-P12–13]

Mitsu's account of Andi is likewise plural and fallible. Prior practices existed; people still quarreled; they also sat together awaiting stories. Residents later adopted and altered selected elements without his total design. The theorized author who unified the island through one new myth is therefore too strong. Shared participation need not have a single shared explanation, and narrative influence need not control its recipients' use. [TC-E15; TC-P13; current Mitsu/Andi/SOC]

## 11. The marked manuscript does not reveal an entire dead mind

Andi's surviving handwritten revisions give the couple a different kind of evidence from resident memory. The represented sequence is `believe` → `tell` → `record` → `wish` → `pray`: earlier verbs are crossed out, the last circled. Those marks are direct represented evidence. Their access is mediated through smartphone translation; they have not become fluent archival authorities with an independently verified English edition. The reason for each deletion remains something they reconstruct. [TC-E15; TC-P13; JVL §13.5]

The sequence is richer than a simple progress from false belief to pure objective recording. `record` is also rejected. That does not make observation worthless; it makes an unowned, supposedly neutral transcription inadequate as the whole act of writing. Choosing what to retain, omit and offer remains an author's action. Fumiya's recollection of Mitsu's folding as prayer is his inference. Kikuchi's reconstruction of Andi's acceptance and wish for beauty is hers. Direct surviving marks constrain those readings without authenticating every inferred motive. [TC-E15; TC-P13]

Kikuchi eventually commits herself to a small prayer in what she writes. The choice follows looking without being mechanically selected by it. She cannot recover Nagisa's last thought by adding enough observation; omission would also be a representational choice. This does not reduce fiction to lying or turn wishing into truth. It identifies the author's responsibility for a finite, situated offer whose effects and permissions must still be judged separately. [TC-E14–15; TC-P13–14]

## 12. The ellipse preserves evidence while changing a model

Within the authored story, rival frameworks fail to fit retained records. Alcia does not simply discard Nagisa and Chris or declare every viewpoint equally valid. The records remain; other witnesses matter; calculation and checking remain available. The perfect-circle assumption is what fails. The ellipse makes a different relation intelligible. This supplies a literary counterexample to both rigid universalization and pure relativism: revising a framework can improve its fit without giving its interpreter ownership of all the people described. [TC-E16; TC-P14]

The evidentiary level matters. Mathematical construction inside a fantasy story does not prove a general psychological law outside it. Aoi already knows the mathematical ellipse; what is newly forceful is how she receives its shape as a possible way to live. Her response can matter without becoming an empirical necessity theorem that everyone needs exactly two supports, or a guarantee that grief and belief now occupy fixed compatible places. The focus is not another physical star, and no real gravitational mass is supplied by the metaphor. [TC-E16; JVL §13.5]

She also resists. She can identify the new fantasy with the kind of consolation she distrusts and still continue reading. The revaluation of achievements does not make all past damage good or every earlier effort right. Mathematics, track, games and public performance can be remembered in different colors rather than as identical certificates of first place. This is an actual change in received meaning, not a rewritten historical causal chain. [TC-E08,16; TC-P14]

The scene consequently gives the reader access to a possible use of a story. It does not give Fumiya the whole private chapter. Nor does it make her earlier contemptuous estimate of Mimimi the study's settled view of Mimimi. Aoi's perspective is significant precisely because it can be challenged by what she reads and feels. Making her focalization omniscient would remove the movement the chapter represents. [TC-E02,16]

## 13. Imagined address can be consequential without becoming testimony

The reunion's semantic ruby makes identification explicit: Alcia and `私` cross, and real-name Nagisa is read through the fictional Nagisa. The later magic/story overlay similarly marks authored mediation. These devices stage Aoi's emotional crossing into an address to a loved sister. They do not introduce a new real Nagisa witness, recover authentic final words or prove a supernatural encounter. The childish phrase and gratitude for birth have force in this received context; they are not a verbal technology guaranteed to repair a person. [TC-E02,10,16; TC-P14]

This distinction does not diminish the encounter to “merely fiction.” An imagined address can change what a reader can bear or say to herself, while the unknowability of the dead person's final intention remains. Love need not be a completed causal explanation. The change is ethically more intelligible when it does not require Nagisa to have died for Aoi's growth, Yoko's account to have been wholly right or the writer to possess an authorized transcript of either sister. [TC-E10,16]

The source then checks its own emotional success. Kikuchi explicitly refuses to treat Aoi's tears as proof of the words' truth or the story's rightness. She can say that writing it was worthwhile and articulate her own reason while retaining that limit. An effect can be good evidence that something happened for a reader without being evidence for every proposition the effect tempts us to infer. [TC-E03,15–16; TC-P16]

## 14. Local return, ordinary causality and an open future

Aoi admits a little current self-liking. Fumiya expressly allows that it might be temporary. She later appears among peers, responds to familiar joking, accepts chosen play and says she has stopped clinging to correctness. These are positive local acts. They are not restored presidency, resumed coaching, full family repair, permanent cure or demonstrated resilience under every later loss. Fumiya's interpretation of her public performance remains his where it is presented as inference; p0026 P267 is her self-description, not his receipt of an instruction. [TC-E17; TC-P16]

Ordinary company gives the ending a kind of causal modesty. A jostle and annoyed reply, a concerned look, a wave whose words cannot be heard and an invitation to enjoy the occasion need not solve the whole life to make participation possible now. The group is not a collective mind. Takei's volume has a useful reach and obscures Yuzu's words; neither fact makes it universally tactful. Haruka's independent reading and pressure, followed by a DM, matter as a younger sister's acts without proving complete anticipation or informed authority over Aoi. [TC-E02,17; SOC §17/REL §17]

External costs remain visible. Fumiya and Mimimi neglect event preparation while absorbed in the novel. Ashigaru's actual public work carries an event whose guest activity has not been settled, although a fallback and printed program exist. Professional competence provides a cause of the local success, not evidence that the entire novel project was costless or that he secretly designed a therapeutic return. His unproven improvisation and any career guarantee remain outside the account. [TC-E03,17; TC-P16; CRI §23]

Finally, chosen Found and an audience-facing aspiration alter the point of the next game. The narrative stops before the match. No winner can be recruited to certify this interpretation, and no later career can be inferred from the mood. The qualified positive claim is enough: action, care, evidence and meaning can continue without being forced into one proof of a person's entire value. The source leaves that practice possible and untested across the rest of their lives. [TC-E17]
