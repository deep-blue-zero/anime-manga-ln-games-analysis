# Fumiya and Kikuchi — choosing a person whose answer remains their own

## 1. The relationship's independent problem

Their relation begins with an apparent shared literary interest that Fumiya has not earned. It develops into a place where both acquire things they really want: an attentive reader, quiet company, another outing, a story made public, touch, a selected partner and a collaborator. That acquired reality does not make the beginning innocent. Nor does the beginning make everything afterward counterfeit. The first important relationship act is a reset that lets the other person choose again with better information. The later sequence repeatedly asks for harder versions of that act. [FK-E01; FK-R01–03]

**The emergent problem is how to keep choosing each other without making the choice answer on the other person's behalf.** Fumiya can choose Kikuchi and still misunderstand what time she wants, what information she needs or what her generosity costs. Kikuchi can choose him and still fear the breadth of his world, subordinate her desire to a perceived ideal or turn actual people into the parts of a persuasive story. Their insights into one another often help. At other times, insight makes unilateral action feel justified. Love supplies a reason to undertake the work; it does not allocate its finite resources or confer authority over every act done in its name.

This problem belongs to the relationship because one person's move changes the other's available answers. Fumiya's growing world is partly what Kikuchi likes in him and partly what leaves her waiting. Her unusual interpretive power is partly what lets him be reached and partly what tempts him to make her art the instrument of another rescue. Her permission can be sincere and costly. His refusal of Rena can be real while his account of that contact remains inadequate to his partner. No inventory of either person's stable traits explains these combinations by itself. [FK-E06–09/E12]

The strongest longitudinal movement is toward **particular, revisable reciprocity**. An authored ending yields to direct discussion in V07. Unilateral sacrifice is interrupted by Kikuchi's own selection in V09. After the V11 call, apology and gratitude restore an injured personal relation before another project is proposed. V12 lets the writer stop a preselected answer, refuse accompaniment and later accept a changed invitation; Fumiya learns to name beloved conduct that does not depend on productive writing. None is a first-ever creation of all agency or care. Each is a more demanding use of capacities already present, and each retains an adjoining failure. [FK-E04/E08/E11–15]

The present endpoint is an actual partnership with a finished shared undertaking, ordinary affection and reciprocal questions about what each wants to offer. It is not a demonstrated permanent balance between romance, gaming, writing and Hinami. Their successful offering does not supply the modeled subject's prior permission, the dead subject's voice or a right to publish private material. The couple can offer an authored prayer while remaining responsible for how they obtained, represented and delivered it. Their future has become more inhabitable without becoming fully governed. [FK-E16–20]

This synthesis owns that dyadic logic. The individual monographs own the two persons' broader development; the relationship ledger preserves directional state and stable REL identities; the ensemble and thematic specialists retain independent responsibilities. The couple's literary value cannot turn every supporting person into evidence of its success. Independent root semantic/source review accepts this local relationship role; the [relationship integration review](../08%20Audits%20and%20Manifests/TOMOZAKI_PRIMARY_RELATIONSHIP_INTEGRATION_REVIEW.md) binds the exact reviewed account and canonical render. This acceptance does not open convergence, final synthesis, release or derived-model gates.

## 2. Eras, access and evidence

The evidence passes through different clocks and different kinds of access. Fumiya's narration supplies much of the interaction, including inward responses that Kikuchi does not hear. Supplemental Kikuchi material supplies a prior private life that neither early Fumiya nor later recipients automatically know. Fiction supplies an authored interior, sometimes overlaid by a reader's identification, rather than a transcript of the people used as models. Their increasing trust does not merge these layers into one shared mind.

## 3. A created opening becomes a wanted shared world

Kikuchi's earlier relation to reading prevents the library from being merely a stage provided for Fumiya's curriculum. Her formation includes acquired book love, animated retelling, difficulty entering another group's game register and a habitat in which interest can be shared without compulsory assimilation. The private high-school hopes and diary connect a possible Andi-reading companion with her wish for a reader. They establish attention before Fumiya possesses that knowledge. They do not establish an already completed romantic choice, or make his later misleading premise harmless because she might have liked him anyway. [FK-R01]

Fumiya initially benefits from her mistaken belief about his reading. When he admits the mismatch, the apparent easy route ceases to be the governing criterion. He withdraws what the error generated rather than using a further tactic to preserve advantage. Kikuchi's decision to continue conversation is her response to the changed information. The relationship's first adequate form is therefore neither pure unmediated destiny nor successful manipulation. It is an opportunity whose terms have been reopened.

V02 then does something an honesty-only account would miss: Fumiya actually reads. A book ceases to be only a password and becomes something he can discuss from experience. Quiet library presence acquires value. This is a relational conversion because the activity gains force through another person's attention while becoming an interest he can inhabit himself. Neither person simply supplies the other's missing half forever. A new practice creates more possible speech and company than the original false premise contained. [FK-E01/E02; FK-R02]

V03 makes the recipient's experience consequential. Kikuchi can hear discontinuity between an inhabited account of something Fumiya enjoyed and material prepared from outside that experience. Her observation is not an omniscient verdict about all social form. Some preparation assists an outing; other preparation produces distance for this listener. Fumiya has to let her account change his use of technique without treating it as a demand that he recover an untouched original self. The later hybrid incorporates something she helped make visible while retaining skills received elsewhere. Their relation changes his model of authenticity before either has chosen dating. [FK-R03]

Reciprocal initiation matters as much as interpretation. Kikuchi asks for another outing and bookstore time; Fumiya recognizes that ignoring her initiative would itself violate the reciprocity he values. Their continuing V04 contact is not an uncompleted assignment waiting for escalation. V05's wider support work lets her see more of his social world and contribute something of her own. Quiet affinity expands into cooperation without requiring that she become universally sociable. The emerging relation has agency in both directions and remains incompletely understood by both. [FK-E02/E03; FK-R03–04]

## 4. Supporting an author without becoming her author

By V06, shared reading becomes work around Kikuchi's writing. She entrusts more than one story to him and accepts particular feedback. His admitted limits as a critic matter: respect for her work does not make him an expert with final jurisdiction. He can help translate what the work offers to the class, identify a practicable route and protect the possibility of declining its public realization. That is substantial collaboration. It would become a different relation if his enthusiasm replaced her decision about the work. [FK-R05]

V07 tests the risk more sharply. Fumiya can initially mistake her strained attempt at broad school participation for a version of his own successful growth. The surface resemblance obscures a difference in wanted destination. Her ideal of the world's proper arrangement can exclude the very life she would like to inhabit. His later support becomes more adequate when it allows a chosen habitat and a writing aspiration rather than universal adaptation. He does not create her integrity or discover every preference she has. He helps make an option legible that she must select. [FK-E03; FK-R06]

Her authorship is neither absolute control over actual people nor empty control over text. She revises, rejects, retains and finishes particular versions; he supports without acquiring those decisions. Yet her impulse to write an ideal arrangement also reaches into romance. The public Libra–Alcia ending is an intentional indirect refusal, and a literary account must count it as such. It is not a context-free spoken no covering every possible direct question. Nor is it meaningless because another version or desire exists. Public and private versions have different histories and recipients; a later private ending cannot rewrite what the class performed. [FK-R07]

The direct exchange exposes genuinely conflicting wants. Kikuchi calls the self-suppressing ideal sincere and beautiful, then cries that she cannot forget the luminous world opened by honoring her own feelings. Fumiya sees a reverse route to his own problem of skill and desire. That recognition is helpful because he remembers receiving a criterion from her. It is also an argument he now uses to obtain something he wants. He explicitly narrates employing a confident tone to reassure her and secure his own desired relation. The persuasive form does not automatically make the request fraudulent; its legitimacy depends on the information introduced and the answer she can give. [V07 p0029 P138–180/P208–319]

He introduces his choice as something she cannot privately write for him. At P319 he asks to date her. At P370–373 she smiles, nods and answers through the key metaphor. The passage does not contain a separate literal affirmative to quote. Its speech context and embodied response nevertheless establish acceptance. This is a particular movement from authored proxy through direct reciprocal disclosure to a direct request and received consent. It is no general instruction to decipher refusals until they become yeses. [FK-E04; FK-R07]

Their complementarity is a locally persuasive account, not a final compatibility theorem. They give one another standards they could not supply alone; they are not therefore exempt from finite time, incomplete knowledge or future disagreement. V09 will explicitly make their first special reason inadequate. The V07 scene is not thereby exposed as false. It establishes a relation whose subsequent practice has not yet been done.

## 5. Ordinary happiness is a baseline, not spare evidence

The first Christmas matters because the later difficulty concerns a life they have already learned to enjoy. A small, unimpressive snowman can be worth making because it is theirs. Their laughter and photograph do not need public recognition or excellent execution. A camera operation learned in another social context serves a memory Fumiya independently wants. Learned form and wanted affection coexist in an ordinary act. [FK-R08]

The scheduling exchange is a small successful correction. Fumiya wants the next meeting and proposes New Year time, then offers to substitute it for an earlier plan because he worries about taking too much of her time. Kikuchi says she wants both. He accepts. The same future partner who will decide too much under guilt can hear a low-pressure preference and revise his assumption. The later failure therefore cannot be reduced to a permanent inability to listen, or to Kikuchi never saying what she wants. Their practices work under some conditions before heavier conditions expose their limits.

Their private information remains uneven even during happiness. Kikuchi's conversation with Hinami has different access for the reader, Yuzu and Fumiya; his decision not to inquire combines enjoyment of the mood with a sense that it is not his place. It is local restraint, not a complete privacy doctrine. The private supplemental Mimimi conversation is another separate route. Kikuchi knows Mimimi confessed and continues to love him; she respects the feeling without surrendering her own partnership. Fumiya does not automatically receive that conversation by dating her. By V09 he hears a report of a recent Kikuchi conversation and shared reasons for liking him, but exact scene identity, confession-disclosure knowledge and private narration remain unestablished. [FK-E05; FK-R09]

V08's bids are correspondingly concrete: more time, a hand, knowledge, inclusion and support for an authored ambition. The award wish is Kikuchi's wanted future at a test stage, not a later guaranteed publishing career. Fumiya's own expanding gaming, work and friendships are real wanted goods. The problem is not that any one of them must be illegitimate. Repeatedly handling collisions as isolated scheduling incidents lets the aggregate relationship communicate that Kikuchi will absorb what is left. Affection can be sincere at every individual invitation while capacity is still poorly governed. [FK-E06; FK-R10]

Rena adds an independent information problem. Fumiya's outward refusal of sex and later removal of unwanted touch remain real regardless of inward attraction. He is not responsible for her decision to disregard refusal. He is responsible for giving his partner a materially inadequate account of the continuing channel. A technically true fragment can leave out precisely what changes another person's understanding of risk. The notification at the V08 endpoint does not reveal a completed affair; it exposes how little his description communicated. Kikuchi's later acknowledgment of unpermitted phone access is a separate act with a separate owner. These wrongs do not cancel one another or turn the conflict into a tally of who is worse. [FK-E07; FK-R11]

## 6. V09: neither sacrifice nor closeness can choose for both people

The first V09 repair separates time scarcity, omission, contact, jealousy and the exceptional Hinami relation enough for conversation and apology. A decision to seek more time is substantive. It does not retrospectively provide every omitted detail or establish all future boundaries. The wording request to Rena is narrower still; her apology is local, and the subsequent image and touch are different episodes. The couple's repair cannot be inferred from a third party's momentary response. [FK-R11]

Fumiya's later simplification is a serious act of will in the wrong direction. He leaves peer structures and removes valued connections as if their deletion could settle the clash. Mimimi makes their lost value visible and tells Kikuchi what he has been doing. Her help is neither neutral nor costless: her love continues. She changes what the couple can know without acquiring the right to select either answer. Her already initiated leaning is later permitted for a brief continuation; that narrow assent should not be expanded backward or forward. [FK-R12/R09]

The subsequent approach to Kikuchi does not begin with a completed solution. Fumiya still sees a choice that may require ending the romance; he inwardly selects continued involvement with Hinami if the two cannot coexist. The seriousness of that priority must not be softened into a harmless abstract friendship or converted automatically into a romantic classification. Kikuchi hears that some commitments remain unchangeable. Her answer is consequential partly because the practical asymmetry is real. [V09 p0031 P24–85]

She insists on choosing too. Her statement that she had not chosen anything belongs to this present self-revision; it cannot literally erase her earlier invitations, creative decisions or V07 acceptance. She asks to choose the actual expansive Fumiya rather than the smaller substitute he makes on her presumed behalf. She finds meaning in his willingness to change for her and asks him not to perform that destructive change. Her selection does not promise that all future costs will be bearable or that jealousy has become invalid. It gives her a voice in deciding what the partnership should preserve. [FK-E08; FK-R12]

Touch then becomes both pleasure and communication. Kikuchi takes his hand; he later draws her into an embrace. They can laugh over an escort of only a few dozen meters and share the embarrassment. Fumiya describes specialness as something they will seek through future practice, potentially including anxiety and injury, rather than a certificate held before dating. The claim is provisional even in his narration; it is not a completed policy or an objective guarantee that any hurt is necessary. [p0031 P127–220]

The bodily ending adds a distinct form of knowledge. After the kiss Kikuchi spontaneously asks for another, then withdraws the request in embarrassment; no repeated kiss is narrated there. Still holding his hand at departure, she says she may know him less than Hinami and asks whether only she knows his kiss. The question seeks a particular intimate place without asserting total knowledge. No explicit answer is shown. Neither the question nor bodily warmth proves exclusivity as an external fact, exhaustive disclosure or standing physical consent. It does establish desire, initiative and insecurity coexisting in the person who has just chosen him. [FK-E09; FK-R13]

This is why the repair cannot be reduced to either submission or complete equality. Kikuchi changes the terms by refusing to let Fumiya author her loss for her. She also accepts a relation containing a difficult priority that has not disappeared. His restored affection matters without making that distribution fair by declaration. Their new task is to practice a relation they both select, including the right to object when the terms hurt again.

## 7. A wider shared world produces reciprocal jealousy and real costs

V10 brings Kikuchi into more of Fumiya's peer, family, travel and gaming life. Her fear is not proof that she should remain protected in a quiet dyad. She chooses entry and develops relations that are not merely extensions of being his girlfriend. Fumiya can make room to decline while wanting her there; she can be frightened and still want the experience. The relation gains a social world rather than simply testing whether she can endure his. [FK-R14]

The train incident makes jealousy reciprocal. Mizusawa catches her because of an actual movement; the source does not make her stage an accident to provoke her boyfriend. Their later discussion exposes feelings in both directions. Fumiya has to recognize in himself something he has found difficult to answer in her. That changed position supplies understanding, not an exact equivalence between every third-party tie or a new right to restrict all contact.

Kikuchi's later decision to yield the private ride to Fumiya and Hinami is chosen and costly. Her jealousy and her wish for that arrangement coexist. A refusal to recognize the choice would erase agency; a claim that the choice makes jealousy costless would erase the person choosing. The permission concerns her participation and relationship interests in that event. It is not Hinami's permission to distribute private history, an onward-fictionalization license or permanent authorization of every future use of the couple's time. [FK-E10; FK-R14]

Their collaboration also has multiple sources of labor. Kikuchi's writing does not absorb Yuzu's coordination, the food and craft work, message gathering, Mizusawa's negotiation or another professional's technical work. A couple who can create together can still become dependent on others' practical capacity. The later school-event neglect will make that external cost direct. The broader social world is both something worth sharing and something that can be used unfairly if their project is treated as its unquestionable center.

## 8. V11: being the partner does not guarantee being heard

Kikuchi's inquiry into Hinami does not stay safely inside literary appreciation. Haruka participates, asks questions and prevents an interruption; she is not simply interrogated through an explicit stop. Her participation does not establish informed anticipation of every emotional impact. The interpretation can be perceptive and injurious. Kikuchi's later acknowledgment of creative absorption makes the harm her own problem rather than a disturbance Fumiya's rescue goal automatically justifies. No apology shown there completes family repair or permissions. [FK-R15]

The publishing approach is conditional and the writing reason is unsettled. That matters when Fumiya, after his defeat, answers the partner trying to reach him. Her care is offered while she has difficulty of her own. She says she is his girlfriend and attempts reassurance; he hears enough to recognize both gratitude and her hurt. When she admits that describing him and Hinami as mutually special is painful, he cannot turn that injury into evidence that she lacks standing to speak. He nevertheless does so, telling her she cannot understand and asserting isolation inside an ongoing relation. His inward recognition of shame does not deliver the apology his words withhold. [FK-E11; FK-R16]

Her statement that her words are not reaching him is an audible failure of reciprocity. The call then ends; the source does not identify who disconnects it. Giving her a deliberate unilateral hang-up would invent an action to strengthen agency that is already present in what she says. Giving his inward shame to her as heard remorse would invent a repair. The injury requires neither addition. [V11 p0033 P76–98]

Her fiction reaches him through another position of reception. The chapter's Alcia interior is authored; Fumiya's changed understanding is actual reading. He can be moved by the configuration without the work becoming an authenticated transcript of Hinami's mind. This distinction is essential because he immediately sees another possible use for the power that has reached him. The effect demonstrates communicative capacity, not unlimited epistemic or ethical authority. [FK-E12; FK-R16]

Reconciliation begins with apology and thanks. Fumiya acknowledges that he may have refused any direct formulation in that state. Kikuchi's continued response is her chosen act, not automatic girlfriend service. Only then does the renewed request appear. He has a reason to reach Hinami and lacks a method; she has a powerful method and lacks a writing reason. That complementary picture makes cooperation imaginable. It also creates a dangerous temptation: he momentarily thinks that perhaps this was why he dated her. The thought is direct narration of that moment, not the recovered historical cause of their romance. Their earlier ordinary relation and independent creative life prevent the analyst from adopting it as fact. [V11 p0033 P520–611]

Kikuchi accepts a starting reason received from him while wanting to find one of her own. She then rejects both an exclusive writer and an exclusive couple as the sufficient unit of rescue. Other people's experiences must matter. This widens the work, but the V11 ending is still a proposal. It does not show that the named friends agree, that Hinami permits the work or that the story will be finished and read. The couple has repaired enough to ask another question together; it has not acquired collective jurisdiction over the answer. [FK-E13; FK-R17]

## 9. V12: received care, persuasive unfairness and a stopped answer

The opening care exchange is a couple scene before it is a means of returning to the Hinami project. Kikuchi challenges Fumiya's self-exhaustion and the arrogance of acting as if he alone must do everything. She asks him to protect his own time and offers physical comfort; his relief is real. Receiving this does not mean that he lacks agency. The expectation that he must generate every sustaining resource alone has already failed. Partnership becomes something he can receive rather than another performance of usefulness. [FK-R18]

The agreement nonetheless contains pressure he directly knows. He wants to help Hinami, and he narrates knowing that this formulation will secure Kikuchi's acceptance and recognizing its unfairness (p0011 P96–98). A merely benevolent summary would omit that knowledge. An account of pure coercion would omit her actual warning, response and affection. The more exact relation contains a sincere end, a particular chosen response and an unequal persuasive position. Permission here is real without becoming a permanent partner-approved priority. [FK-E14]

Nor does the wider party automatically cure their tendency to choose an answer for another person. Author permission for particular unfinished-chapter circulation is received. Mimimi's account, the embodied race and Mizusawa's role-play introduce material the couple did not create. Their first use makes the independent-star conclusion attractive; it does not automatically refute it. The later authored Alcia challenges how Chris has assembled other people's voices toward an answer prepared in advance. This accusation occurs inside Kikuchi's fiction, not as new testimony from the actual Aoi. Mizusawa's reluctant role-play agreement likewise is not a renewed confession to her or a disclosure of her actual present mind.

Kikuchi's stop is therefore a real collaborative act. She can identify that they are steering toward an answer rather than finding one, admit the danger of repeating the retrospective affirmation she criticizes and refuse to continue on those terms. This is neither the first time she exercises judgment nor a renunciation of every story intended to affect a reader. The party's counterarguments preserve a legitimate role for message-bearing fiction. What fails is the confidence that this desired conclusion has already earned its authority over this recipient. Fumiya must let a contribution that obstructs the project still count as help. [FK-E15; FK-R18]

The new block also makes the relation's dependence visible from her side. Kikuchi asks whether she has value without writing, since stories and words have connected her to the world and to him. His immediate assurance that he likes her as a person is sincere; it does not adequately reach the question she is asking. A reply can be loving and still fall short. Her concern is not simply how to produce the next chapter efficiently. It is how to inhabit the relation and world if the capacity through which she has approached them fails. [p0013 P502–545]

She decides on an island inquiry to seek a writing reason of her own. Fumiya offers to accompany her; she refuses because his presence might supply another borrowed reason, and he accepts the refusal. Later his changed invitation gives his own wish to see her and waits for her answer. The eventual acceptance neither invalidates the earlier no nor proves that refusal was only a test. The request's purpose and relational position have changed. She can own the inquiry while also wanting company. Independence need not mean refusing every received resource, any more than partnership requires accepting every proposed form of help. [FK-R18–19]

## 10. The companion is also an ordinary person, and the island is not theirs to use

The journey lets them exist beside the work. Ferry logistics, a date, descriptive play, competing perceptions, mistaken color, food, excitement and awkward lodging give the couple a shared day rather than only a theory of rescue. They can observe the same thing differently. His mistake does not defeat perception in general; her better answer does not make her an infallible interpreter. A shared room is not evidence of a sexual act or a proven hidden lodging plan. These limits protect the actual pleasure from being inflated into a more complete intimacy than the source supplies. [FK-R19]

Later Fumiya names particular things he loves: her laughter, unexpected competitive word choices, excited behavior and the rice ball she divided. That answer differs from the earlier true but generic reassurance. It gives this recipient evidence that his attachment reaches conduct beyond useful writing. Her initiated touch is actual reciprocity. The writing problem has not thereby been solved, and neither is the whole relationship. The scene makes it possible to remain wanted while a productive role is in doubt. [FK-E15]

The island's people have purposes before the visitors arrive. Mitsu's craft deadline, ordinary maintenance, refusals and trusted scoped delegation do not exist merely to instruct a blocked writer. The couple initially leaves after a refused interview and later negotiates access. Age or visible difficulty does not erase the custodian's judgment. The distinction between refused unasked help and accepted particular assistance also exposes how little a general category such as “helping” settles.

Their own subsequent intrusion makes that limit concrete. Kikuchi touches the package cord without pulling; Fumiya proposes opening and opens with her assent, then later owns that proposal to Mitsu. Her assent matters inside the couple but is not Mitsu's permission. The later color trial has another initiation: Kikuchi chooses to continue, selects the colors and pins a proposal, while Fumiya supplies pins and tests movement/snag. Collective shorthand should not hide either person's actual role. [p0016 P643/P685–710/P739–790/P799]

The material fault is a sample/name mismatch. The written blue-color name is correct and the attached scarlet sample does not match it. Fumiya's swap/knot and no-blame account remains his interpretation; neither exact mechanism nor responsible actor is established. Mitsu recognizes the mismatch, accepts part of the color proposal, corrects placement and performs the expert sewing. The useful trial does not make the visitors sole experts or retroactively authorize opening. The couple's capacity to act together can produce competent contribution and a shared overstep in the same sequence. [FK-E16; FK-R19]

The festival similarly resists being the setting that unanimously confirms their lesson. Damaged birds remain damaged, practical repair is imperfect and other people participate for different reasons. The main stay on Nagi/凪島 and the tide-linked visit to Mio/澪島 are distinct locations. At Mio the elders pray, younger people take photographs and children play; their placement of birds does not imply one common theory or complete social harmony. Fumiya and Kikuchi place birds they made, including traces of dirt. Their shared presence is meaningful without being the author of the community's meaning. [p0018 P147–197]

The relation's development here is reciprocal participation in a world that exceeds it. The couple can share observations and be changed by them while the observed people retain their own ends. That is more demanding than moving from dependence to perfect observerhood. Competence in seeing color or moving cloth does not confer a right of access, and observing more of a place does not settle what story ought to be written from it.

## 11. Observation leaves a choice that neither partner can outsource

Kikuchi's observer ideal makes a powerful new method available, but Nagisa is its decisive limit. An actual dead person's standpoint cannot be interviewed or recovered by finer observation. Fumiya never met or spoke to her. He can stay, discuss, read and fear for the person beside him; he cannot provide Nagisa's answer to the manuscript. The calf-deep-water scene gives physical distress and action without certifying a suicide intention for Kikuchi or resolving Nagisa's intent at the road. [FK-R20]

The private manuscript supplies a different kind of posthumous evidence, not an exception that abolishes this limit. Its visible revisions are direct documentary marks: **believe → tell → record → wish → pray**, with the first four crossed out and the final term circled. The couple read difficult English with smartphone translation. The surviving marks are not imaginary; their account of Andi's inward journey is nevertheless an interpretation. They do not obtain an authenticated interior, final lifetime doctrine, last testimony or permission to publish the private papers. Loan, handling and return have their own conditions. [FK-E17; FK-R20]

This produces a significant change in the couple's division of work. Fumiya can contribute observations, his experience and a wanted reason to attempt something. Those contributions cannot decide the writer's remaining choice. Kikuchi's prayer must include her own wish and responsibility rather than merely execute his rescue intention. The borrowed starting reason in V11 was real enough to restart action; V12 shows why it cannot carry the entire decision. To write while uncertainty remains is an owned act, not proof that uncertainty has been conquered.

Nor does prayer create an automatic ethical exemption. A particular fiction may be moving and still expose someone, misrepresent something or arrive through a refused channel. Living sources have different permissions, and a dead source has no newly available answer. Self-awareness does not grant a general license. ## 14. Serious rivals and the current bounded conclusion

**“The relationship is only a mistake produced by Hinami's curriculum.”** The beginning's instrumental condition is real and remains part of its history. **“Kikuchi is passive, or only learns agency after dating.”** Her prior private interests, invitations, reception judgments, artistic choices and later refusals contradict the totality. **“Fiction reaches him, so the writer knows the people completely.”** The reception is actual; the modeled interior remains authored. **“The finished story solves the couple and justifies all its methods.”** Completion and targeted uptake are achieved; prior refusal, manipulation risk, information rights and neglected labor remain.
