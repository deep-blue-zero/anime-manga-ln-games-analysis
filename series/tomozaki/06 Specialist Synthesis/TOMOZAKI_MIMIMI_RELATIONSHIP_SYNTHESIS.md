# Fumiya and Mimimi — reciprocal friendship under unequal desire

## 1. Responsibility and contestable argument

This relationship is romantically asymmetric and substantively reciprocal. The first statement must not reduce the second to consolation, and the second must not disguise the first. Mimimi loves Fumiya; he chooses Kikuchi. They nevertheless make things together, enjoy a particular conversational rhythm, alter one another's decisions, receive and give help, and return to shared work. Their relation continues because more than romantic selection is at stake. It remains difficult because those other forms of attachment do not make the romantic difference cease to matter. [FM-C01; FM-L01–10]

The independent argument is that **their shared history makes certain acts of help possible, but cannot decide in advance which future acts either person may require**. Fumiya's odd seriousness becomes usable through Mimimi's response. Her expressive skill becomes a jointly recoverable performance because he can answer it. The campaign's remembered language later lets her challenge his withdrawal. Their practical partnership can preserve school work when Hinami is absent. Yet none of those achievements creates a debt of romantic choice, an obligation to remain indefinitely available, or a private authority to settle Kikuchi's interests. The relation's growth lies partly in making its forms revisable without treating revision as erasure. Its unfinished work lies in deciding limits before the next difficulty, rather than letting gratitude, guilt or familiar ease decide them afterward. [FM-C02–04,07–13]

This account is falsifiable. A purely unilateral helper relation would not explain Fumiya's recognized contribution to the campaign and to Mimimi's history, her capacity to receive particular help, or the performance that needs both responses. An account of uncomplicated equal friendship would not explain the declaration, non-selection, loss attached to the walk, or the continued need to consider an absent partner. A romance-only account would not explain the earlier camp's different state, intrinsic pleasure in the comedy, her council initiative or the shared failure to prepare the final event. These are counterexamples to rival reductions, not proof that every act is equally good or equally costly.

The [Fumiya monograph](../04%20Character%20Analysis/Tomozaki%20Fumiya/TOMOZAKI_FUMIYA_CHARACTER_MONOGRAPH.md) owns his person-level trajectory; the [Mimimi monograph](../04%20Character%20Analysis/Nanami%20Minami/NANAMI_MINAMI_CHARACTER_MONOGRAPH.md) owns hers. The [relationship ledger](../03%20Longitudinal%20Ledgers/TOMOZAKI_RELATIONSHIP_STATE_LEDGER.md) preserves directional state and historical revisions. This specialist explains what their interaction produces and constrains across time. It does not replace the coequal Mimimi–Tama ensemble responsibility, Kikuchi's own agency, the other two mandatory dyads, or the seven thematic specialists. Independent root review and the exact corrected-object rebind precede this canonical render; the [relationship integration review](../08%20Audits%20and%20Manifests/TOMOZAKI_PRIMARY_RELATIONSHIP_INTEGRATION_REVIEW.md) records the bounded acceptance and continuing gates.

## 2. Before romance: a response that changes what each can do

### 2.1 Being received is not the same as becoming universally fluent

In the early Mimimi/Tama encounter, Fumiya offers an unusually analytical response where casual talk could have demanded speed or a familiar joke. Mimimi laughs and returns the contribution; Tama's response differs. The relation begins through this particular fit, rather than through Fumiya first acquiring an audience-neutral social skill. His thought is not worthless before it is polished, and her response is not merely a reward delivered after his improvement. She makes a way of speaking socially available to him by finding something to do with it. [FM-C02; FM-L01]

The direction also runs back. Mimimi enjoys responses that return something unexpected. Fumiya is neither an empty recipient for her energy nor a perfect performer who always answers at her pace. His earnestness, delay and technical scale can become material. Their rhythm has two contributors: she supplies an opening; he supplies a particular answer; she can retort to that answer. A generic lively girl plus a generic shy boy would not explain which details later make the comedy recoverable.

This is a real resource, not a future romance already hidden in the scene. The V03 camp viewpoint later supplied by V06.5 explicitly places respect and an inability to imagine the proposed pairing before the selected feeling. An analyst who begins with the eventual confession and reads every early laugh as concealed courtship destroys the change the source records. It also makes the nonromantic content of the relationship impossible to see except as preliminary evidence. [FM-C01; FM-L03]

### 2.2 The campaign gives them a shared history, not interchangeable aims

Fumiya and Mimimi's election work makes their different capacities jointly consequential. He finds a change of frame; she can use the altered audience situation. A staged interruption and her live response reveal something that copying Hinami's established credibility could not. The local performance matters even though 456–131 is still a decisive defeat. Their shared end does not mean they experience its result identically. [FM-C03; FM-L02]

For Fumiya, defeat can be information about a strategy. For Mimimi, repeated secondness threatens to decide whether she is special. His respect for her effort initially makes him encourage continuation, precisely when the effort's function requires more scrutiny. The relational failure is therefore more exact than insufficient kindness. He takes her expressed seriousness seriously, but assumes it has the same relation to stopping and self-worth that his own practice has. Sincere recognition is useful without being an adequate diagnosis.

The correction requires another relationship. Fumiya helps make contact with Tama possible; Tama's particular standing carries the decisive recognition. Mimimi participates rather than being transported passively to the right speech. This specialist preserves that distributed process but does not absorb Mimimi–Tama into the history of Fumiya helping a girl. His contribution remains valuable precisely because he cannot replace the other person's history. The later phrase *comrade* has force because their shared effort was real, not because he privately owns the resulting recovery. [FM-C03–04]

## 3. Admiration becomes love without becoming an assignment

The Tama crisis gives each evidence of the other under strain. Fumiya sees Mimimi maintain warmth while another person's pain costs her. Mimimi sees him become capable of consequential action without ceasing to be frightened or awkward. Admiration does not require that the admired person have no weakness. Indeed, her response to his remaining uncertainty distinguishes her desired person from an abstract strong character whose specification any suitable boy could fill. [FM-C04,06; FM-L03–04]

Their shared food scene is useful because neither controls the other's response. Mimimi offers a bite expecting him to be embarrassed; his acceptance unsettles her. Her private view supplies an account he does not receive in full. The moment is playful, bodily and awkward. It cannot establish reciprocal romantic selection, but it does show a person whose familiar social method changes when she herself has something exposed. The same exchange can be pleasurable and difficult without one side being the real feeling and the other a mask.

The V06 declaration then changes the information state. Mimimi briefly makes the apparent romantic meaning deniable, then confirms it. The clarification survives the joke. Fumiya's first obligation is not an instant reciprocal feeling; it is to stop declaring her feeling impossible merely because it contradicts his self-concept. Accepting another person's stated attraction is an epistemic advance even before choosing what he wants. It also deprives him of a convenient escape from the consequences of being someone she can choose. [FM-C06; FM-L04]

Her private aftermath makes a different fact visible to the reader: declaration does not immediately make feeling easier. Checking digital surfaces cannot reveal his unspoken answer. Fear concerns both possible rejection and the possible loss of the existing bond. Those thoughts cannot be inserted into his side of their next conversation. They explain why her request for ordinary treatment is an action with a stake, rather than evidence that the declaration has become unimportant.

In V07 she asks for continuity without asking that the disclosure be forgotten. These are compatible requests. The lunch's comic food logic gives both a way to answer while awkwardness remains. Ordinary interaction is being remade around a newly known fact; it is not restored by pretending that the old information state still exists. Her visible hesitations also test Fumiya's earlier picture of effortless sociability. The person who can readily animate a group can struggle when she risks a direct personal answer. [FM-C06; FM-L05]

## 4. The public pair: a shared competence with a real ending

### 4.1 Recovery needs both partners and a field that receives them

The festival comedy is the dyad's clearest emergent capacity. Fumiya has rehearsed extensively, including abstractions that he can turn into lines rather than only word-for-word recital. Seeing the audience nevertheless makes the sequence disappear. Mimimi changes the exchange. Her improvised explanation of the pair's name ends in a conspicuous failed joke; Fumiya interprets it as help and asks what his ordinary response to her would be. He ignores the flourish, she protests his ignoring, and the audience laughs. [FM-C07; FM-L07]

The rescue cannot be assigned only to her invention. Without Fumiya's response the failed joke could remain the visible failure. It also cannot be assigned to his sudden internal mastery: the useful cue comes from another person. The accumulated ordinary rhythm gives him a response he can still make when memorized structure is unavailable. Their ability exists between them at this moment. It is history made practically usable under pressure.

Nor is the audience a neutral background. Friends provide a warm field, and Fumiya explicitly acknowledges it. Mimimi's changed projection addresses listeners beyond her partner. That public adjustment is skilled, but the recovered exchange still depends on their familiar relation. The result therefore supports neither flawless competence nor a rule that taking embarrassment will always rescue any partner before any audience.

The routine's married-couple premise is performed fiction. The surname joke and some spectators' awareness of the romantic tension do not turn it into actual dating or mutual romantic selection. This distinction is essential: a shared form can be enjoyable and partly suggest a desired future without establishing that future as agreed.

### 4.2 What the ramen claim and repeated enjoyment preserve

Afterward Fumiya apologizes and thanks her. Mimimi asks for ramen in return; he agrees. This is ordinary reciprocity inside a wanted joint activity. It gives her a claim that does not demand romance and allows him to answer without pretending no help was received. Treating the meal as a debt of affection would inflate it; describing her labor as entirely free would erase the small return she actually names. [FM-C07–08]

Their immediate success has an end. Fumiya must go to Kikuchi's play. Mimimi repeats that the comedy was fun while registering the loss of the interval. Her repetition matters: the pleasure was hers even if another hoped-for future will not follow. The analyst should not expose the fun as merely a tactic, nor use genuine fun to deny the hurt. He does not have a simple helpful answer to give her before leaving. Their ordinary companionship has acquired an asymmetry that technical success cannot solve.

This is a major limit on the relation's productive form. Being excellent together at one activity does not authorize Fumiya to select her from gratitude, and does not require Mimimi to stop enjoying the activity because he chooses someone else. The activity's value and the romantic choice have different objects. Their coexistence makes the relationship more demanding than a simple compatibility verdict.

## 5. Choosing Kikuchi: a limit that leaves a person rather than a spare option

### 5.1 Two different acts of helping at the festival

Mimimi's later direction toward the library is not a repetition of the stage rescue with a larger emotional reward. On stage she helps preserve an activity they have prepared and enjoy together. After the play, she urges him to seek a direct answer from the person he wants, at a cost to her own hoped-for route. The audience, desired result and risks have changed. Her strong language operates within the uncertainty of an indirect fictional answer; it does not establish a general right to press after an explicit interpersonal refusal. Kikuchi's later response remains her own act. [FM-C08; FM-L09]

Fumiya's refusal to choose Mimimi as consolation is a consequential limit. Gratitude is not romantic desire, and the recipient of love does not owe reciprocity as repayment for being well treated. An unowned yes would exploit the same particularity that makes her love serious. But non-selection does not supply a complete account of everything he may thereafter do. He has not disposed of a minor romance route; a valued person and shared practices remain.

Their post-selection exchange makes that distinction explicit enough to prevent two convenient endings. Mimimi still loves him; she does not want endless apologies replacing interaction; she does not promise to love him forever. No one of those statements cancels another. The future warning protects change without announcing when it will occur. Ordinary continuity is desired, not guaranteed to be effortless. [FM-C09; FM-L10]

### 5.2 The absent partner becomes a speaking person

Kikuchi is more than a condition that limits the pair. Her interstitial conversation with Mimimi changes what can be known and respected between the girls. Mimimi begins with indirect testing, recognizes the unfairness and moves to disclosure. Their common reasons for loving Fumiya produce respect alongside the painful loss of uniquely seeing him. The argument is no longer only about a desired boy; another person has a distinct relation, and her recognition can be both welcome and hard to bear. [FM-C05,09; FM-L06]

This third relation should not be converted into a treaty authorizing every later private walk or touch. Kikuchi's respect for the feeling is narrower than permission for all conduct. Nor can it be treated as knowledge Fumiya receives automatically. His later V09 conversation establishes some report of an encounter and shared attraction reasons, not the full private contents or a certain identification with the earlier café. The triangle's information is directional and changes over time.

A claim that Mimimi's love is only another contest is therefore inadequate. Comparison and the wish to recognize Fumiya uniquely are genuinely present. So are her earlier absence of selected attraction, person-specific reasons, appreciation of remaining weakness, intrinsic enjoyment, and acts that preserve his chosen relation while closing a route she wants. These positive ends cannot be replaced by an analyst's all-purpose rivalry explanation. Neither a competitive joke nor a third person's conjecture can supply her total motive.

## 6. Walking apart, walking again: a routine becomes a negotiated problem

### 6.1 The first restraint has an object and a cost

At Kita-Yono in V09, after learning more about the couple's jealousy difficulty, Mimimi proposes that they should probably not go home together that day. She leaves. Fumiya has also been uncertain. He values the time and initially believes his own firmness might suffice, but knows their history is not simply that of two neutral friends. His feeling of loss makes the routine's significance visible. The walk is not trivial because nothing spectacular happens in it; it is one of the ways shared life has been available. [FM-C10; FM-L08]

Her softening and jokes do not nullify the decision. The hedge makes a difficult proposition receivable; she nevertheless leaves separately. Neither does the decision specify an unlimited future ban. Later narration supplies approximately a week of separate departures, so the local act has become a practice. Its duration is positive evidence; it cannot be inferred merely from an initial proposed restraint.

The responsibility is not all Mimimi's. Her concern and apology do not assign every cause of Kikuchi's pain to her. Fumiya's allocation, disclosure and interpretation of reassurance remain his; Kikuchi has her own claims. The pair cannot settle the couple's prospective rules solely by deciding what feels considerate between themselves.

### 6.2 The resumed walk reverses a role without making the method innocent

Mimimi notices Fumiya's removed strap, exited chats and increasingly joyless jokes. She insists on walking together and pulls him along despite his initial protest. The walk actually resumes within V09. Her later accuracy and his gratitude do not supply prior permission for that initiation. An act can reopen an important conversation and still overstep in its means. [FM-C11; FM-L08]

She then invokes a more exact history than generic friendship. Fumiya once made an embarrassing public invitation when she was discarding a valued activity to protect another bond. Now he is discarding friends and experiences he still wants in the name of protecting Kikuchi. The analogy is not perfect and does not prove that any change she dislikes is wrong. Its force comes from his present contradiction: he has not ceased valuing the supposedly disposable parts of his world.

Her question asks whether he has come to dislike them. His inability to say yes is consequential. The critique does not require him to choose her romantically; it requires him to face the people and activities his solution treats as expendable. The removed object is one sign among several, not a magical measure of his true heart. Her wish not to see him change this way has a possible controlling edge, which the specialist must preserve alongside the corrective insight.

Fumiya initially refuses her offer of help because he thinks exposing weakness would make the person he has not selected bear responsibility for his choice. This is ethically serious and incomplete. It recognizes a real risk of conscripting her care, yet can also deny her capacity to choose a friendship contribution. Mimimi changes the address from rejected admirer to campaign comrade. That language gives another basis for speaking; it also exerts pressure through remembered gratitude. The shared history enables disclosure without making the appeal free of leverage. [FM-C11–12]

The relation here becomes more than a channel for advice. Each has a prior act of the other that changes what the present words can do. Fumiya hears not only a proposition about healthy social breadth but a response from someone whose life his own earlier act affected. Mimimi is not applying an abstract therapy to an interchangeable friend. The history gives the objection particular standing, while still stopping short of ownership over his future.

### 6.3 A requested moment of comfort is narrower than standing access

Mimimi says that she can like the self who loves him despite the feeling not being returned. That is positive self-regard already in V09, not a capacity first discovered at the ending. She also communicates some conversation with Kikuchi. Her argument names what the two girls value in his widening life, but the report cannot be enlarged into total mutual knowledge. [FM-C05,11]

She leans against his shoulder; in the following member she asks whether she may remain like that briefly, and he agrees. The request concerns continuation of already begun contact. It is neither retroactive authorization of the pulling nor advance permission for future leaning. It is nevertheless a real, bounded movement from unannounced action toward a stated need and received answer. To omit it would make her only a giver; to generalize it would invent a contact rule. [FM-C12]

Her intervention has not completed Fumiya and Kikuchi's work. He leaves with a clearer recognition of the cost but still cannot fit all the commitments together; he moves toward a possible breakup before Kikuchi's reply changes the relation. This is a limit on success, not a denial that Mimimi helped. She can make the wrong solution harder to accept without supplying the final arrangement for two other people.

## 7. V11: practical cooperation and the limits of knowing one another

### 7.1 A self-account that does not become a diagnosis of Hinami

In the group, Mimimi distinguishes confidence in her abilities from confidence in herself. Fumiya, Kikuchi and Mizusawa are recipients. Her physical seesaw demonstration also produces a small permission problem: she shakes Fumiya, he objects, looks toward Kikuchi, and Mimimi withdraws her hands. The same scene contains articulate insight and an impulsive act that needs adjustment. Skill at discussing self-worth is not a completed bodily-boundary practice. [FM-C13; FM-L11]

Her analogy with Hinami has an explicit limit. She thinks visible success and care might have rescued her sooner; she cannot explain why they have not sufficed for Aoi. Fumiya receives a valuable model from someone who has lived comparison and relational self-valuation. He does not receive a complete interior account of Hinami, or evidence that their mechanisms are identical. The dyad's value includes a correction he cannot generate alone and a limit she herself names.

### 7.2 Waiting, selective disclosure and reliance

The night conversation begins in ordinary running and teasing. Mimimi has noticed him sitting too long at a swing. She says she has sometimes refrained from approaching because he is with his sister or because he is dating Kikuchi, and wonders whether even speaking now is a rule violation. Fumiya begins to offer broad reassurance, then stops because the decision is not his alone. Their uncertainty is actual; a settled rule cannot be reconstructed from a smile. [FM-C13; FM-L11]

She then waits. He chooses to tell her some of the inquiry involving Haruka, Kikuchi and Mizusawa and the problem of a reason to write. This is selective disclosure, not a transfer of every private family fact. Mimimi explicitly says what she cannot answer before explaining an admirer's fear that the model's unhappy life invalidates the route the admirer follows. She corrects Fumiya's first interpretation. Her contribution changes his understanding rather than simply endorsing his plan.

The warranted account is selective disclosure with an answer space: she waits, he chooses a partial account, and she names the limits of her knowledge. Later he inwardly recognizes that he may be using the situation as an excuse to rely on her tact. That acknowledgment is his narration, not an apology delivered to her. It makes dependence visible without supplying a completed way of distributing it. [FM-C13]

### 7.3 Reclaiming the campaign as actual work

Mimimi proposes another attempt at the student council. This revisits the campaign through a changed end: preserving work and helping Hinami without needing to win the office that she lost. Her earlier refusal to enter in another role was real, and her later revision is real. Teacher-approved exceptional help and an acting-president joke do not establish a new election or formal succession. The practical responsibility does not become fictional merely because the title is playful. [FM-C14; FM-L11]

Fumiya again supplies planning and access to the gaming field; Mimimi initiates, addresses members and later tries novice commentary alongside Ashigaru. Their former names and rhythm are usable, but the work is distributed beyond the pair. Existing council members, Kikuchi, professional contacts and institutional permission make outcomes possible. This is neither a restoration of assigned Fumiya centrality nor evidence that Mimimi has found a predestined profession.

The V11 end also contains Fumiya's failed exceptional warrant toward Hinami. Its relevance here is bounded. Mimimi's care is one part of the plural life that survives that failure; it does not make her the alternative girl who would have prevented the collapse. V11 already includes reassurance explicitly without grounds alongside the unbeaten credential. V12 is not the first discovery that conviction can lack proof. What changes in the Hinami-facing comparison is the support offered and the standing claimed, not the first existence of ungrounded words. The independent Fumiya–Hinami specialist owns that exact comparison; this dyad must not import a linear cure into either friend's development. [FM-L15]

## 8. V12: the romantic subject becomes a direct listener

### 8.1 A contributor can change the question

Mimimi initially doubts that a story can save someone, then reads particular unfinished material after Fumiya asks Kikuchi for permission to circulate it. Her skepticism is not a refusal ever to help, and authorial circulation permission is not the modeled person's permission. The four-person work actually occurs, replacing the earlier proposal with participation. Her competitive joke about defeating Aoi and the reply correcting the aim to saving her are local comic language within concern; they establish no complete motive hierarchy. [FM-C15; FM-L12–13]

Kikuchi's question about Nana reaches the living model. Mimimi trusts Tama not to leave abruptly, anticipates possible physical separation through education or work, and wonders whether her self-liking could survive without nearby recognition. The “next Tama” is her conditional self-question. Fumiya's image of a later dependency is his inference. Neither is a chosen replacement future or a prophecy that the present bond ends. Tama is absent and has no newly invented answer. This specialist uses the disclosure as part of what Fumiya hears, while the independent Mimimi–Tama responsibility owns the full relationship problem. [FM-C05,15]

Mimimi changes the setting to the track because she thinks running may let her speak. Kikuchi agrees with a limitation about speed; Fumiya joins a competition; Mizusawa says he will watch. The sudden start is unfair within the play, and Fumiya says so. It is not a perfect contest establishing moral superiority. Nor is it equivalent to the romantic opportunism she says she refused. The same word for unfairness can describe acts with different stakes, permissions and consequences.

### 8.2 She owns a refusal without requiring him to love her

She recalls considering whether a forceful invitation to choose her might have redirected Fumiya after the play. He recognizes the moment and cannot confidently say he would have resisted. That supplies seriousness to the temptation; it does not establish the unrealized relationship as a fact. Her earlier actual redirection toward Kikuchi remains the action. The V12 account revises what he knows about the choice without rewriting what happened. [FM-C16; FM-L12]

Her reasons include both people: acquiring him that way would not be good for either, and might make her dislike herself. This is neither pure self-erasure nor pure self-interest. She still wants a person she does not obtain; she can like the self who chose an act she endorses. Her priority is making herself her own rather than making him hers. The statement provides a positive end beyond perpetual service to his life.

Fumiya and Kikuchi hear her directly as the desired person and the selected partner. Their silence and embarrassment do not establish a complete agreement, but they prevent the account being treated as a privately healed rival speaking only to herself. Mimimi does not ask him to choose again. He is not invited to reward her restraint with romance. The relation can recognize the act without changing the romantic direction.

The race does more than illustrate an argument that could have been delivered identically at a desk. Speech is interrupted by exertion; different bodies keep different paces; Fumiya's attention turns to pursuit and breath; Mimimi checks Kikuchi afterward. The group resumes listening through a changed rhythm. Yet no hidden intention to manipulate every physical response should be invented. The source gives her proposed medium and its effects. That is enough to count her as an active contributor to how the work learns.

This strengthens, rather than originates, the V09 affirmative self-account. It also leaves future conditions unknown. Refusing one opportunistic route is not a promise to wait forever, an end to desire, or an all-purpose policy for touch, time and private company. It cannot supply advance consent to the earlier pulling. The relationship has acquired a more explicit account of one chosen act; it has not acquired a completed constitution. [FM-C16–17]

### 8.3 The author's response is part of the event, not the final verdict

Kikuchi initially hears the self-account as a way for Nana to become a self-luminous star. Mimimi's jargon joke does not establish that she endorses the author's complete theory. The larger project later stops because its interviews have been steering toward a preselected answer. Mimimi's defense that stories commonly carry a message is reasonable in general, but does not settle the particular subject's risk. Kikuchi owns the stop. [FM-C15,17; FM-L13]

The new account therefore cannot be used as a final claim that all relational support is inferior or that everyone must become self-sufficient. Mimimi's chosen conduct is grounded in actual relations, and the story's own method changes after the initial star solution. Hinami's later private surprise at Nana exposes a limit in her model of real Mimimi. It does not make every composed Nana speech another real Mimimi conversation, or give Fumiya and Mimimi that private reader knowledge. [FM-C05,17]

## 9. Helping together can displace somebody else's work

The final school event is an essential negative test of the pair's shared competence. Fumiya and Mimimi stand anxious in the wings. Fumiya narrates that both the acting-council work and Brain work have been untouched while their attention was absorbed in Kikuchi's novel. The guest is fixed; what they will do has not been settled. His prepared monitor/projector fallback is real, and Mimimi questions its adequacy. Her retort does not establish that she fulfilled her own part. [FM-C18; FM-L14]

Ashigaru makes the occasion work through an entertaining account and an exhibition plan. That successful output cannot be credited entirely to the pair's preparation, and does not erase the neglected obligation. It also does not establish when he composed his talk, a quantified wage loss or an unshown pattern of unpaid exploitation. The defensible cost is narrower: a wanted intervention crowded out actual work, and another participant's professional capacity carried the occasion.

This changes how the earlier helper problem should be carried forward. Mimimi is not always the person absorbing everyone else's cost. Together the pair can also displace cost onto someone outside their immediate concern. Fumiya is not merely receiving care from a perpetually reliable organizer. Their shared energy, history and good end do not guarantee reliable allocation. The earlier campaign and V11 initiative remain real; the V12 failure tests them rather than erasing them.

The closing ordinary network also matters. Mimimi returns to the audience as Fumiya goes onstage, participates in familiar contact beside Hinami and Tama, and does not become merely a spectator of the boy she lost. The event does not complete her vocation, end love or settle the dyad's routine. It gives a continuing social place with a practical failure still visible. The shared life is valuable enough that it needs governance; its value is not a reason to stop asking who does the work.
