# Romance, care and the limits of acting for another person

This canonical administrative derivative has passed independent review at the R09_5 local literary scope. The [ensemble and thematic integration review](../08%20Audits%20and%20Manifests/TOMOZAKI_ENSEMBLE_AND_THEMATIC_INTEGRATION_REVIEW.md) binds the accepted argument, exact corrections, actual read/source scopes and canonical render. Preserved authoring-history references describe their earlier stage. Cross-specialist convergence, final synthesis, literary release, distinct models and repository publication retain separate gates.

The novels make attachment a reason to act and a recurrent temptation to claim more authority than the relationship supplies. People can see another's exhaustion, offer a needed interpretation, make ordinary life possible and receive particular affection. They can also use that insight to settle an absent person's answer, move private information, obtain a performance or demand a future. The difference cannot be recovered by sorting the cast into sincere helpers and selfish pursuers. Sincerity is widespread; rights, access, costs and actual responses are uneven.

The argument of this study is that **a valuable attachment gives people reasons to answer one another without making either person the author of the other's ends. Its development is visible in particular choices, refusals, corrections and forms of received care; it remains incomplete where those achievements are treated as permission for a different act or as a guarantee of future capacity.** This is an INTERPRETIVE HYPOTHESIS across the completed admitted novels. The accompanying ethical judgments concern shown acts and competing claims, not an external diagnosis or a complete rulebook announced by the text. An intimate relation can matter before its participants have worked out how to govern it. That gap has consequences which affection does not cancel. [RC-E01–03]

## 1. Responsibility, evidence and temporal control

This specialist owns architecture §9 domain 5: romance, care, privacy, disclosure, standing, cost, consent and intervention. The three primary dyads retain their own substantial arguments. This comparison asks what becomes visible when a lover's choice, a friend's useful objection, a rejected suitor's continuing feeling, a parent's explanation and an author's attempted rescue are tested beside one another. Similar words or effects do not make those relations equivalent. No single ethical score replaces their directional histories.

The source ceiling is the fourteen admitted Japanese witnesses: main V01–V12, the locked V07 Special Edition witness, and V06.5/V08.5 at their separately established story-local clocks. The V08.5 parallel karaoke story supplies no mainline relationship event. A supplemental scene published later can show earlier private knowledge; it does not make that knowledge available to an earlier interlocutor. V12's afterword supplies no subsequent romance, vocation or test of contact conduct. The current source lock and all six cumulative owners govern retrieval. The frozen readings are historical; current CRI §§19–23 govern their specific apology, heard-question, island-name/costume, Rena-recipient and event-activity precisions.

**DIRECT** here includes shown speech and action, and identifies whose represented interior is available. A person's report is direct evidence that they make the report; its contents retain their attributable limits. **CORROBORATED INFERENCE** joins particular scenes without claiming an unseen mind. **INTERPRETIVE HYPOTHESIS** supplies this study's contestable explanatory comparison. **VALUE JUDGMENT** evaluates the means or allocation while preserving the source facts. **OPEN** marks an answer the admitted material does not provide. The claim register retains these distinctions. Being repeated by two current studies does not make one underlying scene independent corroboration.

## 2. Desire, standing, knowledge and capacity are different questions

Fumiya's choice of Kikuchi establishes a real relationship and forecloses pretending Mimimi's confessed love is simply neutral friendship. It does not tell either partner how much time every other relationship can receive. Hinami's special importance explains decisions that are costly to the couple without by itself classifying that importance as reciprocal romance. Mizusawa's sincere love does not revise Hinami's actual refusal. Rena's insight into a relationship problem does not authorize touch. Tama's pleasure in received dependence does not guarantee limitless future availability. These distinctions are not a way of refusing to interpret the bonds; they identify what each bond actually changes. [RC-E02; RC-R06–13]

The useful questions are therefore successive. What does this person want? What has the other person actually heard and answered? What information or participation can this speaker give? What costs fall on somebody who is not present? What can the arrangement sustain? A convincing answer to one does not silently answer the others. The novels repeatedly produce precisely that slippage: a meaningful feeling becomes an argument for a particular act, and a successful act becomes a claim that the whole relation has been justified.

There are also positive reasons to keep these questions separate. Someone can refuse romance and still owe a candid answer about a shared practice. Someone can receive comfort without promising love. A partner can choose a costly continuation without thereby being psychologically empty or authorizing every later burden. Calling all such agency false because it occurs under attachment would erase the very choices the source takes care to show. The proper object of criticism is an attributable overstep, omission or unsupported extension, not the existence of need.

## 3. Early intimacy is made through forms and an honesty reset

Fumiya and Kikuchi do not meet in a field free from other people's projects. His reading and approach initially carry strategic elements; her earlier observation and notes have a private history. The source distinguishes what either actually knows from what a later reader learns. Her diary and remembered attention cannot become proof that he entered the first conversation with full access to her liking. His constructed access also cannot make every subsequent pleasure false. The honesty reset changes what she is being asked to receive, and her own initiative gives the relation a history beyond his curriculum. [RC-E04; RC-R01–02]

Reading, preparation, embarrassment and the fireworks supply ways to speak that neither could create by inward sincerity alone. Kikuchi's experienced image of him and Fumiya's preparation are not interchangeable accounts of the encounter. A made form can carry a feeling while still being liable to correction. Their developing closeness therefore resists both the conquest account, in which the right operation obtains a person, and the purity account, in which any learned or strategic origin contaminates the whole relationship forever.

The same distinction appears in friendship. A greeting received by Mimimi and a shared campaign with different motives can make new actions possible before romance is explicitly named. Izumi and Nakamura's earlier school and game interactions involve their own wishes, pride and mediation. The analyst must not import their later couple status into each early act. These are relationships developing through responses, rather than an original essence finally being exposed. Where access was created without adequate candor, the later relation can become real without rewriting its origin as fully fair. [RC-R02–03]

## 4. Care can improve another person's options or replace their choice

The Tama training sequence gives a productive comparison because the friend being helped has an end of her own. She wants to change how her straight judgment reaches people; her pain and Mimimi's distress matter to that decision. A copied curriculum fails to fit what she already can and cannot do. Useful support becomes more distributed and learner-specific. Tama acquires techniques while retaining a capacity to say no, revise a joke or reject a proposed practice. The initial recording defect and the coercive class conditions remain part of the case. Skill acquisition does not retrospectively make the whole available choice field fair. [RC-E05; RC-R04]

Hinami's protective response has a different relation to the subject's end. She values Tama's correctness and can recognize limits on her right to stop the training. Her retaliation nevertheless continues beyond what the injured friend wants. Tama's refusal of revenge is not ingratitude, and Hinami's real concern does not make her the owner of Tama's injury. Fumiya's ethical challenge and Tama's stopping judgment expose this discrepancy without producing a complete subsequent repair. The source supports real attachment and a real appropriation of its standing at once.

This comparison prevents an easy moral progression. Fumiya later repeats jurisdictional risks in his own care for Hinami. Hinami is not the only skilled person whose understanding can become authority; Tama is not the only person whose vulnerability can be taken as permission. The thematic continuity lies in the change from helping a person pursue a chosen end to selecting what their life must mean or require. That distinction also cannot be applied mechanically: an exhausted or refusing person need not have a finished alternative for every particular objection to matter. [RC-E01/05; RC-R15–18]

## 5. Choosing a lover is a beginning with particular alternatives

The festival puts Kikuchi's creative ideal, social habitat and feeling into tension. Fumiya's support does not require her to become a universally fluent public leader. The successful script emerges through selected contribution, revision and other people's work. His refusal of an abstract ideal is directed toward her; the final romantic sequence involves an indirect fictional answer, an apparent refusal, a changed understanding and a particular request received through response. These facts cannot become a general instruction that a rejected suitor should persist until the refusal reveals its secret affirmative meaning. [RC-E06; RC-R05–06]

Mimimi also acts at this boundary. Her public partnership and private help do not make her a second-place prize. She has confessed in a romantic category that Fumiya actually understands. Her redirection toward Kikuchi has a cost, and her own feelings continue after non-selection. The fact that she can help does not prove she secretly wanted only friendship; the fact that she wanted romance does not prove every helpful act is a scheme. Her recipient must answer without borrowing that generosity as advance permission to keep every desired form of intimacy unchanged.

Kikuchi and Fumiya's chosen relationship has positive particularity: shared texts, a private version of a story, warmth, embarrassment, jealousy, bodily desire and ordinary pleasure. Their specificity matters more than whether an external reader would select a different match. The existence of other compelling ties tests the couple's allocations; it does not invalidate its affection. The novel's continuing problem is how chosen romance can inhabit a plural life, not whether the winning confession should end all other interpersonal history. [RC-E06/07; RC-R06–09]

## 6. The supplements give affection more than one observer

The Christmas material has ordinary preparations, mistakes, food, warmth and humor. Its position before V08 matters: it is early couple life, not the payoff of the later V09 conflict. Interpreting it as repaired trust would create a chronology the source does not supply. Kikuchi's private household and focalized recollections likewise enlarge the reader's account without making all their contents information received by Fumiya. [RC-R07]

The café exchange between Kikuchi and Mimimi is particularly important because the two girls become interlocutors. Mimimi names continuing love and a wish to act honestly without doing something wrong. Kikuchi respects the feeling and later catches her own awkward standing in offering that affirmation. The scene supplies neither a declaration of emotional neutrality nor a treaty authorizing every later walk or touch. Validity of feeling and permission for conduct remain different objects. Fumiya's later partial report about a Kikuchi encounter cannot be enlarged into an exhaustive transcript or a certain identification of every prior private conversation. [RC-E08; RC-R08]

Tama then contributes something neither member of the triangle could provide in the same position. She has waited rather than inserting herself into a difficult exchange. She asks about the result, hears Mimimi question dependence and describes the received weight alongside warmth. This response is situated in a valued friendship; it is not a universal assurance that every burden is welcome. Her earlier hidden food adjustment and Mimimi's hidden social accommodation are positive ordinary care with incomplete mutual knowledge. Each chooses a kindness without gaining control of the other's whole life. The coequal ensemble account preserves this relation independently of its usefulness to the romance plot. [RC-E09; RC-R08/10]

## 7. V08–V09: good feeling cannot perform the work of disclosure

Fumiya's widening worlds and Kikuchi's insecurity create a materially difficult relation. Awards, gatherings, games, schedules and other friends compete for attention. Calling her jealousy irrational would erase real omissions and finite time; treating every unsettling contact as a betrayal would erase his actual conduct and other relationships. The notification at the V08 endpoint is not evidence of an affair. Her later acknowledgment of looking at the phone without permission identifies a separate act. Neither partner's wrong cancels the other's legitimate boundary. [RC-E10; RC-R09/11]

Rena's advances sharpen this distinction. Fumiya can be attracted and still refuse sexual contact. He can enjoy games, need a network and seek advice without consenting to touch. Some inward objections are unavailable to Rena; the direct refusals and removals which she actually encounters must be preserved at their full force. His omitted or narrowed account to Kikuchi remains a different responsibility from causing Rena's behavior. It is possible to be wronged in one interaction and insufficiently candid in another. The couple cannot resolve those facts by assigning one global innocence score.

Fumiya first tries reducing the less central contents of his life: walks, groups and expressive routines. Mimimi sees the loss and intervenes. Her insight is useful and her initial pulling exceeds his protest; his gratitude does not create prior assent. The later request to continue leaning for a moment has a different scope and receives a particular answer. Those bodily sequences matter precisely because they are not interchangeable with Rena's acts or a general standard of welcome. Actor, history, wording, response and repetition remain indispensable. [RC-E11; RC-R12]

## 8. Reselection interrupts a unilateral solution without settling a schedule

The bridge scene makes the stakes of Fumiya's attempted solution unusually explicit. He cannot relinquish involvement in Hinami's life or Ataphami even to end Kikuchi's insecurity. He approaches a possible separation, not an adequate agreement already negotiated between equal participants. The narration preserves the importance of the relationship he is preparing to lose; exceptional practical priority toward Hinami should not be softened into a harmless hobby. It still does not by itself prove a reciprocal romantic relationship with her. [RC-E12; RC-R13]

Kikuchi interrupts his conclusion by choosing him herself. She reinterprets his effort to change for her and takes his hand. Her response has its own retrospective reason and is not merely passive acceptance of his definition. The source then supplies mutual bodily reception and a proposal that special reasons can be made through a continuing relation. Fumiya explicitly treats dating as a beginning rather than completed capture. His narration also qualifies the explanation as potentially expedient. This is a hopeful chosen practice, not a theorem that later allocations will work.

Their reselection must therefore retain two strengths and one limit together. Her agency is real; his affection is real; neither has produced a complete timetable, disclosure rule or capacity threshold. The kiss and subsequent particular request do not give universal bodily permission. His feeling of knowing everything while not knowing her thought is a focalized sensation, not authorial certification of total intimacy. Their improved relation becomes more convincing when its incompletion remains visible. The later recurrence of difficulty tests rather than retroactively disproves this achievement. [RC-E07/12; RC-R13]

## 9. Continuing love has no entitlement and need not become self-erasure

Mimimi's later position requires more than the proposition that rejection must count. She also remains a friend with shared routines, insight, comic competence and other valued attachments. Abruptly erasing all those relations can harm both friends and fail to preserve what Kikuchi herself values in Fumiya. Yet shared history cannot be invoked to freeze him in the form Mimimi loves or to reserve indefinite access. The source supplies competing real claims, not a choice between total expulsion and unrestricted intimacy. [RC-E11/13; RC-R12]

Her V12 account of refusing an opportunistic route gives the comparison a further object. She considered whether she could redirect Fumiya while he was vulnerable; he recognizes the scene and cannot assure himself he would have resisted. The unrealized romance is not thereby an event. Her actual refusal rests on the person she wants to be, and her positive account of loving him continues. It strengthens the self-regard she already voices in V09 rather than inaugurating it. Neither act promises perpetual waiting or supplies a universal future contact rule.

Mizusawa presents a different persistence problem. Hinami actually declines his confession in V03. Later waiting, restraint about information, owned pursuit and receipt of private history have different moments and addressees. His greater understanding cannot change the earlier answer on her behalf. Her eventual private reading of a fictionalized Sharif is not a renewed real confession and reply. There is no demonstrated romance and no warrant for assigning her a hidden reciprocal feeling; equally, missing evidence does not prove a total absence of feeling. His sincerity explains why the stake is real without deciding what she owes him. [RC-E14; RC-R14]

## 10. Rena: useful affiliation does not purchase bodily access

The Rena comparison must preserve her person-level breadth. Her historical peer worlds both supply genuine belonging; their loss, later rewards of demand or status, chosen appearance, enjoyment, irritation and gaming competence matter. They do not provide the school couple with her private history. Formation can explain a vulnerability without making every adult act inevitable or excusable. Her deliberate pursuit of attached or prominent people in a particular focalized sequence is evidence about that sequence, not a complete motive assigned to every subsequent contact. [RC-E15; RC-R11]

The actual limits are granular. V08 supplies a spoken sexual refusal and a distinct Saturday invitation withdrawn after his stated plans. V09 gives a narrow wording request, local apology, a later image, renewed approaches, an explicit girlfriend refusal and physical removal. Fumiya chooses Ashigaru's presence for a later consultation because he regards one-to-one contact as risky. The extra participant changes the setting but does not prevent every renewed unwanted touch. A precaution and an incomplete safeguard are both real. There is no comprehensive no-contact agreement whose terms the analyst can invent, and no cancellation of the actual refusals because contact continues.

Rena's advice about incompatibility and particular insecurity is perceptive. Fumiya can learn from it; Ashigaru does not become its hidden author. This positive capacity makes the case harder and more useful: good advice is not a credit which can be spent on bodily access. Nor is her expressed bodily framework the same as an explicit, fully formulated doctrine that speech never matters. The latter is a dangerous rival implication the analysis tests; it must not be falsely put in her mouth.

At V11 the partner speaks directly. Rena's appraising remarks target Kikuchi, while the arm contact with Fumiya is separate and immediately removed. Kikuchi says to stop, names their relation and objects to sexual remarks. Rena's amused compliment and exit end that encounter locally; they are not a general apology or durable adopted rule. V12 supplies no new conduct test. Keeping the uncertainty does not excuse the repeated violation, and condemning the shown acts does not authorize an invented later offense or permanent inability to change. [RC-E10/15; RC-R11]

## 11. Couple trust does not transfer an absent friend's information rights

Fumiya and Mizusawa can trust each other and still make an improper bargain over Hinami's history. Fumiya recognizes his lack of rights and proceeds; serious motives and perceived special standing help him rationalize onward disclosure. The ethical issue is not disproved by later helpful participation. Hinami does not become a consenting party because friends who care about her agree that knowing will help. Her selected speech to Fumiya, the full reader's private access and his transfer to another listener remain different routes. [RC-E16; RC-R15]

V12 supplies an important particular repair within the friendship. Fumiya apologizes about sharing Mizusawa material; Mizusawa accepts that use and treats some couple sharing as ordinary. His own permission has an owner and scope. It cannot license Hinami's different private information, every future fictionalization or all material given to Kikuchi. The scene's ease is evidence of an actual received act within the friendship, not a reason to discount it as meaningless politeness. The remaining third-party defect is equally actual.

The instrumental-origin admission does not retroactively reveal every meal and kindness as false. Painful mixed motives can become speakable within a friendship that now has independent value. That is a real change in intimacy. It can coexist with a reluctance to act, a one-attempt assent and unexamined information costs elsewhere. The thematic point is not that all trust is unsafe. It is that trust between two people cannot create authority over a third person's account. [RC-R15/18]

## 12. Being a helper has limits; refusal can protect someone else

Yuzu's care and Nakamura's refusal during Hinami's absence should not be arranged into generous and ungenerous characters. Yuzu wants to help and starts blaming herself. Nakamura names the hurtful treatment, ignored contact and the cost imposed on his partner; he also denies that friendship has simply ended. Takei's first affirmation and subsequent following are successive acts. Yuzu chooses to follow after apologizing to those remaining. These are different positions in a damaged group, with unequal information. Their choices do not supply a private couple agreement or unanimous abandonment. [RC-E17; RC-R16]

The refusal has a defensible object: neither old friendship nor a crisis makes renewed cooperation automatically available. Its manner still has effects on other people, and Nakamura does not possess the reader's whole family history. Yuzu's guilt is not proof of sole responsibility; her later delivery participation does not show every intervening conversation. An accurate account can value her active organization and inquiry while leaving her capacity threshold unresolved. It can value his concern for her while refusing to make him the sole author of her decisions.

V11's council substitution similarly separates a wished-for return from actual work that others choose to sustain. Mimimi can revise a former refusal without formally succeeding Hinami. Ashigaru can bargain for community contributions instead of being an inexhaustible adult resource. The later joint neglect by Fumiya and Mimimi shows that helpers also move costs outward. Their absorption in a good project does not make the displaced event responsibility somebody else's natural task. These comparisons keep finite labor within the ethics of care rather than treating it as a practical footnote. [RC-R16/24]

## 13. Family care can be warm and injurious in the same history

Hinami Yoko provides meals, touch, concern and affectionate speech. Her explanatory framework also makes experiences of success and loss answerable to an imposed meaning that Aoi cannot bear. The analysis need not deny the warmth to identify the injury. Haruka's different reception and own effort prevent turning the household into one person's invariant effect. Fumiya's or Kikuchi's wish to understand does not give either a clinical account, a complete family interior or the power to determine the mother's intention in every act. [RC-E18; RC-R17]

Nagisa's represented play, care for another child, doubt and unavailable last intention impose a harder limit. Another person's consoling explanation cannot establish what she finally believed, intended or could have been prevented from doing. The unresolved school-year tension must not be silently repaired into a single convenient biography. Neither a fictional reunion nor Aoi's later love is a recovered deathbed testimony. The family dossiers preserve these distinctions at the maximum defensible individual scope.

Other households matter positively rather than serving only as contrasts. Mimimi's mother's advice has professional experience, fatigue, humor and overheard information; she does not read the girl omnisciently. Kikuchi's domestic warmth and brother's recipient-specific behavior are their own evidence. Tama's family, craft and grandfather's quality threshold give her commitments outside emotional support. These ordinary fields make it harder to imagine that the main school relations can define every person's capacity or future. They also make care intelligible as repeated material and conversational work, not only a dramatic rescue. [RC-R10/17]

## 14. V11's failed exceptional warrant does not erase ungrounded care

Fumiya's command to believe him combines already groundless reassurance with a competitive credential: the person Hinami has not beaten claims a special capacity to support her. The defeat invalidates the named warrant; it does not establish that he has no value, that care is impossible or that V12 must invent conviction without grounds. The V11 source already names that lack of grounds. An account in which proof simply gives way to faith would conceal the mixture that makes the failure significant. [RC-E19; RC-R18]

His exceptional involvement also affects the couple. He can feel remorse inwardly while outwardly telling Kikuchi she cannot understand or help. That inward remorse does not reach her as an apology. Later fiction makes more of his difficulty available, but its modeled account is still authored. Her proposal supplies a borrowed starting reason and the possibility of plural participation; his momentary thought about instrumental origins remains his thought at that moment, not a narrator-certified account of why the relationship began.

V12's opening care is consequential and uneven. Kikuchi names exhaustion and his assumption of unique responsibility, offers comfort and asks whether he still chooses to help. He explicitly knows that stating it as his own will makes her likely to accept; the source names his persuasive unfairness. Her acceptance and offered care are actual agency. They are not a prospective agreement that she will absorb unlimited cost. This is a particularly clear instance of chosen involvement and leverage coexisting within love. [RC-E20; RC-R19]

## 15. A collaborative rescue can keep the absent subject outside its authority

The V12 project does change its method. Fumiya asks Kikuchi about sending specified unfinished writing, waits through her fear and receives a concrete permission. Mimimi reads, challenges and contributes; Mizusawa supplies his own account and a reluctant one-attempt performance. These acts matter. They do not combine into a grant of permission from Hinami, who is not present, or from every person whose material enters the fiction. The author, contributor, information subject and intended recipient have different standing. [RC-E16/21; RC-R19–20]

The first collaborative answer still aims toward an end already selected: Alcia should become a self-luminous star. Later authored resistance exposes the arrangement of other people's words toward that answer. This is not Hinami's live testimony in the improvisation. Mimimi and Mizusawa's reply—that stories do carry messages—is a serious countercase, not proof that they favor manipulation without reflection. Kikuchi's block and request to wait interrupt the project in practice. Plurality supplies corrective opportunities without guaranteeing that the desired conclusion has become revisable.

The island then makes other people's ends costly to ignore. Mitsu is already working, distinguishes expert materials, refuses access, bargains for labor and selectively trusts a local helper. Fumiya proposes and opens; Kikuchi assents and participates; Mitsu objects and revises. A corrected practical result does not remove the prior permission defect. The written blue name and scarlet sample do not identify a culprit. Storm repair, damaged birds and different residents' uses similarly retain work and meanings which are not primarily there to educate visiting lovers. The couple's cooperation can be affectionate, useful and intrusive at once. [RC-E21; RC-R21]

## 16. An offer can reach a person without making its means innocent

The manuscript reaches Hinami through differentiated acts. Delivery peers participate after earlier refusals; the intervening agreement is not shown. Aoi refuses contact and reading. Haruka leaves the manuscript, reads it independently and presses it on her sister as another bereaved person. Aoi imagines rejecting it and takes it. Calling this wholly unpressured prior assent would erase the sequence. Calling her a person without agency would erase her resistant, continuing and changing reading. The recipient has an action within the pressure without retrospectively permitting every means. [RC-E22; RC-R22]

Her private response includes anger at exposure, resistance, recollection, doubt and the fictional encounter with Nagisa. Fumiya does not witness all of it. He receives her present admission of a little self-liking, with temporariness explicitly allowed. The subsequent substitution sequence must remain complete: he offers the conventional line about becoming Nagisa and then immediately withdraws it. He cannot become the sister. The changed carrier is a story she has experienced and can return to, rather than his exclusive living claim to fill the absence. This is a changed proposal for support, not proof of a permanent stable orbit. [RC-E19/22; RC-R22–23]

Kikuchi's later judgment supplies a necessary limit from inside the project: tears do not prove the story right, and Nagisa's actual feeling remains unknown. She can nevertheless think writing it was worthwhile and articulate a reason of her own. Fumiya can receive that reason and be asked about his own future work without becoming her employer, sole justification or required successful reader. The relation is more reciprocal when each can ask what the other wants to make, rather than only how one will help the other rescue somebody else. No livelihood or complete future allocation follows from this conversation. [RC-R23]

## 17. Comparative judgments and serious rivals

**Love requires an unconstructed origin. REJECT as a total criterion.** The honesty reset, learned speech, borrowed fiction and awkward friendship acknowledgment show that made forms can carry real feeling. Preserve deception, exposure and pressure as particular defects; do not infer that later affection licenses their origin. [RC-E04/06/16]

**Sincerity or accurate understanding gives the helper the deciding voice. REJECT.** Hinami's protection, Fumiya's onward disclosure, the first star answer and Mitsu's actual refusal separate insight from authority. A good prediction can still be a bad way of allocating a person's choice. The competing error is to deny all usefulness to understanding because it cannot yield complete permission. [RC-E01/05/16/21]

**Every dependence is a failure of selfhood. REJECT as a literary account.** Tama's warmth, particular recognition, Kikuchi's received care, the ordinary company and Hinami's local uptake provide positive evidence of reliance. Preserve unequal weight and future capacity as real risks; do not turn independence into a requirement to produce one's value alone. [RC-E09/18/20/22]

**Affirming a feeling authorizes its expression in any form. REJECT.** Kikuchi's respect for Mimimi's love does not authorize all contact. Rena's attraction or useful advice cannot override refusal. Mizusawa's owned love cannot supply Hinami's affirmative answer. The source permits feeling to remain real while a particular action is declined. [RC-E08/13–15]

**Romantic priority requires all other valued ties to disappear. REVISE.** The couple's insecurity and Fumiya's exceptional Hinami involvement are not trivial. His blanket reduction damages capacities valued by both friends and partner. Reselection makes a plural relation possible locally; it does not exempt him from candid disclosure, attention or limits. No universal ranking of all future claims is supplied. [RC-E07/10–12]

**Rejection ends every further responsibility, or generosity earns romance. REJECT both.** Fumiya does not owe romantic repayment. He remains accountable for what he says, accepts and initiates in shared friendship. Mimimi can refuse opportunism without erasing her own love, and can set limits without proving spite. Past support does not buy a desired answer. [RC-E11/13]

**A useful outcome proves the method was permitted. REJECT.** The receiving manuscript, successful event, costume repair and later warmth retain effects without settling prior authority. Conversely, a defective means does not prove that nobody received anything valuable. This distinction is necessary to preserve both harm and the actual ending. [RC-E05/21–22; RC-R21–24]

**The ending establishes an ethical procedure for future care. DOWNGRADE to particular advances and unfinished rules.** There are actual permissions, stops, renewed choices and reframed offers. There are also pressured delivery, private-information defects, a reluctant performance and neglected public work. No formal future regime, durable recovery, career solution or final competitive result is given. [RC-E03/20–22]

## 18. Positive findings and open responsibilities at the endpoint

The series supports more than a catalog of failures. People can correct how they ask, discover an end with someone else, accept a refusal, make ordinary company welcome, receive a flawed story and value a person whose mixed motives are known. Kikuchi's reselection, Mimimi's owned restraint, Tama's recipient answer, particular friend-sharing permission, an author's actual stop and Hinami's limited return are different gains. Their importance should not depend on pretending they have solved the same problem or reached the same maturity.

The governing distinction is between attachment as a reason to remain answerable and attachment as a claimed license. Answerability here is an interpretation of repeated practices: listening to a changed question, identifying what one can give, preserving the other person's ability to refuse and owning the consequences of a particular choice. It is not a speech the whole cast learns, a formal procedure or a rule imposed by the ending. Many actual practices remain defective. The text's hopeful acts are therefore most persuasive when evaluated beside the costs they have not paid. [RC-E01/03]

Unresolved matters remain attributable: sustainable time and disclosure within Fumiya–Kikuchi; future nonromantic intimacy with Mimimi; her response to physical separation from Tama and Tama's unshown new answer; Mizusawa's renewed confession or stopping rule; Rena's broad future contact conduct; Yuzu's limit and shared group obligations; revenge accountability; family negotiation; Nagisa's intent, causality and prevention; represented subjects' future permissions; public workload allocation; careers, money and durability after another loss.
