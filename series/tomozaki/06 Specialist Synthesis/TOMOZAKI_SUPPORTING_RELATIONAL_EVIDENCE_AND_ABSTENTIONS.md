## Individual and member sections

### C-D01. Akiyama — `akiyama` (C)

**Era, access and recipients.** V05 reversal of the classroom hierarchy and V06 aftermath, received through Fumiya’s observation and attributed peer speech. The earlier unknown cells and later unelaborated appearances are not proof of absence. Akiyama’s relation to Konno and her relation to Tama have different recipients and histories.

Akiyama has actions beyond supporting Erika’s social position. The V05 account preserves her resentment of subordinate treatment, confrontation, injury, thanks to Hinami, and her own apology to Tama. In the closing source sequence Erika has already left before the apology. Akiyama speaks at P17–18; the other girls follow at P19; Tama’s heavy, qualified response follows at P21–24. Later criticism directed at Konno belongs to a changed dyad, not a second apology to Tama. The sequence gives a person able to participate in harm, object to her own treatment, acknowledge another recipient, and remain involved in a cold war.

The strongest bounded reading is altered standing with incomplete accountability. Resentment can be both self-interested and a real objection to being treated as subordinate; neither possibility cancels the actual apology. Calling her simply redeemed would replace Tama’s difficult assent with the analyst’s forgiveness. Calling her an interchangeable bully would erase the differentiating acts and the distinct addressee of each.

**Abstentions.** No surname-to-given-name bridge establishes that Akiyama is Yumi. Do not credit her apology to Konno, extend it to every harmed person, infer completed restitution or a stable private moral conversion, or treat Hinami’s intervention as authorization from Tama. ### C-D02. Goda Sayaka / 郷田沙也加 — `goda_sayaka` (C)

**Era, access and recipients.** Kikuchi’s V06.5 middle-school retrospective, before her high-school bond with Fumiya. Goda is an art teacher and librarian, addressed by Kikuchi in a relationship whose warmth does not eliminate the age/institutional asymmetry. The source gives her name as 郷田沙也加, with 沙〔さ〕也〔や〕加〔か〕 at part0018 P45.

Goda pursues design and collects books with interesting covers. She repeatedly selects covers she thinks Kikuchi will like while openly admitting she has not read their contents. Her teasing invitation to use her given name, recommendations, return to work, notice of Kikuchi’s tears, and engaged questions are different acts, not a single mentorship function. At graduation she has read Popol little by little after Kikuchi objected that their tastes and personalities differed. She offers that acquired common ground as a renewed possibility of friendship, congratulates her, and receives Kikuchi’s explicit happiness.

This is reciprocal revision by the adult: she does not demand that the younger reader first become a universal conversationalist. Her own aesthetic interest remains real even as she learns the contents of a book important to Kikuchi. Kikuchi interprets Goda’s choice to end conversations and sit across rather than next to her as considerate; those are recipient-level interpretations, not independently narrated universal intentions. The friendship furnishes prior selected sociability, so Fumiya cannot be credited with inventing every capacity for connection.

**Abstentions.** Do not turn cover-based selection into content expertise, infer a completed design career, use the exact age that Kikuchi does not know, or treat every teasing exchange as equally comfortable for every student. ### C-D15. Kawaguchi Mutsumi / 川口睦美 — `kawaguchi_mutsumi` (C)

**Era, access and recipients.** Kawaguchi Mutsumi / 川口睦美, V06.5 shopping and indirect-message scene focalized around Yuzu. Her contact with Nakamura predates the immediate inquiry; it is not invented for the plan.

Mutsumi meets the others, joins the shopping and food outing, and becomes the owner of the phone used to inquire after Nakamura. Konno takes it before Mutsumi’s reply; she assents after the taking. Konno then obtains her assent before sending the first message. Mutsumi later recognizes the indirect-photo strategy and speaks with some anticipation about the response. The source attributes optimism and a sense that the problem is someone else’s to her at this point; it does not make her Yuzu’s equally distressed double.

The sequence supports both hierarchy and local permission, at different acts and times.
