# Fumiya and Hinami — credibility, closeness, and the right to decide

## 1. What this relationship makes possible, and what it cannot authorize

Fumiya and Hinami give each other an unusually credible answer. Each can recognize effort that other people do not see, understand a difficult choice through the language of play, and make the other person's world larger. That credibility is productive before it becomes dangerous. Hinami's recognition helps Fumiya try a life he had dismissed; nanashi's mastery gives Aoi a possible companion beyond the summit she had imagined as solitary. Their ordinary instruction, jokes, competition, frustration and pleasure make continuing contact worth wanting. The relationship cannot be adequately described as a false project from which two authentic people must simply escape. [FH-E01–03]

Its recurring danger is the conversion of **credibility within a shared practice into authority over another person's ends**. Hinami can teach a method and therefore appears entitled to assign the life it should produce. Fumiya can interpret her pain, care sincerely and be the player she cannot beat; he therefore comes to claim a special right to tell her what to believe. Private disclosure and reliable understanding make the relation closer, but also increase the temptation to recruit others or construct a remedy without the person whose life is being interpreted. Both can be right about something consequential while exceeding what that rightness licenses. [FH-E04–07]

The relation changes through partial refusals and repairs rather than a clean reversal of teacher and pupil. V03 gives a negotiated rule for Fumiya's ends without making curriculum authorship equal. V09 suspends a comfortable routine because it threatens his feeling and his chosen partnership. V10 opens voluntary family disclosure alongside a separate unauthorized disclosure. V11 defeats his asserted competitive credential for command. V12 permits a different local offer through a jointly made story and shared play; it does not establish that the previous incursions were justified, that either person now knows the other completely, or that the relationship has acquired a durable constitution. [FH-E08–12]

This is the emergent responsibility of the dyad. Neither individual monograph alone can explain how one person's competence changes the other's options, how gratitude becomes debt, how a refusal can be both effective and lonely, or how an attempt to end solitary proof can reproduce it in another person's name. The following argument distinguishes **Fumiya → Hinami** from **Hinami → Fumiya** and keeps their partners, friends and family independently consequential. It treats romance as an unresolved classification at the admitted endpoint. Exceptional attachment is directly evidenced; narrative centrality supplies no reciprocal romantic declaration or permanent non-romance rule. [REL-C02/03/08/09/11; CRI-REL-001/003/004]

This relationship account has passed independent root semantic and source review at its local literary scope. The current individual studies remain inputs with their own responsibilities. The [relationship integration review](../08%20Audits%20and%20Manifests/TOMOZAKI_PRIMARY_RELATIONSHIP_INTEGRATION_REVIEW.md) binds the exact accepted draft, actual inspection and canonical render. No aggregate source, convergence, full-series synthesis, release or reconstruction-model gate is promoted here.

The main chain is V01→V12. The two supplements disclose histories and interstitial states; their publication positions do not make every scene the next stage of this relationship. The V07 Special Edition's main novel belongs to the chain, while `FLY mini ART WORKS` remains edition-specific visual paratext. Appended, parallel, adapted and promotional materials retain the source readings' narrower classifications. This is retrospective synthesis using completed readings, accepted individual studies, current owners and selective precise returns; it is not a fresh fourteen-book primary census. Each entry also has a disclosure clock. A private earlier Aoi can be vividly represented without the earlier Fumiya knowing her. Conversely, later matching speech or his explicit reconstruction can establish actual knowledge; it is too strong to declare him permanently ignorant of every formation detail. The correct question is what a particular recipient is shown receiving, when, and with what authority to pass it onward. The current V10/V11 attribution erratum narrows two frozen DR12 labels rather than rewriting their history. [CRI §20; JVL-08/13; FH-P10/11/13]

Evidence classes in this study are **DIRECT** addressed speech/action; **INTERIOR** represented thought available to the reader, with focalizer named; **ATTRIBUTED REPORT** an actor's account of another event; **CORROBORATED INFERENCE** a bounded account beyond one utterance; **VALUE JUDGMENT** an explicit ethical/literary assessment; and **OPEN** an insufficiently established state. One source can contain several classes. No character's fluent explanation becomes an objective law because both protagonists find it intelligible.

## 3. Recognition precedes the tutorial, but not as equal private knowledge

The online prehistory is directionally uneven. In the V08.5 retrospective, Aoi is excited to face nanashi, loses overwhelmingly and finds that defeat exhilarating. Someone already occupies the height she had imagined as a place of isolation. She wonders whether this person might share that isolation, declines to send a message because she does not yet consider herself entitled to speak as an equal, and makes surpassing him an owned target. The empty name is chosen amid this combination of aspiration, self-negation and possible company. Her interpretation of his departure from the room is hers; the scene supplies no corresponding Fumiya judgment about her worth. [FH-P07; HA-E08/09]

That sequence qualifies two easy accounts. nanashi is not merely a late tool she discovers for validating her life method; he is already an imagined other whose excellence can make effort's destination less solitary. Yet this is not a secret mutual promise or romance before meeting. Aoi's anticipation, her chosen name and her refusal to message are private reader evidence. The relationship to the imagined player must not be collapsed into full knowledge of Fumiya, the schoolboy she has not yet met.

The V01 encounter changes the direction of recognition. Hinami is socially authoritative where Fumiya feels defeated, but she does not obtain his cooperation simply by announcing success. Her claim that life and Atafami share first place surprises him because it recognizes the worth of the effort he had thought nobody around him valued. He is moved before he accepts her proposition. She explicitly rejects lying to obtain trust and leaves the answer to him. He in turn says that he has not yet believed her: he will test the game seriously before deciding. The initial agreement therefore includes skepticism and a retained gamer self. She does not create his discipline from nothing. [FH-P01 P297–347]

Their mutual standards make experimental trust possible. Fumiya believes that a serious player can recognize a serious game; Hinami recognizes the seriousness with which he will test her claim. Each gains something different. **Hinami → Fumiya** offers recognition, methods, access and a testable prospect. **Fumiya → Hinami** offers an unusually demanding participant, a master in another domain and a possible answer her existing success cannot supply. The retrospective reproducibility account later strengthens the second direction without becoming a fully disclosed V01 bargain.

This initial recognition also creates a vulnerability. The person who sees an otherwise unrecognized effort can become disproportionately credible. Gratitude and aspiration can then exceed the exact scope of the original agreement. The possibility of testing the other person's claim is a protection, but a relationship that rests on being the unmatched expert risks making a later local defeat or disagreement feel like loss of the whole bond. That risk is not fully realized in V01; later scenes make it visible. [FH-E01/04/10]

## 4. Coaching builds an option structure; choosing the end remains contested

Hinami's teaching changes Fumiya's practical alternatives. Posture, expression, speaking, clothing, observation and planned contact make actions available that he previously treated as impossible. Those methods operate through repeated time, preparation and calibration, not a single inspirational speech. They also open other relationships. Yuzu, Nakamura, Mizusawa, Tama, Mimimi and Kikuchi become people with whom he can act, learn and incur obligations. That wider network eventually gives him alternatives to exclusive reliance on his coach. Her effective contribution is therefore partly the creation of conditions in which her own authority can be challenged. [DR01–05; CS §6; FH-E02/04]

The change does not mean every target is his. V03's assigned confession crystallizes the difference between being able to perform an act and having selected the relation it is meant to create. Fumiya can confess under favorable conditions and refuses. Hinami can regard both success and failure as useful experience, while his objection concerns whether Kikuchi and the proposed relationship should be used for that experience before the end is owned. His refusal does not require proving a universal theory of authentic desire. It is an actual limit on a particular task. [DR03 §§5/10; LOC05A; HA-P04]

His first withdrawal nevertheless overshoots. Treating learned form as false, he abandons useful practices and contact, including initiatives from Kikuchi. That correction does not respect everyone affected merely because it rejects Hinami's command. The later return retains skills and distinguishes their origin from the purpose they serve. Kikuchi's account of expressive form matters to this repair: another person's reception helps him understand why a trained manner can carry sincerity. The dyad's revised agreement is already shaped by a third party who has her own relation to him. [EFF §9; TF-E routes for V03; FH-E03/08]

The resumption remains negotiated and unequal. Hinami concedes that she cannot simply disprove stable desire; Fumiya also admits that he has not proved it. She continues to demand eventual demonstration. He wants to continue life practice while excluding goals that contradict what he actually wants, and she restates the proposed hybrid. The shared gamer gesture follows an interlocutor response, not an abstract declaration that Fumiya is now free of all scaffolding. [FH-P03 P267–323]

V04 makes the veto prospective: he can approve and constrain a proposed target before its detailed route. That is a real option-structure change. Curriculum generation, however, remains largely Hinami's. Refusal and coauthorship are different powers. Fumiya's execution of a chosen route can also manipulate other people's fields without their participation. His new ability to reject an assigned end does not confer universal competence in obtaining everyone else's consent. [EFF §13; REL-C02/04; DR04 §§4/7–10]

V07's confession boundary is another distinct achievement. He removes the terminal decision from task jurisdiction, chooses Kikuchi and receives Hinami's acceptance of that limit. Her causal part in creating access remains real, but it does not own the romance that follows. The result is neither proof that all coaching was benevolent nor proof that scaffolded origins make later affection false. A learned relation can produce ends its initial designer does not choose. [DR07 §§5–7/13–15; FH-E04/08]

## 5. Shared language can negotiate, recognize, or command

Gaming is a language in which they can take one another seriously. It lets Fumiya make a competing desire claim legible in V03, lets Hinami recognize his teaching and lets both describe difficult work without abandoning the pleasure of a challenge. The problem is not that analogy itself is corrupt. Its force depends on what a particular exchange asks the other person to accept.

V03's appeal to nanashi's undefeated position is already leverage. It makes Hinami's result framework face an unresolved result and supports continued testing. It does not prove desire's ontology; the source explicitly has both speakers retain that limitation. Their practical accommodation changes the terms under which he participates. The leverage succeeds because a proposal becomes discussable and receives a bounded response. It is not evidence that game superiority can settle every future claim. [FH-P03; EFF §10]

The Tama dispute exposes another use. Fumiya names Hinami's last blow as excess; she acknowledges it, owns revenge and names what she loves in Tama. His response is not an uninterrupted delivery of a completed universal ethic. He hesitates, says that it is not bad “but,” and then challenges the purposeless harm as unworthy of NO NAME's way. The relationship-specific standard reaches something that a purely tactical objection did not. It also leaves a limitation: frank disclosure and an effective personal appeal are not apology, restitution or an agreed stopping rule. Tama's refusal of what was done in her name cannot be replaced by the two gamers understanding each other better. [FH-P04 P202–282; DR05 §10; REL §8.1]

V11 raises the stakes of that language. Fumiya has substantial knowledge and admits that logical understanding is not full empathy. He nevertheless claims a special ability to step into Hinami's weakness and tells her to believe him as the one strong character she has been unable to defeat. He **also** says that his affirmation lacks reasons, grounds and logic. The speech is not defeated because it forgot to mention unconditional confidence. Groundless confidence is already explicit; the unique competitive credential still does practical work beside it. [FH-P11 P67–93; FH-P12 P153–194]

Hinami's victory attacks that stated credential. She recalls his words and says the reason to believe him is gone. The set can decisively test who wins this encounter and whether the claimed unbeatable position still holds. It cannot test his total human worth, the truth of every care act, or whether all lives are bad games. Nor does her acceptance of the set mean that she accepted his rescue end in advance. She can agree to the contest while using it to refuse his proposed jurisdiction. [FH-P12 P414–442; LOC04]

The comparison distinguishes a vocabulary from its uses. A proposal about **how we may continue together**, an affectionate boast that seeks recognition, a criticism using an admired identity, and a demand that **my confidence should authorize your belief** can all sound forceful and game-literate. Increasing confidence is therefore not a sufficient description of development. The force of a sentence and the standing it claims must remain scene-indexed. [JVL-03/05/09; FH-E05/10]

## 6. Ordinary company is part of the relationship's logic

The relationship contains pleasure before either participant can adequately explain it. In V01, Hinami's embarrassed account of `おにただ` opens into an animated exchange about Buin. Fumiya recognizes the game and answers from his own memory; she talks about craft, world and characters, then notices that they have left the lesson behind. The interruption is not proof that instruction has ended or that the moment exposes a single unmasked self. It shows how their particular shared culture can suspend the need to demonstrate improvement for a while. The lesson resumes after an actual exchange that both help sustain. [FH-P02]

V02 makes competition enjoyable to Hinami even when Fumiya's scheme opposes her. The same contest can be thrilling to these two and damaging to Mimimi's comparison system. This is a reason to retain pleasure, not a reason to let it adjudicate the whole event. A relationship can be enlivened by an encounter whose costs are unequally distributed. [DR02 §§5/10/15–21; HA-E12]

The shopping unit gives a quieter combination of teaching and company. Hinami has relevant judgment; Fumiya misses aspects of silhouette and learns through particular errors. His independent practice and received clerk advice surprise or earn recognition from her. Their exchange shifts into RPG equipment jokes, and he explicitly experiences the shopping as enjoyable. That pleasure is not an achievement score imposed afterward. It is in the shared activity, even while her corrective and sometimes contemptuous manner remains. His chosen clothes do not become her preferences, and one enjoyable shopping trip does not establish a permanent leisure habit. [FH-P05; SR06.5 §8; TF-P01/HA-P09]

The V06 individual win is another ordinary communication with precise stakes. Hinami sends “my win” after taking a game; Fumiya answers with the set score and then wins the next game to finish 5–1. Their competitiveness belongs to both. The point is not that he benevolently permits her a little pride. He admits his own inability to resist the comeback. Recognition, irritation and pleasure coexist without requiring the relation to be either a curriculum or a confession. [FH-P04 P97–113]

V08 reverses access. Fumiya brings her into the game community where nanashi has standing independent of the school heroine. She participates and later directly says that it was ordinarily fun. The train exchange includes her guardian joke, his insistence that he invited her for enjoyment, and his reply that he is her pupil. Those roles are being played with, not simply abolished. Her possible reflected smile is uncertain in the narration; the later brief amusement he actually sees has its own evidence. His turning away from her phone is also a specific privacy-aware act, which must coexist with his later violations rather than being erased by them. [FH-P06]

These scenes explain why later suspension hurts. The sewing room, rematches, conversational rhythm and invitations have become livable time as well as an apparatus for improvement. Their loss cannot be measured only by the number of unfinished tasks. Nor does missing them establish romance. Ordinary comfort supports attachment across several possible standings, while its very familiarity can make unequal access and obligation harder to notice.

At the V12 school event, the ordinary group arrangement matters for the same reason. Hinami first sits among friends and responds to their familiar teasing before approaching the stage. Fumiya finds that unexceptional sight dazzling. She is present as an audience member at an event she once planned, rather than first proving herself by flawlessly resuming every office. Shared play becomes available from that setting. The source does not need to narrate a victorious match for the change in available company to be real. [FH-P15; FH-E12/14]

## 7. Refusal changes the relation because it can cost both people something

Neither person's agency should be established only when a refusal produces the analyst's preferred outcome. Fumiya's V03 non-confession interrupts Hinami's plan; his withdrawal also damages a life and contacts he values. Hinami's concession supports resumption without granting his entire theory. These are effective refusals and partial repair with residual inequality, rather than one person winning full authorship of the other. [FH-P03; DR03 §§10–12]

V09 makes the cost explicit. Fumiya says that continuing to receive her cold correctness about his girlfriend may make him dislike her before he can understand her. He proposes suspending the regular meetings both to protect that feeling and to spend more time with Kikuchi. Hinami asks why, accepts, and formalizes an irregular arrangement: tasks become his choice and the room is for times they actually want to talk. His resulting loneliness is narrated. Her controlled expression leaves her response opaque, rather than proving she felt nothing. He experiences the distance as her departure even though the proposal was his. The asymmetry is emotionally real without reversing who initiated it. [FH-P08]

Her own refusal to be changed is equally consequential. She can tolerate loneliness without thereby having none. She can keep practicing after the pause, react to the changed game link and decline the relation Fumiya now wants to offer. His wish to reach her is owned; ownership of that wish does not give him ownership of her assent. An invitation into a wider world and a claim to improve the person are different objects, even when both are motivated by affection. [DR09 §4.6; HA-P14/15; JVL-10]

Later care repeatedly passes through limits rather than one final agreement. He receives no answer to invitations she has read; a birthday can create joy without jointly selecting its remedial aim; a video can breach the recipient's tolerance; family disclosure can be chosen without authorizing onward use; a match can be accepted without accepting rescue; a manuscript can first be refused and later read under pressure. The last choice cannot be substituted for all the earlier permissions. [REL §5.1E–G/17; FH-P09–15]

Refusal is therefore neither a magical ethical closure nor a mere obstacle to be bypassed until the intended benefit arrives. The synthesis must ask who refuses what, whether the other person can practically hear it, what part is accepted or circumvented, and what new relationship is actually negotiated. At the admitted endpoint, no standing agreement covers future inquiry, modeled representation, distribution of private information, intervention stops, or restoration of coaching. That incompleteness does not cancel the real choices made in the scenes; it limits their transfer to another occasion. [FH-E08/09/15]

## 8. Disclosure creates an audience, not ownership of a life

Intimacy in this relationship is not a steadily increasing quantity of information. Different disclosures do different work, go to different recipients and leave different limits. Fumiya can know Hinami's game identity and still misread what she wants. She can describe a death and remain unable to supply its cause. A model can be accurate enough to feel invasive without becoming a complete account. More access can expose the other person's vulnerability while leaving the right to decide unchanged. [FH-E07/09]

V10 makes the distinction unusually explicit. Before telling Mizusawa, Fumiya recognizes that the information is not his to give and that forming a coalition without permission contradicts the individual standing he values. He then declares that Hinami is special and that he will tell him anyway. This is deliberate answerability in his own language of violated rights. It is not an accidental misunderstanding that disappears once the recipient is trustworthy. His caring end and Mizusawa's serious interest explain the decision; neither is the missing permission. [FH-P09]

The private ride is a different act. Hinami chooses Fumiya as the hearer of Nagisa's history and speaks about not knowing why the death occurred. She grants access to something she had guarded, without thereby agreeing to be changed, granting an indefinite interview or consenting to onward distribution. Her lighter mode and the later souvenir selection do not retract the disclosure. They also do not tell the reader exactly how three mugs were paid for or allocated. A change of register is not a reliable truth detector. [FH-P10; HA-E23]

V11 again frames a telling. Hinami begins, threatens to stop when his reaction irritates her, and resumes after his apology. The text then changes presentation through a third-person Aoi-centered retrospective and first-person interiority before returning to Fumiya, who says she has finished speaking. These facts establish substantial communicated history. They do not make every thought and remembered scene an uninterrupted transcript. His subsequent interpretation is itself direct evidence of what he can reconstruct, not a license to hand him the reader's whole interior archive. [FH-P11; CRI-SRC-001; JVL-08]

The distinction becomes essential in V12. The reader receives Aoi's first-person account of self-hate, mixed reception, relief at exposure, genuine birthday warmth and fear of renewed effort. She privately wonders whether being with him and speaking about her sister contained a hope that time might begin moving again. That thought is tentative retrospective self-interpretation; it neither cancels reproducibility nor proves one exhaustive original motive. Some recalled questions were already spoken in the V10 ride. Those exact overlaps establish heard content, while the new private reflection remains new reader access. The five-year wording does not assign the whole recollection exclusively to V10 or V11. [FH-P13; CRI §20]

## 9. Other people change the dyad and retain their own lives

The relationship's options are repeatedly altered by people outside it. Their contribution cannot be reduced to helping Fumiya reach Hinami. Some are harmed by the priority he gives her; some refuse the ends pursued in their name; some supply affection, work or knowledge that neither protagonist can produce alone. The dyad becomes more intelligible when these people remain agents rather than evidence for its destiny. [FH-E06]

**Kikuchi** changes what Fumiya can understand about learned form, self-authorship and care. She is also his selected partner, with her own demand for time, truthful reception, privacy, bodily limits and a writing life. Hinami's symbolic importance can hurt her without making her jealousy proof of an affair or making the partnership a false consolation. In V11, when Fumiya is consumed by Hinami, her attempt to answer is not received and the painful call ends; the source does not identify a disconnecting actor. Later repair is real, not an erasure of that failure. In V12 she challenges his sole-rescuer exhaustion, helps him and consents to particular circulation of her manuscript. He explicitly knows that his owned-wish formulation will obtain her acceptance and understands its unfairness. That awareness matters beside the real assent. [REL §6/17; CRI §21; TF-V12-E02; FH-P16]

Her authorship further prevents a two-person rescue monopoly. She stops a project whose desired answer has been predetermined, chooses an inquiry that Fumiya initially cannot accompany, and eventually owns a reason to write that is different from his reason to help Hinami. His later changed invitation and her acceptance are not an annulment of her initial refusal. The written story carries Fumiya's care only because a writer makes choices he cannot make for her. Her final insistence that tears do not certify rightness limits the couple as well as the intended beneficiary. [FH-P16; CRI-KIK-002/REL-002/FORM-001]

**Mimimi** is affected by Hinami's performance, contests its apparent inevitability and supplies a recovery cue when Fumiya fails publicly. She also loves him, is not selected and continues an ethically costly friendship. Her V12 contribution is not the reward of having finally ceased wanting him. She reports a temptation to exploit his vulnerability and owns her refusal because she wants to like the self who acts. Her movement, questions and testimony help alter the group's method; neither protagonist owns the value or future duration of that restraint. Treating her as a cheerful reserve of support would reproduce the allocation problem the relationship needs to understand. [DR02/07/09; REL §7/17; CS §19; EFF-H04/05/17]

**Tama** gives Hinami an attachment that exceeds the stated doctrine of adaptation. She also opposes the revenge carried out to protect that attachment. Fumiya's becoming her teacher requires him to learn recipient fit and accept help from others; Hinami acknowledges that their chosen relation is not hers to prohibit. Tama's independent response is therefore part of the dyad's authority education. In V12 her owned heroic practice and craft remain more particular than a universal solution for Hinami. The dyad has no right to make her constancy a justification for indefinite punishment or for carrying every other person's self-worth. [DR05–06; REL §8.1; FH-P04; CS-TAM-03]

## 10. The V11 defeat is a crisis of a relationship warrant

The failure of Fumiya's argument is not simply an advanced version of losing a game. He has made being uniquely able to reach Hinami part of what gives his life and his gamer status meaning. The former social inferior can now call the former perfect heroine weak, and he treats seeing that weakness as standing no one else has. It is an apparent reversal of their first hierarchy. But reversing who commands does not repair the original problem of who selects the end. [FH-P12; CRI-REL-001/TOM-004]

He tries to give her a belief that does not require her own proof, while grounding his ability to give it in an unbeaten relation. This is his claim to enduring superiority, not a literal record that she has never taken an individual game: V06 already narrates one inside the 5–1 set. Hinami then uses the domain he offered to remove that ground. Her analysis and technical capacity remain available even while school life and ordinary participation have collapsed. The result invalidates the credential he named. It does not demonstrate that all his social development was a lie or that she has finally proven the right philosophy of life. Each person's crisis verdict exceeds what the game can settle. [FH-P04/12; CS-TOM-04/HIN-01/03]

The collapse is relationally selective. Fumiya still has a girlfriend, chosen friends, learned skills, professional route evidence and a history of owned action. Those resources do not vanish, yet he temporarily cannot deploy them against the loss of this unique role. His school absence and loss of desire to play show how a plural life has become subordinated to one account of necessity. V11 is therefore counterevidence to global invulnerability, not a reset to V01. V12's receiving care and accepting other people's work is meaningful partly because it loosens that monopoly. No later new defeat tests whether the change is durable. [TF §16; CS §6/19; FH-E10]

Hinami's victory is equally resistant to a single lesson. She can admire the beauty of nanashi's play and say she once wanted to imitate it, while abandoning that idealized style in order to win. Rivalry is still recognition and contact even when made into a weapon. Her crisis statement that life is a bad game is addressed speech, not omniscient narration establishing the world's nature. The relation is unresolved at the V11 boundary: formal routine has not resumed, the collective project is still proposed, and neither can restore the former credential by insisting more strongly. [HA-E27/P21; LOC04; REL §5.1G]

## 11. V12 changes the offered support, not the past's permissions

The collective project initially risks repeating the dyad's familiar error. Aoi is supposed to become self-luminous; other people's accounts are gathered to support that desired answer. Kikuchi's performed Alcia then challenges the author for hearing only an echo of what she wants. The actual Hinami has not attended that exchange or endorsed its criticism, but the modeled resistance makes the participants stop. This is a material correction of method: neither Fumiya's devotion nor a larger cast is sufficient if the question cannot be changed by what is heard. [FH-P16; EFF §80; CRI-REL-005]

The later practice preserves more than observation. Experiences and contextual craft alter perception, but they cannot supply Nagisa's unavailable final thought or remove the writer's responsibility to choose. Kikuchi ultimately offers an observed yet personally wished-for story, while withholding a claim that its effect proves it right. The method is no guarantee against intrusion. It changes what can be offered and makes its authorship more answerable; it does not turn an authored model into a witness. [FH-P16; CRI-EPI-001/KIK-002]

Delivery and reading have their own mixed agency. Hinami refuses contact and the book, Haruka receives and independently reads it, and emotional pressure contributes to Aoi taking it. She is angry at being defined and can imagine rejecting it, yet continues. The completed experience belongs to her as a reader as well as to the people who made the object. “She has no agency” loses her choices; “she eventually reads, therefore all methods were permitted” loses the earlier refusals. [FH-P13; SOC §17.2]

Fumiya arrives after a reading he did not witness. The reader knows Aoi's private movement through mixed self-interpretations, remembered achievements, doubt, apology, love and imagined reunion. He receives her actual present response. He asks whether she likes herself; she admits a little, and his narration expressly allows that it might be temporary. Her new account does not retrospectively prove him right about every motive, and his confidence that NO NAME can recover the felt state remains confidence. [FH-P13/14]

The Nagisa-substitute offer must be read as a sequence. He intentionally supplies the conventional line, she almost nods, and he immediately retracts it: he cannot become Nagisa and does not offer himself as an inexpensive replacement. Truncating at her near-assent manufactures a promise the scene refuses. It would also make the V12 exchange repeat the V11 unique-person warrant without noticing the changed proposal. [FH-P14 P39–59]

Yet the difference cannot be reduced to “now he finally trusts without reasons.” V11 already explicitly spoke in groundless confidence. What changes is the proposed carrier of support and the relation demanded of the hearer. He points toward the story the group has made, an object she has just experienced as beautiful and usable beside her doubt. The absent sister is not replaced by a monopolizing living rescuer. His own model of the empty focus also suggests that every absence need not be filled by another person. These are interpretive and relational possibilities, not a geometric theorem of human stability. [FH-P12/14; EFF-H28; JVL §13.5]

His assurance remains strong and insufficiently tested. He says the story can continue to affirm her through death and changed circumstances and that the orbit can remain stable. The narration itself calls this groundless confidence with a reason attached. The synthesis therefore preserves the declaration as an offered commitment and model, rather than a demonstrated lifelong outcome. Hinami's limited response and later participation support a local change; they do not certify that every dependency, grief or failure will now be manageable. [FH-P14 P61–102]

The public ending then supplies something different from another private proof contest. Ashigaru proposes the pairing, peers encourage Aoi, she approaches the stage and both players choose Found for a shared story. Fumiya begins with the old rightness challenge; Hinami replies that she has stopped clinging to correctness. This is her statement about herself, not an instruction that he should stop. His next proposal is to enjoy the encounter. The actual match and result remain unnarrated. [FH-P14/15]

That ending allows a precise positive claim. Shared history becomes something they may show and enjoy before a public audience, rather than a credential that must determine which person may define the other's life. Fumiya's expressive professional aim broadens toward valued people and supporters, with Hinami among them. It remains his owned aim and present judgment that life is good at least alongside Atafami, not a proof of objective fairness or practical career viability. Aoi's participation is equally local: school-event attendance, ordinary peer presence and chosen play do not demonstrate resumed presidency, regular attendance, coaching, blanket forgiveness or a complete affirmative life plan. [FH-E12–14; CS §19; CRI-REL-001/003/EFF-002]
