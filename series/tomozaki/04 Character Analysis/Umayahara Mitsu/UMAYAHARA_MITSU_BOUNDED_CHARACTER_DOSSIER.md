# Umayahara Mitsu — craft, custody, refusal and continuing shared practice

This account is accepted at its stated bounded literary scope through the admitted V12 boundary. See the [implementation review](../../08%20Audits%20and%20Manifests/TOMOZAKI_SUPPORTING_CHARACTER_IMPLEMENTATION_REVIEW.md) and [source and review manifest](../../08%20Audits%20and%20Manifests/TOMOZAKI_SUPPORTING_LITERARY_EVIDENCE_AND_REVIEW_MANIFEST.json) for independent acceptance, source returns, corrections and remaining limits. A bounded literary role is separate from a derived reconstruction model or aggregate release.

## 1. Literary responsibility and the scope earned

Mitsu is a person maintaining work, objects and occasions whose value cannot be reduced to the instruction the visiting writer receives from them. Her first concern is a usable piece of cloth, a festival deadline, an adequately formed question, or the conditions under which someone may handle a record. Those concerns persist when Fumiya and Kikuchi are absent from her attention. Her expertise gives her reasons to refuse; bodily limits give work a cost; particular other people make some kinds of help acceptable. Her eventual generosity does not remove any of those distinctions.

The strongest bounded account is that she defends continuity through discriminating, sometimes abrasive practical judgment, while remaining capable of changing a decision when a person or proposal merits it. That account is testable against the scene in which she accepts a costume's proposed colors but rejects its arrangement, the limited delegation to a trusted local, and the damaged birds that she keeps folding even when the available time makes complete replacement impossible. A simple rule that she always refuses, always knows best, or always secretly wants assistance fails those tests. So does an account in which her apparent harshness is merely a shell that the protagonists remove. Her bluntness, exacting craft and refusal survive her offers of access and affection. [UM-E01–E08]

**B, a substantial independent bounded dossier, is earned.** One volume gives several consequential bands and recipients: shop authority and material judgment, bargaining with visitors, experienced collaboration with a local, expert revision of an unauthorized amateur trial, archive custody, disaster work, festival participation, retrospective witness testimony and an unusually personal account of receiving a fictional version of herself. Their tensions require a person-level home. A system-only account would lose what she wants and what these actions cost her. A major longitudinal monograph would promise an independently witnessed life history that this evidence does not provide. Her current encounter and attributed past are therefore kept separate. The fourteen-witness audit context establishes why earlier mentions of Andi matter to literary reception; it supplies no license to invent Mitsu's earlier scenes. Search misses and unfilled audit cells are not absence findings. [UM-E09–E12; receipt]

She is presented as an older woman working in the kimono/cloth shop and as the named local custodian holding the key to the observation tower. No exact age, birth date, legal ownership record, marital status, descendants, estate appointment or national background is established by these routes. An island youth's familiar grandmother address does not identify a biological grandchild. Mitsu reports a woman whose husband died at sea at p0018 P205–207. ## 3. Work before lesson: discrimination, material knowledge and self-presentation

The first observed exchange concerns a local worker who has brought a cloth she distinguishes from the requested shade. Fumiya also initially sees the two as alike. Mitsu corrects the selection and connects the distinction to the tailor rejecting it and the parade missing its deadline. Her judgment is addressed to an existing production chain; the visitor's education is incidental. She has neatly kept nails, a tied-back hairstyle, sunglasses and a vivid deep-green kimono. Later violet and jade garments change the visible presentation. These are attested choices and details, not evidence for an invented lifelong favorite color or a decorative wisdom costume. [UM-P02]

Her craft is perceptual and procedural. Kikuchi does not instantly acquire it by being sensitive or well read. Sorting yields mistakes, repeated comparisons and a change of light and neighboring colors. Naming a shade gives a useful boundary without exhausting its appearance. Mitsu can require a more exact distinction and also know that cloth has to be seen in a setting. That combination matters: she is neither a relativist for whom every answer works nor a pedant applying a context-free dictionary. A sample is materially more dependable for some work than a name, yet the later sample itself can be mismatched with a correct written name. No single representation eliminates the need to check. [UM-P03/P05]

Fumiya sees fingers that do not open fully and repeated attempts at work. Later paper folding becomes visibly slower and awkward. These actions establish bodily limitation and effort, not a medical diagnosis, a history of its onset, or a proved explanation of the earlier sample mismatch. His recognition that she is working with pain is his situated observation and interpretation. A dossier can own that cost without translating it into incapacity or treating every refused offer as pathological denial. [UM-P03/P07]

Ordinary work carries wants of its own: finishing the festival preparation, making a garment function, maintaining tools and records, and avoiding time-consuming visitors. She says she wants to sleep early before the event. She warns about the tide, notices the costume collars during the actual performance, and later tells people to move on rather than remain seated. Her attention stays on timing, placement, movement and practical continuity. None of this establishes a whole business history, income model or comprehensive work ethic. It does show why her agency exceeds the category of teacher. [UM-P04–P09]

## 4. Refusal, negotiated access and recipient-specific help

Mitsu's initial refusal challenges fans who arrive to take away a moving story confirming what they already admire. The couple leave without obtaining the desired explanation or demanding the manuscript. On returning, Kikuchi's purpose becomes more particular: she wants to encounter what Andi observed. Mitsu still does not simply assent. The resulting bargain ties access to useful work completed before the festival. Kikuchi sorts cloth; Fumiya carries the relevant physical load. The tower is opened afterward, when Mitsu explicitly recognizes the condition she had stated. The work is an actual obligation as well as a route to a changed view. [UM-P03/P06]

Her responses to assistance are differentiated:

| Recipient/offer | Her shown response | Literary consequence and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Fumiya noticing her hand and offering unasked help | She refuses; her shop authority and his lack of standing are explicit, including a sharp physical interruption with the pen | Need does not by itself determine who may take over; the scene does not make every refusal ideal or gentle |
| Kikuchi doing the assigned color work | Mitsu corrects concrete failures and requires practice | Cooperation is earned in a particular task; literary talent does not supply immediate craft competence |
| A known local youth who offers and waits | She eventually gives a limited set of seven tags/tasks | Trust and a defined scope support acceptance; neither his familiar address nor the delegation creates a family tree or blanket authorization |
| The couple's costume trial | She is angry about the opened package, examines what they have done, accepts the color idea and rejects placement before making expert changes | Useful contribution and unauthorized initiation remain separately visible |
| The departing visitors/borrowers | She supplies particular access and extra papers with a next-morning return condition | Changed standing is real but does not become general archive access or publication permission |

The first three comparisons arise within p0016 P410–497; the costume and loan have their own later routes. Recipient variation defeats a global label such as cannot accept help. It also defeats the opposite lesson that sufficient warmth always dissolves refusal. The local's established standing, his waiting, and the limited assignment matter. Fumiya's more intrusive approach and lack of local skill matter. The dossier cannot infer that every comparable outsider would receive the same response. [UM-P03/P05/P10]

## 5. The costume: independent initiative, a real objection and expert revision

Mitsu's absence itself has a competing practical cause. A festival committee member asks for her judgment of the tide route after rain has displaced sand. The costume checking remains unfinished, but the route must be examined before the tide changes. She leaves with an explicit instruction not to do anything extra before she returns. Fumiya interprets the irritation as directed at the timing; that motive attribution is his, while her choice to respond and her instruction are direct. Island expertise places demands on her beyond the shop and beyond either visitor's inquiry. [UM-P05, P632–643]

The exact sequence is consequential. Kikuchi touches the package cord without pulling it at p0016 P685. **Fumiya proposes opening at P686–688 and opens it at P690 after her assent.** The written blue-color name is correct at P701; the attached scarlet sample mismatches it at P702–707. Fumiya's imagined swap, knot and non-blame mechanisms at P708–710 are his hypotheses. Neither the text of that window nor the later success demonstrates a culprit or a precise mechanism. Calling this simply Mitsu's wrong label would change the source. [UM-P05]

The ensuing work has differentiated initiative too. Kikuchi asks to continue testing at P739, directs selection of scraps and chooses the colors at P769, then pins the proposal at P780. Fumiya supplies pins at P779–780 and tests movement and the risk of a cord catching at P781–784. They recognize the permission problem at P748–750 and proceed. Their useful, limited trial should neither be attributed entirely to him nor allowed to conceal his earlier opening initiative. [UM-P05]

When she returns, Mitsu asks about the opened package. Fumiya says the proposal was his at P799; Kikuchi explains her assent and their attempted response. Mitsu checks the correct writing against the scarlet sample at P806. Her reaction cannot be flattened into a grateful elder finally admitting young people were right. She examines the experiment, accepts the color relation at P821 and rejects placement at P823. She then sews and adjusts the position, cord, movement and finish. The garments retain different base colors. Their eventual relation is a crafted result requiring her expertise, not a complete amateur replacement. [UM-P05]

The trial offers a precise contradiction for the central account. Her initial resistance does not prevent her revising a technical judgment in light of something usable. Yet she does not surrender jurisdiction over materials or retrospectively approve the opening. Skilled correction is her own action, not merely the protagonists' discovery made visible through her hands. Later, at the parade, her attention to the collars ties the abstract discussion of context back to accountability for a garment seen on actual wearers. [UM-P05/P08]

Kikuchi's recognition that different colors can become a pair is locally supported. It does not prove that all conflicts are complementary, that any two people ought to form a couple, or that a beneficial result gives advance permission for intrusion. The event is most useful as a case in which an unauthorized beginning, a promising proposal and a legitimate expert revision coexist. That mixed record prevents either blanket condemnation or outcome-only vindication. [UM-E05]

## 6. Custody as ordinary maintenance and bounded invitation

The closed tower contains polished cases, replaced explanation sheets and an absence of accumulated dust. Fumiya directly notices maintained objects and infers continuing care; Mitsu's key, tour, complaints about cleaning and intimate handling of the storytelling stand support linking that maintenance to her. The exact cleaning schedule and distribution of every task remain unobserved. The care is ordinary labor before it is a symbol. Her complaint that a fixed telescope is inconvenient to clean belongs alongside the visitors' exalted response to it. [UM-P06]

She can protect an object and remain impatient with the person asking about it. The request about what Andi observed is initially too dependent on her supplying the whole answer. She requires Kikuchi to formulate a question with more of her own labor. When Kikuchi offers the theory of one myth reconciling the island, Mitsu preserves the historical quarrels. They did not suddenly become harmonious; on particular nights, people who otherwise fought sat together waiting for the continuation. Her account limits the pleasing theory instead of furnishing an authoritative slogan for it. [UM-P06/P09]

Inside the cloth room, material arrays are directly shown while the history of producing them comes through Mitsu. She recalls Andi carrying cloth from the shop to compare with the sea, and the humiliatingly funny reversal in which she, the expert in different blues, ended up saying two pieces were the same. Her claim that he took her initial demonstration farther than she did preserves both her contribution and his independent excess. It also gives her a past response other than reverence: he was a nuisance, used things and disrupted her work. The account does not license us to identify a shared romance, an authenticated last intention, or exactly who made every tag. [UM-P06]

She notices Fumiya quietly take Kikuchi's bag and gives him a dry acknowledgment. **Fumiya** then imagines Mitsu may have occupied an analogous role for Andi. The analogy belongs to him. It does not establish the nature of their relationship, and the usefulness of a parallel in the novel is not independent biographical proof. [UM-P06]

The later loan is equally specific. Mitsu returns Kikuchi's forgotten draft with extra aged pages mixed into it and says that, if anything else is included, it should come back by the next morning. Her indirect excuse about poor eyesight accompanies the act; the private continuation is not publicly surrendered. She opens the tower, and the visitors find the inner door unlocked. Kikuchi and Fumiya read the difficult handwritten English line by line using phone translation. Before departure Kikuchi restores the papers' order, waits several minutes in the shop, and leaves the borrowed bundle behind the counter when Mitsu does not return. The text supplies an actual return action, not a witnessed acknowledgment from the custodian. [UM-P10]

Custody, permission to read particular papers, onward circulation, editorial authority and publication rights are different questions. No legal estate ownership or general right to publish Andi's private continuation is shown. A generous loan after changed standing does not retroactively open earlier locked rooms. The record also does not show a later public release or even a complete transcription. Those limits are substantive features of the scene, not holes to fill with a benevolent motive. [UM-E10]

## 7. Damaged birds: costly persistence and a practice she does not own alone

The festival has labor shortages before the storm. A resident says young people have left for the mainland; children take pleasure in the parade without needing a complete account of its origins. Their existing night of preparation is then damaged. Wet paper does not become dry original paper because someone finds a new meaning for it. Food, lanterns and routes also require imperfect repair. That material field prevents the festival becoming a frictionless example of the efficacy of stories. [UM-P04/P07/P08]

Mitsu acts first by dragging out dry reserve paper, sitting in the mud and folding. Her violet hem absorbs dirty water. Her fingers shake, corners misalign, and she pries them open to continue; the birds become increasingly uneven. She begins a practical response without waiting to solve the whole shortage. Others sit beside her, including the couple. The image supports persistence and a willingness to incur personal cost, but neither a diagnosis nor an unconditional claim that enduring any pain is good. Fumiya's thought that she knows complete replacement cannot be done in time is his interpretation of the visible shortage and her impossible-sounding determination. [UM-P07]

This episode tests the craft expert rather than canceling her. Earlier she rejects small differences that would make a costume fail. Now exact replication is unavailable, and even her own birds lose symmetry. An account that she values only perfection cannot explain why she continues with imperfect products. An account that she simply abandons standards cannot explain the expert sewing, the careful final bird, or the continuing effort to provide usable objects. The end matters: this occasion should still take place, with what people can actually make and retain. [UM-E07/E08]

Kikuchi's different contribution is not to repair every object or assure the children that nothing broke. She acknowledges damage, observes particular features, asks and waits; the children supply their own meanings. Combining damaged birds with new ones produces a practically sufficient number. Mitsu watches, finishes her own bird, puts it in their box, and calls people back to the festival. Her acceptance is an action in a collective response. It does not make her the sole source of their meanings or convert Kikuchi into their universal interpreter. [UM-P07/P08]

At the parade, old and new birds coexist. On Mio Island, elders pray, young people take pictures, and children play. An elder says discussing what the place means leads to disagreement while placing a bird is available to everyone. Those are distinct local acts and attributed explanations, not Mitsu's private doctrine or an exhaustive ritual history. She participates in and maintains an occasion whose meanings exceed her control. Her practical warnings about tide and chill remain as much part of her presence as the reflective discussion. [UM-P08/P09]

## 8. Reported history, received fiction and self-theory

Mitsu contests the claim that Andi invented the festival. Her account begins with existing actions: a woman privately folding after losing her husband at sea, elders making crossings, and Andi observing over years. She reports that different residents recognized their own lives in his scene and that young people later carried selected elements into practice. Others added, removed and kept what appealed to them; he did not issue an instruction. She also recalls his surprise at seeing the procession. That is a witness's historical account with particular access, not omniscient certification of every origin or participant. [UM-P09]

When Kikuchi turns this into an explanatory conclusion—that refusing to round things into a neat answer allowed readers to carry it themselves—Mitsu declines to certify the causal theory. She does not know about such difficult explanations. Her refusal is valuable counterevidence against treating her as the novel's all-knowing author surrogate. She can report events and still not authenticate the mechanism the younger writer proposes. [UM-P09]

Her personal self-report is narrower and more intimate. A character modeled on her remained foul-mouthed and stubborn, yet after reading she could like herself a little more. This is direct evidence of **her present testimony about an earlier effect**, not direct access to every earlier feeling or proof that the fictional portrait was fully accurate. The result is not a conversion to a pleasant personality. In the same encounter she remains brusque, reticent and practical. The slight affirmative shift works without an erasure of the traits she names. [UM-P09]

That testimony matters independently of the protagonists' rescue project. Mitsu has been a represented subject who can judge something about her own experience of receiving a story. Her claim gives a concrete counterexample to the proposition that any retained flaw makes representation only harmful. It gives no general permission to fictionalize others, no evidence that Andi obtained prior subject consent, and no guarantee that every unconsenting subject would benefit. The later visitors' own writing problem remains theirs to choose and own. Mitsu neither licenses it nor authenticates Nagisa's unavailable standpoint. [UM-E09/E11]

## 9. Wants, strengths and their competing costs

Her shown priorities cannot be placed into one universal rank, but the evidence supports a contextual hierarchy. During preparation, finishing viable festival work takes priority over visitors' curiosity. In the shop, material judgment and control of handling take priority over an outsider's pity. At the tower she protects objects while allowing selected inspection. During storm recovery, continuation takes priority over immaculate clothing and her own comfort. Near departure she chooses to lend something she had not made generally available. These are actual choices; their full private reasons remain partly unknown. [UM-P03–P10]

Several strengths also create friction. Exactness exposes small defects and can make her intolerant of inexperienced help. Independence protects authority and may leave her bearing painful work. Guarding memory prevents easy extraction and constrains legitimate inquiry. Blunt speech can stop presumptuous questions but also imposes a difficult threshold on people whose intent is sincere. Care can appear in an oblique excuse, leaving the recipients to infer what has been offered. None is an uncomplicated flaw to be cured or a universal virtue to imitate. [UM-E02/E04/E06]

She can enjoy wit without becoming an all-purpose comic elder. The reported reversal over blue cloth, the dry recognition of the bag carrier, the haori explained as something like a kitchen's leftover soup, and praise that the couple look suited precisely while looking unlike each other all have actual recipients and contexts. Her minimal approval of the children's self-named colors is especially useful: correct craft nomenclature is not the only language she permits in every setting. Her work standards remain strict where a tailor and performance depend on them. [UM-P06/P08]

Those unknowns are compatible with substantial personhood; they are not grounds for filling a schema with invented facts.
