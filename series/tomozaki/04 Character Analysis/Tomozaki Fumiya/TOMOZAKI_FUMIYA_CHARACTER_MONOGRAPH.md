# Tomozaki Fumiya — character monograph through Volume 11

> **Authority notice:** This is a valuable pre-remediation character-study candidate, not yet mature literary authority. Its longitudinal, relationship, Japanese-voice, rival-reading, and claim/locator responsibilities must be deepened after the cumulative ledgers converge.

## 1. Responsibility and evidence boundary

This provisional monograph reconstructs Tomozaki Fumiya across the audited Japanese light-novel boundary through V11. Its intended responsibility is narrower than eventual full-series synthesis: distinguish his stable tendencies, changing states, recipient-conditioned conduct, competence, confidence, status, self-concept, agency, values, ordinary life, focalization limits, contradictions, and unresolved hypotheses.

The volume readings remain authoritative for prospective claims at each historical boundary. This document may integrate later evidence but may not make earlier Tomozaki appear more prescient than he was. V06.5 and V08.5 are used only under their story-local chronology. No post-V11, adaptation, translation, diagnostic, or reception claim is admitted.

## 2. Reconstructive thesis

Tomozaki does not develop from effortlessness into effort. He begins with extreme, disciplined effort inside Atafami and a categorical exemption for social life. His central development is the transfer of an already existing learning ethic into unfamiliar domains, followed by increasingly difficult revisions about **who selects the goal**, **who bears the cost**, and **who has authority over another person's end**.

His strongest recurring capacity is not charisma or optimism. It is the ability to act on partial models, review failure, and retain useful technique while revising the model that justified it. That capacity becomes genuine self-authorship, but it never becomes ethically or psychologically secure. V11 exposes the limit: after he makes unique gaming superiority the credential for saving Hinami and loses it, he temporarily loses both his desire to play and his strong-character self-description.

The current Tomozaki is therefore neither Hinami's successful product nor her complete opposite. He is a highly competent, socially embedded, value-bearing strategist whose agency has grown faster than his respect for other people's informational and terminal authority.

## 5. Stable tendencies and state-dependent behavior

### 5.1 Stable tendencies

- **Analytical decomposition:** Tomozaki converts complex outcomes into controllable variables and often improves after losing.
- **Practice tolerance:** he can repeat awkward actions long enough for them to become procedural rather than merely memorized.
- **Rule seriousness:** explicit rules, promises, games, and measurable targets matter to him, even when he later contests their ends.
- **Recipient-specific attention:** he increasingly notices what different people want, fear, conceal, or can realistically do.
- **Moral discomfort with falsity:** concealment and manipulation can still occur, but they generate self-scrutiny rather than effortless rationalization.
- **Change-readiness:** he can revise identity and method without waiting for complete certainty.

### 5.2 State-dependent conduct

- Under ordinary or distributed stakes, Tomozaki delegates, improvises, accepts correction, and functions as a reliable organizer.
- Under singular romantic stakes, his old low self-estimate and need for definite classification return.
- Under Hinami-facing stakes, care combines with competition, debt, interpretive ambition, and the wish to be uniquely necessary.
- When ashamed, he can withhold information, compress another person's options, or attempt self-erasure rather than negotiate a durable boundary.
- After the V11 game loss, he withdraws in a way that would be misread if treated as restoration of his total V01 identity. His relationships and learned capacities remain; the governing story has failed.

## 6. Competence, confidence, status, self-concept, agency, and values

### 6.1 Competence is transferred, not bestowed

Hinami supplies a curriculum and social models, but Tomozaki's progress requires preexisting learning capacities. Evidence of internalization includes independent transfer to Yuzu, revision of Tama's drills, festival leadership, professional route-building, and use of peer specialists. A copied gesture is not yet ownership; the ability to select, adapt, refuse, and teach a method is stronger evidence.

### 6.2 Confidence trails performance

Tomozaki often acts competently before he can classify himself as competent. His social network, girlfriend, leadership record, and public gaming presence accumulate faster than his self-concept. Ashigaru's V09 strong-character description matters because it names process rather than rank: Tomozaki changes without demanding a complete reason first.

The V11 collapse bounds that achievement. Self-concept has become more positive but remains partly collateralized by the one domain in which he expects unquestioned superiority.

### 6.3 Status becomes plural

Early Tomozaki imagines a single hierarchy of strong and weak characters. Later he occupies several partly independent positions:

- unrivaled or elite player within Atafami;
- learner and rival with Hinami;
- friend and organizer within the school group;
- boyfriend and coauthor with Kikuchi;
- teacher or coordinator with Tama and Yuzu;
- peer and professional novice among Jack, Ashigaru, Endo, and the gaming audience.

Plural status weakens the opening fatalism because failure in one field need not define the whole person. V11 tests whether Tomozaki can actually live that proposition after a defeat in the field he used as his deepest warrant.

### 6.4 Agency develops through selective retention

Tomozaki's cleanest agency sequence is not obedience followed by rejection. It is:

`try assigned form -> observe effect -> distinguish method from end -> refuse or revise the end -> retain and adapt useful form`.

V03 is the clearest early instance. V08-V09 repeat it when he abandons group centrality but retains group relationships and selects professional play. The unresolved limit is interpersonal: he can own his caring goal and still treat willingness to pay the cost as permission to set the other person's route.

## 7. Self-presentation, focalization, and the authenticity problem

The novels largely filter the world through Tomozaki. His introspection is direct evidence about his own present classification, not automatic proof about other minds. This distinction becomes especially important with Hinami and Kikuchi: he can identify a pattern correctly and overstate what it authorizes.

Tomozaki initially treats scripted behavior as falsity. Experience produces a more differentiated model:

- memorized content can fail because it carries no lived image;
- practiced form can transmit sincere content more accurately;
- social fluency can become procedural without becoming involuntary essence;
- directness can be sincere and still harmful;
- concealment can arise from shame or conflict avoidance without becoming harmless.

His performed selves—public organizer, boyfriend, commentator, social trainee—are not detachable masks over one true gamer. They are capacities that become more or less answerable to his chosen values.

## 8. Relationship-conditioned reconstruction

### 8.1 Hinami Aoi

Hinami is teacher, rival, proof audience, object of inquiry, source of debt, and uniquely important person. Tomozaki first accepts her jurisdiction because she can translate his own effort ethic into social training. He later contests her ends while retaining her methods. Their Atafami relation is unusually reciprocal, but he misuses gaming superiority as outside-game authority. His V11 failure is therefore relational and epistemic, not merely competitive.

Exceptional attachment is established. Romantic classification is not. His explicit selection and reselection of Kikuchi, coupled jealousy, and lack of reciprocal Hinami declaration prevent the monograph from turning narrative centrality into a confirmed romantic end.

### 8.2 Kikuchi Fuka

Kikuchi first exposes the weakness of instrumental contact: the relationship becomes genuine only after Tomozaki actually reads, talks, and chooses shared time. He later selects her directly and accepts her selection. She gives him a noncompetitive interpretive language and, in V11, reaches him through fiction when direct reassurance fails.

His recurring risk is to treat Kikuchi's generosity as expandable capacity. V08 scheduling and disclosure, V10-V11 Hinami work, the dismissive call, and incomplete standing rules show that love and explicit repair do not eliminate allocation and privacy problems.

### 8.3 Mimimi

Tomozaki recognizes Mimimi's effort because it resembles his own, then learns that honoring effort without inspecting its comparison structure can intensify harm. He does not convert her confession into a debt or consolation match. After choosing Kikuchi, he remains responsible for treating Mimimi's continuing feeling as a boundary-bearing fact rather than pretending it disappeared.

### 8.4 Tama and Yuzu

With Tama, Tomozaki becomes most clearly a teacher who must adapt the curriculum to the learner rather than reproduce Hinami. Tama's stable principle also supplies a stopping rule he lacks during the V05 retaliation. With Yuzu, he repeatedly sees that relational labor can be owned even when it resembles accommodation. Both relationships complicate his temptation to equate visible optimization with agency.

### 8.5 Mizusawa

Mizusawa supplies social form, adult-style interpretation, challenge, and negotiated consequence. Tomozaki can learn from him without treating fluency as a complete life. Their trust deepens, but V10 shows a joint ethical failure: Tomozaki gives private Hinami information because he judges Mizusawa's motive serious.

### 8.6 Rena and the professional-game network

Rena tests whether immediate refusal becomes durable governance. Tomozaki increasingly removes touch and rejects propositions, but the record remains reactive rather than rule-complete. Jack, Ashigaru, Endo, and the tournament audience widen his identity beyond school; they also introduce incentives, contracts, performance conditions, and public visibility that desire alone cannot settle.

## 9. Ordinary-life and preference evidence

Tomozaki's owned life becomes visible in recurring low-stakes choices:

- continuing to practice Atafami for mastery and play rather than only rank;
- reading Michael Andy because the Kikuchi relationship becomes real, not merely to complete a task;
- choosing quiet shared time as well as group activity;
- participating in work, school planning, meals, travel, online posting, and peer routines;
- enjoying tactical discussion and translating game structure into other domains;
- allowing friends to help rather than treating every completed action as proof of solo competence.

Ordinary repetition is essential evidence of change. A climactic speech can be assigned or compensatory; sustained scheduling, reciprocal disclosure, and continued presence show whether a role becomes part of a life. V08 demonstrates the converse: ordinary maintenance can fail even when the initiating romantic choice was authentic.

## 11. Rival readings and counterevidence

### `Tomozaki becomes strong by following Hinami`

Hinami's training is causally indispensable, but the decisive developments involve transfer, refusal, adaptation, owned choice, and correction of Hinami's method. Pure compliance cannot explain V03, V05, V08-V09, or the V11 party revision.

### `Tomozaki's natural self replaces fake social performance`

The evidence instead shows trained behavior becoming usable and sincere. His gamer identity is also practiced and presented. No one mode is certified as the natural whole.

### `Tomozaki is an ethical hero because he accepts consequences`

He knowingly violates privacy and expands inquiry without consent. Owning punishment is morally relevant but cannot supply the authorization he lacks.

### `The V11 loss returns him to square one`

School withdrawal and inability to play are serious. They coexist with friendships, romantic history, professional evidence, strategic skill, and prior revisions that V01 Tomozaki did not possess.

### `His Hinami attachment is confirmed romantic love`

Specialness, sacrifice, and narrative centrality support the possibility, not the classification. The current source keeps his explicit romantic choice with Kikuchi and provides no reciprocal Hinami declaration.

## 12. Current endpoint and open hypotheses

### Established at high confidence

1. Tomozaki's most stable strength is evidence-responsive revision supported by exceptional practice tolerance.
2. Social skill becomes real competence through transfer, adaptation, and use under owned ends.
3. His self-authorship is strong in refusal, romance, profession, and group selection but incomplete in interpersonal authority.
4. He is genuinely socially embedded by V11; crisis withdrawal does not erase those relationships.
5. His recurring ethical failure is converting accurate interpretation and willingness to bear cost into permission over information or ends.
6. His V11 collapse is tied to loss of a unique-usefulness warrant, not simply to losing one game.

### High-medium hypotheses

- Tomozaki can recover by applying his own plural-field logic to nanashi's defeat, but V11 stops before he does so.
- The party model is a genuine improvement because it limits his interpretive monopoly, provided later action adds Hinami's participation and refusal.
- His professional aspiration may become an independent source of identity only if route governance survives loss, workload, and economic evidence.

### Open

- recovery of an owned reason to play;
- school return after the V11 withdrawal;
- a non-savior form of support for Hinami;
- durable privacy, allocation, and third-party rules with Kikuchi;
- professional feasibility, family/university integration, and an exit rule;
- Tomozaki/Hinami romantic classification; and
- whether he can tolerate being valuable without being uniquely necessary.
