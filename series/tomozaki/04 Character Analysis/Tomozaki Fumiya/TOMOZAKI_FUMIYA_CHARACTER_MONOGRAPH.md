## 1. Responsibility and evidence boundary

This monograph reconstructs Fumiya as a particular person rather than a collection of social-improvement milestones. It asks what he notices, values, enjoys, misunderstands, says, withholds, practices, risks, and does when the story he tells about himself becomes difficult to maintain. Literary interpretation and behavioral reconstruction belong together here. His gaming metaphors are formal features of the novels, but they are also ways this narrator organizes attention and makes action possible. His friendships are narrative relationships, but they also expose different versions of his conduct that a single trait label would conceal.

The governing corpus is the locked Japanese V01–V11 sequence plus admitted, story-locally routed V06.5 and V08.5 material. V07 means the audited Special Edition witness; its artbook does not advance the narrative. V08.5’s explicitly parallel karaoke story and adaptation-derived VR bonus do not establish canonical habits, preferences, or actions. No later volume, translation, adaptation, external commentary, or remembered canon supplies an answer here. In particular, **V11 is this analysis’s boundary, not a claim that the fictional series ends there**. [TF-E00](#tf-e00)

The working source hierarchy is the admitted Japanese witness; the frozen reading preserving its prospective state; the reconciled cumulative claim family and its present qualifications; and this monograph’s argument. The R01–R03 corrections govern where older readings or the previous monograph overstate attribution, chronology, or resolution. The original frozen formulations remain historical evidence of analytical development, not invitations to repeat a corrected error.

References such as [TF-E12](#tf-e12) lead to a local evidence route identifying a cumulative family, exact reading claims or sections, and a primary locator where necessary. **TF-E** and **TF-P** identifiers are local to this monograph; they do not impersonate newly approved cumulative identities. The distinction between evidence and reconstruction is maintained throughout: represented speech is not automatically a reliable self-diagnosis; inward narration is not something the listener heard; another person’s interpretation is not direct access to Fumiya’s mind; Kikuchi’s fiction is authored modeling, not a transcript of its human models. Conditional forecasts in §13 are explicitly analytical extrapolations, not additional canonical scenes.

## 2. Reconstructive thesis: who Fumiya is

Tomozaki does not develop from effortlessness into effort. He begins with extreme, disciplined effort inside Atafami and a categorical exemption for social life. His central development is the transfer of an already existing learning ethic into unfamiliar domains, followed by increasingly difficult revisions about **who selects the goal**, **who bears the cost**, and **who has authority over another person’s end**. His strongest recurring capacity is not charisma or optimism. It is the ability to act on partial models, review failure, and retain useful technique while revising the model that justified it. [TF-E01](#tf-e01) [TF-E03](#tf-e03) [TF-E17](#tf-e17)

That retained core needs a less flattering completion. Fumiya’s readiness to change is not self-correcting in every situation. He can change the wrong thing with remarkable determination. He can mistake a solvable problem in communication for a right to organize another person’s life, or confuse accepting responsibility with possessing authority. He can also identify a real relationship cost and respond by deleting the relationships that give his life its breadth. The same decisiveness that lets him abandon a dominant game character can become social self-amputation. His development therefore consists not simply in acquiring agency but in learning what his agency is answerable to. [TF-E18](#tf-e18) [TF-E20](#tf-e20) [TF-E23](#tf-e23)

As a person, he is earnest, competitive, analytically curious, often awkward, and capable of dry or elaborately technical comedy. He can be painfully conscious of how he appears while being unusually willing to expose a weak performance to instruction. In a field he understands, he is proud, discriminating, and willing to contest the terms of judgment. In an unfamiliar social field, he initially searches for a rule before trusting an intuition. Familiarity does not merely make him smoother: it releases more teasing, improvisation, direct liking, and pleasure in activities with no impressive outcome. The boy who defends his ranking can also cherish a badly made snowman because he made it with Kikuchi. Neither is an incidental mask covering the other. [TF-E02](#tf-e02) [TF-P01](#tf-p01) [TF-P02](#tf-p02)

His beliefs are best described as commitments under revision. Effort should have intelligible consequences; genuine wants matter even when they cannot be certified by another person’s logic; learned technique need not make an action false; people should not be reduced to an assigned role or an efficient route. Yet the corpus repeatedly tests his applications. He sometimes uses technique without adequate disclosure. He has to learn that sincere affection does not allocate time, that a person’s acceptance of one request does not settle every neighboring boundary, and that knowing why someone is suffering does not give him the right to supply their final answer. [TF-E03](#tf-e03) [TF-E14](#tf-e14) [TF-E16](#tf-e16) [TF-E23](#tf-e23)

The decisive V11 limit is personal rather than merely theoretical. Fumiya presents himself as able to believe without an external certificate, but his proposed help for Hinami leans on a certificate after all: he is the exceptional person she cannot defeat at Atafami. When that claim fails, his distress exceeds ordinary disappointment at losing. He loses the story of his unique usefulness. The later reconciliation with Kikuchi begins another revision: he can be reached by someone whose contribution is not subordinate to his own expertise, and the next attempt must involve more people than one hero or one specially insightful couple. The endpoint makes that revision available; it does not demonstrate that he has already learned how to practice it without overreach. [TF-E24](#tf-e24) [TF-E25](#tf-e25) [TF-E26](#tf-e26)

A concise reconstruction would therefore be: **Fumiya is an effortful, pleasure-capable learner who increasingly owns the ends of his actions, but whose wish to be useful can turn other people’s unsolved lives into tests of his own value.** His most promising movement is not from weakness to invulnerability. It is from being the only person who can solve a problem toward remaining a responsible participant when no such person exists.

## 3. Developmental chronology and sustained scene analysis

### 3.1 V01: the expert hidden inside the beginner

Fumiya’s initial rejection of life as a bad game has an empirical component. Unequal starting conditions, obscure rules, and the uneven rewards attached to appearance or confidence are not imaginary. The problem is the asymmetry with which he uses that account. Inside Atafami, a bad result calls for investigation. Outside it, the perceived badness of the system can exempt him from the investigation before it begins. His fatalism is selective, not evidence that he lacks will, persistence, or a capacity for sophisticated work. [TF-E01](#tf-e01)

The Nakamura matches establish this before Hinami’s curriculum could plausibly have created it. Fumiya treats successive proposed explanations for defeat as things that can be tested against actual play rather than settled by status. The ability to recognize a countermeasure, separate a change of conditions from a change of skill, and stand behind a practiced result is already his. His respect for NO NAME similarly precedes the revelation that the player is Hinami. He can recognize deliberate improvement in someone else’s performance; his competitive pride is not simply a refusal to admit another person’s merit. This matters to the later coaching relationship: Hinami gains access to a preexisting evaluator, not a person with no standards of his own. [TF-E01](#tf-e01)

Her correction works partly because she addresses the distinction he wants to preserve between earned and unearned superiority. Seeing that her social excellence requires work changes what her success can mean. It is no longer sufficient to dismiss it as a privilege unavailable to practice. That does not prove that every inequality is removable or that the same amount of effort yields the same result for everyone. It gives Fumiya a reason to conduct a local experiment with himself. The relevant conversion is from a categorical verdict to a testable possibility.

His early speech provides a different kind of prior strength. Asked to join Mimimi and Tama’s exchange, he does not produce effortless conversational timing. He hesitates, admits that he does not know whether Tama is childish, and describes the contrast he has actually noticed: Mimimi’s responsiveness and Tama’s tendency to cut through a proposition. He then moves into the language of samples and generalization. The incongruity is funny because he supplies an analytic miniature where the exchange seems to call for a socially quick answer. But the miniature also resists a stereotype. He is already trying to judge two particular people rather than infer all young women from one case. Mimimi laughs; Tama does not respond in the same way. His words have a recognizable character before they have universal social effectiveness. [TF-E02](#tf-e02)

The voluntary teaching of Yuzu is therefore more important than a simple successful assignment. Fumiya uses a competence that is unquestionably his to help with a purpose that is not reducible to defeating her. He attends to what kind of participation she needs and breaks the skill into manageable work. This is an early instance of recipient-sensitive technique: a novice does not need the whole expert’s repertoire at once. It anticipates both his later effectiveness with Tama and the error he will make when he assumes that his own route is the right curriculum for her. [TF-E02](#tf-e02) [TF-E07](#tf-e07)

His interaction with Kikuchi prevents the opening from becoming a clean conversion to honesty. An impression involving Andy’s books gives him access that does not initially rest on the shared literary experience it appears to represent. His discomfort and correction matter, but so does the fact that he benefited from the appearance before correcting it. The reader should neither define him as an inveterate liar nor accept his commitment to sincerity as proof that his information practices are already adequate. The later Rena conflict develops this difference between not wanting to deceive and giving another person a materially complete account. [TF-E02](#tf-e02) [TF-E16](#tf-e16)

The volume’s positive verdict is appropriately qualified. He tells **Yuzu**, not Hinami, that life may not yet be a god-tier game but is at least a good one. He explicitly refuses to assert a certainty he does not possess. The claim is spoken during an exchange in which he can laugh without consciously manufacturing the expression. Learned form and spontaneous response are already becoming difficult to separate. His final unassigned initiative toward Hinami similarly matters because the desired result is his: another person’s availability remains independent, but he has begun to want something specific enough to ask. [TF-E02](#tf-e02)

### 3.2 V02: effort can be admirable and still be the wrong answer

V02 expands the social usefulness of Fumiya’s technical habits. The election gives him a problem with constraints, available information, an audience, and a result. He can formulate tactics and examine defeat without turning it into total personal worthlessness. But the same event also reveals that the meaning of competition differs between participants. A result he can process as evidence about a strategy can strike Mimimi as evidence about whether she is special at all. The successful transfer of a method does not guarantee understanding of the person using it. [TF-E04](#tf-e04)

His initial encouragement of further effort is consequently not simply kindness that fails because the problem is unusually hard. It misidentifies what kind of problem is present. More exertion can help when the obstacle is an unmastered task. It can intensify harm when the person’s standard permits no acceptable position except superiority to someone who keeps moving. Fumiya’s own experience of absorption and improvement gives him a powerful vocabulary for effort, but it does not initially give him Mimimi’s relation to comparison. Kikuchi’s questioning and Tama’s different position are necessary corrections to his frame. [TF-E04](#tf-e04)

The resolution must not make him the discoverer who supplies everyone’s missing truth. Tama can recognize Mimimi as a person-specific first without winning a contest against Hinami. Fumiya can help create access to that recognition; he cannot manufacture Tama’s standing or Mimimi’s attachment to her. This is an early lesson in distributed help. A useful facilitator need not be the person whose words carry the decisive value. It is also an early test he does not fully internalize: V11 will show him claiming a uniquely decisive standing toward Hinami. [TF-E04](#tf-e04) [TF-E23](#tf-e23)

Meanwhile, the ordinary gains are neither trivial nor merely proof that the curriculum is working. The developing ease among male peers, attention to clothes and grooming, and growing ability to participate alter the options available in a day. More revealingly, being alone can now feel different. Solitude is no longer experienced only as the absence of an activity he has already dismissed; it can become the absence of companionship he has learned to value. His reading of Andy also develops beyond its original access function. The quiet, shared literary world with Kikuchi becomes an actual source of interest. A generated opportunity can become a genuine preference without rewriting its opportunistic beginning. [TF-E04](#tf-e04)

### 3.3 V03: desire is a reason, but not an excuse to abandon form

The station confrontation exposes the conflict at the heart of the curriculum. Hinami’s model can treat reluctance as an obstacle to route around: modify the conditions until the task can be completed. Fumiya’s objection concerns the order of operations. Before finding a way to confess, he wants to know whether he wants that relationship. The distinction is not between action and inaction in general. It is between a technique for producing an event and a reason for making that particular event happen to these people. [TF-E03](#tf-e03)

His Japanese is instructive. He says that he wants to **prioritize** what he truly wants; he does not begin with a complete theory of authentic desire. Hinami’s claim that desires fluctuate has force for him. The narration shows him unable to refute it to his own satisfaction while still refusing to let that fact settle what matters. His confidence here is a willingness to act from an incompletely articulated value. It is not a claim to know himself perfectly, nor a discovery that logic has no place in life. [TF-E03](#tf-e03)

That distinction makes the withdrawal afterward more than hypocrisy. Fumiya has found a real defect in the method but has not yet built a stable alternative way to live. In withdrawing, he treats the learned surface and the objectionable governing system as if they had to be discarded together. Yet returning to games does not simply restore the former equilibrium. His experience has changed what he can miss. Kikuchi’s contrast between conversations that felt vividly inhabited and those that felt prepared from outside helps him see that preparation and genuineness are not mutually exclusive categories. Some prepared topics can distance her; acquired experience can also make his spontaneous descriptions more alive. Her reception is evidence about their interaction, not a universal prohibition on scripts. [TF-E03](#tf-e03)

The hybrid settlement therefore deserves its exact dialogue distribution. Fumiya wants to continue learning skills and cancel goals that contradict what he wants. **Hinami** then formulates the proposal as using skills while building goals from those wants. Fumiya names the resulting play style a hybrid. This is a negotiated account, not a fully formed doctrine delivered by him while she merely yields. He borrows Mizusawa’s language, accepts Hinami’s formulation, and owns the resulting commitment without having originated every term. The scene itself performs a version of self-authorship that is not intellectual self-sufficiency. [TF-E03](#tf-e03)

The Found gesture that follows makes the argument bodily and playful. A learned or borrowed form can communicate precisely because the two share it. Fumiya performs a favorite character’s attack rather than standing outside every role. The problem is not that he has a model. The problem is whether using the model helps him inhabit an action he chooses. His later change from Found to Jack will test whether attachment to that form can coexist with revising a competitive route. [TF-E03](#tf-e03) [TF-E17](#tf-e17)

The same volume nevertheless prevents the hybrid from receiving a blanket ethical endorsement. Fumiya’s willingness to help arrange other people’s proximity can coexist with concealment and engineered conditions. The camp material shows that owning a desired result does not automatically settle the means used to produce it. He can refuse being positioned as a piece and still position someone else. This is not an argument that all social facilitation is manipulation. It is a requirement to examine disclosure, reversibility, and whose wish actually governs the arrangement. [TF-E03](#tf-e03)

### 3.4 V04–V05: a teacher who must learn not to reproduce himself

The basketball episode makes social development concrete without granting invulnerability. Fumiya can train, participate, fail publicly, and remain in a social world afterward. The significance of a mistake depends partly on the surrounding relationship and the response it receives. Failure can be incorporated into belonging rather than operate as an expulsion. This is not proof that every audience is safe, nor that he can now laugh off any humiliation. It establishes a local capacity to survive imperfection that the opening self-model would have had difficulty imagining. [TF-E05](#tf-e05)

Yuzu’s case also challenges his tendency to move too quickly from the clarity of an argument to its social force. Responsibilities, loyalties, and desired relationships shape what a person can hear as a persuasive reason. Hinami’s ability to work with those motives can outperform his preferred account of rational persuasion without making her the final authority on what ought to be done. Fumiya gains evidence about reception; he does not prove the superiority of a life philosophy by winning or losing one conversational contest. [TF-E05](#tf-e05)

Tama’s difficulty brings the recipient problem directly into his own teaching. His first curriculum resembles the route that helped him. But her obstacle is not simply his old lack of expressive tools. Treating her as a second Fumiya risks training the wrong capacity. The revision matters because it comes from discovering why the initial intervention does not fit, not from increasing the intensity of the same exercises. Later progress involves access, curiosity, recipient-specific understanding, and public legibility, supported by several people whose contributions he cannot replace. [TF-E07](#tf-e07)

This case complicates any account of him as naturally empathic. He can care sincerely and still impose a diagnostic template. Conversely, finding the template inadequate does not make the resulting learning false. His strength is that the failure can alter his method. The fact that a technique was initially instrumental does not prevent it from becoming a medium for curiosity; the fact that a helpful change occurred does not erase questionable steps such as recording without adequate prior consent. Retrospective success cannot supply permission that was absent when the act occurred. [TF-E07](#tf-e07)

The class conflict also enlarges his moral vocabulary beyond individual improvement. In conversation with Mizusawa, he formulates the shift from an attack that would otherwise seem excessive to an act framed as **punishment**. That reformulation is Fumiya’s, with Mizusawa’s subsequent uptake; it should not be assigned wholly to the more socially fluent friend. Fumiya is beginning to see how a group manufactures standing to injure someone. Public approval can change what conduct appears permissible without changing the conduct’s effect on its target. [TF-E08](#tf-e08)

Yet he does not become the sole ethical center of the resolution. Tama’s refusal of further harm has its own authority. Hinami’s retaliation cannot be validated by saying that she loves Tama, and the end of overt hostility cannot be equated with accountability. Yuzu’s work in making coexistence possible remains valuable without becoming a substitute for acknowledgment or repair. Fumiya’s position is most defensible when it recognizes these distinct responsibilities rather than absorbing them into the story of his successful intervention. [TF-E08](#tf-e08)

Two contextual limits matter. A teacher’s questioning and local response in V04 are present; the criticism is not that no adult ever appears. What remains unestablished is an adequate institutional resolution. Likewise, Konno’s later useful rehearsal contribution is not evidence that Akiyama makes a comparable advance. Neither ordinary cooperation nor a less hostile atmosphere should be used to close an accountability line that the source leaves open. [TF-E08](#tf-e08)

### 3.5 V06–V07: an agent who can choose, lead, and fail in public

By V06, Fumiya can identify an end that is not merely social advancement: he wants Kikuchi’s work to become something shared and realized. His festival participation uses skills that were taught for other purposes, but its governing attraction is now particular. Supporting her authorship means translating constraints and possibilities without taking over the substantive decision. An opt-out matters because a proposal becomes meaningfully different when its recipient can reject it. The choice architecture is still imperfect elsewhere, especially in public-image practices where permission to take a picture and permission to publish it are not the same. [TF-E09](#tf-e09)

This phase also revises what modesty means. Mizusawa identifies a use of self-devaluation that protects Fumiya from taking other people’s interest seriously. Calling himself an implausible candidate can prevent him from acknowledging that someone else has actually chosen to care. It looks like humility while functioning as insurance against consequence. This does not mean that every self-deprecating joke is defensive pathology. His comic narration is often simply comic. The narrower issue is whether a particular disclaimer lets him avoid responding to another person’s real act. [TF-E09](#tf-e09)

Mimimi’s declaration makes that avoidance harder to sustain. He has to recognize her feeling without converting it into a debt he can pay only by reciprocating. The choice in V07 is thus not a ranking of women’s worth. He learns from distinct accounts of romance—complementarity, lived history, admiration, and person-specific desire—but none can mechanically select for him. He removes confession from task compliance and chooses Kikuchi. Hinami’s scaffold remains causally important; it no longer supplies the terminal reason. [TF-E10](#tf-e10)

His support of Kikuchi is nevertheless tested by another mistaken analogy. Her attempt to imitate broad school sociability resembles his own successful movement on the surface. He initially praises it too readily. What he misses is the direction of the wish: she is trying to become what an imagined world requires, even where doing so excludes her own preferences. The selected-habitat argument changes the objective. The point is not to declare sociability worthless or quietness inherently authentic. It is to reject universal adaptation as the only permissible way to belong. Fumiya becomes more helpful when he stops assuming that the destination he wanted must be the destination she should want. [TF-E11](#tf-e11)

Festival performance then gives his acquired confidence a bodily limit. Leadership does not eliminate the possibility that a rehearsed sequence will vanish under public attention. Mimimi’s deliberate failed joke provides a recoverable interaction, and their practiced rhythm lets him continue. This is a stronger account of competence than flawless execution: it includes preparation, a partner who knows what to do, and the ability to use that help. It is also a rebuke to retrospective solo-heroism. The recovery is not something Fumiya could have supplied to himself by believing harder. [TF-E12](#tf-e12)

The library scene with Kikuchi requires equally careful separation. Her public fictional ending communicates an indirect refusal, but it does not exhaust her actual wishes. Fumiya returns for clarification; their direct exchange reveals a conflict between desire and an ideal, and his request receives acceptance. The scene does not support a general rule that rejection is a puzzle a sufficiently insightful man should solve. Its defensible force depends on the specific movement from authored proxy to direct discussion and reciprocal choice. Nor should her acceptance be rewritten as a quoted verbal “yes” absent from the audited passage: the smile, nod, and surrounding exchange establish the response. [TF-E10](#tf-e10)

Mimimi’s contribution is a cost that the choice does not erase. She supports his return to Kikuchi despite what it means for her own hope. Fumiya’s refusal to select her as consolation respects the category of her desire more than a grateful but unowned romance would. Still, neither his choice nor her generous conduct solves their subsequent boundaries. Her continuing love and her refusal to promise that she will love him forever coexist; no waiting period or terminal stopping rule is specified. A responsible Fumiya model must be able to recognize her help without making it permanently available to him. [TF-E10](#tf-e10) [TF-E18](#tf-e18)

### 3.6 V08–V09: affection does not solve capacity, and sacrifice can be a mistake

The first Christmas, positioned between V07 and V08, supplies an important baseline. Fumiya and Kikuchi enjoy making a small, unimpressive snowman, taking a photograph, and arranging to meet again. His happiness is not contingent on turning the activity into an achievement. He uses a camera operation learned through an earlier social task to preserve a memory he now independently wants. The means retain their history while changing their purpose. That is a compact, ordinary-life demonstration of the hybrid he argued for in V03. [TF-P02](#tf-p02)

Even this tender scene contains a small misreading. After proposing the New Year visit, he offers to substitute it for an earlier meeting, partly to avoid occupying too much of her time. Kikuchi says that she wants both. He accepts the correction. His error is not always assuming that everyone should want more of him. Sometimes he underestimates a wanted claim on his time because he is trying not to impose. The later capacity problem cannot be explained by a single trait such as selfishness, inattentiveness, or low confidence; the form of his misreading changes with the situation. [TF-P02](#tf-p02)

V08 increases both his opportunities and the number of people affected by how he uses them. His exploration of professional Atafami arises from an actual preference and an expanding understanding of the field. Offline play reveals a community, a public role, and multiple ways of sustaining involvement rather than merely another ranking table. The self-search activity also has an owned purpose even though it overlaps Hinami’s assigned group-centrality metric. Wanting to explore a future with friends is not the same as wanting to become the permanent center of those friends. [TF-E14](#tf-e14)

Ashigaru’s tournament-format test distinguishes aggregate skill from executing under a singular consequential opportunity. Fumiya can play well and still alter a decision because he wants relief from pressure. He moves between seeking an escape through a high-return risk and seeking safety through excessive control. The lesson is not that the apparently rational player was secretly irrational all along. It is that a decision policy includes the felt cost of remaining uncertain. If the person’s immediate objective becomes ending that feeling, a technically available option can cease to be psychologically usable. [TF-E15](#tf-e15)

In romance, the equivalent problem is not a lack of genuine affection. Kikuchi repeatedly asks for time, information, and closeness, while Fumiya’s expanding work, gaming, and peer commitments displace those bids. He handles the collisions as local scheduling incidents rather than as a recurring structure he must govern. Each invitation can be sincere and each postponement individually explicable while the resulting relationship still communicates that one person is expected to absorb the remainder. The monograph’s judgment here concerns the pattern, not a tally that makes any particular gaming event illegitimate. [TF-E16](#tf-e16)

Rena adds an information and contact problem that must not be flattened into either infidelity or innocence. Fumiya refuses the explicit sexual proposition; attraction and inward response do not cancel that refusal or turn unwanted touch into consent. At the same time, his account to Kikuchi is materially incomplete. Calling Rena someone he has met does not communicate the boundary-relevant nature of the contact. The eventual notification demonstrates that omission, not a completed affair. His responsibility for what he tells his partner is distinct from responsibility for Rena’s decision to disregard his limits. [TF-E16](#tf-e16) [TF-E17](#tf-e17)

The first repair in V09 improves matters without solving every neighboring issue. Apology, more time, and a request not to send certain messages can address particular harms. They do not automatically define a durable contact policy. Nor does an affectionate gesture establish what Kikuchi has agreed to tolerate. Fumiya’s temptation is to read a local sign of continued closeness as permission to stop investigating the underlying uncertainty. That habit is understandable in someone anxious to preserve the relationship, but the wish for a resolved state cannot itself supply the resolution. [TF-E16](#tf-e16) [TF-E17](#tf-e17)

His response to Hinami’s engineered first conflict shows that he can set a substantive limit. He objects to making the couple’s difficulty into an instructional exercise and pauses the regular coaching meetings. This is not merely rebellion against authority; it is a refusal of a particular use of his life and Kikuchi’s feelings. The later disclosure of the coaching history to Kikuchi is made with Hinami’s permission. That distinction must remain available when V10 shows him knowingly disclosing private information to Mizusawa without it. He is neither consistently oblivious to permission nor consistently faithful to it. [TF-E17](#tf-e17) [TF-E20](#tf-e20)

V09’s most revealing mistake is the opposite of weak commitment. Fumiya can make a costly choice too quickly and too comprehensively. Leaving group chats, removing a shared symbol, and canceling social participation appear to simplify the conflict between his partnership and his expanding world. But the action treats the breadth of that world as expendable without checking whether Kikuchi wants the diminished person produced by the sacrifice. Mimimi recognizes the resemblance to her own self-amputating response to distress. Fumiya’s capacity for decisive revision is real; here, its direction is wrong. [TF-E18](#tf-e18)

Kikuchi’s renewed choice matters because it prevents him from being the only author of the relationship’s terms. He had earlier argued that he could select her despite her own restrictive ideal. Now she has to be allowed to select him, including the parts of him that complicate life with him. This does not waive her future interests or make discomfort obligatory. It makes her a participant in deciding what the relationship should preserve. The old emblems acquire force as a model of meaning made through shared history rather than evidence of a preexisting destiny. Their intimacy marks a commitment to work, not a certification that the work is complete. [TF-E19](#tf-e19)

### 3.7 V10: being helpful is not the same as having the right

Fumiya’s Hinami-facing conduct in V10 cannot be evaluated only by its benevolent aim. He explicitly recognizes that the private history he tells Mizusawa is not simply his to distribute. He nevertheless proceeds because Hinami is special and because he believes Mizusawa’s involvement may help. That awareness is ethically significant: the action is not a misunderstanding about ownership. It is a deliberate prioritization of intervention over the boundary. Naming the violation makes his decision more candid; it does not make it authorized. [TF-E20](#tf-e20)

The contrast with Hinami’s later voluntary disclosure is crucial. She can choose to tell Fumiya something after he asks to share her burden, while still refusing the answer he proposes. Consent to a conversation is not consent to onward retelling, a group theory, a recovery plan, or indefinite pursuit. Fumiya’s tendency is to experience increased access as movement toward a shared goal. The evidence allows a narrower conclusion: the relationship has acquired a particular act of reciprocity. Its future purpose remains contested. [TF-E20](#tf-e20)

The birthday project also tests whether this protagonist can remain one contributor among many. Yuzu’s planning, Mimimi and Tama’s food-related memory and labor, Nakamura and Yuzu’s message gathering, Mizusawa’s negotiation, and Kikuchi’s creative participation are not interchangeable extensions of his intelligence. They know different things, can do different things, and bear different costs. His contribution to the commissioned game is specific and valuable: preserving the system feel that matters to Hinami is more important than reproducing an impossible bespoke spectacle. That is recipient-sensitive understanding expressed through a feasible design. It is not a reason to call the whole celebration his achievement. [TF-E21](#tf-e21)

The Endo exchange gives his professional aspiration a material test. Existing skill and public visibility can be exchanged for a service; a promotional term and an audience-growth promise create obligations outside private enjoyment. Yet a recognizable professional form is not a sustainable profession. The text has not supplied a reliable income, demonstrated completion of the follower target, or settled how this route fits family support and education. Fumiya has moved from a wish to an experiment, not from a wish to a guaranteed career. This distinction protects the significance of the experiment from both dismissal and inflation. [TF-E21](#tf-e21)

His relationship with Kikuchi becomes more public and reciprocal in the same volume. He introduces her into peer, family, and emerging professional settings; both can acknowledge jealousy. Her participation in the Hinami project and willingness to allow a private conversation are chosen and costly. These improvements do not create an unlimited exemption for every future Hinami-facing act. A person can consent to a difficult exception without consenting to become the permanent absorber of the pattern that produced it. [TF-E20](#tf-e20)

The birthday’s pleasure also resists an easy verdict. It is genuine pleasure, not something exposed as false when Hinami later rejects the celebration’s implications. But its genuineness does not mean that she accepts the group’s interpretation of her life. Fumiya is strongest when he shows that formed conduct can produce real gratitude beyond its original motive. He exceeds the evidence when beneficial effects are made to stand in for a trusted basis of self-belief or a complete ethical judgment of the method. Care can reach someone and still fail to settle what the carer wants it to settle. [TF-E21](#tf-e21) [TF-E23](#tf-e23)

### 3.8 V11: the collapse of unique usefulness

The third-year environment removes some of the passive access that previously sustained the group. Hinami’s separate course and building, her nonresponse, and her school absence are practical conditions, not merely symbols. Fumiya responds with initiative: he approaches her home, develops a channel with Haruka, involves other people, and helps sustain student-council work that Hinami is no longer performing. Some of this produces concrete support. The covert contact and request for secrecy also repeat the problem of deciding that urgency gives him informational standing. [TF-E22](#tf-e22) [TF-E23](#tf-e23)

Haruka’s account matters because it identifies a beneficiary of Hinami’s example. Her effort is real, and Hinami’s role in making that effort imaginable is real. Fumiya is therefore right to resist the proposition that Hinami’s public form has no value merely because its governing system is in crisis. But the admirer’s need does not make the model responsible for remaining perfect. The same relation can provide direction and impose a burden. A useful reconstruction of Fumiya must preserve the temptation this creates for him: showing that someone needs Hinami seems like evidence that will finally make her accept herself, even though it may reproduce the demand that she justify herself by being needed. [TF-E22](#tf-e22)

His own tournament experience supplies a nearby alternative. Fourth place with Jack and an encounter with a younger admirer show that meaningful influence does not require being the undisputed winner. The value of a player’s presence can exceed a single result. This is important counterevidence to a portrait of Fumiya as simply incapable of valuing himself below first place. He can receive a more plural account of professional significance. The tragedy of the later set is that this broader lesson does not govern the particular proof he offers Hinami. [TF-E22](#tf-e22) [TF-E24](#tf-e24)

His explanation of Hinami becomes more informed, but epistemic improvement must not be confused with omniscience. The V11 family passage is framed by Hinami agreeing to tell him something, then moves into mixed third- and first-person focalized retrospective narration. It is **not** a word-for-word spoken transcript heard in full by Fumiya. The reader receives interior and narrative access that cannot automatically be transferred to him. The school-year conflict between V10 and V11 also remains unresolved, and Nagisa’s intent at the road is not determined. Fumiya can grasp an important structure of the problem without the analyst manufacturing a complete dossier of what he knows. [TF-E23](#tf-e23)

The climactic instruction nevertheless moves from understanding to authority. Fumiya asks Hinami to believe him because he is the exceptional strong character she has not been able to defeat. His care is genuine, and the willingness to stand behind it is not cowardice. Yet the proposed support substitutes his guarantee for her absent warrant. It does not establish a Hinami-owned end. The imperative **俺を信じろ**—“believe me”—is not just a louder version of his V03 negotiation over what he himself wants to do. It tells another person where to place the basis of her belief. [TF-E24](#tf-e24)

The Boxman set then invalidates that particular guarantee. Hinami loses the first two games, adapts, and wins three. This is not her first individual-game victory over him: V06 already contains one. The V11 difference is the decisive set and the role he has made unbeaten superiority play in his argument. Her win proves neither that he has no value nor that life is objectively bad. It removes the asymmetry he invoked as the reason she should trust his affirmation. Even a Fumiya victory would have preserved only that premise, not supplied the missing consent or solved the problem of grounding a life in another person’s superiority. [TF-E24](#tf-e24)

What follows reveals how personally he had invested in the premise. The loss of motivation extends even to Atafami, a more severe change than returning to it as refuge during the V03 withdrawal. His claim to be alone becomes a way of rejecting the person currently trying to reach him. During the call, he recognizes Kikuchi’s hurt and nevertheless answers from shame and defensiveness. Her statement that her words are not reaching him is followed by the call ending; the text does **not** specify who disconnects it. The injury is clear without inventing a unilateral hang-up as proof of her agency or his cruelty. [TF-E25](#tf-e25)

Kikuchi’s posted fiction reaches him by changing the position from which he encounters the problem. Its efficacy does not certify Alcia’s interior as Hinami’s literal interior. The narrative can reorganize his perspective even where the represented motives remain hypotheses. This is a crucial distinction for a character whose strengths often involve argumentative explanation: another person’s words may help not because they defeat his proposition on its own terms but because they let him inhabit a different configuration of vulnerability and agency. [TF-E25](#tf-e25)

The reconciliation then restores the personal relation before turning it into another project. Fumiya apologizes and thanks Kikuchi. She does not simply become a tool he can use to reach Hinami; her reason for writing is itself unresolved, and she distinguishes an initial reason received from him from one she hopes to discover for herself. At the final turn, she rejects both the lone-protagonist formula and the exclusive-couple formula. Their friends’ different weaknesses, strengths, fears, and experiences matter. Fumiya can recognize the familiar game logic of a party and enjoy the renewed shared joke. [TF-E26](#tf-e26)

That last pleasure is meaningful but not a release certificate. The proposed party is not yet assembled; its members have not all agreed; Hinami has not consented; the story is not completed and delivered; no school return or successful intervention is shown. The end gives Fumiya another model that may correct the previous model. The mature reading must stop before awarding him the success that the next test has not supplied. [TF-E26](#tf-e26)

<a id="current-state"></a>
## 4. Current state vector: what survives V11

The current state is **after the reconciliation and co-proposal**, not the moment of deepest withdrawal and not an imagined successful aftermath. The table separates capacities that remain evidenced from the new obligations their use creates.

| Dimension | Supported state at the boundary | What is not established |
|---|---|---|
| Technical competence | Elite Atafami experience; ability to learn and revise play; current Jack tournament transfer; capacity to recognize a technically serious opponent. | Permanent superiority, an optimal permanent main, or a result that certifies general human worth. |
| Social competence | Can initiate, coordinate, seek advice, teach with revision, participate in groups, and repair a damaged exchange. | Constant fluency, flawless reception, or a general right to bypass refusal. |
| Confidence | Has recovered enough to apologize, accept help, and commit to another attempt. Earlier willingness to act without complete proof remains part of his repertoire. | Immunity to collapse when unique usefulness or special standing is threatened. |
| Status and affiliation | Is a recognized participant in several peer and gaming relationships, with a partner and an admirer. These ties survive his temporary rejection of support. | Universal popularity, a restored group-centrality goal, or an obligation on others to remain available. |
| Self-concept | No longer adequately described by the isolated beginner; also no longer adequately described as unconditionally strong. | A settled, rank-independent answer to what makes him valuable to Hinami. |
| Agency | Owns the wish to reach Hinami and accepts a proposed plural method with Kikuchi. | Hinami’s ownership of that wish, the group’s collective consent, or a stopping rule. |
| Values in practice | Can retain useful form, recognize particular attachments, acknowledge an injury, and accept a contribution he could not produce himself. | General resolution of disclosure, fictionalization, allocation, or intervention ethics. |
| Ordinary life | Earlier taste, humor, pleasure, grooming, and companionship evidence remains part of the person model. | Proof that every ordinary routine has already resumed after the crisis. |
| Vocation | Real experimentation, a public-facing obligation, tournament experience, and an aspiration he selected. | Sustainable income, fulfilled promotional outcomes, family/education agreement, or a completed professional route. |

The important formulation is not “his progress remains despite a setback.” That can make the setback sound external to the progress. Rather, **the form his progress took generated the next vulnerability**. Becoming capable enough to help other people made unique helpfulness available as an identity claim. Becoming confident enough to choose made unilateral overcorrection possible. These are developments of the same person, not the return of an unrelated old self. <a id="operating-patterns"></a>
## 5. Stable tendencies and state-dependent behavior

### 5.1 Attention: mechanisms before atmosphere, but not mechanisms alone

Fumiya tends to notice how something works. He looks for a repeatable operation, a difference between successful and unsuccessful attempts, or a hidden variable that explains why the same words have different effects. His vocabulary of samples, skills, equipment, routes, and countermeasures is therefore not ornamental. It directs attention toward what can be changed. This helps him resist fatalism in a field he is willing to enter. It can also make an apparently insoluble situation feel approachable enough to investigate. [TF-E01](#tf-e01) [TF-E02](#tf-e02) [TF-E07](#tf-e07)

The limitation is that an efficient mechanism can become salient before the legitimacy of the desired outcome or the recipient’s actual need has been settled. Tama’s initial curriculum, the romance tasks, and the Hinami interventions test different versions of that mistake. The appropriate correction is not to replace analysis with vague empathy. It is to include the other person’s goal, information, permission, and capacity to refuse among the facts the model must explain. His best revisions move in that direction; his crisis conduct does not consistently maintain it. [TF-E03](#tf-e03) [TF-E07](#tf-e07) [TF-E23](#tf-e23)

He also notices particularity in ways a purely instrumental portrait cannot explain. The early Mimimi/Tama comparison refuses to treat one young woman as a sufficient sample. His developing interest in Kikuchi’s writing attends to what her work wants to become. The small Christmas snowman matters because of the shared making, not because it exemplifies a universal strategy for intimacy. His mechanism-seeking mind does not exclude affection; it can either serve affection or crowd out what affection has not yet understood. [TF-E02](#tf-e02) [TF-E09](#tf-e09) [TF-P02](#tf-p02)

### 5.2 Learning: observation, imitation, trial, and eventually intuition

His learning is neither purely verbal nor purely experiential. He can borrow a form, attempt it before it feels natural, review the response, and gradually act without rehearsing every component. The clothing supplement offers an unusually clear intermediate state. He can make a plausible selection while lacking some of the vocabulary or failing to consider the rear view of an outfit. Action, explicit explanation, and intuitive discrimination develop at different speeds. A model that treats him as either a helpless copier or a fully self-sufficient expert misses that unevenness. [TF-P01](#tf-p01)

He also seeks learning conditions, not just willpower. Paying with his own wages makes waste salient; visiting stores alone creates repeated occasions to compare outfits; receiving a clerk’s advice supplies feedback he cannot generate himself. In his own domain, repeated examples can eventually become an articulated pattern. Outside it, the same process must begin with genuine uncertainty. He is capable of accepting that uncertainty when it does not imply that his whole person is worthless. [TF-P01](#tf-p01)

This pattern is conditional rather than universal. A person he regards as technically credible can make correction easier to receive; a response experienced as dismissal of his underlying wish can make the same analytic tone intolerable. V03 does not show an aversion to all criticism. It shows a wish that his reason for acting be heard as more than an obstacle to efficient progress. V11 reverses the position: Kikuchi’s wish to be heard is one he temporarily fails to honor. [TF-E03](#tf-e03) [TF-E25](#tf-e25)

### 5.3 Pride, shame, and the difference between failing and being disqualified

Fumiya’s competitive pride is not reducible to a demand to win every event. He can accept a defeat, learn from it, and continue; he can make a character change that temporarily worsens his performance. What raises the danger is a change in what the result is taken to mean. A lost match can be information. A lost match made to decide whether he is uniquely qualified to save Hinami becomes a verdict on a role around which he has organized himself. [TF-E15](#tf-e15) [TF-E17](#tf-e17) [TF-E24](#tf-e24)

Shame likewise has several expressions. In low-stakes narration it can produce an exaggerated joke about how inadequate his face would look in a sophisticated outfit. In romance it can encourage disclaimers that reduce the significance of another person’s interest. At peak distress it can turn into exclusionary speech: the partner whose hurt he has just perceived is told that she cannot understand. These are related through threatened self-presentation, but they should not be collapsed into one identical defense. The text gives different stakes, audiences, and consequences. [TF-P01](#tf-p01) [TF-E09](#tf-e09) [TF-E25](#tf-e25)

### 5.4 Goal hierarchy: revisable, not a permanent ranked list

By the later volumes, several ends are clearly person-owned: Atafami and a possible professional route; a relationship with Kikuchi; particular friendships; and knowing and reaching Hinami. Their conflicts cannot be solved by asserting that only one is genuine. Assigned centrality does not survive the same test: when he abandons the metric, the value of the relationships remains. That difference gives a better model than either “he wants popularity” or “he does not care about social standing.” He can want access, recognition, and participation without wanting to remain the group’s organizing center as a terminal achievement. [TF-E14](#tf-e14) [TF-E18](#tf-e18)

His exceptional Hinami-facing priority must be taken seriously without an imposed romantic category. Gratitude, respect, rivalry, identification, curiosity, obligation, and a wish for her flourishing are not mutually exclusive. Some of his own formulations make her importance exceed romance as a priority; they do not automatically define that importance as romance. Nor can an inward qualification settle what another listener believes. His statement to Haruka that he likes Hinami is spoken; the “as a person” qualification is not. [TF-E17](#tf-e17) [TF-E20](#tf-e20) [TF-E23](#tf-e23)

### 5.5 Emotional regulation: action is a resource and an escape

Fumiya often regulates difficulty by making the next action concrete. Practice, research, an invitation, a revised task, or a conversation can keep uncertainty from becoming paralysis. This is one reason the early bad-game verdict is so costly: it closes the field in which he could otherwise act. It is also why the V11 loss of desire even to play is a serious state change rather than ordinary discouragement. [TF-E01](#tf-e01) [TF-E25](#tf-e25)

But action can also function as avoidance. A drastic change can relieve the pressure of not knowing how to preserve several relationships at once. An explanation can displace the harder task of hearing someone’s objection. A promise to carry everything can keep him from acknowledging that he does not control the outcome. The useful distinction is therefore not active versus passive. It is **whether the action remains responsive to the person and problem, or chiefly restores Fumiya’s sense that he is able to act**. That is an analytical formulation supported by the contrast between his revisable teaching, his V09 peer deletion, and his V11 guarantee. [TF-E07](#tf-e07) [TF-E18](#tf-e18) [TF-E24](#tf-e24)

### 5.6 Moral disposition: sincerity plus proportionality, with inconsistent jurisdiction

Fumiya repeatedly objects when the method no longer serves an owned wish or when a successful intervention adds unnecessary injury. His criticism of Hinami’s excessive retaliation is not cancelled by acknowledging her effectiveness. His refusal to treat Mimimi’s care as a reason to choose her romantically similarly preserves a distinction between gratitude and reciprocation. These are substantial ethical capacities. [TF-E08](#tf-e08) [TF-E10](#tf-e10)

The weakness is not an absence of moral concern but an unstable account of jurisdiction: when does caring authorize acting on, for, or around another person? He can correctly defend his own right to refuse an imposed goal and later decide that Hinami’s exceptional importance authorizes crossing her boundary. He can know the act is a violation and proceed. That makes the case more difficult than correcting a missing rule. His hierarchy of values can itself put intervention above permission. The monograph cannot declare that hierarchy resolved merely because the proposed intervention becomes collective. [TF-E20](#tf-e20) [TF-E23](#tf-e23) [TF-E26](#tf-e26)

<a id="six-dimensions"></a>
## 6. Competence, confidence, status, self-concept, agency, and values

These six dimensions must remain separate because Fumiya’s story repeatedly changes one without changing all the others. “He became confident” is too imprecise to explain why he can enter a tournament, mishandle his girlfriend’s request, lose to Hinami, and then refuse comfort from someone who still values him.

**Competence** concerns what he can do. Gaming skill is present at the beginning; social skills, public coordination, and recipient-sensitive teaching expand later. A failed act can reveal the boundary of a competence without erasing it. Festival recovery is evidence of competence partly constituted by a partner. The Boxman defeat is evidence of an opponent’s successful adaptation, not proof that Fumiya’s earlier record was imaginary. [TF-E01](#tf-e01) [TF-E12](#tf-e12) [TF-E24](#tf-e24)

**Confidence** concerns his ability to act while an outcome or judgment remains uncertain. It can increase before mastery, as in trying a learned social behavior, and it can remain domain-specific after broad success. Ashigaru’s account of his willingness to change identifies a real capacity, but it remains an attributed interpretation rather than an objective “strong character” classification. The V11 collapse revises its generality. Confidence in changing a method is not the same as confidence that one still matters after a special role fails. [TF-E17](#tf-e17) [TF-E24](#tf-e24)

**Status** concerns how other people position and recognize him. Public leadership, a partner, peers who seek him out, and a younger admirer all differ from the opening position. These are real social changes. They neither guarantee inner security nor make social centrality his owned aim. The V11 call is especially revealing because his assertion of isolation occurs inside a continuing relationship. His self-description and his relational situation are in conflict; the monograph must preserve both rather than choose the one that produces the simpler portrait. [TF-E18](#tf-e18) [TF-E22](#tf-e22) [TF-E25](#tf-e25)

**Self-concept** concerns what kind of person he takes himself to be and why that person has value. “Nanashi” initially protects a realm of earned competence from the supposedly unfair social world. Later, the gamer and social person become less separable. The danger is that “the person who can change” or “the only person who can reach Hinami” can become another narrow certificate. V11 does not return him to the original certificate unchanged. It shows how a newer, more relational account of value can inherit the old need for a protected exceptional domain. [TF-E01](#tf-e01) [TF-E17](#tf-e17) [TF-E24](#tf-e24)

**Agency** concerns his role in selecting and revising action. Fumiya acquires real agency when he rejects a feasible confession task, proposes an alternative basis for goals, chooses festival work, selects Kikuchi, and explores professional gaming. The same dimension is present when he makes a bad choice knowingly. Unauthorized disclosure is not evidence that he lacks agency; it is a use of agency that needs ethical evaluation. Treating every self-authored act as liberation would make the category incapable of criticizing his most important failures. [TF-E03](#tf-e03) [TF-E09](#tf-e09) [TF-E20](#tf-e20)

**Values** concern the ends and constraints he believes should govern those choices. He increasingly values owned desire, particular relationships, sincerity that can use form, proportionality, and the possibility of revision. Values can be articulated before they are reliably practiced. He can argue against turning a person into a task and later make Hinami’s recovery the task through which he tries to prove himself. The central developmental question is not whether his principles are sincere, but whether he can notice when his conduct violates a distinction he understands elsewhere. [TF-E03](#tf-e03) [TF-E23](#tf-e23)

The six-dimensional model also clarifies why “introvert becomes extrovert” is inadequate. Increased participation does not establish a stable global preference for high stimulation or large groups. Technical pride does not establish general self-esteem. Kindness does not establish good judgment. A mature account must identify **which capacity, in which state, with which recipient, under which pressure** is being discussed.

<a id="voice"></a>
## 7. Speech, focalization, and self-presentation

### 7.1 His analytical voice is itself a social act

Fumiya’s characteristic speech is not limited to fluent statements of the novels’ themes. His early participation often has a delay: he has to work out what question is actually being asked and what response is available. When Mimimi asks for an immediate judgment of Tama, his response begins with uncertainty and becomes an unexpectedly formal observation about variation among people. The words are direct enough to be his own, awkward enough not to be a polished performance, and specific enough to be useful characterization. [TF-E02](#tf-e02)

The comedy depends on a mismatch between analytical scale and conversational scale. A casual exchange receives an explanation about empathy and the danger of generalizing from one sample. Mimimi enjoys the mismatch; Tama does not simply duplicate her reaction. This is important to a reconstructive account of humor. Fumiya is not uniformly funny or unfunny. His literalness, abstraction, and unexpected seriousness can become engaging when the recipient enjoys them, while producing distance or irritation elsewhere. A forecast of his speech should therefore specify the audience, not merely insert game references into every line.

His expertise can make the same structural habit forceful rather than awkward. In Atafami, analyzing an opponent or correcting a proposed explanation is appropriate to the shared activity. With Hinami, technical vocabulary becomes a compact language for testing claims and teasing one another. In a vulnerable romantic exchange, an explanation can fail if it bypasses the emotional question being asked. This is not because a different personality appears in each scene. The same preference for getting the structure right encounters different criteria of relevance. [TF-E01](#tf-e01) [TF-E03](#tf-e03) [TF-E25](#tf-e25)

### 7.2 Casual forms, address, and the limits of an idiolect claim

The directly checked passages support ordinary first-person **俺**, casual formulations with Hinami, and the contrast between conversational address and the labels of their shared game world. His use of **NO NAME** or **nanashi** in their exchanges can reactivate the relationship in which earned competence and reciprocal challenge first mattered. **Kikuchi-san** remains a recognizable address in the cited material, while **Haruka-chan** marks a different age and familiarity relation. These are attested features, not a claim that this document has measured every address form across thirteen books. [TF-E03](#tf-e03) [TF-E23](#tf-e23) [TF-P02](#tf-p02)

A systematic idiolect would require more than the twenty-four selected records in the shared voice ledger. This monograph consequently does not assign an exclusive catchphrase, a precise frequency of particles, a fixed politeness rule for all adults, or a complete inventory of recurrent verbal tics. What it can establish is a set of interactional contrasts: hesitant entry versus domain-authoritative explanation; preparatory speech versus an inhabited account of something he enjoyed; negotiative preference versus directive guarantee; private qualification versus what the listener actually hears.

### 7.3 The force of a modal: wanting, proposing, and commanding

The V03 station passage contains a modest but important verbal form. Fumiya’s **優先したい** expresses what he wants to prioritize. It does not prove an external law of authentic living. His refusal rests partly on an intuition he cannot fully defend in Hinami’s preferred argumentative terms. In the later negotiation, **なしにしたい** makes the conflict with certain goals a proposal about the conditions under which he will continue. Hinami’s paraphrase and his acceptance distribute the authorship of the settlement. [TF-E03](#tf-e03)

By contrast, V11’s **俺を信じろ** is imperative. The altered grammatical force corresponds to a changed relationship to the decision. He is no longer principally deciding what goal may govern his own conduct; he is directing where Hinami should place trust. The phrase is emotionally generous in one respect—he offers to bear something—but that generosity does not remove its claim to authority. The primary scene explicitly links it to his unique unbeaten status. The reading need not pretend that every imperative is coercive to observe how this one functions in its particular argument. [TF-E24](#tf-e24)

The distinction guards against a recurrent literary shortcut: treating increasingly decisive speech as an uncomplicated index of maturity. Fumiya’s ability to say something firmly grows. Whether firmness is appropriate depends on what he is asserting and whose decision the assertion would replace.

### 7.4 Speech and inward narration must not be merged

The Haruka exchange supplies the clearest test. Asked whether he likes her sister, he says yes. Inwardly, he regards a qualification about liking Hinami as a person as unnecessary to add. That qualification helps reconstruct **his represented understanding of his answer**. It is not information Haruka receives. An account that writes “he tells Haruka he likes Hinami as a person” closes a communicative ambiguity the source deliberately leaves open. [TF-E23](#tf-e23)

His inward commentary can also identify a failure at the moment he commits it. During the V11 phone call, he recognizes Kikuchi’s hurt and describes his own words as a response to shame and weakness. That recognition should not be promoted into successful self-regulation. He knows more than he can use well in that state. Conversely, the narration is not grounds for assuming that Kikuchi hears the remorse or explanatory language inside his head. She receives the words that reject her. [TF-E25](#tf-e25)

Nor should another character’s emotional description become a complete account of him. Ashigaru’s “strong character” interpretation, Kikuchi’s account of his speech, and Mimimi’s explanation of what his withdrawal erases are valuable because they reveal things his self-theory misses. Their force comes from situated observation and consequence, not from omniscient authority. A monograph should let them contest him without turning any one of them into the final diagnostic voice. [TF-E03](#tf-e03) [TF-E17](#tf-e17) [TF-E18](#tf-e18)

### 7.5 Humor and embodied talk

The clothing story gives sustained evidence that Fumiya’s ordinary voice can be playful rather than therapeutic or argumentative. He repeats unfamiliar fashion terms, experiences Hinami’s explanation as almost another language, and converts clothing advice into a game analogy. When an apparent success falls apart at the fitting stage, he makes the joke of leveling up in battle; Hinami corrects the setting to an equipment shop, and he extends the premise rather than abandoning it. Their teasing is jointly produced. It depends on both shared vocabulary and freedom to correct each other. [TF-P01](#tf-p01)

The comparison with the Christmas photo is revealing. There, his proudly low threshold that the picture is not blurred is part of the joke. He is not displaying mastery to secure Kikuchi’s admiration. He is enjoying the event with her and letting a small inadequacy remain small. The capacity to be lightly ridiculous in a welcomed interaction belongs beside his high-stakes defensiveness. It is one reason a reconstruction that produces only solemn, analytic responses would not sound like the person evidenced by the corpus. [TF-P02](#tf-p02)

Physical presentation is also not just an external shell. Posture, expression, clothes, gesture, and the ability to perform a familiar pattern under pressure change what actions are available to him. His Found gesture can close an argument playfully; a stage freeze can defeat memorized form; practiced rhythm with Mimimi can restore movement. The body is neither an infallible truth beneath speech nor a surface that can be ignored in favor of doctrine. It is part of the medium in which he becomes able to act. [TF-E03](#tf-e03) [TF-E12](#tf-e12)

<a id="relationships"></a>
## 8. Relationship-conditioned reconstruction

This section owns the variation in **Fumiya’s** behavior. It does not substitute for the independent dyadic and ensemble syntheses required by R07–R08. Each partner has an independent trajectory and cannot be exhausted by the function they perform for him.

### 8.1 Hinami: student, rival, beneficiary, interpreter, and would-be guarantor

**Fumiya → Hinami.** His relation begins with respect for demonstrated gaming effort and becomes gratitude toward someone who gives him access to a wider life. Curiosity, disagreement, admiration, rivalry, and a wish that she experience the world differently accumulate rather than replace one another. He can challenge her because he does not approach her only as the socially inferior student. Their shared domain supplies a basis on which his judgments already have force. [TF-E01](#tf-e01) [TF-E03](#tf-e03)

**Hinami → Fumiya.** Coaching gives her a project through which the generality of her method can be tested, but the reproducibility account does not prove that all resulting value is instrumental. Shared play, frustration, enjoyment, and the value attached to nanashi’s style exceed a description of him as a neutral experimental subject. At the same time, those effects do not erase the instrumental origin or authorize later uses of his relationship. [TF-E17](#tf-e17) [TF-E21](#tf-e21) [TF-E24](#tf-e24)

The informational relation remains uneven. He knows things other peers do not, but privileged access is not total access. His V10 revision that NO NAME may itself be a controlled mode matters because it removes the easy belief that intimacy with the gamer guarantees contact with the whole person. The V11 mixed-person retrospective creates a further distinction between reader access and his access. A reconstructed Fumiya may reason from disclosures and observed behavior; he may not simply possess every family interior the reader has seen. [TF-E21](#tf-e21) [TF-E23](#tf-e23)

His characteristic failure toward Hinami is an escalation of standing. Gratitude becomes a wish to repay; knowledge becomes a belief that he sees the problem others cannot; specialness becomes permission to cross a boundary; competitive superiority becomes a warrant to affirm her. Each transition is understandable, but none is automatic. The V11 defeat exposes the final transition while the earlier privacy problem remains. A credible novel-situation model must therefore allow him to offer informed, caring help and still become too directive when he believes that failing to act would abandon her. [TF-E20](#tf-e20) [TF-E23](#tf-e23) [TF-E24](#tf-e24)

The relationship is exceptional without receiving a final romantic classification. Neither his inward distinction nor his continuing relationship with Kikuchi makes every possible future feeling impossible. Neither intensity nor rivalry makes romance already established. Through V11, the appropriate account is a multiply determined, unusually prioritized bond whose kind and acceptable practical limits remain contested. [TF-E17](#tf-e17) [TF-E23](#tf-e23)

### 8.2 Kikuchi: the desired partner whose words he must allow to matter

**Fumiya → Kikuchi.** He values a quiet, literary, and increasingly collaborative form of intimacy. The interest is not merely compensation for social failure elsewhere. He wants her work to be realized, chooses her romantically, enjoys minor shared activities, desires future time, and brings her into a wider life. He can also idealize what their complementarity will solve and underestimate the practical maintenance required after successful courtship. [TF-E09](#tf-e09) [TF-E10](#tf-e10) [TF-P02](#tf-p02) [TF-E16](#tf-e16)

**Kikuchi → Fumiya.** She does not merely validate his growth. Her account of his speech helps him revise authenticity; her choice of him challenges his unilateral sacrifice; her wish for information exposes what he has not disclosed; her fiction reaches him after direct speech fails. Each action has a cost and a motive of her own. The later publication opportunity and uncertainty about why she writes cannot be treated as convenient resources for his Hinami project. [TF-E03](#tf-e03) [TF-E19](#tf-e19) [TF-E25](#tf-e25) [TF-E26](#tf-e26)

The directional asymmetry of information is central. Fumiya can know that he refuses Rena and still fail to give Kikuchi the context she needs. Kikuchi can know about Mimimi’s confession through her own conversation without Fumiya knowing that she knows. More generally, a reader’s access to private supplemental material does not create mutual disclosure inside the couple. Their repeated difficulty is not simply deficient honesty as a moral trait. It is failure to build a shared account of what each person needs to know and may reasonably ask. [TF-E13](#tf-e13) [TF-E16](#tf-e16)

His characteristic danger toward Kikuchi changes with pressure. Under expanding opportunity, he treats her bids as locally postponable. Under guilt, he may remove too much of himself to spare her. Under defeat, he may treat her inability to occupy his exact position as proof that she cannot reach him. These are three different failures of reciprocity. The correction is not always “give her more time” or “be more independent.” It requires responding to the actual claim she is making rather than selecting a solution from his own fear. [TF-E16](#tf-e16) [TF-E18](#tf-e18) [TF-E25](#tf-e25)

At the endpoint, apology and gratitude reopen this relation. They do not erase the call or settle the future balance between romance, vocation, and Hinami. The strongest supported claim is that Fumiya can again recognize Kikuchi as someone whose contribution changes his model, not just someone who should support the model he already holds. [TF-E26](#tf-e26)

### 8.3 Mimimi: comic reciprocity, unreciprocated love, and the correction he cannot supply alone

**Fumiya → Mimimi.** He responds to her energy, takes her ambition seriously, works with her, and develops a rhythm that can be useful under public pressure. His early inability to distinguish her comparative need from his own mastery orientation contributes to a mistaken effort prescription. Later, he recognizes her romantic feeling without making her support a reason to select a relationship he does not want. Neither mistake nor non-selection cancels the friendship’s specificity. [TF-E04](#tf-e04) [TF-E10](#tf-e10) [TF-E12](#tf-e12)

**Mimimi → Fumiya.** She is not simply the energetic friend who raises his confidence. She supplies a kind of lived criticism that he cannot derive from game optimization alone. Her response to his peer withdrawal recognizes the danger of cutting away a desired part of oneself in order to stop a conflict. Her intervention protects something she values in him while her own romantic desire remains active. The ethical significance depends on that cost; declaring her “over him” would make the act easier than the evidence does. [TF-E18](#tf-e18)

The pair’s ease must therefore remain boundary-bearing. Mimimi’s decision to end a habitual one-to-one walk is a particular act of restraint under a particular relationship configuration. It is neither a complete prohibition on future closeness nor proof that continuing love has acquired a terminal rule. Fumiya should not be reconstructed as entitled to resume the old pattern because a later interaction is warm. Nor should every warm interaction be read as hidden pursuit. What matters is the action, its context, and the knowledge available to each person. [TF-E18](#tf-e18)

Her relation with Tama also limits his centrality. Tama’s role in Mimimi’s self-worth and ordinary life is not interchangeable with his. He can learn from it and help create space for it, but a monograph about him must not make the dyad’s meaning one more instance of his successful insight. That is one reason the ensemble responsibility needs an independent study. [TF-E04](#tf-e04)

### 8.4 Tama: learner fit, principled refusal, and a person he must not redesign

Fumiya’s relation with Tama exposes whether he can help someone without assuming that becoming more like him is improvement. Her directness is not simply a deficient first draft of his acquired social competence. Her wish to adapt is connected to people she cares about, and her refusal of further revenge remains her own. He becomes a better teacher by revising the fit between means and recipient; he would misunderstand that success by claiming to have supplied her integrity. [TF-E05](#tf-e05) [TF-E07](#tf-e07) [TF-E08](#tf-e08)

Tama also supplies an ordinary-life counterweight to his abstraction. Craft, household work, joking, and selective openness show that a person’s adaptation may serve a liked activity rather than a hierarchy of social approval. Her contribution to the birthday is grounded in labor and memory rather than in Fumiya’s privileged interpretation of Hinami. His most adequate relation to her is therefore neither instructor nor moral pupil alone. It is a changing exchange in which he can contribute a method while having to respect a value and a competence he did not create. [TF-E14](#tf-e14) [TF-E21](#tf-e21)

### 8.5 Mizusawa: the fluent peer who makes Fumiya answer for his evasions

Mizusawa is especially important because he can supply social form while questioning the use Fumiya makes of it. His critique of self-deprecation forces Fumiya to treat other people’s interest as real. His account of romance and later disclosure of his own Hinami-facing wish also prevent fluent speech from being mistaken for complete emotional self-knowledge. Fumiya has an interlocutor who is neither an all-knowing mentor nor a novice to be trained. [TF-E09](#tf-e09) [TF-E19](#tf-e19) [TF-E22](#tf-e22)

Their discussions are co-produced. Mizusawa helps articulate how a group acquires permission to attack; Fumiya supplies the punishment reformulation. Mizusawa’s successful negotiation in V10 creates something Fumiya could not simply have obtained by possessing gaming skill. Conversely, Fumiya’s access to Hinami makes him the person from whom Mizusawa seeks information. That request creates a temptation to mistake trust in the recipient for permission from the subject. [TF-E08](#tf-e08) [TF-E20](#tf-e20) [TF-E21](#tf-e21)

Mizusawa’s romantic history must not be rewritten to heighten the later scene. He already confessed and was refused in V03; the anticipated V11 declaration is renewed, and it is not shown taking place. Fumiya’s knowledge of his friend’s love supplies a reason to take him seriously, not a license to decide the route or outcome for him. [TF-E27](#tf-e27)

### 8.6 Yuzu, Nakamura, Takei, and the ordinary network

Yuzu is an early recipient of Fumiya’s freely offered competence and the listener to his qualified good-game verdict. Later, she and Nakamura can help him see why technical innocence does not resolve his partner’s fear. Her coordinating work gives many relationships a practical setting in which they can exist. Treating that work as ambient kindness would conceal the labor on which his opportunities depend. [TF-E02](#tf-e02) [TF-E17](#tf-e17) [TF-E21](#tf-e21)

Nakamura’s relation with him is not reducible to defeated rival or proof of rising status. Recognition develops through practice and later peer life; Nakamura can also object to the distribution of blame when Yuzu is hurt. Takei’s unguarded enthusiasm and participation supply capacities different from Fumiya’s analytic planning. A functioning group does not require every member to become strategically sophisticated in the same way. [TF-E04](#tf-e04) [TF-E12](#tf-e12) [TF-E22](#tf-e22)

For Fumiya, these relations matter precisely because they need not all be organized around his exceptional role. The pain of leaving their structures in V09 and the continued availability of the network in V11 show that ordinary affiliation is not a decorative reward for completing major character arcs. It is part of what his decisions can preserve or damage. [TF-E18](#tf-e18) [TF-E22](#tf-e22)

### 8.7 Rena: resistance without a falsely completed boundary story

Rena elicits attraction, discomfort, uncertainty, and explicit refusal. These states are compatible. Fumiya’s inward bodily response is not consent, and his difficulty managing continuing contact does not make him responsible for her boundary violations. A serious account must preserve his refusals as real actions rather than recoding them as ineffective signs that he secretly wanted the conduct. [TF-E16](#tf-e16) [TF-E17](#tf-e17)

His own responsibility is narrower and still consequential: how fully he informs Kikuchi, what prospective contact rule he states, and whether he distinguishes immediate resistance from a durable arrangement. A declined invitation, a request not to send a type of message, refusal of unwanted touch, and the later arm-contact incident are not one continuous undifferentiated episode. V11 again shows resistance and Kikuchi’s direct objection; it does not show Rena adopting a broad rule. Predictive reconstruction should therefore describe Fumiya’s demonstrated ability to refuse while leaving future contact management uncertain. [TF-E17](#tf-e17) [TF-E25](#tf-e25)

### 8.8 Ashigaru and public apprenticeship

Ashigaru gives Fumiya a context in which correction need not destroy pride. He can recognize elite skill while distinguishing the additional demands of consequential competition and professional life. Fumiya responds not by discarding Atafami but by widening what serious commitment to it requires. His future is no longer answered by being good alone. It has to include a viable route and a public relationship to other people’s attention. [TF-E15](#tf-e15) [TF-E17](#tf-e17) [TF-E22](#tf-e22)

The advice remains attributed. Ashigaru’s definitions of romance or strong-character confidence are useful interventions, not universal truths that the monograph should silently adopt. Their importance lies partly in how later events test their reach. Fumiya can accept a model, profit from it, and still encounter a situation that requires narrowing it. That is the same process the monograph asks the reader to use when reconstructing him.

<a id="ordinary-life"></a>
## 9. Ordinary life, preferences, and material habits

### 9.1 Why the mundane evidence changes the account

Without ordinary-life evidence, Fumiya can become an abstract machine that optimizes, fails, and learns consent. That reconstruction would miss why any of the learning matters to him. He wants a world he can enjoy and inhabit, not merely a defensible method for navigating it. His preferences are not all stable from V01, and some emerge through activities initially undertaken for instrumental reasons. Development can create a real taste. It need not reveal a preference that was fully formed all along. [TF-E04](#tf-e04) [TF-P01](#tf-p01) [TF-P02](#tf-p02)

This section distinguishes a **durable interest**, a **repeated or developing practice**, an **observed occasion**, and an **analytical inference**. Those are evidentiary strengths, not grades of how important a detail is. A single occasion can be highly revealing without establishing a habit. The shared snowman is one such occasion: it shows that unproductive silliness can be intrinsically wanted in a particular relationship, not that building snowmen is a general hobby.

### 9.2 Games: enjoyment, craft, expenditure, and attachment

Atafami is a durable interest and a major practiced competence before the social project begins. It supplies enjoyment, a field in which effort is legible, a language for interpreting unfamiliar tasks, a relationship with Hinami, and eventually a possible vocation. These functions should not be collapsed. A person can enjoy a game, use it as refuge, and turn its results into a dangerous certificate of worth at different moments. The loss of motivation in V11 is serious precisely because the activity has ordinarily served more than one of those functions. [TF-E01](#tf-e01) [TF-E15](#tf-e15) [TF-E24](#tf-e24) [TF-E25](#tf-e25)

The clothing supplement supplies a particularly useful qualification to the image of relentless discipline. Fumiya’s reflection contrasts his own need for enjoyment in sustained gaming practice with Hinami’s unusually relentless effort. His absorption is not adequately modeled as pure self-denial in pursuit of rank. An account of how he might approach a new game should therefore consider not only whether it offers a measurable ladder but whether its activity becomes compelling to him. The source does not justify predicting that he would grind indefinitely at an activity he finds empty merely because a ranking exists. [TF-P01](#tf-p01)

The same scene shows that saved money has concrete leisure value: he thinks of arcade spending and downloadable content. This is direct evidence of priorities on that occasion, not a complete household budget or proof of a universal spending rule. It does, however, make his attention to clothing prices person-specific. Saving is not an abstract virtue in the passage. Lower cost leaves room for things he likes. [TF-P01](#tf-p01)

His attachment to Found includes style, shared recognition, and an embodied model he can imitate. The Found-to-Jack experiment does not make that earlier liking false. It shows that an admired or familiar form can be relinquished as a primary competitive route when he believes a different route serves a valued future. Preference, identification, and tactical selection are related but not identical. A reconstruction that has him choose whatever maximizes the next result without any felt attachment would be too thin; one that makes him incapable of changing a beloved form would contradict V09. [TF-E03](#tf-e03) [TF-E17](#tf-e17)

### 9.3 Clothing: emerging taste rather than a borrowed costume

The early-second-term shopping story in V06.5 is sustained evidence of partial independence. Fumiya has already bought outfits with part-time earnings and can begin to make a selection within a learned framework. He is not yet fluent in the language of fashion. Distinctions among terms for trousers, bottoms, or a garment category can outrun his vocabulary, while visual judgments sometimes work before he can explain them. This unevenness is more informative than calling him either stylish or unstylish. [TF-P01](#tf-p01)

He responds to black as cool in a way connected to his own otaku taste. He considers a coat partly through the impression of its material, works with a simple monochrome arrangement, and later purchases a black Chesterfield coat, a fitting white knit, red socks, and a bordeaux snood. These are **observed selections in one shopping episode**. They do not establish black as his immutable favorite color, luxury fabric as a stable preference, or that particular combination as his habitual uniform. The story is about learning to choose, not a catalogue of permanent brand loyalty. [TF-P01](#tf-p01)

The errors matter as much as the successful purchase. Looking from the front, he misses an effect visible from behind. Socks initially register more as functional than as part of an outfit. Clothes that seem promising off the body fail at the fitting stage. His new vocabulary can arrive before his judgment is reliable: calling something oversized or fashionable does not make the fit correct. This lets the monograph distinguish presentation knowledge, practical discrimination, and confidence. They develop together imperfectly. [TF-P01](#tf-p01)

The fact that he shops alone elsewhere and receives and uses clerk feedback prevents the story from remaining an image of total dependence on Hinami. He can identify the level at which his judgment fails—obvious problems versus finer distinctions—and use that feedback. Her instruction has become part of an environment in which he continues learning when she is absent. That is a stronger sign of acquired agency than merely wearing something she selected. [TF-P01](#tf-p01)

The passage also permits a bounded personality inference. He is capable of enjoying the problem of matching, of incorporating the cost of mistakes, and of turning correction into banter. It does not prove a lifelong passion for clothes. The valuable middle claim is that a domain entered for social reasons can become something in which he has real, growing discrimination and local pleasure.

### 9.4 Reading and creative company

Andy’s books are not simply a password that permanently retains its original deceptive function. Fumiya’s subsequent reading and his engagement with Kikuchi’s work support a genuine literary interest. The quiet shared space becomes a setting in which he can speak from something he has experienced rather than merely deploy a topic. Later, he values the realization of her authored work enough to take on practical responsibilities. [TF-E02](#tf-e02) [TF-E04](#tf-e04) [TF-E09](#tf-e09)

That evidence does not establish that he was always a devoted reader, that all literary genres interest him equally, or that he has a complete independent literary vocation. Nor should the efficacy of Kikuchi’s V11 fiction be made into a general preference for fiction over direct conversation. Its effect occurs under a specific defensive condition. Ordinarily, the two use both conversation and fiction, and each medium has limits. [TF-E25](#tf-e25)

His support of creative work is also not pure selfless administration. He wants to see something he values exist in the world. That desire can make labor enjoyable and make another person’s project partly his own without transferring authorship. The distinction is central to reconstructing his likely response to a friend’s creative request: enthusiasm does not automatically imply a wish to control, but successful coordination must still leave the creator’s decisions intact. [TF-E09](#tf-e09)

### 9.5 Small shared pleasures and the wish for another meeting

The Christmas snowman is visibly unimpressive. Fumiya and Kikuchi make a small form, add eyes, laugh at its oddness, photograph it, and leave it behind because it cannot accompany them. He values the time spent doing something silly with her. The memory does not require a correct technique or an impressive product. The scene establishes a capacity for affection through joint attention to an ordinary object. [TF-P02](#tf-p02)

He also uses learned competence unobtrusively. The quick camera access he knows through earlier Instagram-related training serves the impulse to preserve this particular moment. This is neither a return to an untrained true self nor a calculated manufacture of intimacy. The meaningful action is both learned and wanted. The photograph’s warmth later matters through his experience of it, not through a supernatural property or an objective measure of the relationship’s durability. [TF-P02](#tf-p02)

The scheduling exchange gives his ordinary relational style additional texture. He wants the earliest feasible next meeting, but worries that offering both an earlier date and New Year time may take too much from her. Her clarification that she wants both is easy for him to accept in this low-pressure setting. This small successful correction is useful counterevidence to later unilateral decision-making. Fumiya is not constitutionally unable to hear another person’s preference. The difficulty arises when guilt, exceptional stakes, or a threatened self-model make the other person’s answer harder for him to incorporate. [TF-P02](#tf-p02) [TF-E18](#tf-e18) [TF-E25](#tf-e25)

His decision not to pursue the reason for Kikuchi’s apology to Hinami at the party also deserves a narrow reading. He does not ask, partly because he is in a happy mood and feels it is not something he should intrude upon. This is an observed restraint, not proof of a settled privacy doctrine. It contrasts with later intrusion precisely because context and motivation alter his willingness to leave an uncertainty alone. The comparison should not turn one ordinary choice into an ethical certificate. [TF-P02](#tf-p02)

### 9.6 Solitude, company, and ordinary variation

Fumiya’s early solitary gaming life does not establish that all later sociability is a performance against his preference. The developing pleasure of friends, quiet literary company, romantic time, collaborative performance, and a gaming community is directly consequential. At the same time, the corpus does not demonstrate a global conversion into someone who always prefers a large group or constant company. His social preferences are better modeled by activity and companion than by a single point on an introversion scale. [TF-E04](#tf-e04) [TF-E12](#tf-e12) [TF-E14](#tf-e14) [TF-P02](#tf-p02)

In a quiet exchange with Kikuchi, small details and a shared fictional world can sustain him. With Mimimi, active banter and responsive performance become available. With Hinami, challenge and technical shorthand can communicate a familiarity that a polite exchange would not. With gaming peers, domain recognition changes his social starting position. The question “does he like socializing?” is therefore less useful than “which social practice, with whom, and under what burden?”

<a id="contradictions"></a>
## 10. Central contradictions and stopping conditions

### 10.1 He defends owned desire and overrules another person’s end

The contradiction is not that Fumiya sometimes fails to be true to himself. It is that a principle he correctly applies to his own goal selection does not reliably protect another person’s authority. In V03, the objection is to making connection into an assigned task before his desire is established. In V11, he owns the wish to reach Hinami so strongly that her refusal risks becoming the obstacle around which he organizes the next intervention. The subject of self-authorship has changed, and his rule has not adequately changed with it. [TF-E03](#tf-e03) [TF-E23](#tf-e23)

The necessary stopping condition would distinguish continuing to care from continuing a particular unauthorized method. The corpus does not show him articulating a complete rule for that distinction. His existing capacities make such a revision plausible, but plausibility is not completion.

### 10.2 He values particular people and sometimes turns them into proof

Fumiya’s affection is not fake because it can become bound up with his self-worth. Rather, the same relationship can carry both concern for the other person and a claim about who he is. Hinami becomes the person whose recovery would demonstrate his unique standing. Kikuchi can become the partner he imagines protecting through unilateral self-reduction. Neither person is thereby reduced to a mere instrument in every scene, but the instrumental pressure is real. [TF-E18](#tf-e18) [TF-E24](#tf-e24)

The more adequate question is not whether he “really cares.” It is whether care remains responsive when the other person’s answer threatens his preferred account of being caring. V11’s failed call is a sharp negative test. The reconciliation is a positive local response because he acknowledges the injury and accepts a contribution that changes his position. [TF-E25](#tf-e25) [TF-E26](#tf-e26)

### 10.3 He can lose well until the loss decides who he is

Mastery orientation explains much of his resilience, but not all of it. He can investigate a loss when his right to continue is intact. He has greater difficulty when the result is made to decide whether he is the person another person should trust. This qualifies rather than rejects the mastery account. The failure is not proof that his earlier learning was an elaborate pretense. It identifies a condition under which a generally useful orientation stops governing the interpretation of the result. [TF-E15](#tf-e15) [TF-E17](#tf-e17) [TF-E24](#tf-e24)

### 10.4 He changes readily and needs protection against changing too much

A person who can endure temporary incompetence has a major resource for growth. But willingness to discard a familiar form is not sufficient guidance about what should be discarded. Fumiya’s V09 withdrawal from valued peer structures shows that a local simplification can damage the very life the desired end was supposed to enrich. Mimimi’s correction matters because she identifies a cost his optimization has treated as expendable. [TF-E18](#tf-e18)

The practical stopping condition would be consultation before deleting an identity-bearing relationship or practice on another person’s presumed behalf. Low-stakes evidence shows he can accept a direct correction of his assumptions. High-stakes evidence shows that he does not consistently ask before acting. The difference must remain in the model. [TF-P02](#tf-p02)

### 10.5 He believes in sincerity but has no complete disclosure policy

His preference not to say what he does not think is real. It does not answer when another person needs more than a technically true fragment. Nor does it establish his right to disclose someone else’s information in the name of openness. Rena-related omission and Hinami-related overdisclosure are opposite errors within one broader problem: he has not fully specified how information obligations change with subject, recipient, consequence, and permission. [TF-E02](#tf-e02) [TF-E16](#tf-e16) [TF-E20](#tf-e20)

### 10.6 He learns that help is distributed, then returns to exceptional standing

Tama’s importance to Mimimi, the multi-person effort around Tama, and Mimimi’s festival recovery already demonstrate that one person cannot supply every relevant form of help. The V11 guarantee therefore does not arise because Fumiya has never encountered distributed care. It arises because Hinami’s special position makes the lesson difficult to apply where it matters most to his own self-model. The ending is a return to a repeatedly evidenced possibility at a more demanding level, not the first discovery that friends are useful. [TF-E04](#tf-e04) [TF-E07](#tf-e07) [TF-E12](#tf-e12) [TF-E26](#tf-e26)

<a id="rivals"></a>
## 11. Serious rival readings

### 11.1 “He is fundamentally a social climber, and the ethical language rationalizes it”

This reading has real support: the curriculum uses status-bearing outcomes, he learns public presentation, and social success supplies recognition he previously lacked. It is a useful challenge to any account that declares popularity irrelevant to him. But it cannot explain why he rejects a feasible confession, values a particular creative project, abandons assigned centrality while grieving the relationships, or enjoys a small shared activity without a public audience. The revised formulation is that status gives access and can affect self-concept, while his owned ends increasingly diverge from status as a final metric. [TF-E03](#tf-e03) [TF-E09](#tf-e09) [TF-E18](#tf-e18) [TF-P02](#tf-p02)

### 11.2 “Nanashi is his real self; social Fumiya is an acquired mask”

The gaming identity contains genuine prior competence, pride, and pleasure, so the reading correctly resists treating him as a blank slate before Hinami. Its error is to make origin determine authenticity forever. Reading, friendship, clothes, romantic pleasure, and public collaboration can become wanted practices. The Christmas camera operation shows the point in miniature: something acquired through training becomes a means of preserving a genuinely desired memory. Nanashi is a real part of him, not the only real part. [TF-E01](#tf-e01) [TF-P01](#tf-p01) [TF-P02](#tf-p02)

### 11.3 “He has become strong because he can choose without proof”

Ashigaru’s interpretation and the Found-to-Jack decision make this a serious account, not an empty motivational slogan. Fumiya can act before an external guarantee exists. Yet V11 shows that the confidence is not globally unconditional. He relies on a protected competitive asymmetry when offering himself as Hinami’s warrant. The better formulation is that he possesses a strong change-readiness under many conditions, while unique-usefulness pressure can reveal a more conditional basis of self-belief. [TF-E17](#tf-e17) [TF-E24](#tf-e24)

### 11.4 “His rescue effort is simply altruistic, so the privacy objection misses the point”

The concern for Hinami is sincere, the inquiry generates important understanding, and some actions preserve practical work she cannot currently do. Those facts are substantial. But they do not establish her permission or ownership of the end. Fumiya’s own acknowledgment of a rights violation in V10 prevents the reader from treating privacy as an irrelevant technicality introduced only by the analyst. An action can be costly, loving, useful, and unauthorized at once. The remaining question is not whether he should care but how care should remain answerable to its subject. [TF-E20](#tf-e20) [TF-E23](#tf-e23)

### 11.5 “His care is only narcissistic saviorism”

This rival corrects idealization but becomes reductive when it explains every act as self-aggrandizement. He can teach voluntarily, revise a bad diagnosis, accept help, choose without exploiting another person’s love, and apologize after harm. Ordinary scenes show pleasure in another person’s presence without a rescue project. The narrower supported criticism is that **some high-stakes care becomes entangled with unique usefulness**, not that concern for others is merely a disguise. [TF-E02](#tf-e02) [TF-E07](#tf-e07) [TF-E10](#tf-e10) [TF-E26](#tf-e26) [TF-P02](#tf-p02)

### 11.6 “His difficulty with Kikuchi proves the relationship was a mistaken choice”

The allocation failures, incomplete disclosure, and exceptional Hinami-facing priority are genuine difficulties. The choice cannot be vindicated by pointing to a confession or a kiss. Conversely, conflict does not prove that affection was false or that another pairing was objectively correct. The early ordinary intimacy, later mutual selection, public integration, and repaired exchange provide evidence of a relationship the two continue to choose. Its durability remains unproved. The monograph can evaluate their practices without supplying a romance verdict beyond V11. [TF-E16](#tf-e16) [TF-E19](#tf-e19) [TF-E20](#tf-e20) [TF-E26](#tf-e26)

### 11.7 “He is obviously romantically in love with Hinami, whatever he says”

Exceptional priority, complex responses to Mizusawa, and the time devoted to Hinami make the romantic hypothesis live. They do not settle it. His represented distinctions, ongoing desire for Kikuchi, and lack of a direct reciprocal Hinami declaration also remain evidence. A strong monograph need not resolve the classification to analyze the practical conflict. The open romantic hypothesis and the established problem of disproportionate priority are different propositions. [TF-E17](#tf-e17) [TF-E20](#tf-e20) [TF-E23](#tf-e23)

### 11.8 “V11 proves that his growth was shallow and has been reset”

His withdrawal is severe, and the failed call injures someone who has supported him. It would be evasive to call it a minor wobble. Yet his learned competence, relationships, history, and capacity to be reached are still present. The crisis has a new object: the failure of a special role developed through those relationships. His apology and co-proposal could not be reconstructed as the behavior of the untouched opening-state character. V11 revises the maturity of his self-concept; it does not erase the person who developed. [TF-E24](#tf-e24) [TF-E25](#tf-e25) [TF-E26](#tf-e26)

### 11.9 “A party solves the problem that a lone hero could not”

Plural participation can reduce interpretive monopoly and make different costs visible. The existing corpus gives strong reasons to prefer that possibility over a unique savior’s guarantee. But a group can still organize around someone else’s life without permission, distribute pressure, and mistake agreement among helpers for the subject’s consent. V11 ends before this risk is tested. The warranted formulation is that the co-proposal corrects the imagined distribution of knowledge and capability, while authority and outcome remain open. [TF-E26](#tf-e26)

## 12. The V11 endpoint and unresolved hypotheses

Fumiya’s present direction is toward renewed action with Kikuchi and a proposed wider group. That direction carries three genuine changes: he has acknowledged hurting his partner; he has accepted a mode of communication that his own direct argument could not reproduce; and he has accepted that other people’s experiences are not replaceable by his insight. These are substantive local developments. They do not warrant a final account of secure identity, completed relationship repair, or consent-sensitive intervention. [TF-E25](#tf-e25) [TF-E26](#tf-e26)

Recovery of an owned reason to play Atafami and return to school remain **OPEN**. Renewed commitment to the Hinami-facing story is not evidence that either has occurred. This retains the earlier monograph’s endpoint and the character-state ledger’s §6.5 limit. [TF-E25](#tf-e25) [TF-E26](#tf-e26)

The most important open hypothesis is whether he can remain committed to Hinami’s good **without making her response the test that certifies his value**. A positive result would require more than a successful scene. It would include how he handles her disagreement, what information he regards as hers to control, how he allocates costs to the other participants, and whether he can stop or revise a method without treating that limit as abandonment. The current corpus does not provide that test’s outcome.

A second hypothesis concerns pluralism in his self-worth. His tournament experience gives him evidence that being valuable does not require winning. His relationships give him evidence that ordinary presence and reciprocal history matter. Whether those sources can remain available to him under another unique-usefulness threat remains open. The model therefore retains both the broader repertoire and the possibility of a concentrated crisis response. [TF-E22](#tf-e22) [TF-E24](#tf-e24)

A third concerns vocation. Professional gaming is not merely an inherited goal, and the route has moved through meaningful experiments. But the sponsor-like exchange and tournament appearance do not settle sustainability, family and educational choices, or a stopping condition. Nor has the Endo promotional obligation received its reported outcome. The future should not be silently converted into either success or a cautionary failure. [TF-E21](#tf-e21) [TF-E22](#tf-e22)

Finally, the private relationship remains a site of continuing work rather than a resolved reward. Kikuchi’s willingness to participate does not erase jealousy or establish standing permission for all uses of her time and art. Mimimi’s restraint does not establish emotional closure. Mizusawa’s renewed confession is not shown. Rena’s broad compliance is not shown. These are other people’s open states as well as limits on predicting Fumiya’s conduct. A reconstructed character cannot be made more certain by inventing certainty in everyone around him. [TF-E18](#tf-e18) [TF-E25](#tf-e25) [TF-E27](#tf-e27)

## 13. Evidence-bounded responses to novel situations

### 13.1 What this reconstruction can and cannot predict

The following cases are **new analytical scenarios**, not events in the novels and not newly generated canonical dialogue. They test whether the account can specify a likely response, the conditions that would change it, and the observations that would falsify it. Confidence is qualitative. “Relatively strong” means a pattern is repeated or directly contrasted within the admitted corpus; it does not mean that an exact action is inevitable.

These probes illustrate the literary account’s falsifiability; they do not establish a validated derived-use model or open the reconstruction gate. Any operational reconstruction artifact belongs under `07 Character Reconstruction Models/` only after its architectural prerequisites pass.

No prospective holdout was performed in the original authoring session or during integration. All V01–V11 outcomes were available while constructing these scenarios. The frozen readings preserve genuine historical prediction states, but using their later outcomes here is **retrospective model checking**, not a fresh blind validation. A future validation should freeze the relevant state, specify what the character knows, record a prediction before opening the test scene, and distinguish a mistaken premise from a mistaken behavioral rule.

The minimal input to a useful Fumiya prediction is: **developmental band; audience or relationship; his information; the goal he regards as his; whether another person has agreed; what a failure would mean to his self-concept; and whether he can consult a trusted corrective person**. Without those fields, “what would Fumiya do?” invites a generic good-person answer or an exaggerated game metaphor.

### 13.2 A friend asks him to teach an unfamiliar competitive game

**Scenario.** In a post-V07, non-crisis state, a friend asks Fumiya to help learn a game that neither fully understands. The friend wants enjoyable participation with a group, not high ranking.

**Likely response.** He would plausibly investigate the mechanics, seek manageable fundamentals, and try to produce a usable practice route. His prior teaching and domain confidence make engagement more likely than dismissing the request because he is not already an expert. The important risk is mistaking his own satisfaction in mastery for the friend’s goal. A well-updated version of his Tama experience would lead him to ask what the friend actually wants to be able to do.

**Confidence and falsifier.** Relatively strong for structured, trial-based help; moderate for explicit goal clarification. Evidence that he immediately insists on a rank-grinding regime despite a clearly stated recreational goal would count against the recipient-sensitive part of the current model, not against his general willingness to help. [TF-E02](#tf-e02) [TF-E07](#tf-e07) [TF-P01](#tf-p01)

### 13.3 He loses a low-stakes practice match to a respected newcomer

**Scenario.** The loss does not decide professional viability, a relationship, or his unique standing toward Hinami.

**Likely response.** He is more likely to investigate the opponent’s effective play, feel competitive interest, and seek another test than to conclude that his whole life has been invalidated. His pride can make the result matter without making it intolerable. An exact emotional tone—irritation, excitement, embarrassment—cannot be selected confidently without the opponent’s conduct and the immediate history.

**Confidence and falsifier.** Relatively strong for specific rather than totalized evaluation. A sustained collapse after an explicitly low-stakes loss, with no hidden identity pressure, would require revising the proposed distinction between informative defeat and disqualifying defeat. [TF-E01](#tf-e01) [TF-E15](#tf-e15) [TF-E24](#tf-e24)

### 13.4 A stranger praises him as an inspiring player after a mediocre result

**Scenario.** The admirer names something specific learned from his play, while Fumiya knows he performed below his own standard.

**Likely response.** By the V11 tournament stage, he can plausibly receive the admiration as real without pretending the performance was technically excellent. The encounter may broaden his sense of what public participation gives others. It should not be predicted to cure his dependency on exceptional standing in every other relationship.

**Confidence and falsifier.** Relatively strong as a near-transfer from the fan encounter, but limited in novelty. Automatic rejection of all praise solely because he did not win would conflict with the expanded public-role evidence; automatic secure self-esteem would overgeneralize it. [TF-E22](#tf-e22)

### 13.5 Kikuchi says she wants an additional ordinary afternoon together

**Scenario.** There is no urgent competing obligation, and she directly says that the invitation is wanted rather than a sacrifice.

**Likely response.** He would plausibly welcome the prospect, perhaps initially check whether he is taking too much of her time, and accept her clarification. The Christmas scene supports eagerness and mild hesitation coexisting. It would be inaccurate to predict a cold allocation calculation every time she asks for time.

**Confidence and falsifier.** Moderate to relatively strong under low load. Persistent refusal justified by her presumed preference after she clearly states the opposite would indicate a stronger paternalistic pattern than the low-pressure evidence supports. Under heavy gaming and peer demands, the forecast changes: he may again treat the conflict locally unless the recurring pattern is made explicit. [TF-P02](#tf-p02) [TF-E16](#tf-e16)

### 13.6 Several valued commitments collide, and he feels guilty about disappointing his partner

**Scenario.** He must choose among a gaming event, a group plan, and relationship time, with no standing agreement covering the conflict.

**Likely response.** He is likely to search for a concrete solution, but the model cannot confidently predict a good one. Under moderate pressure, scheduling, disclosure, or consultation is available. Under acute guilt, he may try to remove a whole category of commitments to eliminate the conflict. A trusted person who makes the lost value visible can interrupt that overcorrection.

**Confidence and falsifier.** Moderate for the risk pattern, low for the exact decision. The useful prediction is not “he will abandon his friends,” but that **unilateral simplification becomes more attractive as uncertainty is experienced as personal failure**. Calm consultation before any drastic change would be evidence of a more stable revision than the current endpoint establishes. [TF-E16](#tf-e16) [TF-E18](#tf-e18)

### 13.7 A friend refuses help with a problem Fumiya believes he understands

**Scenario.** The friend is not in an immediate emergency. Fumiya has relevant information and believes that not intervening would allow avoidable suffering.

**Likely response.** With an ordinary friend and bounded stakes, his learned sensitivity to an opt-out gives some basis for restraint or a revised offer. When the friend is Hinami, or when he interprets himself as uniquely able to act, the risk of renewed approach, indirect recruitment, or pressure rises. Correct understanding alone will not reliably prevent overreach.

**Confidence and falsifier.** Moderate for the relationship-conditioned difference. A prediction of universal respect for refusal is contradicted by V10–V11; a prediction of universal coercion ignores his other negotiated and recipient-sensitive behavior. Observed acceptance of Hinami’s refusal without covert continuation would be a meaningful positive test, not an outcome already demonstrated. [TF-E09](#tf-e09) [TF-E20](#tf-e20) [TF-E23](#tf-e23)

### 13.8 A respected mentor proposes an efficient goal that Fumiya does not want

**Scenario.** The goal is feasible and status-enhancing but involves another person in a relationship Fumiya cannot honestly choose.

**Likely response.** After V03, he can object to the goal rather than merely request an easier route. He may be unable to refute every efficiency argument yet still say that the desired end is wrong for him. He is more likely to seek a hybrid or replacement goal than to reject all acquired technique permanently, although sufficiently sharp conflict can still provoke withdrawal.

**Confidence and falsifier.** Relatively strong for the distinction between means and end, moderate for immediate emotional regulation. Quiet compliance simply because the task is achievable would require explanation beyond the current default model. [TF-E03](#tf-e03) [TF-E10](#tf-e10)

### 13.9 A budget-conscious shopping trip contains unfamiliar style advice

**Scenario.** No major relationship conflict is present. He is buying with his own money and has access to a knowledgeable friend or clerk.

**Likely response.** He may reason through combinations, reuse vocabulary imperfectly, compare value, and accept feedback about a distinction he cannot yet judge. He can joke about the learning process and feel satisfaction in selecting something himself. The evidence does not justify an exact brand, garment, or color prediction outside the attested episode.

**Confidence and falsifier.** Moderate: the observed shopping episode is rich but selective. Treating him as indifferent to price, incapable of independent selection, or an established fashion expert would each erase part of that evidence. [TF-P01](#tf-p01)

### 13.10 An acquaintance initiates unwanted physical or sexualized contact

**Scenario.** The contact resembles the boundary-bearing interactions already experienced, but the acquaintance’s future conduct is unknown.

**Likely response.** Fumiya can explicitly refuse and remove unwanted contact. His own attraction or embarrassment should not be modeled as cancellation of that refusal. What remains less predictable is whether he immediately creates and communicates a durable future-contact rule, especially when the acquaintance also has a role in a valued social or gaming environment.

**Confidence and falsifier.** Relatively strong for the capacity to refuse; limited for sustained follow-through. Predicting consent from arousal would be both evidentially wrong and analytically incoherent. Predicting a complete no-contact policy after one refusal would exceed the corpus. [TF-E16](#tf-e16) [TF-E17](#tf-e17) [TF-E25](#tf-e25)

### 13.11 Someone challenges his usefulness immediately after a personal failure

**Scenario.** He believes the failed act was something only he could accomplish, and a loved person tries to console him.

**Likely response.** This is a high-risk state for turning explanation into exclusion. He may insist that the other person cannot understand, experience concern as evidence of distance, and use his old solitary self-description to refuse what is presently being offered. That is not his inevitable or permanent response. The V11 repair shows that another form of communication and later acknowledgment can reopen the relation.

**Confidence and falsifier.** Moderate for acute vulnerability; low for duration and exact words. Stable reception of comfort under a comparable unique-usefulness threat would strengthen a post-V11 model. It cannot be presumed from his general kindness. [TF-E24](#tf-e24) [TF-E25](#tf-e25) [TF-E26](#tf-e26)

### 13.12 A friend asks him to help make a story, not to solve its subject

**Scenario.** A creative collaborator asks for practical support while explicitly retaining authorship and defining boundaries around real people used as models.

**Likely response.** He can become an energetic translator between a desired work and the constraints of realizing it. He may offer organization, identify available skills, and enjoy the fact that the project becomes shared. His restraint depends on whether he continues treating the work as the author’s chosen end or begins treating its subject’s response as his own proof of success.

**Confidence and falsifier.** Moderate. The festival supports cooperative production; the later Hinami-facing project supplies an untested risk. Disregarding a clearly stated authorship boundary would count against the cooperative forecast. Achieving a desired reaction from the subject would not by itself validate the method. [TF-E09](#tf-e09) [TF-E11](#tf-e11) [TF-E26](#tf-e26)

### 13.13 Retrospective checks and known model failures

Three checks help prevent the reconstruction from becoming unfalsifiable. First, “Fumiya responds to distress with more effort” fails to distinguish the useful practice in V01 from the misfit encouragement of Mimimi in V02. The revised model asks what function effort has for the recipient. Second, “his readiness to change is a stable strength” must predict both the productive Jack experiment and the dangerous peer deletion; the direction of change and collateral cost need separate variables. Third, “he values mastery rather than rank” must be qualified by the V11 authority claim, where a competitive result becomes the certificate of his unique relational role. [TF-E04](#tf-e04) [TF-E17](#tf-e17) [TF-E18](#tf-e18) [TF-E24](#tf-e24)

The purpose of these checks is not to make every outcome retrospectively inevitable. Some reactions remain uncertain, and the current model explicitly abstains from exact outcomes. Its advantage over a generic profile is that it identifies **what additional information would change the forecast** and which observed behavior would force a revision.
