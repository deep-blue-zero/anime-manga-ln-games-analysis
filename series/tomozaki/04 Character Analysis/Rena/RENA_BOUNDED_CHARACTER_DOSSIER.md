# Rena — wanting to be selected, making contact and refusing the meaning of refusal

This account is accepted at its stated bounded literary scope through the admitted V12 boundary. See the [implementation review](../../08%20Audits%20and%20Manifests/TOMOZAKI_SUPPORTING_CHARACTER_IMPLEMENTATION_REVIEW.md) and [source and review manifest](../../08%20Audits%20and%20Manifests/TOMOZAKI_SUPPORTING_LITERARY_EVIDENCE_AND_REVIEW_MANIFEST.json) for independent acceptance, source returns, corrections and remaining limits. A bounded literary role is separate from a derived reconstruction model or aggregate release.

Rena's represented life contains genuine interests, two forms of earlier belonging, the pleasure of being wanted, social discrimination, bodily enjoyment, resentment, practical insight and deliberate injury to others. Losing peer standing helps explain why another person's desire and visible status become resources for her. It does not establish that her later acts are involuntary, that every present action has only that origin, or that insight gives her authority over someone else's body or relationship.

The strongest bounded account is that she often seeks an interaction in which selection confirms value while access remains under her tactical control. Desire from a person she does not want can still please her; that person's inability to obtain her can be part of the pleasure. A prominent or attached target can intensify the value of being selected. Yet this is not a timeless description of all Rena. The earlier girl could resent attention to her body, found real pleasure and recognition in two school groups, and liked games for reasons that need not begin with conquest. Present Rena can discuss game skill, enjoy an appearance she has chosen and give a useful answer while also bypassing refusal. The account must explain these combinations rather than rename them one essence. [RE-E01–05]

**A substantial B dossier is warranted.** The retrospective interior, contrasting school and game-community settings, multiple recipients, ordinary particulars, current choices and repeated failures of limits require an individual home. They do not supply the family, work, education, sustained reciprocal ties or revised future conduct needed for a complete biography or general reconstruction. This dossier is independently accepted at bounded B literary scope; derived reconstruction remains separately governed. Its fourteen-witness responsibility is retrospective coverage with explicit unknowns, not a fresh fourteen-book primary appearance census or a maturity/release claim.

## 1. Identity, chronology and who knows what

The source identifies her as **レナ / Rena** in the game community. This is the evidenced appellation, not an authenticated full legal name. Her age is twenty in the V08 encounter and in the adult present of V08.5 Unit 4; the retrospective explicitly reaches three years back to her at seventeen. The two seventeen-year-olds in this account are different people in different states: the earlier Rena and present Fumiya. Knowing the earlier girl's experience does not give present Rena a right over the younger boy. [RE-P01–04]

The supplement's package placement must not be used as chronology. Unit 4, `嘘と朝焼け`, begins with an adult morning, returns to the preceding year-end gathering, reaches the seventeen-year-old school past and then resumes the adult gathering and departure. The particular year-end day is not specified by the inspected prose. It is not a post-V08 response to Fumiya's refusal, and Fumiya is not a participant in that story. Unit 5's karaoke is explicitly parallel; the VR bonus is adapted paratext. Neither is evidence of ordinary mainline Rena habits or acts. [SR08.5 §6/§10–12; RE-P01/P02]

Evidence classes here are **SCENE**, represented current speech/action; **HISTORICAL SCENE**, retrospectively represented behavior; **INTERIOR-RENA** or **INTERIOR-FUMIYA**, whose thoughts the reader receives; **REPORT**, an actor's claim about another event; **INFERENCE**, an explicitly attributed interpretation; **OPEN**, absent or conflicting support. Free indirect judgments about men, women, status and sincerity must not become objective gender truths merely because the narration renders them fluently.

## 2. Two earlier social fields were inhabited, not one true face and one disguise

At seventeen Rena belongs to a group organized partly through appearance and visible standing. They monitor one another's aesthetic changes and compete, but their companionship also supplies recognition. Shoko notices her black-and-pink nails and praises the choice. Rena's later recollection of being told that she is trustworthy and easy to be oneself with has emotional weight precisely because the relation was not entirely empty. Calling it merely fake status would erase something she actually loses. [RE-P01/P02]

She also belongs to a smaller group of people with intensive interests. The interests are differentiated: other members follow bands, streamers and underground idols, while Rena's own named interest is online games. Those other tastes should not be assigned to her as a ready-made list. The prose explicitly resists choosing one group as her authentic face: an appearance-conscious self and an interest-absorbed self both need a place in the school community. Their coexistence is not proof of deceit.

The planned FPS meeting therefore has an actual game purpose as well as the attraction of meeting prominent older former students. At Karen's room, the gathering quickly becomes more physically familiar and socially charged than she expected. Rena feels an adult status and a pleasing sense of being ahead, even while the narration does not describe being especially well cared for. That mixture does not justify inventing a complete awakening, a coercion finding for every encounter, or an uncomplicated purely empowering scene. It makes available the particular young girl's pleasure, comparison and limited reading of the situation. [RE-P02 P13–41]

The dispute with Shoko also has specific grounds. Yosuke is Shoko's recently separated former partner. Rena knows that history but treats the breakup as decisive; Shoko emphasizes that less than two weeks have passed. Rena does not understand the temporal claim as a reason for restraint and deliberately adds an audible insult. This is a disagreement about a particular relation and recent hurt, not evidence that all women are dishonest or that every later rival has the same grounds.

Exclusion then spreads. The high-status group withdraws recognition; the less secure interest group also distances itself rather than risk its standing. Both previously comfortable spaces disappear. Rena talks back to hostile classmates but cannot reverse the collective air by winning an isolated exchange. She stops attending, and later directly confirms that she has left school. The prose supplies neither a completed educational record nor the precise administrative arrangements. The suggested possibility that Shoko might have reconciled later is a narrated counterfactual, not an observed chance she deliberately rejected. [RE-P02 P43–118]

The social injury is real; it does not make every earlier choice harmless. The narrative also gives Rena agency in leaving a setting before she thinks of herself as pitiable and in forming closer relations with the older game-group men, eventually dating Keisuke. Their desirability and prior standing matter to how she values the new place. A broad clinical explanation would add information the text has not supplied. A purely moralizing account in which she never cared about belonging would omit direct inward evidence.

## 3. Adult selection: appetite, status and the body-as-truth account

In the adult year-end gathering, Rena is not equally receptive to everyone. She can enjoy Rambo's and Kaba-jii's attention, read their awkwardness, inwardly dismiss them and leave without returning to the promised seat. Wanting to be wanted is different from wanting that person. Their requests can be tiring while the felt fact of demand remains pleasurable. The scene gives both responses, not a forced choice between invulnerability and secretly disliking all attention. [RE-P01 P26–85]

Her appearance is partly chosen work. Makeup, clothes, shoes and nails are arranged to affect attention. That does not mean that every appearance-conscious person shares her aims or that the earlier body's unwanted visibility was chosen in the same way. Age-seventeen Rena sometimes found her sexualized appearance burdensome; the adult uses it deliberately in this setting. The difference is developmental and situational, rather than a reason to erase either reception.

Ordinary particulars include preparing a Campari-and-grapefruit drink, watching its colors mix, liking its taste, and noticing the relation between her nails and the glass. These are actual pleasures and an act she performs, not merely evidence of someone else's gaze. They establish this drink and this visual attention, not an exclusive favorite, a permanent drinking routine or a diagnosis. Likewise her irritation at possible commuter congestion on departure does not establish an occupation or employment timetable. [RE-P02 P136–166/P298–323]

The private account makes her distrust of words and women explicit. She treats male bodily desire as comparatively honest. In the snow image, the surface is blackened and scraping reveals white. Rena imagines the people she calls liars showing a white face to a man at first contact, while she would rather be black snow from the beginning. This is her interpretive framework, not a fact that men cannot deceive or that women necessarily do. The text gives useful counterevidence inside it: she makes a socially pleasant reply she does not intend to honor, shapes timing, manages appearances and later chooses a literally true public hint for its provocative effect. Truthful wording and full candor are different things. [RE-P01 P55–80; RE-P02 P129–139/P234–323]

Jimmy's prominence changes the target. He is a popular streamer whose voice she already recognizes, and selection by him promises a form of status beyond diffuse attention from the room. Rena leaves space for him, offers her drink and later makes her availability to other men visible while waiting for him to approach again. Her knowledge is specific: Inumaru says within her hearing that he has a girlfriend, and he later confirms it to her. Rena continues. These are owned current acts, not a situation in which attachment was unknowable. The girlfriend is an independently consequential absent recipient of the choices, not simply a device that makes the target more valuable. [RE-P02 P146–294]

Her conflict with Vanilla includes deliberate malice. She identifies hypocrisy and jealousy in the rival, mocks her appearance and touches/pinches her clothing and body while belittling her. Other participants intervene. Rena's wish to wound the woman who reminds her of the school dispute is represented; that does not mean Vanilla and Shoko are the same person or that either has the exact inner motives Rena assigns. Inumaru's stated connection to Jimmy's girlfriend is different from Vanilla's expressed challenge about access and temptation. The two women's roles must not be merged into generic jealousy. [RE-P02 P179–228]

At the end she enjoys incoming follow requests and being among the small set Jimmy follows relative to his audience. That is a direct inward comparison of numbers and privileged access, not an objective value score. She deliberately posts a hint before approving Vanilla's request after Jimmy responds. The intent to provoke is available in this private story. It does not establish that every public post elsewhere in the series has precisely the same intent. [RE-P02 P298–323]

## 4. Gamer and ordinary participant in V08

The game-community role is substantive, even when it also supplies targets and visibility. Rena knows who attends gatherings, discusses Ashigaru's strength, participates in tournaments and talks game-specific difficulties with others. In V09 her current character is Victoria, heard in play against Ashigaru. This is a shown use, not a lifelong favorite or a complete competitive ranking. Her admitted inability to do much against an expert does not imply that she only pretends to like games. [RE-P03; RE-P07 P316]

The V08 gathering gives ordinary snacks, shared drinks and conversation as well as rapid proximity. She tells Fumiya that she is twenty after he has mistaken her age; the narrator is seventeen and has told the group that he is a high-school student. The inspected exchange need not be embellished into proof of the exact moment she learned his numerical age. He shifts toward more formal address, she specifically permits the familiar Rena-chan form for nanashi, and he participates in that local negotiation. Agreement over an address form is not permission for every bodily or sexual act. [RE-P03 P497–547]

Fumiya inwardly objects to early touch and proximity. Some “stop” language in his narration is not spoken dialogue. His experience matters, but the reader must not falsely give Rena a heard prohibition that the scene does not depict. Conversely, incomplete outward articulation does not turn all initiated touch into affirmative permission. The actual later refusals supply their own speech and action evidence. Nor does his response make all twenty-year-olds behave alike; his own narrative briefly generalizes and then doubts the generalization. [RE-P03/P07/P08]

Rena's later call begins through an actual ongoing channel and includes an apology for publicly naming him on Twitter. She says that the concerning online person is unlikely to attend in person. This is a particular acknowledgment and reassurance, not established universal solicitude or proof that the earlier post was designed to harm. Fumiya is reassured by the tone and concerned about resulting risk, while the discussion moves into sexualized questions. The movement should remain sequential, rather than starting and ending with the same unchanging surface. [RE-P04 P12–35]

## 5. Refusal has several different objects

The V08 call does not show Fumiya accepting sex. He speaks about interest with someone he likes, then directly denies wanting the proposed kind of contact with Rena. His narration nevertheless records remembered touch, disturbed attraction and difficulty collecting his thoughts. The direct answer and inward response coexist. Attraction does not cancel refusal, and refusal does not prove an absence of desire or confusion. The latter are his private narration, not an invitation she is authorized to infer. [RE-P04 P41–61]

She then asks about Saturday. He says he has plans; she withdraws that particular invitation and ends the call to take a bath. This local withdrawal is real counterevidence to an absolute claim that she never backs away from anything. It does not show a general acceptance of a sexual, relational, age or contact limit. The invitation's sexual framing is evident from context; the inspected V08 wording does not itself specify a visit to her home. The explicit home invitation belongs to the later V09 scene. Keeping these separate improves both chronology and attribution. [RE-P04 P64–79]

V08 ends when a sexual-conversation notification appears while Kikuchi is holding Fumiya's phone. The older account he gave her was materially incomplete. The notification is not proof of an affair, and Rena is not the sole agent of every subsequent couple problem: his omitted account and scheduling choices, Kikuchi's unpermitted phone access and her real insecurity are different matters. The source clock stops before the next volume's explanation and repair. V09 supplies Kikuchi's own acknowledgment that looking without permission was wrong, distinct from Fumiya's account of what he withheld. [RE-P12; DR08 §4.6; LOC-11A]

In V09 Fumiya sends a request about sudden message wording. Rena apologizes and asks whether somebody saw it. That actual response should not be erased merely because later behavior fails to establish a broader rule. The request is narrower than ending contact or forbidding every image, touch, proposition and use of his name. The later image and contact show why narrow compliance cannot be inflated into settled governance; they also do not justify changing what the first written sentence actually said. [RE-P05]

The next image message arrives before a game gathering. He opens it in part because he expects to see her there, and the image affects him. At the gathering, her familiar naming, shoulder and waist contact have different routes of access: his outward denial of closeness, inward objections and chair-distance response are not one continuous voiced command. He wonders about strategy, but his suspicion is not a direct receipt for everything she consciously planned. [RE-P06/P07]

V09's later home invitation and refusal are more explicit. Fumiya states that he has a girlfriend; she continues bodily escalation after that answer. He takes her wrist, removes her hand and says that it is not allowed, then creates distance. Rena subsequently withdraws with a remark that makes him feel as though he has been refused. The act ends locally, but the following discussion contains renewed touch that he brushes away, and the later bar consultation contains more. This is an important difference between an immediate resisted action and a lasting inward stop. [RE-P08/P09]

## 6. Advice as a real capacity with an unresolved ethical conflict

Rena's account of love includes instability, desire and changes in oneself through attachment. She initially generalizes that girls all enjoy the mixture of love and uncertainty. Aoi immediately says she is different, and Rena locally entertains the possibility that it is only her. The response prevents treating her as literally incapable of acknowledging any counterexample. It does not prove a rewritten general belief or a newly internalized consent practice. Fumiya experiences the smiling exchange as partly competitive; that is his reading, not both women's exhaustive shared interior. [RE-P08 P192–224]

Later he deliberately consults her because her idea about uncertainty differs from the answers he has received elsewhere. He brings Ashigaru because he considers a one-to-one setting risky. That chosen precaution supplies a different setting, not a permanent policy or complete protection: she still touches and he still removes contact within the consultation. The source does not show her volunteering a comprehensive safeguarding rule. [RE-P09]

The advice has real substance. She challenges expecting an ideal relationship immediately, uses a beginner-to-top-player analogy he understands and says that specific causes of insecurity require particular work rather than a magical removal of all anxiety. Fumiya recognizes a basic causal method he had lost sight of in treating romance as exceptional. This contribution belongs to her demonstrated conversational capacity. It should not be reassigned to Ashigaru or reduced to pure seduction because the giver also acts badly. [RE-P09 P137–183]

The limits of her account matter equally. A theory of mutual advantage and enjoying uncertainty can become a convenient defense of imposed insecurity. Her comment that an anxious person is weak or unstable remains her appraisal, not a diagnosis of Kikuchi or a universal statement about partners. Neither the accurate game analogy nor some useful advice buys access to Fumiya's body or determines which discomfort his girlfriend should bear. Domain insight and ethical authority remain separate.

## 7. V11 repeats the test; the ending does not reform her by omission

At the V11 encounter Rena approaches Fumiya and evaluates Kikuchi with an apparently untroubled, provocative manner. Fumiya immediately removes the arm contact; Kikuchi says to stop and states her relationship standing. Rena says she has heard about the girlfriend, calls her cute and receives a further direct request not to make sexual remarks to Fumiya. The narrator's interpretation of the compliment as condescending is situated observation, not authenticated access to all Rena's feelings. [RE-P10]

Rena leaves amused and says he has a good girlfriend. The departure is an actual local end and the compliment is an actual utterance. Neither is an apology or a promise about future contact. Calling it total compliance would erase the earlier repeated sequence; calling it a newly demonstrated permanent incapacity to change would also go beyond what is shown. The remaining question is future general conduct, not whether immediate resistance happened.

Current V12 owners explicitly preserve the no-new-test state. The afterword says that not everything about Rena has been written, among other possible material. It is an author's statement about uncompleted topics, not a new scene or a guaranteed future treatment. The corpus ending cannot supply an apology, mutual policy, stopping rule, settled vocation or rehabilitation by silence. [RE-P11; REL-C14/CRI-OQ-REN-001]
