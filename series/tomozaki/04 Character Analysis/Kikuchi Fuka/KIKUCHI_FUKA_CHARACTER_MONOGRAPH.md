# Kikuchi Fuka — character monograph through Volume 11

**An author learning to belong inside the world she describes**

> **Authority and scope.** This reviewed expansion is mutable current literary authority for Kikuchi’s individual R05 responsibility under the [integration review](../../08%20Audits%20and%20Manifests/KIKUCHI_FUKA_R05_INTEGRATION_REVIEW.md). The later [Mimimi review](../../08%20Audits%20and%20Manifests/NANAMI_MINAMI_R05_INTEGRATION_REVIEW.md) completes the R05 pair; cross-specialist convergence, whole-corpus release and derived-model validation remain unfinished. The current source boundary is V11, not the end of the series.

## 1. Responsibility and evidence boundary

Kikuchi Fuka is a reader, a developing writer, a daughter and older sister, a café worker, a selectively sociable schoolmate, and, from V07, Tomozaki Fumiya’s girlfriend. Those descriptions identify overlapping fields of life, not a hierarchy in which the romantic role explains everything else. Her sustained problem is how to make contact without surrendering the forms of attention through which she understands the world. As her ability to make contact grows, a second problem becomes harder: how to use that understanding without making other people’s lives the property of her interpretation. This study reconstructs the person who acts inside both problems. It does not simply extract the philosophical lessons she supplies to the protagonist. [KF-E01–E04, E11–E13](#kf-e01)

The admitted evidence is the locked Japanese light-novel corpus, V01–V11 and the individually routed V06.5/V08.5 stories. The numbered readings remain historical prospective states. Supplemental reader access cannot be transferred to an earlier character’s knowledge, and an interstitial scene cannot be made to repair a later crisis merely because it was published or consulted later. In particular, the first Christmas, Mimimi’s conversation with Kikuchi, and Kikuchi’s New Year’s Eve at home all belong between V07 and the V08 opening. The parallel karaoke story and adaptation-derived VR bonus do not establish main-continuity preferences or conduct. The V07 artbook does not supply biography. [KF-E02, E10–E13](#kf-e02)

The evidence classes used in the argument are straightforward. An act, an utterance, or represented thought establishes that the act occurred or that the person expressed or entertained the proposition. It does not automatically establish that the proposition is true. Fumiya’s perceptions are evidence of his experience of Kikuchi; her own interior narration offers another, still situated, kind of access. Kikuchi’s representations of Chris, Libra, Alcia, and other fictional figures are authored constructions. Even a remarkably successful construction is not testimony from its human model. The monograph’s explanations, personality descriptions, and conditional expectations are interpretations supported by these differentiated materials, not additional scenes in the novels.

This distinction is particularly important because many early descriptions make Kikuchi seem almost otherworldly. Fumiya’s fairy and angel language conveys attraction, relief, and the special atmosphere he experiences around her. It does not establish literal purity, permanent passivity, or exemption from ordinary jealousy and bodily life. Conversely, the intensity of her later investigative speech does not reveal that a supposedly angelic personality was secretly false. A person who can be gentle, embarrassed, exacting, funny, acquisitive about time, and absorbed in a difficult creative problem need not be divided into a good mask and a bad essence. [KF-E03, E17–E19, E24](#kf-e03)

The study addresses four reconstructive questions alongside the architectural literary contract: who Kikuchi is; how she speaks and behaves; what she believes and prefers, including outside dramatic scenes; and what a careful reader could reasonably expect from her in a new situation. Section 15 supplies explicitly hypothetical, state-conditioned tests. They are not future canon, invented dialogue, blind predictions, or permission to use this monograph as circular evidence for itself.

Evidence references are locally stable. **KF-E** entries in §14 join an argument to existing cumulative identities and exact reading sections. **KF-P** entries record Japanese passages or images inspected for this delivery. Ranges in shorthand such as “KF-E01–E04” identify several individual entries; the link goes to the first entry, not to a new aggregate authority. Existing **CRI**, **CS**, **REL**, **EFFORT**, **JVL**, and **LOC** identities retain their original owners. Fresh ordinary-life and register observations are identified as local enrichments rather than assigned fabricated frozen-reading claim numbers. No source extraction is included in the delivery.

## 2. Reconstructive thesis

Kikuchi’s arc is not a shy person’s linear conversion into universal social ease. It is a movement toward choosing which worlds she can participate in, making forms that communicate what matters to her, and accepting that an author must remain answerable both to her own interests and to people who resist being fully described. Her strongest capability and her characteristic danger arise together. She can perceive relationships as structured wholes, connect otherwise isolated events, and imagine the inner cost of a position that looks privileged from outside. But a compelling whole can become more authoritative to her than a living participant’s unsettled wishes—including her own. [KF-E02, E06–E09, E18–E21](#kf-e02)

The central account is therefore this: **Kikuchi learns to become an inhabitant as well as an author of shared worlds, without erasing herself for their apparent beauty or completing other people on their behalf.** The first half is not finished when she becomes a girlfriend; the second is not solved when her fiction helps someone. V07 gives her a stronger place in her own story. V09 makes that place actively choosing rather than merely gratefully accepting. V11 asks whether the ability to illuminate another person can justify the costs of doing so. These are related developments, but they are not a single improvement score.

The account would be weakened by evidence that Kikuchi consistently chooses social breadth for its own sake, that her major decisions are indifferent to the coherence of other people’s lives, or that she treats literary accuracy as irrelevant when it conflicts with wanted outcomes. It would also be weakened by evidence that her apparent self-exclusion is always a strategic performance designed to secure reassurance. The admitted corpus instead gives sincere attachment to both personal feeling and world-facing responsibility, followed by occasions on which either can become excessive. Yet the account must remain broad enough to include uncomplicated enjoyment. The girl who wants both upcoming dates, enjoys a misshapen snowman, plays with a story’s suspense, and calls a boy’s proposal grandly unrealistic is not continuously performing an abstract ethical dilemma. [KF-E07, E10–E13, E16, E21](#kf-e07)

### 2.1 Her personality is more specific than “gentle and introverted”

Kikuchi is observant, imaginative, conscientious about representation, and responsive to the texture of another person’s words. She generally prefers interaction with an intelligible purpose and a manageable field of attention. Shared literature, a draft, a particular question, or a relationship with established trust can give her far more initiative than a socially open-ended group situation. She is capable of sustained absorption and considerable expressive energy when the subject becomes hers. Her childhood conversation with Goda contains eager retelling, altered pacing, and playful withholding of an ending. Her later café conversation with Mimimi contains gentle teasing and pointed questions. Those are not newly acquired V11 exceptions to an otherwise empty social baseline. [KF-E02–E03, E11; KF-P01–P03, P15–P16](#kf-p01)

She also has a tendency to measure her participation against an imagined proper arrangement. Sometimes the arrangement protects legitimate difference: people do not all have to inhabit the same social environment. Sometimes it turns against her: the girl who belongs in a small lake should not interfere with the boy whose nature is to travel widely, or the writer should preserve the world’s beautiful relation even when she wants another ending. Her self-restraint can therefore be thoughtful, fearful, generous, or self-excluding, sometimes within the same conversation. Describing all of it as sweetness misses the work her judgments are doing. Describing all of it as repression misses occasions on which she clearly likes a quieter life. [KF-E06–E07, E14–E15](#kf-e06)

Her strengths do not guarantee ease. She is better at making another person’s contradiction articulate than at preventing a known contradiction from recurring in her own relationships. She can identify that ideals and feelings pull apart while still finding it difficult to say what distribution of time or information would be acceptable. She can worry about exposing Hinami and later become intensely absorbed in an inquiry that hurts Haruka. These are not evidence that her reflective language is fraudulent. They show that insight is a resource, not an automatic control over conduct. [KF-E12, E14, E18](#kf-e12)

### 2.2 She wants reception, not merely refuge

Books initially offer Kikuchi somewhere other than the ordinary peer world to live imaginatively. But her response to *Popol* does not end with wanting to remain alone. She wants the kind of connection its story makes possible. Her diary asks whether what she writes is something she would really like to tell a friend. Her first disclosures to Fumiya connect an admired author, her own manuscript, and a hoped-for reader. At the festival, having her play received feels like having herself received. The continuity is not simply “she escapes into fiction.” It is that fiction provides a medium through which she can risk contact. [KF-E01–E02, E05–E06, E10](#kf-e01)

That need helps explain why readership matters at several scales. Goda reading the same book changes the adult from a benevolent outsider into someone willing to enter a shared subject. Fumiya reading her work gives her an interested interlocutor without making him an infallible critic. Classmates debating the play provide a response beyond romance. An editor’s interest opens the possibility of a public career. None is interchangeable with the others. A book contract would not settle whether her boyfriend hears her, and a boyfriend’s praise would not by itself establish professional readiness. [KF-E02, E05–E06, E10, E17](#kf-e02)

### 2.3 Her development concerns whose interests count

A recurring correction in Kikuchi’s life is to enlarge the set of people whose interests the model acknowledges. In her early account, the socially different person seems liable to damage others simply by entering their environment. Goda offers a counterexample in which the environment and the other participant can change. In V07, the author must include herself among the people whose happiness matters. In V09, the girlfriend must have a choosing position rather than living indefinitely inside a persuasive explanation offered by the person who chose her. In V11, the living model and the younger relative must remain people with stakes that cannot be exhausted by their usefulness to the author’s understanding. [KF-E02, E06–E07, E15, E18](#kf-e02)

This enlargement is not a doctrine of unlimited inclusion. Kikuchi’s refusal of large-group karaoke is not a failure to count her classmates. She explicitly likes them while declining the format. Nor does respecting Mimimi’s continuing love require giving up her own relationship. Nor must a writer promise that art will never disturb anyone. The meaningful question is whether the relevant person’s interests remain visible, discussable, and capable of changing the decision. That is the standard this monograph uses to distinguish an expansion of agency from an increasingly elegant explanation for someone’s disappearance.

## 3. Developmental chronology

The chronology below keeps diegetic time separate from disclosure time. The supplemental formation account and diary deepen our understanding of an earlier Kikuchi; they do not improve what Fumiya or the analyst knew at the earlier numbered boundary. Later ethical vocabulary also should not be read backward as the motive for every quiet choice.

### 3.1 Before Fumiya: a person already capable of enthusiasm and attachment

The V06.5 retrospective begins with Kikuchi looking at ordinary peer friendship and romance from outside. The distance is painful partly because she finds something attractive there. She does not possess a comprehensive preference for solitude that makes exclusion irrelevant. Reading becomes a refuge, but the refuge acquires positive content: invented places, a particular atmosphere, stories that retain loneliness without reducing life to it, and language capable of making an otherwise inaccessible experience shareable. The story’s movement from looking at other people to retelling *Popol* is an emergence into relation, not simply a retreat. [KF-E02; KF-P01](#kf-p01)

Goda’s importance is specific. She notices an aesthetic response and recommends a book partly through its design, without pretending to have read its contents. Later she actually reads it to share Kikuchi’s interest. Those two acts model a kind of epistemic honesty and reciprocity absent from the idea that a socially capable adult merely teaches the child to assimilate. Goda can offer an entry point without owning its meaning in advance. Kikuchi can then become the more enthusiastic and knowledgeable speaker. The person receiving help also gives something to the relationship.

The attempted entry into the girls’ game goes badly, but the sequence should not be inflated into an all-purpose persecution origin. There is initial welcome. Kikuchi does not know the rules, the explanation is deferred, and her polite register becomes conspicuous in a context she cannot read. Her subsequent account—that incompatible beings may hurt one another by sharing a habitat—is an interpretation produced from this experience. It protects her from concluding that all difference is inferiority, yet risks making one failed coordination attempt evidence of permanent incompatibility. Goda’s offer of a library friendship introduces an alternative: a person and a setting may be fitted to each other without either becoming universal. [KF-P02](#kf-p02)

The graduation visit is therefore consequential. Kikuchi seeks Goda, notices how to address her in that setting, and enjoys a conversation that is not a remedial lesson. The later high-school transition retains loneliness precisely because she has learned what meaningful companionship feels like. Some quiet contact with classmates exists; she is uncertain whether it amounts to friendship. This is a more discriminating state than either “no social needs” or “no social capacities.”

### 3.2 V01–V03: a hoped-for reader becomes a particular person

Kikuchi’s first image of Fumiya is partly a projection. Seeing what appears to be a boy reading Andi suggests the companion she has wanted. The private retrospective and diary preserve both attraction toward that possibility and hesitation about approaching it. The June records matter because they place tissue-giving, workplace observation, questions about cross-habitat contact, and the eventual library conversation on different dates. They do not establish that she already knew the person’s full character when she first wanted to speak. [KF-E01–E02; KF-P03–P04](#kf-p03)

Fumiya’s admission that the Andi premise was not originally genuine could have destroyed a relationship whose whole value depended on perfect preexisting similarity. Instead, an honest reset makes further contact possible. He actually reads, they spend time around books, and the connection becomes built rather than simply discovered. That distinction should also be applied to Kikuchi. She is not merely the reward for his decision to tell the truth. She chooses whether his imperfect participation is worth receiving and shares work that matters to her. [KF-E01](#kf-e01)

In V03, her account of his speech gives the relationship a new reciprocal function. When he explains his training, she distinguishes learned form from content that is only memorized. She describes the images his words evoke, while explicitly declining certainty that they are identical to his experience. Her intervention is useful because it separates a genuine change in communicative force from the simplistic demand that sincerity must sound untrained. Yet she is not simply instructing him from a finished position. She has watched his world acquire color, and this also becomes evidence about whether change is possible for her. [KF-E03; KF-P05](#kf-p05)

The ordinary setting is important. This is a café conversation with food, hesitation, and mutual explanation, not a philosophical pronouncement delivered from outside the story. The omurice on the table and Fumiya’s awareness of the intimacy of the discussion locate the exchange in a developing relationship. Kikuchi’s insight works through the attention of a particular recipient. It does not mean every future listener will experience Fumiya’s speech as she does, or that she can certify which of his statements are true.

### 3.3 V04–V06: the observer becomes a contributor and a public author

Kikuchi’s participation in the Tama project shows her attention becoming useful beyond the dyad. She resists reducing classmates to supporting roles in the protagonists’ problem. A person can be weak, resentful, or badly situated and still be responsible for harmful conduct. Her contribution broadens explanation without making wrongdoing disappear. That is a real ethical capacity. It is also a literary way of looking: someone’s apparently minor part in this story is not the whole of that person’s life. [KF-E04](#kf-e04)

V06 makes the material existence of her writing much more important. Fumiya reads several short stories, not just a single allegory about the central romantic triangle. The collection contains different premises, and the unfinished *私の知らない飛び方* becomes the basis for later public work. Her imagination can include strategic deception, conflict, and people unlike the soft image Fumiya has formed of their author. His surprise is a useful warning to the analyst: a gentle social manner is not a limit on the moral or formal range of what she can imagine. [KF-E05; KF-P06](#kf-p06)

The movement into performance gives her new tasks. She must make private material available, consider whether it works when spoken and embodied, answer questions, and revise. Her pleasure in a reader’s concrete response already appears before professional attention. So does her concern for what happens to the characters. The relevant distinction is not that she has no personal preferences about the ending. It is that she does not regard preference alone as sufficient authority to move every inhabitant of the created world. That concern will later both enrich and constrain her life.

The adoption of the work into class production is neither wholly spontaneous collective love nor a theft of her authorship. The source preserves mixed reactions, coordination by more socially fluent classmates, and moments of assent. It also preserves Kikuchi’s continued role in the work. The later monograph should not simplify those layers into “Fumiya gave her confidence” or “the class discovered a genius.” What she gains is a public field in which her particular skill can matter. [KF-P07](#kf-p07)

### 3.4 V07: voluntary effort can be the wrong kind of self-change

Kikuchi’s attempt to become a better rehearsal leader initially resembles the social development she admires in Fumiya and Tama. She volunteers, tries, and is visibly strained. The failure is not that effort cannot improve a quiet person’s skills. It is that she has made a supposedly proper kind of person—the universally affiliative inhabitant of a well-made world—into the end of the effort. The technical problem and the normative problem interact: borrowed brightness does not fit the immediate group well, while failure threatens her right to belong at all. [KF-E06; KF-P08–P09](#kf-p08)

Her answer to Fumiya’s player-centered language is already an act of resistance. She tells him, politely but distinctly, that this is his vocabulary. She understands herself through an author’s view. That view is not simply a mistake to be replaced by his. It expresses a sincere concern with the arrangement of the world and the claims of people beyond a single player’s desire. The failure appears when she treats her own interests as the one element an ethical author should suppress. An interpretation that makes her a passive recipient of Fumiya’s solution would repeat that exclusion at the analytical level.

The return to *Popol* supplies a shared correction. The fire people need not become universally mobile to have a place in a good story. An affinity community can be a meaningful field rather than a consolation prize for failure at school. Kikuchi’s online identification as an aspiring writer then gives the alternative positive content. She is not only allowed to refuse a social ideal; she can pursue a craft. Her revised Chris similarly gains a path through an existing practice and a returnable home. The change is hers in execution and creative judgment, though it is relationally enabled. [KF-P10](#kf-p10)

### 3.5 V07: the public ending and the private choice do not cancel each other

Kikuchi’s public coda places Libra with Alcia, while the later private version gives Chris and Libra the intimate ending she wants. These should not be decoded as one secret message with a single true answer. They reveal that her world-facing ideal and her personal feeling are both active. The public version is not an emotionally neutral exercise; it performs an indirect refusal. But the private wish does not make her stated concern for the world’s arrangement a sham. She has tested whether admitting the feeling makes the world brighter and still fears that choosing it would subordinate what ought to be true to what she wants. [KF-E07–E08; KF-P11–P12](#kf-p11)

The interpersonal conversation matters because it does work neither artwork can do alone. Fumiya directly asks for a relationship, Kikuchi confronts the conflict, and acceptance is conveyed through action and their shared metaphor. No separate invented literal “yes” is needed. The relationship begins through real choices, not because a symbolic pattern has demonstrated an inevitable pairing. Her later insistence that he read the private ending there and then also matters. The polite, quiet character can want an immediate response to something she has made. She is not without preferences until a later crisis teaches her to speak. [KF-P12–P13](#kf-p12)

### 3.6 Between V07 and V08: ordinary life tests the improvement

The supplemental first Christmas gives the selected-habitat account a particularly valuable non-crisis test. Classmates enjoy and discuss Kikuchi’s work. She experiences this as a form of personal acceptance and wants closer relations gradually. When asked about a large-group karaoke continuation, she declines that format while affirming that she likes the people. She also wants Fumiya to enjoy his time with them and tell her about it. That is not complete indifference to separation. It is a locally workable arrangement that honors both her limits and his relationships. [KF-E10; KF-P14](#kf-p14)

The couple’s later play with the snowman, a shared photograph, gift expectations, and calendar coordination show how wanting becomes concrete. Kikuchi does not merely accept whichever date Fumiya offers. When he proposes replacing an earlier meeting with New Year time, she wants both. The request is small, but it establishes a person with appetites for shared life rather than an infinitely accommodating listener. The later V08 failure is more serious because this warmth was real, not because it was an illusion that the narrative finally exposes.

The Mimimi story opens another field. Kikuchi receives the disclosure of continuing love without defining romance as warfare. Her sympathy does not erase her own partnership, and her recognition of a feeling’s legitimacy does not authorize every act that might follow from it. At home on New Year’s Eve, she is an older sister participating in affectionate teasing, a daughter sharing a family ritual, and a writer handling the marked-up script she treasures. None of these roles is invented by romance, though romance now changes the horizon she anticipates. [KF-E11–E12; KF-P15–P19](#kf-p15)

That same home story already contains an ethical alarm. Kikuchi knows she may have pushed too far by writing Hinami-facing lines and interpreting their performance. She considers responsibility, curiosity, and the irreversibility of having disturbed something. V11 does not introduce artistic conscience into a previously innocent practice. It tests an existing conscience against stronger curiosity, opportunity, and a living recipient’s vulnerability. [KF-E12; KF-P18–P19](#kf-p18)

### 3.7 V08–V09: an owned beginning does not supply a maintenance system

Kikuchi’s New Year wish is to win a writing prize, not a wish that simply confirms romantic centrality. She is testing what she can do. The same early dating sequence lets her say that choosing the place and person changes an ordinary outing. V09’s later account of not having chosen must therefore be understood as a local reassessment of who has defined the relationship, not an objective erasure of all prior initiative. [KF-E13, E15; KF-P20–P21, P23](#kf-p20)

As Fumiya’s activities multiply, Kikuchi repeatedly asks for time, information, and contact. Her responses are not uniformly silent. Yet she often fears that asking too much would damage the expanding life she admires. The habitat metaphor that once released her from compulsory assimilation now risks explaining why she should accept being left behind. It remains partly true that their lives are not identical. The mistake would be to turn that truth into a rule that her loneliness has no claim on the relationship. [KF-E14; KF-P22](#kf-p22)

The phone incident and the subsequent repair distinguish her own wrong from his omissions. She acknowledges looking at private messages without permission. He acknowledges leaving her anxious and insufficiently informed. Neither admission cancels the other. Later, when he tries to resolve incompatibility by cutting away relationships and preparing to withdraw from the romance, she rejects the substitute person. She chooses Fumiya with the social history that helped make him, rather than demanding that he become someone more containable. That is a costly choosing position, not a pledge never again to feel jealousy. [KF-E14–E15; KF-P22–P23](#kf-p22)

### 3.8 V10–V11: wider participation, sharper stakes, unfinished authorship

In V10 Kikuchi enters more of Fumiya’s peer, family, and gaming life. Her participation is meaningful partly because she can name fear and proceed for a reason, rather than pretending the field is already easy. The train incident also gives her pleasure in discovering that Fumiya can be jealous: the relationship is not one-sided in vulnerability. Her choice to leave space for his private ride with Hinami remains visibly mixed—she wants the arrangement and is jealous. It is a local decision made by a person with continuing interests. [KF-E16; KF-P24–P26](#kf-p24)

V11 then separates several advances that would be easy to conflate. Editorial interest makes publication possible, not secured. A partly corroborated interpretation of Hinami makes Kikuchi perceptive, not omniscient. Haruka’s participation makes the inquiry interactive, not fully informed or automatically authorized on Hinami’s behalf. Kikuchi’s apology and self-questioning make the harm acknowledged, not repaired. Her later fiction reaches Fumiya, but that effect does not retroactively license the material or resolve her own reason to write. [KF-E17–E21; KF-P27–P35](#kf-p27)

The endpoint is consequently active without being complete. Kikuchi is willing to begin again, to receive a reason from Fumiya without declaring it finally her own, and to ask for other perspectives rather than make the next story a two-person monopoly. The wider party remains proposed. The important achieved change is her ability to state both participation and incompletion: she can want to write with others and still preserve the question of why she writes. She has not solved herself by becoming either the perfect girlfriend or the perfectly conscientious author.

## 4. Current state vector

Kikuchi’s present capacities must not be collapsed into the proposition that she has “become confident.” She is increasingly capable of choosing, speaking, collaborating, and defending an interest. She can still be frightened, uncertain, self-critical, or unable to resolve competing claims. V11 is especially clear that recognition of ability can arrive before a settled reason to use it.

| Dimension | Supportable V11-boundary account | Important non-equivalence |
|---|---|---|
| Literary competence | Sustained story construction, revision, differentiated characterization, adaptation for performance, and a work receiving conditional editorial interest | Competence is not omniscience, completion, or publication |
| Social competence | Effective selected conversations; café work; participation in collective production, dates, trips, and difficult inquiries | Selective competence is neither universal ease nor a universal deficit |
| Confidence | More willingness to initiate and defend a claim, especially within craft and close relationships | Willingness to act is not absence of fear or stable self-certainty |
| Status | Recognized class author, girlfriend, peer contributor, and prospective professional interlocutor | Recognition is not ownership of others’ information or a guaranteed career |
| Self-concept | Reader and writer who can help make a world, but whose reasons and responsibilities remain under examination | An aspiring-writer identity is not an already settled life justification |
| Agency | Chooses participation, refuses formats, revises work, reselects a partner, names injury, and proposes collaboration | Agency may be exercised under pressure and need not produce a harmless outcome |
| Value ownership | Affinity, literary craft, particular attachment, coherence, and care are repeatedly her concerns | A desired act is not automatically reconciled with every other value she holds |
| Current vulnerability | Self-exclusion in an ideal arrangement; interpretive absorption; difficulty converting insight into prospective limits | These vulnerabilities do not exhaust her ordinary personality |

### 4.1 Her attention is organized by meaningful relations, not just hidden motives

Kikuchi’s attention frequently goes to the relation between an outward form and the experience it carries. She notices how speech can evoke a scene, how a fictional character’s apparent capacities fail to explain what the character wants, how a group’s response changes what a text means to its author, and how a life’s visible success may conceal a missing basis of value. This is not simply an ability to discover secrets. It is an interest in how things belong together and what becomes intelligible when they are placed in a different relation. [KF-E03–E06, E18, E20](#kf-e03)

The same attention is present at lower stakes. The cover, material surface, and atmosphere of a book invite her to imagine the care that went into making it. A marked-up script is not just a record of corrections; it carries the memory of a wish realized with other people. A snowman made from available materials can become a small person to address and a memory to preserve. The general tendency is to invest objects and forms with the relationships they carry. That does not mean she is always symbolic or incapable of practical thought. It means that the value of an object to her is often inseparable from the experience around it. [KF-E02, E10, E12, E23](#kf-e02)

Her attention also has limits. When a difficult character problem becomes salient, she may narrow toward it rather than maintain equal awareness of the recipient in front of her. The Haruka scene makes this shift visible in the represented delivery: her words become less like ordinary dialogue and more like thinking aloud toward a model. This does not prove that she ceases to care about Haruka. It shows a competition within attention. Care remembered afterward is not the same as care governing the pace and depth of the question while it is being pursued. [KF-E18; KF-P29–P30](#kf-p29)

### 4.2 Her motives do not form one immutable ranking

A useful reconstruction identifies several recurring ends without pretending that they retain the same priority in every state. Kikuchi wants meaningful companionship; to read and make work that has expressive integrity; to preserve what she regards as the proper relation among people or characters; to be chosen and to choose in love; to avoid harming or burdening others; and, increasingly, to test whether writing can become a public vocation. The conflict is not usually between having a motive and having none. It is between motives that cannot be satisfied by one simple arrangement. [KF-E02, E05–E08, E13–E18](#kf-e02)

In the library or at home, pleasure and affinity can govern without a rank being at stake. In the V07 world-facing argument, integrity of the imagined arrangement can override personal desire. During V08–V09 romantic strain, protecting Fumiya’s wider life competes with wanting presence and reassurance. During the V11 inquiry, completing an understanding of Hinami competes with caution about exposure. When publication becomes plausible, artistic ambition, professional answerability, and a moral reason to continue must be considered together. None of these conflicts is accurately described as a permanent absence of self-interest.

Her stopping conditions are uneven. She can stop an ill-fitting social format by declining karaoke. She can accept assistance with facilitation rather than persist in being a universally capable rehearsal leader. She can challenge an interpretation and insist on a present response to a manuscript. But she has no demonstrated general procedure for stopping inquiry before another person is overwhelmed, no settled rule for how much relationship accommodation is too much, and no final criterion for when a fictional model is safe or justified to circulate. The monograph therefore treats her as having usable local boundaries, not complete boundary governance. [KF-E06, E10, E18–E19, E26](#kf-e06)

### 4.3 Her beliefs are serious, but often expressed through revisable analogies

Kikuchi believes, at several points, that shared objects and common understanding can connect people otherwise separated by experience. She resists the idea that one kind of social life exhausts legitimate belonging. She distinguishes a feeling from its moral condemnation merely on the basis of how it originated. She values other people as more than their role in a favored person’s narrative. She also believes that an author’s responsibility reaches beyond personal preference. These are supported positions, not a complete political or philosophical system. The corpus does not establish her views on unrelated public issues, religion, or abstract theories of justice. [KF-E02, E04–E07, E11](#kf-e02)

The fire-person, lake, player, and author analogies are consequential because she uses them to reason about action. They are also corrigible. Her first habitat explanation emerges from an experience, her later use is challenged by new experience, and the boyfriend she calls a *Popol*-like person can surprise the model by trying to abandon his own broad relations. It would be a mistake to treat her metaphors as diagnoses or fixed species assignments. Their power lies partly in giving a private difficulty a shareable form; their danger lies in making a contingent arrangement sound like a natural law.

### 4.4 What she fears losing

Several fears recur without supplying a clinical diagnosis. She fears misfitting a social field, disturbing what is valuable to others, being the obstacle to a loved person’s growth, and discovering that what she makes harms people it tries to understand. She also fears the distance opened by Fumiya’s expanding life and the specialness of a bond she cannot duplicate. These fears are not mutually exclusive with ambition. Wanting to become a writer exposes her to readers; wanting a relationship exposes her to needs she cannot satisfy through solitary interpretation. [KF-E02, E06–E07, E14–E18](#kf-e02)

A plausible organizing hypothesis is that she often finds it easier to defend a position once it can be expressed as a relation among legitimate interests, rather than as an unsupported demand that her own feeling should win. This helps explain both her strengths and her delays. She can formulate a generous understanding of Mimimi, distinguish her own privacy wrong from Fumiya’s omissions, and defend the actual Fumiya against his self-erasure. But she can also wait too long to make her own need count, because she is trying to establish how it fits the whole. The hypothesis is not universal: immediate enjoyment, irritation, and wanting both dates supply important simpler counterexamples. [KF-E10–E11, E14–E15](#kf-e10)

## 5. Selective habitat is not a universal social deficit

### 5.1 The habitat model begins as both protection and limitation

The middle-school sequence establishes why Kikuchi’s account of difference is emotionally credible. She does not enter the girls’ game with knowledge of its rules, its conversational speed, or its expected register. Her failure is experienced not merely as losing a game but as becoming the cause of an awkward atmosphere. A model in which different beings require different environments can protect the self from a destructive conclusion: inability to fit this field does not mean there is no field in which one can live. [KF-E02; KF-P02](#kf-p02)

But the model can preserve the injury inside its solution. If the wrong presence inevitably chills or burns others, the socially different person becomes responsible for preventing contact. The original experience is then generalized beyond what it demonstrates. Initial welcome, the absence of shared rules, and the possibility of another person adjusting their own behavior are discounted. Kikuchi’s account is not simply false; it turns a real mismatch into a stronger theory of incompatibility than the scene requires. That is why the adult response matters so much.

Goda does not settle the problem by announcing that Kikuchi is secretly normal or that the other girls are wholly bad. She creates a more habitable relation. The library remains a place of quiet and books, but it becomes a place where someone else also wants to meet Kikuchi. Reading the same work is a cost borne by the other participant, not just an exercise imposed on the child. The asymmetry of adult and student remains, yet the exchange is not one-way normalization. Kikuchi’s world becomes a subject another person can voluntarily enter.

This formative relation prevents a later analytical exaggeration. Fumiya is important, but he is not the first human being to make Kikuchi’s interior communicable. Nor does the existence of a warm family eliminate her difficulty with school peers. Home, library, classroom, and romance have different entry conditions. A monograph that explains all sociability through one rescuer would erase the very environmental differentiation the source develops. [KF-E02, E12, E27](#kf-e02)

### 5.2 A proper environment is not one in which nothing changes

Kikuchi’s preferred habitat is not a sealed place in which she must be left untouched forever. Her library life changes when she retells a book, receives a companion’s response, or shares her writing. Her quiet dyad with Fumiya changes when he becomes a boyfriend and when she wants to enter more of his life. Her writing practice changes when it becomes a script, an online work, and a possible book. The continuity lies in retaining meaningful forms of attention while acquiring new ways to act. [KF-E01–E03, E05–E06, E16–E17](#kf-e01)

This makes “selective” more precise than “small.” A relationship can be intimate and still be ill-fitting at a particular moment, as the V11 call shows. A group can be larger and still offer an intelligible role, as the festival’s reception of her work shows. A setting does not become suitable just because it is quiet, and unsuitable just because it contains many people. Familiar purpose, shared references, role clarity, the ability to refuse, and the way others respond all matter. The source supports this multi-variable account more strongly than an invariant preference for the smallest possible number of people.

The café worker also complicates the caricature of total social incapacity. Work supplies a defined role and conventions for interaction. The source does not provide an exhaustive account of her performance at work, but her presence there cannot be ignored because it does not fit a picture of someone unable to address strangers. Similarly, the conversation with Mimimi after work moves from an initially asymmetrical encounter into a reciprocal exchange. A work role may provide entry; it does not explain all the personal ease that follows. [KF-E11, E23; KF-P15–P16](#kf-p15)

### 5.3 Why the rehearsal failure is not merely stage fright

Kikuchi’s strained rehearsal leadership involves a practical mismatch: she attempts a kind of brightness and coordination that does not reliably carry the group. If the only problem were missing technique, the obvious solution would be more practice of the same ideal. But the source places the technique inside a self-conception. She believes that becoming more universally sociable is what a properly included person ought to do. Failure therefore acquires significance beyond one rehearsal. It suggests that she is the wrong kind of inhabitant. [KF-E06; KF-P08–P10](#kf-p08)

Fumiya initially misreads this because the surface resembles his own path. Deliberate effort and public participation helped him, so he is inclined to see the same behavior as the same development. Mizusawa’s comparison and Kikuchi’s own explanation expose the inverted motive. Fumiya was pursuing something he wanted to make possible; Kikuchi is trying to satisfy a world-level prescription. That is a useful distinction, but it must not become the new absolute rule that externally articulated ideals are always alien. Kikuchi’s authorial responsibility is genuinely hers. The problem is the exclusion of her preferred form of life from what the ideal permits.

The practical correction likewise is not to remove her from the project. Facilitation can be distributed, and her writing can remain central. Her artistic revision makes the new account concrete: Chris’s future grows from an existing practice rather than requiring total assimilation, and the garden remains a place that can be returned to. The result is not an anti-development moral. It is a different relation between continuity and change. A person can enter a wider world through something already loved, rather than proving worth by discarding the place from which she came.

### 5.4 Declining karaoke is positive evidence, not an absence

The Christmas conversation provides an unusually clean test because there is no need to infer hidden catastrophe. Kikuchi likes the classmates who received the play. She does not want the large-group karaoke format. She tells Fumiya both things and encourages him to enjoy the connection that matters to him. She also wants his later stories. Her refusal therefore does not withdraw from all reciprocity; it selects a form of continued relation. [KF-E10; KF-P14](#kf-p14)

The scene should be described precisely. The immediate late-night karaoke proposal is curtailed in the group’s discussion, and the question to Kikuchi concerns participation in that sort of continuation. This is not an event in which she abandons an ongoing room full of singers. Nor should the parallel karaoke story in the same volume be imported to prove that she later takes part. The positive evidence is her stated preference, its warmth, and Fumiya’s reception of it.

V08–V09 complicate the local success. Encouraging him to enjoy other ties is easier when the couple’s own time is secure than when invitations, omissions, and scheduling collisions accumulate. Her earlier permission does not become a waiver of later loneliness. The habitat model must therefore be supplemented by relationship maintenance. Different habitats can remain legitimate while the connection between them needs time, information, and repair. A philosophy of difference cannot by itself decide how often two people see each other.

## 6. Observation, interpretation, and epistemic authority

### 6.1 Her perceptiveness is a practice of listening and reconstruction

Kikuchi often receives information by listening to Fumiya’s experiences, comparing them with observed conduct, and constructing possible inner arrangements. This matters because her insight can otherwise appear supernatural. The V03 conversation explicitly shows a new inference being made after he explains his training. Her account of memorized material is not proof that she had already known the origin of every earlier utterance. The source gives an observant listener revising a model, not a mind reader discovering facts without input. [KF-E03; KF-P05](#kf-p05)

She is attentive to experiential specificity. Fumiya’s speech matters to her not because it always sounds like polished prose, but because it can evoke a lived scene. She also qualifies the relation between that image and his original experience. This is a small but important epistemic boundary: communication can be vivid without producing identical interiors. The distinction belongs in any account of her voice and any attempt to use her as an authority on someone else’s authenticity.

Her later treatment of classmates as full people extends this practice. The person who appears to be merely a follower, an antagonist, or an obstacle to Tama may have a private history and contradictions not visible from the intervention’s immediate goal. Enlarging the person does not require acquitting the conduct. Kikuchi’s contribution is strongest when it prevents explanatory compression while preserving moral distinction. It is weaker when imaginative completion supplies certainty where evidence is missing. [KF-E04](#kf-e04)

### 6.2 Focalization gives her access and also a biased input stream

Fumiya repeatedly speaks to Kikuchi about his experiences. That makes her a recipient of information not available to every classmate. It does not make the information a neutral record of all actors. His account selects events, labels motives, and emphasizes what was salient to him. When she transforms that material into fiction, the new perspective is partly an answer to his blind spots, but it is also built from his telling. A second perspective can correct a first without becoming independent primary testimony. [KF-E20; KF-P33](#kf-p33)

The V11 reception scene makes this circulation explicit. Fumiya recognizes events he told her about, then sees them reorganized through an Alcia-centered account. He experiences the result as access to what his own story could not show. That experience is narratively significant. Yet his recognition of the reconstruction’s force does not certify each imagined thought. He himself distinguishes the fictional state from what can be known about Hinami, even while finding the model compelling. The monograph must preserve that hesitation rather than convert the emotional success of reading into evidence of omniscience.

The knowledge map is also uneven between recipients. Kikuchi knows of Mimimi’s confession through the interstitial conversation, while Fumiya is absent and is not shown knowing of that encounter at its own chronological horizon. By V09 he hears Mimimi report a recent conversation with Kikuchi and their shared reasons for liking him. This gives him some communicated knowledge; it does not identify the encounter as exactly the V08.5 café scene or establish that he learns its full contents, including the specific confession disclosure. She receives some Hinami-related information through allowed disclosure and is present for other transfers whose authority is problematic. She does not acquire every private supplemental perspective simply because the analyst has read it. “She understands the group” is therefore too broad unless the particular route of understanding is named. [KF-E11, E16, E26–E27](#kf-e11)

### 6.3 The lost-god inference is powerful because it revises, not because it is magic

In the Haruka conversation, Kikuchi’s attention sharpens around the account of an affirming household. A simple deprivation model would expect emptiness to follow from never having received warmth or a trustworthy basis of value. The new information allows another possibility: a comprehensive basis once existed and was subsequently broken. That is a substantive revision. It helps explain why additional praise might fail to answer Hinami’s problem. [KF-E18; KF-P29](#kf-p29)

But the inference operates at a particular level of abstraction. It does not identify every event of the family history, the exact school-year chronology, Nagisa’s immediate intent, or a named religious doctrine. Later reader access corroborates a major formation mechanism while retaining these limits. Kikuchi’s phrase about losing a god is interpretive language about a source of belief and value; it is not proof of literal theology, supernatural history, or a clinical condition.

The scene also refuses a simple opposition between a passive child and an invading analyst. Haruka has joined the conversation, asks what Kikuchi thinks, and later asks the others to wait when Fumiya attempts to interrupt. Her question about how Kikuchi understands signals both recognition and vulnerability. These are real acts of participation. They make it inaccurate to say Kikuchi simply continued against Haruka’s explicit refusal. They do not settle whether Haruka could anticipate the depth or emotional effect of the inquiry, and they do not grant permission on Hinami’s behalf. Local assent, comprehension of risk, disclosure rights, and subsequent fictional use remain different questions. [KF-P29–P30](#kf-p29)

This fuller sequence sharpens rather than erases Kikuchi’s accountability. The harm cannot be explained only by a missing verbal invitation, because invitations are present. The failure involves pace, attention, informational asymmetry, and the author’s urge to complete a model in the presence of someone personally implicated in it. Kikuchi’s later acknowledgment that she hurt a younger girl for creative inquiry is strong evidence of her own assessment. It should not be weakened into mere accidental awkwardness. Equally, Haruka’s reaction must not become a license for the analyst to narrate exactly which memory caused each tear. Fumiya supplies a conjectural explanation; the immediate affect is observed, the full inner mechanism is not. [KF-E18](#kf-e18)

### 6.4 Insight does not settle who may decide what happens next

A good reconstruction distinguishes at least four separate achievements. Kikuchi can form an accurate or plausible model. She can express it vividly. A recipient can be affected by it. The expression can lead to a useful action. None automatically establishes that the source person consented to being interpreted, that a particular delivery was prudent, or that wider circulation would be justified. Conversely, the absence of complete certainty does not make all interpretation illegitimate. Literary and interpersonal understanding often operate under uncertainty. The question is how that uncertainty and the affected person’s agency are carried into use. [KF-E18, E20–E21, E26](#kf-e18)

This is an ethical distinction, not a legal ruling about fiction. The admitted novels do not supply a comprehensive policy for autobiographical borrowing, consent, anonymization, or artistic transformation. The monograph therefore does not invent one and attribute it to Kikuchi. It identifies the unresolved responsibilities her actual practice creates. The fact that a character resembles a person does not prove every invented scene violates that person; the fact that a story changes names or genre does not prove there is no exposure. Recognition, intimacy of material, audiences, foreseeable effects, and the possibility of refusal matter separately.

Kikuchi’s current advance is to recognize that “I can understand this” and “I want to write this” are insufficient answers to “why should I give this priority when it hurts someone?” The current limit is that the question remains live while she begins another project. That is not hypocrisy by definition. It is an unfinished attempt to act without falsely claiming that her motive has already been purified.

## 7. Fiction as form, relationship, and risk

### 7.1 The writing career begins before the romantic answer

Kikuchi’s authorship cannot be treated as a mechanism invented to communicate whom she wants to date. The private diary already connects writing with a wished-for reader, and V06 gives Fumiya several different stories to read. The unfinished work later used for the festival is one artifact among others, not the entire extent of her imaginative life. Her interest in a writer she admires, her absorption in storytelling, and her pleasure in a concrete response all precede the established couple. [KF-E02, E05; KF-P04, P06](#kf-p04)

Her literary ambition is likewise not reducible to being encouraged by a boyfriend. Fumiya’s support matters, but he cannot create the object of her sustained attention out of nothing. When her profile becomes *作家志望*, the positive end is a development of an existing practice. The newcomer-prize aspiration then turns identity toward an evaluable project. She wants to test what she can do. The V11 editorial approach increases the credibility and stakes of that route, but the desire does not first come into existence because a professional notices it. [KF-E06, E13, E17](#kf-e06)

The sequence also distinguishes types of completion. Sharing several stories is not the same as finishing every story. Completing a play is not the same as completing a prose novel. Posting part of an online work is not the same as completing a book-length manuscript. An editor’s interest in a chapter and a possible publication schedule are not a contract, an accepted finished manuscript, income, or a sustainable occupation. Precision about these stages is necessary to reconstruct a developing writer rather than celebrate a career the source has not yet shown.

### 7.2 Her craft involves revision, not just inspired perception

The source supports concrete work beneath the reputation for insight. Kikuchi revises after writing, adapts speech to performance, answers questions, considers staging, accepts useful practical contributions, and changes an ending when the world it creates becomes inadequate. The marked-up script has interpretive force because it is materially worked on. Its annotations record the conversion of private invention into instructions other people can act on. Her treasure is therefore not merely the first pure version of a solitary inspiration; it is an artifact of collaborative realization. [KF-E05–E06, E12; KF-P06–P10, P18](#kf-p06)

Her control is also not identical to refusing every suggestion. A writer can preserve final judgment while allowing performers’ knowledge of rhythm or delivery to improve the work. Hinami’s acting, Tama’s presence, Fumiya’s questions, and the production group’s practical needs affect what the play becomes. The important distinction is between contribution and replacement of the author’s responsibility. Kikuchi’s authority is clearest when she considers and makes a revision, not when the text remains untouched by anyone else.

This matters for her social development. Writing gives her a way to participate that is not an imitation of the most socially fluent person in the room. She can make something to which others respond, and she can be accountable for its form. The rehearsal failure does not disprove this competence. It shows that writing and leading a group through rehearsal are different tasks. Delegating facilitation does not make her artistic participation less real; it makes the distribution of competencies more accurate.

<a id="her-fiction-predicts-the-correct-romance"></a>
### 7.3 The two endings preserve an unresolved ethical division

The public play and the private prose ending must be kept distinct. Their different conclusions are not evidence that one can simply be discarded as a lie. Kikuchi’s world-facing responsibility is sincere enough to constrain her own wanted relationship. Her personal feeling is sincere enough to produce another version. The conflict concerns the authority each should have in shaping a shared world. [KF-E07–E08; KF-P11–P13](#kf-p11)

A reductive romantic reading treats the public Libra–Alcia coda as a code whose only function is to reject Fumiya. That interpersonal effect is real, but it does not exhaust the author’s problem. She also believes the pairing fits an ideal arrangement. A reductive philosophical reading does the opposite: it makes the coda a neutral statement about coherence, ignoring the personal cost and the communication it performs. A mature account holds both. The story can be artistically reasoned and emotionally interested at once.

Fumiya’s challenge does not require her to abandon responsibility to the world. It requires her to count herself among the people whose feelings the world contains. This is a stronger correction than “stop thinking and follow your heart.” A world in which the author’s own desire is excluded in advance is not automatically more impartial. Yet admitting the desire does not mean it must always win. The resulting position is a difficult hybrid: preserve what she judges necessary, acknowledge what she wants, and seek a viable arrangement rather than assuming one must erase the other.

The private version is therefore movement, not total integration. It gives desire made form and invites a particular reader’s response. It does not show that all future public work can reconcile the same claims, nor that a real relationship has been guaranteed by a revised fictional pairing. The actual couple still requires time and reciprocal choice after the literary problem has been articulated.

### 7.4 Chris and Alcia do different work for their author

Chris provides Kikuchi with a way to represent enclosure, difference, and a path into a wider world. The movement toward flower-ornament craft and a returnable garden is a structural answer to compulsory universality. It should not be treated as evidence that Kikuchi herself makes flower ornaments, wants a craft-shop career, or has a particular gardening habit. Those are choices within the authored model. Their literary significance is that an existing affinity can become a route outward without abolishing a home. [KF-E06–E08; KF-P10](#kf-p10)

Alcia is more dangerous because the model invites a completion of another person’s hidden interior. Her competence, severity, and apparent emptiness draw on Hinami, but the royal expectations, blood structure, and many inner scenes belong to fiction. Kikuchi’s own New Year’s Eve reflection recognizes the risk that writing a role for Hinami to perform may have probed more than she could answer for. The source even preserves uncertainty about whether Kikuchi’s perception of the encounter was sharpened or distorted by her own Alcia image. This is an author aware that the model can shape the evidence she thinks she is receiving. [KF-E09, E12; KF-P18–P19](#kf-p18)

The later *純混血とアイスクリーム* is not simply the same play with one more ending. It reorganizes character relations and world-building into another work, with its own partial publication state and editorial possibilities. Shared names and source models do not collapse all versions into one document. When an argument cites a fictional event, it must name which work and version carries it. Otherwise an imagined thought from the later novel can silently become evidence for what Kikuchi or Hinami knew during the festival.

<a id="professional-publication-is-secured"></a>
### 7.5 The V11 professional offer changes the cost of incompletion

The editor’s approach is meaningful because it recognizes something beyond private encouragement: a work may be suitable for a public route. Kikuchi nevertheless says publication is not yet decided. Later she reports discussion of continuing the work and a possible September schedule conditional on quality and timing. That is a fictional calendar proposal within the story, not an instruction to assign the real-world year of this analysis or a confirmed release date. [KF-E17; KF-P27–P28](#kf-p27)

Her response contains ambition and caution. She wants a long writing life, not merely a one-time compliment. She also thinks Alcia is unfinished because Hinami remains unresolved to her. This can be read in more than one way. It may show ethical reluctance to publish a person she has not adequately understood. It may show a craft problem: the character will not function coherently without a stronger model. It may show a self-imposed requirement that a professional writer should be able to solve the difficult subject. The source gives support to these overlapping motives and does not require selecting a single pure one.

Fumiya interprets part of her postponement or redirection as practiced self-suppression. That interpretation is plausible in light of the V07 pattern, but it is not the only legitimate account. A developing writer can rationally postpone an intrusive project or write something else. Refusal to continue immediately is not automatically fear that a more confident person should overcome. The monograph therefore treats the reported editorial route as a pressure on judgment, not proof that the correct next act is to finish Alcia at any cost.

### 7.6 “Why write?” is not the same question as “Do I enjoy writing?”

Kikuchi’s V11 question should be preserved at its actual level. Her love of Andi, her pleasure in making stories, her aspiration, and her history of writing remain evidence. What becomes inadequate is using that history as the whole reason to prioritize writing when inquiry wounds someone. Her self-questioning asks what could justify proceeding, not merely whether a forgotten hobby can be rediscovered. [KF-E18; KF-P30](#kf-p30)

This is why a simple answer such as “because it makes her happy” would flatten the problem. Happiness is not irrelevant, but she already knows that writing matters to her. She is trying to understand its relation to other claims. Nor would “because it helps people” be sufficient. The same practice helps Fumiya and hurts Haruka; even a helpful reception might use material whose source person has not accepted its circulation. An answer must therefore contend with mixed effects, not select the pleasant consequence as the final verdict.

The inquiry is also not evidence that she should necessarily abandon art. The source does not equate every painful representation with wrongdoing, and it does not prescribe a life without interpretation. Kikuchi’s present uncertainty is valuable because it refuses an easy escape in either direction: neither the sanctity of art nor the avoidance of every hurt has been established as an adequate rule. She is a writer in formation whose moral and vocational questions have become inseparable, not a finished moral theory temporarily inconvenienced by publication.

### 7.7 Why the second chapter reaches Fumiya

The posted second chapter changes the position from which Fumiya encounters a familiar pattern. He recognizes a transformed version of the election and comparison problem, but the work dwells on Alcia’s reasons and self-experience rather than keeping his own tactical viewpoint central. Victory no longer appears merely as the achievement of a powerful opponent. It is placed inside a pressure to make value provable. This reorganization lets the reader revisit events he thought he understood. [KF-E20; KF-P33](#kf-p33)

The most ethically charged fictional passages must remain fictional. Alcia’s death-adjacent thought, her inability to choose defeat, and the invasive force of her self-condemnation are written inner events. They do not establish Hinami’s suicidal intent, Nagisa’s intent, or an actual unshown event. Fumiya explicitly does not know how far the correspondence extends. He even considers that assigning such states to a recognizable model may be a serious violation. The artwork’s power therefore arrives with an objection inside its own reception, not after the analyst adds one.

What is observed at the end of the reading is narrower and stronger than a generalized cure. Fumiya wants to read more. He wants to speak with the person who wrote it. He contacts Kikuchi. These are changes in his action and self-reported motivation. His interpretation of the update as a message and his account of how she reconstructed his experiences are partly inferences. The later conversation supports the practical importance of the work, but not a claim that every aspect of his crisis, school attendance, or professional desire is now restored.

For Kikuchi, this reception joins earlier forms of reading without making them identical. Goda entered her world through the same book. Fumiya entered through listening and manuscripts. Now he returns to a shared world because her transformation of what he told her makes his own experience newly available to him. That is a substantial literary function. It still does not make her the new exclusive savior or establish the correctness of all her interpretive choices.

### 7.8 Distributed authorship is a correction with its own risks

Kikuchi’s proposal to involve others limits a two-person monopoly. She does not claim that she and Fumiya, simply by understanding each other, can now finish Hinami’s story. Other people know other aspects of Hinami and bring different competencies, flaws, and attachments. This is a meaningful response to the limits of one viewpoint. [KF-E21; KF-P34–P35](#kf-p34)

Yet more viewpoints can also produce a more elaborate unconsented representation. The group has not assembled, agreed, shared a complete information set, obtained Hinami’s participation, or demonstrated a beneficial result. Calling Hinami a formidable antagonist in the party analogy does not make the plan an actual hostile operation, but it does retain a structure in which others define the problem and propose how to reach her. Diversity of interpretation is not the same as subject authority.

The strongest present formulation is therefore deliberately double. Kikuchi has proposed a less monopolistic way to make the next work, and she has preserved the need for her own reason rather than declaring Fumiya’s sufficient forever. Neither action settles what the work should be allowed to do. Her authorship becomes more relational, but its ethical adequacy remains to be demonstrated.

## 8. Relationship-conditioned reconstruction

<a id="81-tomozaki-fumiya"></a>
### 8.1 Tomozaki Fumiya: reader, partner, beneficiary, and source

Kikuchi’s relation to Fumiya changes the kinds of initiative available to her. Early on, she approaches through a hoped-for shared author. Later she invites a response to her own writing, interprets his experience, receives his developing trust, and becomes a partner whose needs cannot be contained in the role of helpful reader. She can be shy in delivery and exact about what she wants from him: present feedback, another date, an explanation, the actual person rather than a self-amputated substitute. [KF-E01–E03, E07, E10, E14–E15](#kf-e01)

The relation is directional. Fumiya gives Kikuchi access to some broader social and professional worlds, supplies an interested first-reader position, and helps challenge the belief that she must become socially universal. Kikuchi gives him a form of listening and modeling that changes how he interprets his own actions. She also becomes vulnerable to his allocation of time and disclosure of information. Reciprocity is substantial, but it is not symmetry in every domain. He can decide that she is too delicate to join an event; she can interpret his nature so comprehensively that her own requests seem improper. Both apparently considerate moves can exclude the other from the decision.

V09’s reselection corrects one such asymmetry. Kikuchi wants to choose Fumiya as he actually exists, including ties that make her uneasy. This does not mean she must regard every future act toward Hinami as beyond complaint. V11 supplies the retest. She says that speaking of his special bond with Hinami hurts because he is her boyfriend. When he dismisses the attempt to reach him, she names the failed reception. The call’s terminating actor is unspecified. Her agency lies in the words she does say, not in an invented hang-up. [KF-E15, E19; KF-P23, P32](#kf-p23)

The later repair matters because Fumiya recognizes more than her willingness to tolerate him. He apologizes and thanks her for making renewed action possible. Her work and judgment have affected him. But she is still not simply promoted from quiet supporter to therapeutic provider. He becomes, at the same time, a source of stories and private information that can enter her fiction. The couple therefore needs a distinction between intimacy, creative collaboration, and permission to represent third parties. That boundary remains unfinished. [KF-E20–E21, E26](#kf-e20)

<a id="82-hinami-aoi"></a>
### 8.2 Hinami Aoi: admired competence, artistic model, and resistant person

Kikuchi’s Hinami-facing behavior cannot be reduced to romantic rivalry. Hinami represents a level of social and practical competence that Kikuchi tries to understand and, during the rehearsal period, partly imitate. She is also a difficult authorial problem: visible excellence does not disclose the desire that organizes it. That gap attracts Kikuchi’s perceptual and formal attention. The same person can therefore function as social model, relational pressure, and artistic subject without any one role exhausting the others. [KF-E06, E08–E09, E12, E18](#kf-e06)

Their knowledge is not equivalent. Hinami can perform Alcia and question Kikuchi’s interpretation; neither act means she has endorsed the fictional interior. Kikuchi can recognize a pattern and still lack the history that might explain or qualify it. The New Year’s Eve retrospective preserves that uncertainty. It also distinguishes the reader’s later access from Fumiya’s incomplete observation of the earlier private exchange. The monograph does not make his witnessed apology a transcript of everything represented in the later Kikuchi-focalized story. [KF-E09, E12; KF-P18–P19](#kf-p18)

By V11, Kikuchi’s concern for Hinami has practical effects: she joins inquiry and a prospective project, and she explicitly worries about what her writing does. That concern is not permission. Hinami remains the person whose affirmative end, desired relation to the project, and willingness to be represented are not established. The mature account must leave her able to reject a model that Kikuchi and Fumiya find illuminating. Otherwise the monograph would praise Kikuchi’s person-centeredness while treating the person at its center as an object to be completed.

<a id="83-mimimi"></a>
### 8.3 Nanami Minami / Mimimi: a reciprocal interlocutor, not a defeated rival

The interstitial café conversation is among the best tests of Kikuchi’s ordinary relational range. Mimimi arrives with continuing love, insecurity, and her own interpretation of Kikuchi’s beauty and apparent virtue. Those private thoughts are not knowledge Kikuchi possesses. Kikuchi can ask a perceptive question and respond to the disclosed feeling without having penetrated every layer of the focalizer’s mind. [KF-E11; KF-P15–P16](#kf-p15)

Her rejection of romance-as-war matters because it separates her relationship from a winner’s authority over another person’s feeling. She does not demand that Mimimi certify emotional closure. She recognizes that attraction may arise through shared aims, enjoyment, need, pursuit, or jealousy without becoming false solely because of that origin. At the same time, she does not abandon Fumiya or give permission to interfere with the relationship. Respect for feeling and governance of conduct remain distinct.

The conversation is not solemn tolerance all the way through. Kikuchi teases, questions, briefly apologizes when she may have touched something sensitive, and shares more than an abstract moral rule. She also indicates that she has experienced liking someone before. That local statement prevents the false inference that a first boyfriend means an absence of every earlier attraction; it does not identify those people or supply an unshown romantic history. Her capacity for lightness and directness with Mimimi should remain part of the character model, not disappear behind the label of rival sympathy.

Later Mimimi’s concern helps prevent Fumiya’s social self-erasure and contributes information to Kikuchi’s choice. The relationship therefore has effects beyond competition for a partner. Yet no complete three-person agreement exists, and the source does not make the situation costless. Kikuchi can respect Mimimi and still be insecure; Mimimi can help the couple and continue to love Fumiya. Neither has to become emotionally neutral for the other to remain a person worth respecting. [KF-E11, E15, E27](#kf-e11)

### 8.4 Goda: an early model of reciprocal attention

Goda’s relation to Kikuchi is developmentally indispensable even though it is not a main romantic or peer dyad. She notices a response, offers a book, asks about friendship, apologizes when an encouraged attempt proves painful, and creates a different form of companionship. The later act of reading the book allows Kikuchi’s enthusiasm to become shared rather than merely observed. The adult does not make the child’s meaningful world a private defect to cure. [KF-E02; KF-P01–P03](#kf-p01)

The source calls her 郷田沙也加; the ruby supports **Goda Sayaka**. The inherited supplemental reading uses “Saya Goda.” This study uses Goda or the Japanese name; character-state §8.6 and the integration review record the narrow transliteration correction. No new identity or extra biographical source is inferred from it.

Kikuchi’s register and behavior with Goda also demonstrate development within a relation. An initially reserved student becomes an excited reteller and, later, someone who seeks the adult out and can tease her. The respect attached to the teacher’s position and the warmth of companionship coexist. That combination is a better guide to Kikuchi than an assumption that politeness always marks emotional distance.

### 8.5 Family: the older sister is not the classroom outsider

The New Year’s Eve story supplies direct Kikuchi focalization in a warm household. Her younger brother Riku has a public face she recognizes as different from his behavior at home. She can be affectionate, mildly corrective, flustered by teasing, and amused by the fact that someone who is capable elsewhere still acts childishly with her. The narration itself acknowledges multiple modes. This helps prevent the analyst from treating social performance as a pathology unique to Hinami or as evidence that a person has no self. [KF-E12; KF-P17–P19](#kf-p17)

The family scene also gives her practical participation: food preparation and serving are shared, she clears space, and the marked script remains present amid an ordinary annual ritual. Her father’s work and interest in the script are bounded observations, not a complete account of household economics or parental reading habits. Her brother’s affectionate conduct does not make every teasing act welcome in all circumstances. The immediate picture is nevertheless incompatible with explaining her school difficulty through an invented loveless home.

### 8.6 Haruka: warmth and inquiry can collide in the same encounter

Kikuchi can meet Haruka warmly and then become intensely focused on what Haruka’s account makes inferable. The turn is part of the scene’s force: the person is not introduced as an uncaring interrogator. Their interaction contains food, friendly address, invitations to ask, and later a request to continue. Those social forms make access possible. They do not guarantee that the inquiry remains appropriately paced or that its purpose is fully shared. [KF-E18; KF-P29–P30](#kf-p29)

Haruka’s distress brings Kikuchi back from interpretive absorption, and Kikuchi subsequently names her own motive problem. The apology is not evidence of completed repair; neither is the younger girl’s earlier participation evidence that no harm occurred. The relation remains a limit case for Kikuchi’s care: she needs a way to let the recipient’s vulnerability constrain the process before the model is finished, not only to acknowledge that vulnerability afterward.

<a id="84-the-peer-and-professional-worlds"></a>
### 8.7 Classmates, collaborators, and editor: different audiences make different claims

The class receives Kikuchi as the maker of something they can perform, debate, and enjoy. That response can enlarge belonging without making her a general social leader. Tama, Yuzu, Mizusawa, and the other contributors have distinct practical roles; their support should not be merged into an undifferentiated approving audience. The work’s realization depends on several kinds of competence, including competencies Kikuchi does not claim. [KF-E04–E06, E10](#kf-e04)

The editor introduces another kind of response. Professional interest concerns a public artifact and its completion, not only the author’s emotional need to be understood. Kikuchi’s worry about doing justice to the people who might produce the book shows that she recognizes this difference. But the editor has not been shown endorsing every route she takes to understand Hinami, and the source does not provide a complete editorial contract or pressure regime. It would be equally wrong to make the professional field a guaranteed liberator or an invented coercive institution. Its present role is to make an existing aspiration plausible and to sharpen the unfinished decisions that accompany it. [KF-E17](#kf-e17)

## 9. Self-presentation and focalization

### 9.1 The “angel” is partly a way other people see her

Fumiya’s recurrent idealizing language is not disposable decoration. It tells us why Kikuchi’s presence matters to him: she seems to provide a quieter, more comprehensible field in which he can speak and be received. Her appearance and manner become associated with relief from social strain. But those associations are his experience, not a complete specification of her personality. Treating them as fact would turn the character into the very function—safe listener, beautiful exception, untroubled source of insight—that later scenes complicate. [KF-E01, E03, E24](#kf-e01)

Mimimi’s focalization supplies a parallel warning. She encounters Kikuchi partly through comparison with her own insecurity and the pain of non-selection. What looks to her like effortless goodness or intimidating perfection cannot simply be adopted as an objective measure of Kikuchi. The conversation itself gives a more particular person: someone who works, becomes surprised, teases, asks a difficult question, and qualifies her own standing to pronounce on another person’s feeling. The contrast between the observer’s image and the actual exchange is part of the characterization. [KF-E11; KF-P15–P16](#kf-p15)

Nor should the later force of Kikuchi’s inquiry be sensationalized as the revelation of a hidden monstrous self. Fumiya’s narration deliberately changes its imagery and describes a disturbing intensity. That is direct evidence of how he experiences the scene and of the text’s represented delivery. It is not a diagnosis. The important behavioral observation is that her attention becomes concentrated, her speech becomes less dialogic, and a model takes shape with insufficient restraint. The same person can afterward be shocked and remorseful. [KF-E18](#kf-e18)

### 9.2 Japanese voice: politeness can carry initiative, resistance, and desire

The attested samples support a range of speech acts rather than a comprehensive statistical idiolect. Kikuchi often uses polite forms with peers in the scenes inspected here. But polite grammar does not tell us whether she is yielding. She can qualify an inference, ask for a response, reject an imposed vocabulary, declare whom she chooses, refuse an unwanted act, or say that her words have not reached someone. The content and the available answer space matter more than a simple ranking of soft versus forceful speech. [KF-E22; JVL-12–JVL-15](#kf-e22)

V07 offers a particularly important early counterexample to passive politeness. Her statement that the player-centered formulation belongs to Fumiya’s language is a refusal of conceptual substitution. She does not become rude to claim a different account of herself. The V09 choosing request likewise uses permission-oriented grammar—*選ばせてください*—to demand a position in deciding, not to renounce decision. In V11, *やめてください* remains polite while plainly opposing Rena’s conduct. Hesitation and intensity can coexist with a real limit. [KF-P09, P23, P31](#kf-p09)

The early game-entry scene also makes the social force of register explicit. Her politeness is conspicuous to the other girls, and that contributes to her sense of mismatch. It is not proof that politeness is inherently inauthentic. In another setting, the same carefulness can become part of a successful relation. Goda, Fumiya, and Mimimi receive different mixtures of reserve and animation. Speech is not simply an internal trait projected unchanged onto every recipient.

### 9.3 Her characteristic qualifications are not all signs of uncertainty about herself

Kikuchi’s speech often distinguishes what she thinks from what she can certify. In V03 she does not claim that the images her listener’s words evoke are identical to his experience. In the Hinami inquiry, however, cautious-looking grammar can coexist with an increasingly forceful and exposing interpretation. A phrase framed as a thought is still capable of wounding. Hedging should therefore not be treated as a guarantee of harmlessness or epistemic humility. [KF-P05, P29](#kf-p05)

Other mitigations have different functions. She apologizes for suddenly naming joy or wanting time; this can soften intimacy rather than withdraw it. She uses a modest formulation for her prize attempt while clearly intending to try. She can also become mildly insistent when Fumiya rejects her account of his importance to her. The V08 *私がそう思うんです* claims authority over her own evaluation. This is not the same speech act as assigning an interior to Hinami. Her right to say what Fumiya’s help means to her does not depend on his agreeing that he possesses a mysterious power. [KF-E13, E22; KF-P21](#kf-p21)

The contrast offers a useful reconstructive rule. Kikuchi can be most firmly entitled to a proposition precisely when it concerns her own feeling or choice, even if the delivery is embarrassed. She is on weaker ground when a beautifully coherent proposition describes another person’s unspoken inner life, even if her delivery becomes unusually assured. Confidence of manner and authority over the proposition vary independently.

### 9.4 She has humor and a capacity to control conversational pacing

The childhood retelling to Goda is a valuable voice sample because its liveliness is not a technique learned from Fumiya. Kikuchi raises the energy of her narration, withholds the ending, and enjoys the recipient’s interest. She can turn knowledge into a small playful advantage. This is a communicative strength already available in a fitting field. [KF-P01](#kf-p01)

With Mimimi, humor is quieter but still active. She recognizes something amusing about Fumiya’s concentrated interest and joins a light exchange about love and prior liking. At home she corrects and is teased by Riku, and her speech can be shorter and plainer than the careful peer register. With Fumiya, the snowman and the insistence on both dates show an ordinary playful wanting. At the V11 repair, she can respond to his large proposal with amused skepticism rather than solemnly receiving it as a command. These are recipient-conditioned forms of ease, not evidence that she has become uniformly outgoing. [KF-P14, P16–P19, P34](#kf-p14)

A faithful account of how she “sounds” should therefore not consist of adding ellipses and honorifics to every sentence. Those surface devices cannot represent the difference between an enthusiastic reteller, a polite dissenter, an intimate partner insisting on a preference, and an absorbed writer testing a model. The evidentiary question is what she is trying to do with this person in this state. No invented dialogue is needed to make that distinction useful.

### 9.5 Narration, writing, and speech remain separate voices

Kikuchi’s diary is written private evidence. The New Year’s Eve unit supplies represented first-person narration and interior reflection. The play and the later novel supply deliberately authored character voices. None should be treated as a direct transcript of her ordinary conversation. A fictional queen’s wording is not automatically Kikuchi’s habitual register, and Alcia’s first-person statement is not Hinami’s confession. [KF-E02, E08, E12, E20, E22](#kf-e02)

This also constrains acoustic claims. The present evidence is Japanese prose and inspected diary images, not audio performance. Terms such as clear, trembling, gentle, or forceful refer to delivery represented by the prose or to the structure of the speech act. No claim is made about an actor’s voice, habitual pitch, accent, timing measured from sound, or a frequency distribution across all her speech. The selective samples are enough to establish meaningful variation, not an exhaustive verbal fingerprint.

## 10. Ordinary-life and preference evidence

### 10.1 Reading is tactile, imaginative, and social

Kikuchi’s repeated attachment to Michael Andi is well supported. The more specific characterization lies in how she reads. She can become absorbed enough to lose track of time, feel moved while continuing, and want to retell what affected her. The book’s cover and material surface also matter. Goda notices her response to a fantastical, lonely, nostalgic atmosphere, and Kikuchi’s attention to the physical book prompts an imagination of the care behind it. This is a situated appreciation, not evidence that a particular cover color is her favorite color or that she always selects books by design. [KF-E02, E23; KF-P01](#kf-p01)

The diary’s old collection is similarly more than a bibliographic find. Some contents overlap what she already knows, but the object still pleases her, and she wants to show it to Goda. Reading supports a relationship as well as solitary experience. The pattern continues when Fumiya actually reads the author they share. The book does not only identify a compatible kind of person; it gives two imperfectly matched people something to do together.

The source does not establish a categorical rejection of electronic reading. In V07, availability of the relevant work electronically surprises her, while Fumiya contributes his own aesthetic preference for the image of her holding a physical book. His preference is not hers. The accurate distinction is that material books are positively meaningful in several scenes, while an absolute medium rule remains unproved. [KF-P10](#kf-p10)

### 10.2 Writing includes objects she keeps, not only crises she dramatizes

Kikuchi keeps a diary, but the evidence does not support a rigid daily schedule. Her May entry reflects on gaps, on a second notebook, and on whom the writing might address. The notebook can be record, practice, conversation with a future self, or a substitute for something she wants to tell a friend. Its uncertainty is revealing: writing already sits between self-reflection and hoped-for communication. [KF-P04](#kf-p04)

The annotated festival script is more explicitly valued. She regards it as a treasure because it carries the time in which other people realized a wish she had made. The red marks are evidence of practical revision and transmission, not blemishes on an otherwise perfect text. This gives a concrete form to her preference for meaningful making. She does not only cherish finished beauty; she can cherish the worked object and the collaborative process it records. [KF-E12; KF-P18](#kf-p18)

She also wants responses. The present-tense request that Fumiya read the private ending immediately is small but decisive. A writer can be generous toward a reader and still impatient to know what he thinks. Such wanting should not be smoothed into a portrait of unlimited delicacy. It is part of the ordinary appetite that makes authorship a lived practice rather than a philosophical function. [KF-P13](#kf-p13)

### 10.3 Food, café life, and the limits of a preference claim

**Omurice is a supported recurring preference, though no exclusive favorite is established.** It appears in the V03 café context, and in V08 Fumiya again notices it and explicitly interprets the recurrence as her liking it. That combines repeated situated evidence with an attributed judgment. It is stronger than inferring a taste from one shared meal, but weaker than a self-declared ranking of favorite foods. [KF-E23; KF-P05, P21](#kf-p05)

Other foods should be described at their actual level. She eats a mandarin under the kotatsu on New Year’s Eve and participates in the family soba ritual. The food-court meal with Haruka includes matching cheese dakgalbi orders. Those acts do not establish that mandarins, soba, cheese, or that dish are her enduring favorites. In particular, Hinami’s separately attested cheese preference cannot be transferred to Kikuchi merely because the group eats the same food. The café-work story likewise does not make a staff-provided cake her favorite dessert. [KF-P15, P17–P19, P29](#kf-p15)

Work gives her a routine outside reading, school, and romance. The café uniform and glasses in the Mimimi encounter belong to a particular role and scene. They do not establish an everyday fashion fixation, a medical need, or an attraction to costume play. The account of beginning that work around the start of the second high-school year is a bounded self-report, not a complete employment history. Her interactions there are useful evidence of practical participation without licensing an invented wage, schedule, household need, or long-term career intention. [KF-P15](#kf-p15)

### 10.4 She likes selected company, not necessarily silence without interruption

Quiet shared reading is important, but Kikuchi is not simply a person who wants others to remain silent around her. When a story is meaningful and a listener receptive, she talks at length and with energy. When feedback matters, she asks for it. When a partner’s plans affect her, she can name what she wants. The supported preference is better described as **interaction in which attention can be sustained and meaning can be shared**, often in a manageable one-to-one setting. [KF-E02–E03, E10–E11, E23](#kf-e02)

Her response to classmates is similarly differentiated. She enjoys reception of the play and wants gradual closeness. She declines a high-intensity group format. She can laugh at the boys’ snow play and appreciate the group’s attempt to give the new couple time alone, even while recognizing that they are partly amusing themselves. This is not an anti-social principle or a demand that every interaction be solemnly sincere. Mixed motives can produce an enjoyable ordinary occasion.

The difference between choosing a small field and accepting isolation becomes especially visible after she has known companionship. The high-school retrospective does not show solitude remaining equally sufficient once a meaningful shared world has become possible. Her preferences are historical: an activity can remain loved while its solitary form becomes less satisfying. The monograph therefore resists both the extroversion cure and the romantic idealization of untouched solitude. [KF-P03, P14](#kf-p03)

### 10.5 Small affection is a form of agency

The first Christmas gives Kikuchi an appetite for shared memory and future time. She wants a photograph, imagines future gift exchange, treats a makeshift snowman with playful tenderness, and asks for both planned meetings rather than accepting a well-intended reduction. None of this requires a high-stakes conflict to become meaningful. It establishes the kind of ordinary relationship she wants to live. [KF-E10; KF-P14](#kf-p14)

These acts are also evidence against the claim that she cares only about ideal compatibility. The little object is valuable because of who made it and when, not because it proves a perfect match. The future date is wanted because she wants to be with this person. Her response can include embarrassment and apology without becoming indifference. The later loss of time in V08 should therefore be measured against an already expressed appetite for contact, not against an assumption that a quiet girlfriend has few needs.

The source does not establish that every photograph or every public display is welcome. Earlier image-sharing limits remain specific to the occasion, and a privately welcomed image does not imply permission to post it. Similarly, wishing for next Christmas does not prove that the later event occurs. The ordinary scene matters without becoming a promise the admitted boundary has fulfilled.

### 10.6 Domestic warmth and embodied awkwardness

Kikuchi’s home voice includes short replies, mild correction, embarrassment, and affectionate evaluation of her brother. She is neither an ethereal visitor to her own household nor a uniformly formal speaker. Her attention to Riku’s different public and private behavior shows that she already understands people as relation-conditioned before applying elaborate authorial models to Hinami. [KF-E12; KF-P17–P19](#kf-p17)

At the same time, warmth should not be turned into fabricated competence. Clearing a table or helping serve food is not evidence of a comprehensive cooking repertoire. Bringing a bento does not identify who prepared it. Fumiya’s supposition that she seems like someone who goes to bed and rises early is not an established sleep habit. A single winter outfit does not establish a favorite palette or budget. The everyday record is rich enough to make Kikuchi a person without filling every field in a lifestyle questionnaire. [KF-P06, P19–P21](#kf-p06)

### 10.7 Explicit everyday abstentions

The admitted and inspected evidence does not establish a complete diet, an exclusive favorite food, favorite season, comprehensive music taste, routine exercise program, regular sleep hours, household income, shopping budget, political preferences, religion, or an exhaustive friendship list. The parallel karaoke material is not used to repair missing mainline music preferences. The beautiful-book evidence is not a universal ban on digital reading. The fictional flower-ornament craft is not promoted to Kikuchi’s personal hobby.

These limits are part of an adequate reconstruction, not missing decorative detail. For a new low-stakes situation, the strongest inference usually concerns what kind of interaction or meaning would appeal to her, not which exact product, flavor, color, or song she would select without further evidence.

## 11. Central contradictions

<a id="her-support-for-tomozaki-proves-she-is-no-longer-jealous"></a>
### 11.1 Generosity can preserve another person and exclude herself

Kikuchi’s generosity is genuine enough to make an easy manipulation theory inadequate. She encourages Fumiya’s wider relations, respects Mimimi’s feeling, and tries to honor the claims of characters other than the one closest to her own wish. But generosity can become self-exclusion when the supposed proper arrangement gives her no position from which to ask for change. The distinction cannot be made from the outward sacrifice alone. It depends on whether she can name the cost, revise the arrangement, and remain one of the people whose interests count. [KF-E07, E11, E14–E16](#kf-e07)

V09 is powerful because she does not solve the problem by choosing either total sacrifice or total possession. She rejects Fumiya’s attempt to erase his other ties, while claiming her own choosing position. V10 then shows that a costly permission can be real. V11 shows that the same person can later be hurt and object. The continuity is not inconsistency to be corrected by making her always accommodating or always assertive. It is the development of a relationship in which support must remain renewable rather than assumed.

### 11.2 Accurate modeling can coexist with inadequate restraint

Kikuchi’s failures are not all caused by misunderstanding. In the Haruka encounter, the force of the model is precisely part of the risk. Her absorption lets a partly correct structure become articulable faster than she can judge what the articulation will do to the person receiving it. Subsequent guilt is not a substitute for the missing restraint. [KF-E18](#kf-e18)

Yet an account of total loss of control would also be too easy. It could turn a difficult act into an involuntary symptom and remove agency. The source gives self-report of having become absorbed, observable changes of delivery, and later acknowledgment of the cost. It does not establish a clinical compulsion or an inability to act differently in all future inquiries. The appropriate conclusion is a specific failure of judgment and attention under a highly salient creative problem, with a new unresolved need for practical limits.

### 11.3 Affinity can become a cage when treated as nature

The habitat metaphor helps Kikuchi refuse compulsory sociability, but later threatens to tell her what she is allowed to want from a more socially expansive partner. This is not simply proof that metaphors are bad. The same form can clarify a pressure in one state and conceal a new pressure in another. A mature reader must track what decision the metaphor is doing work for. [KF-E02, E06, E14–E15](#kf-e02)

The correction is not a final replacement metaphor that can never fail. It is willingness to return from the category to the actual person. Fumiya’s realization that he is facing Kikuchi rather than a species in a book is an important act of attention. Kikuchi’s choice of the actual Fumiya performs a corresponding move. Neither makes future conflicts impossible. It keeps the analogy from acquiring permanent authority over the relationship.

### 11.4 Confidence can be local, and weakness can coexist with resolve

Kikuchi’s Rena-facing protest is visibly strained. She does not win an effortless contest of composure. That is not evidence that she fails to act. She names a boundary, states her relationship standing, and objects to sexualized speech toward Fumiya. His immediate removal of the unwanted touch is also part of the event; she is not the sole agent who protects him. [KF-E19; KF-P31](#kf-p31)

This matters for reconstruction because a character can become more agentic without adopting a dominant social style. Hesitation, blushing, or being outmatched in ease do not cancel a refusal. Nor does a moment of force prove that she now possesses universal confidence. The strength lies in acting despite the discomfort and in the proposition she makes audible, not in replacing one personality with another.

### 11.5 Self-criticism can lead to repair or become another disappearance

Kikuchi can acknowledge a privacy wrong, regret exposing someone, and wonder whether she communicated badly. These are capacities for accountability. But they can also invite another pattern: making her own inadequacy the explanation for a relational injury caused in part by someone else. Fumiya’s apology after the failed call matters because it does not allow her self-criticism to absorb his dismissiveness. [KF-E14, E18–E21; KF-P22, P30, P32, P34](#kf-p22)

The same distinction governs writing. Asking what she owes living models is serious. Concluding that she should disappear as a writer because she cannot guarantee harmlessness would be another totalization. The source does not establish that conclusion. Her willingness to begin again while retaining the question is evidence of movement, though not a complete solution. Accountability becomes stronger when it changes how she acts rather than becoming a global verdict that she should not act at all.

### 11.6 Her ethical significance is not a reward for being the selected girlfriend

The monograph’s value judgments rest on conduct and consequences, not on romantic outcome. Kikuchi’s respect for Mimimi does not become correct because she “won,” and her privacy breach does not become harmless because the couple repairs. Her artistic contribution does not justify every inference, and her hurt does not give her ownership of Fumiya’s entire social world. Conversely, his independent friendships do not make her need for time trivial. The narrative’s selected relationship is an object of analysis, not a moral certificate. ## 12. Rival readings and counterevidence

<a id="kikuchi-is-naturally-complete-and-needs-no-social-development"></a>
### 12.1 Rival: Kikuchi is the already-complete ethical alternative to Hinami

There is real evidence for the attraction of this reading. Kikuchi provides a quieter field, resists reducing people to tactical functions, and helps Fumiya articulate what acquired skill should serve. Her concern for a whole world appears more generous than a relentless proof regime. She can recognize a person behind an antagonistic classroom role and respect a rival’s continuing feeling without treating it as an offense. An interpretation that denied these strengths would be less accurate than the idealization it criticizes. [KF-E03–E04, E11](#kf-e03)

But ethical completeness is contradicted by the development. Her world-facing ideal can exclude herself; her reading of Fumiya can make loneliness seem illegitimate; she breaches phone privacy; her artistic inquiry hurts Haruka; and she has not resolved what permits the use of Hinami-derived material. Nor is Hinami merely a foil with no genuine care or value. The contrast is between different practices and different failure mechanisms, not a morally complete girl and a morally defective one.

**Disposition:** retain Kikuchi’s independent ethical contribution; reject her use as a finished moral standard. Her function includes making the costs of sympathetic interpretation visible, not only correcting instrumental strategy.

### 12.2 Rival: a fixed introverted temperament explains the entire trajectory

The reading has a strong descriptive basis in preferred quiet, difficulty with large-group formats, and the continuing appeal of books and selected company. It correctly resists an interpretation in which growth must mean extroversion. But it does not explain enough. The lively retelling to Goda, café work, reciprocal conversation with Mimimi, public authorship, and later participation show that recipient and purpose matter. A single trait cannot explain why the same person can be inhibited in rehearsal management and exacting about how a story should end. [KF-E02, E06, E11–E12, E22–E23](#kf-e02)

It also leaves out development in what she thinks difference means. A protective explanation formed after rejection is not simply a label for an unchanged temperament. The lake metaphor becomes enabling in one state and constraining in another. An adequate model can retain a preference for lower-intensity interaction while explaining changes in confidence, chosen roles, and self-concept.

**Disposition:** preserve selective social preference; reject a one-variable or clinical explanation. The corpus does not warrant a diagnosis or a claim that a trait alone causes the later relational and artistic conflicts.

### 12.3 Rival: her growth is dependence on Fumiya disguised as self-authorship

Fumiya is important enough that this objection deserves more than dismissal. He helps create access, challenges her universal-assimilation ideal, reads her work, and supplies a reason for the next project at V11. She herself credits his effect on her. The relationship can make his expanding world and special bond with Hinami disproportionately important to her sense of what she should do. Dependence is therefore not an imaginary concern. [KF-E06–E07, E13–E16, E21](#kf-e06)

Nevertheless, the strong version erases earlier authorship, Goda’s role, family life, her independent judgments, the class’s reception, and the editorial route. It also ignores her refusals. She rejects Fumiya’s vocabulary when it fails to fit, asks to choose rather than simply be chosen, declines a social format, and preserves the search for her own reason at the end. Relational scaffolding does not automatically transfer authorship wholesale to the helper.

**Disposition:** preserve dependence as a variable and possible failure mechanism; reject the claim that her choices are therefore unreal. The relevant future test is whether she can revise or refuse Fumiya-supplied reasons without losing her position as writer and partner, not whether she can become independent of every relationship.

### 12.4 Rival: the repeated conflicts prove the couple is fundamentally incompatible

This reading has serious evidence. Different social ranges, uneven time demands, incomplete disclosure, jealousy, and Hinami’s exceptional importance repeatedly defeat elegant explanations of compatibility. V09’s repair does not prevent the V11 injury. A prediction of easy permanence would be unjustified. The source permits the possibility that the arrangement may remain difficult or fail under later pressures. [KF-E14–E16, E19](#kf-e14)

But difficulty does not invalidate every achieved choice. Kikuchi does not simply discover that she selected the wrong person because a category was mistaken. She chooses the actual Fumiya after confronting some of what makes him hard to contain in an ideal. Their ordinary pleasure, specific repairs, and capacity to revise are real. The admitted boundary does not provide the future evidence needed to decide durable compatibility.

**Disposition:** preserve the relationship’s structural risks; leave its long-term outcome open. Neither literary complementarity nor recurring pain should be used as a deterministic prediction of the ending. R07’s independent relationship study must own the full dyadic judgment.

### 12.5 Rival: Kikuchi uses fiction and modesty to control other people indirectly

The strongest evidence is the public ending’s interpersonal force and the fact that fiction can direct a recipient toward a feeling without asking for it in ordinary language. A highly perceptive person can exercise influence through apparent delicacy. The V11 update affects Fumiya at a moment when direct speech failed, and he interprets the timing as meaningful. It would be naive to deny that authored form can be an instrument of influence. [KF-E07–E08, E20](#kf-e07)

The claim becomes too strong when it treats every indirect act as a calculated strategy for control. The source represents genuine conflict between personal feeling and a world-facing ideal, not merely an efficient way of making Fumiya pursue her. Much of her restraint costs her something she wants. She also asks directly in ordinary scenes, admits wrongs, and qualifies what she can know. Influence, even intentional influence, is not identical to a comprehensive manipulative personality.

**Disposition:** preserve the power and possible ethical defects of indirect communication; reject the total motive theory. Particular acts need separate adjudication. The question is not whether she can influence people—she can—but whether she leaves them room to understand, answer, and refuse the use of that influence.

<a id="because-her-hinami-interpretation-is-accurate-using-it-is-justified"></a>
### 12.6 Rival: concern about exposure wrongly treats art as something requiring universal permission

This is an important objection to an overmoralized analysis. Fiction necessarily imagines minds, transforms experience, and can disturb its readers. No source establishes that every model must approve every invented thought or that the absence of complete consent makes a work artistically illegitimate. Kikuchi’s vocation would become impossible if responsibility meant certainty that no one could be hurt. The novels also show that a disturbing story can create valuable new understanding. [KF-E18, E20, E26](#kf-e18)

Yet the opposite rule—that artistic transformation automatically authorizes any use of intimate information—also outruns the evidence. These are recognizable peers, information moves through particular confidences, and a younger relative is hurt in the process of inquiry. Kikuchi herself makes the relation between her desire and others’ pain the problem. Fumiya’s powerful reception includes an explicit doubt about assigning extreme inner states to a living model.

**Disposition:** reject both a universal prohibition and a universal artistic exemption. The monograph identifies unfinished ethical questions about access, recognizability, timing, purpose, recipients, and onward use. It does not invent a complete legal or aesthetic doctrine and put it in Kikuchi’s mouth.

### 12.7 Rival: her apparent insight is merely the narrative rewarding a favored interpreter

There are reasons for caution. Many assessments come through Fumiya, whose attraction and dependence can inflate the authority of Kikuchi’s speech. The fictional reconstruction is partly built from stories he has supplied, so its agreement with his experience is not independent corroboration. A carefully made character can feel psychologically true without proving the real model’s interior. [KF-E03, E20, E24](#kf-e03)

But dismissing the capacity entirely would ignore observable work: the revisions, questions, changed interpretation after new information, class reception, and conditional editorial interest. Her lost-god model also receives partial support from later family evidence, though not every detail is established. The responsible interpretation does not have to choose between magic and illusion. It can describe a substantial, fallible skill whose evidence and limits are both visible.

**Disposition:** preserve the capacity at a bounded level. Do not infer that every successful model independently proves all others, and do not let the word “insight” replace the actual information route.

### 12.8 Rival: the V11 ending resolves her writer identity and turns the group into a safe alternative

The ending supplies genuine advances: her work reaches Fumiya, he recognizes her contribution, they repair the immediate conflict, and she proposes including other perspectives. These are not empty promises with no preceding evidence. They grow from demonstrated literary and collaborative capacities. [KF-E20–E21](#kf-e20)

Nevertheless, she explicitly retains the search for her own reason. The wider project has not been executed, and Hinami has not agreed to it. More contributors can distribute labor and correct blind spots while also increasing the reach of private information. A future work’s expected benefit cannot be counted as an observed result, and a motive borrowed as a starting point cannot be declared finally self-authored merely because it is acknowledged.

**Disposition:** preserve renewed agency and the less monopolistic proposal; reject completion. The unresolved endpoint is part of Kikuchi’s mature account, not a deficiency to be repaired by predicting what later volumes must do.

## 13. Current endpoint and open hypotheses

### Established at high confidence

At the V11 boundary, Kikuchi has a substantial life and capability set that should not be reduced to the crisis. She reads, writes, revises, works, participates in family life, maintains selected relationships, and can contribute in public. She is Fumiya’s chosen and choosing partner, not merely a quiet possibility. She is an aspiring writer with external interest, not an already published professional. Her confidence and agency have expanded, but they remain local and vulnerable to the authority of an ideal whole. [KF-E01–E23](#kf-e01)

The strongest current account distinguishes **liking writing**, **being able to write**, **wanting to become a writer**, **having an opportunity to publish**, and **having a sufficient reason and practice for using particular people’s lives as material**. The first four have different degrees of support. The last is not resolved. Her final willingness to proceed does not erase that distinction.

### High-medium hypotheses

Three hypotheses remain useful but defeasible. First, her most promising development joins selected habitat with public authorship rather than treating one as the enemy of the other. Second, she is more likely to sustain care when her own interest is explicitly included than when she must be the invisible guarantor of another person’s proper life. Third, her interpretive practice becomes more accountable when the model remains revisable by its subject and the recipient’s state can interrupt the inquiry. These are analytical expectations, not source-certified prescriptions or guarantees of recovery.

### Open

The principal open questions are her own continuing reason to write; completion and publication of the work; an acceptable practice for fictionalizing recognizable people; durable couple arrangements for time, disclosure, jealousy, and third-party contact; the actual membership, method, and effects of the proposed group project; and Hinami’s participation or refusal. The corpus also does not establish a final balance among artistic work, romantic life, family, and a profession. These are future-state questions, not blank spaces to fill from remembered canon.

The person reconstructed here is therefore neither a completed angel nor a secret manipulator. She is an imaginative, conscientious, sometimes playful and sometimes overabsorbed adolescent who wants meaningful contact and the right to make something of it. The difficulty is that contact gives her both a place to live and material she might use. Her development must preserve the first without assuming unlimited authority over the second.
