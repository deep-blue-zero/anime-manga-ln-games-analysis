# Hinami Nagisa — play, moral action and an answer that remains unavailable

This account is accepted at its stated bounded literary scope through the admitted V12 boundary. See the [implementation review](../../08%20Audits%20and%20Manifests/TOMOZAKI_SUPPORTING_CHARACTER_IMPLEMENTATION_REVIEW.md) and [source and review manifest](../../08%20Audits%20and%20Manifests/TOMOZAKI_SUPPORTING_LITERARY_EVIDENCE_AND_REVIEW_MANIFEST.json) for independent acceptance, source returns, corrections and remaining limits. A bounded literary role is separate from a derived reconstruction model or aggregate release.

Nagisa is represented first as a child who wants another game, changes her tactic, wins, thanks the sister cheering her and throws herself toward the sister she has defeated. The later family history represents a girl who sees another child's persecution, chooses to intervene, bears retaliation, continues to help and eventually questions the words through which she had understood doing right. That sequence is a substantial individual account. Its final uncertainty is equally substantial: the novels do not authenticate what she intended when she entered the road or which alternative intervention would have prevented her death.

This proposed **B posthumous dossier** owns those represented acts, attributed statements, relationships, changes and limits. It is not an account of every part of her life, a clinical case or a voice authorized to supply her missing final wishes. A bounded account can preserve both moral agency and the inability to recover all of it. Later characters' need for an answer does not constitute the answer.

## 1. Who speaks, and when the reader learns it

The chronology has three separate clocks. Childhood games and the later persecution belong to Nagisa's life. Their disclosure arrives retrospectively through the V08.5 family unit, Aoi's chosen V10 speech and V11's disclosure-framed history. V12 gives a new account of what Aoi thinks and how a fictional story alters her possibilities; it does not introduce a newly living Nagisa witness.

**HISTORICAL SCENE** below means speech/action represented in retrospective narrative. **REPORT** identifies a surviving character's account, including nested reports. **INTERIOR-AOI** identifies the survivor's direct reader-facing thought. **FICTION/ADDRESS** identifies the authored or imagined encounter. **INFERENCE** is a bounded interpretation. **OPEN** is an unresolved fact or conflict. None of these labels dismisses the literary force of the representation; they keep its actual route visible.

V11 begins with Aoi agreeing to tell Fumiya, then changes into third-person Aoi-centered historical presentation and, at p0030 P1092, first-person inward narration. The reader therefore receives more than a demonstrably verbatim heard transcript. Nagisa's represented dialogue in that retrospective is not a surviving audio record or her direct present testimony. The narrative nevertheless differentiates her own expressed questions and choices from Aoi's silent self-accusation. Preserving that distinction protects the girl from being absorbed into the survivor's later causal model. [HN-E03]

## 2. Playing to win and playing together

The childhood match contains ordinary agency that later tragedy cannot cancel. Nagisa initially loses to Aoi and is disappointed. She immediately wants another attempt and insists that the result need not repeat. In the next game she draws the familiar response and sends a second bomb before the returning shot arrives, neutralizing it and hitting Aoi. This is a represented tactical change, not simply a moral lesson about perseverance. Aoi's greater general skill does not make Nagisa incapable of noticing, adapting or winning a particular encounter. [HN-E01]

Her victory is social. She celebrates, thanks Haruka for cheering, laughs and approaches Aoi bodily. Aoi catches her and accepts affection despite losing. Nagisa's direct declaration that it is fun includes the shared occasion, rather than proving that she only cares about the score. There is no need to choose between a competitive child and a loving sister. The same scene supplies both.

Buin's phrase belongs first to this playful world: repeated character speech, sibling teasing, sound, victory and enjoyment. Later V11 says the sisters had enjoyed the sound before fully understanding it. This history prevents treating the line as a doctrine that Nagisa simply received once and obeyed mechanically. Familiar words acquire a more serious use through lived choice; that later use does not retroactively abolish their joke, rhythm and affection. [HN-E01, HN-E04]

No comprehensive favorite-character or favorite-game ranking follows. The source supports attachment to the shared character and repeated family play, not every leisure interest, an adult gaming identity or a standing habit at every stage. Nagisa's ordinary personhood is carried by the specific actions that are shown, rather than invented preferences supplied to round out a profile.

## 3. Choosing to help another child

At the family meal Nagisa reports that a classmate is being ignored and subjected to hurtful words, says she finds this intolerable, asks how she can help and proposes cautioning the others. Her first relation to the problem is not solely proving that she is good. The particular other child and what is being done to that child are present in her speech. Asking for advice also complicates a wholly self-contained righteousness model: she has an intended action and seeks a response from her mother. [HN-E03]

Yoko praises her wish and says she is on her side, while also suggesting that watching without acting may be an option and that she does not want Nagisa overexerting herself. The family narrative presents danger and uncertainty in trying to intervene, rather than a simple guarantee that the right act secures the right outcome. Nagisa chooses to defend the targeted child and later reports that she herself has become a target. Her decision therefore cannot be reduced to obediently doing exactly what her mother recommended.

The retrospective attributes courage and strength partly to the affection and affirmation in which she grew. That is a narrated formation account, not an exhaustive causal proof. Her mother can have supplied confidence while also recommending a consoling acceptance of events. Nagisa can use a game character's words as a personal reason without being only an echo of either mother or game. The important action remains hers: she intervenes and continues to describe what she has done for another person. [HN-E04]

Her reports become specific: she says that she cautioned the perpetrators again and listened to the victim's concerns. This gives her more than the single role of an admired pure resistor. Listening to the harmed child and confronting those causing harm are distinct recipient-directed acts. Their efficacy is not comprehensively established. The other child's full response, the whole class structure and all institutional/adult actions are not independently supplied by this account.

## 4. Conviction, performance and growing doubt

After retaliation begins, Nagisa smiles and insists that she is all right because she can think herself correct and fight correctly. The game phrase supplies a vocabulary in which maintaining one's own right conduct can itself count as completing the game. This is her expressed reason in the historical representation. It does not establish the narrative's universal moral law or a guaranteed protection from harm. [HN-E04]

As persecution escalates, the scene distinguishes her composed encouragement of worried family members from the exhaustion and urgency visible through it. She can continue to act and believe while becoming less able to bear the situation. Describing this change requires no diagnosis. It also does not justify calling all her smiles fraudulent: their relation to conviction, care for relatives, endurance and performance is mixed rather than fully resolved.

The later question to Aoi is a consequential change in her own account. Nagisa asks whether Buin could still say that correct living alone completes the game while looking at her present condition. She goes on to wonder whether doing right had any meaning. This is not a minor footnote to an unchanged emblem of righteousness. A reason she used to act no longer seems able to encompass the result she is living. [HN-E05]

Two errors follow if this doubt is flattened. One would preserve a perfectly unwavering moral icon by treating the question as merely another way of affirming the same certainty. The other would convert expressed doubt into proof that she had renounced helping, ceased loving the other child or selected death. The text establishes the question and distress, not those unshown decisions. A bounded account can allow her beliefs to change without inventing the last state of her mind.

Aoi's response is also partly withheld. The reader receives the reassuring words she wants to give and the doubts that prevent her speaking them. Her later sense that she failed is direct evidence about the survivor. It does not establish that one missing sentence would have saved Nagisa. The account should retain Nagisa's question to her older sister as a request addressed to a person, while refusing to turn that person's inability to answer into an adjudicated cause of death.

## 5. Death: a reported event without a recovered intention

V10 Aoi tells Fumiya that Nagisa died in a traffic collision. She reports the driver's remorse and his description of a sudden, apparently unsteady entry into a road without a signal or crossing. The driver's words reach the reader through Aoi's report to Fumiya. They are neither direct Nagisa focalization nor an independently depicted final scene. The remorse and reported willingness to compensate do not select a mechanism or replace missing evidence. [HN-E06]

Aoi explicitly considers incompatible possibilities: fatigue or dizziness and an unlucky collision, or a deliberate act after everything became unbearable. She cannot know whether to regret not urging sleep, not discouraging intervention against bullying, or not offering a different kind of support. The branching matters. A universal “she should have helped more” makes causal responsibility falsely simple when the source precisely denies a known counterfactual. [HN-E06]

V11 depicts the hospital notification, grief and confrontation over meaning, rather than filling in Nagisa's last road-entry consciousness. V12 p0022 P504–513 states the uncertainty again after more than five years. The later literary response has not supplied an accident finding, a suicide finding, a physical explanation or a way to test what would have prevented the death. These are explicit continuing unknowns, rather than incompleteness to hide with psychological inference. [HN-E07, HN-E08]

The school-year conflict also remains. V10 `text/part0024.html` P139 calls Nagisa a sixth grader when the bullying history begins. V11 p0030 P826–827 places Aoi in sixth grade and identifies Nagisa as two years younger; the historical meal then calls her a fourth grader at P842. P1010 describes the surviving Aoi and Haruka as sixth and third graders. The dossier preserves these locators without calculating a harmonized birthday, inventing an edition correction or choosing which witness to silently discard. The conflict limits exact age/date reconstruction; it does not eliminate the represented moral action and relationships.

## 6. Living relationships and posthumous representations

| Recipient / field | Represented Nagisa relation | What survives as a limit |
|---|---|---|
| Aoi in play | competes, changes tactics, celebrates, seeks contact and shares enjoyment | no fixed skill hierarchy in every match; affection coexists with rivalry |
| Haruka in play | receives cheering and thanks her by name | Haruka's later whole inner life and Nagisa's later obligations are not supplied |
| Targeted classmate | objects to harm, intervenes and reports listening to the child's concerns | complete benefit, class dynamics and institutional response unestablished |
| Perpetrating classmates | reports cautioning them despite retaliation | correctness does not guarantee control or establish every incident |
| Yoko | seeks advice, hears support/caution and receives repeated encouragement | maternal speech is not Nagisa's whole motive; no complete agreement with its meaning framework |
| Aoi in distress | asks whether inherited words can still address her actual condition | a real question; not a proven final intention or assigned causal responsibility |
| Later readers/authored figures | remembered, compared, modeled and addressed | those recipients' interpretations and needs cannot speak as the deceased person's authenticated new voice |

The V10 comparison to Tama and Fumiya is Aoi's. It can illuminate a resemblance in principled action without making the three interchangeable or proving that later care for Tama has one exclusive hidden cause. The avoided implication is particularly serious for Nagisa: describing another person as a replacement can erase differences in circumstance, choice, survival and the later person's own independent reasons.

## 7. The V12 reunion belongs to an address, not a testimony archive

Kikuchi names a central authorial limit: Nagisa is no longer available to observe, hear or ask. Invention necessarily imports what the writer wants. The novel's later force does not repeal that limit. [HN-E09]

Within Aoi's private reading, the story permits an imagined encounter and a thirty-second opportunity to ask or speak. Semantic ruby crosses Alcia/I, the remembered 渚 and fictional ナギサ, and later gives 魔法 the reading ものがたり. The base and reading layers make the relation intimate and unstable; they do not certify a new external witness. The accompanying original visual receipt locates the child-register Nagisa image within this imagined/remembered sequence. This dossier inherits that inspected role and asserts no newly viewed pixels. [HN-E10]

Aoi anticipates a question about the final step but finds words of apology, admiration, love and thanks. Her gratitude for Nagisa remaining herself to the end is an affirmative address made in that encounter, not independent proof that the real girl's last consciousness maintained one unchanged ideal. The imagined sister's birth-thanks and game phrase can move the surviving reader without becoming recovered last words. A statement can transform a present relation to loss while leaving the historical question unanswered.

This distinction preserves more than epistemic caution. Granting the imagined girl final authority over all actual events would remove the real Nagisa's earlier doubt and the ongoing absence of her answer. The historical child who questions a beloved phrase must remain alongside the fictional sister who speaks it affirmatively. Neither cancels the other; they occupy different acts, times and sources of voice.

## 8. Rival explanations and bounded scenarios

| Rival account | Evidence that limits it |
|---|---|
| Nagisa is only an origin for Aoi's later behavior | Tactical play, thanks, pleasure, help to a peer, advice-seeking and doubt give her acts and recipients of her own. |
| Pure unwavering righteousness is her complete self | She asks for advice, performs reassurance under exhaustion and later questions the phrase's adequacy. |
| Doubt proves suicide or abandonment of moral action | Final intention remains explicitly unknown in V10 and V12; the V11 hospital scene supplies no missing final viewpoint. |
| Maternal encouragement alone caused every choice and the death | Nagisa acts despite maternal caution; victimization and incomplete institutional context remain; no tested counterfactual establishes singular cause. |
| Aoi's silence proves preventability and guilt | Survivor self-accusation is direct inward evidence; several incompatible prevention hypotheses remain. |
| Fiction gives the authentic last answer | The authorial limit, private reading, semantic ruby and continuing explicit uncertainty keep modeled voice separate from testimony. |
| Later love makes Nagisa's doubts less real | Her expressed historical question remains consequential even when a present reader finds new grounds for affirmation. |

Permissible reconstruction is era- and domain-bound. In the childhood play state, persistence and tactical adjustment are demonstrated; exact invented taunts or an invariant refusal to lose are not. In the early intervention state, she articulates concern for another child and seeks advice; do not presume that every later risk will be accepted without deliberation. In the distressed state, she can ask whether a trusted principle addresses her condition; do not force her to answer as an immutable symbol. A scenario asking what she intended in the road or what she would endorse after death requires abstention. These are literary constraints, not a clinically or experimentally validated decision model.

**Do not infer:** diagnosis; suicide or accident certainty; final words or afterlife contact; guaranteed preventability; sole responsible family member; a harmonized grade/date; exhaustive school/adult response; every preference; a full personality from a remembered phrase; or a right to use her as moral proof for the surviving characters' projects.
