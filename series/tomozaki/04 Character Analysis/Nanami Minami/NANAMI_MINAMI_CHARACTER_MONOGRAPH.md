# Nanami Minami / Mimimi — character monograph through Volume 11

**Making life lively without having to earn the right to remain in it**

> **Authority and scope.** This independently reviewed expansion is mutable current literary authority for Mimimi’s individual R05 responsibility under the [integration review](../../08%20Audits%20and%20Manifests/NANAMI_MINAMI_R05_INTEGRATION_REVIEW.md). Together with Kikuchi’s accepted study it completes the R05 pair. Aggregate specialist completion, R10 convergence and whole-corpus release remain unpassed. V11 is an admitted boundary, not the ending of the series.

## 1. Responsibility and evidence boundary

Nanami Minami, usually called Mimimi and called *Minmi* by Tama, is an ambitious athlete, an energetic schoolmate, a practiced maker of jokes and social openings, a daughter in a two-person household, a close friend of Natsubayashi Hanabi/Tama and Hinami Aoi, and a girl who falls in love with Tomozaki Fumiya without becoming his chosen romantic partner. By V11 she also takes initiative in a stalled student-council project and makes a consequential first appearance as an improvised game commentator. None of these identities exhausts her. The girl who loses an election is also the girl who likes a noisy karaoke gathering; the girl who fears having nothing inside her is also someone with particular tastes, impatiences, memories, ideas, and pleasures. This study reconstructs that person rather than treating her as either a lesson about second place or the emotional cost of someone else’s romance. [NM-E01–E05, E20, E23–E27](#nm-e01)

The evidence boundary is the locked Japanese V01–V11 corpus with V06.5 and V08.5 admitted only under their existing story-local routes. Supplemental publication order is not a second chronology. In V06.5, the girls’ conversation belongs to the V03 camp; the running story belongs after the Tama crisis and before the V06 declaration; the restaurant scene re-presents an encounter already narrated in V06; and only the final immediate aftermath advances beyond that volume’s ending. V08.5’s conversation between Mimimi and Kikuchi belongs to the early dating interval between V07 and V08, not to a recovery after the V08 crisis. The appended two-viewpoint Tama/Mimimi story in V05 remains an explicitly routed bonus, not an automatically post-V05 event. [NM-E06, E08, E10–E11, E15](#nm-e06)

No later volume, adaptation, translation, external character profile, remembered canon, or parallel karaoke episode supplies evidence here. The V08.5 *if* story cannot establish Mimimi’s main-continuity song choices. The V07 artbook cannot establish an unshown personal history. Prose descriptions of delivery and movement are literary evidence, not an independently heard performance. This operation introduces no image-dependent finding and uses no OCR. Its exact-source returns are selective, not a claim to have repeated the sequential project.

The principal access distinction is between **what Mimimi does, what she says, what her private narration thinks, and what someone else makes of her**. Fumiya can see an energetic girl and infer effort behind the smile. Tama can describe the care she receives. A former team manager can remember Mimimi’s middle-school frustration. A private Mimimi chapter can disclose the disappointment she conceals from all of them. These are different warrants. Her interior account is particularly valuable, but it is not infallible: the claim that she is merely an empty, decorated frame conflicts with the same chapter’s record of desires, decisions, attachments, and judgments. [NM-E02, E07–E09, E22](#nm-e02)

Interpretive claims in this monograph are therefore explicitly bounded. A recurring pattern can support a personality description without becoming an invariant. A sincere refusal can be local without being meaningless. An effective intervention can be generous without being costless. Conversely, an unarticulated lifelong rule is not proof that she never stops, never chooses, or never cares for herself. Her refusal of a rematch, her earlier refusal to join the council in another role, and her later alteration of a particular walk are important counterexamples to such totalization. [NM-E09, E17, E24](#nm-e09)

**NM-E** records in §14 join the analysis to existing cumulative identities and exact reading sections. **NM-P** records identify the Japanese windows inspected for this delivery. These are local retrieval handles, not new frozen-volume claim IDs or replacements for CRI, CS, REL, EFFORT, JVL, or LOC owners. Section 15 contains hypothetical situations designed to expose the model’s conditions and limits. They are not new story events, blind predictions, or a separately released reconstruction model.

## 2. Reconstructive thesis

Mimimi is not a cheerful facade hiding one true defeated self, and she is not a rival cured by learning to accept second place. She is a highly capable, socially inventive, affectionate, and competitive person who has difficulty making demonstrated qualities count as sufficient grounds for liking herself. Under pressure, the question “What do I want to do?” becomes entangled with another: “What would establish that I am not replaceable or empty?” First place is one proposed answer. Being someone’s uniquely effective helper can become another. Neither captures all her motives, but both can make a wanted activity carry more existential weight than it can bear. [NM-E02–E05, E07, E22](#nm-e02)

The central account is this: **Mimimi’s development consists in widening the reasons for which she may strive, belong, receive care, and stop—without requiring victory, indispensable usefulness, or the disappearance of inconvenient feeling as the price of remaining herself.** Her growth does not mean becoming less lively. It means that the lively person must also be allowed to have interests and limits. The V02 crisis makes the problem visible through competitive comparison. The post-retirement running story makes it visible through changes in a valued friendship and a mother’s criticism of concealed cost. The romance makes it visible through the difference between continuing love and a claim on another person. V11 gives her new capacities and sharper language without completing that adjustment.

This thesis is a contestable synthesis, not Mimimi’s stated doctrine. It would be weakened by evidence that her comparison concerns have no effect outside sporting results, that the sense of being needed is irrelevant to her self-appraisal, or that her help consistently functions as a calculated demand for repayment. It would also be weakened by evidence that she invariably sacrifices her own interests and cannot enjoy an activity unless it proves something. The admitted source contradicts that last overextension. She likes loud fun, accepts particular pleasures, can refuse a second race, and sometimes claims a joking meal repayment rather than pretending she has no wants. An adequate account must explain that range, not dissolve it into hidden suffering. [NM-E06–E09, E12–E13, E26](#nm-e06)

### 2.1 Who she is when no crisis is in progress

In ordinary interaction Mimimi is quick to supply a topic, exaggerate a role, invent an interview, attach a nickname, or turn a minor awkwardness into a shared joke. She is comfortable drawing attention and often enjoys the attention itself. Her expressiveness includes voice, posture, movement, proximity, and touch. She likes conversation that can bounce back at her. Tama’s sharp retorts and Fumiya’s earnest, sometimes peculiar answers provide different kinds of return. She is not simply a broadcaster supplying energy to passive recipients: much of her pleasure lies in provoking and receiving a particular response. [NM-E01, E06, E10, E12](#nm-e01)

She is also more observant and more intellectually active than an “airheaded extrovert” summary permits. She notices changes in friends’ capacities, models why an admirer can be shaken by a model’s unhappiness, identifies Fumiya’s withdrawal as a damaging response to relationship conflict, and distinguishes confidence in her abilities from confidence in herself. Yet she does not always listen well. She can become distracted while Tama talks, assume she can predict Fumiya’s embarrassment, or test Kikuchi indirectly before recognizing the unfairness of doing so. Social fluency gives her a large repertoire; it does not remove mistakes or make every use of the repertoire considerate. [NM-E06–E07, E10, E15, E18, E22–E23](#nm-e06)

Her ambition is equally real. She dislikes losing, takes practice seriously, and wants more than some of her teammates want. The claim that she wants to win only because Hinami exists is too narrow: in V02 she says she wanted first place even before Hinami became the immediate reference. Hinami’s importance is that she validates the intensity while becoming its most difficult measure. The desire to excel precedes the particular comparison; the comparison changes how defeat is interpreted. [NM-E02; NM-P01–P02](#nm-p01)

### 2.2 The distinction between qualities and the person who has them

Mimimi can acknowledge that she is good at things. Her V11 account does not deny her appearance, athleticism, academic ability, or ease of conversation. She explicitly distinguishes those *specifications* from “me” as a whole. Earlier private narration makes a similar distinction: the things people praise can feel like decoration applied to a hollow frame. This is not evidence that the skills are fake or that the interior really contains nothing. It is evidence of a difficulty incorporating achievements and social responses into an acceptable self-image. [NM-E07, E22; NM-P10, P23](#nm-p10)

That difficulty matters because ordinary praise can miss its object. Telling her she is impressive may confirm precisely the layer she already believes is impressive without answering whether she is worth choosing when she is not useful, first, entertaining, or improving. Tama’s response in V02 is different because it names a history of being helped by this person. It does not simply promote Mimimi to a higher universal tier. Even then, the later fear that Tama no longer needs her shows how easily a particular affirmation can be reinterpreted as a role she must keep performing. The response has real value; its reception is not permanently secured. [NM-E04, E07](#nm-e04)

The distinction also prevents a false parallel with Hinami. Mimimi can use her own fluctuating self-worth to formulate a useful hypothesis about Hinami, but in V11 she acknowledges that the analogy stops short: she thinks the birthday and support would have reached her more decisively. Similar language about emptiness does not establish identical histories, motives, or remedies. Her capacity to state that limit is part of her intelligence. [NM-E22; NM-P23](#nm-p23)

### 2.3 Liveliness is a preference, a skill, and sometimes a defense

Mimimi’s brightness has at least three different statuses. It is something she enjoys: the noisy life, the comic rhythm, the pleasure of friends responding. It is something she can do for a group: make entry easier, redirect attention, give a partner a recoverable mistake. And it can be something she uses to prevent a difficult subject from entering the conversation. The same topic-generating fluency that makes companionship enjoyable can become an uninterrupted stream that keeps Tama from asking about her resignation from track. These are not three different personalities. They are different uses of one learned and inhabited repertoire. [NM-E01, E03–E04, E12, E15](#nm-e01)

The distinction should not be settled merely by how effortless a joke sounds. The day after the declaration, the comic exchange about lunch is smooth in outline while both participants remain visibly awkward. It is partly a temporary repair, and that temporary repair genuinely helps. The failure to settle the romantic question does not make the shared relief unreal. Likewise, a bright farewell after non-selection can convey a real wish for continued friendship while costing her effort. Surface fluency and interior ease must remain separate variables. [NM-E11, E14; NM-P16–P17](#nm-p16)

### 2.4 She changes without becoming a finished ethical ideal

Mimimi increasingly distinguishes what she feels from what she may ask another person to do. She can admit envy without deciding that Hinami should stop striving. She can continue loving Fumiya without demanding romantic compensation. She can admire Kikuchi and still feel hurt that Kikuchi understands Fumiya’s strength too. She can limit an ordinary walk and later still approach a distressed friend. These developments are not the elimination of conflict; they are more discriminating ways of acting within it. [NM-E03–E04, E14–E18, E23](#nm-e03)

But reflection does not operate in advance of every action. Physical familiarity sometimes precedes recognition of its cost. Joking can become prying. Concealed kindness may spare someone embarrassment while leaving them unable to recognize or share its burden. These failures belong in the same account as her generosity. A monograph that makes her permanently wise after V02, or permanently selfless after V07, would be unable to explain the ordinary corrections through which her relationships actually proceed.

### 3.1 Before the main relationship: ambition she was already trying to conceal

The middle-school history in V02 arrives through two different routes. Yamashita, a former manager who admires Mimimi, describes an athlete whose effort and dissatisfaction exceeded the team’s prevailing expectations. That admiration is part of the testimony’s position: it makes her a valuable observer, not an omniscient one. Mimimi’s own later recollection adds the experience of watching Hinami’s team and being struck by the freedom Hinami seemed to have to be disappointed. Neither source should be made a full, neutral biography. Together they establish a specific pressure: Mimimi wanted to take competition more seriously than the surrounding group could comfortably receive. [NM-E02; NM-P01–P02](#nm-p01)

The problem was not simply that everyone rejected her. The account includes achievement, teammates, and a team that could celebrate a result. What hurt was the discrepancy between that celebration and her own unexhausted desire. Expressing dissatisfaction could make her seem difficult or excessive. She learned to manage the response by making less of what she wanted. This is an early version of a pattern later recognizable in other domains: understanding how to keep a group comfortable can require hiding the fact that its comfort does not include her.

Hinami matters partly because she interrupts that arrangement. Mimimi remembers seeing Hinami cry when another team was announced as the national winner. The timing is important. Hinami’s disappointment is not a theatrical response to being awarded her own place; it is the response of someone for whom not winning still matters. Mimimi sees in a defeated competitor a form of “specialness” she cannot yet secure for herself: someone who can remain answerable to her desire rather than suppress it because others regard the result as sufficient. Later, when Hinami recognizes the work Mimimi has done, she becomes both rival and validating witness.

There is a productive contradiction here. Mimimi increasingly treats winning as what would prove she is special, yet one of the scenes in which Hinami first appears special is a loss. What she admires is not only a score but a relation to one’s own aim. This does not mean her wish to win is insincere. It means the result has become a proposed route to something more difficult to name: the right to want seriously and to believe that the person wanting is substantial. [NM-P02](#nm-p02)

### 3.2 V01: the cheerful encounter is already character evidence

Mimimi enters Fumiya’s world as someone who makes an unusual response usable rather than merely punishing it. His abstract, unpolished contribution can become material for interaction with her and Tama. That matters before any later disclosure of insecurity. The series does not introduce a socially empty person whose only hidden importance is a crisis to come. It introduces a girl with comic timing, appetite for contact, a close existing friendship, and the capacity to make someone else’s awkwardness less excluding. [NM-E01]

The relationship with Tama also begins with more than a helper/dependent division. Mimimi supplies movement and translation, but Tama’s constancy and direct response are part of what Mimimi values. Their difference creates a rhythm. Later evidence clarifies the reciprocity; it does not retrospectively make every early playful moment a therapeutic exchange. Some of the relationship is simply enjoyable company. That ordinary value is essential to understanding why the prospect of changing roles will eventually hurt.

### 3.3 V02: the election makes one strength visible without making it sufficient

The election demonstrates Mimimi’s public competence in a field where Hinami has much greater accumulated credibility. Fumiya’s strategy does not ask her to become a less accomplished imitation of Hinami. The staged interruption creates room for attention management, improvisation, and live comic response. Mimimi can use that opening. The effect belongs neither to a solitary strategist nor to a naturally magical performer: preparation, the intervention in the audience’s expectations, and her delivery work together. [NM-E02; DR02 §§8–9]

The 456–131 defeat then separates a successful local maneuver from victory in the whole contest. Mimimi has shown that she can command attention; she has not acquired the school-wide trust and preparation behind Hinami’s candidacy. The monograph should not treat this as proof that she lacks political or institutional capacity. V11 will show useful council initiative under very different conditions. Nor should it treat audience response as a consolation prize sufficient to answer what this particular loss means to her.

For Fumiya, defeat supplies analyzable information. For Mimimi, it joins a wider sequence in which being second threatens to classify the person rather than merely the performance. This divergence is the beginning of the failure in his advice. They can share seriousness and still need different answers about whether more effort is the right next action.

### 3.4 V02: persistence becomes a way to avoid a worse self-verdict

When Fumiya asks whether she is pushing herself too hard, Mimimi does not simply deny that anything is wrong. She hesitates over what counts as overdoing it and says that she has thought about the decision. She then explains that stopping now would feel worse than continuing, even though continuing already hurts. The statement preserves agency and suffering together. She is not an unthinking victim of an external instruction; she is making a choice inside an evaluative structure that has made the available exits seem intolerable. [NM-E03; NM-P03](#nm-p03)

Fumiya responds through a gamer’s respect for serious effort. He wants to avoid patronizing someone who has chosen to continue. That respect is not worthless, but it is incomplete. Further fatigue, little sleep, and the expansion of competition into study show that the question is no longer merely whether perseverance can improve performance. It is whether any incremental improvement can satisfy the criterion governing the effort. If Hinami remains the moving reference, improvement can be absorbed as evidence that Mimimi is still not enough.

The rain scene in the frozen reading sharpens the limit. Hinami’s continued preparation does not establish that everyone ought to train that way. It makes visible the instability of making another autonomous person’s output the condition of one’s own acceptable self. Mimimi cannot decide when Hinami will stop. Even surpassing one earlier result would not control the next comparison. [DR02 §§13–15; NM-E03]

There is also a relationship-specific difficulty in receiving advice. Hinami can recognize depletion and want Mimimi to rest, but advice from the person she wants to beat may sound like another reminder of the asymmetry. Fumiya initially has more usable standing as a fellow campaign participant, yet his comparable seriousness leads him to the wrong simplification. The correct recipient is not simply the most skilled or best informed person. What can be heard depends on the relationship in which it is said.

### 3.5 V02: the feeling she cannot morally permit herself to have

Mimimi’s resignation from track is not adequately explained by losing interest in sport. She says she cannot bear what repeated comparison is making her feel toward Hinami. She loves the friend who recognized her work, yet catches herself wanting that friend to stop training, to read the atmosphere, even to be absent from the competitive field. The frightening recognition is that she is beginning to want the same reduction of another person that she once experienced as a wound. [NM-E03–E04; NM-P04](#nm-p04)

This adds moral self-condemnation to competitive disappointment. Mimimi does not merely envy someone who seems undeserving. She thinks Hinami has worked harder and therefore doubts that she even has a right to feel envy. The analysis must not adopt that rule as its own. An unwanted emotion does not become a chosen injury merely by occurring. But it is her rule in this scene, and it helps explain why reassurance about her talent does not settle the problem. What needs acknowledgment is the coexistence of love, gratitude, rivalry, resentment, and shame.

Her cheerful management of the resignation also has a concrete function. She can answer people brightly while losing the ordinary playfulness that made the brightness easy. On the walk, she fills the available space with comic and entertainment topics so effectively that the difficult subject has no natural opening. Tama cuts through the stream. This is an example of social competence used defensively, not evidence that social competence itself is fraudulent. [NM-P04]

Mimimi also participates in the eventual encounter. Fumiya makes the conspicuous invitation and Tama steps forward, but Mimimi agrees to leave with them and makes that change legible to the surrounding peers. She is not simply delivered to the correct rescuer. The response she is able to give matters to what follows.

### 3.6 Tama’s particular recognition—and its later vulnerability

Tama’s answer names what Mimimi has done for her. The person who smiles, works, hides strain, and helps is not interchangeable with the person who wins another race. Tama’s insistence that Mimimi is *her* hero does not deny Hinami’s excellence. It denies that Hinami’s excellence can displace this relationship’s history. The comic “number one” formulation then makes the alternative receivable through a familiar style rather than an abstract lecture. [NM-E04; NM-P05](#nm-p05)

The immediate change is real: Mimimi can return to the relational play from which self-condemnation was excluding her. The later return to track, apology, and restored participation are therefore genuine changes, not mere suppression of the crisis. Yet the frozen reading already refuses to call envy abolished. Subsequent cheerful scenes cannot be used as equivalent retests of the same pressure.

There is also a subtle limit inside the recognition. Tama praises care that includes working and concealing strain. As a statement of gratitude, this is not a command to keep suffering invisibly. But the later running story shows Mimimi wondering whether she can remain first in Tama’s eyes now that Tama needs less social help. A received affirmation can be narrowed into a service requirement even when its speaker did not intend one. This is why relational value should not be described as a new guaranteed metric that permanently solves the old one. It is a different kind of relation to value, one that still has to be lived and interpreted. [NM-E07; NM-P07](#nm-p07)

### 3.7 V03–V05: restored life and the work of supporting Tama

V03 gives Mimimi ordinary participation and gratitude toward Fumiya without establishing a romantic feeling already complete and hidden. The private camp conversation later supplied by V06.5 is explicit: she has no particular romantic preference among the named boys, respects Fumiya, and cannot really picture the proposed pairing. The narration marks the later reversal as something not yet present in her expectation. Respect, compatible humor, and gratitude are developmental conditions, not retrospective proof that she was already in love. [NM-E05, E10; NM-P13](#nm-p13)

The Tama crisis then tests her brightness under another person’s suffering. Mimimi stays close, tries to keep interaction possible, and contributes to the distributed support that helps Tama develop a more effective public interface. She is not the sole teacher, sole planner, or sole moral authority. Her recipient knowledge and expressive example are particular contributions inside a wider effort. [DR04 §12.3; DR05 §§4–6, 11.3; NM-E05]

The reciprocity matters. Tama’s wish to change is partly a response to Mimimi’s pain. That does not mean Mimimi deliberately coerces her, nor that Tama’s choice occurs under fair circumstances: harassment constrains the field. It means each person’s suffering alters the other’s reasons. The destroyed keepsake is painful because the object carries an actual shared history, not because an abstract friendship symbol has been assigned magic power. The later repair of material connection and the end of visible harassment are meaningful, but they do not establish full accountability by the aggressors or a permanent distribution of care work.

### 3.8 An ordinary meal: care that does not announce itself

The appended dual-viewpoint story in V05 is especially valuable because it does not need a climax to expose reciprocal agency. Mimimi declines a large karaoke invitation because she is considering Tama’s fit, while concealing that the refusal was made for her. She likes the noisy activity; the cost is therefore not fabricated. She frames the concealment as her own aesthetic of kindness, not as an invoice she intends Tama to pay. [NM-E06; NM-P06](#nm-p06)

Tama independently arranges the food so that shrimp, which Mimimi dislikes, is omitted. Mimimi enjoys the meal without knowing that this adjustment has occurred. The symmetry is not exact—different acts carry different costs—but it defeats a one-way account in which Mimimi is always the knowing caretaker and Tama the oblivious beneficiary. It also shows that concealed kindness can be both warm and difficult to distribute: if the recipient does not know what has been done, recognition and future renegotiation become harder.

The scene also prevents excessive idealization. Mimimi has not listened carefully to everything Tama says, takes food with more enthusiasm than deliberation, and can misread a mundane bathroom departure as emotionally significant. Her eventual proposal of a small two-person karaoke trial is better fitted than forcing the large-group continuation. Tama accepts a limited experiment. The text establishes that agreement and departure toward it, not an unshown successful performance or a permanent revision of Tama’s preferences. [NM-P06]

### 3.9 After Tama’s expansion: gladness does not abolish the fear of being unnecessary

The post-V05 running story places Mimimi amid the result she wanted: classmates can now enjoy Tama’s directness, and Tama can enter conversation without Mimimi constantly mediating it. Mimimi is genuinely pleased. The prose says that pleasure can exceed the sadness of reduced need. This is decisive counterevidence to the claim that she wants Tama dependent. At the same time, the changed role leaves her unsure where her own importance resides. [NM-E07; NM-P07](#nm-p07)

Fumiya’s changed ease intensifies that self-comparison. She can admire his capacity to act and still find reassurance in a glimpse of the familiar uncertain boy. She can recognize the value of Tama’s growth and still wonder whether she is being left as someone merely decorated rather than transformed. These reactions are not a covert program to prevent other people from growing. They are a problem in how she locates herself within changes she endorses.

The chapter’s internal “black” and “white” Mimimi voices are figurative self-debate. They make competing interpretations audible: perhaps the nationally ranked gamer was always fundamentally unlike her; perhaps believing she could resemble that strength was overreach. They are not evidence for a diagnosis or literally separate selves. The important event is that she can hear a harsh interpretation, partly believe it, and then use a joke to keep it from becoming public. [NM-P07]

### 3.10 Her mother’s intervention: harmony that excludes one participant is not harmony

The domestic sequence introduces a different kind of corrective relationship. Mimimi’s mother works in cosmetics sales, and the daughter is proud of her polished professional presence even while seeing the tired person who returns home. The scene includes small reciprocal acts: Mimimi offers bath priority, changes a dirty mat, notices exhaustion, and teases affectionately. Limited time together is not sufficient evidence of emotional absence or neglect. The source later establishes divorce and a two-person household, but it does not authorize using that fact as a master cause of Mimimi’s self-worth. [NM-E08, E20; NM-P09–P10, P22](#nm-p09)

Her mother’s question is not simply whether she is sad. It asks whether she has been arranging matters so that everyone else gets along provided she alone puts up with the unpleasant part. The answer offered is sharper than generic reassurance: that arrangement is not, in the relevant sense, working well. Her professional experience as a supervisor supplies the stated background for the advice. A group’s smooth surface can conceal an excluded participant’s costs. The principle applies to Mimimi without demanding that she cease being sociable or generous.

The scene also punctures an easy fantasy of perfect maternal intuition. Her mother later admits she heard enough of the daughter’s private muttering to frame the question. She has performed some impressive parental perceptiveness rather than transparently read a face. Mimimi’s amused reception does not annul the help. Here the series gives a small, ordinary version of the distinction between the origin of a performance and the truth or benefit it can carry. The mother’s imperfectly presented advice can still reach the daughter. [NM-P10]

Mimimi does not disclose everything or emerge with a finished personal doctrine. She receives an actionable possibility: immerse herself in something she likes. The subsequent running is neither obedience to a life coach nor miraculous recovery. It is an existing activity approached under altered attention, with maternal advice, Hinami’s suggestions, Fumiya’s remembered language, and Mimimi’s own experiment contributing different parts.

### 3.11 Running for her own stride, and choosing when the race is enough

The return to running is unusually specific in its pleasures. Mimimi likes the fit of the shoes, movement through the evening, the texture of changed routes, and the sense of activity belonging to her body. The neighborhood has domestic warmth: lit windows, the suggestion of dinners, ordinary lives in progress. The same outing also includes getting lost, practical help from a clerk, and embarrassment around seeing Fumiya near his home. A process-valued activity is not a sealed zone without comparison, attraction, or practical error. [NM-E08–E09; NM-P08–P10](#nm-p08)

Her athletic specificity must be retained. She identifies high jump as her specialty, while Hinami’s is sprinting. The farewell race is a 100-metre contest, not evidence that Mimimi has become generally superior across athletics. Her target becomes a personal best, but the narrative does not provide a numerical time proving that target was achieved. What it does show is a change during the contest: the sound of Hinami behind her first recruits the old comparative fear, then Mimimi redirects attention toward her own footfalls and the enjoyment of running. [NM-P11]

She wins. The result matters to her, and the text does not require an analyst to pretend that genuine process enjoyment has made victory irrelevant. More revealing is what happens next. When Hinami wants another race, Mimimi declines and jokes that she should stop while ahead. That is an actual bounded stopping act. It does not prove a general capacity to stop every self-worth threat, but it refutes the claim that her striving has no possible local endpoint. She can have the pleasure of this result without making immediate repeated proof compulsory.

This is a richer revision than “she finally beats Hinami.” The source has changed the object of attention, the relation to effort, and the next decision. One win alone would not settle the earlier problem. The ability to let one win remain one win is part of what makes the scene different. Its durability across later emotional fields remains unproven.

### 3.12 V06: teasing becomes disclosure, and disclosure does not make feeling easy

The gyoza scene demonstrates that Mimimi’s usual advantage in comic initiative is conditional. She expects Fumiya to be too embarrassed to accept the offered bite. When he does accept it, her own self-consciousness becomes difficult to control. The private re-presentation makes clear that she is neither an invulnerable flirt nor a passive object of his growing confidence. She begins a game of embarrassment and discovers that the other person can alter its terms. His subsequent awkwardness reassures her because the competent and the uncertain boy remain recognizably one person. [NM-E10; NM-P14](#nm-p14)

The eventual declaration similarly moves through teasing without remaining only teasing. She names the qualities she respects: Fumiya is fearful yet capable of decisive action, and he has worked for her and Tama. When the word *like* appears, she briefly makes him doubt its romantic meaning, then confirms that meaning. The final clarification must not be swallowed by the preceding joke. Nor should the declaration be recast as an explicit request to begin dating. It communicates feeling and leaves an answer space. [NM-E11; NM-P15](#nm-p15)

Her immediate private aftermath is not triumphant emotional honesty. She runs away, recognizes the feeling more fully, repeatedly checks digital surfaces that cannot actually disclose his mind, and oscillates between waiting, saying more, and wishing ordinary life could remain unchanged. She knows refreshing a page will not produce the needed answer; knowing does not stop the impulse. This is a bounded post-declaration state, not a diagnosis or a claim that she habitually monitors everyone. The most feared loss is not just a rejected romance but the disappearance of the relationship that already matters. [NM-P12]

### 3.13 V07: keeping a conversational route open

The next-day lunch exchange is a small labor of continuity. Mimimi supplies noise and comic food logic while both participants remain uneasy. Her request for ordinary treatment specifically does not ask Fumiya to forget the declaration. The distinction is precise: she wants communication to remain possible without pretending the new fact has never occurred. The humorous surface is a bridge into further relation, not a cancellation of her own speech. [NM-E11; NM-P16](#nm-p16)

The scene also warns against assuming that social competence automatically conceals high-stakes feeling. Her lowered voice, broken phrasing, and difficulty meeting his eyes are visible. Elsewhere in the volume the surrounding group can notice that something has changed. She is much less secure when she herself is the exposed object of romantic inquiry than when she animates a conversation about somebody else. Recipient, stakes, and self-implication change how the same repertoire works.

### 3.14 V07: a joint performance, not a solo rescue machine

The comedy performance gives Mimimi a distinctive practical intelligence. She adjusts her address to include the audience, notices when Fumiya loses the sequence, and introduces an unscripted opening. The immediate joke does not receive laughter. Fumiya interprets the intervention as her willingness to take visible embarrassment in order to give him something recoverable. His answer uses the pair’s ordinary rhythm rather than a recovered perfect script, and the routine resumes. [NM-E12; NM-P18](#nm-p18)

This is neither purely spontaneous genius nor an act whose value is exhausted by saving him. Prior practice, the established conversational partnership, a relatively supportive audience, and both performers’ responses contribute to the result. Mimimi has worked on a shared performance she enjoys. Her request for a ramen treat after the apology gives the aftermath a small reciprocal claim, not a romance debt. The exchange can remain playful without requiring that her effort be described as free.

Afterward she repeatedly says that the comedy was fun while becoming aware that Fumiya must return to Kikuchi’s play. That repetition holds the experience as hers even while it is ending. Her disappointment is not evidence that the performance was merely a tactic to win him. A sincerely enjoyed activity can also have carried a hoped-for relationship. Losing one possible future does not retroactively empty the shared time. [NM-P18]

### 3.15 V07: sending him to Kikuchi without making loss virtuous by definition

Mimimi recognizes enough of the play’s correspondences and the two collaborators’ behavior to understand that an important answer seems to have been given indirectly. She does not know every private motive merely because she recognizes a model. When Fumiya concludes he has been rejected, she tells him not to let the stage alone replace an interpersonal answer and sends him toward the library. Tears, forceful proximity, a sharp imperative, and the familiar gamer/Brain language coexist. [NM-E13; NM-P19](#nm-p19)

The act is generous because it risks closing the route she wants. It is also an intervention, not passive withdrawal. She supplies a reason and a destination; she does not decide Kikuchi’s response. Her language about persisting belongs to this incomplete, indirect-answer situation. It is not a general permission to continue after a direct refusal. The later request and acceptance between Fumiya and Kikuchi must retain their separate agency.

Fumiya’s refusal to take Mimimi as a rebound or as repayment matters, but it does not make her loss painless. The strongest reading preserves the uncomfortable remainder: she can do something she regards as right and still want the result to be otherwise. No narrative reward is required to make the choice real, and no ideal of noble suffering should turn the choice into an obligation to repeat indefinitely.

### 3.16 V07–V08: love remains, and the rival becomes a person to talk with

After Fumiya chooses Kikuchi, Mimimi says that she still likes him and that the feeling did not arise merely from momentum or a joke. She rejects repeated apologies and attempts to retain ordinary mutual conduct. Her final warning not to assume she will always love him protects a future she has not yet determined. It names neither a duration for waiting nor a date by which she will stop. The admission of present feeling and refusal of presumed permanence are compatible. [NM-E14; NM-P17](#nm-p17)

The V08.5 café conversation then gives the current partner independent presence. Mimimi initially fishes for what Kikuchi knows about the confession, using joking familiarity to keep her own purpose obscured. She recognizes that this is unfair and changes the method by telling her directly. The honesty is a correction, not evidence that every earlier question was already transparent. [NM-E15; NM-P20](#nm-p20)

When Kikuchi rejects the “war” metaphor, Mimimi is unsettled rather than automatically persuaded by a maxim. The discussion becomes more difficult because Kikuchi understands qualities in Fumiya that Mimimi wanted to feel she uniquely saw. Shared insight hurts her, yet it also makes Kikuchi harder to dislike: this is someone who genuinely appreciates the person she loves. Jealousy and respect intensify together. That is more specific than a defeated rival learning to be nice to the winner. [NM-P21]

Mimimi states continuing feeling and an intention not to do wrong. Kikuchi respects the feeling; she does not authorize every possible act performed in its name. Later, Tama’s account of reliance as both weight and warmth prevents another simplification: needing someone does not automatically make love false. But it also does not certify every dependency as healthy, reciprocal, or consented. Mimimi is offered a way to stop condemning the origin of her feeling, not a universal permission slip.

### 3.17 V08–V09: setting a limit without demanding disappearance

V08 lets Mimimi participate in future inquiry without already knowing her vocation. She can join a shared search, name household circumstances, and enjoy the ordinary work of discovering what people want. Her mother’s work and their two-person household belong to those circumstances; they do not provide an unspoken instruction that she must choose the same career. The later commentary skill likewise cannot be projected backward as a hidden predetermined destination. [NM-E20; NM-P22](#nm-p22)

The V09 walk is a specific ethical adjustment. After learning more about Kikuchi’s jealousy and the couple’s conflict, Mimimi proposes that it may be better not to walk home together that day. The hedge does not make the act unreal: she leaves separately. Yet neither its wording nor the later corpus supports turning it into a permanent rule against all one-to-one contact. The shared routine matters enough for its loss to be felt. Humor organizes the departure but does not abolish that loss. [NM-E17; NM-P28](#nm-p28)

The later Japanese scene supplies a duration and an exception that the initial local refusal alone cannot. Fumiya describes roughly a week of separating at the station. Mimimi then refuses that day’s automatic parting and pulls him into a walk because she has noticed the removed strap, abandoned groups, and joyless improvement in his jokes. A temporary practice has existed; a permanent prohibition has not. The renewed walk is already shown within V09, so it must not be deferred as though the source only raises the possibility in V11. [NM-E17–E18; NM-P27–P28](#nm-p27)

Her resistance to Fumiya’s sweeping withdrawal is therefore not hypocrisy. Limiting an activity that may burden a relationship is different from treating every friendship and shared object as expendable. She recognizes in his retreat something like the self-amputation she attempted around the track crisis and invokes their former campaign standing to make a concealed problem discussable. She also tells him that she has recently spoken with Kikuchi and relays their shared reasons for liking him. This establishes some communicated knowledge of a conversation; it does not automatically establish that he knows every detail of the V08.5 exchange or that the two references name exactly the same encounter. [NM-P27]

The intervention is not ethically frictionless. She physically initiates the walk over his initial protest, and her objection can sound like a claim that he must preserve the person she loves. Her later accuracy and his gratitude do not retroactively supply advance permission for that initiation. The stronger defense of her argument is contextual: he still values the people he is discarding and is trying to solve guilt by narrowing his world, rather than expressing a settled wish to stop caring about them. She makes that contradiction visible, but his own choices and Kikuchi’s reply remain necessary.

The scene also gives a more affirmative self-account than unending inadequacy. Mimimi says that, although the love has not been reciprocated, she can like the self who loves him. After the hard conversation she asks whether she may remain against his shoulder briefly, and he agrees. That later, bounded request for comfort is distinct from the earlier unagreed pulling of his arm. She is not only a giver refusing all needs of her own. Neither act creates standing permission for future closeness, and the immediate response does not solve the couple’s wider allocation problem. [NM-P27]

### 3.18 V10–V11: from comparison witness to initiative-bearing friend

Mimimi’s contribution to Hinami’s birthday is grounded in particular friendship history and collaborative care, not a generic declaration that the group is good. The celebration’s eventual rupture does not make those contributions false. Neither does their sincerity make the intervention informed or guarantee that Hinami will receive its meaning as intended. Mimimi is one participant in a differentiated network, not an owner of Hinami’s hidden history. [NM-E21; DR10 §5.5]

In V11 her account of confidence becomes more explicit. She can distinguish the abilities she recognizes from the self she finds harder to affirm and can explain how another person’s visible unhappiness threatens the route an admirer has followed. She also marks where her analogy with Hinami fails. The result is a stronger interpretive contribution without omniscience. She does not claim to know the family past or to have solved Kikuchi’s creative problem. [NM-E22–E23; NM-P23–P24](#nm-p23)

Her renewed approach to the student council is her initiative. She had earlier declined an invitation to participate without becoming president; she now changes her mind. A teacher’s formal objection to her nonmembership is answered through an exception allowing help. Her playful self-description as acting president must not be turned into evidence of a new election or an explicit appointment to the office. The practical responsibility is nevertheless real. She opens access, speaks with the existing members, works with Fumiya, and helps organize the response to the stalled event. [NM-E24; NM-P25](#nm-p25)

The commentary then tests transferable skill in a genuinely new domain. Mimimi is not an experienced Atafami player disguising expertise. Her projection, diction, timing, and capacity to ask an outsider’s question become useful alongside Ashigaru’s expertise and framing. She also slips into personal nicknames and creates distraction. The favorable reception is thus a bounded event, not evidence of flawless delivery or an already chosen media career. The initial request is limited; the segment runs longer, and Ashigaru acknowledges that extension. Both the negotiated scope and its actual alteration belong in the account. [NM-E25; NM-P26](#nm-p26)

At the boundary, Mimimi has not solved what makes her worthy when she is not first or needed. She has, however, acted from more kinds of reason than that question alone can describe. The record contains ambition, enjoyment, gratitude, judgment, refusal, friendship, romantic desire, and newly exercised public ability. Her life has become more diversified; the conditions under which she can trust that diversity remain open.

## 4. Current state vector

The V11 boundary should be described through several variables, not through “more confident” as a universal improvement. Mimimi’s own distinction between *specifications* and the self makes this separation source-responsive rather than an externally imposed personality grid. [NM-E22]

| Dimension | Demonstrated state | Important variation | Boundary on the claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| Social competence | Fast topic production, teasing, invitation, audience adjustment, correction of conversational atmosphere | Can become awkward when her own romantic position is exposed; can overstep while joking | Fluency is not universal tact or complete knowledge of another person |
| Athletic competence | Serious prior basketball work, track/high-jump experience, purposeful sprint preparation, one farewell 100-metre win | Performance and enjoyment change with the comparison field and attentional target | No universal superiority, measured career trajectory, or confirmed personal-best time |
| Performance competence | A recoverable comic intervention, joint stage work, energetic novice commentary | Depends on partner, audience frame, preparation, and what the task asks | Not already a professional entertainer or game expert |
| Confidence in capacities | Can openly acknowledge appearance, sport, study, and conversation as strengths | Praise can feel credible at the ability level while failing to settle self-regard | Neither false modesty nor the absence of all confidence |
| Status | Recognized and socially connected; former election candidate; useful senior in council work | Less accumulated school-wide credibility than Hinami in V02; an outsider to formal council membership before V11 help | Popularity is not office, universal approval, or unlimited practical authority |
| Self-concept | Can articulate comparative and relational vulnerability | Solitude, others’ growth, and romantic exposure can reactivate inadequacy | V11’s account does not establish either serene acceptance or permanent despair |
| Agency | Confesses, refuses a rematch, corrects an indirect test, changes a walk, returns to help in a new role | Some actions are reactive, some are self-selected; some are chosen under costly conditions | No complete lifelong stopping rule or independent long-horizon vocation |
| Values | Serious effort, liveliness, particular loyalty, fairness to others’ choices, meaningful shared time | Values conflict: winning with friendship, contact with restraint, harmony with her own inclusion | No single value explains every act |

### 4.1 Confidence is domain-indexed, not merely hidden beneath a smile

Mimimi’s V11 statement about her strengths should be taken seriously. She does not need an analyst to discover that she is capable despite supposedly believing herself incapable. She already differentiates capability from self-approval. Calling her “insecure” without specifying the object can therefore obscure more than it explains. She may be confident that she can manage a conversation and still fear that what people enjoy is only a performance that could be replaced. She may believe she looks good and still fear the fragility of making appearance the content of the person. [NM-P10, P23]

That distinction changes the interpretation of compliments. A joke about the world finally noticing her charm can be actual enjoyment of successful attention, not necessarily a lie concealing misery. It also does not settle the larger self-worth question. Both readings that flatten the statement—“she knows she is amazing, so she cannot be insecure” and “she is insecure, so the boast cannot be enjoyed”—ignore the source’s explicit separation of levels.

### 4.2 Agency includes the ability to revise a previous refusal

The earlier refusal of a lesser council role and the later decision to help are not mutually discrediting. A choice can be genuine at one time and revised when the end changes. The earlier context remains linked to a failed candidacy. The later context offers a concrete way to act for Hinami and the school without requiring the old electoral victory. Mimimi does not become less herself because she is willing to enter through another role. [NM-E24; NM-P25]

Nor does the later choice prove that every assigned burden is now self-authored. She still works inside institutional permission and relies on the existing members’ needs, Fumiya’s planning, and Ashigaru’s cooperation. Agency is not solitary control. It is the capacity to select and participate in a course of action while its distributed conditions remain visible.

### 4.3 The current future is possible rather than chosen

V11 makes leadership and public commentary credible possibilities. It does not identify them as settled ambitions. The distinction is important because Mimimi has repeatedly been hurt by the idea that visible aptitude ought to determine who she is. An analyst would reproduce a version of that pressure by concluding that a successful performance reveals her “real calling.” [NM-E20, E24–E25]

The same caution applies to athletics. The running story establishes a personally valued practice and an actual competitive result, not a decision about university sport, training as a profession, or the duration of the routine. Her open future is not an analytical omission to be filled with the most attractive extrapolation. It is part of the V11 state.

## 5. Public energy and interior cost

### 5.1 What her humor actually does

Mimimi’s humor is not one technique. It includes exaggerated self-introduction, mock interviews, playful status claims, deliberate nonsense, fast retort, staged overconfidence, teasing familiarity, and the conversion of an awkward pause into a manageable exchange. Each creates a different answer space. Calling Fumiya *Brain* can revive a shared project identity; pretending to interview him lets her ask an otherwise embarrassing question; an absurd claim about a meal invites correction rather than solemn disagreement. [NM-E01, E10–E12, E26]

This is why “she makes people laugh” is insufficient as a reconstruction. Sometimes she wants a laugh. Sometimes she wants a response from one particular person. Sometimes she wants to preserve ordinary continuity after a change in the relationship. Sometimes she wants to stop a difficult subject from being discussed. The outcome also varies. A theatrical entrance can confuse a junior. An unscripted stage joke can fail immediately while making a useful response possible. A joke about romance as war can prompt Kikuchi to reject the premise. Humor does not give Mimimi control over how the next person answers. [NM-P18, P21, P25]

The source repeatedly shows her capable of living with correction. She can accept Tama’s retort, laugh at the exposure of her mother’s bluff, or move from a failed tactic toward direct speech. But the capacity is not automatic. High emotional stakes can make her fill more space, conceal the aim of a question, or retreat before an answer arrives. The function of the humor has to be reconstructed from the local sequence rather than diagnosed from the style alone.

### 5.2 The difference between making room and occupying all of it

In its affiliative mode, Mimimi’s energy makes participation easier. She notices a possible shared subject and gives someone a way to answer. Her ordinary relationship with Fumiya develops partly because his peculiar seriousness can become material rather than grounds for exclusion. The style can affirm difference without requiring an abstract declaration that difference is good. [NM-E01]

In a defensive mode, the same energy can occupy the whole conversational field. During the V02 walk, topic follows topic so efficiently that the subject she does not want raised has no opening. The text does not require an inference from a faint smile to a concealed motive: the sequence itself shows a relevant question being kept out until Tama asks directly. Mimimi’s skill has not disappeared. It is performing a different job. [NM-P04]

This contrast is useful for a novel situation. A lively performance around a newly included friend should not automatically be read as evasion. A lively performance that repeatedly diverts a specific question after a painful event is stronger evidence of protective avoidance. Even then, the analyst should distinguish what the avoidance protects: privacy, a friend’s comfort, her own self-image, or a valued routine she fears losing. The same surface can serve several ends.

### 5.3 Self-deprecating performance is not consent to unlimited diminishment

Mimimi can joke about being foolish, invoke a failed candidacy, or accept Tama’s affectionate “idiot” framing. The force depends on who says it, in what relationship, and what the joke makes possible. Tama’s V02 language particularizes affection. It is not a general authorization for classmates to humiliate Mimimi, nor proof that she prefers being regarded as unintelligent. Her own tactical use of a comic persona should not be confused with the absence of a serious claim to recognition. [NM-E04, E24]

Likewise, her willingness to take a public comic failure for Fumiya is not an unlimited readiness to be embarrassed on demand. The performance is jointly prepared and valued. She acts in an immediate breakdown, and he responds. A new situation involving repeated humiliation, no shared end, and no reciprocal attention would lack important conditions of that example. The model should predict possible strain or resistance there rather than mechanically replay the rescue.

### 5.4 The cost of being legible as someone who can handle it

A person who can recover quickly in public can make her own need for recovery less visible. That is a risk supported by Mimimi’s combination of performance and private strain. It is not proof that every friend knowingly exploits her. Tama sees and responds to hidden work; her mother explicitly criticizes the arrangement in which only Mimimi endures; Fumiya sometimes recognizes cost and sometimes misreads it. The network contains both blind spots and corrections. [NM-E04, E08, E12–E14]

The ethical concern is therefore distribution rather than the elimination of generosity. Does the helpful person have a place to state that the task is too much? Can the recipient recognize assistance without converting it into dependence or debt? Can she receive help in a form that does not make her feel fraudulent? The mother’s observation matters because it changes the unit of evaluation: a group does not succeed merely because its visible members avoid conflict while one participant conceals the loss.

### 5.5 Pleasure is not evidence against labor

Some of Mimimi’s most skilled behavior is also enjoyable to her. She likes boisterous talk and public play; she enjoys the performance with Fumiya; she finds a foothold in the novelty of commentary. It would be inaccurate to describe all this as exhausting work imposed upon a secretly quiet person. The problem is not that she has been assigned a completely alien personality. It is that genuinely valued abilities can become harder to refuse precisely because they are hers. [NM-E12, E15, E25–E26]

Enjoyment and effort can coexist. Enjoyment and loss can also coexist: she enjoyed the comedy and mourns the end of the shared interval. A mature account need not choose which feeling is the “real” one. It asks how the two affect the next decision and whether either is being used to erase the other.

## 6. Effort, comparison, and relational firstness

### 6.1 Four different ways effort can be organized

Mimimi’s history contains several distinct effort regimes. They should not be collapsed into one trait called hard-working.

| Regime | Immediate object | What makes success count | Characteristic danger | Actual counterpressure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Competitive self-proof, especially V02 | Defeat the salient comparison target | First place seems to certify specialness | The target improves; physical cost rises; defeat becomes a verdict on self | Tama’s particular recognition; the acknowledged relational damage |
| Process-valued running | Run, improve a personal performance, inhabit the movement | Enjoyment and her own stride become meaningful during effort | Comparison can return when another runner is audible | Attentional redirection; accepting a bounded result and declining a rematch |
| Recipient-directed care | Help Tama, Fumiya, Hinami, or a shared project | Something becomes better for a particular person | Help may become the only available proof that she matters | Reciprocity, the mother’s cost critique, differentiated roles |
| Exploratory public work | Try a role whose value is not settled in advance | A useful contribution and new experience occur | Competence is mistaken for an obligation or predetermined vocation | Task boundaries, collaborators, explicit uncertainty about the future |

The categories can overlap within an event. A farewell race is both competition and enjoyed running. Commentary is both assistance and a new performance opportunity. A person does not become inauthentic merely because more than one reason is active. [NM-E03–E04, E08–E09, E24–E25]

### 6.2 Why winning cannot do all the work assigned to it

Winning can establish that she won a contest under its rules. It can give pleasure, confirm practice, and change how other people see her. It cannot by itself guarantee that the next comparison will be favorable or that she will always be important to a friend. In the V02 regime, those additional tasks become attached to the result. This is why more practice is not necessarily an answer even when practice can improve performance. [NM-E02–E03]

Hinami is particularly destabilizing as the reference because she is not a passive measuring instrument. She has her own reasons to train, her own capacities, and her own limits. Mimimi’s wish for a stable threshold confronts another person who is continuing to act. The ethical horror of wanting Hinami to stop follows from that fact: the desired relief would require the other person to change her life for Mimimi’s self-evaluation. Recognizing the unfairness does not instantly remove the desire, and the gap between recognition and feeling becomes another source of self-attack.

The same structure can recur without an athletic ranking. Tama’s new independence can be received as welcome growth and as a threat to Mimimi’s place. Kikuchi’s understanding of Fumiya can be admired and experienced as the loss of uniquely seeing him. These are not identical competitions; the comparison shifts from performance to relational particularity. The recurring risk is that another person’s growing life will be processed as evidence that Mimimi’s own place has diminished. [NM-E07, E15]

### 6.3 “No stopping rule” needs a precise object

The source leaves a general rule for romantic persistence unresolved. It does not leave every stopping decision unobserved. Mimimi leaves the track setting during V02, later returns; declines another race after a win; refuses a large karaoke continuation on Tama’s behalf while offering a smaller alternative; declines the earlier council invitation and later revises that choice; and separates from Fumiya after proposing a local contact limit. These actions differ ethically and motivationally, but all show that she can terminate or redirect a course of action. [NM-E03–E04, E06, E09, E17, E24]

The V02 resignation is particularly important. It is a stop, but one designed partly to escape an intolerable self-judgment and protect the friendship from envy. Its existence does not prove that the effort regime has acquired a sustainable limit. Conversely, the race refusal is a modest, successful local endpoint, but it cannot be generalized into mastery of every emotional situation. The correct question is not whether Mimimi “has boundaries” as a binary trait. It is which boundary she can formulate, enact, and maintain under which pressure.

The same distinction should govern the V09 walk. It would be too weak to dismiss the hedge as no real decision. It would be too strong to derive a permanent prohibition from it. Her resumed V09 walk and later V11 approach to a distressed Fumiya are intelligible precisely because she is trying to judge changed situations rather than enforce a complete rule already agreed by everyone.

### 6.4 Goal ownership is more than origin

Mimimi begins some activities partly by following or responding to another person. That does not settle whether she can later make them her own. Track is linked to Hinami, but running becomes directly enjoyable. Council help responds to Hinami’s absence, but Mimimi is the one who proposes going back. The shared search for a future develops around Fumiya’s inquiry, but participation can still be wanted. An externally occasioned activity is not therefore an alien goal. [NM-E08–E09, E20, E23–E24]

The stronger test concerns what reasons remain available when conditions change. Can she still value running without immediately beating Hinami again? The farewell ending provides a local yes. Can she value friendship with Fumiya while he chooses Kikuchi? Several later acts provide a difficult, qualified yes. Can she remain important to Tama without retaining a monopoly on assistance? The running story shows both pleasure in the answer and unresolved fear. These are better tests of ownership than a simple classification of who first suggested the activity.

### 6.5 The danger of turning self-sacrifice into a new ranking

It is plausible that Mimimi can replace the demand to win with a demand to yield or help more than anyone else. Her distress at no longer being necessary and her repeated conversion of pain into useful action support that concern. But the corpus does not establish that she secretly calculates all generosity as a route to superior moral status. Such a theory would be difficult to falsify and would erase the person-specific interests she explicitly expresses. [NM-E07, E13–E15]

The bounded formulation is narrower: **care can become an unstable basis for self-worth when losing a particular helping role seems to threaten the self, even if the care itself remains sincere**. The criterion is not how impressive a sacrifice appears to the analyst. It is whether the character can continue to belong when the service is unnecessary, declined, shared, or too costly. The current source raises that test more clearly than it finishes it.

Her mother’s advice is useful here because it refuses the apparent accounting victory. An arrangement in which everyone else is comfortable and Mimimi absorbs the loss does not become successful merely because she is competent at maintaining it. But the advice is not a finished operating policy delivered by the author through a parent. Mimimi has to discover how to apply it amid activities she really enjoys and people she really loves. [NM-E08]

## 7. Love, restraint, and non-entitlement

### 7.1 Attraction is a history, not a personality match calculated in advance

The V03 camp evidence prevents the assumption that Mimimi’s later love was simply present all along beneath playful interaction. She can admire Fumiya as a campaign partner before she can picture romance. Later attraction develops through changes she notices: his capacity to act for Tama, his increasing ease, the combination of decisiveness and remaining uncertainty, and the experience of herself when she is with him. These are not interchangeable properties of any high-ranked boy. [NM-E05, E10–E11, E15]

Her private account also preserves ordinary attraction without requiring an exhaustive sexual or romantic biography. The monograph need not make her purely ethical in order to take the love seriously. She notices presence, expression, and the person’s growing confidence. She enjoys teasing him and wants to matter in his attention. At the same time, his unreliably bashful responses are part of the intimacy rather than defects she wants fully removed. The desired person is not merely an idealized invulnerable hero.

### 7.2 Reliance can be a component of love without being its complete explanation

Mimimi worries that she likes Fumiya because leaning on his strength makes it easier to like herself. This is an important self-question, not a settled explanation that renders the feeling counterfeit. Kikuchi asks what the experience is like; Tama later contributes the recipient’s perspective. Being relied upon can cost something and still feel warm. The response distinguishes dependence as a possible relational feature from dependency as a total moral verdict. [NM-E15–E16; NM-P21]

It would be an equal mistake to use that exchange to declare every form of reliance justified. The warmth of a recipient does not establish unlimited capacity, and the conversation does not take place with Fumiya as a participant agreeing to all future terms. What the scene makes possible is less punitive self-understanding. Mimimi can acknowledge a need without deciding that the existence of need invalidates the love. How the need should shape conduct remains an additional question.

### 7.3 Non-selection does not reduce a person to second place

The romance invites ranking language but does not make it adequate. Fumiya chooses Kikuchi; that is a consequential particular choice. It does not establish that Kikuchi is globally better, that Mimimi’s humor and presence are less valuable, or that every future relation is governed by this outcome. Mimimi’s own “war” framing receives resistance from Kikuchi, and the conversation that follows becomes more productive when it concerns different reasons people love rather than a single competitive result. [NM-E14–E15]

Nevertheless, the analyst should not make the distinction easy for Mimimi. Seeing another person occupy the chosen position and understand Fumiya’s qualities hurts. She wants some of that understanding to have been uniquely hers. The point is not that she has transcended comparison. It is that she can stay in contact with an inconvenient personhood on the other side of it: Kikuchi is neither a thief nor a generic superior contestant. Respect makes the situation morally richer without making it emotionally lighter.

### 7.4 Her ethical development concerns acts, not compulsory emotional erasure

Mimimi does not owe the immediate disappearance of her feeling. She does owe attention to what she does with it and to the standing of the people involved. The novels give concrete efforts in that direction: acknowledging the chosen relationship, warning against assuming permanent love, limiting a walk, noticing physical proximity, and refusing to endorse Fumiya’s shrinking of his social world. These are different acts rather than one all-encompassing vow. [NM-E14–E18, E23]

The distinction also protects her from being made the sole administrator of the couple’s security. Fumiya’s time use, disclosure, and response to third-party contact are his responsibilities; Kikuchi’s preferences and limits need her voice. Mimimi can choose care and restraint without taking ownership of everything either partner feels. Her quick apology that she has become part of the cause is evidence of concern, not a complete allocation of fault. [NM-E17]

### 7.5 Helping is neither a claim on him nor proof that nothing is wanted

Mimimi’s support of Fumiya’s chosen route is often interpreted through one of two reductive stories. In the first she is secretly earning future repayment; in the second she has ceased to want anything and become an ideal friend. The source supports neither. She states continuing love and behaves in ways that protect the chosen relationship. Those facts can stand together without a hidden future reward or emotional purification. [NM-E13–E18]

Her small meal demands after difficult help are useful counterexamples to both extremes. They show a playful wish to remain a participant who may ask for something. They do not constitute a contract exchanging rescue for affection. The distinction between ordinary reciprocity and a debt of romantic choice must remain clear.

The unresolved part is the future. The corpus does not tell us when or whether her feeling will end, what new relationship she may choose, or which forms of one-to-one intimacy she will find sustainable. It also does not demonstrate a second confession that reopens Fumiya’s choice. An evidence-bounded monograph preserves that space rather than writing a consoling endpoint.

## 8. Relationship-conditioned reconstruction

This section owns the variations in **Mimimi’s** conduct across relationships. It does not replace the independent Fumiya/Mimimi specialist or the core-peer ensemble with its co-equal Mimimi/Tama responsibility. Each partner’s full trajectory remains with its appropriate owner.

### 8.1 Tama

With Tama, Mimimi’s expressive energy has a particular receiver. She can exaggerate, seek a retort, admire directness, and enjoy a refusal that is legible within an established bond. Tama is not simply a fragile person for whom Mimimi performs normality. She is someone whose judgment Mimimi finds credible, whose presence can interrupt destructive self-evaluation, and whose own care often arrives without elaborate self-advertisement. [NM-E04–E07, E16]

Mimimi’s role changes as Tama’s reach expands. She supplies knowledge and form during the training period, then sees Tama function with other people more freely. The source preserves delight in that expansion, not just loss of monopoly. The accompanying loneliness matters because it reveals how much Mimimi had invested in being needed. It is an insecurity within a loving response, not sufficient evidence of a wish to keep Tama small.

The two-viewpoint meal adds ordinary ethical texture. Each makes a concealed accommodation; neither fully sees the other’s act. That reciprocity does not make the arrangement perfectly transparent. Tama anticipates she may quietly adjust the food again, and Mimimi’s avoidance of the group invitation could remain unknown. Such care can be a gift, but gifts can also become recurring labor without a shared account of their cost. The relationship study must keep both possibilities available. [NM-P06]

Tama’s directness is often what Mimimi needs, but the monograph should not promote every abrupt question or bodily intervention into a universal method of care. In V02 the direct question works within a specific history. In V08.5 Tama also knows when to wait outside a conversation and when to offer an opening without forcing disclosure. Her later concern is not merely the same bluntness applied harder. Mimimi is therefore responding to a person capable of timing and restraint as well as firmness. [NM-P04–P05, P21]

At V11, the relation remains strongly reciprocal in evidence but not invulnerable. Neither girl should be made the other’s sole regulator. A future severe conflict could test what happens when they disagree about the help wanted, when one cannot be available, or when the task is to value a person without needing her in the old role. Those are real uncertainties, not reasons to downgrade the observed affection.

### 8.2 Hinami

Hinami is the friend who can read Mimimi’s effort and the competitor whose continuing effort destabilizes her. Removing either side makes the V02 crisis unintelligible. If Hinami were merely an enemy, envy would not wound Mimimi’s moral self-image in the same way. If she were merely a benevolent example, repeated loss would not become such an immediate pressure. [NM-E02–E04]

Their activity also includes ordinary pleasure and useful advice. In the running story, Hinami can talk about goals and frustration, admit that not everything has gone as expected, and suggest a relation to enjoyable activity. Mimimi’s later win is followed by competitive banter, not a declaration that the hierarchy has been permanently inverted. Care survives the comparison, and competition survives the care. [NM-E07–E09]

V11 changes Mimimi’s potential contribution. She can recognize something in the separation between visible ability and self-confidence, and can describe the predicament of someone who has invested in an admired model’s route. But she does not become an expert in Hinami’s family formation by analogy. Her own acknowledgement that she might already have felt helped under similar visible circumstances is evidence against treating their crises as duplicates. [NM-E22–E23]

Her wish to become Hinami’s friend in a fuller sense should be read as an aspiration about the present relationship, not as literal proof that all previous friendship was fake. The observed past includes shared activity, concern, comparison, and celebration. What seems insufficient now is access to the person’s suffering and a way of helping that does not reproduce the old asymmetry. Mimimi can choose to contribute without thereby obtaining Hinami’s consent to inquiry, narrative representation, or return to public roles. [NM-P24]

### 8.3 Tomozaki

Fumiya meets different parts of Mimimi at different stages. Early on he experiences a socially capable peer whose responsiveness makes his own awkwardness more usable. During the election they become collaborators. In the V02 crisis he has some trusted standing but cannot supply the most effective answer alone. Later he becomes a person whose action for Tama is admired, a comic partner, a romantic subject, and eventually a friend whose chosen partnership makes old routines more complicated. [NM-E01–E05, E10–E18]

Her manner with him is therefore not adequately described as flirting. Mock interviews can carry curiosity, competitive teasing, jealousy, or an attempt to preserve ordinary contact. A sharp command can come from genuine concern. A tentative question about whether talking is now a “rule violation” can reveal the absence of a settled rule rather than knowledge that the present act is forbidden. The register changes with what she risks losing. [NM-P15–P17, P24, P28]

She admires his capacity to hold an aim, but that admiration does not leave her unable to judge him. V09’s intervention is important precisely because she resists a decision he is making with conviction. The trait she values—his expansion through chosen action—is being damaged by his proposed solution. She can invoke their history to challenge rather than merely endorse him. By V11, she also contributes an analysis he did not generate himself and proposes a concrete project.

The relation remains asymmetric romantically and reciprocal in other respects. He does not owe her selection for help received. He does owe attention to the costs of help and to the fact that her present warmth is not a promise of indefinite availability. She does not own his other relationships; he does not own her emotional timetable. Their ordinary shared language survives, but survival is not a guarantee that every old practice should return unchanged.

### 8.4 Kikuchi

Kikuchi first appears to Mimimi partly through comparison: quiet charm and apparent heroine-like suitability can become the qualities Mimimi fears she lacks. The café story then replaces some of that comparison with actual exchange. Mimimi discovers a person who can be hesitant, funny, direct, and serious about the legitimacy of feeling. The dialogue does not erase the fact that Kikuchi occupies the relationship Mimimi wanted. [NM-E15–E16]

Mimimi’s indirect testing deserves its full ethical weight. She initially seeks knowledge while obscuring why it matters. Her own discomfort with that maneuver leads to a better act: direct disclosure. Kikuchi’s answer is then not a trophy awarded for perfect conduct but a response to the changed conversation. Their emerging respect is earned through fallible interaction.

The agreement is narrower than a friendship treaty. Mimimi says she will not do wrong and wants to remain honest about feeling. Kikuchi respects the feeling. Neither defines every future private walk, touch, message, disclosure, or allocation of time. That incompleteness is not insincerity. It means later situations still require judgment. The wider relationship specialist must preserve Kikuchi’s own interests, not treat her generosity as a standing concession to whatever Mimimi may next desire.

### 8.5 Peer and institutional groups

Mimimi’s peer life is broader than the three most analyzed relationships. Sakura and Yuki, the wider class, sports contacts, and later council members supply contexts in which she is neither a rejected lover nor a rival to Hinami. Her ability to inhabit those settings matters in its own right. A monograph that only follows the central triangle would falsely make every joke and outing an attempt to regain a romantic position. [NM-E07, E11, E20, E24]

At the same time, group success is not a uniform property. Her election performance works locally but cannot overcome Hinami’s stored credibility. Her comic partnership benefits from friends in the audience. Her council entrance requires permission and an explanation of why a nonmember should help. Her novice commentary works within a format that gives an outsider’s view value. In each case the field and other participants help determine what her capacity can accomplish. [NM-E02, E12, E24–E25]

Mimimi can accept a division of labor. She asks Fumiya for planning help, solicits information from existing members, and considers contacts through her mother’s work. These are not failures to be independently capable. They are concrete counterexamples to the idea that her only way of helping is to absorb everything personally. The larger danger remains that familiar competence may invite unexamined expansion of responsibility, as the commentary’s extension beyond the initial bounded request illustrates. [NM-P25–P26]

### 8.6 Her mother and the limits of a family explanation

The mother/daughter relationship gives Mimimi both ordinary affection and a perspective not reducible to school competition. Mimimi sees the labor behind polished professional appearance, can be proud of the worker and considerate toward the tired parent, and receives advice grounded in workplace coordination. She also chooses what not yet to disclose. The parent can offer an insight without possessing complete access. [NM-E08; NM-P09–P10]

V08’s divorce disclosure is direct and socially contextualized. Mimimi does not want Fumiya to become solemn as though he has uncovered an unmentionable secret. Her joking generalization about divorce belongs to that speech act; it is not a demographic claim this monograph adopts or verifies. The supported facts are the reported middle-school divorce, only-child status, two-person household, and frequent time alone because her mother works. The source does not establish the father’s present relationship, custody arrangements, finances, or a causal equation between divorce and comparative self-worth. [NM-E20; NM-P22]

A strong interpretation may notice thematic continuity between a mother who performs a professional role and a daughter who manages social appearances. The daughter herself senses resemblance. But a resemblance is not a total developmental cause. Athletics, particular friendships, experiences of unequal commitment, and her own habits of self-judgment remain independent evidence. The family scene should enlarge the person, not replace one reductive origin with another.

### 8.7 Observers, juniors, and temporary collaborators

Yamashita’s admiring testimony matters because it establishes that Mimimi’s effort was already noticed before Fumiya’s involvement. That observer does not have the same intimacy as Tama, but she prevents a protagonist-centered account in which Mimimi first becomes recognizable only when he sees her. [NM-E02]

The council juniors and Ashigaru reveal another kind of standing. They can receive Mimimi’s energy as useful, surprising, or initially confusing without knowing her private romantic history. Ashigaru identifies audible qualities in the represented performance—projection, diction, intonation—and offers a novice-friendly role rather than pretending she has gaming expertise. These relations show that her capacities have value outside the people whose approval most burdens her. They do not prove she has already incorporated that value into stable self-belief. [NM-E24–E25]

## 9. Ordinary-life and preference evidence

Ordinary life is not a decorative interval between Mimimi’s meaningful crises. It is where the content of the person becomes least compatible with her harshest descriptions of herself. The source gives tastes, practical habits, sensory pleasures, small lapses, chosen company, and particular forms of play. None needs to be elevated into a secret explanation of everything else. [NM-E06–E10, E15, E20, E26]

The register deliberately keeps direct preference, observed choice, another person’s knowledge, and analyst inference separate. It also leaves gaps. The admitted scenes do not establish a comprehensive music list, favorite colors, a complete reading profile, general sleep habits outside particular stress, a settled occupation, or an exhaustive family routine. Those should remain unknown rather than be filled from an adaptation, a profile, or the analyst’s sense of what an energetic athlete “would like.” [NM-P06, P08–P10, P12, P16, P20, P22, P27]

### 9.2 Food is both pleasure and a way of relating

Mimimi’s meals often involve exchange, teasing, and another person’s response. The gyoza offered to Fumiya is not evidence of a general preference for feeding people; it is a particular attempt to upset his composure that becomes embarrassing to her. The food-sharing with Tama similarly mixes appetite, familiarity, and imperfect attention to permission. These scenes should not be flattened into either harmless universal intimacy or calculated flirtation. Their significance comes from sequence and recipient. [NM-P06, P14]

The shrimp adjustment is unusually useful evidence because it comes from the person who knows the preference, not from a generalized character label. It also exposes an information asymmetry: Mimimi enjoys a dish without knowing the accommodation behind it. The warmth of that care is real, but an analyst should not credit Mimimi with gratitude for a specific act she has not been told occurred. Nor should the shrimp aversion be widened into seafood avoidance when the same meal supports enjoyment of crab.

Her V07 lunch joke about chicken nanban and tartar sauce belongs to an effort to keep the post-declaration conversation moving. It includes a remark about dieting, but the scene does not supply a stable nutritional regime. A responsible monograph does not turn one joke into a health diagnosis or a quantified routine. What it does show is the simultaneous presence of appetite, appearance awareness, absurdity, and the wish to make an awkward interaction easier.

### 9.3 Taste can be modified without becoming a repudiation of the self

The café tea passage offers a small version of a much larger developmental principle. Mimimi normally sweetens and softens the drink substantially. In this setting she tries it with less, attends to its fragrance, and likes the experience. She has not discovered that her old preference was false. A preference is capable of variation because the object and situation invite a different way of attending. [NM-P20]

The grooming contrast after retirement is similarly concrete. Practicality around sweating had governed some choices; outside that routine, scent and texture can receive more attention. The source distinguishes ordinary use and the items previously reserved for weekends. This is not a transformation from an athlete into a purely appearance-oriented girl. It is a shift in what a daily context makes useful or pleasurable. The same person can have both practical and aesthetic reasons.

These observations matter for hypothetical reconstruction. A novel situation involving a small, reversible experiment in food or clothing need not be treated as a threat to Mimimi’s identity. Her difficulty is not a general inability to change. High-stakes comparison and self-evaluation create different conditions from trying a cup of tea another way.

### 9.4 Running is also a sensory practice

The running chapter is not only an argument about goal ownership. Mimimi notices shoe fit, the sound of clothing, air, movement, route, and the presence of other households. The activity gives her something to attend to besides whether she is keeping up with someone else. During the race, attention to her own footfalls is therefore not merely a slogan supplied by another person; it is a concrete change in how the event is experienced. [NM-P08, P11]

At the same time, the ordinary run does not make her invulnerable to social feeling. Seeing Fumiya near his home recruits curiosity and jealousy; recognizing the other girl as his sister changes the interpretation. The transition is evidence of a responsive, revisable emotional reading, not a settled possessive belief. She also recognizes that some information is not hers to demand. The scene preserves desire to know alongside a limit on entitlement. [NM-P08–P09]

Getting lost is equally valuable. Public confidence is compatible with a mundane navigation failure. She considers practical options and asks a clerk for directions. The monograph should be able to imagine her laughing at such a mistake or seeking help without making it a crisis of worth—while acknowledging that the evidence does not tell us how she would behave if the error had serious consequences for someone else.

### 9.5 Domestic care without an invented household biography

The mother/daughter scene establishes more than loneliness at home. Mimimi can notice work fatigue, offer priority, make a small practical adjustment, and enjoy affectionate teasing. Her mother can notice enough to ask a useful question without being granted full access to every worry. Their relation contains warmth and limited time, presentation and fatigue, advice and bluff. [NM-P09–P10]

It would be an overreach to infer that Mimimi routinely performs all domestic labor or acts as a substitute adult. The scene gives bounded actions, not a household census. It would also be an overreach to infer that her mother’s long hours explain every need for peer recognition. The more useful conclusion is that Mimimi has a meaningful family relationship capable of furnishing an independent perspective on her social behavior.

### 9.6 Objects and routines make loss more concrete

The shared strap is meaningful because it belongs to a history of friendship and joint repair. Fumiya’s removal of it becomes visible evidence within a larger pattern of leaving groups and suppressing pleasure. Mimimi does not read an isolated accessory as a magical lie detector. She combines the missing object with changed conduct and the knowledge that he has been withdrawing. [NM-P27]

Likewise, the walk from Kita-Yono matters because it is ordinary. Altering it changes a practice through which companionship has been repeatedly available. That does not prove the routine must be preserved in exactly its old form. It explains why its loss can hurt even when both participants regard a limit as considerate. The ethical question is not whether a walk is intrinsically romantic. It is what this walk means to these people, what others know, and what permissions or expectations have actually been discussed. [NM-P28]

### 9.7 Low-stakes evidence changes the account of her personality

Without these scenes, Mimimi might look like a person who only competes, compares, rescues, and suffers. With them, she is someone who likes particular food, tries a drink differently, enjoys a curt message from a friend, gets lost, knows a comic reference, changes how she dresses for activity, and notices the warmth of a neighborhood. The details do not solve the self-worth problem. They show why its language of “nothing inside” is an inadequate total description.

They also constrain prediction. She is not likely to approach every trivial choice as a moral referendum. She can improvise, indulge, tease, and act carelessly. An evidence-bounded model must leave room for that ordinariness rather than produce a miniature thesis in every imagined response.

## 10. Self-presentation and focalization

### 10.1 What can be said about her Japanese voice

The attested samples support a differentiated voice, not a complete statistical idiolect. Mimimi frequently appears in familiar, casual exchanges, often with elongated spellings, emphatic exclamations, playful role language, and quick changes of direction. Mock-formal self-reference such as `ワタクシ` or the naming of “Mimimi” as though she were a public character can create comic distance. That distance sometimes makes an awkward admission possible and sometimes delays it. Its force must be read locally. [NM-E28; NM-P14, P20–P21]

Forms of address also carry particular histories. `ブレーン` activates the campaign partnership and later shared problem-solving with Fumiya. `友崎` can occur in more direct personal statements. `たま`, `葵`, and `風香ちゃん` locate different familiar relationships; they are not interchangeable intimacy scores. The source does not warrant a claim that one form always marks romance or that another proves emotional detachment. Her use of a name in a public stream can even have an unintended effect different from its ordinary private use. [NM-P15, P19, P21, P25–P27]

### 10.2 The same comic form can carry different degrees of ownership

Mimimi’s V06 clarification and her V07 lunch chatter illustrate why surface category is insufficient. In the first, a tease becomes the vehicle for an actual proposition about love. In the second, nonsense helps keep a conversation possible while the substantive answer is still pending. Both are playful, but they accomplish different things. Treating all play as concealment would miss the declaration. Treating all play as transparent ease would miss the awkwardness. [NM-P15–P16]

Her private narration can likewise use comic distance while conveying a serious state. After the declaration she mocks the futility of checking digital surfaces and describes her own agitation in a recognizably lively voice. The humor is evidence of how she represents distress to herself, not evidence that the distress is light. Nor does distress invalidate the humor. The two can occur in the same sentence without one being the hidden truth and the other the false cover. [NM-P12]

### 10.3 Other people’s admiration can overstate her control

Fumiya often sees Mimimi as a person who can do socially what he cannot. That admiration identifies real differences in fluency and public range. It can also make her look more naturally secure than the private evidence permits. The surprise when she becomes romantically awkward is partly a correction to his earlier model, not proof that a new personality has suddenly appeared. [NM-E01, E11–E12]

His descriptions of her generosity are similarly moving but situated. “She thinks of me more than herself” conveys how he experiences being helped. It does not give the analyst access to an exhaustive motive hierarchy. The same record contains her wish for particular recognition, her enjoyment of the joint act, and her own pain. A monograph should preserve his gratitude without using it to conscript her into permanent selflessness.

Tama’s and Yamashita’s views provide different corrections. Tama can name the help that ordinary class perception misses. Yamashita can recall effort before the main relationship. Neither alone is a complete interior model. Multiple observers increase the range of evidence; repetition across observers is not automatically independent proof of every inferred motive.

### 10.4 Her own account is privileged access, not a final verdict

The decorated-frame metaphor is direct evidence of how she experiences a problem, not an ontological inventory. The chapter containing it also records love for her mother, concern for Tama, attraction to Fumiya, sensory enjoyment, practical decisions, and an ability to reconsider. Those contents do not mean she is lying about emptiness. They mean the felt absence concerns how she authorizes or values those contents as *hers*. [NM-P07–P12]

In V11 she can name the fluctuation between social confidence and private doubt. Fumiya himself is unsure whether part of her account expresses acceptance or resignation. That uncertainty should survive. The analysis may say the distinction is articulate and useful; it cannot certify that articulation has solved the state being described. [NM-P23]

### 10.5 Gendered jokes are not universal social laws

Mimimi sometimes explains feeling through a generalization about girls, or urges Fumiya forward through an expectation of masculine decisiveness. Those utterances belong to particular rhetorical acts. They should not be imported as the narrator’s sociology, the analyst’s ethics, or an exhaustive account of Mimimi’s gender beliefs. [NM-P19, P24]

The same applies to an idealized contrast between a quiet “heroine” and a noisy girl like herself. The café sequence shows that comparison as part of Mimimi’s insecurity, then complicates it through Kikuchi’s actual responses. The monograph should not endorse the imagined hierarchy by describing one personality as the natural romantic winner. Nor should joking admiration of other girls be converted into an unestablished orientation or romantic history.

### 10.6 Silence, touch, and timing are evidence only at their actual scope

Mimimi can become quieter when she risks a personal answer; she can also wait for Fumiya to speak in the night conversation. That waiting is a useful contrast with the topic-filling defense of V02. It does not establish a universal rule that quietness is sincere and loudness evasive. The actual question, relationship, and sequence determine the force. [NM-P04, P24]

Physical familiarity deserves the same precision. The novels often use it comically, but a recipient’s objection remains part of the evidence. Mimimi can notice a problem after an impulsive gesture, ask for brief comfort after a difficult conversation, or press past an initial resistance when she thinks intervention is needed. Those acts should not be conflated into either perfect consent awareness or constant disregard. A serious reconstruction treats bodily and conversational permission as act-specific, even where the scene is warm or ultimately helpful.

## 11. Central contradictions

Mimimi’s contradictions are not all errors waiting for a hidden unifying motive. Some are durable conflicts between things she genuinely values; some are differences of state; some are self-theory/conduct gaps; and some remain unresolved at the boundary.

### 11.1 Strengths that can generate their own failures

Her sensitivity to audience response helps her include people and recover interaction. The same sensitivity can make her suppress dissatisfaction before anyone has heard it. Her loyalty helps her bear difficulty for a friend. The same loyalty can make stopping feel like abandonment. Her competitiveness sustains practice. The same competitiveness can make another person’s progress feel like the moving border of her own inadequacy. Her ability to recognize a friend’s pattern can produce a useful challenge. The same confidence in recognition can encourage her to initiate an intervention before the friend has agreed to it. [NM-E02–E04, E07–E08, E18]

These are not reasons to remove the strengths. They are reasons to reconstruct their operating conditions. The problem is not that she possesses too much empathy or too much ambition as a timeless quantity. It is how these capacities are organized by the current goal, relationship, and perceived cost of refusal.

### 11.2 Better language is a resource, not a completed transformation

By V11 Mimimi can articulate distinctions that earlier scenes only enact. She can explain why a model’s unhappiness threatens an admirer’s own investment and distinguish the self from its specifications. She can also identify when her analogy does not answer the case. That is meaningful development. [NM-E22–E23]

But vocabulary does not prove that she can use the distinction reliably when she herself is hurt. The private café scene already shows her recognizing that a question is unfair while struggling to stop testing; the V09 intervention combines deep insight with forceful physical initiation. Reflective capacity should therefore be treated as something available under conditions, not a certificate that all later conduct will meet its standard.

### 11.3 Received care need not cancel agency

Mimimi’s strongest changes often involve other people. Tama supplies particular recognition; her mother gives a cost critique; Fumiya gives shared language and occasions for action; Kikuchi helps distinguish feeling from moral failure. None of that makes the resulting choices unowned. She has to receive, resist, translate, or enact the contribution. The race is run by her; the confession is hers; the decision to return to the council is hers. [NM-E04, E08–E09, E15, E24]

The converse also matters: because the choices are hers, their success cannot be credited entirely to an adviser. Fumiya’s remembered language about her own stride becomes effective only inside a changed practical experience. A prior accurate idea does not automatically produce a later act. The character’s own attention and willingness are part of the causal account.

## 12. Rival readings and counterevidence

### `Mimimi loses because Hinami is naturally better`

The rivalry does involve unequal performance, and the source does not permit an exact division of aptitude and practice. But reducing the losses to natural superiority omits the repeated attention to accumulated labor, preparation, institutional credibility, and different effort regimes. It also cannot explain why similar objective performance can have different meanings to the same person across contexts. Mimimi’s crisis concerns what defeat proves about her, not simply the existence of a stronger opponent. **Disposition: reject the reduction; preserve uncertainty about aptitude.** [NM-E02–E03]

### `Tama teaches her that rankings never matter`

Tama’s response is particular, not an abstract abolition of competition. She names Mimimi’s importance to her and uses ranking language playfully to make that value hearable. Mimimi returns to sport, remains competitive, and later enjoys winning. The strongest supported revision is that a ranking cannot exhaust a person’s value or displace a particular relationship. **Disposition: reject the universal anti-ranking thesis; strengthen limited jurisdiction.** [NM-E04, E09]

### `Her romance is just another contest with Hinami or Kikuchi`

Comparison is present in her romantic self-understanding. She fears that other people possess the qualities she lacks and is hurt when Kikuchi sees strengths in Fumiya she wanted to recognize uniquely. But the source gives a developmental, person-specific attachment, a clear absence of settled attraction at the earlier camp, appreciation of Fumiya’s remaining weakness, and conduct that supports his choice of another partner. Those facts exceed a competition for a trophy. **Disposition: retain comparison as a pressure, reject it as the whole motive.** [NM-E10–E16]

### `Helping Tomozaki choose Kikuchi proves uncomplicated maturity`

The act is generous and agentic. It also costs her a hoped-for future and is followed by continuing pain and love. The same character can make a defensible choice and remain vulnerable to turning useful suffering into a measure of worth. The narrative does not require immediate reward, but neither does it authorize a moral ideal in which she must keep yielding to qualify as good. **Disposition: preserve the act’s value; reject costless or terminal maturity.** [NM-E13–E16]

### `V11 shows she has found her vocation`

The commentary displays real transferable strengths and favorable local reception. Council work also shows initiative beyond a failed election. Neither supplies a long-term choice, training plan, livelihood, contract, or sustained test. Her own future inquiry remains incomplete. Calling the new role a destiny would confuse what she can do with what she has chosen to make her life about. **Disposition: strengthen demonstrated capacity; keep vocation open.** [NM-E20, E24–E25]

### 12.1 Rival: the bright public self is fake, and private sadness is the real Mimimi

This reading has some initial plausibility because the private chapters explicitly disclose feelings hidden from friends. It becomes inadequate when it treats that access as an exhaustive essence. The same private narration likes noisy fun, enjoys food and running, admires friends, makes jokes, and chooses action. Her public capacities are real and often wanted. The sad account is evidence about a state and a self-evaluation, not a discovery that the rest never existed. **Disposition: reject the binary; preserve concealment where the sequence supports it.** [NM-E07–E12, E15, E22, E26]

### 12.2 Rival: she is universally considerate and therefore never manipulative

Her generosity is repeatedly evidenced, but the universal claim cannot survive the café testing, inattentiveness to Tama, impulsive touch, or the forceful beginning of the V09 walk. Some of these acts are minor, some are more consequential, and she can notice and correct them. The appropriate conclusion is neither sainthood nor a new label of manipulator. It is that a large social repertoire can be used thoughtfully, carelessly, defensively, or intrusively. **Disposition: reject moral infallibility; retain differentiated conduct and correction.** [NM-E06, E15, E18–E19, E23]

### 12.3 Rival: every kind act is an attempt to remain necessary

Role-loss anxiety makes this an important rival rather than a straw man. Mimimi does sometimes fear that without being needed she lacks a place. But the running story explicitly preserves her delight in Tama’s independence, and the romantic record includes choices that reduce rather than monopolize access. She values people who can increasingly act without her. A theory that calls every contrary act a subtler strategy for indispensability would be unfalsifiable. **Disposition: retain usefulness-based self-worth as a risk under specified pressure, reject total motive reduction.** [NM-E07, E13–E18]

### 12.4 Rival: process-valued running permanently solves comparison

The race supports a real shift in attention and a bounded refusal to keep proving the result. It does not establish that later romantic or relational comparison has vanished. The café chapter shows renewed comparison in another domain, and V11 retains the ability/self distinction. The change is a resource and an example of a different effort regime, not an all-context cure. **Disposition: strengthen local achievement; reject cross-domain immunity.** [NM-E09, E15, E22]

### 12.5 Rival: non-entitlement requires her to stop all private contact

The source shows a local restraint becoming a short-term practice and then being reconsidered in a changed situation. It does not establish a jointly negotiated permanent ban. Her later contact can still be ethically difficult, especially where touch or intervention precedes permission, but difficulty is not proof that every private conversation is inherently wrongful. A stronger account asks what each person knows, wants, and has agreed to, and what the contact actually does. **Disposition: reject categorical prohibition and categorical permission alike.** [NM-E17–E19, E23]

### 12.6 Rival: she ought to make Fumiya preserve the person she loves

Mimimi’s V09 argument has a possible controlling edge: she names the expansive person she and Kikuchi love and objects to his change. Love cannot confer ownership over a partner’s or friend’s identity. That is a serious counterreading, especially because she initiates the walk forcefully. Yet the context shows Fumiya sacrificing relationships he still values out of an over-narrow model of responsibility, not calmly choosing an unrelated new life that she refuses to accept. She makes that contradiction discussable, while the final couple’s choice remains theirs. **Disposition: preserve the corrective insight and its positive effect; retain the method and authority question.** [NM-P27]

### 12.7 Rival: her mother’s advice is the completed answer the rest of the story merely applies

The advice supplies an important independent principle: a group that functions by making one person absorb the cost is not simply functioning well. But the mother does not know the whole situation, Mimimi does not disclose everything, and later cases still create difficulty. The advice is a resource in her life, not an external authorial verdict resolving every sacrifice. **Disposition: strengthen the mother’s distinct contribution; reject a total cure narrative.** [NM-E08]

### 12.8 Rival: divorce explains the need to be chosen

The household facts make solitude and maternal work relevant context. They do not establish an attachment diagnosis, a paternal absence narrative, or the causal origin of comparative self-worth. The direct family scene is warm and practically attentive. School competition and particular relationships supply their own mechanisms. **Disposition: preserve stated family circumstances; abstain from an unearned causal biography.** [NM-E08, E20]

### 12.9 Rival: her understanding of Hinami proves that the two girls share the same inner problem

The analogy is useful precisely because Mimimi can identify a gap between capacities and self-confidence. But she explicitly cannot explain why the visible care has not sufficed for Hinami as it might for herself. Hinami’s family history is a separate evidentiary object, and reader access cannot be transferred to Mimimi. **Disposition: preserve local inferential value and explicit limit; reject identity of mechanism or remedy.** [NM-E22–E23]

### 12.10 Rival: continued love means she has made no progress

The claim assumes that development is measured by the disappearance of feeling. The corpus instead shows changes in disclosure, self-understanding, conduct, and the ability to respect other people’s positions. In V09 she can even say that she likes the self who loves him despite non-reciprocation. That is not emotional closure, but it is incompatible with the claim that nothing has changed. **Disposition: reject stasis; preserve the unresolved duration and future form of feeling.** [NM-P27]

## 13. Current endpoint and open hypotheses

### Established at high confidence

Mimimi’s liveliness is a genuine preference and capacity, not merely camouflage. She is seriously competitive, and V02 links some of her suffering to making first place certify the person rather than only the result. Her friendship with Tama is reciprocal and particular; it both supports her and exposes a vulnerability when being needed seems to diminish. Her running story establishes process pleasure, a specific competitive success, and a local stopping choice without ending ambition. Her love for Fumiya develops over time, remains after non-selection, and is accompanied by real but incomplete efforts to distinguish feeling from entitlement. By V11 she contributes new interpretation and initiates practical work rather than functioning only as emotional support. [NM-E01–E27]

### High-medium hypotheses

A recurring vulnerability is not simply dependence on other people but a difficulty allowing herself a place when she is neither outperforming nor supplying a distinctive service. That formulation is stronger when applied to role-loss and comparative states than as a universal motive. A plausible positive trajectory would preserve ambition and sociability while making received care, ordinary pleasure, finite availability, and nonexclusive significance easier to accept. The current source provides examples that move in that direction; it does not establish that such a trajectory will occur or that one arrangement would solve it.

Her increasing ability to name differences—between specifications and self, feeling and conduct, support and complete understanding—may make future corrections more available. It may also coexist with repeated mistakes when her own standing is threatened. The evidence supports the availability of reflective resources, not their infallible deployment.

### Open

The source does not settle how Mimimi will choose a long-horizon future, whether athletics or performance will remain important in that future, how far she can generalize the mother’s cost critique, or what form her love for Fumiya will take. It does not establish a complete rule for renewed private routines, bodily familiarity, or the division of responsibility among Mimimi, Fumiya, and Kikuchi. It does not test the Mimimi/Tama relation under a new equivalent comparison crisis or prove that usefulness no longer governs any part of self-worth.

Hinami’s response to the proposed collective narrative project is also open. The proposal belongs to Fumiya and Kikuchi at the V11 boundary; naming a wider party does not establish every person’s agreement, a completed work, Hinami’s consent, or a successful intervention. Mimimi’s earlier council action is an actual contribution and should not be confused with that still-unexecuted proposal. [NM-E27]

The current endpoint is therefore **expanded range with unresolved self-valuation and future choice**. The admitted literature supports real changes without establishing a completed arc.

**Hypothetical conditions:** later than the V05 training; Tama is happy about a new relationship or competence, and Mimimi has no evidence of danger or exploitation.

The strongest prediction is **mixed feeling rather than obstruction**. The source gives direct support for genuine delight in Tama’s independence, alongside uncertainty about the old helper role. Mimimi might join the new conversation enthusiastically and feel the loss more sharply later when alone. She may look for a new way to share time, but the model does not license an intention to make Tama dependent again. [NM-E07, E16]

**Confidence and falsifier:** moderately strong for the availability of both gladness and role-loss concern, weaker for whether the concern becomes prominent. A specific new shared practice could reduce that threat. Evidence that she consistently supports the change without monitoring her own place would narrow the usefulness-based hypothesis. Evidence of deliberate sabotage would require a substantial revision, not a claim that such conduct was secretly implied by every earlier act of care.

### 15.4 A group repeatedly relies on her to absorb its unpleasant work

**Hypothetical conditions:** after the maternal conversation; the work serves people she values, but the allocation is visibly unequal and begins to interfere with her own wanted activity.

Her concern for group continuity makes initial accommodation plausible. Her competence may let her disguise the strain through humor. Yet the mother’s distinction gives her a reason to question the arrangement rather than interpret smooth outward functioning as proof that her sacrifice is right. Plausible later responses include naming the cost, asking for a narrower role, or continuing while privately conflicted. The source is insufficient to select one confidently. [NM-E08, E24]

**Confidence and limit:** high that these motives can conflict; low-to-moderate on whether she refuses. The V11 use of collaborators is evidence that she need not absorb every component herself. A clear deadline, trusted organizer, accepted redistribution or explicit freedom to leave would materially change the forecast. This probe must not substitute what she *ought* to do for what the evidence predicts she reliably does.

### 15.5 A performance partner freezes in front of an audience

**Hypothetical conditions:** a prepared joint activity that she values; compare Fumiya, with whom she has a practiced comic rhythm, to a relatively unfamiliar partner.

With Fumiya, Mimimi’s attention would plausibly shift toward finding a familiar response he can still make rather than demanding perfect recall. A comic diversion, exaggerated self-error or an ordinary exchange is supported as a class of possible action; reproducing the same joke is not. The prediction concerns using relationally practiced timing under pressure. Her own desire for the performance to be enjoyable remains part of the motive. [NM-E12]

With an unfamiliar partner, confidence falls. She may still manage audience attention, but cannot assume which cue will restore that person’s footing. Their consent to being teased, the nature of the event, and the audience’s response matter. **A failed attempt would not automatically falsify her competence:** it might reveal missing shared history or a hostile field. The model would be challenged more directly by repeated inability to adjust to a partner’s clear negative response under conditions where adjustment was available.

### 15.6 She is offered a more substantial public-commentary opportunity

**Hypothetical conditions:** after the V11 event, someone offers another bounded role and recognizes her delivery skill, but the opportunity now competes with school or another valued commitment.

The observed pleasure in performance and successful novelty make curiosity plausible. They do not establish that she will accept, select commentary as a career, or prefer it to athletics and other interests. She would have relevant experience of learning through collaboration and of a task extending beyond its initial scope. A reconstruction can therefore ask whether the proposed role explains preparation, duration, anonymity, support and division of expertise; it cannot assert that she already has a settled professional negotiation policy. [NM-E24–E25]

**Confidence and abstention:** moderate that recognition and new experience can attract her attention; low on the choice. An offer framed as “only you can save this” may engage a different vulnerability from an invitation that permits refusal. No salary expectation, university direction, vocational identity or future success should be supplied. The evidence establishes a possible capability, not her chosen destination.

### 15.7 Fumiya proposes spending time alone after he and Kikuchi are dating

**Hypothetical conditions:** after the V09 developments; the purpose, Kikuchi’s knowledge and the form of contact must be specified. An ordinary shared task and a concealed romantic-style outing are not equivalent prompts.

Mimimi may want the time and also consider its effects on the couple. She has demonstrated particular restraint, a roughly week-long altered routine, reconsideration in a changed circumstance, and later proximity monitoring. Those facts support deliberation, not a universal yes or a permanent prohibition. The model should not erase her continuing feeling to make the interaction ethically simple. [NM-E17–E19]

**Confidence and limit:** moderate for competing desire and boundary awareness, low for a particular decision absent details. Fumiya’s invitation alone does not resolve Kikuchi-facing questions, and Kikuchi’s earlier respect for Mimimi’s feelings does not authorize every act. Conversely, the mere existence of feeling does not make all one-to-one activity an intrusion. Evidence of an explicit joint rule would take precedence over extrapolating from the existing incomplete practices.

### 15.8 Someone offers her a friend’s private history as a way to help

**Hypothetical conditions:** the friend has not authorized onward disclosure; Mimimi is concerned, has only partial knowledge, and a trusted peer says that knowing more would make assistance effective.

The source supports an urge to understand and a readiness to help; it does **not** establish a reliable Mimimi policy of refusing all unauthorized information. Her acknowledgment of the limits of her Hinami analogy shows epistemic restraint, while the wider history shows that sincere care and intrusive methods can coexist. It would therefore be overconfident to predict either immediate acceptance or exemplary refusal. [NM-E19, E22–E23]

The meaningful forecast is conditional: a concrete, time-sensitive need may draw her toward action; visible subject reluctance or lack of relevance may strengthen hesitation. **Normative distinction:** trust in the messenger is not the subject’s permission. **Literary limit:** that distinction does not itself prove how she will behave. A new scene showing her inquiry into authorization, choice to receive, or decision to limit use would discriminate among possibilities the present corpus leaves open.

### 15.9 A friend clearly refuses her proposed help

**Hypothetical conditions:** the refusal is direct, the friend understands the offer, and the situation is not an immediate physical emergency. The friend may still be making a choice Mimimi thinks harmful.

Mimimi’s V09 conduct makes gentle automatic acceptance an unsafe prediction. She can press, invoke shared history, and make a concealed contradiction discussable; the same repertoire can cross a boundary. Yet that particular episode involved a friend discarding relationships he still valued, not a general right to prevent any change she dislikes. A different refusal might be more intelligible to her and require no further intervention. [NM-E18–E19]

**Confidence and discriminating evidence:** moderate that concern may coexist with persistence, low on whether she actually continues. Her first response could be joking deflection, a serious question, or withdrawal; the relationship and what the refusal threatens matter. A clear statement that no more discussion is wanted would strengthen the ethical reason to stop, but compliance cannot be invented. The model must preserve the difference between a valid judgment of overreach and a behavioral prediction of perfect restraint.

### 15.10 A new person expresses romantic interest in her

**Hypothetical conditions:** after V11; the person is not Fumiya and there is no established history of attraction to them in the admitted corpus.

The monograph cannot responsibly select a yes, no, new romance, or persistence in the old feeling. Her warning against assuming permanent love preserves the possibility of change; it does not predict a replacement partner or a timetable. Her past admiration suggests that effort, directness, particular care and a person’s way of acting can become meaningful to her, but such features are not an algorithm that manufactures attraction. [NM-E10–E16, E27]

A bounded expectation is that the relationship’s history and her actual feeling would matter more than treating the offer as repayment for having been rejected. She may use humor to manage embarrassment, but even that is conditional. **Explicit abstention:** no invented preference hierarchy for an ideal partner, orientation claim beyond the represented attraction, future exclusivity commitment, or assurance that a “better match” will change her heart. This is a case where preserving an open answer is part of reconstructive accuracy.

### 15.11 She gets lost during a low-stakes outing

**Hypothetical conditions:** a safe ordinary setting, no urgent deadline, and an available way to ask directions.

The running story supplies a concrete pattern: she can lose her route despite confidence in moving through familiar life, obtain practical help, and recognize a simpler navigation option afterward. A humorous recovery and willingness to ask are therefore more defensible than predicting either helplessness or flawless planning. Her stated difficulty with direction is a bounded everyday imperfection, not evidence that she lacks general intelligence. [NM-E08, E26; NM-P08–09]

**Confidence and limit:** moderate for practical fallibility and recoverability, low for any exact mistake. An urgent appointment, responsibility for a less mobile companion, or embarrassment before a valued romantic recipient changes the stakes. The source does not establish how she manages those variations. Successful use of a map on a later occasion would not contradict the character; learning and context remain available. The purpose of this probe is to keep mundane behavior in the model without turning one anecdote into destiny.

### 15.12 A public effort receives criticism rather than praise

**Hypothetical conditions:** distinguish useful criticism of an act, humiliating attack on the person, and an assessment by someone whose judgment has become central to her self-worth.

Her serious practice history supports taking a specific technical criticism seriously. Her comic repertoire may make it possible to keep participation going even while embarrassment remains. The V02 comparison state predicts more danger when the criticism becomes evidence that she is fundamentally less special than a salient other; the later process and maternal resources make alternative interpretations available but do not guarantee them. [NM-E02–E04, E08–E09, E12, E22]

**Confidence and falsification:** moderate for this differentiation, low for the immediate display. A smile cannot certify that the criticism was harmless or fully accepted. A sharp objection cannot certify fragile vanity: the comment may be unfair. The strongest prospective test would examine the next action—whether she can separate useful feedback from whole-person judgment, seek clarification, continue a wanted practice, or impose a limit on humiliating treatment. No single public expression settles that question.

### 15.13 What these probes do—and do not—validate

The scenarios expose why a flat trait list is inadequate. “Competitive” does not predict whether she continues after one win. “Generous” does not predict whether she respects the means of helping. “Cheerful” does not distinguish enjoyment from a diverted question. “Insecure” does not explain her explicit confidence in capacities or her V09 statement that she can like the self who loves someone. Each prediction needs the specific state and the object of concern.

Retrospectively, the model has to survive contrary cases: her genuine happiness about Tama’s independence, her refusal of a rematch, the mother’s non-school contribution, accepted comfort, and imperfect intervention methods. These are checks on the account’s breadth, not held-out tests. The author had access to the later scenes while developing the thesis. No score, blind predictive success rate, simulated dialogue accuracy, or reconstruction-release status is claimed.

A future test would need to freeze the era, available knowledge, proposed behavior and disconfirming alternatives **before** opening the relevant unread material. None of the present scenarios licenses admitting V12 or later sources silently. Until that happens, the useful output is a disciplined set of conditional expectations and explicit abstentions—not a completed person who can be made to say or do anything on demand.

## 16. Handoff to neighboring literary responsibilities

This monograph owns Mimimi’s independent developmental and person-level account. It does not replace the required Fumiya/Mimimi relationship synthesis or the ensemble study’s co-equal Mimimi/Tama responsibility. Those works must retain both directions of the relation, ordinary presence, unequal knowledge and cost, and the other participants’ authority. They should also preserve the independent mother/daughter evidence rather than presenting every correction as something supplied by Fumiya.

The effort specialist should carry forward ambition’s coexistence with process pleasure and actual local stopping choices. The social-system and ensemble specialists should distinguish performance skill from supportive fields and from the burden placed on a familiar helper. The care/intervention specialist should keep accurate correction, forceful initiation, later permission and beneficial reception separate. The Japanese specialist should use the new windows as attested local evidence, not as an exhaustive idiolect. None should manufacture a vocation from one successful commentary segment or a permanent romance rule from a particular restraint.

The proposed next collective story still has no observed Mimimi agreement, complete membership, Hinami consent or result merely because she is named among useful contributors. R05’s independently reviewed character pair is complete while R06–R09 remain necessary and R10–R12 remain unpassed. The integration review records this study’s literary/source acceptance and bounded propagation separately from its original authoring handoff and the eventual exact-commit publication checks.
