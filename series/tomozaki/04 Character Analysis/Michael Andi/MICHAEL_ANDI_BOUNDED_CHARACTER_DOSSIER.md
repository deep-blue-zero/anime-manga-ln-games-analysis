# Michael Andi — a received author, an inconvenient observer, and an unavailable interior

This account is accepted at its stated bounded literary scope through the admitted V12 boundary. See the [implementation review](../../08%20Audits%20and%20Manifests/TOMOZAKI_SUPPORTING_CHARACTER_IMPLEMENTATION_REVIEW.md) and [source and review manifest](../../08%20Audits%20and%20Manifests/TOMOZAKI_SUPPORTING_LITERARY_EVIDENCE_AND_REVIEW_MANIFEST.json) for independent acceptance, source returns, corrections and remaining limits. A bounded literary role is separate from a derived reconstruction model or aggregate release.

## 1. Governing account and the independent responsibility earned

Andi first matters to this corpus as an author people read, admire and sometimes misunderstand one another through. V12 makes a more particular responsibility possible: a dead writer is recoverable through **published reception, surviving materials, remembered conduct and other people's explanatory models**, each with a different warrant. The distinction is part of his literary characterization. It lets an author have appetites, jokes, relationships, labor and failures without pretending he has returned to give an authenticated account of why he wrote.

The strongest account is therefore deliberately conditional. The surviving record and converging local reports support a writer who worked with unusual persistence to preserve differences that an easy conclusion would erase. The fish account, night-sea cloths, reported storytelling and visible revisions give that account substance. They do not establish complete access to his mind. His effects also exceed his command: residents adapt what they read, while much later Kikuchi finds uses for his work in a problem he could not have known. A strong account must retain both the labor and the distance. [MA-E01–E08]

**A substantial B dossier is earned at a posthumous, artifact-and-testimony scope.** Earlier mentions alone would justify an important literary-reception section carried by Kikuchi and the relationship/specialist owners. V12 adds several roles and recipients, ordinary contradiction, reported self-theory, an extensive material practice, local consequences and a directly represented revision trace. Reducing those to a wise predecessor who hands the heroine her answer would lose independent person responsibility. Promoting them to a fully witnessed longitudinal biography or an authoritative psychological monograph would outrun the evidence. This dossier owns the harder middle: the person whom the record can support, the limits of each route, and the explanations that remain rival or unconfirmed. [MA-E09; inherited fourteen-witness audit context]

The account is falsifiable. If the ordinary reports are omitted, Andi becomes an implausibly disembodied sage. If local memories are treated as direct interiority, the account becomes omniscient biography. If the manuscript's marks are reduced to a general lesson about empathy, the actual revision is lost. If the final circled verb becomes a general permission to represent others, a document of choice becomes an invented ethical license. Each failure would change the literary evidence, not merely its tone.

## 2. Identity, clocks and knowledge

The source appellation is **マイケル・アンディ / Michael Andi**; this project also uses **Andy** in English. These are retrieval variants for the same reported author, not evidence of multiple people. The Japanese appellation is directly spoken in V12 p0013 P568–571 and p0016 P5. The dossier does not establish whether it is his legal birth name, a pen name, a particular official Roman spelling, or his nationality. English handwriting does not function as a passport. Exact age, birth date, family history, education, dates and mechanism of death, and complete publication chronology remain unknown at this scope. [MA-P01]

Four temporal/access bands must be selected before making a claim:

| Band | Available evidence | Knowledge that cannot be assigned |
|---|---|---|
| Earlier reception in the protagonists' lives | Kikuchi's reading, retelling, private diary and writing practice; Fumiya's mistaken premise, honest reset and later actual reading | Andi's knowledge of these particular future readers or their unshared lives |
| Andi's historical island presence | Named/role-identified witnesses' memories, reported speech, present material traces and the tower exhibits | Complete historical scene access, an independently narrated inner monologue, or every witness's exact chronology |
| V12 inquiry after his death | What the visiting couple hear, see and read; Mitsu's current testimony and bounded loan | Andi's present endorsement of their interpretations, their project or use of his private papers |
| Later unshown life/release | Nothing beyond the admitted endpoint | An eventual contract, estate clearance, public archive, new novel or final authoritative explanation |

The innkeeper reports visits over more than ten years, including stays of months and long hours at a desk in the last year. She also says she does not know whether the last manuscript was completed, and explains her own limited role at the time. That is valuable access and an explicit limit. It is not a complete itinerary. A shopwoman remembers a tall, thin, preoccupied man writing while watching the coast and being seen with a woman; a different shop account describes a woman who helped translate. Those reports do not by themselves identify the women as each other or as Mitsu. A later parallel imagined by Fumiya also does not determine a romantic relationship. [MA-P02/P03]

## 3. Earlier books: reception history before biography

The fourteen-witness coverage context gives a necessary contrast. In V01, apparent shared admiration becomes part of a mistaken relationship premise, followed by Fumiya's admission and a continued possibility of honest conversation. V02 supplies his actual reading and more meaningful shared literary presence. That development concerns two readers and their relation; it is not evidence that Andi intentionally designed the relationship. V03–V07 and the supplements carry Kikuchi's developing account of form, transmission, actual friends, private hopes, writing and audience. Later volumes give her own fictional models and ethical problems. His name can remain influential without every reference becoming an episode in his life. [MA-P09]

The accepted Kikuchi study already owns excited retelling with Goda Sayaka, selected social habitat, private diary material, formal reflection, art-making and the tension between a reader and a represented person. V06.5's actual diary images include an old Andi collection and a wish to share it with Goda. The diary's private voice and visual route are accepted historical evidence for **Kikuchi**, not newly inspected pixels in this author's earlier Kikuchi task and not direct evidence for **Andi's** private life. The early-bucket reviewer did independently view them; that distinct inspection is retained in the receipt. [MA-P09]

This reception history matters positively. It prevents the island from introducing an entirely new universal sage only when a blocked writer needs one. His published works have long supplied attention, pleasures and forms through which people learn to meet. It also makes V12's ordinary anecdotes consequential: Kikuchi arrives with an image assembled from books and limited public appearances, and encounters reports that disturb it. The difference is development in her view of an author, not an authenticated transformation in the already dead man during the present journey. [MA-E01/E02]

No earlier audit row or search miss proves an exhaustive absence of Andi-related material. The inherited selection covers Kikuchi's meaningful rows across all fourteen admitted witnesses and the two V12 island-person rows, with findings from all three buckets. Its function is contextual, not a mechanical appearance census. V05's appended earlier bonus, V07's Special Edition/artbook distinction and V08.5's mixed clocks remain as governed by their original audit routes. A late dossier cannot relocate those units simply to make an influence history linear.

## 4. Ordinary reports that resist the flawless-author portrait

The island witnesses do not tell one perfectly synchronized story. The innkeeper's repeated breakfast fish and habitual claim that he could not write without it are one report. Festival workers add an earlier dislike, followed by continued experiments and attempts to recover the feeling of dislike. The accounts can coexist at different times, but the dossier should not silently turn them into one directly observed continuous biography. The locals themselves begin by correcting a too-simple favorite-food summary. [MA-P03/P04]

The fish account contains several stages in their telling. He initially cannot swallow the raw fish; he returns and tries again; he becomes excited when it tastes good; then he keeps interviewing people who find it ordinary and people who prefer it seared. He varies preparation and seasoning, becomes excited at understanding an ordinary response, and later seeks out people who dislike it. One speaker recalls his reported wish to recover the initial sensation because liking was displacing it. Recovering dislike takes further time and becomes another occasion for excitement. A local finally proposes that perhaps he most hated feeling he had understood. **The acts and quotations are attributed recollections; the final motive is explicitly the resident's hypothesis.** [MA-P04]

The anecdote supports openness to incompatible responses more concretely than an abstract injunction to understand everyone. It does not say that everyone must learn to like the same fish. It preserves sensory aversion and indifference rather than treating pleasure as the final correct perception. It also has cost and social friction: he asks repeatedly, consumes time, experiments and confuses people. The men call him troublesome. Their affectionate recollection does not make the labor effortless, the questions universally welcomed, or the method a law all writers must follow. [MA-E03]

Other accounts are less exalted still. A shopkeeper recalls pranks on a well-liked woman who helped with translation: a live crab placed on her chair, his laughter, and her angry ashtray throw that sends him running. These are the shopkeeper's historical report and the narrator's subsequent imagination, not a current witnessed scene. They preserve mischievous pleasure, a recipient's anger and a reversal in who controls the joke. The anecdote does not establish harmlessness, romantic reciprocity, a habitual license for pranks, or the identity of that woman. It should not become a behavior to imitate merely because the teller remembers it fondly. [MA-P02]

The liquor seller remembers him drinking and saying he had never yet written a novel, only observation records. She cannot explain what he meant and says she heard it as a drunk person's talk. That explicit memory limit is part of the evidence. The statement can be considered a reported self-theory, perhaps self-deprecating or exacting, but none of those motive explanations is confirmed. Published novels and readers' actual pleasure are counterevidence to taking it as a literal bibliographical claim that he produced no fiction. It is also not a complete final manifesto to which every later object must conform. [MA-P02]

## 5. Material observation, a custodian's counterclaim and the cost of differences

The tower gives a different kind of evidence. The couple directly see an old telescope, exhibits, revised-looking lines and star diagrams, the storytelling stand, and a well-maintained space. A fixed position matters to Kikuchi's response; Mitsu complains that it makes cleaning inconvenient and remembers instructions not to move it. The visible objects are direct within the outer fictional world. The past order and its motive are supplied by testimony and present interpretation. A telescope fixed in place does not prove a godlike mind or an exhaustive observational method. [MA-P05]

The inner room contains numerous cloths that at first look like the same dark blue. Tags discriminate nights by weather, season, quarrel, tears, silence and other circumstances. Direct access establishes the array and the written differences, not who experienced every listed event or the full meaning of every tag. Mitsu says he brought cloth from the shop to compare with the sea; she first showed him differences among blues, and he went beyond that into saying no sea was ever the same. Her rueful recollection that she herself once called pieces the same challenges the craft expert as well as the visitors. [MA-P05]

Fumiya imagines the repeated observation and carrying and finds it impressive and frightening. That cost inference has material and reported support, but the exact count of trips and every production step are not independently witnessed. The work did not guarantee a public final manuscript. The continuation remains private in Mitsu's custody, and the visible effort cannot certify either completed artistic success or a morally sufficient method. Persistence is not interchangeable with legitimacy. [MA-E04]

These details support an independent relation rather than a one-directional master-teacher chain. Mitsu offers a distinction, Andi reportedly takes it farther, and she remembers the resulting nuisance and surprise. She now maintains access and objects, complains about their inconvenience, and corrects visitors' assumptions. The writer's archive relies on another person's continuing ordinary labor. Fumiya's thought that she may have been a bag-carrier-like presence for Andi is a literary analogy from him, not authenticated mutual standing. No wife, lover or shared romance follows. [MA-P05]

## 6. Story nights and appropriation: influence without total authorship

Mitsu refuses Kikuchi's initial neat theory that Andi united the island by creating one myth everyone could believe. Her account preserves people continuing to quarrel about language, color and prayer, while sitting together on particular nights to await a story's continuation. Shared listening is a meaningful change, but not a comprehensive reconciliation. Fumiya's imagined tableau of the listeners is his visualization after her report, not an additional historical witness. [MA-P05]

Her account of the festival likewise starts before his fiction. A woman privately folds after her husband's death at sea; elders cross at low tide for reasons not all disclosed. Andi observes over years and composes a scene. Different residents say it is their story; young people later bring selected elements into a procession; subsequent participants add, remove and retain what they like. She recalls him surprised at seeing it. Her statement that he never ordered this is **Mitsu's historical testimony about the practice**, not a direct census of every utterance or a general theory of authorial innocence. [MA-P06]

The directly observed V12 occasion makes plurality concrete. Children enjoy it without full origin knowledge. A storm destroys objects; ordinary work resumes with damaged and new birds, fewer intact lanterns and reduced food. On Mio Island, elders pray, young people take photographs and children play. An elder says debating the place's meaning causes conflict while placing birds permits shared action. A usable occasion can survive divergent meaning. That is a smaller and more defensible achievement than one author creating consensus. [MA-P06]

Mitsu's personal reception gives another scale. A character modeled on her keeps her bad mouth and stubbornness, yet she reports liking herself a little after reading. The self-report belongs to her; the narrative does not authenticate every detail of the original portrait or show prior permission. When Kikuchi proposes an explanation for why the story reached people, Mitsu does not certify it. Her testimony supports a possible benign effect of being represented without proving that any accurate-seeming portrayal is authorized. [MA-P06]

Andi consequently has effects on people who can answer back, alter things and make their own uses. Those recipients deserve independent agency. They are not all evidence of an infallible author successfully implementing one hidden plan. The festival's disorder, continued disagreement and ongoing local work also make room for effects whose causes exceed a single writer. [MA-E05]

## 7. The private continuation: what the document directly shows

Mitsu provides access to an aged handwritten continuation titled **『星々の軌跡　下巻』** with a next-morning return condition. The pages are in English, difficult and partly illegible; Kikuchi types passages into phone translation and Fumiya reads with her. The narrative identifies the handwriting and continuation as Andi's. That is the outer fiction's direct represented document, with mediated reading, rather than an original English literary archive independently available to us as a separate witness. The dossier has no basis for retrotranslating a complete original sentence or granting the couple perfect English fluency. [MA-P07]

The documentary sequence must remain exact:

| Order | Represented English verb | Japanese meaning supplied in the narrative | Documentary mark / exact all-P route |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **believe** | 信じる | First choice then red deletion/cross-out; p0021 P267–274 |
| 2 | **tell** | 伝える | Replacement then red deletion/cross-out; P278–284 |
| 3 | **record** | 記録する | Replacement then red deletion/cross-out; P287–296 |
| 4 | **wish** | 願う | Replacement then red deletion/cross-out; P301–312 |
| 5 | **pray** | 祈る | Final choice circled; P322–329 |

The **first four are crossed out; the last is circled**. The sequence is **believe → tell → record → wish → pray**. These exact documentary marks constrain the interpretation. Other language elsewhere would require its own actual speaker and context rather than being substituted for the words on these pages. [MA-E06; MA-P07]

The marks directly support revision and a selected last term **on these pages**. They do not authenticate why each choice failed, whether this was his only sequence, a universal final doctrine, or his last thought before death. Their literary force increases when the couple compare them with the questions already troubling them, but that very comparison requires attribution. The manuscript is not the same kind of evidence as Nagisa's wholly unavailable final standpoint, yet neither route supplies an unrestricted posthumous interior. [MA-E06/E07]

## 8. The couple's psychological reconstruction remains theirs

After the fish and cloth accounts, Kikuchi proposes that Andi sought to stop being only himself, relinquish his particular perception and approach a world before language distorts it. Fumiya calls that godlike. The hesitation, analogy to Alcia and immediate relevance to her own block identify this as their developing explanatory model. It is not Andi's newly recovered declaration. Their first model also proves insufficient for Kikuchi: observing more does not decide what to write when the actual Nagisa cannot be asked. [MA-P05/P08]

The manuscript produces a later interpretation. Fumiya associates the deleted terms with answers they have considered; Kikuchi reconstructs a writer who could not wholly remove himself and who let a remaining wish enter as prayer. This is an important psychological and artistic account **offered by the couple**, especially Kikuchi. It may fit the marks, plural observations and material history. It remains a hypothesis about an unavailable person, not authenticated access to his interior or proof that every stage of his actual practice followed the same progression. [MA-P07/P08]

The distinction gives the document more, not less, analytical force. They have something concrete that can disturb their current model: **record** is itself deleted before the eventual **pray**. A theory of pure transcription cannot simply absorb that mark. Yet the surviving final choice also does not prove that the actual writer considered prayer morally sufficient, expected future therapeutic effects, or authorized these particular readers to use his private paper in a public argument. The strongest claim is that the document enables a more adequate owned choice for Kikuchi. It does not outsource her responsibility to him. [MA-E07]

Mitsu's costly folding also enters Fumiya's comparison in the manuscript scene. His association between her actions and prayer is an inference; she has not supplied a general self-theory to that effect. The dossier therefore refuses a convenient chain in which Andi's final word, Mitsu's labor, Kikuchi's manuscript and Hinami's response become one universally endorsed doctrine. Each person has a different standing, clock, act and available voice. [MA-P07]

## 9. Wants, limits and recipient variation

The record supports several **reported** wants: continued work, returning to the island, tasting and recovering different responses, describing a changing sea, and reading continuations to listeners. Their hierarchy is not known. A habitual breakfast phrase can be practical ritual or social exaggeration; the observation-record statement may be drunk self-criticism, exacting ambition or something else. The final circled verb establishes a selection within one document without resolving every prior want. The dossier should preserve this incompleteness instead of constructing a flawless motive tree. [MA-P02–P07]

Different recipients reveal different dimensions:

| Recipient/domain | Evidence and access | What it can support | What it cannot support |
|---|---|---|---|
| Innkeeper | Retrospective daily-work/breakfast memories and admitted limits | Repeated stays, work ritual and long desk hours | Complete writing chronology or knowledge of completion |
| Fish workers | Plural recollections, corrections, reported quotations | Persistence through liking/ordinary/dislike; inconvenience and surprise | Direct interior or a rule to erase everyone's aversions |
| Shopkeeper/translation helper | Reported prank, anger and reversal | Humor, mischief, social fallibility and a working relationship | Recipient's permanent consent, romance or verified identity as Mitsu |
| Liquor seller | Remembered drinking speech with an explicit memory gap | A reported observation-record self-theory | A final complete artistic manifesto |
| Mitsu | Shared material-history testimony, model-recipient testimony and present custody | Craft exchange, nuisance, reception and custodial dependence | Marriage, legal estate authority or total mutual interiority |
| Historical listeners/local readers | Mitsu's account plus present independent practice | Shared listening and selective appropriation | Universal harmony or perfect author control |
| Kikuchi/Fumiya as later readers | Their actual reading, mediated document access and psychological model | Consequential reception and their own revised choices | Andi's endorsement of them or knowledge of their future subjects |

A tendency toward patient observation, if inferred, has a competing face: great expenditure of work, repeated demands, an unreleased continuation whose completed state is unestablished, and a practice that another person has to preserve. It must not become proof that the author was emotionally flawless, socially harmless, or always right to continue. His joking can be welcome in one witness's memory while another recipient was actually angry. His wish to understand can coexist with the final continuation remaining private. The available record is most human where these tensions remain. [MA-E02–E04]

## 10. Private source, custody and the limits of later use

The continuation is **unpublished private source within the story**, not a public declaration. Mitsu's act gives particular access and a next-morning return obligation. The visitors read through translation and return the borrowed pages by leaving them behind the shop counter after waiting; no hand-to-hand receipt is shown. Neither her custody nor their reading establishes legal editorial rights, onward circulation rights, permission to publish the continuation, or permission to fictionalize any modeled subject. [MA-P07]

This matters beyond formal ownership. A dead author's inability to answer a psychological reconstruction is not evidence of agreement. A custodian's generous act is not a proxy declaration from him on all future uses. The narrative permits readers to compare the represented document with Kikuchi's problem; it does not show the fictional couple entering a publication agreement or receiving a general ethics waiver. Their actual use as learners and the dossier's literary interpretation are distinct from claiming a right over the papers. [MA-E08]

Likewise, the usefulness of Andi's model must not certify Nagisa's actual wish. Andi leaves a represented document and plural witnesses; Nagisa's final standpoint remains expressly unknown, including after the later novel has affected Aoi. The fictional/ruby reunion supplies an authored address within reading, not real last testimony. A comparison between these limits can sharpen the ethics of interpretation without equating their very different kinds of surviving evidence. [MA-P08]

## 12. Bounded comparison domains and current abstentions

These domains constrain literary comparison. They are not scenario outputs, an idiolect generator or a reconstruction model.

| Domain | Defensible use | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Later readers encountering an admired author | Compare their earlier received image with ordinary attributed reports | Do not turn a reader's correction into a newly witnessed change in the dead person |
| Retrospective motive inference | Weigh direct marks, reported speech, material arrays and witness gaps separately | Do not speak from Andi's authenticated interior or collapse all accounts into one doctrine |
| Observational labor and incompatible responses | Preserve liking, ordinary response, dislike, context and repeated effort | No universal training requirement or guarantee of empathy |
| Shared story and community practice | Compare a common occasion with divergent interpretation and independent adaptation | No complete ritual history, abolition of conflict or total authorial causation |
| Material archive access | Distinguish seeing, reading through translation, bounded borrowing and return | No editorial, circulation, publication or estate authority inferred |
| Private manuscript revision | Use actual believe/tell/record/wish/pray marks to test an interpretation | Do not infer a final testament, complete revision chronology or general permission |
| Historical relationship with Mitsu/others | Retain reported exchange, irritation, care and labor | No invented family, romance, sexual relation or full biography |

The current state is not a living author poised for a new answer. It is a **record available to later people under conditions**: public books already received, local memories with gaps, an archive actively cared for, and a private continuation briefly loaned. His authentic interior, full intentions, exact relationship to each witness and the future legal/public status of the papers remain OPEN. The literary achievement of the dossier is to make those limitations usable while keeping the substantial surviving person evidence visible.
