# Ashigaru — competing, making a scene, and taking work seriously

This account is accepted at its stated bounded literary scope through the admitted V12 boundary. See the [implementation review](../../08%20Audits%20and%20Manifests/TOMOZAKI_SUPPORTING_CHARACTER_IMPLEMENTATION_REVIEW.md) and [source and review manifest](../../08%20Audits%20and%20Manifests/TOMOZAKI_SUPPORTING_LITERARY_EVIDENCE_AND_REVIEW_MANIFEST.json) for independent acceptance, source returns, corrections and remaining limits. A bounded literary role is separate from a derived reconstruction model or aggregate release.

## 1. The individual responsibility

Ashigaru wants a competitive scene to remain interesting, wants people to encounter its players, and makes practical exchanges to bring that about. He competes, explains, introduces, pays, recruits, jokes and performs. The advice Fumiya receives is only one result of those activities. A professional's own need for an audience and new participants, his playful pressure on acquaintances, and his report of learning to care about admirers require an individual account. Otherwise a person doing work and pursuing ends becomes a convenient device who appears whenever the protagonist needs a better theory.

The strongest bounded account is that Ashigaru connects competitive craft with a public occasion. Skill matters to him, but a single event asks for more than repeatable execution, and a public career asks for more than a score. He can recognize superior technical play without surrendering the importance of the set he wins. He can turn someone else's unexpected voice into an attraction and then do the complementary explanatory work. He can offer practical care without treating everyone's time as free. These capacities are consequential, but they also contain pressure, tactical recruitment and a taste for other people's embarrassment. His own theory and observed conduct therefore need to be kept in conversation. [AS-E01–E08]

**A substantial B dossier is justified.** The fourteen-witness retrospective context establishes meaningful positive bands in V08–V12 and unknown review-limit cells elsewhere. The unknown cells are not absence findings. Across the positive record he appears as opponent, professional adviser, conversational peer, adult intermediary, recruiter, commentator, recipient of fan belief and actual school guest. Multiple recipients and competing costs make an individual home more reliable than a mentor paragraph. At the same time, his private life, complete career and interior remain too thinly supplied for a principal-scale longitudinal monograph or a broadly predictive reconstruction. This dossier owns the supported roles and tensions without manufacturing biography. [AS-E12]

## 2. Identity, era and knowledge

The attested public handle is **足軽 / Ashigaru**, with あしがる ruby. His legal name, exact age, education, household, romantic status, employer, employment terms and full financial history are not established here. He directly reports that he also works an ordinary job and sometimes gives talks in that capacity (V11 p0030 P23), but the job is not named. His main competitive character in the V08 test is Lizard. A nostalgic response to Buin establishes a particular taste and memory, not an exhaustive favorite-game hierarchy. [AS-P01/P06/P09]

His evidence has distinct clocks and warrants:

| V08 gathering and test | Direct play, conversation, a small agreed penalty and professional route advice | One set is not a career outcome, a full personality test or a universal rank hierarchy |

Fumiya supplies the dominant observational access. An unreadable flat expression or a dry tone is evidence about what Fumiya sees, not proof Ashigaru has no feeling. Conversely, Ashigaru's confident interpretation of someone else is direct evidence that he advances the model, not direct evidence that it exhausts that person's mind. His own V11 history is explicit self-report about changing reasons. It is more than an inference from his professionalism, but it is not a complete independently witnessed career chronology.

## 3. V08: taking the opponent seriously without making one result everything

Fumiya requests professional tournament rules; Ashigaru asks why. Hearing that he wants to know what competing with a professional would mean, Ashigaru agrees to give his full effort. The encounter does not start as an unsolicited lesson imposed on an unwilling novice. The request comes from a highly skilled player, and the first game demonstrates it. Ashigaru loses, acknowledges the quality of Fumiya's play and jokes that praise sounds odd from the player currently behind. His ability to recognize that evidence is part of his own competitive conduct. [AS-P01]

He then distinguishes the same rules from the same conditions. A small embarrassment penalty makes the set matter in a new way. He includes his own embarrassing history, asking Harry to hold it available just as Hinami holds Fumiya's. The wager is locally accepted, though Fumiya feels carried along and fears what might be disclosed. It is not ethically weightless merely because its stakes are lighter than a professional tournament. The actual disclosed anecdote is used for further teasing at departure. [AS-P01/P03]

The fifth game separates an opponent's internal reasoning from Ashigaru's read. Under pressure Fumiya first chooses a higher-return route to escape a sustained uncomfortable state. Later, facing a decisive hit, he declines a practiced throw technique with a reported success rate above ninety percent because the remaining failure risk has become intolerable. He chooses guard; Ashigaru predicts it, grabs and holds him in the falling attack. The result is a directly narrated loss. Ashigaru's postgame explanation is his reported reasoning; Fumiya explicitly recognizes the pressure account as describing what he had not noticed in himself. That reception supports the local diagnosis of a decision process without making it a clinical diagnosis or a rule for every risky move. [AS-P02/P03]

Ashigaru does not use the win to claim complete superiority. He says that over a hundred games Fumiya would probably win more, and identifies better movement, execution and adaptation. The hundred-game estimate is **his prospective comparative judgment**, not a completed hundred-game experiment. The actual later free-play record is Fumiya's approximate impression, with caveats about character choices. Neither should be mistaken for a controlled ranking. What the set does establish is that one consequential encounter and long-run repeatable performance are different evidence objects. [AS-P02/P03]

He wins partly by attending to this particular opponent's earlier conversation and changed policy. That is situated strategic work, not proof of mind-reading. The recognition also need not annihilate pride: Fumiya's strong technique remains valuable while a new task is identified. Ashigaru's refusal to erase it is as important as his ability to punish the guard. A generic “the adult proves the child weak” summary would lose both the professional's judgment and the experience the scene constructs. [AS-E02]

## 4. A dream's practical route, and the limits of the adviser

The postgame conversation names professional requirements that a game ranking does not supply: public presentation, adapting when patches change a character, producing under unfamiliar conditions and sustaining a livelihood. When Fumiya asks about abandoning university for full-time play, Ashigaru does not make determination sufficient. He discusses dependence on parents, concrete numbers, practice time, uncertainty and the possibility that leaving home merely replaces study with paid work. He treats other people's support as a real stake rather than an obstacle to be overcome by moral intensity. [AS-P03]

His memorable claim that a dream needs a realistic process therefore need not be read as hostility to desire. He shares the attraction of living through games and smiles at its possibility. The wish matters enough to need a route. Nor does he decree one correct university decision: he names competing considerations and says immediately going full-time seems difficult. This is situated advice from an experienced participant in a changing occupation, not a current external labor-market survey or a universal rule every aspirant must follow. [AS-P03]

The dossier must not turn his discussion into facts about Fumiya's future income, family consent, tournament standing, sponsors, sustainable audience or exit plan. Those remain open later. It also must not invent Ashigaru's own salary, sponsorship arrangements or university history to explain why he gives the advice. The direct self-report that he also has another job arrives in V11 and cannot be assigned to Fumiya's earlier knowledge without a shown route. The advice can be adequate at V08's information boundary without being the last word on either person's life.

## 5. V09: a forceful interpretation of confidence, with a limited warrant

Ashigaru's V09 confidence argument begins from an actual discrepancy: Fumiya calls himself weak while readily changing a highly successful game character and an already tolerable way of life. He asks what confidence means, rejects Fumiya's answer that it must rest on proof, and offers a striking reverse account. A person who can relinquish results and move without a guarantee may possess a confidence the proof-dependent person lacks. He explicitly notes that change can be regression as well as improvement. The argument is more precise than generic encouragement to believe in oneself. [AS-P04]

He gathers information in dialogue. Has Fumiya met other professionals? How did the earlier set compare with aggregate performance? Why change a main while already at the top online? His inference relies on those answers and the career/gaming context he has seen. The speech then expands into the provocative life-level **strong-character** label. The force of his claim should remain visible; attribution is not a reason to rewrite him as having offered only a timid observation. Its evidentiary status nevertheless remains an experienced observer's explanatory model, not an objective ontology of human tiers. [AS-P04]

The distinction is tested by later evidence. Fumiya's readiness to change is real, but choosing a change does not prove it will help others, and V11's collapse after losing a unique relational warrant limits a claim of global invulnerability. The narrower reusable finding is a strong capacity to revise methods and identities under some conditions. It does not establish that he will never need received care, that social relationships are replaceable game assets, or that Ashigaru has resolved every source of self-worth. The later limit concerns the scope of the model; it does not erase the actual main-character change or make the V09 observation worthless. [AS-E04]

The setting supplies another limit on adult competence. Fumiya invites Ashigaru to a later meeting with Rena as a local buffer, and Ashigaru agrees if their schedules fit, partly finding the situation amusing. His presence gives another participant and perspective; it does not prevent every subsequent sexualized intrusion in the scene or establish a durable boundary policy. Neither their shared gaming community nor his useful advice vindicates Rena's conduct. Ashigaru is a participant with limited standing, not an omnipotent safeguard. [AS-P04; DR09 §5.5]

## 6. Relationship talk as conversation rather than a final definition

In the earlier V09 discussion, Ashigaru moves from Rena's account of constraint to a team-game analogy: lovers may enter domains friends ordinarily cannot and share some responsibility for their lives. He initially speaks in a language Fumiya immediately finds intelligible while Hinami and Rena tease them for needing translation into ordinary Japanese. His enthusiasm for the analogy and his interlocutors' reactions make the exchange personal and comic, not a philosophy lecture detached from the room. [AS-P05]

Fumiya does not fully adopt the model. He describes parallel individual contests in which each person owns choices, even within a valued relationship. Ashigaru understands the alternative, recognizes some of himself in gamer individualism, and says he is not as extreme. That explicit difference prevents projecting every statement about Fumiya back into an identical Ashigaru self. He can identify with a structure and still claim another relationship to it. [AS-P05]

His sharp suggestion that Fumiya may be ill-suited to romance is consequential, as is his later refusal to call the individualism inherently bad. He distinguishes minority practice from moral wrongness and predicts that unequal desires for closeness can hurt people. The eventual word **業** is an attributed account of a gamer's cultivated orientation, not a diagnosis or unavoidable fate. The dialogue leaves room to ask whether the team metaphor gives enough protection to consent and difference; it does not grant automatic interference rights merely because two people are dating. [AS-P05]

No evidence here settles Ashigaru's own romantic status, partners, history or complete governance of intimacy. His statement that the model is personally applicable gives some self-positioning, not a license to create those facts. The important person-level evidence is that he listens, reformulates, recognizes a partial resemblance and still makes strong judgments whose scope can be contested. It is not that every line he speaks is the series' moral law. [AS-E05]

## 7. V10: a professional network, ordinary pleasure and a useful contradiction

Before Endo arrives, Ashigaru says he deliberately gave him a meeting time half an hour later so that he could first hear the unfamiliar request himself. Ashigaru introduces Endo as a working game developer with relevant experience. Some of the employment detail is his report; Endo supplies his own description and practical constraints. When the initial private-game request is infeasible, Ashigaru presses Endo to find an idea anyway. Endo responds that this is familiar behavior from him. The scene gives a useful tension: the advocate of realistic routes can make an unreasonable request of another professional and rely on that person's expertise to constrain it. His network is active cooperation with friction, not a supply cabinet he can empty on the students' behalf. [AS-P06]

Endo retains the right and ability to say what can be done. Mizusawa produces the eventual exchange involving promotion. Ashigaru does not personally perform all the technical and negotiating labor, and his introduction does not transfer rights over Endo's time or judgment. The particular barter and follower target are real route evidence for Fumiya, but neither Ashigaru's presence nor his approval proves a viable career or a complete commercial contract. The dossier need not adjudicate an external legal regime to keep these shown responsibilities distinct. [AS-P06/P07; DR10 §4.4]

His reaction to Buin adds ordinary pleasure that has explanatory force. He becomes animated, praises the game's play and story, and recalls the pig character's catchphrase as cute. The teenagers' simultaneous shock then puzzles him. He shares a game memory but lacks their school-specific use of the phrase and the hidden history motivating the gift. The professional gamer is also a fan, and shared taste does not make every participant equally informed. [AS-P06]

After the negotiation he quietly pays the café bill while ostensibly leaving for the bathroom, despite the students' attempt to cover it. Fumiya finds this adult generosity exasperatingly deft. It is an actual small material act, not proof he always pays, is wealthy, or has no boundary about work. The same adult who can absorb a particular bill later bargains for reciprocal professional contributions. [AS-P07]

His invitation to Mizusawa is also specific. He praises the negotiation, refuses the teenager's easy dismissal as empty polite talk, gives a card and offers future contact if he still lacks a direction after university. That is stronger than generic encouragement and narrower than a present job offer. Mizusawa's response is visible, but his full thoughts remain unavailable. A different recipient's skill has caught Ashigaru's attention independently of Fumiya's need for a mentor. [AS-P07]

## 8. V11: building an event and negotiating what he wants

At the tournament venue, Ashigaru is already needed by other people. Staff call him to the opening commentary before the students can finish their request. He knows players, adjusts a headset and addresses an audience with a brighter voice. Fumiya observes knowledge and humor working together. The public role is skilled labor, not simply a more authentic hidden self than the flat conversational manner offstage. His professional usefulness exists beyond this particular school proposal. [AS-P08]

He jokes about introducing his handle to a schoolgirl and about Fumiya's apparent abundance of female company; the gaming/internet register does not land equally with Kikuchi and Mimimi. Later he asks whether he may tell a publicly embarrassing incident on stream, and Fumiya emphatically refuses. The source does not show him subsequently broadcasting it. A taste for embarrassment, an actual permission request and an actual refusal can all remain visible without a blanket harmless-joker or privacy-violator verdict. [AS-P08/P09]

The school speaking role is requested and discussed as work. Ashigaru reports experience giving talks through gaming and his other job. Rather than take a fee from an aspiring junior, he asks for reciprocal contributions. His own stated purpose is to refresh the competitive scene: repeated top names can lose casual viewers, and he wants new wind. Fumiya's tournament debut serves that purpose. Mimimi's clear, varied, carrying voice suggests another resource; he also names the scarcity of women active in that scene. These are his professional aims, not merely the protagonist's lesson disguised as altruism. [AS-P09]

He redirects Mimimi's protest about not knowing the game into a novice-commentary format, explains the premise and asks for one game with him. A no-face condition is explicitly stated on stream. She does perform, their contrasting responses entertain the room, and the audience/channel response is reported as favorable. But the cost is not erased by success: the initial resistance and asymmetry remain, the event continues beyond the first game, and he later apologizes for keeping her involved. That mixture supports professional judgment and opportunistic recruitment more honestly than frictionless benevolence. It also makes the limits of a particular image/voice agreement recoverable. [AS-P09]

The **actual V11 performance** is the community commentary, Fumiya's first offline tournament and their interactions there. The **school guest role** is still an agreed future appearance. At the departure Fumiya tells Haruka that the guest seems likely to be secured. Collapsing those clocks would lose the difference between recruiting someone and doing the organizing work needed for the event. [AS-P09/P10]

## 9. A reported change of reason: receiving admirers' belief

When Fumiya is dissatisfied with fourth place, Ashigaru brings over Shimaaji, a younger participant who already speaks to him at tournaments and admires nanashi's play. He had told the boy in advance that Fumiya would probably enter, before that entry was formally shown. This is deliberate scene-making; it is not an accidental appearance of a fan exactly when advice needs confirmation. The boy has his own testimony about starting offline play and hoping to compete with Fumiya. He is not simply a manufactured compliment. [AS-P10]

The encounter demonstrates that a viewer can value a player's decisions and developing route despite a nonwinning finish. It does not prove every viewer agrees, that fourth place is commercially sufficient, or that Fumiya has a sustainable audience. Ashigaru's broader model is that a professional becomes a story to which other people attach themselves. His examples preserve strength as a prerequisite while arguing that strength alone cannot explain why people invest. This is his professional theory, supported locally by the encounter and actual commentary response; it is not a universal theory of spectatorship. [AS-P10]

Kikuchi then asks why he wanted to become professional. Ashigaru answers by challenging the idea that one reason must remain fixed. He reports initially thinking of himself as selfish, caring about his own wins rather than defeated opponents. As recognition grew, admirers said they began playing or pursued Lizard because of him. He recalls being unexpectedly glad and embarrassed at finding that moving. Later, when experience tells him a difficult position may be hopeless, remembered faces and belief can help him continue. [AS-P10]

That is a substantive developmental band **in his own retrospective self-account**. It should not be narrated as an independently documented conversion from selfishness to pure altruism. Competition and his own scene-building goals persist. The account nevertheless gives him more than expert competence: he has been changed by receiving something he did not initially identify as his motive. He describes becoming an example and being sustained by admirers as a reciprocal possibility. His final suggestion is that a reason can be discovered after beginning, not a guarantee that any leap will produce one. [AS-E09]

The comparison with Hinami belongs to the younger listeners afterward. It does not establish that she is literally already employed as a professional, that she owes admirers an indefinite flawless performance, or that Ashigaru knows the private family mechanism the reader later learns. The same general structure can support him and burden another person under different conditions. A literary account needs that difference to keep both people intact.

## 10. V12: an actual guest, neglected preparation and an unshown match

The promised school appearance finally occurs in V12. Fumiya directly narrates that his and Mimimi's minds have been occupied by Kikuchi's novel and that their acting-council preparation has been left untouched. The guest is chosen; what the guest will do has not been settled. A monitor and projector have been arranged as a fallback. That is actual neglected responsibility, not merely private anxiety that everything may be insufficient. [AS-P11]

Ashigaru speaks and gets the gym laughing. The fragment heard is his own anecdote that a particular uploaded video left that month's advertising revenue at zero. It is professional testimony presented to an audience, not an audited account of his entire finances, proof every month was unpaid, or current evidence for Fumiya's earning prospects. The performance also shows timing: he leaves a beat before the room laughs. We can describe the observed delivery without claiming when he wrote or rehearsed the talk. **Whether he improvised it is unestablished.** [AS-P11]

The practical consequence is that his performance carries an underprepared event. That cost cannot be paid retrospectively by the novel's benefit to Hinami. Nor is every part of his labor a one-sided free gift: V11 already established an exchange, and the students actually provided tournament participation/commentary. Both points matter. A reciprocal agreement does not excuse neglecting the host's remaining preparation, and negligence does not prove a quantified fee loss or a permanently damaged professional relationship.

He then says he has heard some games may be played and calls for the school's aspiring professional. Fumiya expects to face him; Ashigaru instead proposes the pairing he wants to see with Aoi. This is another act of programming an occasion, with public pressure and actual subsequent participation. It is not an established therapeutic intervention backed by knowledge of Aoi's private chapter, or a demonstration of comprehensive prior consent. Her response, peer encouragement and chosen stage approach remain distinct actions. [AS-P11]

The source reaches Fumiya and Hinami's character selection and shared story-bearing play offer. Ashigaru's proposal does not determine the winner; the final match and result are unshown. Nor does he choose every meaning the players make of it. His professional contribution is a public situation and a pairing; they make their own subsequent choices within it. Fumiya's later expressive purpose can draw from Ashigaru without becoming proof that the mentor authored the entire ending. [AS-P11; DR12 exit]

Several rivals fail at this bounded scope. **Omniscient sage** loses the uncertainty, forceful overgeneralization and other people's independent choices. **Only a mentor prop** loses his scene-building wants, self-reported change and material work. **Cold industry operator** loses ordinary generosity, game pleasure and embarrassment at being moved by fans; **unconditional benevolent adult** loses his unreasonable pressure on Endo, tactical recruitment, extended commentary and taste for embarrassment. **The true measure is winning** loses his own recognition of aggregate strength; **only aggregate strength matters** loses the specific read and public labor he actually performs. **Audience feeling validates everything** loses negotiated permission, friction and the organizers' later neglect.

The dossier supports bounded comparison domains rather than new scenario outputs. A competition comparison must specify stakes, format, information and opponent; a hundred-game estimate is not a measured record. A career discussion can identify kinds of work and dependence without supplying new market facts. A relationship comparison must keep Ashigaru's own model attributable and preserve the other party's refusal or alternative. A community event can compare voice, expertise, new participants and costs, but cannot assume permission to broadcast a person. A fan-recognition comparison can preserve reciprocal motivation without requiring permanent flawless performance. Unknown private biography and unshown future responses remain abstention domains.
