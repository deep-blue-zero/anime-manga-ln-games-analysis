## 4. Calculability is Hinami's competitive strength and a chosen limit

The opening Atafami match provides a compact model of Hinami's effort regime before the volume returns to school life.

Tomozaki deliberately destroys the accumulated read by entering a high-variance position. The resulting state is approximately even in expected value, but its branches are not yet calculable. Hinami responds by retreating in a way that guarantees a modest loss. She prefers a known negative value to a roughly neutral but unmodeled gamble.

This is not simple cowardice or inability:

- Hinami correctly identifies what cannot be modeled;
- she pays a bounded price to restore a calculable state;
- she then wins the individual game by accumulating small, disciplined advantages;
- Tomozaki still wins the five-game set, preserving their broader asymmetry.

The scene therefore clarifies two distinct variables. Hinami's technical improvement and persistence are formidable. Her aversion is not to loss itself but to action whose probability field she cannot confidently represent. She can choose a certain local disadvantage as the cost of returning the contest to her preferred epistemic regime.

Her post-win message, `私の勝ち`, also marks reciprocal competition rather than detached optimization. It is an unrequired boast after her first individual-game victory over Tomozaki. Their shared game remains one of the few spaces in which she visibly wants recognition from him, though V06 does not establish why that recognition matters.

## 5. Tomozaki judges Hinami's method, and Hinami admits revenge

V06 immediately prevents the V05 climax from becoming attractive mystery alone.

Tomozaki accepts that much of Hinami's intervention may have been necessary to stop Konno quickly. He also identifies an exact stopping point: after Konno had cried, lost the room, and ceased resisting, making Nakamura present Yuzu's tissue cover served no protective function. He calls it `死体蹴り`, an attack on someone already defeated.

Hinami does not hide behind efficiency. She agrees that the final blow was unnecessary and says it was revenge. Her explanation is personal:

- she likes Tama because Tama is `正しくて` and preserves herself;
- she found Tama's uncalculated public defense of Hirabayashi beautiful;
- she could not accept a world in which that strength was driven to “I want to run away”;
- she therefore wanted to crush Konno completely.

This both answers and deepens the Tama exception. Hinami can name a value she loves even when it conflicts with her ordinary doctrine that people should alter ineffective conduct. Emotion does not merely energize an existing optimization target; it specifies a non-instrumental end and disables the usual proportional stopping rule.

Tomozaki's challenge is real but not ethically complete. He says purposeless injury is not the right way for `NO NAME`. That formulation reaches Hinami through an identity she respects and makes her hesitate. It also partly internalizes the objection as inconsistency with her own competitive style rather than fully articulating Konno's standing against private punishment. V06 therefore **strengthens** Tomozaki's boundary-setting while leaving room for a more general account of justice.

Hinami's final answer—she too has things she cannot forgive—is neither an apology nor a reconciliation of doctrine. It is, however, explicit ownership. V05's inferred contradiction becomes a conflict she acknowledges in first person.

## 6. The class has political peace, not completed justice

V06 supplies immediate negative evidence against a completed-repair reading of V05.

Konno and Akiyama remain outwardly friendly enough to preserve the group, but each criticizes the other in her absence. Yuzu describes the situation as the world of girls and continues to mediate because she believes she is the person who can do it. No scene shows:

- Konno apologizing to Tama;
- Konno acknowledging the harassment;
- Akiyama accounting for her earlier participation;
- restitution for the damaged keepsake;
- an institutional response or new safeguard;
- restored trust between Konno and Akiyama.

Ordinary class life has resumed, but the conflict has moved from open attack to managed cold war. This is durable enough for collective activity and insufficient for justice.

Yuzu's labor has two simultaneous effects. It prevents a lasting factional expulsion and lets the class proceed into the culture festival. It also absorbs consequences that the responsible actors do not visibly address. Her statement that this is something only she can do establishes chosen responsibility, not costlessness.

Her later election as the overall festival committee chair matters independently. She is not only repairing other people's relationships. She volunteers for public coordination and recognizes her own recent change. Relational effort is becoming broader initiative, even while the maintenance burden remains.

## 7. A self-authored target can remain inside a managed choice architecture

Hinami responds to Tomozaki's earlier demand for owned goals by asking whom he genuinely wants to date. The correction is substantive: she no longer assigns one person simply because the relationship is strategically convenient. Tomozaki receives time to examine his own feeling.

The resulting agency is nevertheless constrained in several ways:

1. the superordinate goal—obtain a girlfriend before third year—remains part of Hinami's curriculum;
2. she defines the initial candidate set, even including partnered Yuzu and herself;
3. she requires two simultaneous targets as a strategy for composure, leverage, and confidence;
4. she frames romantic interest as a field in which visible alternatives increase market value;
5. Tomozaki negotiates time, but not the two-target rule itself.

Hinami does add an important ethical limit. She does not instruct Tomozaki to make two confessions or promise exclusivity to multiple people. Her stated model is to face two people directly and later choose the person he genuinely wants. That is not equivalent to betrayal.

The remaining issue is not whether Tomozaki has literally consented. He has. It is whether his answer is authored under a frame that makes a particular form of answer mandatory. V06 therefore **preserves** negotiated coaching but does not upgrade it to equal curriculum authorship.

## 8. Public self-presentation produces access, with uneven consent

The Instagram quest operationalizes Mizusawa's V05 idea of a stable public character at the level of Tomozaki's own status.

The assignment trains him to:

- initiate outings rather than wait to be included;
- request specific social situations;
- recognize how photographs communicate affiliation;
- maintain a legible position inside Nakamura's group;
- cross from existing ties into casual relations with classmates he had not previously spoken with;
- treat the record of an enjoyable moment as part of participation rather than as proof detached from it.

Several episodes show proceduralization. Tomozaki invites Nakamura's group to the arcade, approaches unfamiliar classmates, proposes group pictures, and later wants a photograph with Kikuchi partly because the moment itself matters to him. An assigned form creates encounters that become sincerely enjoyable. This strengthens the V05 sequence from technique to access to owned interest.

Consent is not uniform, however.

| group photograph | participants knowingly pose; Tama places Tomozaki in the frame | shared participation |

The task is therefore neither pure manipulation nor cleanly transparent practice. Tomozaki is increasingly capable of asking directly when the relationship supports it, and he respects an explicit publication boundary. He still hides the assigned purpose and optimizes other people's behavior in several cases. Bounded agency improves unevenly rather than linearly.

## 9. Derived competence becomes owned culture-festival leadership

The culture-festival arc separates motive origin from skill origin.

Tomozaki first enters the committee partly because Hinami would likely require it and partly because Mizusawa recruits him. Once involved, he acts with techniques learned from Hinami:

- prepare an argument before a public decision;
- identify stakeholder incentives;

- use an accessible intermediary when speaking to a group;
- structure options before voting;
- recruit high-status voices without surrendering the proposal;
- translate specialized or private material for a broader audience.

His manga-cafe proposal succeeds because it joins a conventional school form to a benefit many classmates want. His suggestion of an original play initially comes under pressure from Hinami's demand for maximum initiative, but the decisive project is not assigned. After reading Kikuchi's unfinished story, Tomozaki wants to see her world performed. He asks her first, gives her room to decline, and presents the proposal only after she chooses it.

This is a strong case of derivative method and owned end. The same strategic forms that once executed Hinami's target now carry Tomozaki's desire to help another person's work enter public life.

The risk is overextension. By the end of V06 he is a festival committee member, initiator of the class play, translator/supporter for Kikuchi, and Mimimi's prospective comedy partner. Competence creates invitations faster than the volume tests his capacity to refuse or allocate effort.

## 10. Tama's public character functions as equipment rather than a demonstrated cage

V06 provides the first post-crisis test of Tama's new public legibility.

Several pieces of evidence matter:

- she participates naturally with Mimimi and other classmates;
- the class laughs at her direct refusal of Mimimi's committee invitation before Mimimi supplies a rescuing reaction;
- she can decline participation without social punishment;
- she says she no longer has to overexert merely to remain with everyone;
- she knowingly participates in playful photographs;
- the class chooses her for the central role of Chris because traits once treated as abrasive are now read as innocence and directness;
- she instructs Tomozaki in comic facial expression, reversing the V05 teacher relation.

The crucial evidence is not repetition of the small-stature routine. It is her ability to be direct, refuse, vary her role, and still be received. Her public character appears to have altered the interpretive prior through which classmates hear her rather than forcing a single repeated performance.

This does not establish permanence. The volume covers a short, favorable interval and does not test serious disagreement with the class. Broadened person-specific curiosity is also only partially observed: wider ordinary contact persists, but V06 does not supply a new detailed case in which Tama's curiosity about a previously dismissed person becomes the central action.

## 11. Kikuchi's authorship crosses from private interpretation into public risk

Kikuchi's V05 movement becomes a project with stakes.

She gives Tomozaki five stories she wrote, accepts specific feedback, and explains her aspiration toward Michael Andi's work. The exchange is reciprocal knowledge, not only Tomozaki receiving emotional comfort. He learns how her values organize a created world; she receives evidence that the atmosphere she sought reached another person.

Her unfinished `私の知らない飛び方` then creates a second movement. Kikuchi initially fears choosing an ending because she loves all three central characters. She nevertheless accepts Tomozaki's invitation to turn the story into a class play. Her reason is partly observational: Tomozaki left a solitary world without ceasing to be himself, and she wants to see the wider world too.

Tomozaki's role is accurately described as translation. He cannot author the story or speak for her aesthetic judgment. He can make the project's value legible to the class, reduce the social burden of presentation, and preserve her authority over the script. This repeats the strongest V05 coaching structure in a new domain: support is successful when the recipient owns the end and retains substantive authorship.

Kikuchi's refusal to choose the ending from private preference is analytically important. She distinguishes:

- what she personally wants;
- what the characters' relations support;
- what would be ideal for the world she has made;
- what she owes the work as its author.

The volume does not decide whether that distinction is wise or self-effacing. It does establish that “owned desire” is not the only value relevant to an authored decision. Responsibility to other agents and to the structure one has helped create can constrain personal preference.

## 12. Mizusawa's owned desire persists, but his model is not an ethical endpoint

Mizusawa again acts from a desire he states without embarrassment. He still likes Hinami and intends to make her turn toward him. Rejection has not returned him to detached performance.

His behavior at the girls' school festival has two separable motives:

- flirting and meeting girls is enjoyable to him in itself;
- visible alternatives, confidence, and value signaling also support his longer pursuit of Hinami.

This strengthens the claim that his activity is inhabited rather than merely instrumental. It does not make every method exemplary. His field lesson openly treats pretexts, controlled initiative, scarcity, and social proof as ways to increase another person's interest. Those techniques may enable contact while also structuring asymmetry and incomplete disclosure.

His most consequential intervention is not the demonstration of pickup skill. It is his diagnosis of Tomozaki's self-denigration.

Mizusawa notices that Tomozaki looks relieved when he lowers his own value. Self-deprecation sets a low expectation in advance and makes failure less threatening. It is therefore not only inaccurate self-description; it is a stopping rule that permits non-participation.

He adds a relational correction: devaluing oneself also devalues the judgment of people who care about that self. This converts self-respect from private confidence into responsibility toward others. Tomozaki can no longer treat “nobody would choose me” as harmless modesty once Mimimi's feeling is possible.

## 13. Mimimi's feeling becomes direct evidence; Tomozaki's response remains open

V06 differentiates Mimimi's behavior without making Tomozaki's choice for him.

Before the final declaration, the text supplies a pattern:

- she becomes self-conscious during physical closeness;
- she interprets his invitation to eat as potentially meaningful;
- she responds to shared food with a deliberately intimate countergesture;
- she monitors his work with Kikuchi and asks how it began;
- she reacts to the girls' school visit and Gumi with jealousy-like irritation;
- she explicitly says she respects the way Tomozaki and Tama act from owned conviction.

These signs remain open to several readings until she states that her `好き` is meant in the romantic sense. That statement establishes her feeling at this point. It does not establish a formal request to date, duration, reciprocity, or eventual outcome.

Tomozaki does not answer her. The important change is epistemic: after speaking with Mizusawa, he stops using his low self-concept to classify her feeling as impossible. He states to himself that Mimimi likes him. Acceptance of evidence precedes a romantic decision.

## 18. Rival readings and counterevidence

### 18.1 Rival: Hinami's revenge proves she is becoming authentic

V06 strongly supports short-term durability and role flexibility. It does not test sustained conflict, status loss, or whether future audiences demand repetition.

### 18.6 Rival: Mizusawa supplies the mature answer to romance

His ownership, confidence, and refusal of self-erasure are corrective. His market-value and controlled-pretext model also carries asymmetry and instrumentalization. The text uses him as a teacher, not as an uncontested moral endpoint.

### 18.7 Rival: Kikuchi's story transparently maps the series' final romance

The cast and triangle invite comparison, but the story is unfinished and its author explicitly cannot yet choose. It is evidence about the problem of decision, not a coded outcome warrant.

### 18.8 Rival: Mimimi's declaration means Tomozaki must choose her

It makes her feeling something he must acknowledge. It does not determine reciprocity, timing, response, or the ethically correct relationship.

### 18.9 Rival: self-deprecation is harmless modesty

Mizusawa observes relief, and Tomozaki recognizes its function as insurance against failure and responsibility. The behavior can be socially soft while still operating as avoidance.
