## 7. Hinami's pre-series regime: control, self-proof, and no demonstrated stopping condition

Unit 1 begins after Hinami has already spent roughly a year pursuing change with unusual intensity. It therefore moves the earliest observed point of her deliberate optimization backward, but it does not reveal the initiating event or the source of the emptiness named near the unit's end.

Her middle-school regime is systematic across domains:

- she repeats test preparation until scores become controllable;
- she drills sport and basketball rather than treating ability as fixed;
- she models socially successful answers and status behavior;
- she accepts unfamiliar experiences when they may add a new dimension of competence;
- she treats outcomes as effects with discoverable causes and looks first for an actionable escape route.

This is not effortless perfection. When older basketball players resent her visibility, Hinami does not expect correctness alone to protect her. She apologizes strategically, identifies divergent interests within the opposing group, and promises not to appear in second-year matches. The maneuver preserves social control, but its price is real: she gives up deserved court time despite judging the demand unjust. Calculability can therefore lead Hinami to accept a large certain personal loss rather than tolerate a socially volatile field.

Her brief relationship with Hattori follows the same experimental structure. She accepts his confession because dating appears to be a new stage from which she might learn, not because the unit establishes prior affection. When he attempts to kiss her and treats the couple's combined social value as a source of his own worth, she refuses. She later ends the relationship after deciding it no longer contributes to growth. Her claim to prior sexual experience may be truth or a defensive bluff; the unit supplies no warrant for choosing between them.

The partner she can imagine is not merely high status. She describes something closer to a comrade who faces the same direction and sharpens her through mutual effort. That criterion is compatible with later competitive respect, but it is not evidence that any later person has already been selected romantically.

The most important result comes when first place becomes proof that both her effort and she herself are correct. The achievement supplies an intense reward to an identified emptiness, yet does not fill even its first measure; she immediately searches for the next target. This grounds result/self-worth coupling and the absence of a demonstrated internal stopping condition. It still does not establish why the emptiness exists, whether it is stable across every domain, or what event first produced her effort regime.

## 8. Form can become intuition without losing its learned history

Unit 2 supplies an ordinary maintenance case for the series' learned-form model. During early-second-term winter-clothing shopping, Tomozaki can infer combinations by looking at store mannequins. The judgment feels increasingly intuitive, but its history is repeated exposure and structured practice. Learned form has become perceptual fluency rather than a script he must consciously recite.

Hinami also states a general motivation technique: build an environment in which the target must remain cognitively present. Tomozaki continues to benefit from such external structure. Hinami says her own motivation toward effort does not need the same support. Unit 1 qualifies that difference: high drive does not mean her targets are free of outcome dependence or that she possesses a stopping condition.

The unit therefore supports a proceduralization sequence:

> repeated modeled exposure → faster pattern recognition → apparently intuitive choice

The result is neither proof of an innate fashion sense nor evidence that the acquired judgment is false.

## 9. Kikuchi's “different habitats” model begins as protection after failed translation

Unit 4 gives the formation history behind Kikuchi's relational language. In middle school she sees peer romance and ordinary social activity as a gray world she cannot enter. Reading first functions as refuge, then becomes an inhabited source of value. An adult teacher and librarian, Saya Goda, introduces Michael Andi's `猛禽の島とポポル`, whose othered protagonist reaches a sunset through companions' descriptions. Kikuchi wants that kind of connection and attempts to enter a classmates' game.

The attempt fails because she does not share its rules or conversational register. From that rejection she constructs the “different habitats” account: a fire person or snow woman may harm others simply by entering the wrong environment. The metaphor is psychologically and ethically useful because it protects difference from compulsory normalization. It is not presented as an untouched natural truth. It is also a defensive theory built after a painful coordination failure.

Goda does not force Kikuchi back into the failed group. She instead treats the library as Kikuchi's lake, asks to become her friend there, and reads the same book so that Kikuchi's world can be shared. Recipient fit and a common semantic object create access without requiring universal adaptation.

The four image-only diary pages make the later transition unusually precise:

- **May 3:** Kikuchi begins her second high-school year and asks whether the diary addresses a future self, practices writing, or records something she really wants to tell a friend.
- **June 7:** she finds an old Andi collection, wants to show Goda, and happens to lend Tomozaki a tissue after Yuzu speaks to him; she is too nervous to ask him about Andi.
- **June 11:** she sees Tomozaki visit her workplace with Hinami and wonders whether a fire person—or snow woman—can become close to a human; she associates the problem with Popol's cross-boundary friendships.
- **June 17:** she speaks properly with Tomozaki in the library, tells him she likes Andi, discloses more than intended, and records that she is writing a novel she once hoped an Andi-loving companion might read.

These pages show authorship, shared culture, and direct approach growing together. They also remain private evidence. Tomozaki cannot be treated as knowing the diary's fears, metaphors, or hopes at the time.

## 10. Private romance states develop on different clocks

The anthology supplies several private states that were not available to other actors.

### 10.1 Mimimi

During the V03 summer camp, Mimimi reports no particular romantic interest and cannot imagine either herself or Hinami dating Tomozaki. The narration explicitly marks the later reversal as unforeseeable to her at that point. Her V06 feeling is therefore not a stable preference merely waiting to be disclosed.

Unit 7 traces a later transition. After the Tama conflict, Mimimi admires both Tama's changed social reach and Tomozaki's capacity to design and realize a solution. That admiration also activates comparison: other people appear to be transforming while she experiences herself as an ordinary, decorated shell. Her anxiety is not a simple replay of the V02 rank crisis. It combines uncertainty about intrinsic worth, the loss of a needed support role toward Tama, and fear that Tomozaki's life may change without her.

Running creates a domain she owns for its process as well as its result. She returns to it because the activity itself is pleasurable, sets a personal-record target, shifts attention from escaping Hinami to her own stride, and beats Hinami once in the farewell race. The victory produces real pride, but the text does not present one win as a permanent cure. Mimimi can value non-comparative process while still carrying comparison sensitivity and uncertainty about what fills the self beneath social decoration.

Unit 8 re-presents the V06 gyoza exchange from her side. Her challenge becomes embarrassment when Tomozaki accepts it; her attraction and attention are direct, while Tomozaki's category remains unavailable to her and to the analysis. Mutual awkwardness is not equivalent to reciprocal romantic choice.

Unit 10 begins immediately after her V06 declaration. Mimimi can no longer deny that she loves Tomozaki romantically. The feeling has grown through specific observations—his effort, strength in crisis, changed presence, ties with other girls, and still-recognizable uncertainty—not through Hinami's candidate list. Disclosure does not relieve her. It creates fear of rejection, of losing the existing relationship, and of either pressing too hard or waiting too passively. She now waits for an answer; the supplement supplies no Tomozaki response.

### 10.2 Hinami

During the V03 girls' conversation, Hinami says Mizusawa or Tomozaki would be the plausible choices within the named boys. Her reasons are comparative fit: Mizusawa's competence and similar thinking, and Tomozaki's strictness and competitive orientation. This proves that she could privately model Tomozaki as potentially compatible by V03. It does not establish chosen affection, future intent, or a contradiction with the absence of romance evidence in the numbered state.

### 10.3 Mizusawa

Near the end of summer vacation, Mizusawa tells the bar manager without hesitation that he likes a same-age girl. The final interior passage identifies her as a difficult unknown whose depths he cannot yet measure. His desire for Hinami therefore exists before the later V05-V06 direct pursuit evidence.

At this earlier point, however, he frames waiting as more natural than chasing. That is not lack of ownership; he readily distinguishes casual play from the person he actually likes. It is an earlier action policy. The later decision to pursue, disclose, and accept visible risk is consequently a change in method and presence, not the creation of desire from nothing.

## 11. Yuzu's trust remains maintenance labor; Konno's help is not restitution

Unit 6 occurs during V06 culture-festival preparation. Yuzu becomes anxious when Nakamura repeatedly declines weekend meetings and a rumor places him with another girl. She does not want to interrogate or control him because she wants their relationship to rest on trust. She nevertheless participates in indirect verification through Konno's group.

Konno treats the old rivalry over Nakamura as discussable, takes Yuzu's concern seriously, and offers practical strategy. She uses a friend's account to prompt Nakamura into sending a contextual photograph, notices a woman's phone in it, and recommends later checking Mizusawa rather than escalating directly. Her judgment is socially sharp, and her support of Yuzu is real. Her handling of the friend's phone also preserves the group's visible hierarchy, and the investigation remains a form of covert monitoring.

The apparent threat is Nakamura arranging Yuzu's birthday gift with Mizusawa and female acquaintances. When Yuzu anticipates a breakup and declares that she still loves him, Nakamura reassures her that he has no interest in another girl. The episode demonstrates continuing affection alongside substantial asymmetry in communication and reassurance.

Konno's assistance is evidence of ordinary prosocial capacity and continuity with Yuzu. It is not apology, restitution, acknowledgment of the Tama/Akiyama harm, or repair with those harmed actors. The V06 cold-war judgment therefore stands.

## 15. Rival readings and counterevidence

### 15.1 Rival: Unit 1 finally explains why Hinami became Hinami

It explains an early operating regime and names an emptiness. It begins after intense effort is already underway and does not identify the emptiness's cause. Formation evidence is not a complete origin story.

### 15.2 Rival: V06.5 should be read as one post-V06 bridge

Nine of its ten units are retrospective, concurrent, or interstitial by their own dates and event dependencies. Only Unit 10 is directly post-V06.

### 15.3 Rival: Hinami's middle-school relationship proves she cannot love

The episode shows instrumental entry, a consent boundary, aversion to borrowed status, and exit when learning value disappears. A single adolescent relationship cannot establish an enduring incapacity.

### 15.4 Rival: Mimimi likes Tomozaki only because she needs someone to compare herself with

Comparison and fear of being left behind are part of the transition, but she names person-specific features, shared interactions, his ethical strength, and his still-familiar vulnerability. Reducing the feeling to rank omits direct evidence.

### 15.5 Rival: beating Hinami cures Mimimi's comparison problem

The race demonstrates a successful process-focused shift and produces deserved pride. It does not erase the preceding decorated-shell anxiety or establish durability across domains.

### 15.6 Rival: Konno's help redeems the V05 harm

Her support of Yuzu is prosocial ordinary-life evidence. There is still no apology, restitution, acknowledgment to Tama or Akiyama, or safeguard against recurrence.

### 15.7 Rival: Kikuchi's habitat difference is simply who she naturally is

Her difference is real, but the particular explanatory metaphor is constructed after rejection and revised through Goda and Tomozaki. It is both self-knowledge and a protective adaptation.

### 15.8 Rival: Mizusawa's summer waiting contradicts his later owned pursuit

The desire is already owned. What changes later is whether he remains performatively uninvested or enters the uncertain field directly.

### 15.9 Rival: dual embarrassment at the gyoza table proves reciprocity

Tomozaki is affected and awkward; Mimimi is attracted. Neither viewpoint supplies a settled romantic decision by Tomozaki.
