### 4.1 Repair begins by naming several distinct wrongs

The opening does not convert the notification into proof of an affair. Kikuchi's phone access, Tomozaki's incomplete account, Rena's sexualized contact, the allocation of time, and the symbolic status of Hinami are distinct problems. Apology repairs the immediate breach enough for conversation to continue, but neither apology nor increased time supplies a complete future rule.

Tomozaki's first direct message to Rena is narrower than a relationship boundary: do not suddenly send messages of that kind. Her escalation shows why content-specific prohibition is inadequate when the other actor treats attention, bodily response, and attached status as rewards.

### 4.2 Choosing time is not the same as sharing authority

Tomozaki does make costly choices. He pauses regular coaching, gives Kikuchi a date instead of attending an existing group plan, and later attempts to reduce his entire peer load. The question is not whether he will sacrifice anything. It is whether the sacrifice preserves the values the relationship is meant to hold.

Kikuchi's initial permission for the next game meeting is not free consent in any robust sense: the narration marks visible fear and self-suppression, and Tomozaki knows this while accepting the answer. The volume later replaces this pseudo-solution with her affirmative choice of the person he already is, without representing her future insecurity as cured.

### 4.3 Tomozaki's strong-character property is change-readiness

Ashigaru's distinction does not recode social status as natural superiority. Tomozaki's unusual strength is his ability to act from `自分がそう思う` without requiring an external certificate that the change will improve him. That allows him to abandon a nationally dominant main character, accept temporary losses, and risk identity revision.

The same power creates collateral danger. Under romantic pressure he can discard friendships and social structures with the same decisiveness used to revise a game plan. Change-readiness is therefore a capacity, not a moral guarantee.

### 4.4 Hinami's reason system and the reproducibility experiment

Ashigaru observes that Hinami does not merely minimize waste: each action requires a reason. Hinami herself is surprised that others act from habit, feel, or `なんとなく`. Kikuchi links reasons to externally recognized proof; Tomozaki then links Hinami's repeated language of reproducibility to the coaching project.

The resulting account is strongly supported but bounded. Hinami's non-denial and praise confirm that Tomozaki reached something central. They do not prove that method validation was the only motive at every moment, that no attachment emerged, or that the complete origin of her proof need is known.

### 4.5 Two vocations expose two forms of `業`

Tomozaki's gamer ethic privileges owned choice, self-correction, and personal responsibility. Kikuchi's novelist ethic privileges truthful characterization, even when close observation or interpretive exposure injures the observed person or herself. Neither vocation is reduced to pathology, and neither actor is exempted from responsibility by naming the pattern.

Their renewed relationship is not based on discovering a timeless compatibility proof. It begins when both knowingly choose the relationship and accept that its reasons must be produced through later shared history. The old emblems become an explicit model: material with no inherent romantic power becomes meaningful through repeated social authorship.

### 4.6 The Hinami bond is prioritized but not classified as romance

Tomozaki states that coloring Hinami's world matters more to him than romance, yet he also explicitly denies that this means he wants Hinami as a lover. The supported claim is priority and exceptional attachment. Romantic destiny, eventual pairing, and exclusive compatibility remain unsupported.

Hinami's `寂しくても平気` likewise establishes tolerated loneliness, not an absence of loneliness and not a request for rescue. Tomozaki notices the distinction; the text does not authorize him—or this analysis—to supply her consent or outcome.
