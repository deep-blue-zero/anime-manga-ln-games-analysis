### 3.2 Chapter one: an input record is not a motive; collaboration starts with costs

**Focalizer: Fumiya.** The replay of the V11 Jack/Boxman set supplies thirty-one input-empty frames, yet does not determine why Hinami waited: intention is not read directly from an exhaustive input log (p0011:P3–25). This opening gives the book an epistemic problem before its literary solution is attempted. A novelist may offer a consequential model; she does not thereby obtain the missing ground truth. The final character-selection pause must not be substituted for this separate earlier record.

Kikuchi interrupts Fumiya's apparently generous self-exhaustion. She names the arrogance in assuming that only he can reach Hinami, asks him to protect his own time, and gives physical comfort. He then explicitly owns wanting to help while recognizing that he knows how likely she is to accept that wish (P46–113). Their exchange is DIRECT local agreement and reciprocal vulnerability. It supports agency within dependence, but neither costlessness nor a permanent rule that Hinami may always take priority. Kikuchi's head-patting and his relief matter as ordinary partner care rather than merely therapeutic machinery.

Her hand-drawn stellar research grows out of Andy's unfinished posthumous *Star Trajectories*, not an independently established lifelong astronomy hobby (P121–147). Their initial fiction imagines Alcia as a bloodless exception to an ostensibly inclusive world; their working model treats Hinami as a planet receiving her mother's light and proposes making her an independent star (P149–229). These are the collaborators' hypotheses. The fictional mother's bedtime words do not disclose an additional actual Yoko conversation. Their proposed cure already contains an imposed destination: Hinami should acquire the kind of selfhood they believe she lacks.

Mimimi initially challenges the claim that a story can save someone. Fumiya asks Kikuchi before circulating unfinished chapters to her, and Kikuchi fears the model will discover how she has been seen (P233–278). The permission obtained is the author's permission for a particular circulation. It is not blanket permission from the people modeled in the story, nor authorization for commercial publication. The embedded Sharif's emotional manipulation and Alcia's self-destruction remain fictional incidents (P293–319).

At a karaoke-work break, Mizusawa brings a large yakitori bowl and teases Fumiya. The familiar register and food place a difficult acknowledgment inside routine friendship (P331–370). Fumiya admits more pessimism to him than to Kikuchi: recipient-conditioned disclosure is observable, so an invariant 'always straightforward' personality would be false. Mizusawa accepts this particular sharing of his past with Kikuchi, while the narrator retains uncertainty about how much of the response is performance (P374–390).

Mizusawa then acknowledges that approaching Fumiya to learn about Hinami was **one** motive, not the sole explanation of every act. He describes instrumental selection and greater recent access to what he likes. Fumiya feels the sting but revises his natural-only account of sincerity: practiced interpersonal skill can enable honest speech, as it did when he borrowed Mizusawa's manner with Haruka. An instrumental origin does not fix the entire later relationship (P417–471). The awkward fictional borrowing through which he calls Mizusawa his best friend, and Mizusawa's embarrassment, teasing, gratitude, and offer to help, demonstrate a new local relation; they do not excuse all earlier instrumental conduct (P471–504).

Several days later, **Fumiya, Kikuchi, Mizusawa, and Mimimi actually meet** in the second sewing room. The old tutorial room becomes a collaborative workroom, with Hinami's chair empty (P511–543; p0012 illustration). Execution revises V11's merely proposed party. The empty chair also preserves the missing subject's agency problem: collaborators gathering is not Hinami agreeing to be helped through fictionalization.

### 3.3 Chapter two: listening can falsify the helpers' answer

**Focalizer: Fumiya.** Kikuchi challenges the sufficiency of being special only to Tama. Mimimi acknowledges that physical separation could make her seek another person to supply that role (p0013:P26–79). The older rescue remains valuable; it does not settle the whole question of self-worth. This is Mimimi's present account, not an analyst's diagnosis of dependence.

Mimimi changes the medium of the conversation by proposing a 200-metre race. Her bodily fluency and insistence on trying rather than merely explaining make her a contributor to the method. She admits both continuing love and the temptation to exploit Fumiya's post-play vulnerability, then identifies refusing that opportunity as an act she can like in herself (P86–169, especially P143–154). She can choose what kind of person she will be while not getting the romantic result she wants. This strengthens an affirmative self-regard already partly voiced in V09; it is neither her first possible agency nor evidence that love has ended. Fumiya and Kikuchi directly receive this account. Nearby Mizusawa's presence does not certify that every private line was heard.

Kikuchi and Mizusawa next role-play Alcia and Sharif. He initially resists, then agrees to one attempt under social pressure (P206–260). Their improvised exchange is neither a direct Hinami interview nor Mizusawa's renewed confession. The scene shows the strength and danger of form: acting can uncover a contradiction in a model precisely because the improvised figure does not obediently repeat the author's desired conclusion.

The intended conclusion, 'we will make her a star,' is exposed as a premise rather than a discovered answer (P264–270, P384–430). Kikuchi explicitly recognizes confirmation bias. Mimimi and Mizusawa supply a reasonable countercase: messages and desired effects are normal features of stories, not automatically defects (P432–434). Yet the risk is specific here. To prescribe retrospective meaning for Hinami while opposing her mother's prescribed retrospective meaning may reproduce the structure under a friendlier content. Kikuchi's fear of harming a person makes her halt the meeting and seek another way (P443–487). Ethical hesitation is a consequential stop, not mere lack of confidence.

Fumiya's assurance that he values her person beyond her productivity is sincere, but generic reassurance does not answer her own reason for writing (P502–540). She chooses a solo trip to Nagijima, where Andy lived, in search of what made his work possible. The missing-manuscript rumor is still a rumor. She refuses Fumiya's accompaniment because she needs to make this inquiry her own; he accepts (P563–591). A chosen initial refusal remains valid even though she later accepts a changed invitation.

### 3.4 Chapter three: ordinary work corrects the heroic model

**Focalizer: Fumiya; embedded fiction and island testimony retain separate sources.** Tama's family cake shop supplies a concrete answer before the journey supplies an abstract one. She is practiced and fluent with customers. When a child's one birthday cake is damaged, a quick repair is operationally available; Tama insists on remaking it, the mother initially declines the trouble, and then accepts. The ten-minute remake costs staff time and queue capacity (p0014:P30–76). Her care is real and has externalities. The fact that it wins admiration cannot make it the uniquely correct management decision.

Over unsellable cake, Tama admits that smarter ways exist and that her position as the manager's daughter helps her act this way. Childhood imitation of an unnamed manga/anime hero has become something she owns through repeated conduct. She also describes a distortion: compromising can make her dislike herself (P80–140). Learned form can sustain integrity and rigidity at once. Neither perfect moral independence nor an involuntary cage adequately describes all the evidence. The scene strengthens R06 breadth across work, competence, ordinary pleasure, privilege, cost, and self-theory. It does not establish shop succession as her chosen vocation. Her calm recollection of Konno is not an apology or restitution by Konno.

Fumiya replaces his abstract categorization with Tama's remembered words, allowing Kikuchi interpretive work of her own (P146–152). When Kikuchi remains blocked on the third day, he changes the offer: he wants to come because **he** wants to see her. He waits for acceptance (P158–206). This is a new negotiated invitation, not permission to override her earlier refusal. His mother's concern, sister's souvenir request, father's encouragement, early train connections, and ferry journey show ordinary family and practical initiative; he does not tell the family the full couple purpose (P210–284).

Kikuchi has shut herself indoors reading and drafting, treating leisure as undeserved while the work is unfinished. She accepts a date and extends her hand (P292–326). The couple's fan pilgrimage then gathers shopkeepers' fallible Andy memories: practical jokes, fish, translation assistance, an ashtray chase, and a drunken statement that he wrote observational records rather than novels (P331–465). These accounts undo an effortless-sage projection. They remain attributed recollections with acknowledged gaps. They do not make every reported event independently verified or establish Andy's complete interior motive. Fumiya's jealous query and Kikuchi's direct acknowledgment of him as her partner are local reciprocal reassurance, not a new standing jealousy protocol (P417–425).

The elderly craft expert first refuses their interview and they leave without forcing the manuscript question (p0016:P1–30). At sunset, the couple instead plays a descriptive word game. Fumiya's mistaken aquamarine answer and Kikuchi's playful verbal competitiveness show pleasure and difference outside rescue or productivity (P35–80). The inn has only a shared room available according to its keeper; Fumiya's suspicion of contrivance is unverified. Separate futons and bathing, shared rice balls, and Kikuchi's larger portion for him are the actual events; no sexual encounter follows by implication (P91–137).

She admits loneliness and gladness that he came. This time he identifies particular acts he loves—her shouting, laughter, competitive words, and divided rice ball—rather than repeating an abstract assurance. She initiates touch and he reciprocates (P156–190). Particular observation reaches her where universal reassurance did not, but this moment is not a durable cure or resolution of the writing problem.

The innkeeper names **Umayahara Mitsu / 馬屋原ミツ** as custodian of Andy's tower and describes his years there; the exact manuscript's completed state remains unknown (P195–260). Festival work brings the couple back to Mitsu. The locals' labor shortage and children's enjoyment without knowing Andy's meaning prevent an author-centred account of every participant (P265–301). She refuses again, then accepts a scoped bargain: help with the work, and tower access after completion. Kikuchi asks to see what Andy observed rather than demanding an explanation of his intention (P305–400).

Mitsu's hands have limits, but no diagnosis is supplied. She rejects Fumiya's unsolicited help and strikes his offered hand with the pen. A trusted local young person later offers, waits, and receives a seven-tag assignment with constraints (P410–497). Refusal and later delegation are recipient- and task-specific; age or impairment does not erase authority over her own work. Kikuchi's colour learning takes repeated attempts in changing light. Labels and isolated swatches fail to determine the colour a recipient will see beside another fabric (P423–456, P606–617). The epistemic point is contextual discrimination, not that every colour judgment is equally correct.

Mitsu's Andy story about Mioko fish distinguishes first dislike, converted liking, indifference, and recovered dislike. Learning more ways to perceive does not require turning every aversion into approval (P517–580). Her tide-path knowledge and craft work are practical capacities; she is not merely an oracle for the protagonists.

A costume problem emerges: a scarlet sample bears the wrong blue label. Fumiya entertains mistaken tags or a loose knot, but the responsible actor is not established. Do not blame Mitsu's body or the helper (P649–720). The couple opens the material without prior permission and makes a temporary proposal. Their different strengths—movement and colour—contribute, but Mitsu is angry before reviewing it. She accepts the colour pairing while correcting placements and cords and doing the sewing (P721–857). Expert approval is narrower than total correctness, and later usefulness does not retroactively authorize the intrusion.

The tower is a maintained private archive, with a fixed telescope, cloths recording subtly different nights, and restricted inner material. Mitsu grants promised access and sets handling limits (P861–1055). Multiple people once joined story nights; the narrator's picture of their feelings is imaginative reconstruction, not eyewitness history. The volume can make Andy's painstaking observational labour impressive and frightening without declaring that unpublished work was never written. The couple's resulting godlike-observer theory of Alcia/Andy is another provisional model, not direct revelation about Hinami (P1085–1129).

Rain damages the festival's paper birds. Mitsu persists at imperfect refolding despite the scale of the loss and difficulty; others return to help (P1157–1239). Kikuchi accepts that a bird is broken (P1255–1257) before children create a use and meaning for damaged birds. Their words do not reverse the rain or supply its hidden purpose. Material observation constrains new interpretation; it does not itself dictate one interpretation (P1242–1308; p0017 illustration).

Mitsu's haori gift uses the couple's selected scraps; different colours make a workable pair under local light (P53–143). Elders pray, young people take photographs, children play, and participants can place a bird without agreeing on the whole ritual's meaning (P147–180).

Mitsu corrects the claim that Andy invented the festival from nothing: he observed an existing widow's folding and elders' crossing; later people recognized, borrowed, altered, and expanded the story (P198–235). This is her account of plural appropriation, not a comprehensive neutral ritual history. She says that reading a flawed fictional model of herself let her like herself slightly more (P245–250). No spouse or romantic-partner relation to Andy is stated.

Kikuchi now commits to observation that asks questions rather than predetermining answers. She resumes writing that night (P268–432). Fumiya sees the luminous night-sea writer and projects something godlike onto her; that projection fails immediately when she says she cannot write Nagisa's words (p0020:P1–66). Productive resumption and a specific block coexist. Chris's opened second eye explicitly supplies disjunctions rather than complete truth (p0020:P13).

### 3.5 Chapter four: observation does not eliminate the author's remaining choice

**Focalizer: Fumiya.** Nagisa is dead; Kikuchi cannot observe the voice the desired reunion requires. Inventing it would introduce a wish (p0021:P11–33). When Kikuchi steps into calf-deep water, Fumiya fears she is departing, takes her wrist, and she grips his clothes and returns with him (P40–61). The source gives distress and physical action, not an explicit suicide intention, attempted suicide, or diagnosis. Neither this scene nor Alcia's earlier fictional self-harm establishes Nagisa's final intention.

A reasonable alternative is to omit resurrection and prove the observations instead. Kikuchi recognizes that this ending would still be her choice; even silence can be arranged to serve her desired result (P95–149). Fumiya explicitly never met or spoke to the actual Nagisa (P124). He cannot certify which speech or omission she would prefer. Kikuchi also admits that collecting observations partly deferred asking what **she** wanted to choose. That insight does not make all her research insincere.

The needed independent-star choice turns back onto Kikuchi. Fumiya's interpretation of the fictional mother is his inference, not new Yoko interiority (P153–192). Mitsu then lends an extra handwritten English continuation of *Star Trajectories*, with a return deadline. The couple works through it with smartphone translation: no unmediated English fluency or right to publish the manuscript is established (P209–249).

The visible draft revises one verb through believing, telling, recording, wishing, and finally praying. The first four are deleted; the final choice is circled (P250–330). This is DIRECT documentary evidence of revision. The psychological journey reconstructed from it remains the couple's interpretation. Prayer here names an offered desire after exhaustive observation, not factual verification that the world is beautiful or that a loss was necessary (P331–391). The difference from imposed retrospect is responsibility for the remaining subjective act: the author does not present her wish as discovered testimony.

Kikuchi returns the loaned papers before the ferry, then changes from being able to write to deciding that she will write. Her chosen procedure is honest observation followed by a small irreducible prayer (P402–433). Completion, delivery, and reception are not yet proven at this source position; later chapters, not this intention alone, must establish them.

### 3.6 Chapter five: Hinami reads a model and revises her relation to herself

**Focalizer changes to Hinami's first-person 私.** This is new direct reader access to her thought and memory. Fumiya and the peer group do not automatically receive it. Self-blame for Nagisa's death is evidence of guilt, not a causal finding (p0022:P1–10). Her response to the V07 play mixed felt accuracy with anger at invasive observation and a marriage/dependence solution. V09 exposure brought relief as well as emptiness. Neither surprise authorizes a retrospectively total account of every motive (P16–40).

Her birthday pleasure in the cake and game was genuine. The acute trigger was specifically the remembered prayer room and illegible scroll within the wooden cabinet, not simply seeing her mother's or Haruka's familiar face (P47–78). The religious room is explicit; a named religion, cult, doctrine, diagnosis, or abusive intention is not. The recollected memory gap is not a clinical classification. Her whispered self-hatred is described as probably unheard by Fumiya (P104–110).

Yuzu, Nakamura, and Takei come to the house while Hinami continues to refuse contact. Her theories that they intend apology or Fumiya's trap are her conjectures. Takei's loud delivery reaches her room; Haruka leaves the paper bundle despite Hinami saying she will not read it (P114–158). Later Haruka independently reads and cries, identifies Nagisa as her sister, and urges Aoi to take the story written for her (P180–226). Hinami chooses to take it under emotional pressure. This is neither physically forced reading nor unpressured prior consent. Positive later reception cannot erase the refusal.

The final title is *Pure Mixed Blood and God* (`純混血と神さま`), revising the earlier ice-cream title. Hinami's initial response is anger at being defined and exposed; the absence of real names does not remove her experience of identification or possible harm (P230–284). She continues. Her appraisal of real Mimimi as dependent, weak, or a degraded copy is a defensive, incomplete **Hinami model**. Nana's self-regard appears unlike the Mimimi she knows (P294–373), but the reader has already seen Mimimi's real interview and earlier V09 evidence. Her surprise reveals a knowledge difference; it is not proof that Kikuchi invented all Mimimi agency.

Sharif's charge makes Hinami directly recognize that she herself discarded genuine birthday care after nearly receiving it (P375–398). This strengthens a particular mechanism of self-disqualification while resisting the claim that all affection has always been empty. Her inference that Fumiya told the author nearly everything does not certify the extent of the transfer (P400). A real accidental paper cut contrasts with a fictional bodily fantasy; do not invent a new actual self-harm episode (P443–447). She leaves for a nearby park, not yet for school (P449–454).

The text explicitly retains the crucial absence: after more than five years she does not know whether Nagisa entered the road accidentally, intentionally, from exhaustion/dizziness, or from despair, and cannot select a proven prevention or lesson (P504–513). The novel will alter what can be done with that uncertainty. It will not reveal a concealed true death mechanism.

Within the fiction, Alcia tests data rather than choosing between two existing circles. The ellipse supplies two foci, publicly checkable records, and a revised assumption about which shape was required (P523–702). Hinami already knew the geometry (P675–676); the novelty is its emotional and interpretive application. Public verification inside the fiction prevents the lesson from collapsing into 'all facts are subjective.' The new value does not mathematically follow from geometry either: moving from an ellipse to a way of living remains metaphor and chosen meaning.

Hinami begins to question whether the self, rather than the frame in which she evaluated it, was defective. She still objects that the story's method resembles her mother's meaning-making, then continues (P708–742). The successful reception includes resistance. Fictional Alcia and Nagisa's star-gazing and costumes borrow island imagery; they are not newly disclosed real sister scenes (P753–812). Revaluing her actions as traces of attachment does not exonerate every manipulation or erase other people's costs.

Her recollection of V11's spoken questions certifies some overlap with what Fumiya heard, without making all V11 retrospective interiority a transcript. She tentatively wonders whether coaching and disclosure contained hope that time might move again; this is new self-interpretation, not a single proven original motive (P825–841). Figurative throwing herself into disclosure is not a new physical suicide attempt.

The imagined thirty-second reunion is explicitly bounded as fiction. Semantic ruby overlays Alcia, 私, Nagisa, and story/magic, letting the reader's own speech and the authored scene interpenetrate (P846–979). Hinami anticipates asking why Nagisa died; what comes out is apology, love, admiration, and thanks for having been herself (P881–932). The shift is from recovering a cause to finding an address she wants to make. It supplies **Hinami's affirmation**, not Nagisa's authenticated final testimony. The reused birth-thanks phrase can be received as a loving prayer without becoming a cosmic guarantee. The moon's reflected light can be beautiful: she need not prove she is an isolated star. Neither ghostly appearance nor hallucination diagnosis is warranted. The following sister illustration supports the remembered/imagined child register, not literal resurrection.

### 3.7 Chapter six: a local affirmative return, with future viability still open

**Focalizer returns to Fumiya.** Haruka's DM about Aoi leaving unsteadily prompts his search, and he reports finding her (p0024:P9–12). This route gives him the encounter, not direct access to the entire preceding Hinami chapter.

Hinami says she likes herself a little. Fumiya explicitly allows that it may be temporary (p0026:P15–23). He jokingly offers to replace Nagisa and immediately withdraws the offer (P40–47); it is not a new role, romantic promise, or substitute-sister contract. The second focus need not be filled by a strong self that can prove it has content. His promise of enduring reassurance is marked by his own narration as unfounded confidence (P94–96), a chosen relational commitment rather than evidence of permanent recovery.

At the Omiya café, a chamomile trial, the couple's discussion, and Kikuchi's new question about what Fumiya wants to show as a professional return the story to ordinary exchange and prospective work (P106–173). Kikuchi expressly rejects the inference that Hinami's tears prove the novel right; Nagisa's actual feelings remain unknown (P114–119). She is glad she wrote and owns a reason to offer believable anchors through which weak, ordinary readers might like themselves and try living again (P121–163). This is an affirmative authorial end, not a completed publication contract or universal right to represent others.

Several days later, the school career event exposes costs. Fumiya and Mimimi neglected practical acting-council preparation while working on the story; Ashigaru carries the underprepared event with an account of his zero-ad-revenue period (P177–202). A rescue plot has displaced real responsibilities onto others. His professional history is attributed event testimony; the scene does not certify Fumiya's or Endo's economics.

Hinami actually returns in uniform as an audience member, seated between Mimimi and Tama. Familiar peer banter and Yuzu's wave re-establish local ordinary participation (P212–234). Yuzu's words are inaudible under Takei, so no dialogue should be invented. Fumiya lip-reads Hinami's quiet response about unnecessary meddling rather than hearing it (P220–222). The return is DIRECT; full attendance, presidency, coaching, forgiveness, and permanent repair remain unshown.

She accepts a stage match. Fumiya infers that performance no longer has to be wrong for her (P233); the retained polished entrance is observable, the universal interior conclusion is not. Both select **Found** after hovering Boxman/Jack (P231–258). The match itself and its result are not narrated. The brief cursor pause at P254 does not establish why the earlier thirty-one empty frames occurred.

Fumiya's professional image is still incomplete, but he wants to make the pleasure of living and playing visible to friends, supporters, and Hinami. She says she has stopped clinging to correctness; he then proposes enjoying the match as both prepare for shared play (P261–284). The final declaration that life is a god-tier game is Fumiya's owned value judgment, not proof that every life is fair, suffering is necessary, or mastery guarantees a viable career. ## 4. Developmental, relational, and knowledge consequences

### 4.1 Fumiya: ownership without the unique-savior credential

The new movement is not from having no resources to being given strength. He already has a chosen girlfriend, skill, peers, and the ability to revise. V12 makes him accept that the reason he wants to help is his own while relinquishing the premise that his superiority makes him uniquely entitled to do it. He can borrow a voice, rely on another author, accept a specialist's correction, and still own an action. His island journey, concrete appreciation of Kikuchi, friendship acknowledgment to Mizusawa, and final prospective performance give the claim more than one crisis scene (§§3.2–3.7).

His recurrent failure remains visible: he treats desired rescue as a reason to press, underestimates practical obligations, initially projects godlike authorship, and promises more durability than he can know. The final game-as-life affirmation is credible as his value because it survives a lost credential; it does not become a transferable proof over Hinami, Kikuchi, or the reader. Professional purpose now concerns what he wants to show, while financial, educational, family, route, and exit constraints remain distinct OPENs.

### 4.2 Hinami: a revised frame, not a newly complete essence

The private viewpoint strengthens the model of self-disqualification: real pleasure and care can be received and then dismissed because the self is thought defective. The birthday trigger narrows a formerly diffuse mother/video explanation to a specific remembered religious-room image. Yet her own historical self-explanation remains a self-explanation; guilt does not prove responsibility, and late access does not make all earlier analysts or actors know the same thing (§3.6).

The intervention works partly because the story stops demanding that she become the independent star the helpers first prescribed. Reflected light, attachment, doubt, and inherited language need not invalidate her. She can like herself a little and re-enter ordinary relations without explaining Nagisa's death. This is a substantial local change. The rival 'temporary affect after an intense story' survives because the narration expressly allows temporariness and shows only limited subsequent participation. No durability, complete forgiveness, named diagnosis, romance, or singular cause is established.

### 4.3 Kikuchi: authorship includes an accountable remainder

Her arc differentiates three inadequate ideals: deliver the correct answer, accumulate observations until the answer writes itself, or eliminate all subjectivity. The party's improvisation makes her first model fail. Tama and Mitsu show that chosen acts can be learned, distorted, privileged, costly, and still owned. The festival lets observable damage constrain meaning without dictating a unique meaning. The manuscript's final prayer makes the remaining wish attributable to its author (§§3.3–3.5).

She therefore owns a purpose more precise than being useful to Fumiya or reaching a contest result: offer a believable anchor without calling it Nagisa's testimony. Her final caution about tears is indispensable counterevidence to an author-saves-subject triumph. Prior permission and future publication remain unresolved ethical responsibilities. Her play, irritation, competitive description, affection, appetite-sharing, and independent travel also prevent the monograph from collapsing her into a writing function.

### 4.4 Mimimi, Tama, Mizusawa, and the wider labor system

Mimimi can affirm a chosen refusal while retaining unwanted longing. Her dependence question is not solved by replacing Hinami's rank with Tama's exclusive recognition; nor is Tama's help dismissed as a mistake. The real interview prevents Hinami's later stale model from becoming analyst authority. No terminal romantic waiting or interference rule follows (§3.3).

Tama's shop scene establishes an independently consequential work-self. The same hero-derived integrity that protects a child's birthday can impose costs on staff and customers. Explicit privilege and willingness to call herself distorted complicate both the ideal moral core and the cage hypothesis. Succession, generalized flexibility, and hostile-context retests remain open (§3.4).

Mizusawa's instrumental admission and friendship response prevent two caricatures: flawless social savant and empty manipulator incapable of feeling. He can be embarrassed, refuse an improvisation, learn that a constructed form carries sincere care, and contribute without receiving Hinami's answer. Neither an earlier refusal nor lack of a renewed confession is erased (§§3.2–3.3).

Yuzu, Nakamura, Takei, Haruka, and Ashigaru provide channels, noise, labor, and practical repair that the core authors do not control alone. Haruka is an independent reader and a sister who can name her stake; this does not yet establish independence from Aoi as her model. The event-preparation failure records costs outside the rescue dyad. Mitsu's expertise, boundaries, humor, body, work, chosen delegation, gift, flawed self-liking, and custodial role justify later bounded individual attention. Andy's wider ordinary persona is assembled from testimony and surviving material; omniscient interior reconstruction remains unjustified.

## 5. Counterevidence, rival readings, and the limits of resolution

**Interpretive hypothesis:** the ending makes a different relation to uncertainty possible rather than recovering the missing correct cause. The opening replay, Alcia's recalcitrant data, dead Nagisa's unavailable voice, final fictional prayer, and Kikuchi's refusal to infer truth from tears mutually support this account. It would fail if the text supplied an authenticated death explanation or made fictional speech authoritative testimony. It does neither.

**Rival: the book succeeds through the same imposed meaning it criticizes.** There is material support: the project chooses a desired recipient effect, circulation and delivery are pressured, the story uses private pain, and Hinami herself notices the resemblance to her mother's method. The strongest distinction is the authors' revision of their premise, acknowledgment of residual subjectivity, recipient resistance, and refusal to certify their prayer as Nagisa's truth. **Disposition: REVISE a simple contrast, preserve the ethical tension.** The observed benefit does not settle permission, future publication, or whether another recipient would be harmed.

**Rival: Hinami merely receives a new external dependency.** The moon and second-focus images do not establish autonomous self-sufficiency. That is part of the revision: received meaning need not be disqualifying. Her local self-liking, school presence, acceptance of play, and her declared change in relation to correctness show more than passive exposure; the short retest still leaves durability OPEN. **Disposition: DOWNGRADE the claim of mere passivity; OPEN long-term stability.**

**Rival: observation means faithful transcription without invention.** The festival already combines existing practice, selection, fictional composition, and plural reception; the final handwritten verb is prayer. Nagisa's missing voice cannot be honestly transcribed. **Disposition: REJECT the no-subjectivity ideal; PRESERVE accountable observational constraint.** Physical damage, labels, data, and actual refusals cannot be wished away.

**Rival: performance and sincerity are finally identical.** Tama's imitation and Mizusawa's learned voice show that performance can become owned or carry truth. Their costs, imperfect motives, and changed recipients show that it is not invariably sincere, harmless, or sufficient. **Disposition: REJECT the binary and the universal equivalence alike.**

The source gives local narrative resolution, not complete causal biography. Nagisa's intent and grade tension, Rena's internal boundary, Konno/Akiyama accountability, Mizusawa's renewed pursuit, Yuzu's absorber limit, future contact policies, publication, and viable professional routes remain evidence-bound. The afterword's declaration of a thematic ending does not answer these factual questions.
