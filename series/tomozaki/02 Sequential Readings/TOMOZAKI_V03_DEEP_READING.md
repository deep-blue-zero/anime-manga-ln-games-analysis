## 3. Volume movement and evidence map

V03 is organized around a progression from successful execution to a dispute over who is entitled to define the end being executed. Its six chapters form three linked movements:

1. **Proceduralization and expansion:** Tomozaki's trained behavior works across clothing, messaging, dating, employment, teasing, and group logistics. He can now act without receiving every sentence or move from Hinami.
2. **Goal-ownership rupture:** Hinami makes dating Kikuchi the summer end-state and later assigns a fireworks confession. Tomozaki becomes capable of the confession but chooses not to make it because he does not yet know whether the relationship end is his.
3. **Integration and renegotiation:** Tomozaki initially mistakes unstyled behavior for sincerity, then recognizes that learned form can express an owned intention. He returns to Hinami with a hybrid rule: retain skills, but derive goals from what he is genuinely trying to do.

## 4. Starting-state confirmation: trained behavior has become usable competence

### 4.1 Proceduralization is directly visible

At the opening, Tomozaki notices that entering a clothing store no longer produces the earlier level of fear. Hinami identifies the relevant threshold as being able to perform the behavior habitually and without conscious assembly. The volume then tests this claim in distinct contexts:

- Tomozaki composes his own LINE message to Kikuchi rather than merely transmitting dictated wording.
- He leads parts of the film outing, retrieves prepared topics, expands them, and discusses a film from genuine interest.
- In a formal job interview, the accumulated habits make adult-facing conversation comparatively manageable; he is accepted and judged capable of brisk customer interaction.
- At camp, he uses teasing and disagreement, recovers from one mistimed attempt, contributes to strategy, and participates in male-group banter.
- He identifies Takei's predictable Twitter behavior and designs the key transmission mechanism in the Nakamura/Yuzu operation.

**DIRECT:** the same class of practiced control now appears across peer, romantic, employment, and group-strategy contexts.

**CORROBORATED INFERENCE:** competence is no longer best described as a temporary Hinami overlay. The skills remain prompted and uneven, but Tomozaki can retrieve, combine, and adapt them himself.

### 4.2 Transfer remains context-sensitive

The first attempt to apply Hinami's “tease or contradict Nakamura” task briefly creates an awkward atmosphere. Tomozaki then falls back on prepared topics. In the film outing, his stored subjects keep the conversation moving, but Kikuchi later identifies some of those intervals as harder to inhabit. At camp, the same tactical family works better because the male group's joking frame already legitimizes reciprocal attack.

The evidence therefore does not support a context-free social stat. It supports a growing repertoire whose effects depend on recipient, timing, group norm, and purpose.

### 4.3 External validation broadens

The job interview is the clearest new external test. The evaluator does not know the training arrangement and accepts Tomozaki into a role requiring quick, public service. Mizusawa also observes that Tomozaki has visibly worked and treats his election strategy as evidence of intelligence, even while calling him both smart and foolishly direct. These judgments do not establish broad confidence or high status; they establish that acquired competence is legible outside Hinami's evaluation system.

## 5. Goal ownership becomes the central conflict

### 5.1 Hinami defines the end before Tomozaki has chosen it

Hinami announces the summer objective as dating Kikuchi and later specifies a confession at the end of the fireworks. Tomozaki immediately notices that the first formulation bypasses whether either person actually wants that outcome. The assigned steps are efficient and some become genuinely enjoyable, but the ultimate relationship state remains externally selected.

This is not evidence that every suggestion from Hinami violates autonomy. She seeks Kikuchi's permission before passing contact information, offers Tomozaki a real financial opt-out from camp, and later allows him to write his own message. The sharper problem is hierarchical: she can preserve consent at the level of particular moves while still claiming authority over the relationship end those moves are intended to produce.

### 5.2 Tomozaki can confess and chooses not to

At the fireworks, Tomozaki has enough confidence, preparation, atmosphere, and reciprocal interest to say the assigned line. He explicitly distinguishes inability from refusal: he does not fail to confess; he decides not to confess. That decision is based on uncertainty about his own feeling, not lack of attraction or proof that Kikuchi rejects him.

**DIRECT:** Tomozaki selects non-completion of a difficult task after determining that its end is not yet owned.

**CORROBORATED INFERENCE:** V03 converts strategic self-authorship from choosing methods into contesting who selects the goal. This is the strongest support so far for the developmental axis frozen after V02.

### 5.3 The first argument fails for a real competence reason

At the station, Tomozaki can state the problem—human connection should not be judged only as task completion—but cannot yet supply a framework capable of meeting Hinami's objection. Hinami argues that a “true desire” may be only a temporary preference mistaken for a stable good, while planned productive action has observable results. Tomozaki rejects her conclusion intuitively but cannot yet show why technique and sincerity need not be alternatives.

His later self-blame is therefore not wholly false. The rupture is value-level, but the inability to articulate an alternative contributes to its severity. What the scene does **not** establish is that Hinami would have accepted the claim if it had been more eloquently expressed.

## 6. Kikuchi and the difference between fluent technique and transmissible experience

### 6.1 The film outing gives mixed, not negative, evidence about preparation

Prepared topics help Tomozaki sustain an unfamiliar one-to-one outing. Genuine discussion of the Michael Andy film produces some of the most reciprocal interaction. Kikuchi's report is deliberately double: Tomozaki sometimes becomes unusually easy to speak with and sometimes suddenly difficult. She also clarifies that talking with boys is normally difficult for her and asks for another outing.

The scene therefore cannot be reduced to “technique fails” or “natural chemistry makes technique unnecessary.” Technique enables the encounter and carries parts of it; recipient-specific genuine interest produces another quality of contact; unintegrated prepared material can create discontinuity.

### 6.2 The fireworks experiment isolates a variable but does not prove a universal rule

Tomozaki deliberately uses no newly memorized topics and avoids retrieving old ones. Conversation is more halting, yet Kikuchi reports that he was easy to talk with throughout. This strongly supports his hypothesis that her earlier difficulty tracked the felt discontinuity of prepared material rather than simple low fluency.

The result applies to this recipient and this interaction. It does not establish that unprepared speech is universally more sincere or effective, nor that every learned conversational form is perceptible as a mask.

### 6.3 Kikuchi names Tomozaki's particular communicative value

In the café, Kikuchi says that Tomozaki's speech can transmit an image: atmosphere, emotion, texture, and what he saw arrive relatively unprocessed, like the experience of reading a novel. Memorized subjects sometimes lack that image. Her account is a recipient report, not an omniscient diagnosis, but it explains both the ease and the discontinuity better than a generic authenticity binary.

She then distinguishes the form of his speech from the world it conveys. Early images were monochrome; over the past months, his accounts have acquired color. The change is not that he ceased learning technique. It is that effort, relationships, and new experiences changed what he had available to communicate.

**CORROBORATED INFERENCE:** Kikuchi recognizes a stable expressive disposition in Tomozaki while also perceiving real developmental change. She is not merely affirming an untouched “true self.”

### 6.4 The relationship becomes materially more owned

Kikuchi initiates the bookstore outing after the fireworks. Tomozaki initially fails to answer during his withdrawal, then recognizes that ignoring her would contradict the responsibility he has claimed. He accepts because he wants to respond to an action she chose, not because Hinami issues another instruction. Kikuchi also discloses her own attachment to books, romantic aspiration, and sense that ordinary reality can look gray.

The relationship is still not assigned a settled romantic label. V03 does establish reciprocal initiation, genuine shared interest, and disclosure beyond Hinami's original premise.

## 7. The false choice between “mask” and “self”

### 7.1 Tomozaki's overcorrection is explicit

After the rupture, Tomozaki stops styling his hair, wearing the clothes Hinami selected, controlling posture, and intentionally shaping expression. He treats the absence of learned form as fidelity to his “real” self. The mirror scene disconfirms that equation from inside his own experience: as the character living the life—not merely an external evaluator—he dislikes the slack posture, poorly fitted clothes, and disengaged expression.

This does not mean conventional attractiveness is inherently authentic. It means that, for Tomozaki, dropping every acquired practice is no more automatically self-authored than following every assigned task.

### 7.2 Achievement-produced feeling is also real feeling

Tomozaki recalls genuine pleasure when he recognized himself as more socially legible, participated successfully with peers, and saw his own level increase. Those outcomes originated in player-set exercises, yet the resulting satisfaction belonged to the character experiencing the world. V03 therefore rejects an origin fallacy: a practice does not become alien merely because another person taught it or because it began as deliberate rehearsal.

### 7.3 Skill becomes equipment

When Tomozaki thanks Kikuchi, he uses a practiced serious tone precisely to carry a sincere feeling accurately. When he meets Hinami again, he styles himself and wears the selected clothes because those forms support what he has chosen to do. The volume's operative distinction becomes:

| Question | Insufficient binary | V03 revision |
|---|---|---|
| Was it learned? | learned = false | learned form may become owned competence |
| Was it spontaneous? | spontaneous = true | spontaneity can also be avoidance, neglect, or inarticulacy |
| Is it effective? | effective = justified | effectiveness cannot choose the end by itself |
| Is the feeling sincere? | sincerity requires no technique | technique can transmit sincere content |
| Who chose the goal? | mentor expertise settles the end | the actor must be able to own, question, or refuse the end |

**HIGH-CONFIDENCE V03 CONCLUSION:** the volume distinguishes **goal authenticity** from **method naturalness**. Tomozaki does not need untrained methods; he needs ends he can answer for.

## 8. Hinami: result confidence, containment, and partial openness

### 8.1 What V03 directly establishes

Hinami:

- treats repeated practice until unconscious execution as the standard for skill ownership;
- chooses multiple summer targets and optimizes time across them;
- states that “what one truly wants” may be a temporary misidentification rather than a reliable planning authority;
- treats observable result as a privileged basis of confidence;
- can instantly produce an emotionally apt role when Mizusawa challenges her self-distance;
- initially terminates the coaching arrangement when Tomozaki rejects its goal structure;
- later concedes that she cannot prove true desire nonexistent and accepts a testable hybrid arrangement;
- displays competitive interest when Tomozaki frames the disagreement through their Atafami asymmetry.

These are evidence about her explicit theory and behavior. They do not disclose the history that produced them.

### 8.2 Mizusawa's challenge is not equivalent to source omniscience

Mizusawa describes living one step outside himself: he can obtain desirable results, but the acted character rather than the experiencing self receives pleasure or pain. He identifies a similar distance in Hinami. Tomozaki agrees internally and interprets her response as a perfect “school heroine” role containing the threat rather than a disclosure by NO NAME.

The performance evidence strongly supports the existence of active self-presentation and emotional containment. It does **not** prove that every word of Hinami's response is false, that there is one singular hidden self untouched by every role, or that Mizusawa fully understands her motive.

### 8.3 Ordinary preference and reciprocity complicate pure instrumentality

Hinami wants the fireworks pin despite normally avoiding unnecessary purchases, creates an equal exchange by giving Tomozaki the damaged bag, competes with him in Atafami, and uses a Joker to stop his Daifugo win. She also tells him that maintaining the “cat” role continuously is tiring and that his presence lets her reduce it.

These moments do not resolve whether affection, friendship, control, rivalry, or curiosity dominates. They do show that her relation to Tomozaki contains non-instrumental preference and reciprocal enjoyment not exhausted by teacher efficiency.

### 8.4 The rupture contains counterevidence to simple coldness

When Tomozaki cannot answer her boundary statement, Hinami appears briefly hurt, says “you are different too,” returns the pin, and ends the arrangement. Because this is focalized through Tomozaki and quickly suppressed, the exact feeling remains interpretive. Her later concession is narrow but real: she accepts uncertainty in her own proposition and allows the relationship to continue on altered terms.

The bounded conclusion is not that Hinami has accepted Tomozaki's values. It is that V03 makes her responsive to a challenge posed through shared rules and evidence.

## 9. Mizusawa: effortless success becomes an ownership problem

### 9.1 His regime differs from both Tomozaki and Hinami

Mizusawa says that many things come to him without strenuous effort. He can pursue a high-specification older girl by calculating likely benefit and method, but cannot locate love, dislike, excitement, or personal desire inside that success. His problem is therefore not insufficient performance and not comparative defeat. It is distance from the self that receives the outcome.

### 9.2 Tomozaki's “foolish” directness becomes a rival strength

Mizusawa admires Yuzu, Nakamura, and Tomozaki for fully identifying with what they want and feeling the result. Tomozaki's unoptimized question—whether Mizusawa actually likes the older girl—surprises him because it bypasses the ordinary social answer. Later, Tomozaki honestly emerges from hiding after overhearing the confession; Mizusawa recognizes this unreasonable directness as part of the thing he lacks.

### 9.3 Confession produces loss and vitality at once

Mizusawa tells Hinami he probably likes her and wants someday to hear her genuine view. She refuses a relationship. He reports that the rejection hurts, but also that the act feels clarifying and that he has not faced something he wanted so directly in a long time. He refuses to erase the event afterward.

**DIRECT:** an owned end makes failure personally costly and personally meaningful.

**CORROBORATED INFERENCE:** V03 uses Mizusawa as evidence against equating frictionless success with fulfillment. It does not establish that all planning is alienating or that painful action is inherently authentic.

## 10. Camp strategy, other people's agency, and `空気`

### 10.1 Tomozaki becomes a genuine group strategist

The group wants Yuzu and Nakamura to progress. Tomozaki identifies the structure of the obstacle: Nakamura must learn what Shimane is doing without feeling that his peers can mock him for immediately changing targets. He proposes using Takei, whose position outside the tease/status game lets Nakamura accept the information. The group then engineers a seemingly accidental Twitter discovery and removes evidence of Mimimi's relay.

The operation works at the information level. Nakamura does not confess on command; he and Yuzu merely arrange a later outing. The group accepts and even cherishes this small result.

### 10.2 Bounded agency generalizes only partially

The strategy respects one limit: friends can remove an obstacle and create opportunity but cannot generate Nakamura's decision. Mizusawa states this principle explicitly. Yet the method also deceives Takei, conceals the group's intervention from Nakamura, and treats several people as predictable inputs.

V03 therefore **revises**, rather than simply strengthens, the V02 bounded-agency claim. Tomozaki understands that final choice belongs to another actor, but he remains willing to manipulate the informational and social field extensively.

### 10.3 `空気` changes through both skilled and accidental local acts

Examples include:

- Hinami and the others changing pairings and delaying departures;
- Tomozaki's teasing attempts altering whether Nakamura can treat him as an equal participant;
- bath banter creating a frame in which reciprocal attack is legitimate;
- Takei's unplanned river accident bringing Yuzu and Nakamura physically close despite the formal plan failing;
- Nakamura's command that Takei keep the Twitter matter private creating a new non-teasing boundary.

This strengthens the field model: `空気` is produced by local expectations and response costs and can shift without a full campaign or singular high-status sponsor. It also resists a total-control reading, because accidental or resistant action repeatedly redirects the planned sequence.

### 10.4 Care and instrumentalization coexist

Mimimi, Mizusawa, Hinami, and Tomozaki are sincerely invested in their friends' happiness. That care does not erase the deceptive method. Conversely, the use of manipulation does not establish that care is fake. The scene's ethical difficulty lies in their coexistence.

## 11. Tomozaki's temporary regression and recovery

### 11.1 Withdrawal is a real failure mode

After the rupture, Tomozaki spends roughly two weeks almost entirely in Atafami, ignores his phone, abandons training, and loses the absorbed pleasure that formerly defined his play. His sister observes that even gaming now makes him look bored and that this withdrawn face is less recognizably him than the recently changed one.

This is not a categorical return to “life is a bad game.” It is behavioral retreat and self-neglect under relational/value conflict. V03 therefore narrows any claim that the `良ゲー` correction makes him uniformly resilient.

### 11.2 Responsibility restarts motion

Kikuchi's unanswered invitation exposes a contradiction: Tomozaki has criticized treating people as task objects, yet is ignoring an initiative from someone he involved. He answers not to earn a point but because he judges reciprocity and follow-through to be required by what he says he values.

### 11.3 Recovery requires both another person's interpretation and his own action

His sister notices the regression and returns the phone. Kikuchi supplies the distinction between empty prepared content and a world made richer through effort. Tomozaki then decides to contact Hinami, uses a strategic pretext to secure a meeting, prepares his reasoning, and performs the reconciliation. The recovery is relationally enabled but not passively delivered.

## 12. The revised Tomozaki/Hinami contract

### 12.1 Tomozaki translates rather than capitulates

He first acknowledges the full value of Hinami's instruction: it taught him how to move his real-world character and made life perceptually richer. He then uses Hinami's own rules—play before judgment, result requires cause, opponent's ground, and competitive proof—to make her uncertainty actionable.

His Atafami claim is explicitly not a proof that true desire exists. He exploits an unresolved result: despite Hinami's supremacy across domains, she cannot defeat him in the game he most deeply wants to master. This converts a metaphysical deadlock into an experiment Hinami's competitive style will accept.

### 12.2 The agreement is partial and falsifiable

Hinami does not affirm the existence of stable true desire. She agrees only not to assume its nonexistence and requires Tomozaki to demonstrate it over time. Tomozaki asks to continue life strategy while rejecting goals that conflict with what he judges he genuinely wants.

The new contract can be summarized as:

`owned or answerable end → deliberate goal → practiced skill → experienced result → revision`

rather than:

`expert-selected end → compliance → externally scored completion`.

Whether future goals are actually co-authored remains OPEN for V04.

### 12.3 Reciprocity changes level

Tomozaki now offers Hinami instruction in how to become absorbed in life and locate desire, while she continues teaching him its controllable mechanics. This does not make their competence symmetric. It makes the direction of possible learning bidirectional.

The returned bag/pin exchange seals the change without erasing conflict. Tomozaki returns the bag because he wants to buy his own, and returns the pin as thanks for the color Hinami helped create. Hinami accepts it. The exchange separates gratitude from dependency.

## 17. Rival readings and counterevidence

### 17.1 Rival: V03 proves authenticity requires abandoning social technique

**Rejected.** Tomozaki's unstyled period produces neglect, self-disgust, and poorer responsibility. The resolving act explicitly uses trained tone, appearance, strategic wording, and Hinami's logic to express an owned goal.

### 17.2 Rival: V03 vindicates Hinami because desire is unstable

**Not established.** Hinami correctly identifies that preferences change, and Tomozaki does not disprove it. But instability does not grant an expert authority to select another person's relational end. Their agreement preserves the question for longitudinal testing.

### 17.3 Rival: Tomozaki simply replaces Hinami with Kikuchi as authority

**Too strong.** Kikuchi supplies language and perceptual evidence Tomozaki lacks. He decides to contact Hinami, builds the argument, accepts its limits, and negotiates the new terms. His agency is relationally scaffolded rather than solitary or transferred wholesale.

### 17.4 Rival: Hinami's public self is wholly fake

**Rejected as overreach.** Her capacity for constructed roles is direct, and Tomozaki interprets the response to Mizusawa as total containment. Yet a performed response may contain true propositions, and V03 offers no privileged exhaustive account of a singular hidden self.

### 17.5 Rival: the camp plan demonstrates benevolent social mastery

**Incomplete.** The plan intelligently handles Nakamura's pride and leaves final action to him, but it depends on deception and covert use of Takei. The intended good does not erase the autonomy cost.

### 17.6 Rival: Kikuchi's affinity establishes a romantic outcome

**Rejected.** Attraction and reciprocal initiative are materially supported; relationship status and future choice are not settled by V03.
