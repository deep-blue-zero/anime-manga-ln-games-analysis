## 5. Tomozaki takes the terminal romance decision away from the task system

V07 does not abolish Hinami's choice architecture. She still defines the event map, recommends simultaneous exploration unless Tomozaki is already certain, specifies escalating interactions, and sets the expectation that he will ultimately date someone. Tomozaki also uses some of those prompts. The assigned structure therefore remains causally important.

The terminal act nonetheless becomes meaningfully his in three separable steps.

First, Tomozaki tells Hinami that both Mimimi and Kikuchi interest him. This is not merely a list of viable candidates supplied by social rank. Mimimi's declaration forces him to recognize a person-specific relationship, and his prior collaboration with Kikuchi supplies a different person-specific attraction. Eligibility becomes acknowledged interest.

Second, he explicitly refuses to make confession itself a task. He will confess only if and when he wants to. His formulation preserves a result-oriented `nanashi` style—he still intends to advance and accepts measurement—but places felt commitment before the irreversible move. Hinami accepts the boundary while warning that uncertainty cannot become indefinite evasion.

Third, the decisive invitation and confession arise from Tomozaki's unassigned desire. He asks Kikuchi to speak after the performance before consciously converting the moment into an event-map completion. In the library he persists because he wants an explicit interpersonal answer rather than accepting the play as a sufficient proxy. He then states that he likes her and asks her to date him.

This is bounded rather than absolute autonomy. Hinami created the field, accelerated access, and normalized choosing. Tomozaki owns whom he selects and why he acts, not every condition that made the choice possible.

## 6. “The meaning of dating” is resolved by particularity, not a universal rule

Tomozaki asks several people what makes a romantic relationship distinct. Their answers are not redundant.

| Speaker | Model | What it supplies | What it cannot decide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kikuchi | two people become mutually irreplaceable because each supplies what the other lacks | structural complementarity and reciprocal need | whether a proposed pair actually wants that relation |
| Nakamura | the history actually lived with Yuzu makes her irreplaceable, regardless of hypothetical alternatives | contingent shared time and the priority of the real person over imagined substitutes | whether friendship history must become romance |
| Mimimi | Tomozaki embodies a form of self-belief and world-changing action she admires and fears losing | a person-specific account of why attraction formed | reciprocal choice by Tomozaki |
| Tomozaki at decision | reasons can describe a feeling, but do not manufacture the uncontrolled feeling they describe | permission to choose from desire while still articulating responsibility | a guarantee that the relationship will work |

Mimimi's account deserves special care. She does not reduce Tomozaki to a trophy or a replacement for Tama. She loves both Tama and Tomozaki, but only the latter relation is romantic. Her admiration contains comparison—she sees in them a capacity to trust a chosen value that she lacks—but the attraction also depends on observed actions, shared time, and fear that Tomozaki will move beyond her reach.

Tomozaki eventually concludes that a “special reason” is often language attached after a non-calculable feeling appears. This does not make reasons false or relationships arbitrary. It limits the power of abstract criteria: compatibility can explain and test a choice, but it cannot substitute for making one.

## 7. Kikuchi retains authorship by revising, refusing, and making two endings

Kikuchi's authority survives the play's conversion from private manuscript to collective performance.

She adapts dialogue for performers, rewrites large portions when the characters begin to speak more vividly to her, answers interpretive questions, and accepts practical changes when they improve spoken rhythm. Hinami supplies exemplary acting and group momentum; Tomozaki supplies editorial questions and conceptual challenges; performers contribute embodiment and line-level knowledge. None of them receives final control.

Her most consequential revisions are also hers:

1. The first completed ending makes Chris assimilate to ordinary urban life through generalized effort.
2. After Tomozaki challenges the premise, Kikuchi gives Chris a path through her existing love of making flower ornaments, preserves the garden as a place to return, and makes external life fit the person rather than erase her.
3. Without telling Tomozaki, Kikuchi adds a public coda in which Libra and Alcia marry. This functions as her indirect refusal and as her attempt to preserve what she calls the world's ideal.
4. After the relationship begins, she writes a private novel ending in which Chris and Libra share matching flower ornaments and Chris declares love. Kikuchi identifies this version as her own ideal rather than the official one.

The last two versions should not be flattened into one stable message. They expose a still-active split between public/world-facing responsibility and private desire. Producing the private version is genuine movement, but V07 does not yet show that Kikuchi can hold both within one shared public life.

## 8. The play is evidence about its author, not an automatic romance cipher

The main roles draw on recognizable people:

- Libra's direct speech, curiosity, lock-opening, and insistence on accompanying Chris draw heavily on Tomozaki.
- Chris's enclosure, gray world, authorship, and struggle between assimilation and chosen habitat draw heavily on Kikuchi.
- Alcia's competence, protective severity, total possession, and empty center draw heavily on Hinami.

The mappings are textual and eventually recognized by several actors. They are still transformations rather than identity equations. Kikuchi combines observation, personal conflict, genre form, and the interview material she has gathered. Hinami's powerful performance of Alcia is performance evidence; it is not a direct confession that every Alcia line describes her.

The two endings make deterministic decoding especially unsafe. The public marriage of Libra and Alcia initially appears to settle the real triangle, yet the interpersonal conversation changes the result, and Kikuchi later authors a private Chris–Libra ending. Fiction here is best treated as a diagnostic and negotiating instrument: it makes values visible, lets actors test consequences, and can carry an indirect message. It does not supersede direct consent.

## 9. Hinami's construction becomes clearer while her origin remains closed

The inquiry produces several evidence layers that must remain distinct.

### 9.1 Direct and attributed observations

- Hinami gives a polished public explanation of her effort: habit, environmental expectations, option preservation, and future choice. Tomozaki judges the account false or accidentally overlapping with truth; Kikuchi independently notices that it contains no visible central motive.
- A middle-school classmate remembers Hinami as initially unremarkable, later increasingly optimized and famous. He recalls her circulating profile questionnaires to much of the class. Tomozaki interprets this as audience-data collection; the action is attributed recollection and the purpose is his inference.
- The classmate also recalls a first-year remark after hearing that her name meant growing straight toward the sun: “it has nothing to do with me.” Its meaning remains undefined.
- An elementary-school acquaintance remembers an obedient, non-demanding, family-oriented child in a warm and socially welcoming household.
- The elementary witness speaks of younger sisters, while the middle-school witness remembers one sister. The characters list possible explanations and then stop the inquiry because Hinami has not disclosed the matter.
- When Tomozaki asks for an efficient way to identify his own feeling, Hinami says she does not know. His narration marks unusual resignation and an apparent void in her voice before she returns to goal language.

### 9.2 Interpretations strengthened but not converted into fact

Mimimi proposes that Hinami, like her, may pursue first place because she cannot regard herself as special without external proof. The V06.5 retrospective supports a result/self-proof loop in middle school, but neither Mimimi nor Tomozaki has that reader knowledge. Even at reader level, the initiating cause remains unknown.

Kikuchi's Alcia says that possessing everything leaves her with nothing. That formulation is a strong model built from inquiry and observation. Hinami performs it with exceptional force and later asks how much Tomozaki influenced the script. Neither act proves that Kikuchi has recovered a complete hidden biography or that Hinami endorses the model.

V07 therefore advances a negative conclusion more securely than a positive one: Hinami's public competence does not reveal a stable, articulated “want” at its center. It does not establish bereavement, family rupture, lovelessness, or any singular cause.

The characters' decision to stop at the family discrepancy is also ethically important. Permission to ask acquaintances about Hinami's past does not become unlimited authority to investigate an intimate ambiguity she has never volunteered.

## 10. Kikuchi's failed assimilation distinguishes chosen effort from normative self-erasure

Kikuchi initially volunteers to run rehearsals so Tomozaki can practice with Mimimi. The choice is hers, but its motive is not therefore automatically owned in the stronger sense used by this project. She believes she must become a universally sociable `Popol` because doing so would make her fit the world's ideal.

The result is effort without recipient fit. She imitates Hinami's public manner, strains to produce brightness, and disrupts group coherence despite sincere work. Mizusawa identifies the governing difference: Tomozaki changed toward something he personally wanted, whereas Kikuchi is changing toward a world-level prescription. Tomozaki had initially praised the effort because he recognized his own surface trajectory and missed the inverted motive.

Kikuchi refines `player` into `author`. She experiences life as a composition whose beauty and other characters' claims must be considered. That is not merely false consciousness; it names a real ethical and creative capacity that repeatedly helps the group. The danger appears when the author excludes herself from the cast and treats her own desire as an impermissible distortion.

Tomozaki's response has two stages:

- The `炎人` exception shows that a well-made world can include beings who cannot or need not share one habitat. School-wide sociability is not the sole measure of belonging. An affinity community organized around Michael Andi offers a different lake rather than a remedial copy of the same school.
- The later hybrid argument does not ask Kikuchi to discard world-responsibility. It asks her to treat her own feeling as one of the world's real constraints and work for an arrangement that preserves both.

Kikuchi's change is thus not from introversion to extroversion. It is from compulsory universality toward selected habitat, valued craft, and an attempt to include the self among the people to whom an author owes care.

## 11. Festival performance tests competence as recovery, not perfection

Tomozaki's festival leadership remains self-authored. He studies rehearsal practice, calls the group together, translates next steps, coordinates with Yuzu, Kikuchi, performers, and technical work, and continues even when Hinami is absent. He values the play and its author, so the effort is not merely a status display.

His limits are equally visible. Hinami's quick interventions improve early rehearsal. When Tomozaki must divide time between the play and comedy, Kikuchi first overextends herself and later recruits Tachibana to handle coordination. The project succeeds partly through delegation, not through Tomozaki becoming an inexhaustible organizer.

During the comedy performance, Tomozaki's rehearsed sequence disappears under audience attention. Mimimi intentionally produces a conspicuous failed joke to give him a recoverable interaction. He responds through the ordinary rhythm the pair have built rather than by recalling a perfect script. Practice matters, but what survives pressure is relationally embodied timing and trust.

The episode qualifies any clean “character change completed” thesis. Tomozaki can look polished, attract attention, lead, and perform, yet still freeze. Growth is demonstrated by recovery, use of support, and continuation after failure—not by immunity to failure.

## 12. Mimimi bears the cost of recognition without becoming a consolation route

Mimimi's conduct is the volume's clearest test of whether another person's love will be treated as a strategic asset.

She asks Tomozaki to behave normally and makes sustained ordinary interaction possible. She supplies a direct account of her attraction. She notices the likely meaning of the play before he can fully face it. Most painfully, after seeing that Kikuchi's public ending appears to reject him, she sends him back to the library and insists that indirect fiction is not enough reason to surrender.

This action costs her. It advances the relationship she fears will close her own route. The narrative does not reward it by immediately giving her Tomozaki, nor does Tomozaki select her out of gratitude or rebound pain. He explicitly recognizes that doing so would exploit her care.

After Tomozaki and Kikuchi become a couple, Mimimi says her feeling remains, rejects repeated apology, and asks for ordinary mutual conduct. Her declaration that she will not love him forever preserves future agency rather than guaranteeing waiting. V07 supplies no evidence that the pain is resolved, that comparison has disappeared, or that “normal” interaction will be costless.

Tomozaki's response is responsible but incomplete in the ordinary human sense. He does not manipulate, deny, or reciprocate Mimimi's feeling. His chosen partnership communicates non-selection, and he listens to her account without false consolation. The coming boundary between friendship, lingering desire, and respect for Kikuchi remains to be negotiated.

## 13. Kikuchi's initial refusal and eventual consent must stay distinct

The public coda is an intentional indirect refusal. Tomozaki recognizes it as such and initially leaves. Returning is ethically defensible here only because he seeks direct clarification once rather than treating any rejection as a puzzle to defeat. Mimimi explicitly points out that a fictional ending is not a complete interpersonal answer.

In the library, Kikuchi confirms that she chose the coda, then reveals two facts the coda concealed: she wants the bright alternate world, and she regards wanting both integrity and personal love as selfish. Tomozaki does not argue that her ideal is irrelevant. He argues that his own selection is new world-level evidence: a relationship is not produced by Kikuchi privately rewriting everyone else's roles; he can choose her directly.

He then asks to date her. Her joyful response, their hand contact, and the next chapter's explicit shared understanding establish acceptance. The scene does not license persistence after an explicit direct no. It instead moves from an indirect artifact, through direct disclosure of conflict and reciprocal desire, to a direct request and acceptance.

The argument nevertheless leaves a live risk. Tomozaki's confidence and interpretive fluency could become invasive if future disagreement is always reframed as a lock he is entitled to open. V07 resolves this instance through reciprocal speech; it does not establish a general right to overcome another person's boundaries.

**Answer: yes, within a retained external scaffold.** He identifies real interest, refuses assigned confession, and selects Kikuchi because he wants that relationship. Hinami's architecture remains a causal condition rather than the terminal reason.

## 19. Rival readings and counterevidence

### 19.1 Rival: Hinami's family ambiguity explains everything

The discrepancy about the number of sisters is real attributed evidence. Death, separation, witness error, and other explanations remain possibilities raised by characters, not facts. The inquiry stops, and no causal bridge to Hinami's effort is established.

### 19.2 Rival: Alcia is Hinami's direct confession

Alcia is written by Kikuchi from observation and interview, then performed by Hinami. The model has strong resonance, especially around competence and emptiness, but neither authorship nor intense acting converts every line into Hinami's direct self-report.

### 19.3 Rival: Tomozaki's confession proves Hinami's curriculum was simply right

The curriculum creates opportunities, but Tomozaki explicitly changes its rule at the decisive point. The result supports structured practice and chosen action together; it does not show that assigned intimacy alone produces a sincere relationship.

### 19.4 Rival: choosing Kikuchi invalidates Tomozaki's bond with Mimimi

The comedy performance, Mimimi's direct explanation, and her costly intervention show deep person-specific trust. The absence of reciprocal romantic selection distinguishes category; it does not erase significance.

### 19.5 Rival: Mimimi's self-sacrifice is uncomplicated maturity

Her intervention is generous and agentic, but it intensifies her own pain and may repeat a pattern of converting personal hurt into performance for others. V07 gives no recovery evidence sufficient to call the cost harmless.

### 19.6 Rival: Kikuchi's public ending proves she did not want Tomozaki

Her direct later speech establishes the opposite. The ending expresses her belief about what she ought to want and whom the world requires, not absence of desire.

### 19.7 Rival: the private ending completely solves Kikuchi's split

It gives desire authored form and is genuine progress. Calling it unofficial and private may also preserve the division between public duty and private self. Integration remains prospective.

### 19.8 Rival: selected habitat means social skill and school friendship do not matter

Tomozaki explicitly describes school as one possible source of real, varied relationships and uses social skill throughout the volume. The correction is against compulsory universality, not against participation.

### 19.9 Rival: persistence after rejection is the lesson

Tomozaki returns after an indirect fictional message, asks why once, discovers concealed reciprocal desire and value conflict, and then makes a direct request. The scene does not support overriding an explicit direct refusal.

### 19.10 Rival: Tomozaki has now equaled Hinami as a fully stable social actor

He leads and chooses, but freezes publicly, requires help, and remains inside her broader curriculum. Growth is substantial without becoming equivalence or completion.
