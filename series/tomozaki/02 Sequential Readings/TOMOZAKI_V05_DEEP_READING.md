## 4. The coaching inversion: teaching begins by failing to clone the teacher

V05 makes Tomozaki a coach, but it does not validate him merely by changing his side of the desk.

His first move is to reproduce Hinami's method:

- define the social problem as insufficient handling of attacks;
- secretly begin an audio recording;
- make the learner hear the difference between intended and received expression;
- prescribe posture, expression, tone, and reduced-vocabulary drills.

This inheritance has two different meanings.

First, it proves that the V03-V04 hybrid is durable. Tomozaki possesses the exercises as usable knowledge, can explain why they work, and can deploy humor, tone, retort, and self-observation without Hinami scripting him. The skills are his equipment.

Second, it exposes the limit of equipment copied without a learner model. Tama already has:

- vivid expression;
- upright posture;
- clear projection;
- strong affective tone;
- fast retorts;
- little dependence on verbal sophistication to communicate emotion.

On Tomozaki's own evaluation, her fundamentals exceed his. The first curriculum therefore fails because it is optimized for his old deficit, not hers. V05 rejects the convenient idea that a successful student can teach by replaying his own route.

The secret recording also preserves an ethical defect from Hinami's instruction. Tama immediately calls it eavesdropping. Tomozaki continues because he believes the diagnostic benefit warrants concealment. Her later participation is willing, but later assent does not retroactively make the first capture consensual. This matters because V05 improves Tomozaki's bounded agency without making that improvement clean.

### 4.1 Error correction is the real evidence of pedagogical growth

Tomozaki does not defend the copied exercises after the mismatch appears. He changes the hypothesis.

The emerging diagnosis is not “Tama cannot communicate.” It is:

1. she closes herself to people associated with someone she distrusts;
2. the same direct statement is received differently depending on speaker history, tone, and settled public character;
3. she has little curiosity about much of the class and therefore few reasons to discover what to ask or share;
4. the current hostile atmosphere makes ordinary trial-and-error unusually costly.

The method then changes with the model:

- Mizusawa becomes a low-risk recipient;
- Takei becomes a comparison case and practice partner;
- Mimimi supplies a same-gender expressive model and person-specific knowledge;
- Kikuchi provides a neutral encounter and a theory of classmates as full people;
- Tama invents parts of her own practice;
- Tomozaki and Mizusawa iterate after observation instead of enforcing a fixed syllabus.

That is closer to generative co-authorship than anything in Hinami and Tomozaki's coaching so far. Tomozaki remains organizer and evaluator, but neither the goal decomposition nor the curriculum belongs to him alone.

## 5. Authentic change: preserve the governing judgment, change its reach

Tama's case gives the series its clearest answer yet to the problem of “changing oneself.”

The false binary is:

> remain exactly the same and stay authentic, or alter behavior and surrender the self.

V05 instead separates at least four layers:

| Layer | What changes? | V05 result |
|---|---|---|
| governing end | protect Mimimi and act according to Tama's sense of right | preserved |
| evaluative core | group attack is wrong even when the target has done wrong | preserved and publicly demonstrated |
| relational orientation | classmates are no longer dismissed as an undifferentiated outside world | broadened |
| expressive interface | brighter tone, visible vulnerability, comic repetition, and recipient-sensitive entry | deliberately changed |

At the climax, the distinction becomes causal rather than theoretical.

Tama sees the class convert Konno into an authorized target. She makes the same substantive kind of objection she would have made before training: collective retaliation is still wrong. But she now has:

- relationships that give her credibility;
- a public character the class can interpret;
- a brighter delivery that does not sound like rejection of everyone present;
- a repeatable comic line that releases tension without withdrawing the principle.

Her core did not need replacement. It needed a route to other people.

This confirms goal ownership but also deepens it. Ownership is not only the right to accept or refuse an assigned end. It includes the right to decide which part of oneself is non-negotiable and which parts are tools, habits, or horizons that can be revised in order to make the non-negotiable part effective.

### 5.1 The cost of chosen adaptation remains real

Voluntary change does not erase coercive circumstances.

Tama chooses the training for Mimimi, but she makes that choice under continuing harassment. The first tactical success requires her to stop protesting when her desk and possessions are attacked. That reduces the class's resentment, but it transfers immediate cost onto the victim and leaves the aggressor untouched.

The narrative does not let endurance scale indefinitely. When the shared strap is destroyed, Tama says she wants to flee. Her strength is not infinite, and the class's dependence on her ability to absorb harm is itself part of the wrong.

The strongest formulation is therefore:

> Tama's adaptation is authentic because she owns its governing reason and retains judgment, not because the environment placed a fair choice before her.

## 6. Social legibility: content, recipient, history, and “consistent vulnerability”

V05 compares Tama, Tomozaki, and Takei because all three can say what they think directly while producing different social results.

The difference cannot be reduced to propositional content. During the earlier event selection, Takei can openly want soccer and be treated as familiar comic excess; Tama can openly want volleyball and create tension. The reception function includes:

- delivery;
- perceived hostility;
- existing relationship;
- status;
- prior expectations;
- whether the speaker exposes a stable, safe-to-recognize vulnerability.

Mizusawa calls the last feature 一貫した隙: a consistent opening or vulnerability. Hinami's conspicuous cheese enthusiasm and Takei's permanent lack of restraint allow others to feel that they know how to read them. Repetition turns an isolated act into an established character, and “that is so like them” replaces threat or uncertainty.

The small-stature routine gives Tama a repeatable invitation for others to join. It works because:

1. it uses a visible trait she can acknowledge;
2. it fits her existing retort strength;
3. it is deliberately exaggerated rather than plausibly deceptive;
4. it gives the audience a known response;
5. it creates shared play without changing the substantive point she may later make.

This is not proof that public self-branding is inherently liberating. A settled “character” can simplify a person and become another constraint. V05 shows its enabling side under crisis conditions; it does not yet test whether Tama can refuse the routine or outgrow the class's new interpretation.

### 6.1 Recipient fit defeats a universal-deficit reading

Tama and Mizusawa become comfortable quickly once he changes how he approaches her. He asks directly, accepts direct answers, withdraws when she appears closed, and returns as an explicitly willing collaborator. Her bluntness is enjoyable to him rather than socially defective.

The relationship changes through mutual adjustment:

- Tama risks contact across an old group boundary;
- Mizusawa changes register and pacing;
- Tomozaki creates the opening;
- both discover compatibility not visible inside Nakamura-group conflict.

The result is strong counterevidence to any claim that Tama carries a portable communication deficit that produces the same outcome everywhere.

## 7. Curiosity is not prior purity; practice can create sincere interest

Tomozaki's most important teaching comes from his own history.

He first entered conversation for instrumental reasons: tasks, level gains, and proof that the game could be learned. Only after repeated contact did classmates stop looking like scenery. Skill enabled access; access produced information; information produced curiosity; curiosity then supplied sincere conversational content.

The sequence is:

> trained entry → encounter → person-specific knowledge → curiosity → reciprocal conversation.

This extends the hybrid model. A relationship need not begin from spontaneous fascination in order to become genuine. Deliberate practice can create the conditions under which unforced interest emerges.

Tama's starting limit is narrower than Tomozaki's old contempt, but structurally related. She can understand an individual in front of her, yet treats much of the class as a world she cannot or does not want to enter. Her missing first step is not a fabricated topic. It is the provisional belief that the people inside that world may be worth knowing.

### 7.1 From “followers” and “villagers” to people

Tomozaki catches himself treating Akiyama as one of Konno's “followers.” Mizusawa's correction and Kikuchi's later account dismantle that compression.

Akiyama is simultaneously:

- subordinated within Konno's group;
- a likely instrument of prior harassment;
- resentful;
- responsive to an outside alliance;
- capable of retaliation;
- later capable of an apology to Tama.

These positions do not collapse into innocence or guilt alone.

Kikuchi generalizes the point. Every classroom “character” has circumstances, frustration, growth, and self-deception. She describes Konno's aggression and the class's conformity as weakness—flight from unresolved discomfort—but immediately maintains that the conduct is wrong.

Her account preserves a distinction V04 left open:

> explanation can enlarge the person without reducing accountability.

## 8. The crowd system: how aggression becomes “punishment”

Mizusawa identifies the mechanism before the climax. If Tama continues visibly disrupting class peace, classmates may acquire a justification for attack: she does not read the room, therefore she deserves correction. Aggression becomes “punishment,” and the punitive label functions as a license.

V05 then runs the mechanism twice.

### 8.1 First cycle: Tama as authorized target

1. Konno keeps attacks deniable and below the sympathy threshold.
2. Tama protests visibly.
3. The class experiences the protest, not the covert initiating act, as the repeated disruption.
4. Stored irritation recodes Tama as the problem.
5. Avoidance risks becoming participation.

Tama's restraint interrupts accumulation but does not address Konno's aggression.

### 8.2 Second cycle: Konno as authorized target

Hinami builds a condition in which Konno crosses her normal political limit:

- she cultivates Akiyama's existing grievance and supplies an outside evaluative frame;
- she has already constrained Yuzu and Nakamura's contact, producing additional strain in the relevant relationship network;
- she provides Tama an alibi when Konno's lead case is found damaged;
- she presses for apology at the moment Konno is vulnerable;
- she uses bodily orientation subtle enough that Konno can misread Nakamura and Yuzu as intervening together;
- she publicly names Konno's romantic feeling;
- she arranges for Nakamura's tissue—inside Yuzu's handmade cover—to become the final relational cue.

Some causal details remain unproved. V05 does not identify who damaged Konno's lead case, and Tomozaki does not have access to every prior conversation between Hinami and Akiyama. What is directly visible is enough: Hinami deliberately exploits the confrontation, controls cue direction, predicts the emotional wound, and keeps escalating after Konno is already defeated.

Konno pushes Akiyama and refuses to apologize. Those are her actions. The class's stored resentment then receives permission from Hinami's condemnation. Former allies, sports-group students, and anonymous classmates convert humiliation and petty violence into “justice.”

The structural recurrence is exact:

> grievance → justification → moral license → distributed attack → target unable to answer without confirming the charge.

Changing the target does not change the system.

## 9. Hinami's Tama exception: desire appears, but self-examination does not

Hinami makes two unusually clear concessions.

First, she recognizes authority:

- Tama wants to change;
- Tomozaki wants to help;
- Hinami has no right to stop them.

Second, she recognizes preference:

- she does not say change is strategically inferior;
- she says she does not want Tama to change;
- she says she does not want the original direct resistance to have been wrong.

Tama identifies the difference between a recommendation and a wish immediately. Hinami's position is now evidence of desire that redirects her own target selection. It cannot be reduced to failure to understand feeling or to neutral optimization.

Her wording also sounds like atonement to Tomozaki, but V05 provides no event that identifies what would be atoned for. The source supports affective structure, not origin.

### 9.1 Emotional comprehension becomes a weapon

V04 showed that Hinami can model emotion inside optimization. V05 removes any remaining ambiguity.

She understands:

- Akiyama's humiliation inside the group;
- Konno's fashion pride;
- Konno's former desire for Nakamura;
- the pain of seeing Nakamura and Yuzu act as a couple;
- the class's accumulated resentment;
- the importance of public cue direction;
- the threshold at which condemnation becomes self-propelling.

The problem is not emotional illiteracy. It is that accurate understanding does not constrain use.

Tomozaki stops one continuation with a touch and a warning look. Hinami appears to close the scene, then embeds the tissue-cover strike inside the closure itself. Her action therefore cannot be dismissed as a brief loss of control. Rage supplies the end; disciplined technique supplies the means.

### 9.2 Tama sees the act and preserves the person

Tama does not merely notice that Hinami was cruel. She infers that Hinami did something she herself did not want to become because Tama had been hurt.

This interpretation does not excuse the act. Tama says she did not want to see Hinami act that way, then refuses to accept the polished denial: “So it really is like that.”

Her trust is neither blindness nor complete access. She sees enough to reject the mask's account while continuing to believe there is a person worth protecting behind it.

Hinami does not reciprocate that exposure. She denies, restores the public face, and offers no doctrinal revision. The exception is exposed interpersonally but remains unprocessed by Hinami.

## 10. Tama's intervention is the ethical endpoint, not Hinami's victory

Hinami succeeds at making Konno vulnerable. She does not succeed at controlling the resulting crowd.

The class:

- mocks Konno's tears;
- repeats her own language against her;
- kicks her desk and possessions;
- treats her prior wrongdoing as permission for unlimited return harm.

Tama interrupts as the person with the strongest plausible revenge claim. That position matters, but her reason is not private forgiveness. It is a general rule: if everyone does the same thing back to one person, they become equally wrong.

She then uses the training to make the boundary socially receivable. The comic line does not dilute the judgment. It prevents the class from construing her judgment as another rejection of the class.

This produces a bounded result:

- reverse persecution stops;
- the class's interpretation of Tama changes;
- Konno eventually re-enters her peer group;
- Konno's harassment of Tama ends.

It does not produce:

- a demonstrated apology from Konno to Tama;
- restitution for the destroyed strap;
- institutional recognition;
- a general anti-bullying norm;
- a procedure for future covert aggression;
- proof that Konno accepts Tama's judgment.

Visible peace is therefore real, valuable, and incomplete.

## 11. Relationship and supporting-character developments

### 11.1 Mizusawa: owned desire persists and changes his conduct

Mizusawa's V03 confession is directly retested.

He does not retreat into frictionless competence after Hinami's rejection. He:

- visits Tomozaki and Tama without being asked;
- accepts social risk inside Nakamura's group;
- supplies political knowledge and practice design;
- reveals that he hoped Hinami would be present;
- says he is acting partly because he wants to;
- credits Tomozaki with teaching him to move toward what he wants;
- adopts Tama and Tomozaki's directness;
- tells Tomozaki plainly that he still likes Hinami.

This is persistent owned effort after failure. It does not guarantee a renewed romantic attempt or desired outcome.

Mizusawa also sets an important epistemic boundary. He suspects Tomozaki has privileged knowledge of Hinami but decides it would be unfair to extract that knowledge from him. He asks Tomozaki for his own feeling instead. This is a mature contrast with both covert recording and Hinami's manipulation: curiosity does not automatically license access.

His relationship with Tomozaki now has direct dyadic substance. They plan alone, share food, discuss private motive, correct one another under pressure, and exchange a potentially rivalrous question without the friendship dissolving.

### 11.2 Kikuchi: movement becomes social and contributive

Kikuchi's post-V04 intention to move is behaviorally visible.

She accepts Tomozaki's independently initiated invitation, enters a multi-person setting despite anxiety, speaks with Tama, offers an interpretation no one else had supplied, joins the larger after-school group, and describes the experience as enjoyable.

Her contribution is not decorative support for Tomozaki. The “every classroom character has a life” model changes Tama's understanding and surprises Mizusawa. Kikuchi enters the social world as an interpreter who affects it.

Tomozaki and Kikuchi's reciprocity also strengthens:

- he thinks of her as the best low-risk collaborator for reasons grounded in her actual position;
- she accepts a shared goal;
- she tells him that he repeatedly shows her unknown scenes;
- seeing his peer world helps her understand his change.

Tomozaki's attraction becomes harder for him to ignore, especially when Mizusawa calls her cute and when the group leaves them alone. This is evidence of feeling, not a settled relationship label.

### 11.3 Mimimi and Tama: care is costly, reciprocal, and often hidden

Mimimi's cheer remains deliberately performed and sincerely directed. Tomozaki notices reduced energy in her manner; she supplies replacement lead, decorates a hidden case, stays close, and tries to keep Tama from treating the destroyed keepsake as an irreparable relational loss.

Tama's entire training effort is hidden from Mimimi because it is meant as repayment for Mimimi's earlier unannounced care. When the strap is destroyed, Tama's collapse centers on having harmed Mimimi, even though she did nothing wrong. Their protective secrecy is loving, but it also hides the other's burden and makes each vulnerable to taking excessive responsibility.

The appended dual-viewpoint bonus confirms reciprocity:

- Mimimi silently declines a group karaoke invitation because she knows Tama dislikes the setting;
- Tama silently has shrimp removed from Mimimi's meal because she knows Mimimi dislikes shrimp;
- neither treats care as a debt to announce;
- Mimimi later proposes lower-risk dyadic karaoke;
- Tama accepts because trying it with Mimimi may change the experience.

The bonus does not prove a post-V05 state. Its own placement is paratextual and discussed separately below.

### 11.4 Yuzu: maintenance after judgment

Yuzu remains the series' clearest maintenance actor.

She:

- accepts Hinami's request to reduce visible contact with Nakamura in order to limit Konno's stress;
- notices Hinami's strain and volunteers to monitor it;
- offers to repair the torn strap using a skill she has been developing;
- follows Konno when the rest of the class does not;
- stays near her during the subsequent status collapse;
- materially helps her return to ordinary peer life.

This labor prevents “defeating Konno” from becoming permanent expulsion. It does not establish that Konno apologized or that Yuzu's support produced accountability. Care and adjudication remain separate.

### 11.5 Konno: wrongdoer, strategist, and vulnerable adolescent

V05 adds person-level detail without reversing moral responsibility.

Konno:

- conducts sustained covert harassment;
- manages the sympathy threshold strategically or instinctively;
- uses lower-status group members for dirty work;
- can be kinder to those same members in private;
- retains political skill after defeat;
- is deeply vulnerable around Nakamura and Yuzu;
- reacts to public shame by doubling down until she loses control.

Her tears and humiliation matter because she is a person, not because they cancel her actions. Conversely, her actions do not authorize the class's unlimited retaliation. V05 insists on both propositions.

### 11.6 Male-peer belonging and team identity

“Team Tomozaki” begins as Mizusawa's joke and becomes an accurate functional description.

Tomozaki:

- convenes people;
- allocates tasks;
- accepts advice;
- recruits across group boundaries;
- is trusted to act without explaining every detail;
- uses “we” when he tells the others to leave Tama to him and Mizusawa.

Takei participates as more than a joke even when he does not follow every analytical step. His openness supplies a safe practice relation, a comparison case, emotional endorsement, and later shared group membership. Nakamura remains less directly connected to Tomozaki, but the final outing places Tomozaki inside the ordinary mixed peer group rather than outside it.

## 14. Adjudication of frozen V05 questions

### V05-Q01 — Can Tama acquire technique without ceding authorship?

**SUPPORTED STRONGLY.** She accepts, invents, tests, and applies technique while retaining veto and moral judgment. Her climactic defense of Konno is the strongest evidence: altered expression serves an unchanged principle.

### V05-Q02 — Does Tomozaki identify the learner's goal before selecting methods?

**PARTIALLY SUPPORTED / REVISED.** He preserves “do not make Mimimi sad” as the governing reason, but does not fully decompose the goal before copying his own drills. The learner model and curriculum emerge through failure, observation, and collaborative correction.

### V05-Q03 — What ends Konno's harassment, and who bears the cost?

**ANSWERED AS A COMPOUND PROCESS.** Tama's restraint and new peer ties weaken the original atmosphere; discovery of the team triggers escalation; Hinami engineers Konno's public overreach and status collapse; Tama stops reverse persecution; Yuzu supports reintegration. Tama bears the longest direct cost, Konno bears engineered humiliation and temporary persecution, Akiyama is instrumentalized and injured, and the wider coalition supplies repair and maintenance labor.

### V05-Q04 — Can Hinami examine her Tama exception?

**EXPOSED BUT NOT RESOLVED.** She admits preference and lack of authority, then acts from the exception. Tama identifies the contradiction and cruelty; Hinami denies rather than examines it.

### V05-Q05 — Does Tomozaki's bounded agency improve?

**MIXED-IMPROVING.** He uses explicit learner choice, distributes planning, models risk, stops his own angry retaliation, and checks Hinami. The covert recording, victim-borne restraint, premature celebration, and missed discovery risk prevent a clean success.

### V05-Q06 — Can atmosphere change without creating a new enemy?

**FALSIFIER OCCURS, THEN IS CORRECTED.** Hinami changes the class by making Konno the authorized enemy. Tama rejects the substitution, and Yuzu's later care helps prevent permanent expulsion. The better equilibrium is produced only after the dangerous reversal occurs.

### V05-Q07 — What is the cost of Mimimi's caregiver role?

**SUPPORTED, WITH DISTRIBUTION.** Her performance weakens, she supplies material and emotional care, and the damaged symbol hurts her through Tama. The team reduces isolation, while the appended bonus proves Tama's care for Mimimi is reciprocal rather than one-way.

### V05-Q08 — Does Kikuchi's movement become observable?

**SUPPORTED.** She accepts a group task, crosses a social boundary, changes Tama's model, joins the wider interaction, and values the experience. No romance outcome follows.

### V05-Q09 — Does male-peer belonging persist?

**SUPPORTED.** Mizusawa and Takei participate outside the tournament; Mizusawa-Tomozaki gains direct friendship evidence. Nakamura-Tomozaki remains mostly group-mediated.

### V05-Q10 — Does goal negotiation become generative co-authorship?

**SUPPORTED IN THE TAMA PROJECT.** The plan is produced by Tomozaki, Tama, Mizusawa, Takei, Mimimi, and Kikuchi. Hinami neither defines nor controls its end.

### V05-Q11 — Does the series distinguish causal explanation from justice?

**SUPPORTED STRONGLY.** V05 explains Konno, the class, and Akiyama without excusing them; then shows explicitly that justified grievance can become unjust collective punishment.

## 15. Rival readings and counterevidence

### 15.1 Rival: Tama succeeds by becoming less authentic

Her most consequential post-training act is the same kind of principled objection she would previously have made. What changes is relational reach and expressive form. The evidence supports preserved evaluative authorship, not self-erasure.

### 15.2 Rival: Tama was already perfect and did not need to change

Her moral judgment did not need replacement, but she acknowledges disinterest in classmates, closes herself across group boundaries, and cannot initially make an identical directness land as Takei can. “The class is unjust” and “Tama has learnable relational limits” are simultaneously true.

### 15.3 Rival: Hinami simply gives Konno deserved consequences

Konno deserves accountability for harassment and for pushing Akiyama. Hinami goes beyond securing accountability: she engineers misperception, exposes a private romantic wound, continues after defeat, and releases a crowd that damages possessions and mocks tears. Proportional judgment and humiliation are not identical.

### 15.4 Rival: Hinami loses control because she is emotional

Her anger is real, but her execution requires sustained control. She recruits, primes, observes, uses subtle directional cues, and hides a final strike inside an apparent de-escalation. Emotion changes her end; it does not eliminate planning.

### 15.5 Rival: Tomozaki solves the crisis alone

He initiates and coordinates the training, but the working model depends on Mizusawa's political analysis, Tama's consent and invention, Takei's contrast, Mimimi's relational knowledge, Kikuchi's person model, and Yuzu's repair labor. Hinami independently ends the original power balance, and Tama supplies the ethical limit.

### 15.6 Rival: the peaceful ending proves justice was completed

The harassment ceases and ordinary group life returns. The source does not show Konno apologizing to Tama, admitting the harassment, replacing the damaged object, or accepting a durable rule. Peace and repair overlap without becoming equivalent.

### 15.7 Rival: interest in people is authentic only if spontaneous

Tomozaki's interest develops after instrumental practice. Tama's expands after deliberate exposure and borrowed knowledge. V05 treats cultivated conditions as compatible with later sincerity.

### 15.8 Rival: Kikuchi excuses wrongdoing as weakness

She explicitly says the conduct is wrong and that the situation must be solved. “Weakness” explains the flight mechanism and preserves human complexity; it does not remove responsibility.

### 15.9 Rival: Tama forgives everything

She stops retaliation and receives Akiyama's apology with a weighty acknowledgment. She does not announce that nothing happened, excuse Konno, or erase the cost.
