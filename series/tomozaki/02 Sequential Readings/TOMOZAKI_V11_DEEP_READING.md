### 4.2 The video breaks the celebration's implied contract

The mother's message does not reveal the Nagisa event or directly condemn Hinami. It lovingly praises Aoi's transformation from a child who nearly finished last in a marathon to a nationally successful runner. The problem is the explanatory form. The message turns adversity into a prewritten route toward present excellence and treats the result as evidence that the path was meaningful.

Hinami's reaction is therefore not adequately described as embarrassment at family intimacy. She shouts for the video to stop, demands responsibility from Yuzu and Nakamura, says the group lacks the information required to judge her life, and leaves. When Tomozaki follows, she first refuses explanation and then distinguishes three propositions:

1. the party really was enjoyable;
2. the care and gratitude demonstrate that `日南葵` has produced valuable effects;
3. none of those effects proves that the standards behind her life are objectively correct or that the resulting persona affirms the self beneath it.

Tomozaki initially answers with consequence: people are grateful, so her way cannot be wholly wrong. Hinami answers at the level of warrant: what proves that the conventional values supporting those judgments are themselves correct? This is not merely perfectionism asking for more praise. It is an infinite regress about the authority behind praise, winning, and social value. Her phrases `私は空っぽ` and `私は弱キャラ` directly overturn the public strong-character classification as a self-description. They do not establish an omniscient diagnosis, but V11 later supplies substantial developmental corroboration.

### 4.3 Public competence collapses when its governing reason collapses

The V10 state allowed private rupture to coexist with public functioning. V11 ends that coexistence. Hinami does not return calls during spring break, fails to attend school at the start of third year, and leaves student-council work without the handoff that her established competence would predict. The absence matters because the source shows that her skills remain available: she can still analyze, train, and defeat Tomozaki in Atafami. What has failed is not generic ability but the reason for deploying it across ordinary life.

The third-year class division materializes the relational consequence. Tomozaki, Kikuchi, Mizusawa, and Mimimi share the regular humanities course; Nakamura, Yuzu, Tama, and Takei enter science; Hinami enters the advanced course in a different building with a separate gate and routine. Administrative placement does not cause the rupture, but it removes passive opportunities for repair. Nakamura's refusal to help is also not simple cruelty. He was directly attacked after participating in the video and objects to Yuzu being made to feel personally responsible. The group retains care but loses coordinated confidence and shared access.

### 4.4 Admiration has a two-way causal structure

Haruka and Mimimi clarify why Hinami's collapse has effects outside Hinami.

Haruka says she became a student-council president by following the sister who promised to show her a perfect example. Mimimi says that an admired person's visible unhappiness can make the admirer doubt the route they are following. The structure is not merely `fan wants idol to be happy`. It is:

`model supplies a credible life route -> admirer invests effort through that route -> model's collapse threatens the meaning of the admirer's own effort`.

This does not make Hinami responsible for maintaining a false face forever. It does show why `just stop performing` is not cost-free. Her public form has become part of other people's practical reasons. The friends' takeover of the student-council event temporarily preserves one result of that form without requiring Hinami to perform it herself. This is a bounded support act, not a restoration of Hinami's belief.

Ashigaru supplies the complementary direction. A professional is not only a winner; the professional becomes a story to which spectators attach hope. Being believed in can itself become a reason to persist. Tomozaki experiences this when a younger player describes him as a model even after his fourth-place finish. The volume therefore rejects the simple claim that external recognition is fake. Recognition can create real reason and reciprocal obligation. The danger lies in making all self-worth depend on it or treating the admirer as a resource the model must never disappoint.

### 4.5 The family history changes the causal model without closing it

Haruka first describes a household saturated in praise: Yoko repeatedly told the daughters that they were good, remarkable, loved, and welcome in the world. Kikuchi correctly infers that Hinami was not originally empty in the sense of never having possessed a governing belief. She had a comprehensive one. Yoko converted bad events into meaningful steps toward later good, and the young Aoi trusted those stories as a source of safety.

V11 then gives a long, first-person, internally focalized history from Hinami. Within that account:

- Yoko's loving conduct is direct and repeated; the account does not support a simple neglectful or affectionless-parent reading;
- an unnamed private room and repeated practice appear to support Yoko's metaphysical certainty, but the source does not name a religion, organization, doctrine, or supernatural fact;
- Nagisa objects to classmates bullying another child and uses Buin's `おにただ` ideal as a reason to intervene;
- Yoko praises Nagisa's goodness but frames even the target's suffering as a meaningful trial and does not take shown concrete action to alter the school situation;
- Nagisa becomes a target, deteriorates, and eventually questions whether correct action has any meaning;
- Nagisa later dies after entering the road; V11 does not narrate the entry itself or provide intent;
- at the hospital, Yoko grieves and cares for Aoi and Haruka while still framing Nagisa's death as a meaningful event selected by the world;
- Aoi experiences that statement as proof that the entire family meaning system is false, and forcibly rejects the inherited standard.

The strongest source-grounded formation model is therefore not merely `sister died -> Hinami became perfectionist`. It is:

`total retrospective meaning supplied by beloved authority`

`-> growing contradiction between reassuring story and unaltered harm`

`-> Nagisa's death makes the contradiction impossible to defer`

`-> inherited meaning is rejected as self-protective falsification`

`-> the self formed inside it is experienced as contaminated and empty`

`-> responsibility is moved before the result: only controllable causes and achieved outcomes may be trusted`

`-> victory, demand, and reproducibility become substitute warrants for value`.

This materially strengthens the causal account of Hinami's later system. It still does not resolve whether Nagisa entered the road intentionally, identify the complete bullying sequence, establish what school or adults knew, or prove that every later mask and goal has only one cause.

### 4.6 Self-responsibility is both acquisition rule and punishment rule

Tomozaki identifies the double edge of Hinami's regime. If every result is attributable to the actor's own controllable effort, success can be fully counted as personal value. But the same rule makes loss personally attributable too. The regime therefore offers a replacement for Yoko's retrospective meaning while also making Hinami vulnerable to limitless self-blame.

Hinami confirms the central formation facts through the history she chooses to tell and by describing victory, need, and recognition as replacements for the lost god. The text supports the mechanism more strongly than V10 did. It does not make Tomozaki omniscient. His exact formulation that Hinami thinks everything about Nagisa is her responsibility remains an interpretation, though her account of trying to eradicate her inherited self and her later dependence on controllable outcomes substantially corroborate it.

The title of Chapter 4 foregrounds the remaining limit: even maximal level and preparation cannot control bad luck and random variation. Tomozaki offers this as permission to put down some responsibility. Hinami does not accept the move because it leaves her without a trusted basis for value. Telling her that she is fine without a basis repeats the shape of the reassurance system she has already experienced as false.

### 4.7 Tomozaki repeats the ownership error at a higher level

Tomozaki knows he is crossing boundaries. He waits outside Hinami's home, develops a private channel with Haruka, asks Haruka not to tell Hinami, recruits Kikuchi and Mizusawa, and presses Hinami after she refuses. His care is real, his analysis improves, and his actions produce evidence that no one person had. None of those facts gives him ownership of Hinami's end.

His climactic offer first asks Hinami to believe herself without reason, then asks her to believe him instead. The second offer sounds relational rather than individualistic, but its authority remains unilateral: Tomozaki declares that he will guarantee Hinami's correctness because he is the one person she cannot beat. Hinami accepts a one-on-one Atafami match and defeats him with Boxman after deliberately studying his patterns during her absence. This does not prove Tomozaki has no value or that one match objectively decides life's quality. It does invalidate the specific asymmetric credential on which his command rests.

The defeat also exposes a limit in Tomozaki's strong-character self-concept. He earlier calls himself a strong character because he can believe himself without external reason. When Hinami removes his unique superiority, he collapses, skips school twice, rejects Kikuchi's speech, and loses the desire to play. The volume does not erase his development. It shows that his confidence also relied on a protected domain and a story about what only he could do for Hinami.

### 4.8 Atafami remains relationship, laboratory, and weapon

Tomozaki's move from Found to Jack receives its first offline tournament test. He finishes fourth at his first event, discovers a young admirer, and receives Ashigaru's professional distinction between strength and a compelling player story. This is meaningful route evidence but not a completed career proof.

Hinami's Boxman selection is equally important. She abandons the visually admired Found-like ideal and chooses a defensive, resource-accumulating character built around low-risk advantage and even timeout judgment. She loses the first two games, records Tomozaki's patterns, adapts, and wins the next three. The play directly demonstrates learning, patience, and ruthless result orientation. It does not show that she has no feelings about style: she explicitly says Tomozaki's play was beautiful and that she wanted to resemble it, then says she could imitate only winning.

The set is therefore not a pure return to gaming intimacy. It is simultaneously:

- voluntary contact;
- highly personal knowledge of Tomozaki's play;
- reciprocity through a game both value;
- proof that Hinami's competence remains active;
- rejection of Tomozaki's authority claim; and
- a new rupture when she declares life a bad game.

Calling it reconciliation would erase the result; calling it only hostility would erase why this is the medium Hinami chooses for the confrontation.

### 4.9 Kikuchi's creative problem becomes motive, consent, and consequence

The publication approach from Shobunsha gives Kikuchi's writing its first professional gate: an editor has read the posted novel, proposes continuation with publication in view, and offers a possible September target. The offer is conditional on the completed work and does not establish a final contract, publication, income, or career sustainability.

Kikuchi initially considers rebuilding Alcia to avoid using Hinami. Her fluent reasons for saving the original inquiry for a later book are shown as a prepared self-persuasion rather than settled desire. When she meets Haruka, her interpretive drive resumes. From sparse family evidence she constructs the `lost god` model and reaches Haruka's painful memory. The inference is powerful and substantially corroborated later. Its accuracy does not make the way it was obtained harmless.

Kikuchi then revises the missing-motive question. She does not only need Hinami's motive; she needs her own reason for writing when the act can wound real people. Tomozaki's early answer—that this invasive force is simply her writer's nature—protects her vocation from shame but risks naturalizing harm. Kikuchi refuses that shortcut. She asks whether writing deserves priority over another person's feelings.

The final movement does not fully solve consent. Kikuchi writes and posts Alcia's internal material using stories Tomozaki told her, then asks the whole group to help finish the project. No permission from Hinami has yet been obtained. What changes is the governance model and purpose:

- Kikuchi names the harm capacity rather than treating artistic insight as self-justifying;
- Tomozaki supplies an explicit reason—reach and affirm Hinami—rather than merely demanding content;
- fiction is used because it reaches Tomozaki when direct propositions fail, not because it is declared infallible;
- authorship expands from a dyad to a party of people whose different limits and experiences can contest one another; and
- the project remains prospective at the endpoint, so success, consent, and collateral effect are unresolved.

### 4.10 Fiction does not become factual testimony

The posted second chapter of `純混血とアイスクリーム` reworks the V02 election structure from Alcia's interior. Alcia beats Nana in craft, questions the value of empty victory, plans to lose a later duel, and finds herself unable to do so because defeat threatens the only value she can still recognize. This is strong evidence of Kikuchi's current model of Hinami and of Tomozaki's response to that model. It is not direct evidence that Hinami literally tried to lose to Mimimi, felt suicidal in the forest scene, or would speak Alcia's exact propositions.

The novel's successful intervention in Tomozaki demonstrates a distinctive affordance. A proposition he can reject when addressed directly becomes an experience he can inhabit when distributed across characters and consequences. Fiction can therefore bypass defensive argument and reorganize perspective. It can also overreach, assign interior states without consent, and make the recipient feel seen in ways the writer cannot control. V11 preserves both properties.

### 4.11 The endpoint transfers responsibility; it does not resolve the crisis

Tomozaki and Kikuchi reconcile after he apologizes for rejecting her and thanks her for reaching him. Kikuchi accepts his offered reason as an initial reason to write while reserving the need to discover one of her own. She then rejects the formula `the two of us can do it` and asks for Mimimi, Mizusawa, Yuzu, Tama, and the other important characters to participate.

This is a structural correction to the lone-hero model. Different people understand different costs: Mimimi and Haruka understand admired models; Mizusawa understands unspoken loneliness; Yuzu understands maintenance and affiliation; Tama understands principled refusal; Kikuchi understands narrative motive; Tomozaki understands games, self-belief, and Hinami's direct disclosures. The endpoint proposes that no single interpretation is enough.

But the party has not yet assembled, Hinami has not consented, the story has not been completed or delivered, and the school absence has not ended. V11 closes on project formation, not rescue.

Tomozaki's most consequential V11 error is not inadequate cleverness. He performs extensive inquiry and correctly reconstructs much of the causal structure. The failure is treating correct understanding, specialness, and willingness to bear responsibility as sufficient authority to choose Hinami's route. His final party model is a promising revision, not yet proof that the same error has been solved.

### 5.2 Hinami

Hinami's public performance breaks after the video and remains suspended through the volume. Her private capacity does not disappear: she can narrate the family history, train Boxman, analyze Tomozaki's habits, and reverse a two-game deficit. The separation between competence and governing reason is therefore unusually clear.

Her developmental history supports a three-stage model:

1. inherited retrospective meaning and unconditional family affirmation;
2. catastrophic collapse of that meaning when it validates Nagisa's death;
3. substitute value through antecedent cause, self-responsibility, victory, demand, and repeatable proof.

This makes her later system intelligible without making it complete or healthy. It also explains why consequence-based reassurance is insufficient. Praise is not scarce. Trusted warrant is.

Hinami says she can only win and ultimately declares life a bad game. These are crisis self-descriptions after she defeats Tomozaki, not permanent authorial verdicts. She names no affirmative future end, does not return to school within the source, and does not accept the planned collective intervention.

### 5.3 Kikuchi

Kikuchi gains a real publication opportunity and simultaneously loses a simple moral warrant for using her strongest ability. Her insight into the family system is accurate enough to unlock Haruka's disclosure, yet its immediate effect is to make a younger girl cry. She refuses to let artistic vocation automatically excuse that harm.

As Tomozaki's partner, she directly asserts their relationship when Rena touches and belittles him. Later, Tomozaki's obsession with Hinami makes her say that describing Tomozaki and Hinami as mutually special hurts because Tomozaki is her boyfriend. When he rejects even her speech, she ends the call rather than silently absorbing it. The later reconciliation is therefore not costless support; she names the injury, receives an apology, and accepts a co-authored project whose reason is explicit.

Her final request for the whole group prevents both novelist-as-omniscient-author and couple-as-exclusive-rescue-unit. The source still does not show a standing rule governing use of friends as fictional material, disclosure to Hinami, or withdrawal if the project harms its subject.

### 5.4 Haruka

Haruka becomes a direct participant rather than a family-video object. She is a middle-school student and student-council president who credits Hinami's example with helping her recover direction after family loss. She wants access to Hinami's school life, fears what Hinami's collapse means for her own route, and initially cannot discuss Nagisa with her sister.

Her dependence is not passive imitation. She campaigns, works, and chooses the student-council role. Hinami supplies the believable model through which Haruka organizes that effort. Haruka's plea to Hinami therefore carries both love and burden. The source does not establish that maintaining Haruka's faith should take priority over Hinami's need to stop performing.

### 5.5 Mizusawa

Mizusawa explains that he first wanted Hinami to understand his loneliness without his having to disclose it. He now believes he can understand her loneliness even if she cannot disclose it. This reverses dependency into a rescue-oriented identity and helps explain his continuing love. It is also self-aware: he calls the model a possibly childish hero fantasy.

He joins the Haruka inquiry and supports the group, but V11 contains no confession to Hinami and no terminal pursuit rule. His V10 negotiation-vocation possibility receives no sustained vocational test.

### 5.6 Mimimi

Mimimi supplies the volume's clearest accounts of fluctuating self-worth and model dependence. Her `seesaw` account distinguishes confidence in skills from confidence in the self. She joins the student council not to replace Hinami as an identity but to preserve work and a reason Haruka can still trust. Her improvised tournament commentary produces genuine audience growth for the event and demonstrates an unplanned communication skill.

She also carefully monitors physical and conversational boundaries around Tomozaki because he is dating Kikuchi. The source does not show that her romantic feeling ends or becomes a complete stopping policy.

### 5.7 Yuzu, Nakamura, Tama, and Rena

Yuzu tries to repair the group and reports the practical student-council failure, but she also absorbs guilt for initiating the video. Nakamura refuses participation in order to reject the claim that Yuzu should bear sole blame; he still says the friendship itself is not necessarily destroyed. Neither position becomes a durable group rule.

Tama appears in the divided peer group but receives no vocational continuation or new accountability evidence. Rena again takes Tomozaki's arm and provokes Kikuchi. Tomozaki removes the contact and Kikuchi directly objects. Rena leaves amused and complimentary toward Kikuchi; the scene demonstrates resistance, not adoption of a broad boundary by Rena.

## 7. Rival readings and counterevidence

### 7.1 Rival: Yoko never loved her daughters and simply indoctrinated them

Hinami's account repeatedly depicts affection, care, grief, food, touch, and attentive reassurance. It also depicts an inflexible meaning system that discourages concrete response and later validates an intolerable death. Love and harmful doctrine coexist; neither cancels the other.

### 7.2 Rival: the unnamed private room identifies a specific religion or cult

The source gives a room, displayed words, repeated practice, secrecy anxiety, and Hinami's belief that it supported Yoko. It does not name an institution, deity, doctrine, membership, or supernatural claim. Specific identification would be imported inference.

### 7.3 Rival: V11 proves Nagisa died by suicide

Nagisa's deterioration and final doubt make intentional action more imaginable. They do not narrate her road entry, supply a note, or remove the physical-collapse/accident alternatives Hinami named in V10. Increased plausibility is not resolution.

### 7.4 Rival: Haruka's success proves Hinami's perfection regime was correct

Hinami's example genuinely gives Haruka a route and produces self-authored effort. It also creates dependence on the model's visible coherence and burdens Hinami with being an unbroken sun. Beneficial effect is real evidence, not a total ethical verdict.

### 7.5 Rival: Tomozaki could have saved Hinami if only he had won

His `believe the one person you cannot beat` claim makes the set relevant. Winning would have preserved that premise, not solved the infinite regress or transferred ownership of Hinami's life. The deeper problem precedes the match.

### 7.6 Rival: Hinami's victory establishes that life is objectively a bad game

One adapted three-first set defeats Tomozaki's particular authority claim. It does not prove a metaphysical verdict about life. `人生はクソゲー` is Hinami's crisis conclusion and a deliberate reversal of the series opening, not an uncontested narrator statement.

### 7.7 Rival: Hinami is unable to choose anything except victory

She chooses to tell Tomozaki the family history, chooses Boxman, chooses the set, and expresses admiration for another style. Her self-report that she can only win describes a powerful constraint, not literal absence of agency in every domain.

### 7.8 Rival: Alcia's internal states are direct Hinami evidence

They are Kikuchi's reconstruction based partly on Tomozaki's reports. Their fit and effect make them important hypotheses. They cannot establish that Hinami tried to lose, wanted death, or experienced the election in exactly that form.

### 7.9 Rival: Kikuchi's insight vindicates invasive art

The lost-god model is substantially corroborated. Haruka is also made to cry, Hinami has not consented, and Kikuchi herself identifies the harm. Accuracy and ethical authority are separate.

### 7.10 Rival: the final party plan solves consent through numbers

More perspectives can reduce interpretive monopoly and distribute effort. A group still cannot manufacture Hinami's consent or ownership by consensus. The plan is not yet executed.

### 7.11 Rival: V11 converts Tomozaki/Hinami into confirmed romance

Tomozaki says he likes Hinami and treats her as special. He also remains explicitly partnered with Kikuchi, reconciles with her, and asks her to co-create the next intervention. No romantic self-classification or reciprocal Hinami declaration occurs.

### 7.12 Rival: Kikuchi's publication is now secured

An editor proposes continuation with publication in view and a possible schedule. The manuscript, editorial acceptance, contract, release, sales, and sustainable career remain future contingencies.
