### 4.1 Care operates by exceeding Hinami's control

The friends do not produce a single diagnostic intervention. They create repeated situations in which strangers, staff, children, familiar foods, game spaces, and remembered relationships address Hinami from directions she cannot fully precompute. Several reactions—fear on the roller coaster, protest at another ride, delight at Found, and emotional strain during the messages—escape her usual public range.

This does not prove access to an unmediated true self. Tomozaki explicitly revises his own two-layer model: the public heroine and `NO NAME` may both be player-controlled characters. The volume therefore supplies less confidence about which presentation is ontologically real even as it supplies more direct disclosure and affect.

### 4.2 Couple practice becomes more public and reciprocal

The relationship does more than exchange reassurance. Kikuchi enters Tomozaki's ordinary peer environment, meets his family and gaming contacts, helps pursue a difficult shared aim, names her own fear, and deliberately gives him one-to-one space with Hinami. Tomozaki introduces her as his girlfriend, admits his jealousy after Mizusawa catches her on the train, and hears her corresponding reassurance.

The improvement is real but bounded. Kikuchi's consent to help with Hinami is costly, and the narration repeatedly marks sadness or complexity. Tomozaki declares another girl uniquely special in front of her, while his internal narration calls the attachment a form of `不誠実`. A chosen exception under known conditions is not yet a general privacy, disclosure, allocation, or third-party-contact rule.

### 4.3 Tomozaki crosses a privacy boundary deliberately

Mizusawa's request creates a real conflict: withholding Hinami's secret leaves her isolated, while disclosure recruits someone she has not authorized. Tomozaki does not pretend the information belongs to him. He says that he lacks the right, then decides that Hinami's special status is precisely why he will violate the boundary and tell Mizusawa.

That candor establishes ownership of the act, not ethical completion. Mizusawa's seriousness and Tomozaki's trust reduce some risks, and Kikuchi is present, but none of them can substitute for Hinami's permission. Later, Hinami voluntarily tells Tomozaki about Nagisa after he asks to carry part of the burden. The contrast distinguishes recruited support through unauthorized disclosure from reciprocal disclosure by the person whose history it is.

### 4.4 Aspiration enters an economic relation

The Endo negotiation is the strongest career evidence so far. Tomozaki's existing performance, public account, age, appearance, and developing speech become a marketable bundle. The consideration is explicit: he will promote Endo's work for six months, and Endo will supply the commissioned game. Mizusawa adds a measurable three-month follower target.

The event is a real experiment, not a complete profession. The text shows no written agreement, rate, deliverable specification, advertising-disclosure practice, revenue, family discussion, university comparison, tournament result, or termination mechanism. Mizusawa's claim that this is already what professional gamers do is useful recognition of form, not proof of sustainable employment.

### 4.5 Form is not the opposite of sincerity

Mizusawa has treated form as evidence of emotional deficiency. V10 revises that binary. Yuzu's conventional itinerary and birthday devices, Mizusawa's polished commercial language, the commissioned game, and the video-message montage all use prepared forms. Each can carry effort that changes another person's life or creates a real result.

The revision is not that all performance is authentic. Hinami can still use social form instrumentally, and Tomozaki can still object to manipulation. The narrower result is that origin in technique does not determine terminal meaning: repeated form can acquire sincere investment, produce consequences outside the actor's motive, and become a medium for relation.

### 4.6 Nagisa's death supplies facts and preserves uncertainty

V10 directly resolves several long-protected unknowns. Nagisa was the middle of three sisters, was compared by Hinami to Tomozaki and Tama, acted against bullying in sixth grade, entered the road, was struck, and died. The driver reportedly expressed lifelong remorse and promised compensation. Hinami herself presents accident and suicide as irreducible alternatives.

The evidence does not authorize a suicide finding, a responsible bully, a precise date, a complete chain of events, or a singular-cause diagnosis of Hinami. It does authorize a more specific mechanism: causal ambiguity deprives Hinami of a correct counterfactual action. Her proof system can be read as structurally responsive to that deprivation, but V10 does not say it originated only there or explain every component of her later identity.

- Her Alcia framework remains interpretive language. V10's direct Nagisa testimony neither makes the fictional blood premise factual nor validates every earlier inference.

### 5.3 Hinami

- Her private rejection coexists with stable public performance, but friends can now detect degraded energy and small failures without access to the secret conflict.
- She says the coaching relation cannot continue because she used another person's life for herself and identifies that action with the kind of person she is.
- She responds most spontaneously when control is disrupted by play, fear, embarrassment, and unsolicited care.
- She permits renewed direct conversation and voluntarily discloses part of the Nagisa history to Tomozaki.
- Her central trauma includes not knowing whether Nagisa's death was accidental or intentional and therefore not knowing which past intervention to regret.
- She does not accept rescue, a shared burden, Tomozaki's self-affirmation, or a renewed coaching relation by the V10 endpoint.

### 5.4 Mizusawa

- He tells Tomozaki, Kikuchi, Ashigaru, and Endo that he loves Hinami and plans another confession without claiming a high probability of success.
- He asks for access to the hidden Hinami history and accepts the responsibility of knowing it, but obtains it without Hinami's consent.
- His polished social performance creates a concrete sponsor-like agreement and a possible vocational direction.
- He chooses to give Tomozaki the private ride with Hinami, delaying his own romantic objective. He does not thereby establish a terminal stopping condition.
- Nakamura independently observes that Mizusawa now states preferences, resists accommodation, and appears more engaged in his own life.

### 5.5 Yuzu, Mimimi, Tama, and the peer network

Yuzu converts diffuse concern into logistics, welcomes Kikuchi, creates one-to-one opportunities for Mizusawa, and uses ordinary theme-park forms to make Hinami lose control safely enough to react. This is substantial facilitation, but her long-term absorber limit remains untested.

Mimimi and Tama use remembered food rather than psychological interpretation. Their labor shows that Hinami's prior actions created histories other people own. Tama's first original cake is also a small independently chosen vocational experiment. V10 supplies no new Mimimi pursuit or terminal romantic rule.

Nakamura identifies Mizusawa's increasing self-assertion and regards it as good even when it inconveniences him. Takei remains socially useful through unguarded enthusiasm. The class, track team, former teacher, athletic rival, and restaurant owner provide a distributed record of Hinami's effects beyond the focal group's interpretation.

## 7. Rival readings and counterevidence

### 7.1 Rival: V10 confirms that Tomozaki is romantically in love with Hinami

The volume makes his attachment more exceptional and ethically disruptive. It also has him select Kikuchi, introduce her publicly, desire private time with her, feel jealousy, and reaffirm exclusive romantic love. His unease about Mizusawa is deliberately unnamed. Priority and romantic classification remain distinct evidence objects.

### 7.2 Rival: Kikuchi's cooperation proves she has overcome jealousy

Kikuchi says that yielding the ride makes her jealous and repeatedly shows mixed affect. Her action is a costly choice made with knowledge, not evidence that the cost or future conflict has vanished.

### 7.3 Rival: Tomozaki's disclosure to Mizusawa is justified because it helps Hinami

Tomozaki himself calls the act a violation of Hinami's rights. Good intent, trusted audience, and later useful care may mitigate risk or explain the choice; they cannot retroactively create consent.

### 7.4 Rival: Nagisa's death caused all of Hinami

The death and its causal ambiguity illuminate Hinami's need for reasons, proof, and correct counterfactual action. V10 does not state that one event exclusively produced her masks, effort, gaming identity, coaching project, or every later decision.

### 7.5 Rival: Nagisa died by suicide

Hinami explicitly cannot distinguish an accidental collapse or dizziness from intentional self-endangerment. Selecting one alternative would erase the uncertainty the scene identifies as central.

### 7.6 Rival: the birthday celebration saves Hinami

The celebration produces spontaneous joy, disclosure, and visible emotional strain. Hinami opens the volume saying she will not be easily saved, rejects Tomozaki's simple reassurance, and never accepts a cure or shared plan. Care reaches her; cure is not established.

### 7.7 Rival: positive consequences make Hinami's methods acceptable

Many people genuinely benefited from her conduct. That proves effect can exceed instrumental motive, not that manipulating Tomozaki, managing peers, or hiding material facts is ethically harmless.

### 7.8 Rival: the barter means Tomozaki is already a professional gamer

It has the recognizable structure of sponsorship and creates real obligations. No income, sustainable business, tournament credential, contract governance, or career transition is shown. It is a strong route experiment, not completed professional status.

### 7.9 Rival: Mizusawa has finally found a permanent vocation

He discovers pleasure and aptitude in negotiation and receives an adult contact. He calls it a possible vocation. No sustained practice, education choice, compensation, failure test, or enduring commitment is established.

### 7.10 Rival: the final family video repairs or destroys the celebration

V10 ends after the image appears, the mother says one birthday greeting, and Hinami drops the controller. The rest of the message and every consequence belong to V11 evidence and cannot be supplied at this freeze.
