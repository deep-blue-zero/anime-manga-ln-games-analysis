## 4. The revised coaching contract: real negotiation, limited co-authorship

### 4.1 Consent to a target is now explicit

At the first V04 meeting, Hinami does not simply announce the next medium or daily objective. She proposes that Tomozaki and a girl mutually disclose secrets, asks whether he objects, and receives a conditional answer. Tomozaki immediately checks that the target will not require an assigned confession or insincere speech. The narration itself marks the new situation: Hinami confirms goal content, and Tomozaki can state what he wants before accepting it.

This is direct evidence that the V03 rupture changed procedure. V03-C03 and V03-C11 therefore strengthen. The veto is usable, not ceremonial.

### 4.2 The hierarchy has not become equal authorship

The same exchange preserves important limits:

- Hinami still proposes the relationship target and links it to her pre-existing girlfriend objective.
- Tomozaki approves and constrains the proposal; he does not originate it.
- Hinami independently selects the volume's principal applied assignment—mobilizing Konno's group—and determines the skill domain it should train.
- Tomozaki controls execution and can recruit Hinami as one piece of his plan, but she remains the evaluator who declares the task complete and assigns the next exercise.

The accurate description is therefore **negotiated target acceptance inside an unequal training relationship**, not fully co-authored curriculum. V04-Q01's behavioral prediction is supported, while its title question receives only a partial answer.

### 4.3 Tomozaki's participation becomes proactive

Tomozaki anticipates and requests daily work, searches for high-experience situations, and treats the larger aim—entering life seriously enough to enjoy it—as his own. This is stronger than compliance with a filtered list. His motivation now supplies energy upstream of Hinami's commands.

The distinction matters: the assignment architecture remains largely Hinami's, but the reason Tomozaki enters it no longer belongs solely to her.

## 5. Hybrid competence survives ordinary use

### 5.1 Trained form carries sincere content

V04 repeatedly makes the V03 formula explicit in action. When Yuzu reports progress with Nakamura, Tomozaki uses expression and vocal tone to make his genuine happiness legible. He names this the hybrid style: skill serving honest feeling.

The pattern then recurs without requiring a theoretical crisis:

- teasing and retorts emerge automatically with Takei;
- Tomozaki speaks up in mixed male groups and recovers from awkwardness;
- he uses observation to distinguish individual technique from accumulated character position;
- he plans contact with Kikuchi because he values the relationship, while still accepting logistical help from Hinami;
- after his spectacular basketball failure, expressive technique lets embarrassment become shared laughter rather than withdrawal.

This is not proof that every learned technique is authentic. It is strong evidence that learned origin does not prevent a technique from becoming owned and sincere in use. V03-C01 and V04-Q02 are confirmed at high confidence.

### 5.2 Proceduralization is real but uneven

Automatic teasing works with familiar, forgiving recipients and fails or becomes strained under high-status threat. Konno's direct challenge produces a weak joke and rapid loss of confidence. The three-day layup exercise also fails under match pressure because the sequence is not yet proceduralized.

The parallel is useful. Repetition can make a move available without making it universal. Skill acquisition has recipient, pressure, and rehearsal thresholds; it is neither identity replacement nor instant mastery.

### 5.3 Failure can now generate affiliation

Tomozaki's three simultaneous basketball violations are objectively a performance failure. Yet he responds, is teased, joins the shared joke, and gains an opening with classmates outside Nakamura's group. The result separates competence from status and self-concept:

- basketball competence remains low;
- social recovery is materially better;
- the public mistake expands rather than destroys local belonging;
- he does not convert the failure into a global verdict on himself.

This supports confidence recovery in ordinary public friction. It does not retest the severe relational trigger that caused V03 withdrawal.

## 6. Yuzu: the same action changes meaning when its reason is owned

### 6.1 Refusal is only the first form of agency

Yuzu initially refuses Konno's attempt to make her tournament captain. She recognizes that volunteering merely to absorb pressure would repeat the self she wants to change. This directly continues her earlier struggle against automatic accommodation.

After choosing extensive effort to help Nakamura, however, she reaches a more exact rule. Whether another person has also demanded an action is not decisive. She can take over the captaincy because she wants to help Hirabayashi; the governing reason is hers.

The outward act—accepting a burden—can therefore instantiate either submission or agency. Its meaning depends on authorship, not on a simple refusal/acceptance binary.

### 6.2 Chosen effort reorganizes capacity

Yuzu repeatedly volunteers for the less efficient role, studies mathematics despite probable failure, insists on speaking to Nakamura herself, and earns the target score. V04 does not establish that desire magically defeats limits. Hinami's instruction and group assistance remain necessary. What desire supplies is persistence, priority, and tolerance for uncertain return.

The resulting causal sequence is also narrower than Tomozaki first claims. Yuzu's effort reaches Nakamura emotionally and brings him back to school before the group changes his mother's rule. That shows an unmodeled route, not that non-rational action is categorically superior.

### 6.3 Self-authorship is relational rather than isolated

Yuzu's goals concern other people: help Nakamura, relieve Hirabayashi, let the class enjoy the tournament. They are still owned. V04 therefore rejects the idea that an authentic goal must be private, spontaneous, or free of social influence. Agency concerns who can endorse and revise the reason, not whether the end is individualistic.

## 7. Tomozaki and Hinami debate desire on rationalism's home field

### 7.1 Tomozaki's first demonstration is insufficient

Tomozaki argues that Yuzu's desire found a faster route than Hinami's rational plan. Hinami identifies the problem correctly: if desire is valuable because it produces the shortest route, its value has merely been translated back into efficiency. A better goal specification could incorporate emotion as an input and reproduce the route.

The text does not let Tomozaki win by assertion. Stable desire still lacks a proof of value independent of ranked outcome.

### 7.2 Hinami can model emotion without surrendering rational control

Hinami's counterclaim is substantive. She can treat affection, worry, pride, and other people's likely reactions as variables inside optimization. V04 therefore narrows any claim that she simply “does not understand emotions.” She understands and can deploy many of them with exceptional precision.

Her limitation is different: she tends to require a specified result before emotion becomes instrumentally legible. Tomozaki's problem is to show what an owned end contributes when it cannot be defended merely as a better path to an already accepted metric.

### 7.3 Her own preference disrupts the clean argument

Hinami admits that her focus on ending the Atafami ban rather than merely returning Nakamura to school arose from her own attachment to the game. She calls this non-rational and uses it against Tomozaki. The admission is important even though it does not concede his thesis. Hinami has ordinary attachments capable of redirecting her target selection.

V04-Q08 thus receives direct evidence of gamer preference and, later, of attachment to Tama. It does not disclose the origin or full structure of Hinami's perfectionism.

## 8. Influence, consent, and the limits of an honest goal

### 8.1 The Nakamura intervention is bounded more carefully

The group initially distinguishes preparation from execution. Tomozaki supports gathering resources while refusing to force Nakamura out of the house or approach his mother without his assent. Yuzu obtains an abrasive but recognizable permission before the planned intervention proceeds.

This is positive evidence for V04-Q06. Tomozaki does apply goal ownership beyond himself in at least one case: the target retains a stopping point.

### 8.2 The Konno plan uses persons as levers

The tournament plan has a different ethical structure. Tomozaki triangulates three inferred wants:

1. make Yuzu happy;
2. avoid being looked down on by Hinami;
3. appear attractive to Nakamura.

He then asks others to activate all three. The plan includes status threat and a strategically planted claim about Nakamura preferring athletic girls. Tomozaki notices that exploiting romantic interest is questionable, but proceeds because the end feels legitimate and because he thinks an honest goal licenses hard means.

This is a material limit on his V03 advance. He distinguishes his own owned end from Hinami's imposed end, yet does not give Konno comparable authority over the motivational frame being constructed around her.

### 8.3 Outcome success does not settle the means

The plan works locally: Konno's group engages, both teams win, and the class shares a real celebration. Those benefits should not be erased. They also do not retroactively neutralize the manipulation.

More importantly, the apparent success changes the social system. The tournament and Nakamura's return contribute to Yuzu and Nakamura dating; Konno then displaces humiliation and jealousy onto a low-risk target. V04 does not say Tomozaki caused Konno's abuse in a culpable or foreseeable sense. It does show that his success criterion was too narrow to govern downstream effects.

The resulting correction is stronger than “ask whether the goal is sincere.” Bounded agency also requires scrutiny of means, target consent, asymmetric risk, substitutable victims, and delayed feedback.

## 9. `空気` becomes a dynamic system rather than a single obstacle

### 9.1 Atmosphere allocates permission and cost

Konno's first move against Hirabayashi works without an explicit order. Her posture, rank, labels, and supporters make refusal expensive. “Atmosphere” here is not mood alone; it distributes who may speak, who must carry a burden, and whose account will be believed.

Tomozaki's observations of Nakamura's absence add a second mechanism. When a central actor disappears, an apparently unified male group decomposes into individual preferences. When Konno later signals engagement, her followers' latent and borrowed motivations align. Central actors do not wholly control the group, but they change the cost of expressing individual will.

### 9.2 Atmosphere can be changed through coalitions

Tomozaki succeeds by combining information and other people's capacities. He remains the planner while Yuzu, Hinami, and Mizusawa perform actions he cannot safely perform himself. This is genuine higher-order competence: knowing when to borrow a skill without giving away the objective.

The result strengthens V03-C14. `空気` is locally modifiable through repeated, distributed nudges rather than one decisive speech.

### 9.3 The system redirects pressure

The closing movement supplies the counterweight. Konno's loss of an available romantic outcome does not end her need to preserve status. Harm moves to Hirabayashi because she is unlikely to answer, then to Tama when Tama intervenes. Once Tama resists publicly, repeated conflict lets classmates recode the victim's defense as the source of collective discomfort.

Thus atmosphere has at least four V04 properties:

- **path dependence:** earlier successes alter the next field;
- **substitution:** pressure can move to a safer target;
- **accumulation:** small impressions harden through repetition;
- **feedback:** resistance to abuse can itself be redescribed as disruptive conduct.

Local modifiability is confirmed; durable control is disconfirmed.

## 10. Causality, fairness, and conditional aid

### 10.1 A causal chain is not a moral defense

When Tomozaki questions whether a life permitting arbitrary harm can be a good game, Hinami answers that the harm has reasons: a chain of prior events made the present outcome intelligible. This is a strong causal explanation. It does not establish that Hirabayashi's treatment is deserved, fair, or unavoidable in every relevant sense.

Tomozaki senses the gap but does not formulate it fully. The question “why did this occur?” and the question “what should anyone owe the person harmed?” remain distinct. V04 ends without resolving either life's objective game quality or the distribution of responsibility inside it.

### 10.2 Hinami separates fault from entitlement to help

Hinami directly says Konno is wrong and Hirabayashi is the victim. She nevertheless withholds active aid while Hirabayashi makes no visible attempt to change her situation. Her position is not simply that the victim caused or deserves the abuse. It is that assistance is normally conditional on visible self-directed play, with an emergency exception if harm crosses her threshold.

The position preserves personal agency but risks making safety depend on already possessing the capacities that coercion exploits. V04 provides no evidence that Hinami asks Hirabayashi what she wants, what she has tried privately, or what retaliation she fears. “No visible move” is therefore not equivalent to “no agency” or “no claim on support.”

### 10.3 Tomozaki begins from aid, then checks the recipient's value

Tomozaki first imagines escape—temporary absence—as the safest outcome for Tama. Unlike the Konno plan, he does not implement the idea around her. He speaks with her, learns that surrendering her judgment would be worse to her than the ongoing costs, and withdraws his proposal.

This is a meaningful bounded-agency improvement: risk analysis generates an option, but the recipient decides what damage matters most.

## 11. Hinami's Tama exception

### 11.1 The contradiction is source-visible

Hinami's established rule is to adapt to the field, build the capacity needed to make one's judgment effective, and assist a player who is trying. In the Tama crisis, she refuses the apparently efficient adaptation. Because Tama is right and speaks without disguise, Hinami insists the class must change while Tama remains untouched.

Tomozaki explicitly recognizes the discontinuity. The analysis therefore need not invent one: V04 itself marks Hinami's position as inconsistent with her prior doctrine.

### 11.2 Attachment, not ignorance, best explains the immediate break

Hinami is not unable to see that asking Tama to reduce open confrontation may lower short-term risk. Tomozaki raises the possibility, and her rejection is immediate and emotionally charged. She also expends substantial social capital trying to reverse class sentiment.

The strongest bounded inference is that Tama occupies a protected value position for Hinami. This is evidence of non-instrumental attachment and of a stopping condition inside her usual optimization. It is not evidence of the origin of that attachment, of a general change in worldview, or of a hidden trauma.

### 11.3 Hinami becomes vulnerable without yet becoming Tomozaki's pupil

She engages Tomozaki's arguments, permits his plans, and reveals a contradiction he can diagnose. Yet she does not adopt his account of desire or ask him how to revise her system. V04-Q03 is therefore only partially supported: the relationship becomes more reciprocal as inquiry, but the teacher/learner reversal remains incomplete.

## 12. Tama and Mimimi: authenticity includes chosen adaptation

### 12.1 Tama's non-comparative self remains intact under pressure

Tama can distinguish pain from self-doubt. She admits the persecution hurts, but her certainty that Konno is wrong prevents the group's judgment from redefining her. This directly retests and strengthens the V02 model of non-comparative valuation.

Her certainty should not be romanticized as invulnerability. Tomozaki notices threat sensitivity, and the group atmosphere is already narrowing her ordinary speech. Moral confidence protects self-definition; it does not cancel social or physiological cost.

### 12.2 Choosing change does not surrender the self

Tama first rejects escape because changing herself to satisfy a false norm would be worse than enduring attack. She then asks to change because Mimimi's attempt to support her is causing Mimimi pain. These statements are not contradictory if goal ownership remains primary.

Tama is not conceding that Konno or the class was right. She is selecting a different fighting method for an end she endorses. V04 therefore supplies the strongest series evidence so far that authenticity is not behavioral stasis. A person can deliberately acquire a mask, tactic, or social skill without being assimilated if she retains authority over why, how far, and for whose sake she changes.

### 12.3 Mimimi's performance is sincere and costly

Mimimi keeps joking, skips practice to stay beside Tama, and asks Tomozaki whether her cheerfulness looks convincing. Her public brightness is consciously produced, but its purpose—protecting someone she loves—is genuine. The scene independently confirms the hybrid thesis outside Tomozaki: performance and sincerity are not opposites.

It also introduces caregiver strain. Mimimi's V02 comparative crisis does not visibly recur; instead, insecurity attaches to whether she is helping well enough. Her ambition is not retested, and her capacity to sustain the care role remains open.

## 13. Kikuchi, male peers, and supporting-character updates

### 13.1 Kikuchi/Tomozaki remains reciprocal

Tomozaki independently initiates an after-work meeting because Kikuchi matters to him, not because an assignment requires escalation. Kikuchi notices his forward movement, provides psychologically precise information when asked, and says she too intends to move forward. Their interaction contains shared interest, mutual observation, teasing, and voluntary contact.

This confirms V03-C06 and V04-Q05 within the V04 horizon. Kikuchi's destination and any romantic outcome remain unlicensed.

Nakamura's absence produces sustained concern, collective planning, permission-seeking, and relief when he returns. ### 13.3 Mizusawa's V03 ownership problem is not retested

Mizusawa remains socially capable, perceptive, caring, and effective. He shows pleasure at Yuzu and Nakamura's relationship and participates in the Nakamura intervention. V04 does not revisit his rejection, confession, or self-description. V03-C07 and V03-C08 remain open rather than falsified.

### 13.4 Konno's inferred motives require classification discipline

Konno's coercion, harassment, and target switching are directly observed. Her desire not to be looked down on, residual interest in Nakamura, care for Yuzu, and effort-cost calculation are interpretations assembled by Tomozaki and partly corroborated by Kikuchi or behavior. They are useful predictive models, not omniscient access to Konno's interior.

The distinction matters because the plan's success validates practical prediction only partially; it does not prove every inferred motive true.

## 17. Rival readings and counterevidence

### 17.1 Rival: the tournament proves benign social engineering

The class's enjoyment and double victory are real benefits. The later abuse does not make them false. It does show that a local target can succeed while the altered system creates or redirects harm. “It worked” is therefore inadequate as a complete ethical or causal judgment.

### 17.2 Rival: Konno's abuse was caused by Tomozaki

Tomozaki's intervention is one event in the narrated chain, but Konno chooses the harassment. The text supports contribution to conditions and failure to model externalities, not transfer of Konno's agency or moral responsibility to Tomozaki.

### 17.3 Rival: Hinami's causal explanation justifies victim suffering

Hinami explains how the pieces formed a chain and separately states that Konno is wrong. Her argument challenges randomness, not moral wrongness. The stronger criticism concerns her conditional rule for aid and her inference from invisible inaction, not a claim that she approves abuse.

### 17.4 Rival: Tama proves people should never adapt to unjust systems

Tama herself rejects this reading. She asks to learn a new fighting method for Mimimi's sake. What she refuses is externally governed self-erasure, not all deliberate change.

### 17.5 Rival: Mimimi's cheerfulness is fake

Her performance is conscious, strained, and nevertheless sincere in purpose. V04 repeats the volume's central correction: an expression can be made deliberately without its governing care being counterfeit.

### 17.6 Rival: Hinami has accepted Tomozaki's theory of desire

She engages it and supplies evidence that her own system has exceptions, but she defeats his first attempted proof. A general conversion is not present.

### 17.7 Rival: Hirabayashi's inaction makes her responsible for the abuse

Konno's deliberate targeting and the asymmetry of retaliation remain explicit. V04 provides limited access to Hirabayashi's private reasoning and no evidence that passivity constitutes consent. Capacity to alter a situation and responsibility for being harmed must remain separate.

## 21. Longitudinal promotion decision

V04 materially changes the effort/competition/goal-ownership ledger in five respects:

1. Yuzu establishes that the meaning of accepting a burden depends on reason ownership, not the action's surface form.
2. Tomozaki's attempted proof distinguishes route discovery from independent value, but Hinami successfully shows that a route argument can remain rationalist.
3. Hinami's Atafami preference and Tama exception show desire redirecting her own target selection.
4. Tomozaki's Konno plan shows that an owned end is necessary but not sufficient for bounded intervention.
5. Tama chooses self-modification for a relationally owned end, making authentic change a major forward question.

Those updates belong in the existing longitudinal ledger. A new permanent `空気` ledger is not yet justified before the V05 crisis resolves; V04 supplies a dynamic model, but the closing conflict is intentionally incomplete.

## 22. Handoff to V05

The controlling V05 state is deliberately asymmetric:

- Tomozaki can now own a curriculum's purpose without co-authoring all of it.
- He can plan through other people, but the Konno success exposes why local outcome, honest intent, and ethical boundedness are different tests.
- Hinami's system is tactically flexible enough to calculate emotion, yet her attachment to Tama creates an explicit doctrinal exception.
- Tama does not ask to become socially acceptable because the class is right. She asks for a new fighting method because Mimimi is being hurt.
- Mimimi's brightness is both genuine care and work she may not be able to carry alone.
- Konno's harassment, Hirabayashi's safety, Tama's place in the class, and the fairness question all remain unresolved at the source boundary.

V05 must begin from this freeze. It must not retrofit its outcome into V04's open conflict, and it must keep **goal authorship**, **method acquisition**, **recipient-defined harm**, **atmosphere effects**, and **moral responsibility** analytically separate.
