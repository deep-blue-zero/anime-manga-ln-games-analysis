## 2. Pre-V02 prospective freeze

The controlling pre-read state is the frozen end of V01. It is not rewritten here. The important questions carried into V02 were:

1. Does Tomozaki's `良ゲー` revision survive meaningful negative evidence, or does confidence collapse faster than competence?
2. Will self-authorship increasingly diverge from Hinami's optimization rules at the level of ends and values rather than technique?
3. Can Tomozaki's direct considered expression integrate with learned social technique without one erasing the other?
4. How broadly does `空気` generalize across different groups and status configurations?
5. What mixture of gamer respect, mentorship, project investment, friendship, control, curiosity, and possible affection explains Hinami's investment?
6. Does Nakamura's demonstrated effort produce a more reciprocal rivalry?
7. Does Yuzu's costly resistance to group pressure generalize?
8. Can the Kikuchi relationship proceed honestly after the false shared-interest reset?
9. Does Tomozaki overgeneralize the V01 lesson and begin treating every outcome as controllable through sufficient correct effort?
10. Which dimensions move next: competence, confidence, status, self-concept, agency, or values?

The principal V01 claim state carried forward was:

- Tomozaki already possessed a strong effort ethic and disciplined learning capacity before Hinami's intervention.
- His original fatalism was a domain-specific exemption applied to “life,” not generalized passivity.
- Several social subskills were demonstrably trainable.
- Competence moved faster than confidence and self-concept.
- Learned technique had begun becoming owned competence.
- Agency had increased through unassigned and value-driven choices.
- Tomozaki had revised life from categorically unwinnable `クソゲー` to at least a sufficiently learnable `良ゲー`.
- Hinami's universal `人生は神ゲー` thesis remained unproven.
- `空気` was useful as a local coordination model but not an absolute law.
- Tomozaki's sincerity / anti-excuse ethic could override locally optimal tactics.

No new V02 prediction was added after the V01 freeze and before opening V02.

## 3. Volume structure and analytical movement

V02 keeps the game-language chapter naming while changing the object of inquiry. V01 asked whether social life contains learnable mechanics. V02 increasingly asks what happens when **effort, competition, and optimization themselves become unstable variables**.

## 4. V01 gains persist: Tomozaki begins V02 as changed but not transformed

V02 does not reset Tomozaki for convenience.

He still experiences awkwardness, miscalibrated topics, anxiety around high-skill peers, and strong sensitivity to Erika's contempt. But the baseline is materially different from V01:

- he has several classmates with whom he now routinely speaks;
- he can hold ordinary conversations with Yuzu, Mimimi, Tama, and Hinami;
- he can deliberately draw on techniques learned from Mizusawa;
- he can recognize when a conversational tactic succeeds;
- he is increasingly willing to enter difficult social situations rather than classify them as impossible;
- he can sometimes enjoy the activity itself rather than treating every interaction as an externally assigned trial.

This matters for V01-Q01. The `良ゲー` revision is already functioning as behavior rather than slogan. Tomozaki repeatedly treats difficulty as a reason to inspect variables rather than withdraw.

**STRENGTHEN:** V01-C07, the claim that life has become sufficiently learnable to merit continued effort.

## 5. Tomozaki and Hinami: mentorship becomes explicit reciprocal competition

### 5.1 Atafami remains asymmetrical in the opposite direction

At the beginning of V02, Hinami continues playing Tomozaki online as NO NAME. Tomozaki remains undefeated after roughly fifty matches.

The relationship therefore contains an unusual dual asymmetry:

- in social life, Hinami is the established expert and Tomozaki the trainee;
- in Atafami, Tomozaki is the established number one and Hinami the challenger.

Hinami's refusal to stop rematching is directly legible to Tomozaki as both persistence and a refusal to count a compromised win as satisfying. He recognizes in her the same serious-practice orientation that first made NO NAME credible to him.

### 5.2 Hinami's investment is now partly competitive pleasure

The election later makes the duality explicit. During several days in which Tomozaki is planning against Hinami through Mimimi's campaign, Hinami says she has been bored because she has not been able to play nanashi in Atafami.

Tomozaki replies that nanashi has been fighting NO NAME the entire time.

Hinami enjoys that framing.

After the election she says Tomozaki's strategy surprised her and was **fun**. Her excitement is unusually open. She then tells him to keep improving at “life” because his development is producing an opponent she can enjoy facing.

**REVISE / STRENGTHEN V01-Q05:** Hinami's investment can no longer be modeled only as project ownership, altruistic coaching, or curiosity. Competitive pleasure and respect for nanashi are explicit components.

This still does **not** establish romance.

## 6. Social learning becomes increasingly self-directed

### 6.1 Mizusawa's methods become portable

Tomozaki repeatedly applies interaction techniques acquired through observation of Mizusawa: light teasing, commenting on the other person's possessions, shifting topic focus to the other person, and calibrating the exchange through response.

The important change from early V01 is not that every attempt works. It is that Tomozaki increasingly retrieves and applies techniques without needing Hinami to specify the next sentence.

That is evidence of **procedural internalization**, even where conscious effort remains high.

### 6.2 Higher-level social groups expose cognitive load rather than impossibility

The four-person outing with Hinami, Mizusawa, and another socially fluent peer places Tomozaki inside a party where his relative skill is low. The chapter title's leveling metaphor is apt: because the surrounding interaction is fast and well-practiced, Tomozaki is forced to notice timing, banter, rhythm, role allocation, and implicit coordination at higher resolution.

He remains anxious and sometimes falls behind. But the event does not return him to categorical outsider status.

**STRENGTHEN V01-C02:** competence continues to improve through practice while remaining uneven by context.

### 6.3 Self-concept begins catching up

V01 gave strong evidence that appearance and behavior changed before Tomozaki's self-image did. V02 begins showing partial catch-up:

- he buys the wax that Mizusawa previously selected;
- he sometimes uses grooming as deliberate “equipment” before a difficult task;
- he recognizes some of his own appearance improvement;
- he notices ordinary peer contact as something he has begun to possess rather than merely observe;
- after becoming accustomed to walking home or interacting with others, solitary return can feel lonely rather than simply normal.

This is not broad confidence. But V02 makes the self-concept lag smaller.

## 7. Kikuchi: honesty creates a genuinely acquired shared world

The V01 Kikuchi interaction began from a false premise: she mistook Tomozaki's strategic use of a Michael Andy book for genuine fandom.

Tomozaki confessed the misunderstanding rather than exploiting it.

V02 validates that choice in a way the original coaching model did not predict. He actually starts reading Michael Andy's work and finds it compelling on its own terms. The interest is no longer merely instrumental.

The library sequence is important ordinary-life evidence:

- Tomozaki chooses to read;
- he becomes absorbed for hours;
- he can articulate what he finds attractive about the worldbuilding;
- sitting silently beside Kikuchi becomes meaningful without needing constant conversational performance.

This is a strong example of a relationship that moves from **false shared-interest premise → honest reset → genuinely acquired overlap**.

**STRENGTHEN V01-Q08.**

The analytical lesson is not “lying led to a good outcome.” It is almost the reverse: once the lie is removed, the originally artificial contact point can become a real interest because Tomozaki is allowed to encounter it as himself.

## 8. Election arc: Tomozaki starts becoming a strategist rather than a student following scripts

### 8.1 Mimimi's candidacy initially looks hopeless

Hinami enters the student-council election with an enormous structural advantage:

- high school-wide recognition;
- accumulated trust;
- strong academic/athletic reputation;
- proven competence;
- high social fluency;
- Mizusawa as an exceptional recommendation speaker;
- credible issue knowledge and execution ability.

Mimimi has charisma and energy but lacks Hinami's stored credibility.

Tomozaki correctly recognizes that copying Hinami's campaign more competently is unlikely to close the gap.

### 8.2 `別の土俵を作る`: Tomozaki's own competitive style

Tomozaki develops a strategy familiar from how he thinks about games: when an opponent dominates the current arena, **create a different arena**.

The key phrase is `別の土俵を作る`.

This is a significant self-authorship event because it is not a Hinami script. It is Tomozaki translating his own Atafami identity into social strategy.

Instead of trying to make Mimimi deliver a weaker version of Hinami's polished, credibility-centered campaign, he tries to create conditions in which Mimimi's improvisation, comic timing, energy, and ability to pull attention become the relevant advantages.

### 8.3 The staged Siri interruption

Tomozaki's most conspicuous tactic is the staged phone/Siri “accident” during Mimimi's speech.

The point is not simply deception for its own sake. Structurally, the tactic:

1. interrupts the expected formal register;
2. makes the audience attentive;
3. creates an apparent unplanned event;
4. gives Mimimi permission to improvise;
5. shifts evaluation away from “who gives the most polished normal election speech?” toward “who can command the room in live interaction?”

It succeeds locally. The audience reaction is strong.

Tomozaki has therefore produced a real tactical result through an independently constructed strategy.

## 9. The 456–131 defeat: V02's strongest test of the `良ゲー` revision

Mimimi still loses, **456–131**.

This is crucial because the tactical success does not scale into overall victory. Hinami's advantages are too deep.

Tomozaki's reaction is the best direct test yet of V01's end-state.

He does **not** conclude:

- the election was rigged;
- effort is meaningless;
- social life is unknowable;
- his earlier success was a fluke;
- therefore life is again a `クソゲー`.

Instead he analyzes what happened.

Hinami had:

- anticipated likely lines of attack;
- neutralized or preempted efficient proposals;
- accumulated trust far in advance of the election;
- deeper social technique;
- broader knowledge;
- more practiced public performance;
- a larger reservoir of prior effort.

The loss is painful precisely because Tomozaki treats it as real competition. But it is still interpretable.

**V02-Q01: STRENGTHENED strongly.**

Tomozaki's updated worldview survives decisive negative evidence because the defeat supplies causal information rather than epistemic collapse.

## 10. Tomozaki versus Hinami: the first major philosophy-of-effort split

### 10.1 Tomozaki's effort model is not actually “first place”

After the election, discussion turns toward competition and improvement.

Tomozaki explains that in Atafami his deepest opponent is himself. Winning matters, and rank matters, but his central reason for continuing is the experience of becoming better.

This is not a denial of competition. He is extremely competitive.

But the hierarchy of ends is:

**mastery / improvement → competitive testing → ranking outcome**

rather than:

**first place as the final proof of value.**

### 10.2 Hinami is surprised

Hinami had inferred that someone who reached nanashi's level must understand first place essentially as she does.

Her surprise matters because V01 had made the two appear philosophically aligned around effort. V02 shows that the alignment is incomplete.

They agree that:

- excuses without relevant effort are weak;
- skill can be trained;
- serious practice matters;
- high achievement requires disciplined repetition.

They do **not** yet appear to agree on what achievement is **for**.

**V02-Q02: STRENGTHENED strongly.**

The likely future conflict is increasingly about ends, identity, and stopping conditions rather than whether technique works.

## 11. Mimimi's history: effort becomes entangled with comparative identity

### 11.1 Middle-school alienation

Mimimi's background changes the interpretation of her rivalry.

In middle school, she worked harder than the surrounding team and wanted more from competition than many teammates did. What others could celebrate as success, she experienced as insufficient.

The resulting social friction taught her that serious effort itself could make her strange.

This makes Hinami's arrival psychologically powerful.

### 11.2 Hinami validates Mimimi's effort

Hinami is among the first peers to recognize how much Mimimi has actually practiced.

She does not trivialize the effort or ask Mimimi to become less intense for group comfort. Instead she can read the accumulated work and responds from a comparable competitive register.

That recognition is emotionally significant.

Hinami therefore becomes simultaneously:

- a validating witness;
- an admired peer;
- an idealized competitor;
- the person who proves that Mimimi's intensity is not unique;
- the person against whom Mimimi repeatedly becomes second.

### 11.3 “Special” becomes comparative rather than intrinsic

Mimimi's later crisis makes the underlying variable clearer.

She does not merely want to improve.

She wants to be **special**, and she increasingly treats first place as evidence that she is special.

That produces a dangerous structure:

- the comparison target is Hinami;
- Hinami is unusually capable and unusually relentless;
- Mimimi's worth becomes coupled to beating the comparison target;
- each loss therefore threatens identity, not merely performance;
- more effort becomes the obvious response;
- the stopping condition recedes.

This is not simply healthy competitiveness intensified.

It is an effort regime in which the criterion of enough is external and moving.

## 12. Tomozaki initially gives Mimimi the wrong answer

This is one of the most important V02 corrections to any protagonist-centered reading.

Mimimi says:

`今すぐやめたいくらいつらいけど、たぶん、ここでやめたらもっとつらい`

She is suffering enough that she wants to stop immediately, but believes quitting would hurt even more.

Tomozaki understands this through his own gamer experience. He knows the pain of abandoning a serious effort before satisfying oneself that the attempt was complete.

He therefore tells her, in substance:

**keep going.**

At that moment this is not callousness. It is an earnest extension of his own ethic:

- do not take away another competitor's agency;
- do not tell someone to quit merely because the effort is hard;
- respect serious commitment;
- accept pain as part of mastery.

But V02 then shows the limitation of that analogy.

Tomozaki has treated “Mimimi is suffering because serious competition is hard” as the whole problem.

It is not.

## 13. Overtraining reveals a missing variable in Tomozaki's effort ethic

Mimimi begins escalating:

- insufficient sleep;
- falling asleep in class;
- early training;
- late training;
- extending competition into academics;
- attempting to match Hinami's schedule;
- visible physical depletion;
- reduced ordinary joking and play.

At first Tomozaki continues respecting the effort.

The problem is not that effort has become causally useless. Mimimi may indeed improve.

The problem is that **improvement has ceased to be a sufficient criterion for whether the effort regime is good for her**.

V01-Q09 asked whether Tomozaki might overgeneralize his anti-fatalism and treat every outcome as controllable through enough correct effort.

V02 activates the risk in a more precise form:

> Tomozaki can overapply the moral prestige he gives disciplined effort and thereby underweight the goal's ownership, the comparison structure, health costs, relational costs, and the possibility that success would fail to resolve the underlying identity problem.

**V02-Q09: REVISE / ACTIVATED.**

## 14. The rain scene: the “effort limit” is comparative, not absolute

Tomozaki sees Hinami continuing to practice alone in the rain, equipped to keep training when Mimimi finally does not.

The sight is devastating because of what it means inside Mimimi's chosen comparison structure.

Measured against almost any ordinary peer, Mimimi's effort is extraordinary.

Measured against Hinami, it is still not enough.

Tomozaki recognizes that what he has just seen is effectively **Mimimi's limit** relative to this opponent.

The analytical point is not that Mimimi is weak.

It is that a comparative identity system can convert even extraordinary effort into repeated proof of insufficiency.

If the target is “be first relative to Hinami,” then:

- Hinami's additional effort resets the threshold;
- Mimimi cannot secure stable self-worth through improvement alone;
- every evidence of progress can be reclassified as failure if Hinami remains ahead.

This is the strongest reason V02 deserves a dedicated longitudinal ledger on effort / competition / goal ownership.

## 15. Kikuchi asks the question Tomozaki's game model had omitted

Kikuchi asks Tomozaki:

**Why is Hinami such a perfectionist? Why does she work this hard?**

Tomozaki realizes he has not seriously asked.

He has largely treated Hinami's drive as a basic character parameter.

Kikuchi reframes the problem around **motive and stopping conditions**.

Her insight is that competing against someone whose motive you do not understand is frightening because you cannot see the finish line. You do not know what would ever satisfy them enough to stop.

That idea is directly useful for understanding Mimimi's situation.

It also opens a major unresolved Hinami question.

Later, Hinami asks whether pursuing a goal with `一切の妥協なく`—without compromise—is really bad. She says she does not think it is, but also implies that in “life” she has experienced situations that cannot be resolved so simply merely by continuing to accumulate effort.

V02 does not disclose the experience she is referring to.

**OPEN:** Hinami's motive for perfectionism and the history behind this caveat.

Do not fill the gap from later-series knowledge.

## 16. Mimimi's crisis: admiration, gratitude, envy, and self-disgust coexist

Mimimi eventually admits that her feelings toward Hinami have become intolerably mixed.

She:

- admires Hinami;
- loves her as a friend;
- is grateful that Hinami validated her effort;
- wants to defeat her;
- envies her;
- increasingly resents the existence of the comparison;
- has begun wishing, in effect, that Hinami would stop or disappear from the competition.

What horrifies Mimimi is not merely envy.

She recognizes a reversal.

Her middle-school teammates had implicitly wanted **her** to stop trying so hard because her intensity disturbed the group's comfortable norms.

Now Mimimi catches herself wanting Hinami to reduce herself for Mimimi's emotional convenience.

In other words, Mimimi becomes afraid she is reproducing the demand that once wounded her:

**read the room; stop trying so hard; make your effort smaller so I can feel okay.**

This self-recognition helps explain why simply telling Mimimi “you are already good enough” cannot resolve the problem.

The issue is bound to identity, comparison, gratitude, and moral self-judgment at once.

## 17. Tomozaki cannot solve the event by argument

Tomozaki tries to help.

He sincerely tells Mimimi that she already appears “shining” to him.

The sentiment is real. It does not reach the core wound.

This is analytically valuable because V02 refuses the protagonist a universal persuasion mechanic.

Tomozaki's improved directness is not magic.

He is not the right relationship-holder for every intervention.

The solution begins when he recognizes that **he cannot clear this event alone**.

The chapter title states the principle directly in game language:

`低レベルのキャラだけじゃ解決できないイベントもある`

Some events cannot be solved by the low-level character alone.

## 18. Agency advances by recognizing limits, not by maximizing personal control

Tomozaki's most important action is embarrassingly simple.

He asks Mimimi to walk home with him **and Tama**, publicly enough to expose himself to ridicule.

The act requires:

- observing that Tama is struggling over how to approach Mimimi;
- accepting that his own words have been insufficient;
- understanding that relationship-specific authority matters;
- tolerating social embarrassment;
- creating an encounter rather than controlling its outcome.

This is a more mature form of agency than V01's “I can act instead of withdraw.”

It is:

**I can act responsibly without pretending the whole outcome belongs to me.**

That is a direct counterweight to the V01-Q09 overcontrol risk.

## 19. Tama's answer: a non-comparative identity anchor

Mimimi says that without first place, without Hinami's excellence, and without Tama's uncompromising selfhood, she feels empty.

Tama answers from a different evaluative system entirely.

She tells Mimimi:

`みんみは、私のヒーローなんだよ？`

Mimimi is **Tama's hero**.

The relationship creates a value that Hinami cannot displace by running faster, scoring higher, winning elections, or training longer.

Tama then converts Mimimi's obsession with number one into a joke:

if Mimimi absolutely must be first at something, she can be the world's number-one idiot **in Tama's eyes**.

The joke matters because it relocates value from universal ranking to relationship-specific recognition.

That produces a criterion that is:

- non-transferable;
- non-zero-sum;
- not dependent on beating Hinami;
- grounded in what Mimimi has actually done for Tama;
- compatible with Hinami remaining exceptional.

This does not mean comparison or ambition disappears.

It means Mimimi is offered a self-state that does not collapse when somebody else is better.

## 20. Mimimi and Tama: the relationship is mutually regulative

V01 already suggested that the Mimimi/Tama bond should not be reduced to Mimimi helping a socially rigid Tama.

V02 strongly confirms reciprocal regulation.

Mimimi historically:

- draws Tama outward;
- protects her;
- adds play and movement;
- treats her uncompromising directness as something lovable rather than merely inconvenient.

Tama now:

- sees through Mimimi's forced energy;
- refuses to validate the self-destructive comparison structure;
- names Mimimi's importance in personal rather than ranked terms;
- provides an identity anchor that Tomozaki cannot supply.

**STRENGTHEN:** the pair should be modeled as mutually stabilizing, not helper/dependent.

## 21. Mimimi's return to track: recovery is not defeat of ambition

Mimimi returns to the track club after the crisis.

Importantly, the resolution is not:

- stop caring about first place forever;
- become less ambitious;
- accept mediocrity;
- renounce Hinami;
- abandon competition.

She apologizes, returns, and re-enters the group with characteristic humor.

Her envy and competitive feelings are described as reduced, not erased.

That distinction protects the analysis from a sentimental flattening in which healthy self-worth requires loss of ambition.

V02's stronger claim is:

**ambition becomes dangerous when comparative victory is required to stabilize identity.**

Competition itself is not rejected.

## 22. `空気` scales upward but remains contestable

The student-council election broadens the V01 `空気` model beyond small-class interactions.

Tomozaki learns that large-group persuasion also depends on:

- what the audience is prepared to hear;
- established reputations;
- role legitimacy;
- attention;
- framing;
- social proof;
- who can credibly say what;
- whether the proposal fits the current evaluative field.

Hinami dominates partly because the school already possesses a coherent “Hinami is competent and trustworthy” atmosphere.

Tomozaki's campaign strategy works locally by **reframing the field** so that Mimimi's strengths become salient.

This produces a useful refinement:

`空気` is neither an invisible absolute nor merely conformity pressure. It can be understood as a **locally coordinated field of expectations, legitimacy, salience, and response costs** that skilled actors can sometimes alter.

**V02-Q04: STRENGTHENED but still bounded.**

## 23. Nakamura: rivalry becomes minimally reciprocal

Tomozaki's respect for Nakamura's effort survives into V02.

The controller gift matters because Tomozaki does not give it out of generic friendliness. It is tied to recognition of Nakamura as someone who actually trained after losing.

Nakamura accepts.

This still does not justify “friendship.”

But the relationship has moved from:

**contemptuous social antagonist / defeated challenger**

toward:

**social friction + recognized competitor + limited reciprocal acknowledgment.**

**V02-Q06: STRENGTHENED modestly.**

## 24. Yuzu: insufficient new evidence for the costly-resistance question

V01-Q07 asked whether Yuzu's willingness to oppose local pressure would generalize outside the exact V01 confrontation.

V02 gives more ordinary interaction with Yuzu and continued social integration, but not enough comparable high-cost resistance evidence to adjudicate that question.

**V02-Q07: OPEN.**

Do not upgrade a character trait from absence of contradiction.

## 25. Hinami and Mizusawa rumor: meaningful affect, insufficient romantic proof

Tomozaki becomes notably preoccupied with the rumor that Hinami and Mizusawa may be dating.

When the rumor is resolved, Hinami deliberately tells Tomozaki that they are not dating while looking directly at him, then enjoys his flustered reaction.

This supports:

- Tomozaki is not affectively neutral to the possibility;
- Hinami is aware that the information matters to him;
- Hinami enjoys testing or teasing his reaction.

It does **not** establish:

- Tomozaki is in love with Hinami;
- Hinami is romantically interested in Tomozaki;
- the teasing is a confession-by-proxy;
- a romance route is settled.

Relationship state remains **OPEN / mixed**.

## 26. Dimension-by-dimension Tomozaki update

### Competence — STRENGTHEN

Tomozaki can:

- survive higher-level group interaction;
- consciously deploy Mizusawa-style techniques;
- construct an election strategy;
- improvise around audience attention;
- analyze failure causally;
- identify relationship-specific intervention limits.

### Confidence — MODEST STRENGTHEN, still volatile

He remains highly anxious and self-deprecating. Social threat still produces strong anticipatory stress.

But severe failure no longer automatically collapses his model of possibility.

### Status — MODEST STRENGTHEN

He is increasingly present in ordinary peer interaction and can participate in socially consequential activity.

He is still far from Hinami/Mizusawa status and remains an easy target for Erika.

### Self-concept — REVISE upward

V01's largest lag begins narrowing.

Tomozaki can increasingly recognize:

- appearance changes as real;
- acquired skills as partially his;
- loneliness as evidence that social connection has become normal enough to miss;
- strategic competence as transferable outside Atafami.

### Agency — STRONG STRENGTHEN

The election strategy is self-authored.

More importantly, the Mimimi crisis adds **bounded agency**: Tomozaki learns that responsible action can mean creating conditions for somebody else to act rather than personally solving the problem.

### Values — STRENGTHEN / DIFFERENTIATE

Tomozaki's effort ethic persists.

V02 differentiates it from Hinami/Mimimi first-place orientation and forces the first correction to its overapplication.

The emerging value structure increasingly includes:

- mastery;
- sincerity;
- respect for effort;
- self-authorship;
- limits on instrumental manipulation;
- recognition of other people's agency;
- recognition that not every costly effort deserves endorsement merely because it is sincere.

## 27. V01 question adjudication

### V02-Q01 — Does the `良ゲー` revision survive negative evidence?

**STRENGTHEN.**

The 456–131 defeat is decisive negative evidence, yet Tomozaki responds through causal analysis and renewed practice rather than categorical fatalism.

## 29. Rival readings and failure modes to avoid

### Rival reading A: “V02 proves hard work is bad”

Too broad.

The volume repeatedly values practice and improvement. The problem is not effort as such but effort whose goal structure, comparison target, self-worth coupling, and collateral costs become destructive.

### Rival reading B: “Mimimi loses because Hinami is naturally more talented”

Poor fit.

V02 repeatedly emphasizes accumulated labor and discipline on Hinami's side. Talent may matter, but the source does not support reducing the gap to innate capacity.

### Rival reading C: “Tomozaki is right because he tells Mimimi to keep trying”

Incorrect.

V02 deliberately shows that his initial answer is incomplete and partly harmful because he maps his own competitive psychology onto Mimimi.

### Rival reading D: “Tama solves Mimimi by telling her rankings don't matter”

Too simple.

Tama does not deliver an abstract anti-ranking philosophy. She gives Mimimi a **relationship-specific identity**: hero, friend, and uniquely valued person.

### Rival reading E: “Hinami is emotionally empty because she optimizes everything”

Unsupported.

Her excitement about competition with nanashi is direct evidence of genuine-seeming pleasure. Her motive for perfectionism remains unresolved.

### Rival reading F: “The election loss proves social structure defeats technique”

Too strong.

Technique materially changes audience response and demonstrates strategic competence. Hinami wins because technique, reputation, accumulated trust, preparation, and prior effort interact.

### Rival reading G: “Tomozaki/Hinami romance is confirmed”

Unsupported.

The rumor reaction and teasing are meaningful affective data, not a sufficient romantic conclusion.

### Rival reading H: “Mimimi's envy means her friendship with Hinami was fake”

Contradicted by the source.

Gratitude, admiration, affection, competition, envy, and resentment coexist. The difficulty is precisely that the friendship is real enough for the envy to feel morally intolerable to Mimimi.
