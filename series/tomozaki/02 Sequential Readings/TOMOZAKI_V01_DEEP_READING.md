## 3. Volume structure and analytical movement

The chapter titles themselves maintain the game's-language frame while the volume progressively tests whether that frame can be transferred into social life:

The important progression is therefore not simply **isolated boy becomes popular**. V01 gives much stronger evidence for a sequence of **fatalistic model → controlled experiment → trainable subskills → localized self-efficacy → self-authored transfer → value-consistent action**. Social status improves only modestly and unevenly relative to those deeper changes.

## 4. Starting state: Tomozaki's fatalism is selective, not global passivity

### 4.1 The opening theory

The prologue establishes Tomozaki's central evaluative vocabulary. A good game allows effort and correct practice to predictably affect results; a game dominated by luck or fixed starting parameters is badly designed. From that premise, he classifies life as `クソゲー` because its rules are unclear, initial conditions are unequal, and one cannot simply select a different character.

**DIRECT:** This is Tomozaki's explicit starting theory.

It would be analytically incorrect to promote it to an objective statement about the novel's world. The volume immediately supplies reasons to doubt its completeness, but it also does not prove Hinami's opposite claim that life is a universally fair `神ゲー`.

### 4.2 The starting contradiction is internal to Tomozaki's own values

Tomozaki is already Japan's top-ranked Atafami player, `nanashi`. His behavior around the rematch with Nakamura is highly diagnostic. When Nakamura implies that equipment, character choice, seating, or familiarity explains the loss, Tomozaki deliberately changes variables: characters, controllers, seats, stock count, and even the use of knowledge-dependent techniques. His aim is to isolate the causal variable of skill as cleanly as possible.

He also strongly dislikes people who rationalize losses without first making the relevant effort.

This means V01 does **not** begin with a boy who lacks discipline, standards, or causal reasoning. It begins with a boy who possesses all three but has domain-bounded them. He believes Atafami is learnable enough to deserve effort and life is structurally unfair enough not to deserve it.

**CORROBORATED INFERENCE:** Hinami's argument succeeds partly because she exposes this selective exception. Her strongest rhetorical move is not “be confident”; it is “you are behaving toward life like the kind of losing player you despise.” That attack is powerful because it uses Tomozaki's pre-existing ethic rather than supplying an entirely foreign one.

### 4.3 NO NAME already foreshadows transferable learning

Before Tomozaki knows NO NAME's identity, he infers that the player has deliberately trained combo escapes by accepting disadvantageous states that ordinary top players tend to avoid. He recognizes the training signature because the defensive skill is too developed to be explained by normal match exposure.

This matters for two reasons. First, Tomozaki can already distinguish **performance optimization** from **skill acquisition**: short-term losses may be rational if they improve a strategically chosen weakness. Second, he respects the willingness to sacrifice immediate wins for long-term mastery.

When NO NAME turns out to be Hinami, her later claim that she has practiced social life acquires credibility in Tomozaki's own epistemic framework. He has already independently detected that her game skill contains evidence of deliberate training.

## 5. Hinami's reveal: public perfection, private coaching, and the mistake of “true self”

### 5.1 Two clearly different presentation states

At school Hinami is the polished, academically and socially successful class center Tomozaki regards as a “perfect heroine.” In the private meeting she becomes much more severe, competitive, game-obsessed, blunt, and contemptuous of his lack of social effort.

**DIRECT:** The two presentation states differ markedly by recipient and context.

**OPEN:** V01 does not establish that one state is “real” and the other “fake.” The private state may permit greater disclosure; the public state is demonstrably practiced; both may still express genuine parts of Hinami. The analytical method therefore treats them as recipient-conditioned states until later evidence can establish motive and ownership more securely.

### 5.2 Hinami's life theory is a character-held model

Hinami argues that life has learnable rules whose interactions can be understood and exploited. She presents herself as evidence: facial expression, posture, grooming, conversation, status management, and other social behaviors are not simply gifts but trainable skills she has systematically acquired.

Her central counterclaim to Tomozaki is effectively `人生は神ゲー`.

V01 supports only part of this theory. It repeatedly demonstrates that **some** social outcomes are responsive to learnable behavior. It does not establish that all important life outcomes are fair, that everyone has equal access to learning, or that every human value can be reduced to optimization.

The volume's own strongest bounded result is Tomozaki's later revision to `良ゲー`, not full adoption of Hinami's `神ゲー` verdict.

### 5.3 Why Tomozaki accepts the experiment

Tomozaki is not most persuaded by Hinami's attractiveness or social status. He becomes emotionally engaged when she treats Atafami—the domain in which he has invested enormous private labor—as equal in value to the life game she prizes. Her claim validates effort that his ordinary social environment regards as excessive or useless.

His agreement is therefore best classified as **provisional experimental consent**:

- he has not accepted her full social philosophy;
- he agrees that he has not properly tested his contrary claim;
- he decides to play seriously enough to evaluate the system from experience.

That distinction matters later. His continued development does not require him to become Hinami's ideological copy.

## 6. Coaching contract: asymmetrical, often abrasive, but not simple domination

### 6.1 Goal structure and information control

Hinami quickly builds a game-like curriculum:

- large goal: become socially successful at approximately her level;
- medium goal: obtain a girlfriend before the school-year promotion;
- small goals: measurable near-term behaviors or observable outcomes;
- daily briefings and debriefings;
- repeated drills in expression, posture, speech, conversation, clothing, and interaction;
- explicit trial/error and feedback cycles.

She chooses most early goals and methods. She also has a large information advantage: she understands class dynamics, conventional appearance norms, conversation patterns, and the likely response to various approaches far better than Tomozaki.

The power asymmetry is therefore substantial.

### 6.2 Coercive language versus actual refusal space

Hinami sometimes speaks in the language of orders and initially humiliates Tomozaki. Her criticism can be insulting and morally loaded. A purely celebratory reading of the mentorship would erase those features.

At the same time, V01 provides meaningful counterevidence to a “Tomozaki has no agency” reading:

- he explicitly chooses to undertake the experiment after arguing with her;
- he continues to question her premises;
- he objects when her romance strategy conflicts with his sense of sincerity;
- she eventually states that she is an `攻略本`—a guide—and the final choice is his;
- he later departs from her recommended handling of Kikuchi;
- he initiates actions that she did not assign.

**CORROBORATED INFERENCE:** V01 depicts a mentorship with strong asymmetry and abrasive pressure, but Tomozaki's trajectory is already toward increasing self-authorship rather than obedient absorption.

Whether this balance holds in later volumes remains open.

## 7. Social learning: technique precedes self-concept

### 7.1 Early failures are data, not disproof

Tomozaki's first assignment—to speak to girls at school—produces awkwardness. His practiced smile under a mask is misread. Prepared topics fail when their premises are wrong. He overextends newly learned ideas in conversation. The important pattern is that Hinami treats these failures as training data.

Her model of social practice resembles skill acquisition in games:

- reduce a difficult task into subskills;
- observe higher-level players;
- imitate reliable forms before claiming personal mastery;
- deliberately expose oneself to low-cost losses;
- debrief the exact failure;
- repeat until cognitive load decreases.

This is directly instantiated through posture, facial expression, topic preparation, recording and replaying voice, constrained-vowel response drills, grooming, clothing, and repeated conversation attempts.

### 7.2 Imitation is explicitly transitional

The Omiya clothing and grooming sequence is especially useful for the authenticity question. Hinami recommends copying expert-curated results—magazine looks, mannequin coordinates, salon judgment—rather than requiring Tomozaki to possess aesthetic intuition he has never trained.

The rationale is not “pretend forever.” It is closer to: use externally reliable patterns first, then develop the underlying sense through exposure and repetition.

That supplies direct source evidence against the simplistic equation:

`learned / copied behavior = fake self`.

V01 instead makes at least four states analytically distinct:

1. externally supplied technique;
2. conscious imitation;
3. demonstrated competence;
4. self-authored use or rejection of the technique.

Tomozaki reaches different levels in different domains by the end of the volume.

### 7.3 The clearest competence-confidence split

After changes to hair, clothes, eyebrows, posture, and expression, Tomozaki's sister reacts strongly enough to satisfy Hinami's objective small-goal criterion. The external change is therefore behaviorally/socially visible before Tomozaki has fully updated his self-concept.

Later he can still catastrophize after one failed conversation with Yuzu, reverting immediately toward “this is impossible for someone like me.”

**DIRECT:** performance has improved while confidence remains fragile.

**CORROBORATED INFERENCE:** V01 strongly validates the architecture's decision to separate competence, confidence, status, and self-concept. They do not move as a single variable.

## 8. Tomozaki's existing strength survives the training: considered directness

### 8.1 Mimimi and Tama expose a non-deficit model

A major correction occurs when Tomozaki speaks spontaneously with Mimimi and Tama. Instead of relying on a rehearsed topic, he gives his actual thought about youth and `共感能力`. The result is unusual but socially productive: Mimimi laughs and engages, and Tama eventually responds positively.

Hinami identifies this as a genuine strength. Tomozaki is unusually willing and able to formulate a thought and say it directly rather than merely emit socially safe agreement.

This changes the analytical model from:

> Tomozaki lacks social ability and needs an imported personality.

Toward:

> Tomozaki has uneven social competence: weak conventional delivery and situational fluency, but a potentially valuable pre-existing capacity for direct considered expression.

### 8.2 Training becomes additive rather than substitutive

The most promising V01 integration is therefore not replacement of Tomozaki's old self with a Hinami-built persona. It is the combination of:

- his existing analytical discipline;
- his existing anti-excuse ethic;
- his existing directness;
- newly learned delivery, observation, and interaction techniques.

By the second half of V01, some of his strongest moments occur precisely when the new skill scaffold lets the old value structure reach other people more effectively.

**INTERPRETIVE HYPOTHESIS:** Tomozaki's durable growth may depend less on approximating Hinami's exact social style than on using technique to make his own values and thoughts socially actionable. V02 and later volumes must test this.

## 9. `空気`: social ecology as local normative coordination

### 9.1 The home-economics vote

The Nakamura-group interaction provides the first sustained case. A joking majority-vote structure pressures the room toward a predetermined answer about whether Tomozaki is funny. Tama refuses to yield to the group atmosphere, creating tension. Hinami then changes the permissible frame by joking about Nakamura's prior loss and rejection, turning previously dangerous subjects into acceptable shared humor.

Hinami explains `空気` as something like the local criterion for good/bad behavior inside the group.

This should remain a **character-held framework**, but V01 supplies multiple observations consistent with it:

- people alter responses after a high-status participant changes the permissible interpretation;
- Tama is unusually resistant to this pressure;
- Yuzu is unusually sensitive to it;
- Mimimi deliberately works to keep interactions smooth;
- Erika later attempts to define Tomozaki's earnestness and game commitment as socially contemptible, with followers aligning to her frame.

### 9.2 What the model does and does not explain

By V01's end, `空気` has more than one independent scene supporting its usefulness. It helps explain local coordination, taboo management, status-linked permission, and why identical words can land differently depending on speaker and setting.

It still should not be promoted to a total theory of social life. The same volume includes people who resist local pressure, revise it, misread it, or act from values that override it. `空気` is therefore best retained as a **partially corroborated local social mechanism**, not a universal law.

## 10. Peer-network differentiation: bending, constancy, and support

### 10.1 Tama

Tama is directly characterized through repeated refusal to bend merely because a group wants easy conformity. That produces both strength and vulnerability: she can maintain a position that others abandon, but she may also be exposed when the local atmosphere turns against her.

Hinami's metaphor that Tama's unprotected heart needs surrounding “armor” is itself interpretive, but the observed group scene supports the broader point that social nonconformity has recipient- and status-dependent costs.

### 10.2 Mimimi

Mimimi explicitly describes herself as using energy and smiles to keep situations enjoyable and says she often changes herself for the moment. She contrasts this with Tama's refusal to bend.

Tomozaki then forms a more interesting hypothesis: Mimimi may not simply support Tama; Tama's constancy may psychologically support Mimimi by providing a stable object of admiration and meaning.

**INTERPRETIVE HYPOTHESIS:** the Mimimi→Tama relationship may be more mutually regulative than its visible caretaker pattern suggests.

V01 does not yet supply enough direct interior evidence to establish the strength or direction of that dependence.

### 10.3 Yuzu

Yuzu initially appears highly responsive to group evaluation. Her desire to become useful to Nakamura gives her a concrete reason to learn Atafami, but her broader desire to change is less stable. Tomozaki notices that she bends like Mimimi without yet seeing the same clear purpose in it.

That interpretation remains focalized, but Yuzu's later costly choice to defend Tomozaki and Atafami gives direct evidence that she can act against the immediate social atmosphere when a value or relationship becomes important enough.

## 11. Tomozaki becomes a teacher: the strongest evidence of internalization

### 11.1 The Yuzu lesson is unassigned

Tomozaki's encounter with the dejected Yuzu is important because it occurs after he has completed his assigned conversation quota. He chooses to continue the interaction and eventually agrees to teach her Atafami so she can become a practice partner for Nakamura.

This is a clear increase in **agency**: the behavior is not merely compliance with Hinami's task list.

### 11.2 He reproduces Hinami's pedagogy without being told to

When teaching Yuzu, Tomozaki spontaneously uses the same learning architecture Hinami has been applying to him:

- diagnose current skill;
- identify the goal rather than train randomly;
- choose a character appropriate to that goal;
- isolate a foundational mechanic;
- create an “always available” drill that does not waste scarce console time;
- add memorization of expert play and complete move knowledge;
- separate baseline training from applied matches.

This is one of V01's strongest pieces of evidence that learned technique is becoming **owned competence** rather than costume.

Tomozaki does not imitate Hinami's surface social persona. He recognizes an abstract training principle, maps it into his own expert domain, and independently deploys it for someone else's goal.

**CORROBORATED INFERENCE:** at least part of Hinami's framework has been cognitively internalized because Tomozaki can transfer it across domains without prompting.

## 12. Values under pressure: effort, sincerity, and self-authorship

### 12.1 Tomozaki's anti-excuse ethic is PRESERVED

The volume does not replace Tomozaki's core value system. His contempt for excuse-making remains stable from the first Nakamura match through his later reaction to Nakamura's genuine training.

What changes is the scope of that ethic. Once Tomozaki accepts that social skills may be trainable, he can no longer exempt himself so easily from the effort standard he applies in games.

Revision state: **PRESERVE + EXTEND**.

### 12.2 Kikuchi and the refusal to optimize through a lie

Kikuchi has noticed Tomozaki repeatedly in the library and incorrectly infers that he is a devoted reader of Michael Andy. Excited by the apparent shared interest, she reveals that she writes fiction and asks him to read her work. Tomozaki initially allows the false premise to stand.

Hinami sees the situation as an unusually favorable romantic opening. Tomozaki instead becomes increasingly uncomfortable with the fact that the intimacy was triggered by a lie.

In Chapter 6 he confesses the truth, withdraws the earlier promise that was based on the false premise, apologizes, and asks to continue talking on an honest basis. He also deliberately declines to use Hinami's movie-ticket opportunity immediately after the confession because he does not want to exploit the residual emotional momentum of the false shared interest.

This is a pivotal self-authored divergence.

**DIRECT:** Tomozaki sacrifices an instrumentally favorable move because he regards the basis as insufficiently honest.

**CORROBORATED INFERENCE:** V01 establishes that he is not simply optimizing whatever objective Hinami sets. As soon as a tactic conflicts with a value he recognizes as his own, he can choose the lower-probability route.

### 12.3 Counterevidence: he is not already perfectly authentic

Tomozaki's statement that he does not say things he does not think is an important self-description, but V01 itself supplies counterevidence: he did initially pretend to share Kikuchi's author interest.

The more defensible claim is narrower. By the latter half of V01, Tomozaki increasingly treats **alignment between speech and considered belief** as an important self-authored norm, and he is willing to incur social cost to restore that alignment after violating it.

This distinction prevents his own authenticity theory from becoming another untested self-description.

## 13. Nakamura: from excuse-making opponent to effort-bearing rival

### 13.1 Early state

In the first match, Nakamura attempts to explain away the gap through character, controller, seating, and other external variables. Tomozaki despises this response and systematically removes those variables.

### 13.2 Later state

By Chapter 6, Nakamura has trained substantially. He uses techniques and combinations that require deliberate practice, continues rematching despite serial losses, and no longer relies on the same excuses.

Tomozaki notices the change.

When Erika mocks Nakamura for wasting effort on a game he keeps losing, Tomozaki publicly defends him. The defense is not rooted in friendship; it is rooted in the fact that Nakamura is now doing the thing Tomozaki respects: taking loss seriously, working, and becoming stronger.

Nakamura's departing `次は勝つ` marks a changed dyadic state. It is evidence for an emerging rivalry based on acknowledged effort. It is not yet sufficient to call them friends.

Revision state for Tomozaki→Nakamura: **REVISE** from primarily contemptuous opponent to opponent whose effort Tomozaki can respect.

## 14. The Erika confrontation: agency rises before confidence

This is the clearest scene for separating competence, confidence, status, agency, and values.

Tomozaki knows Erika has more social leverage. He notices the hostile `空気`. He is physically frightened and does not believe his own social skill can guarantee a successful intervention. His hands shake.

He speaks anyway.

His intervention defends both Nakamura's effort and the legitimacy of caring seriously about Atafami. He effectively says that mocking another person's earnest effort is worse than making excuses for one's own failure.

The action is therefore **not** evidence that Tomozaki has become broadly confident. The text gives almost the opposite evidence: fear remains high.

It is strong evidence that:

- agency has increased;
- his values can override anticipated status cost;
- his trained ability to express himself now serves a pre-existing moral commitment;
- social courage is becoming distinct from social comfort.

Yuzu's intervention then matters for similar reasons. She is visibly frightened yet acts against the local group pressure to defend Tomozaki and the activity she has begun to value. Her action is a costly behavioral data point consistent with her stated desire to change. It does not yet prove a stable new trait.

## 15. Hinami's labor becomes visible

Tomozaki accidentally hears Hinami's private recording practice and discovers that a line she delivered fluently in a prior social rescue had been rehearsed repeatedly.

This is one of V01's most important corrections to his original image of her.

He had treated “Hinami is amazing” almost as a fixed parameter. The recording converts that impression into a process model: she is amazing in part because she is continually trying to become amazing.

The distinction is semantically captured by his thought that she is `ちゃんとすごくなろうとしている`.

This observation has three analytical consequences:

1. it supplies direct evidence that at least some of Hinami's public fluency is trained;
2. it weakens a natural-gift explanation for all of her social performance;
3. it makes Tomozaki's own improvement less incompatible with his identity, because the person he considered naturally “top tier” is also grinding.

It still does not prove that all of Hinami's public behavior is strategic or insincere. Practice and sincerity are not opposites.

## 16. Ordinary-life evidence prevents reduction to optimization machines

V01 repeatedly interrupts abstract social strategy with low-stakes preferences and habits.

Hinami:

- genuinely enjoys food and especially cheese;
- can become embarrassed over ordinary preferences;
- enjoys obscure games enough to use their slang (`おにただ`);
- combines intense planning with spontaneous appetite and playful responses.

Tomozaki:

- remains deeply attentive to games even while training socially;
- has a teasing, conflict-prone but informative relationship with his sister;
- notices small pleasures, awkwardness, food, and humor rather than processing every interaction purely as a metric;
- derives special satisfaction not merely from “status gain” but from seeing his own effort causally produce a result.

These observations matter because a purely schematic reading could turn both central characters into embodiments of competing philosophies. V01 already gives enough mundane evidence to resist that collapse.

## 17. Relationship-state ledger at the V01 boundary

### 17.1 Tomozaki → Hinami

**DIRECT evidence:**

- respects her as the only Atafami player close enough to challenge him;
- initially resents and argues with her social judgment;
- accepts her coaching as an experiment;
- increasingly admires her effort once he discovers its extent;
- sometimes feels gratitude and irritation simultaneously;
- independently invites her to a movie in Chapter 7 and is genuinely disappointed when she initially says she is unavailable.

**Current state:** strong gamer respect + coach trust + growing personal interest/attachment, but the exact category of that interest remains **OPEN**. V01 does not justify a romance conclusion.

### 17.2 Hinami → Tomozaki

**DIRECT evidence:**

- respects `nanashi` enough to seek a meeting;
- is initially shocked/disappointed by Tomozaki's offline condition;
- invests substantial time in his training;
- observes him closely enough to calibrate assignments;
- protects him strategically in group situations and performs post-conflict reputation management;
- becomes willing to go to another movie with him after declining his specific next-day invitation.

**Current state:** highly invested mentor/competitor relationship with real personal time expenditure. Whether that investment is friendship, gamer affinity, project commitment, affection, or a mixture is **OPEN**.

### 17.3 Tomozaki ↔ Yuzu Izumi

Yuzu moves from an assigned conversation target to someone who voluntarily discloses her desire to become useful to Nakamura. Tomozaki becomes her Atafami teacher and uses his own life revision to encourage her.

Yuzu later acts at substantial social cost to defend Tomozaki during the Erika confrontation.

**Current state:** emergent reciprocal support with teacher/student and peer components. Romance is not established.

### 17.4 Tomozaki ↔ Fuka Kikuchi

Kikuchi has independently noticed Tomozaki's library routine. The mistaken author-interest premise creates rapid disclosure, including her private writing. Tomozaki then deliberately resets the relationship by confessing the mistake rather than exploiting it.

She accepts continued conversation after the confession.

**Current state:** a potentially significant relationship has survived an honesty reset. Hinami's interpretation that Kikuchi may be romantically interested remains a hypothesis, not a fact.

### 17.5 Tomozaki ↔ Nakamura

The relationship begins with resentment, humiliation, and unilateral skill superiority. Nakamura later trains enough that Tomozaki recognizes his effort; Tomozaki publicly defends that effort; Nakamura ends with a future-oriented competitive statement.

**Current state:** adversarial relationship revised toward meaningful rivalry and respect potential; friendship remains unestablished.

### 17.6 Mimimi ↔ Tama

Mimimi describes bending herself to preserve a pleasant atmosphere while admiring Tama's refusal to bend. Tomozaki hypothesizes that Tama's constancy may emotionally support Mimimi as much as Mimimi visibly protects Tama.

**Current state:** directly reciprocal companionship is evident; the deeper psychological dependence model remains **INTERPRETIVE HYPOTHESIS**.

## 18. Chapter 7: consequences, reputation, and the meaning of “success”

The aftermath of the Erika conflict is notably less catastrophic than Tomozaki's old model might predict. Class hierarchy does not collapse. Nakamura remains socially central; relations between groups become awkward but repairable; classmates approach Tomozaki mostly from curiosity rather than hostility.

Hinami also performs deliberate reputational repair, reframing Nakamura's Atafami obsession and the game's appeal in ordinary group conversation. This is direct evidence that she uses social framing strategically after an event rather than treating status consequences as fixed.

Two outcomes stand out:

- Yuzu's Atafami practice becomes common knowledge as part of her interest in Nakamura, creating a warmer class interpretation of her behavior;
- Nakamura becomes even more obsessed with training, to the point that the attempted romantic “support” has arguably had the opposite short-term effect.

This is useful counterevidence to simplistic optimization. Social interventions produce second-order effects that are not fully controllable, even for people actively modeling the system.

## 19. The ending: causal self-efficacy, not ideological surrender

Tomozaki chooses another unassigned action: he invites Hinami to the movie screening connected to the unused tickets. She declines the exact proposal because of a prior commitment, then offers to go to a different movie with him immediately.

Tomozaki's internal reaction is unusually precise. He does not identify the joy primarily as becoming a “riajuu” or merely being able to go out with a girl. He identifies it as the experience of producing a result he wanted **in reality, through his own effort**.

That is the most important endpoint of V01.

The volume begins with:

- life is a `クソゲー`;
- important parameters are fixed;
- effort cannot be trusted to map to results;
- therefore serious engagement is irrational.

By Chapter 5 Tomozaki explicitly says that life may not be a `神ゲー`, but it is at least a `良ゲー`. By the final page he describes himself as a beginner who intends to keep grinding this game.

This is not total conversion to Hinami's worldview. It is an experimentally earned revision in causal belief:

> some real-world outcomes are learnable enough that effort is worth testing.

That narrower conclusion is both better supported and more consistent with Tomozaki's own epistemic style than “Hinami was simply right.”

## 22. Rival readings and failure modes to avoid

### Rival reading A: “Hinami proves life is fair”

Too strong. V01 demonstrates trainability of several social behaviors, not universal fairness. Tomozaki's own bounded conclusion is weaker: life is at least worth playing seriously.

### Rival reading B: “Tomozaki becomes fake by learning social tricks”

Poor fit to the evidence. He increasingly transfers techniques into self-chosen behavior and rejects optimized tactics that conflict with his values. Practice is not enough to establish inauthenticity.

### Rival reading C: “Hinami is simply manipulative”

V01 does contain harsh pressure, instrumental framing, and asymmetric control. It also contains extensive labor on Tomozaki's behalf, rescue behavior, reputation management, respect for final choice, and willingness to spend unstructured time with him. A unitary motive is not established.

### Rival reading D: “Tomozaki's success is just makeover/status uplift”

The makeover creates observable improvement, but the deepest changes occur when Tomozaki acts despite low status and fear. Chapter 6 is almost designed to separate agency from surface confidence.

### Rival reading E: “The romance targets are already settled”

Not supported. Hinami proposes Kikuchi as a potential target; Yuzu has explicit interest in Nakamura; Tomozaki independently invites Hinami at the end. These are relationship data, not a justified ranking or endpoint forecast.

### Rival reading F: “Nakamura is only an antagonist”

The later match complicates that role. His sustained effort earns Tomozaki's respect, and `次は勝つ` establishes future competitive orientation. The relationship remains adversarial but is no longer analytically reducible to contempt.
