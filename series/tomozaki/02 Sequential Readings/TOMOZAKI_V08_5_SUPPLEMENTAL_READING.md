## 7. The first Christmas establishes an ideal baseline, not later repair

Unit 1 opens on December 24, at the class's combined festival wrap party and Christmas gathering. It establishes a relationship only days old and a class still processing Kikuchi's play.

### 7.1 Selected belonging becomes reciprocal

Classmates praise and debate the play rather than merely tolerating its author. Kikuchi says that seeing her work received makes her feel that she herself has been accepted, and she wants to become closer to the class gradually. This is direct evidence that selected craft can create reciprocal social access.

She nevertheless declines the large-group karaoke continuation without hostility. Her refusal is compatible with enjoyment of the party and desire for closer ties. V08.5 therefore strengthens the selected-habitat model: successful belonging is not measured by accepting every available social format.

### 7.2 The relationship begins with mutual bids and calendar coordination

Tomozaki and Kikuchi spend private time together after the party, build a snowman, take a photograph, and discuss their first Christmas as a couple. Kikuchi makes the wish for future gift exchange explicit; both treat next Christmas as a shared horizon. They also coordinate December 26 and January 1 plans.

This is a useful counterfactual baseline for V08:

| Early interstitial state | V08 maintenance crisis |
|---|---|
| both actors name wanted future time | obligations accumulate without an explicit priority rule |
| scheduling is anticipatory and mutually visible | later scheduling becomes reactive and fragmented |
| touch, photographs, and promises are jointly welcomed | later affection is used as reassurance without settling disclosure |

The contrast does not prove that initial intimacy was false. It shows that genuine affection and competent early coordination do not by themselves govern later capacity.

### 7.3 Hinami's play interpretation remains indirect evidence

Hinami questions whether Alcia can marry Libra and remain the strong, just queen the role requires. Her objection suggests a value conflict between attachment and uncompromised strength. It is a statement made through interpretation of Kikuchi's fiction, not a direct disclosure that Hinami rejects dependence in her own life.

Kikuchi privately apologizes to Hinami near the end of the party. Yuzu hears only the apology and Tomozaki sees the exchange without hearing its reason. The source does not establish what Kikuchi is apologizing for; the later Unit 6 conversation cannot be silently substituted for this missing content.

## 8. `名もなき花`: loss, team-goal failure, and the construction of NO NAME

Unit 2 provides the first direct pre-series chain joining Hinami's sisters, basketball defeat, Atafami, nanashi, and the `NO NAME` identity. It narrows the family ambiguity sharply while still withholding the decisive event.

### 8.1 Three sisters are directly established; Nagisa's fate is not

In childhood, Aoi plays games with two younger sisters, Nagisa and Haruka. Nagisa wins by adapting; the three enjoy playing together. By Hinami's third middle-school year, Haruka remains at home and plays Atafami with her, while a third controller belongs to the sister who is no longer present.

The prose refers to `that day`, an irretrievable regret, and the death of Hinami's former true self. It does **not** state:

- what happened to Nagisa;
- whether Nagisa died, left, or became absent through another event;
- when the change occurred;
- who was responsible; or
- whether the event initiated Hinami's entire effort regime.

The earlier one-sister/two-sister discrepancy is therefore no longer a witness inconsistency. It is evidence of changed family composition between childhood and middle school, with the mechanism still deliberately withheld.

### 8.2 A supplied team goal can mobilize effort without becoming jointly owned

After taking first place on three examinations, Hinami loses interest in that field because it can no longer prove more. She turns the basketball team's existing ambition into a rehearsed collective declaration of national first place and uses persuasive leadership to raise commitment.

The team reaches second place nationally. Hinami experiences this as a decisive, identity-threatening failure; teammates are disappointed but recognize that the target was fundamentally the dream Hinami supplied. They worked for her and relied on her without acquiring the same need to win.

This is stronger evidence than a simple leader/follower contrast:

| Layer | Hinami | Teammates |
|---|---|---|
| stated goal | national first place | verbally and behaviorally adopted |
| motivational engine | proof through victory; failure is intolerable | care, team participation, and response to Hinami |
| defeat meaning | inability to erase the failed goal; exposed emptiness | regret without the same identity collapse |
| ownership result | personally owned, severely self-coupled | committed but not equivalently owned |

Commitment and exertion are therefore insufficient tests of shared goal ownership.

### 8.3 Atafami begins as warmth and becomes individually measurable proof

After the basketball loss, Haruka invites Hinami to play Atafami. Hinami loses repeatedly and is surprised when her mother and Haruka describe her as visibly enjoying herself. She continues playing, first with Haruka and then online. The game reconnects her to the atmosphere once shared among all three sisters.

Its structure also meets her proof criteria: correct effort reliably produces results; rank makes strength numerical; and no teammate limits the outcome. Hinami concludes that basketball failed partly because it was not an individual event.

Both functions matter. Atafami is neither only grief substitution nor only optimization technology. It carries familial warmth and authentic excitement while offering a field in which responsibility and proof appear individually controllable.

### 8.4 nanashi becomes an aspirational answer to isolation

Hinami's first match with nanashi is a complete defeat. Unlike the team result, the loss exhilarates her: it demonstrates that someone already occupies the height she wants to reach and may share the isolation of total effort. She declines to message him because she does not yet regard herself as worthy of equal address, studies his videos, and sets the medium goal `nanashiを超える。`

She then replaces the ordinary online name `Aoi` with `NO NAME`. The narration ties that move to a belief that her former self died and that an empty identity can be filled by victories earned alone.

This establishes a formation chain, not a total diagnosis:

`changed sister structure / unrevealed regret → team-goal isolation and uncontrollable dependence → Atafami warmth plus measurable individual competition → nanashi as proof that the summit is inhabited → NO NAME as self-authored competitive identity`

The first link's event and causality remain incomplete.

## 9. Mimimi, Kikuchi, and the ethics of continuing feeling

Unit 3 is privately focalized through Mimimi. It occurs while Tomozaki and Kikuchi are already dating, but before the V08 relationship sequence.

Mimimi recognizes that sending Tomozaki back to Kikuchi cost her the object she wanted. She continues to love him and has not internally given up. In conversation with Kikuchi, she first tests whether Tomozaki has disclosed her confession, recognizes that the testing is unfair, and tells Kikuchi directly.

Her chosen boundary is important: she intends to act honestly toward her feeling but says she will not do anything wrong. The source does not yet define the complete conduct rule, and Tomozaki is not present, but this is more than private stasis.

Kikuchi rejects the metaphor of romance as a war. She treats attraction as potentially grounded in shared aims, enjoyment, complementarity, dependence, pursuit, or jealousy, and states that the feeling is not made incorrect by its origin. She respects Mimimi's feeling without ending her own relationship or authorizing interference.

Tama later reframes reliance from the recipient's side: being leaned on can be heavy, but the point of contact can also feel warm. This does not prove every dependency reciprocal or healthy. It corrects Mimimi's assumption that needing strength from another person makes her love unreal by definition.

Epistemic boundary after Unit 3:

- Kikuchi now knows Mimimi confessed and still has feelings;
- Tama had inferred Mimimi's attraction but did not previously know the confession;
- Mimimi knows Tomozaki had not disclosed her confession to Kikuchi;
- Tomozaki does not know this conversation occurred; and
- continuing love is established, but active interference is not.

## 10. Rena's belonging substitute explains risk without excusing it

Unit 4 begins after Rena has sex with an adult game-community participant who has a girlfriend, then reconstructs the social history behind her pattern.

At seventeen, Rena occupied both a high-status appearance group and a smaller group organized around intensive niche interests. After she became physically close to a friend's recently separated former partner, conflict with that friend spread into exclusion from both groups. Rena stopped attending school and built new access through older men from the gaming circle.

Her later regime is explicit:

| Need / wound | Substitution |
|---|---|
| belonging after group exclusion | being sexually wanted by many men |
| trust in speech | belief that bodily desire is more honest than words |
| status in a peer group | selection by older, prominent, or high-follower men |
| self-confirmation | follows, requests, bodily reaction, and conquest-like access |
| boundary rule | tactical withdrawal from inconvenience; no ethical prohibition on attached targets |

At the present gathering she recognizes that the chosen man has a girlfriend and intensifies the encounter. She later uses a public hint and follow approval to provoke another interested woman. Her behavior toward Tomozaki in V08 is therefore continuous with an established validation strategy, not a uniquely reciprocal bond inferred from him.

This history does not excuse sexualizing a seventeen-year-old, bypassing an existing relationship, or making private contact visible. It helps identify the risk: attention, secrecy, and a target's attachment can all increase rather than stop her pursuit.

## 11. Alternate karaoke and the canonical New Year's seam

### 11.1 `みんなのうた` is explicitly parallel

Unit 5 depicts a year-end karaoke gathering in which Hinami assigns Tomozaki the task of singing once with every member. He observes preferences, engineers pairings, and finally uses a group song to satisfy the wording. He also realizes that singing with friends was genuinely fun.

The afterword explicitly calls this an `if` story: because the named songs reveal a publication-era date, it should be understood as what the second-year cast might sing at the time the book appeared. Its events, task, and outcomes are excluded from canonical chronology.

The story may be retained as a thematic model—assigned participation can create access to sincere enjoyment—but it cannot confirm that the specific assignment occurred or that the main-continuity group relationship advanced.

### 11.2 `炬燵の天使` ends at the V08 threshold

Unit 6 follows Kikuchi at home on December 31 and ends when she receives Tomozaki's New Year message about the planned shrine visit. This is the immediate prelude to V08 rather than a response to V08.

Kikuchi identifies the past year as a transformation she participated in making. Her marked-up play script is evidence that collective realization of her authored wish has become a valued object, not merely an imposed social exercise.

The story also reveals that Alcia was deliberately modeled on Hinami. Kikuchi wrote the `everything, therefore nothing` structure and the need for world-certified proof with Hinami in mind. Hinami's performance and later questions make Kikuchi feel that the model touched something real.

Limits are equally important:

- Kikuchi's account of Alcia being shaped by royal expectations is her character construction, not verified Hinami history;
- Hinami's questions show attention and possible recognition, not endorsement of the whole model;
- Hinami's sharp response to mention of her sisters shows guarded significance, not the missing event;
- Kikuchi says only that elementary- and middle-school witnesses referred to different sisters; and
- Hinami confirms that Tomozaki knows this discrepancy but does not explain it.

## 15. Rival readings and counterevidence
