### 4.1 First dating practice: assigned form and chosen person coexist

Tomozaki and Kikuchi's New Year outing begins as genuinely selected time. Both explicitly describe choosing the place and the person, and Tomozaki develops an outfit from learned principles plus his own emerging taste rather than copying a mannequin wholesale. His prayer—that effort return a proportionate result—also exposes a remaining control assumption: he accepts help as part of his own choice but distrusts outcomes that seem larger than effort can warrant.

Hinami's small task, holding hands by the third date, overlaps Tomozaki's desire. He awkwardly completes it and buys a matching charm with Kikuchi. The task therefore supplies timing and form without manufacturing the wanted act. More importantly, Kikuchi's shrine wish is not romantic possession. She wants her current novel to win a newcomer award. Her V07 `作家志望` identification has become a public, evaluable submission goal, and she asks Tomozaki to help her move toward it without surrendering authorship.

### 4.2 The new medium goal is assigned before its end is owned

Hinami assigns Tomozaki the medium goal of becoming the central person in a group of at least four—the nucleus of a recognizable `Tomozaki group`. The first task is to organize an outing. Tomozaki accepts the curriculum and believes he can probably manage it, which shows real confidence, but V08 never establishes that group centrality itself is something he independently wants.

The first four-person outing emerges when information gathering about careers leads Tomozaki, Mimimi, Takei, and Tama to Tama's family patisserie. The resulting `self-search alliance` with Mimimi and Takei is strongly owned at the level of purpose: Tomozaki wants diverse experience to help discover a long-term end. It also incidentally satisfies Hinami's task. The distinction matters. A useful imposed metric can overlap an owned practice without becoming the practice's ultimate reason.

### 4.3 Atafami becomes a social world rather than a sealed ranking ladder

At the Atafami gathering Tomozaki enters a field in which `nanashi`, rather than his school persona, is the obvious center. He accepts praise for appearance and sociability as evidence of practiced change, then later recognizes that he no longer finds his unstyled private self disgusting. He does not make attractiveness his value: the meaningful object is the will and action by which he changed.

Hinami initially refuses to reveal herself as NO NAME but cannot resist playing. Her secondary-character strategy compresses practice into a few perfected routes, and her mirror-match proposal to Tomozaki creates an equal contest that protects her identity while satisfying competitive desire. She loses 3–1, shows unperformed frustration, returns for another elite gathering, and eventually says plainly that it was fun. V08 therefore supplies a bounded field in which she volunteers pleasure and rivalry. It does not supply a wider life end or a disclosure about her origin.

The gatherings also reveal an ecosystem Tomozaki had ignored: local scenes, tournaments, professional players, commentary, video production, sponsorship, travel, and mixed work/hobby arrangements. His former objection to tournaments treated one-day variance as contamination from life's unfairness. He now accepts contingency because a singular high-stakes match can create a kind of investment unavailable in aggregate win rate.

### 4.5 Ashigaru's test: skill under stakes is a different evidence object

Tomozaki asks professional player Ashigaru to use tournament rules. After winning the first game and playing at least as well technically across the set, Tomozaki loses the deciding fifth game. The added embarrassment penalty is deliberately small but makes the current result matter. Under that pressure, Tomozaki first selects a high-return route to escape sustained tension and later rejects a practiced move with roughly nine-in-ten reliability because its failure would end the set. Ashigaru predicts the defensive choice and punishes it.

This is not evidence that Tomozaki lacks skill. Ashigaru judges that Tomozaki would likely win a hundred-game set. It is evidence that aggregate superiority, repeatable technical execution, and action under one consequential opportunity are different competencies. Professional play also requires public self-presentation, adaptation across patches and characters, tournament composure, and an economically credible route.

Ashigaru's rule—because the end is a dream, the path must be realistic—does not subordinate desire to prestige. It requires Tomozaki to give an owned end enough structure to survive contact with dependence, money, time, and other people's legitimate stakes.

### 4.6 Relationship maintenance fails through accumulation and partial disclosure

Kikuchi repeatedly initiates: she asks to return home together, proposes a themed café, asks to meet on short notice, raises concerns about Mimimi and Hinami, and asks whether Rena will attend an event. Tomozaki values each request, but a Tama outing, work, two Atafami gatherings, the larger group outing, and a forgotten message repeatedly displace time with her. The pattern is not infidelity by simple event count; it is a capacity-allocation failure combined with incomplete explanation.

Rena, a twenty-year-old player, uses rapid physical familiarity and sexualized conversation with the seventeen-year-old Tomozaki. He directly denies wanting sex with her and does not accept her invitation. He nevertheless experiences attraction, keeps the call secret, and tells Kikuchi that Rena is merely someone he met once. His discomfort accurately signals that the description is incomplete. Hinami's reassurance that Kikuchi's hand-holding request means there is no problem encourages closure without direct inquiry.

Kikuchi's jealousy is neither pure passivity nor fully direct conflict. She asks for information, reaches for touch, reverses a wish to return alone, and waits while Tomozaki completes group business. Yet she apologizes for asking and often substitutes reassurance-seeking gestures for naming the feared boundary. Tomozaki, in turn, treats each local acceptance or affectionate gesture as if it resolves the underlying issue. Their V07 complementarity does not automatically produce mutual interpretation.

The final crisis occurs when a notification from Rena apologizes for their sexual conversation while Kikuchi is holding Tomozaki's phone. The message does not prove an affair. It proves that the account Kikuchi received was materially incomplete and that a boundary-relevant interaction was withheld. V08 ends before explanation, response, or repair.

## 8. Rival readings and counterevidence

### 8.1 Rival: professional gaming is a sudden fantasy

The declaration is rapid, but it follows career interviews, two offline gatherings, ecosystem research, an elite tournament-format loss, and direct professional advice. The target is owned; its feasibility is expressly unresolved.

### 8.2 Rival: becoming a pro proves Tomozaki has solved goal ownership

It proves a self-authored direction, not a complete plan. His group-centrality target remains assigned, and his relationship capacity is poorly governed.

### 8.3 Rival: the Rena crisis proves Tomozaki cheated

He refuses the sexual proposition and does not meet her privately. The supported failure is attraction plus weak boundary-setting, partial disclosure, and concealment of a relevant call—not a completed affair.

### 8.4 Rival: Kikuchi is simply irrationally jealous

Her fear is amplified by indirect communication, but Tomozaki gives an incomplete account, repeatedly deprioritizes requested time, and allows an adult stranger's sexualized access to remain unnegotiated. Her phone access is itself boundary-relevant; it does not erase the informational basis of her alarm.

### 8.5 Rival: Kikuchi's hand-holding proves the issue was resolved

The gesture communicates a need for closeness. It never establishes an agreed rule about Rena, Mimimi, disclosure, or time. Treating affection as adjudication is the precise local mistake.

### 8.6 Rival: Hinami is fully authentic in Atafami spaces

Her desire is unusually visible there, but she still hides NO NAME and manages public presentation. Bounded authentic investment can coexist with strategic identity control.

### 8.7 Rival: Hinami's career plan is owned because it is detailed

Detail proves route construction. It does not answer Tomozaki's question about whether she wants the end. Prestige and competition remain the only stated selectors.

### 8.8 Rival: aggregate skill or tournament victory is the true measure

Ashigaru explicitly distinguishes them. Long-run rate estimates repeatable performance; a bracket tests execution under singular stakes. Neither invalidates the other.

### 8.9 Rival: family circumstance explains Mimimi or Hinami

V08 supplies real household facts but no causal demonstration connecting a single fact to either character's complete effort regime. The sister discrepancy remains an ethical stop, not a clue to fill with invention.

### 8.10 Rival: forming a group satisfies Hinami's whole medium goal

One outing and a three-person chat structure make future contact easier. They do not yet establish that Tomozaki is the durable center of a four-person group, much less that he owns that status as an end.
