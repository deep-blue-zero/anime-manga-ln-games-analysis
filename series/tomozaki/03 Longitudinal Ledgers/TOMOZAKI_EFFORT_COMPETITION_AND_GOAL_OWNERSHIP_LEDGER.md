# Tomozaki — effort, competition, and goal-ownership ledger

## 1. Responsibility

This ledger tracks how *Bottom-Tier Character Tomozaki* distinguishes and revises different regimes of effort across volumes.

Its responsibility is narrow:

- what goal a character is pursuing;
- who or what supplied that goal;
- whether success is defined by mastery, ranking, approval, identity, relationship, duty, or another criterion;
- what comparison target structures the effort;
- what would count as “enough” or permit stopping;
- how tightly self-worth is coupled to performance;
- what collateral costs the effort produces;
- how failure is interpreted;
- whether the effort remains owned, revised, refused, or externally compelled.

It is **not** a general character-state ledger, relationship ledger, moral scorecard, psychiatric assessment, or repository of every training scene.

The ledger is instantiated after V02 because V01 and V02 together establish a recurring analytical problem: the series does not treat “trying hard” as a single stable category. Similar-looking effort can have different motives, stopping conditions, identity consequences, and ethical implications.

## 2. Method

For each material effort regime, track these fields where evidence supports them:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| **goal** | What outcome is being pursued? |
| **goal origin** | Self-authored, jointly negotiated, assigned, socially inherited, status-driven, or unclear? |
| **comparison reference** | Self, named rival, peer group, norm, objective threshold, or none? |
| **success criterion** | Mastery, first place, relationship outcome, approval, completion, survival, etc.? |
| **stopping condition** | What would permit rest, revision, withdrawal, or completion? |
| **self-worth coupling** | How strongly is personal worth being inferred from success/failure? |
| **feedback model** | Is failure treated as data, shame, injustice, identity threat, motivation, or something else? |
| **collateral cost** | Sleep, health, relationships, honesty, autonomy, joy, time, status, etc.? |
| **ownership state** | Owned, provisional, contested, internalized, externally imposed, or unclear? |
| **revision state** | PRESERVE, STRENGTHEN, REVISE, DOWNGRADE, REJECT, OPEN |

A character can occupy multiple effort regimes simultaneously. Do not assign one permanent “work ethic” label.

## 3. V01 baseline

### 3.1 Tomozaki — Atafami mastery ethic

**Evidence state:** V01.

- **goal:** become stronger / win at Atafami / understand the game more deeply
- **goal origin:** strongly self-authored
- **comparison reference:** primarily self-improvement, with opponents as tests
- **success criterion:** demonstrable skill growth and competitive performance
- **stopping condition:** not clearly fixed; practice continues because mastery remains rewarding
- **self-worth coupling:** meaningful but not shown as total
- **feedback model:** loss/problem → isolate variables → train weakness → retest
- **collateral cost:** substantial time investment; no major V01 evidence of destructive cost
- **ownership:** strongly owned

**Interpretive state:** this regime is Tomozaki's pre-existing strength. Hinami does not create it; she leverages it.

### 3.2 Tomozaki — original “life is a kusoge” exemption

**Evidence state:** V01 opening.

- **goal:** social/life improvement is largely refused before experiment
- **goal origin:** n/a because domain is classified as irrational to invest in
- **comparison reference:** people with better starting parameters
- **success criterion:** assumed to be inaccessible due opaque/unfair rules
- **stopping condition:** effort is rejected before serious testing
- **self-worth coupling:** social inferiority contributes to self-concept but is rationalized structurally
- **feedback model:** unequal starting conditions → conclude system not worth grinding
- **ownership:** self-authored belief but empirically under-tested

**V01 revision:** **REJECT / REVISE.** Tomozaki accepts that at least some social subskills are learnable and revises life from `クソゲー` to at least `良ゲー`.

### 3.3 Hinami — disciplined optimization

**Evidence state:** V01.

- **goal:** broad excellence across school, social, appearance, athletics, and game domains
- **goal origin:** unclear
- **comparison reference:** high objective/competitive standards
- **success criterion:** strong performance, often near top-level
- **stopping condition:** unclear
- **self-worth coupling:** unclear
- **feedback model:** weakness → deliberate practice → repeated refinement
- **collateral cost:** hidden labor is extensive; V01 does not establish destructive cost
- **ownership:** appears strongly owned, origin unresolved

**V01 state:** **OPEN.** Do not equate disciplined practice with either healthy mastery or pathological perfectionism.

### 3.4 Nakamura — effort after defeat

**Evidence state:** V01.

- **goal:** improve enough to challenge Tomozaki again
- **goal origin:** self-authored after competitive humiliation
- **comparison reference:** Tomozaki
- **success criterion:** defeat or meaningfully challenge Tomozaki
- **feedback model:** loss → training rather than excuse
- **self-worth coupling:** unclear
- **collateral cost:** not established

**V01 result:** Tomozaki's respect increases because Nakamura behaves according to the effort ethic Tomozaki values.

## 4. V02: Tomozaki's regime differentiates from Hinami's

### 4.1 Tomozaki — mastery first, ranking second

**Evidence state:** V02 post-election.

Tomozaki explains that the deepest competition in Atafami is with himself.

- **goal:** improve mastery
- **goal origin:** self-authored
- **comparison reference:** self over time; opponents provide high-quality tests
- **success criterion:** becoming stronger, with winning as evidence rather than sole end
- **stopping condition:** no fixed endpoint; effort remains rewarding while growth/problem-solving continues
- **self-worth coupling:** present but not shown as dependent on first place
- **feedback model:** defeat → information
- **collateral cost:** manageable in evidence seen so far
- **ownership:** strong

**Revision:** **STRENGTHEN.**

This explains why the 456–131 election loss does not collapse his `良ゲー` revision. The loss can be metabolized as information because ranking is not the sole proof that the effort had value.

### 4.2 Tomozaki — life-training regime

**Evidence state:** V02.

- **goal:** improve social competence and increasingly participate in relationships/groups
- **goal origin:** initially Hinami-assigned, increasingly negotiated/internalized
- **comparison reference:** partly Hinami / high-skill peers, increasingly self over time
- **success criterion:** specific assignments plus growing self-authored outcomes
- **stopping condition:** still strongly shaped by Hinami's curriculum
- **self-worth coupling:** declining relative to V01 fatalism; self-concept beginning to catch competence
- **feedback model:** awkwardness/loss → analysis rather than categorical withdrawal
- **collateral cost:** anxiety, cognitive load, embarrassment; no destructive physical cost shown
- **ownership:** **REVISE upward** from provisional experiment toward partial ownership

Key evidence:

- self-directed use of Mizusawa-style methods;
- independently constructed Mimimi campaign strategy;
- voluntary reading of Michael Andy;
- willingness to create the Tama/Mimimi encounter without controlling its content.

## 5. V02: Hinami's regime becomes more distinct

### 5.1 First-place orientation

**Evidence state:** V02.

Hinami is surprised that nanashi's highest-order motivation is not simply first place.

This supports a distinction:

- **goal:** excellence / victory / top performance across many domains
- **goal origin:** unresolved
- **comparison reference:** objective competition and top rank
- **success criterion:** strongly outcome-sensitive; first-place logic appears salient
- **stopping condition:** unresolved
- **self-worth coupling:** unresolved
- **feedback model:** failure → more training / further optimization
- **collateral cost:** unknown; V02 hints that Hinami knows effort in life can create problems not solved by more effort
- **ownership:** apparently strong
- **motive:** OPEN

**Revision:** **REVISE.**

V01's “Hinami and Tomozaki share an effort ethic” remains true but is insufficient. They may share methods while differing over the meaning of achievement.

### 5.2 Competitive pleasure toward Tomozaki

Hinami explicitly enjoys Tomozaki's election tactics and wants him to improve because facing nanashi in “life” is fun.

This matters because not all of Hinami's effort is grim obligation. Competition can also be intrinsically pleasurable.

Do not reduce her regime to compulsion without later evidence.

## 6. V02: Mimimi's comparative self-worth regime

### 6.1 Goal and comparison structure

**Evidence state:** V02 election aftermath and track crisis.

- **goal:** become first / become “special”
- **goal origin:** substantially self-authored but psychologically shaped by prior exclusion and Hinami comparison
- **comparison reference:** especially Hinami
- **success criterion:** first place becomes identity evidence
- **stopping condition:** unstable / receding
- **self-worth coupling:** high
- **feedback model:** being behind → increase effort; improvement without overtaking can still feel like failure
- **collateral cost:** sleep, physical recovery, ordinary play, emotional stability, friendship feelings
- **ownership:** sincere but increasingly self-destructive
- **revision state:** **NEW / HIGH confidence**

### 6.2 Middle-school antecedent

Mimimi's earlier team experience matters because she learned that her intensity could make other people uncomfortable.

Hinami then validates precisely the intensity others had implicitly asked her to suppress.

This gives Hinami dual significance:

1. proof that Mimimi's seriousness is not inherently ridiculous;
2. proof that Mimimi is not uniquely the most serious / strongest.

The same person therefore supports and threatens Mimimi's self-concept.

### 6.3 Why “try harder” cannot resolve the problem

Mimimi's target is moving.

If Hinami works harder again, then Mimimi's improvement does not produce stable arrival.

The structural problem is:

`self-worth -> first place -> outperform Hinami -> Hinami continues improving -> threshold moves`

This is a self-reinforcing comparison loop.

### 6.4 Tomozaki's initial misread

Tomozaki hears Mimimi's desire not to quit and maps it onto his own mastery ethic.

He respects the seriousness and endorses continued effort.

**Revision to Tomozaki model:** respect for effort can become a blind spot when he assumes the other person's goal functions like his own.

## 7. Tama as a different evaluative system

Tama's intervention does not optimize Mimimi's ranking.

It supplies value from a different domain:

`みんみは、私のヒーローなんだよ？`

Mimimi is Tama's hero.

This relationship-specific value:

- cannot be taken by Hinami;
- does not require universal superiority;
- is grounded in Mimimi's history of action toward Tama;
- remains compatible with Mimimi losing races or elections.

Tama's “world's number-one idiot” joke then repurposes ranking language without preserving its universal comparison logic.

**Ledger implication:** relationships can supply identity criteria that are not reducible to public rank.

## 8. Bounded agency and effort

V02 adds a new distinction relevant to future effort analysis:

**effort to control an outcome** versus **effort to create conditions in which another person's agency can operate**.

Tomozaki cannot personally persuade Mimimi out of the crisis.

His successful contribution is to bring Tama into the situation.

This should be tracked as an important correction to optimization logic:

- not every problem is a solo skill check;
- other people have relationship-specific authority;
- recognizing one's limit can be a form of competence;
- responsible effort can be facilitative rather than controlling.

## 9. V03: Tomozaki contests the right to choose the goal

### 9.1 Assigned relationship end

Hinami defines the summer goal as Tomozaki dating Kikuchi and later assigns a confession at the fireworks.

| Field | V03 state |
|---|---|
| goal | establish a romantic relationship with Kikuchi |
| goal origin | assigned by Hinami; Tomozaki does not yet know whether he wants the end |
| comparison reference | predicted success probability and developmental efficiency |
| success criterion | confession / relationship escalation |
| stopping condition | successful completion or experience gained from failure |
| self-worth coupling | Tomozaki's later self-blame couples refusal and failed articulation to being a `弱キャラ` |
| feedback model | Hinami treats either success or failure as useful experience; Tomozaki treats lack of ownership as prior to that calculation |
| collateral cost | risk of treating Kikuchi and the relationship as a developmental instrument |
| ownership state | **CONTESTED, THEN REFUSED** |

Tomozaki has enough skill and situational support to confess. His non-completion is a choice rather than inability. This is the first decisive case in which he refuses a feasible task because the end itself is not owned.

### 9.2 The first correction overshoots

After breaking with Hinami, Tomozaki abandons styling, selected clothes, posture, intentional expression, and most life training because he temporarily equates acquired form with a mask. He also retreats into Atafami, ignores his phone, and loses much of the pleasure formerly present in play.

The mirror and sister scenes correct the overreach:

- as the character living the life, Tomozaki dislikes the neglected presentation;
- achievement produced by planned training had generated genuine pleasure and richer experience;
- ignoring Kikuchi's initiative contradicts the relational responsibility Tomozaki claims to protect;
- spontaneous or unstyled action is not automatically owned action.

### 9.3 Hybrid effort regime

Kikuchi's account distinguishes memorized content that transmits little lived image from Tomozaki's learned ability to carry a sincere image more clearly. Tomozaki then uses a practiced serious tone to express genuine gratitude and uses deliberate appearance and strategic wording to repair the Hinami relationship.

His revised regime is:

| Field | Post-V03 state |
|---|---|
| goal | keep engaging seriously with life while locating ends he can answer for |
| goal origin | self-authored, relationally informed, and open to negotiated formulation |
| comparison reference | self over time; opponent/result as tests rather than sole source of worth |
| success criterion | greater capacity plus a life experienced as meaningful and vivid |
| stopping condition | revise or refuse a subgoal when it conflicts with the owned end; global endpoint remains open |
| self-worth coupling | still significant under rupture, but no longer determined by compliance alone |
| feedback model | results matter, and felt experience is also evidence to interpret |
| collateral cost | vulnerability to withdrawal; risk that “true desire” becomes an unexamined veto |
| ownership state | **OWNED, PROVISIONAL, TESTABLE** |

This regime does not reject deliberate optimization. It places method after an answerable end.

## 10. V03: Hinami's result regime meets an ownership challenge

### 10.1 Explicit theory

Hinami argues that what a person calls a true desire can be a temporary mistaken preference and that abandoning productive action for it is weak and meaningless. She grounds confidence in accumulated effort, result, and the causal structure linking them.

| Field | V03 state |
|---|---|
| goal | optimize life and continue producing strong or top results |
| goal origin | asserted as rationally selected; deeper origin unknown |
| comparison reference | objective performance and top opponents |
| success criterion | demonstrable productive outcome; first place remains materially salient |
| stopping condition | still unknown |
| self-worth coupling | still unknown; brief hurt during rupture is insufficient to settle it |
| feedback model | result implies cause and supports confidence; assertions without proof do not |
| collateral cost | possible distance from felt desire and tendency to instrumentalize relationships; extent unresolved |
| ownership state | strongly defended, but underlying desire relation remains open |

### 10.2 Containment under Mizusawa's challenge

When Mizusawa suggests that Hinami also lives one step outside herself, she immediately produces an emotionally apt account of pressure and expected performance. Tomozaki judges that response to contain no NO NAME disclosure. The safe longitudinal finding is narrower: Hinami can absorb a direct challenge into a functional social role without exposing the interior state the challenger requested.

Do not promote Tomozaki's interpretation into proof that every proposition in her response is false or that one untouched hidden self explains all behavior.

### 10.3 Partial concession through competition

Tomozaki does not prove that stable true desire exists. He makes Hinami's inability to defeat him in Atafami the result she must explain, arguing that his deeper wish to master the game is causally relevant. Hinami calls this sophistry but concedes that she cannot prove desire nonexistent. She accepts longitudinal testing.

Competition here becomes a protocol for value negotiation: Tomozaki can make an opposing claim legible to Hinami by attaching it to an unresolved result on shared ground.

## 11. V03: Mizusawa's effortless-success regime

V03 adds a distinct effort problem that cannot be represented as low effort or failed achievement.

| Field | V03 state |
|---|---|
| goal | previously, execute advantageous social outcomes; newly, hear Hinami's genuine view and pursue a feeling he owns |
| goal origin | former goals selected by benefit calculation; confession self-authored |
| comparison reference | social specifications and predicted advantage; Tomozaki/Yuzu/Nakamura as examples of full investment |
| success criterion | former: smooth acquisition; new: genuine engagement, not guaranteed acceptance |
| stopping condition | he explicitly refuses to treat rejection as erasure; future boundary unknown |
| self-worth coupling | not clearly rank-coupled; dissatisfaction concerns lack of self-presence |
| feedback model | ordinary success feels external; rejection hurts but also produces relief and vitality |
| collateral cost | emotional pain once distance is abandoned |
| ownership state | **TRANSITION FROM INSTRUMENTAL TO OWNED** |

Mizusawa describes operating his social self like a character whose pleasure and pain do not reach the player. He admires Tomozaki and the slow Nakamura/Yuzu pair because they fully identify with what they want. His confession to Hinami fails as a relationship request but succeeds as an owned act: he feels both injury and release.

This evidence rejects any simple mapping from ease to health or from pain to failure. It does not make painful action inherently authentic.

## 12. V03: other maintained regime updates

### 12.1 Mimimi

Mimimi remains energetic, competitive, grateful, and behaviorally engaged without abandoning ambition. This provisionally preserves the V02 correction: healthier movement need not mean reduced effort. V03 does not recreate equivalent comparison pressure, so durability remains open.

### 12.2 Tama

V03 supplies no material new evidence about the non-comparative relationship anchor or Mimimi/Tama reciprocity. Preserve the V02 state without promotion.

### 12.3 Nakamura and Yuzu

Both desire connection but move more slowly than their friends' optimization plan. The group can remove an information obstacle and create opportunity; it cannot directly produce their chosen step. The small result—an agreement to go out together—remains meaningful to the group even though it misses the planned confession criterion.

### 12.4 Tomozaki's intervention regime remains ethically mixed

Tomozaki identifies a bounded endpoint in the Nakamura/Yuzu operation: final action must belong to Nakamura. Yet his method deceives Takei, obscures peer involvement, and manipulates the social field. Goal ownership and bounded agency must therefore be tracked at both the **end** and **means** levels.

## 13. V04: negotiated effort and the difference between veto and authorship

### 13.1 The revised training contract becomes operational

Hinami asks whether Tomozaki accepts the next relationship target before assigning its detailed route. Tomozaki checks that it will not require an insincere confession or speech and then accepts it. This is a real change from V03's unilateral fireworks assignment.

The goal-origin field nevertheless remains mixed:

| Layer | V04 origin |
|---|---|
| long-term life engagement and enjoyment | Tomozaki-authored |
| girlfriend-before-third-year hierarchy | Hinami-originated and retained |
| mutual secret-sharing target | Hinami-proposed, Tomozaki-approved and constrained |
| Konno-group mobilization exercise | Hinami-assigned at outcome level |
| execution route | Tomozaki-authored with recruited assistance |

**Ledger revision:** late veto has become prospective consent and constraint, but curriculum authorship is not yet equal. Classify the V04 regime as **NEGOTIATED ACCEPTANCE WITH UNEQUAL GENERATIVE AUTHORITY**.

### 13.2 The hybrid survives ordinary use

Tomozaki repeatedly uses practiced tone, expression, observation, and teasing for purposes he endorses. Some moves become automatic. This confirms that method ownership can follow acquisition and that deliberate form can transmit sincere feeling.

The regime is still context-sensitive: Konno's direct pressure disrupts execution, and a newly rehearsed basketball move fails under match pressure. “Owned” does not mean universally available or fully proceduralized.

## 14. V04: Yuzu separates burden from submission

Yuzu first refuses the tournament captaincy because accepting Konno's pressure would repeat automatic accommodation. Later she volunteers for the same burden to protect Hirabayashi.

| Field | V04 Yuzu state |
|---|---|
| goal | help Nakamura; later help Hirabayashi and enable shared tournament enjoyment |
| goal origin | self-authored, relationally motivated |
| comparison reference | none as primary driver |
| success criterion | the other person receives meaningful help; she can endorse her own action |
| stopping condition | recipient outcome and her own judgment, not Konno's approval |
| self-worth coupling | not clearly rank-coupled |
| feedback model | uncertain return does not erase the reason to prepare |
| collateral cost | intense study, anxiety, risk of conflict with Konno |
| ownership state | **OWNED AND RELATIONAL** |

**Ledger revision:** acceptance/refusal is not a reliable proxy for agency. The governing reason determines whether a burden is compelled or owned.

Yuzu's mathematics effort also shows that owned desire can increase persistence without removing dependence on instruction and group support. Desire supplies priority and tolerance for uncertain return; it does not substitute for skill.

## 15. V04: desire, route discovery, and Hinami's rebuttal

### 15.1 Tomozaki's first proof remains inside rationalism

Yuzu's concern reaches Nakamura before the planned parental intervention changes the Atafami ban. Tomozaki argues that her desire found a route Hinami's rational plan missed.

Hinami's reply is decisive for the ledger. If desire is good because it produces the shortest route, then value is still being assigned by efficiency. A sufficiently accurate goal model can include emotion and reproduce the route. Tomozaki has shown **route discovery from a differently selected priority**, not desire's independent normative value.

### 15.2 Hinami can calculate emotion

V04 rejects a simple “reason versus feeling” model. Hinami can use affection, pride, worry, and likely reactions as inputs. Her effort regime does not exclude emotion; it subordinates emotion to an already specified result.

### 15.3 Hinami's own target selection is not affect-free

Hinami admits that her wish to defeat the belief that Atafami makes students unintelligent distracted her from the narrower return-to-school goal. Later she insists that Tama must not adapt because Tama is right, even though this contradicts her usual field-adaptation doctrine.

These cases provide two distinct affective redirects:

- attachment to a game changes problem specification;
- attachment to Tama creates a protected exception to ordinary optimization.

They strengthen evidence that Hinami has non-ranked preferences. They do not establish her deepest motive, self-worth coupling, effort origin, or stopping condition.

## 16. V04: owned ends are not sufficient for bounded effort

Tomozaki's tournament plan pursues an end he and Yuzu sincerely endorse. It mobilizes three inferred Konno motives through other actors, including status threat and a strategically planted claim about Nakamura's preference.

| Agency layer | V04 result |
|---|---|
| planner owns end | yes |
| recruited allies understand assigned action | substantially yes |
| intervention target chooses motivational frame | no |
| target can veto information environment | no |
| immediate outcome is beneficial | yes, at the tournament |
| delayed effects are governed | no; hostility is displaced after Yuzu/Nakamura pair |

**Ledger revision:** EFFORT-H09 strengthens. Goal ownership is necessary for Tomozaki's self-authorship but insufficient for ethical boundedness. Means, target consent, third-party exposure, and delayed system effects require separate tracking.

The Nakamura intervention supplies positive counterevidence. The group prepares without execution, Yuzu seeks permission, and Nakamura retains the ability to refuse. Tomozaki can perform a bounded check; he does not yet apply it consistently.

## 17. V04: Tama and Mimimi under social pressure

### 17.1 Tama's evaluative system is retested

Tama is harmed and increasingly isolated but does not infer that group disapproval makes Konno right or herself worthless.

| Field | V04 Tama state |
|---|---|
| goal | oppose conduct she judges wrong; later change fighting method to reduce Mimimi's suffering |
| goal origin | self-authored, then relationally expanded |
| comparison reference | internal judgment and relationship, not public rank |
| success criterion | remain answerable to her judgment while protecting Mimimi |
| stopping condition | unresolved at cliffhanger |
| self-worth coupling | low coupling to class approval in direct report |
| feedback model | persecution is pain, not proof she is wrong |
| collateral cost | vigilance, property damage, social isolation, narrowed ordinary speech |
| ownership state | **STRONGLY OWNED; METHOD REVISION REQUESTED** |

Tama's request to learn Tomozaki's methods is not surrender. She chooses self-modification for a relationship-specific end. This strengthens the distinction between authentic change and externally governed assimilation.

### 17.2 Mimimi's performed care

Mimimi deliberately produces ordinary brightness to support Tama, skips practice, and asks whether the performance is convincing. The effort is both strategic and sincere.

Her V02 first-place/self-worth loop is not directly reactivated. A different risk appears: self-evaluation becomes tied to whether she can successfully sustain someone else. Track caregiver burden separately from comparative ambition.

## 19. Longitudinal hypotheses at the V04 boundary

### EFFORT-H01 — Similar intensity can hide different value structures

**State:** HIGH confidence through V04 / STRENGTHEN.

Yuzu's uncertain-return care, Hinami's result optimization, Tama's value defense, Mimimi's performed support, and Tomozaki's mastery practice can all look like intense effort while serving different ends.

### EFFORT-H02 — Tomozaki's correction is not “don't try” but “ask what the effort is serving”

**State:** HIGH confidence / CONFIRM.

The hybrid survives ordinary life. V04 adds a necessary correction: after identifying the end, ask whose end it is and what the chosen means expose others to.

### EFFORT-H03 — Hinami's motive is the key unresolved variable

**State:** OPEN / HIGH priority, with stronger constraints.

V04 shows gamer attachment and a protected Tama exception, while leaving origin, self-worth coupling, stopping condition, and broader motive unresolved. Hinami's regime cannot be reduced to affect-free calculation.

### EFFORT-H04 — Comparative self-worth is unstable when the comparison target is autonomous

**State:** HIGH confidence as a V02 mechanism / PRESERVE.

V04 does not recreate the relevant Mimimi/Hinami ranking crisis.

### EFFORT-H05 — Non-comparative relationship value can stabilize identity without erasing ambition

**State:** MEDIUM-HIGH / STRENGTHEN.

Tama's class-independent judgment survives pressure, and Mimimi's value to Tama remains intact. The relationship now motivates Tama to revise method rather than abandon self.

### EFFORT-H06 — Goal ownership and method ownership are separate variables

**State:** HIGH / STRONGLY STRENGTHEN.

Tomozaki's practiced forms become sincere equipment; Yuzu can own an externally requested action for her own reason; Tama requests a method change without accepting the class's judgment.

### EFFORT-H07 — Personally inhabited failure can carry more value than detached success

**State:** MEDIUM-HIGH through Mizusawa / PRESERVE.

Mizusawa is not retested. Tomozaki's sports failure becomes affiliation, but it is lower-stakes and should not substitute for the V03 case.

### EFFORT-H08 — Competition can carry mutual recognition and value negotiation

**State:** MEDIUM-HIGH / PRESERVE.

Hinami seriously tests Tomozaki's proof rather than dismissing it, but she does not yet revise her value model. Reciprocity remains inquiry, not agreement.

### EFFORT-H09 — Respecting final choice does not by itself make an intervention non-controlling

**State:** HIGH / STRONGLY STRENGTHEN.

The Konno plan changes motives without her participation and succeeds locally while failing to govern delayed effects. Nakamura's permission shows the bounded alternative is available.

### EFFORT-H10 — The meaning of adaptation depends on authority over its reason

**State:** HIGH through Yuzu and Tama / NEW.

Refusal can protect agency, but accepting a burden or changing one's social method can also be authentic when the actor owns the relational end and can revise the means.

### EFFORT-H11 — Emotion can alter goal specification, not merely supply energy toward a fixed goal

**State:** MEDIUM-HIGH / NEW.

Yuzu identifies helping as the relevant end; Hinami's Atafami attachment selects a broader target; Hinami's attachment to Tama creates a non-negotiable constraint. A model that treats emotion only as fuel misses target selection.

The distinction matters outside Hinami. Tomozaki also retains social support and learned competence after losing the set, but temporarily stops school and Atafami because his unique-savior story fails. Neither case can be reconstructed as simple loss of skill.

## 72. V11: retrospective meaning and antecedent control are different effort regimes

Yoko's family system gives events meaning after they occur. A quarrel becomes preparation for a later relationship; pain makes a sweet shared object more valuable; adversity becomes a necessary trial. This structure can reduce despair and make continued action possible. It can also leave the causal situation unchanged and convert preventable harm into a reassuring story.

Hinami's later regime reverses the temporal order:

| Dimension | Inherited retrospective meaning | Hinami's antecedent-control regime |
|---|---|---|
| where value enters | after the result, through authoritative interpretation | before the result, through controllable cause and effort |
| trusted source | beloved external authority and an implied world order | the actor's own preparation, measurable outcome, and reproducibility |
| strength | can preserve hope under uncontrollable events | produces agency, learning, and answerable causal models |
| failure mode | rationalizes harm and displaces concrete intervention | overclaims control, converts luck into fault, and makes loss self-condemnation |
| V11 breaking case | Nagisa's death cannot be made acceptable by later meaning | Nagisa's death cannot be fully owned or learned from because intent and causation remain underdetermined |

The two regimes are not simple optimism and realism. Each is a method for turning events into reasons. Each becomes destructive when treated as exhaustive.

## 73. V11: admiration creates real reasons in both directions

V11 supplies two complementary movements.

### Model to admirer

Haruka says Hinami's promised example gave her a route when she did not know how to act. She follows that route through actual election work and becomes a student-council president. A model can therefore produce owned effort even when the initial standard comes from another person.

### Admirer to model

Ashigaru says a professional can continue because a fan believes in them. Tomozaki experiences this when a younger player interprets his fourth-place finish as the beginning of a compelling competitive path. Recognition can supply new reason after an outcome that the actor first treats as inadequate.

| Risk | V11 evidence |

| model becomes obligated to preserve a persona | Hinami apologizes to Haruka for appearing uncool while already unable to sustain the role |

Recognition is therefore neither intrinsically fake nor sufficient. It is a relational source of reason that must remain compatible with revision, refusal, and the model's personhood.

## 74. V11: self-responsibility is a value-acquisition and punishment rule

Hinami's maximal self-responsibility allows her to treat successful results as value she personally caused rather than luck she merely received. V11 shows the inverse: if control is made total, loss must also become personal fault.

| Operation | Product | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| attribute achieved result to preparation | result can be counted as personal competence/value | randomness and assistance become undercounted |
| treat failure as controllable | specific corrective action becomes possible | uncontrollable harm becomes personal indictment |
| demand reproducibility | method can be tested across persons and domains | another person's life can become proof material |
| reject after-the-fact consolation | real causal intervention remains visible | grief may become unusable when the complete cause cannot be known |

Tomozaki's V11 response partly recognizes the punishment side but overcorrects into unconditional guarantee. `Believe me because you cannot beat me` replaces one external warrant with another rather than giving Hinami an owned end. Hinami's Boxman victory falsifies that credential. The result does not prove that relational trust is impossible; it proves this asymmetric proof cannot bear the proposed authority.

Tomozaki gives an initial reason—use the story to affirm Hinami—and Kikuchi agrees to begin while seeking a reason of her own. This is a provisional borrowed reason, explicitly recognized as such, rather than settled goal ownership.

Mimimi's improvised commentary produces attention and shows a new audience-facing competence; she does not select it as a vocation. Mizusawa's V10 negotiation possibility receives no sustained V11 test. A successful performance remains evidence of possible route, not ownership of that route.
