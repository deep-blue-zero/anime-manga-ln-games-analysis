# Tomozaki — effort, competition, and goal-ownership ledger

## 1. Responsibility

This ledger tracks how *Bottom-Tier Character Tomozaki* distinguishes and revises different regimes of effort across volumes.

Its responsibility is narrow:

- what goal a character is pursuing;
- who or what supplied that goal;
- whether success is defined by mastery, ranking, approval, identity, relationship, duty, or another criterion;
- what comparison target structures the effort;
- what would count as “enough” or permit stopping;
- how tightly self-worth is coupled to performance;
- what collateral costs the effort produces;
- how failure is interpreted;
- whether the effort remains owned, revised, refused, or externally compelled.

It is **not** a general character-state ledger, relationship ledger, moral scorecard, psychiatric assessment, or repository of every training scene.

The ledger is instantiated after V02 because V01 and V02 together establish a recurring analytical problem: the series does not treat “trying hard” as a single stable category. Similar-looking effort can have different motives, stopping conditions, identity consequences, and ethical implications.

## 2. Method

For each material effort regime, track these fields where evidence supports them:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| **goal** | What outcome is being pursued? |
| **goal origin** | Self-authored, jointly negotiated, assigned, socially inherited, status-driven, or unclear? |
| **comparison reference** | Self, named rival, peer group, norm, objective threshold, or none? |
| **success criterion** | Mastery, first place, relationship outcome, approval, completion, survival, etc.? |
| **stopping condition** | What would permit rest, revision, withdrawal, or completion? |
| **self-worth coupling** | How strongly is personal worth being inferred from success/failure? |
| **feedback model** | Is failure treated as data, shame, injustice, identity threat, motivation, or something else? |
| **collateral cost** | Sleep, health, relationships, honesty, autonomy, joy, time, status, etc.? |
| **ownership state** | Owned, provisional, contested, internalized, externally imposed, or unclear? |
| **revision state** | PRESERVE, STRENGTHEN, REVISE, DOWNGRADE, REJECT, OPEN |

A character can occupy multiple effort regimes simultaneously. Do not assign one permanent “work ethic” label.

## 3. V01 baseline

### 3.1 Tomozaki — Atafami mastery ethic

**Evidence state:** V01.

- **goal:** become stronger / win at Atafami / understand the game more deeply
- **goal origin:** strongly self-authored
- **comparison reference:** primarily self-improvement, with opponents as tests
- **success criterion:** demonstrable skill growth and competitive performance
- **stopping condition:** not clearly fixed; practice continues because mastery remains rewarding
- **self-worth coupling:** meaningful but not shown as total
- **feedback model:** loss/problem → isolate variables → train weakness → retest
- **collateral cost:** substantial time investment; no major V01 evidence of destructive cost
- **ownership:** strongly owned

**Interpretive state:** this regime is Tomozaki's pre-existing strength. Hinami does not create it; she leverages it.

### 3.2 Tomozaki — original “life is a kusoge” exemption

**Evidence state:** V01 opening.

- **goal:** social/life improvement is largely refused before experiment
- **goal origin:** n/a because domain is classified as irrational to invest in
- **comparison reference:** people with better starting parameters
- **success criterion:** assumed to be inaccessible due opaque/unfair rules
- **stopping condition:** effort is rejected before serious testing
- **self-worth coupling:** social inferiority contributes to self-concept but is rationalized structurally
- **feedback model:** unequal starting conditions → conclude system not worth grinding
- **ownership:** self-authored belief but empirically under-tested

**V01 revision:** **REJECT / REVISE.** Tomozaki accepts that at least some social subskills are learnable and revises life from `クソゲー` to at least `良ゲー`.

### 3.3 Hinami — disciplined optimization

**Evidence state:** V01.

- **goal:** broad excellence across school, social, appearance, athletics, and game domains
- **goal origin:** unclear
- **comparison reference:** high objective/competitive standards
- **success criterion:** strong performance, often near top-level
- **stopping condition:** unclear
- **self-worth coupling:** unclear
- **feedback model:** weakness → deliberate practice → repeated refinement
- **collateral cost:** hidden labor is extensive; V01 does not establish destructive cost
- **ownership:** appears strongly owned, origin unresolved

**V01 state:** **OPEN.** Do not equate disciplined practice with either healthy mastery or pathological perfectionism.

### 3.4 Nakamura — effort after defeat

**Evidence state:** V01.

- **goal:** improve enough to challenge Tomozaki again
- **goal origin:** self-authored after competitive humiliation
- **comparison reference:** Tomozaki
- **success criterion:** defeat or meaningfully challenge Tomozaki
- **feedback model:** loss → training rather than excuse
- **self-worth coupling:** unclear
- **collateral cost:** not established

**V01 result:** Tomozaki's respect increases because Nakamura behaves according to the effort ethic Tomozaki values.

## 4. V02: Tomozaki's regime differentiates from Hinami's

### 4.1 Tomozaki — mastery first, ranking second

**Evidence state:** V02 post-election.

Tomozaki explains that the deepest competition in Atafami is with himself.

- **goal:** improve mastery
- **goal origin:** self-authored
- **comparison reference:** self over time; opponents provide high-quality tests
- **success criterion:** becoming stronger, with winning as evidence rather than sole end
- **stopping condition:** no fixed endpoint; effort remains rewarding while growth/problem-solving continues
- **self-worth coupling:** present but not shown as dependent on first place
- **feedback model:** defeat → information
- **collateral cost:** manageable in evidence seen so far
- **ownership:** strong

**Revision:** **STRENGTHEN.**

This explains why the 456–131 election loss does not collapse his `良ゲー` revision. The loss can be metabolized as information because ranking is not the sole proof that the effort had value.

### 4.2 Tomozaki — life-training regime

**Evidence state:** V02.

- **goal:** improve social competence and increasingly participate in relationships/groups
- **goal origin:** initially Hinami-assigned, increasingly negotiated/internalized
- **comparison reference:** partly Hinami / high-skill peers, increasingly self over time
- **success criterion:** specific assignments plus growing self-authored outcomes
- **stopping condition:** still strongly shaped by Hinami's curriculum
- **self-worth coupling:** declining relative to V01 fatalism; self-concept beginning to catch competence
- **feedback model:** awkwardness/loss → analysis rather than categorical withdrawal
- **collateral cost:** anxiety, cognitive load, embarrassment; no destructive physical cost shown
- **ownership:** **REVISE upward** from provisional experiment toward partial ownership

Key evidence:

- self-directed use of Mizusawa-style methods;
- independently constructed Mimimi campaign strategy;
- voluntary reading of Michael Andy;
- willingness to create the Tama/Mimimi encounter without controlling its content.

## 5. V02: Hinami's regime becomes more distinct

### 5.1 First-place orientation

**Evidence state:** V02.

Hinami is surprised that nanashi's highest-order motivation is not simply first place.

This supports a distinction:

- **goal:** excellence / victory / top performance across many domains
- **goal origin:** unresolved
- **comparison reference:** objective competition and top rank
- **success criterion:** strongly outcome-sensitive; first-place logic appears salient
- **stopping condition:** unresolved
- **self-worth coupling:** unresolved
- **feedback model:** failure → more training / further optimization
- **collateral cost:** unknown; V02 hints that Hinami knows effort in life can create problems not solved by more effort
- **ownership:** apparently strong
- **motive:** OPEN

**Revision:** **REVISE.**

V01's “Hinami and Tomozaki share an effort ethic” remains true but is insufficient. They may share methods while differing over the meaning of achievement.

### 5.2 Competitive pleasure toward Tomozaki

Hinami explicitly enjoys Tomozaki's election tactics and wants him to improve because facing nanashi in “life” is fun.

This matters because not all of Hinami's effort is grim obligation. Competition can also be intrinsically pleasurable.

Do not reduce her regime to compulsion without later evidence.

## 6. V02: Mimimi's comparative self-worth regime

### 6.1 Goal and comparison structure

**Evidence state:** V02 election aftermath and track crisis.

- **goal:** become first / become “special”
- **goal origin:** substantially self-authored but psychologically shaped by prior exclusion and Hinami comparison
- **comparison reference:** especially Hinami
- **success criterion:** first place becomes identity evidence
- **stopping condition:** unstable / receding
- **self-worth coupling:** high
- **feedback model:** being behind → increase effort; improvement without overtaking can still feel like failure
- **collateral cost:** sleep, physical recovery, ordinary play, emotional stability, friendship feelings
- **ownership:** sincere but increasingly self-destructive
- **revision state:** **NEW / HIGH confidence**

### 6.2 Middle-school antecedent

Mimimi's earlier team experience matters because she learned that her intensity could make other people uncomfortable.

Hinami then validates precisely the intensity others had implicitly asked her to suppress.

This gives Hinami dual significance:

1. proof that Mimimi's seriousness is not inherently ridiculous;
2. proof that Mimimi is not uniquely the most serious / strongest.

The same person therefore supports and threatens Mimimi's self-concept.

### 6.3 Why “try harder” cannot resolve the problem

Mimimi's target is moving.

If Hinami works harder again, then Mimimi's improvement does not produce stable arrival.

The structural problem is:

`self-worth -> first place -> outperform Hinami -> Hinami continues improving -> threshold moves`

This is a self-reinforcing comparison loop.

### 6.4 Tomozaki's initial misread

Tomozaki hears Mimimi's desire not to quit and maps it onto his own mastery ethic.

He respects the seriousness and endorses continued effort.

**Revision to Tomozaki model:** respect for effort can become a blind spot when he assumes the other person's goal functions like his own.

## 7. Tama as a different evaluative system

Tama's intervention does not optimize Mimimi's ranking.

It supplies value from a different domain:

`みんみは、私のヒーローなんだよ？`

Mimimi is Tama's hero.

This relationship-specific value:

- cannot be taken by Hinami;
- does not require universal superiority;
- is grounded in Mimimi's history of action toward Tama;
- remains compatible with Mimimi losing races or elections.

Tama's “world's number-one idiot” joke then repurposes ranking language without preserving its universal comparison logic.

**Ledger implication:** relationships can supply identity criteria that are not reducible to public rank.

## 8. Bounded agency and effort

V02 adds a new distinction relevant to future effort analysis:

**effort to control an outcome** versus **effort to create conditions in which another person's agency can operate**.

Tomozaki cannot personally persuade Mimimi out of the crisis.

His successful contribution is to bring Tama into the situation.

This should be tracked as an important correction to optimization logic:

- not every problem is a solo skill check;
- other people have relationship-specific authority;
- recognizing one's limit can be a form of competence;
- responsible effort can be facilitative rather than controlling.

## 10. Longitudinal hypotheses after V02

### EFFORT-H01 — Similar intensity can hide different value structures

**State:** HIGH confidence through V02.

Tomozaki, Hinami, and Mimimi can all look “competitive” or “hardworking” while using effort for different identity functions.

### EFFORT-H02 — Tomozaki's long-term correction is not from “don't try” but from “ask what the effort is serving”

**State:** MEDIUM-HIGH confidence.

V02 corrects his initial overapplication without rejecting mastery.

### EFFORT-H03 — Hinami's motive is the key unresolved variable

**State:** OPEN / HIGH priority.

Do not infer a pathology, trauma, ideology, or family explanation without later source evidence.

### EFFORT-H04 — Comparative self-worth is unstable when the comparison target is autonomous

**State:** HIGH confidence as a V02 mechanism.

Hinami's independent effort means Mimimi cannot set the threshold she is using to prove herself special.

### EFFORT-H05 — Non-comparative relationship value can stabilize identity without erasing ambition

**State:** MEDIUM confidence.

Tama's intervention works in V02, but durability is untested.

## 11. V03 retrieval questions

When V03 is read, update this ledger only if evidence bears on:

1. whether Tomozaki retains the distinction between serious effort and destructive persistence;
2. whether he asks who owns the goal before optimizing it;
3. whether Hinami's perfectionism motive or stopping condition becomes clearer;
4. whether Mimimi remains ambitious without returning to identity collapse;
5. whether Tama's relational reframing remains durable;
6. whether competition between Tomozaki and Hinami becomes more value-conflicted;
7. whether another character supplies a different effort regime that requires expanding the matrix.

## 12. Abstentions

At the V02 boundary this ledger does **not** claim:

- Hinami has a disorder or trauma;
- Mimimi has a disorder;
- extreme effort is inherently unhealthy;
- first-place goals are inherently unhealthy;
- self-improvement is inherently healthy;
- Tomozaki's mastery ethic is universally superior;
- Tama's relational valuation is always healthier than competition;
- competition and friendship are mutually exclusive;
- ambition must be reduced for recovery;
- any character's V03+ outcome.

The ledger tracks source-grounded structures, not diagnoses or universal prescriptions.
