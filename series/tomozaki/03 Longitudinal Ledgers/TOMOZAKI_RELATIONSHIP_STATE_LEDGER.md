# Bottom-Tier Character Tomozaki — relationship-state ledger through Volume 11

## 1. Responsibility, authority, and boundary

This is the canonical cumulative home for **directional relationship and network state** in the admitted Tomozaki Japanese-light-novel corpus. It tracks what one actor wants from another, what each knows, what each has disclosed, where trust and leverage sit, how care and conflict alter the relation, whether refusal is usable, what ordinary presence sustains, which third parties change the field, and who actually has authority to choose an end.

The ledger is longitudinal rather than terminal. A relation that is intimate at V11 was not therefore intimate at V01; a later disclosure does not become earlier actor knowledge; a repaired conflict does not become proof that a standing rule existed before the repair; and a current crisis does not erase prior reciprocity. V11 is the present source boundary, not a timeless endpoint and not the series ending.

### 1.1 Evidence authority

The authority order used here is:

1. the audited Japanese EPUB witnesses identified by TOMOZAKI_SOURCE_LOCK_AND_INVENTORY.md;
2. the preserved prospective V01–V11 deep readings and the story-local V06.5/V08.5 readings;
3. this cumulative ledger, which reconciles those frozen states without rewriting them;
4. current character monographs and the full-series synthesis only as provisional retrieval aids.

No primary EPUB needed to be reopened for this first backfill. Every promoted relationship claim below is already represented in a source-facing reading. Where those readings do not preserve enough Japanese wording, address, or local register evidence, this ledger marks the gap instead of reconstructing it from memory.

### 1.2 Continuity control

The main progression is V01 → V02 → V03 → V04 → V05 → V06 → V07 → V08 → V09 → V10 → V11.

V06.5 and V08.5 are not inserted as homogeneous bridges:

- V06.5 evidence is routed unit by unit according to its own chronology. Only its final Mimimi unit is directly established as post-V06 progression; other units are retrospective, concurrent, or interstitial.

No V12 event, adaptation, translation, retailer bonus outside the lock, interview, or fandom account enters this ledger.

### 1.3 Bounded responsibility

This document is not:

- a shipping ranking or prediction of the final romantic pairing;
- a list of every conversation or a plot recap;
- a character monograph that assigns permanent traits;
- a claim that affection, insight, persistence, sacrifice, or beneficial outcome confers consent;

## 2. Directional state schema

### 2.1 State tuple and evidence language

Every material entry should be recoverable as:

**〈source position, diegetic position, continuity layer, observer or focalizer, actor knowledge, reader knowledge, evidence class, revision state〉**.

Evidence classes used here:

| Class | Use |
|---|---|
| **DIRECT** | An actor speaks, acts, refuses, discloses, or receives an observable result in the admitted source. |
| **ATTRIBUTED** | A source character reports another person's act or statement; the attribution is direct, but the reported event is not independently observed in that scene. |
| **CORROBORATED INFERENCE** | Multiple observations support a bounded model beyond any one explicit statement. |
| **HYPOTHESIS** | A plausible interpretation remains underdetermined or awaits a discriminating test. |
| **VALUE JUDGMENT** | An ethical assessment is stated as assessment rather than disguised as plot fact. |
| **NEGATIVE EVIDENCE** | A source boundary supplies no required disclosure, repair, rule, response, or result. Absence is used only where the missing item is itself material. |
| **OPEN** | Evidence is insufficient to adjudicate the question. |

Revision dispositions are **PRESERVE, STRENGTHEN, REVISE, DOWNGRADE, REJECT,** and **OPEN**. “No change” is recorded only when continuity or non-resolution matters.

### 2.2 Required fields for A→B and B→A

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| **desired relation / end** | What relation, result, or degree of access does A seek from B? Is it named, inferred, mixed, or unavailable? |
| **standing** | Are they strangers, assigned partners, student/teacher, rivals, friends, romantic partners, collaborators, family, or members of a group? Several standings may coexist. |
| **knowledge** | What does A know about B, and what only the reader or a third party knows? |
| **disclosure** | What does A voluntarily tell B? What is concealed, selectively framed, inferred, or passed onward? |
| **trust / reliance** | What does A entrust to B, and in what domain? Trust is domain-specific rather than one global score. |
| **leverage** | What skill, status, information, access, institutional role, affection, or dependency lets A shape B's options? |
| **care / obligation** | What effort, protection, maintenance, recognition, or debt does A direct toward B? Does care become a claim of jurisdiction? |
| **boundary / refusal** | What limit is stated or enacted? Is refusal practically available, heard, narrowed, bypassed, or punished? |
| **conflict / repair** | What was injured, who names it, what changes, and what remains without a durable rule? |
| **ordinary presence** | What low-stakes time, routine, play, work, food, travel, messaging, or silence sustains the relation outside crisis? |
| **third-party effect** | Who changes the dyad's information, cost, legitimacy, alternatives, or public meaning? |
| **consent / authority** | Who may decide the immediate act, disclosure, relationship end, intervention, or use of personal material? |

### 2.3 Non-equivalences

The following distinctions control every later section:

- reciprocal does not mean symmetrical;
- chosen participation does not mean equal bargaining power;
- affection does not mean romance;
- romance does not mean unlimited access;
- apology does not mean durable repair;
- insight does not mean informational authority;
- willingness to accept consequences does not mean permission;
- collective agreement does not create the absent person's consent;
- an indirect refusal is not identical to a direct no, but neither is it an invitation to indefinite persistence;
- one accepted request does not create standing permission for later requests;
- a relationship may depend on learned or assigned form and still become genuinely owned; and
- an actor may care sincerely while selecting an ethically defective method.

## 5. Tomozaki Fumiya ↔ Hinami Aoi

### 5.1 Phase history

#### Phase A — reciprocal gamer recognition inside asymmetric coaching (V01–V02)

Before either knows the other's offline identity, nanashi and NO NAME already share a serious competitive language. Their first in-person relation reverses status by domain: Tomozaki is the superior Atafami player, while Hinami controls social knowledge, class status, the training curriculum, and most success criteria. Tomozaki's initial consent is real but experimental. He accepts a test of learnability, not Hinami's whole philosophy or permanent jurisdiction over his life.

Hinami's V01 conduct includes insult and pressure, but it also includes large time expenditure, close calibration, strategic protection, and eventual acknowledgment that she is a guide rather than the final chooser. V02 then makes competitive enjoyment direct: she misses playing nanashi while he plans Mimimi's campaign against her, enjoys his framing of that campaign as their match, and calls the surprise fun. Her investment can no longer be explained solely as altruism or project ownership.

At the same time, V02 discovers a value divergence. Tomozaki treats mastery and improvement as primary and rank as a test; Hinami appears to treat first place as a more terminal warrant. Mutual respect therefore creates the possibility of conflict rather than ideological sameness. The relation is reciprocal in attention and challenge, not equal in social authority.

#### Phase B — end-authority rupture and negotiated resumption (V03–V04)

V03 moves the disagreement from method to relationship ends. Hinami can preserve local permissions while still assigning the girlfriend target and planned confession. Tomozaki reaches the point where he **can** complete the task and chooses not to, because neither his own feeling nor the proposed relation has been adequately selected. His station argument fails partly because he cannot yet articulate an alternative framework, but the refusal itself is effective.

The rupture causes real withdrawal: Tomozaki abandons training and ordinary communication for roughly two weeks. This cost is evidence of attachment and dependence; it does not establish romance. Repair occurs when he both credits Hinami's contribution and translates his objection into shared experimental and competitive terms. Hinami accepts only a falsifiable hybrid: she will no longer assume stable desire absent, and he may refuse ends he cannot own. The bag/pin exchange expresses gratitude without restoring simple dependence.

V04 confirms that the veto changes procedure. Hinami asks whether Tomozaki accepts a proposed target, and he limits it before agreeing. Yet she still originates most targets and evaluates performance. The accurate state is negotiated acceptance inside an unequal curriculum. Their inquiry becomes more reciprocal—Tomozaki can test Hinami's model of desire, and Hinami's own attachments complicate it—but she does not become his pupil or surrender control of her self-account.

#### Phase C — ethical checking, protected exceptions, and explicit motive ownership (V05–V06)

The Tama crisis lets Tomozaki act as a different kind of teacher while Hinami becomes both collaborator and counterexample. Hinami acknowledges that Tama wants to change, that Tomozaki wants to help, and that she has no right to stop them. This is a meaningful recognition of another dyad's authority. Yet her protection of Tama culminates in a retaliatory action after Konno is already defeated. Tomozaki attempts to stop the continuation; Hinami embeds it in the closure anyway.

V06 turns the disagreement into direct ethical judgment. Tomozaki names the last strike as “死体蹴り,” while Hinami calls it revenge and explains that she loves Tama's correct, uncalculated constancy. The exchange increases disclosure and moral frankness. It does not produce apology, restitution, or doctrinal repair. Tomozaki reaches Hinami partly by invoking NO NAME's identity, which remains a relationship-specific language rather than a complete public ethic.

Their Atafami play concurrently becomes more reciprocal. Hinami's unrequired “私の勝ち” after a hard-won individual game seeks recognition from Tomozaki. The game supports pleasure and mutual estimation. It also begins to function as leverage in arguments whose proper authority lies outside the game.

#### Phase D — owned romantic choice and inquiry without full access (V07–V08)

V07 sets a successful boundary: Tomozaki accepts exploration but refuses confession as an assigned task, and Hinami accepts that limit. When he chooses Kikuchi, Hinami congratulates him and remains personally engaged. Her role in producing opportunity remains causal without owning the terminal relationship.

The same volume makes Hinami an object of inquiry. Tomozaki and collaborators investigate her earlier presentation and motive, then stop when sister information becomes intimate and ambiguous. That stopping point matters: permission to ask some questions does not imply unlimited access. Their bond gains parity because Tomozaki can set questions and invite Hinami somewhere of his choosing; her central desire remains undisclosed.

V08 supplies Tomozaki's clearest gift of access. He brings Hinami into his Atafami community, where nanashi rather than the school heroine is socially central. Hinami initially protects NO NAME's identity but cannot resist participation, shows frustration, returns, and says the experience was fun. This is local voluntary pleasure and rivalry, not proof that NO NAME is the single true Hinami. She still sets the group-center curriculum and gives Tomozaki premature reassurance about his couple conflict, so reciprocity in one domain does not erase advisory asymmetry elsewhere.

V08.5 Unit 2 revises **reader knowledge**, not contemporaneous actor knowledge: after basketball failure and family change, Hinami treats nanashi as an aspirational summit and possible answer to isolation before she ever meets Tomozaki. This strengthens the relationship's prehistory while leaving Tomozaki unaware of it through the admitted endpoint.

#### Phase E — coaching suspension and the reproducibility account (V09)

Hinami admits that she expected Tomozaki and Kikuchi's first conflict to be easy and allowed it as relationship practice. Tomozaki refuses the instrumentalization and suspends the regular sewing-room meetings. The pause costs him; therefore it is not evidence that the relation was merely imposed. It is an owned boundary precisely because the bond matters.

Tomozaki then identifies the coaching project as a reproducibility experiment: Hinami shifted the player-controlled character from herself to a weak starting character in order to test whether her life method generalizes. Hinami does not deny the account and recognizes nanashi's insight. This **revises**, rather than cancels, earlier motive claims. Reproducibility is a central initiating structure; later competition, care, amusement, and attachment remain separately evidenced and need not be retroactively declared fake.

The Found-to-Jack change also exposes a shared-link dependency. Hinami reacts as if Tomozaki's character change threatens part of their relation and uses the released coaching time to practice. She says she can tolerate loneliness and does not want him to change her. Tomozaki's desire to reach her becomes his own goal, while the former teacher refuses that goal's jurisdiction.

#### Phase F — disclosure, unauthorized recruitment, and care without assent (V10)

After V09, Hinami reads but does not answer Tomozaki's invitations and stops using the sewing room, while preserving public school performance. Tomozaki shifts from direct correction toward a birthday project intended to generate joy and dialogue. The group produces real pleasure and access, but Hinami has not jointly selected the project as a remedy.

The volume contains two sharply different disclosure routes:

1. Tomozaki tells Mizusawa Hinami's gaming, coaching, proof, and reproducibility history without her permission. He explicitly knows the information is not his and calls the disclosure a violation. His trust in Mizusawa and care for Hinami explain the choice; neither creates authority.
2. On the private ride, Tomozaki asks to carry part of Hinami's burden, and Hinami voluntarily tells him about Nagisa. This is direct, recipient-selected disclosure. It increases trust and access without accepting rescue, shared ownership, or onward permission.

The birthday intervention also corrects Tomozaki's simple privileged-self model. He recognizes that the public heroine and NO NAME may both be cultivated, relationship-conditioned modes. Intimacy therefore grows by abandoning the claim that one private presentation is the whole person.

#### Phase G — failed replacement warrant and unresolved rupture (V11)

The family video violates the intervention's informational fit. Hinami rejects the group's standing to judge her life, leaves, and later stops school attendance. Tomozaki continues to care but expands covert inquiry: he waits outside her home, develops a channel with Haruka, asks for secrecy, and recruits others without Hinami's knowledge. The information gained is consequential and partly accurate. Accuracy does not retroactively confer permission.

Hinami voluntarily narrates the fuller family history to Tomozaki. He reconstructs much of her reason system correctly, then repeats the earlier ownership error at a higher level. His instruction changes from “believe yourself” to “believe me,” backed by his claim to be the one strong character she cannot beat. Hinami accepts a match, studies him, reverses a two-game deficit with Boxman, and defeats him. The result invalidates his **credential for command**, not his human value or all prior care.

The set is simultaneously contact, disclosure through style, rivalry, analysis, and weapon. Hinami calls Tomozaki's play beautiful and says she wanted to imitate it, yet uses victory to reject his authority and conclude that life is a bad game. The relationship is therefore neither reconciled nor destroyed at the V11 boundary. Formal coaching has not resumed; Hinami has not accepted the proposed collective project; and Tomozaki's former unique-rank leverage no longer stands.

### 5.3 Information and disclosure map

At the current boundary, information is not evenly shared:

- **Hinami → Tomozaki:** voluntary direct Nagisa and family history; bounded by recipient and context.
- **Tomozaki → Kikuchi:** the coaching/NO NAME history is disclosed in V09 with Hinami's permission.
- **Tomozaki → Mizusawa:** substantially overlapping private material is disclosed in V10 without Hinami's permission.
- **Tomozaki ↔ Haruka:** contact and continued discussion develop without Hinami's knowledge in V11.
- **Kikuchi → fiction/public audience:** Hinami-derived models enter recognizable fiction; their accuracy and communicative force do not settle consent.
- **group → proposed intervention:** different actors hold different pieces, but no complete shared record or subject authorization exists.

The core distinction is **access versus transfer authority**. Being selected as a confidant proves that disclosure occurred; it does not prove that the recipient may redistribute the information or build a remedy around it.

### 5.4 Atafami as relational medium

Atafami performs at least six relationship functions across the dyad:

1. **pre-contact recognition** — each values the other before offline status enters;
2. **reversed expertise** — Tomozaki possesses mastery unavailable to Hinami in their social hierarchy;
3. **shared register** — game structure lets them translate value disputes into testable terms;
4. **ordinary pleasure** — rematches, frustration, boasts, community entry, and practice sustain real enjoyment;
5. **identity attachment** — nanashi, NO NAME, Found, Jack, and Boxman carry histories neither actor treats as neutral equipment; and
6. **jurisdictional danger** — local rank becomes rhetorical authority over outside-life questions.

The V11 set does not negate functions one through five. It demonstrates the danger in function six. A fair match can determine its own result while remaining incapable of adjudicating who may define another person's self-worth or future.

### 5.5 Controlling contradiction

The central contradiction is not “mentor becomes student” or “rivals secretly love each other.” It is:

> Each recognizes in the other an unusually credible partner in effort and revision, yet each repeatedly converts that credibility into a claim about who may define the other's play.

Hinami does this through curriculum and reproducibility. Tomozaki does it through interpretation, specialness, and nanashi's status. Their strongest progress occurs when they narrow jurisdiction—Tomozaki's V03/V07 refusals, Hinami's voluntary disclosures, bounded invitations into each other's worlds. Their sharpest failures occur when care and understanding are treated as authority.

## 6. Tomozaki Fumiya ↔ Kikuchi Fuka

### 6.1 Phase history

#### Phase A — assigned contact, honesty reset, and acquired shared world (V01–V02)

The relationship begins inside Hinami's curriculum and through Kikuchi's mistaken belief that Tomozaki loves Michael Andi. Kikuchi's independent observation and willingness to disclose her writing are real; the apparent shared interest is not. Tomozaki first lets the premise stand, then admits the truth, withdraws the promise generated by the mistake, and refuses an immediately advantageous movie tactic. Kikuchi accepts continued conversation.

V02 matters because it prevents the honest reset from becoming mere negative purity. Tomozaki actually reads Andi, becomes absorbed, and can explain what he values. Silent library time becomes meaningful ordinary presence. The sequence is not “deception successfully produced romance.” It is:

**instrumental point of contact → admitted mismatch → recipient retains choice → genuine interest and shared practice develop afterward**.

This is the corpus's clearest early evidence that an assigned or artificial origin does not determine a relation's mature ownership.

#### Phase B — reciprocal initiation and recipient-specific knowledge (V03–V05)

V03 gives Kikuchi epistemic authority over her own reception of Tomozaki. Prepared material helps some parts of their outing and creates discontinuity in others. She does not pronounce social technique universally false; she distinguishes speech that transmits Tomozaki's lived “image” from memorized content without that image. Tomozaki tests the account at the fireworks and obtains a recipient-specific result rather than a general law.

Both begin initiating. Kikuchi asks for another outing and later the bookstore; Tomozaki emerges from withdrawal because ignoring her initiative would contradict his own reciprocity standard. They disclose literary aspiration, attraction, uncertainty, and different experiences of the social world. In V04 he meets her because she matters rather than because an assignment requires escalation. In V05 she enters a difficult multi-person support project, contributes a model that changes Tama's understanding, and learns more of Tomozaki's peer life. Quiet affinity expands without being replaced by group conformity.

V06 makes the relation collaborative. Kikuchi entrusts multiple stories to Tomozaki and accepts specific feedback. He cannot author her work but can translate its value to the class and protect her choice over whether it becomes a play. Attraction is increasingly direct in Tomozaki's focalization, but neither person has yet selected dating.

V06.5 supplies important **reader-only** formation evidence. Kikuchi's dated diary shows that she noticed Tomozaki, wanted cross-habitat contact, and linked him to her hope for a reader before he knew any of this. Those private pages cannot be retroactively treated as Tomozaki's V01 knowledge.

#### Phase C — coauthorship, indirect refusal, and direct romantic consent (V07)

The culture-festival play tests whether help transfers authorship. It does not: Kikuchi revises, accepts or rejects suggestions, and controls both public and private endings. Tomozaki supports the work without becoming its final author.

The public Libra–Alcia ending is an intentional indirect refusal of Tomozaki's romantic route. It must be counted as a refusal; it must not be inflated into a direct, context-free no. Tomozaki initially leaves, returns once to ask for direct clarification, and learns that Kikuchi wants incompatible things: what she sees as the world's ideal and her own love. He does not claim that the artifact is meaningless. He introduces his own choice as information she cannot privately author for him, directly asks to date her, and receives an explicit joyful acceptance.

The ethical sequence is narrow: indirect artifact → one direct clarification → reciprocal disclosure → direct request → direct consent. It does not license persistence after a direct refusal. Dating begins here; it is not retroactively present in the earlier affinity and is not a completed developmental reward.

#### Phase D — warm baseline and maintenance failure (V08.5 interstitial; V08)

V08.5 Unit 1 occurs before V08. Tomozaki and Kikuchi spend private Christmas time, build a snowman, photograph it, name future gift exchange, and coordinate later dates. Genuine early affection and anticipatory scheduling are direct. This baseline makes the later crisis more specific: warmth was not false, but it did not create a capacity or disclosure rule for a busier relational system.

V08 preserves mutual bids. Kikuchi repeatedly asks for time, touch, knowledge, and inclusion; Tomozaki values those bids and chooses affectionate acts. She asks for his support in seeking a newcomer award. Yet work, Tama, gaming, groups, and a forgotten message repeatedly displace her. He processes each collision locally rather than governing total capacity.

Rena introduces a second failure. Tomozaki refuses sex and does not accept her invitation, but experiences attraction, keeps the call secret, and tells Kikuchi that Rena is merely someone met once. When Rena's notification references sexual conversation, it reveals not an affair but a materially incomplete disclosure. Kikuchi's handholding and questions are genuine agency, but she often apologizes for wanting information and substitutes reassurance-seeking for an explicit boundary. The V08 endpoint is an unprocessed breach.

V08.5 Unit 3 is placed earlier than that crisis. Mimimi tells Kikuchi about her confession and continuing love. Kikuchi knows a fact Tomozaki did not give her, respects Mimimi's feeling, and does not surrender her relationship. Tomozaki does not know that conversation occurred. This asymmetry must not be projected into V08 dialogue as if all actors share one ledger.

#### Phase E — repair, false self-amputation, and mutual reselection (V09)

The first repair succeeds by separating injuries: time scarcity, incomplete disclosure, Rena's conduct, Kikuchi's loneliness and jealousy, and Hinami's special status. Each apologizes; they agree to choose more time. Tomozaki's message to Rena remains too narrow, and no general future rule is produced.

Kikuchi later gives visibly strained permission for another game meeting. Because Tomozaki recognizes her fear and accepts the answer, this is not robust evidence that shared authority has been solved. He then overcorrects—cancels a group outing, leaves LINE groups, abandons the group goal, and removes Mimimi's strap—so thoroughly that he prepares to end the romance rather than require Kikuchi to suffer.

Kikuchi rejects the smaller substitute person. She chooses Tomozaki as socially expansive, acknowledges the beauty and pain of his Hinami bond, and asks him not to erase the relations that helped form him. Their kiss and reciprocal selection restart the partnership. Specialness becomes a future practice rather than a property he must prove in advance. This is more mature coauthorship than unilateral sacrifice; it does not make her later jealousy irrational or costless.

#### Phase F — public integration, costly permission, and a second repair (V10–V11)

V10 moves Kikuchi from a protected dyadic habitat into Tomozaki's peer, family, travel, and game-professional worlds. She names fear, enters anyway, develops independent ties, and joins the Hinami project. The pair can state reciprocal jealousy after Mizusawa physically catches her. Kikuchi also deliberately yields the private ride to Tomozaki and Hinami. Her choice is valid and visibly costly; it is not permanent permission for every later Hinami-centered demand.

V11 tests that distinction. Kikuchi directly asserts the couple's boundary when Rena takes Tomozaki's arm and belittles him. Later she tells Tomozaki that describing him and Hinami as mutually special hurts because he is her boyfriend. When he rejects even her attempt to reach him, she ends the call. That refusal protects her standing rather than silently absorbing another cost.

Her fiction later reaches Tomozaki when direct reassurance cannot. He apologizes for his conduct and thanks her for making action possible. They reconcile around an explicit reason for the next story, while Kikuchi retains her own question about why she writes when interpretation may wound. Her request to involve the larger party prevents the couple from claiming exclusive rescue competence. It also opens new consent risks: the story uses Hinami-derived material, Hinami has not agreed, and no standing fictionalization rule governs the couple's work.

### 6.3 Controlling contradiction

Their recurrent risk is not lack of love. It is the assumption that love plus insight will automatically perform governance.

Tomozaki can mistake Kikuchi's generosity for available capacity. Kikuchi can delay explicit conflict because she fears burdening him and then use touch, questions, or fiction as proxy. Both improve: direct jealousy, reselection, refusal, apology, and collaborative purpose become available. The current relationship is therefore **chosen and developmentally real**, but its rules remain event-built rather than fully prospective.

## 7. Tomozaki Fumiya ↔ Nanami Minami / Mimimi

### 7.1 Phase history

#### Phase A — spontaneous rapport, failed rescue, and relation-specific limits (V01–V03)

Tomozaki's first easy contact with Mimimi and Tama comes when he speaks a considered thought without a script. Mimimi receives his unusual directness through humor rather than punishment, giving early evidence that social value is recipient-specific.

In V02 Tomozaki works closely around Mimimi's election campaign and recognizes her effort through his own ethic. He then gives the wrong answer when her effort becomes destructive because he treats persistence as sufficient. His later help improves only when he accepts that he is not the right relationship-holder to solve the crisis and creates contact with Tama. Mimimi's recovery therefore binds Tomozaki to her history without making him its savior.

V03 preserves gratitude and ordinary play. Mimimi calls him her hero and contributes to group strategy. That language establishes meaningful recognition, not yet romance and not permanent authority.

#### Phase B — Tama crisis, admiration, and direct romantic disclosure (V04–V06.5)

During the Tama crisis, Tomozaki sees Mimimi's bright public conduct as deliberate, sincere, and costly. Their shared intervention work strengthens trust, while the deepest dyadic care remains Mimimi/Tama's. Tomozaki does not displace it.

V06 supplies a gradual pattern of attraction—self-conscious proximity, shared food, monitoring of Kikuchi and other girls, jealousy-like irritation, and explicit respect for Tomozaki's and Tama's owned conviction—before Mimimi directly states that her “好き” is romantic. Tomozaki does not answer. His immediate advance is epistemic: he stops treating low self-worth as proof that her stated feeling cannot be real.

V06.5 must be routed carefully. Unit 7 retrospectively shows admiration and fear of being left behind developing after the Tama conflict; Unit 8 re-presents the V06 meal from Mimimi's perspective; Unit 10 alone moves directly beyond V06. In that final unit she internally accepts love, fears rejection and loss of the existing bond, and has no chosen pursuit policy. Tomozaki's response remains absent.

#### Phase C — direct account, costly support, and non-selection (V07)

Mimimi gives a person-specific account of her feeling: Tomozaki's self-belief, world-changing action, uncertainty, and movement matter to her. Her attraction includes comparison but is not reducible to competing with Hinami or Kikuchi.

She then bears the cost of sending him back to Kikuchi when the play's indirect ending appears to close his route. Tomozaki refuses to select Mimimi as consolation or repayment. After he and Kikuchi begin dating, Mimimi says her love remains, rejects repeated apology, and requests ordinary treatment. Her statement that she will not love him forever preserves future agency but does not supply when or how feeling changes.

The achieved state is unusually specific: romantic feeling is one-directionally direct; romantic non-selection is behaviorally clear; friendship continuation is mutually intended; ordinary boundaries remain to be made.

#### Phase D — ordinary continuity, local restraint, and corrective friendship (V08–V09)

V08 shows Mimimi continuing friendship and participating in the self-search group. The familiar one-to-one route and playful intimacy are not wrongdoing, but they affect Kikuchi's security because no explicit boundary has been negotiated. Mimimi's feeling remains active without sabotage or a second demand.

V08.5 Unit 3 is interstitial, not post-crisis. Mimimi directly tells Kikuchi that she still loves Tomozaki and intends no wrongdoing. This is a real ethical limit, but “wrong” is not yet a complete conduct specification.

V09 converts intention into one concrete boundary: Mimimi ends the habitual private walk from Kita-Yono because it may burden the relationship. She also refuses neutrality when Tomozaki tries to amputate his social world. She identifies the maneuver as structurally similar to her own earlier self-erasure, tells Kikuchi, and helps make a repair possible. This action protects a valued person without claiming him romantically.

Continuing love and ethical restraint therefore coexist. Neither cancels the cost of proximity; neither makes every future interaction suspect.

#### Phase E — group care and current incompletion (V10–V11)

V10 gives no new romantic pursuit or terminal rule. Mimimi contributes ordinary history and care to the Hinami birthday project. V11 gives more active boundary monitoring: she regulates physical and conversational proximity because Tomozaki is dating Kikuchi, enters student-council work, and supplies model-dependence insight. She is also named as part of the prospective group story.

These roles demonstrate that non-selection has not reduced her to a waiting romantic option. She has friendship, institutional capacity, humor, sport, and group authority beyond the dyad. The source still does not establish that her love has ended, that private routine has resumed, or that a final stopping condition exists.

### 9.3 Group development and relational capacity

The group evolves through several different structures:

1. **temporary strategic coalition (V03–V04):** Tomozaki recruits others to alter Nakamura/Yuzu and class outcomes. Coordination grows faster than his consent analysis.
2. **distributed learner support (V05):** “Team Tomozaki” combines Tomozaki, Mimimi, Mizusawa, Kikuchi, and others around Tama's selected end. Specialist contribution is productive because Tama remains the learner with the governing goal.
3. **public coauthorship (V06–V07):** festival work distributes rehearsal, performance, translation, and writing. Kikuchi retains final authorship; Tomozaki's leadership depends on delegation and Mimimi's live recovery support.
4. **owned affinity and self-search networks (V08):** Tomozaki discovers an Atafami community in which he is central and a self-search alliance whose purpose he genuinely shares. These are distinct from Hinami's assigned group-centrality metric.
5. **anti-isolation network (V09):** Tomozaki's attempt to leave groups is rejected as self-amputation. The relation with Kikuchi is rebuilt by preserving rather than erasing chosen friendships.
6. **distributed care project (V10):** the birthday trip aggregates ordinary memories, logistics, game design, food, professional contacts, and family messages. It creates real joy and also reaches beyond the group's knowledge.
7. **fracture and provisional party (V11):** school-course separation and Hinami's absence remove passive repair opportunities. Friends maintain a council event and propose a collective story, but assembly, subject assent, delivery, effect, and stopping rule remain open.

The group is thus both a relationship resource and an intervention risk. Distributed competence corrects lone-hero insufficiency. It does not automatically correct collective overreach.

### 9.4 Ordinary presence is a state variable

Low-stakes interaction is not filler in this corpus. It establishes whether a relation can exist without crisis, formal task, or declaration.

| Ordinary practice | Relationship evidence |

| walks, meals, jokes, comedy timing, and group errands | Tomozaki/Mimimi's friendship remains substantive after non-selection; ending one walk is therefore a meaningful boundary rather than trivial scheduling |

Ordinary presence has capacity requirements. Affection can be sincere while a schedule repeatedly communicates lower priority; absence can be protective, coercive, symptomatic, or simply logistical depending on the actor's stated state. This ledger does not infer motive from frequency alone.

### 14.3 Escalation rule

Return from this ledger to the primary EPUB only when:

- the reading does not preserve enough local context for the claim;
- a Japanese address, register, ambiguity, or exact wording claim is proposed;
- two reading entries appear contradictory and focalization/chronology cannot resolve them;
- an image-only or gaiji-bearing passage controls meaning; or
- a publication-ready specialist requires a more exact locator than the heading route.

When escalating, record witness, volume, chapter or source-unit identity, focalizer, stable local phrase, and whether the result confirms, narrows, or revises this ledger. Do not edit the old freeze to make it look as if the evidence was always known.
