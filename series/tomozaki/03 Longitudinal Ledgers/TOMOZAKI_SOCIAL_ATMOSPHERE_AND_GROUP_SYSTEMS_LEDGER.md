# Bottom-Tier Character Tomozaki — social-atmosphere and group-systems ledger through Volume 11

## 1. Responsibility, authority, and boundary

This is the canonical cumulative home for the independently recurring **social-system state** of *Bottom-Tier Character Tomozaki*. It tracks how local `空気`, status, reputation, information, inclusion, exclusion, informal punishment, facilitation, public roles, institutional capacity, cessation, repair, re-entry, ordinary maintenance, and distributed intervention change what people can do and what it costs them to do it.

The unit of analysis is neither a single character nor a generic “society.” It is a bounded social field at a stated time: a classroom, friendship cluster, campaign audience, trip group, festival production, game community, council, family-facing intervention, or network assembled around a problem. The ledger asks how individually chosen acts aggregate into effects no one actor wholly owns.

## 2. Social-system state schema

### 2.1 Evidence and time tuple

Every material state should be recoverable as:

**〈source position, diegetic position, continuity layer, bounded field, observer/focalizer, actor knowledge, reader knowledge, evidence class, revision state〉**.

Evidence classes are:

| Class | Social-system use |
|---|---|
| **DIRECT** | An observable act, public statement, refusal, role, sanction, response, result, absence, or institutional fact. |
| **ATTRIBUTED** | One actor reports another actor's conduct or a wider reaction not independently shown in the current scene. |
| **CORROBORATED INFERENCE** | Repeated effects across actors or scenes support a bounded mechanism beyond one statement. |
| **HYPOTHESIS** | A plausible causal or motivational account remains underdetermined. |
| **VALUE JUDGMENT** | An ethical evaluation is marked as evaluation rather than presented as event fact. |
| **NEGATIVE EVIDENCE** | A materially expected acknowledgment, rule, handoff, repair, safeguard, or result is absent at the stated boundary. |
| **OPEN** | Available evidence cannot responsibly adjudicate the state. |

Revision dispositions are **PRESERVE, STRENGTHEN, REVISE, DOWNGRADE, REJECT,** and **OPEN**. `NO CHANGE` is used only where continuity or non-resolution itself matters.

### 2.2 Required state fields

| Field | Governing question |
|---|---|
| **field / membership** | Which people and institutions are inside this local interaction, and who is peripheral, absent, or excluded? |
| **public issue** | What action, person, resource, dispute, or role is being collectively evaluated? |
| **operative norm** | What currently counts as acceptable, funny, loyal, disruptive, competent, cruel, admirable, or embarrassing? |
| **status / centrality** | Who can frame events, speak, refuse, recruit, or violate expectations at lower cost? Who bears higher response cost? |
| **reputation prior** | What stored interpretation makes the same act land differently by speaker or target? |
| **frame / salience** | Which aspect of an event is made visible, and which initiating fact is hidden or discounted? |
| **information topology** | Who knows what, through which authorized, covert, rumored, mistaken, or reader-only route? |
| **enforcement mechanism** | How are norms maintained: teasing, silence, avoidance, burden assignment, laughter, rumor, exclusion, humiliation, role loss, or direct attack? |
| **target and substitution** | Who bears the current pressure, and can it move to a safer or newly licensed target without changing the underlying mechanism? |
| **bystander threshold** | What makes nonparticipation, aid, or joining the attack less costly? |
| **facilitation / translation** | Who changes form, pacing, audience, logistics, register, or information so another person can participate? |
| **public / institutional role** | What formal office, committee, team, course, event, or professional scene carries authority or continuity? |
| **cessation** | Has the immediate harmful conduct or open conflict stopped? |
| **justice / accountability** | Is there acknowledgment, apology, restitution, safeguard, norm revision, trustworthy procedure, or repaired standing? |
| **re-entry** | Can an excluded or fallen person return to ordinary participation, and through whose labor? |
| **ordinary maintenance** | What repeated low-stakes practices keep membership, legibility, and trust available? |
| **distributed effect** | What outcome emerges from several specialized contributions, accidents, resistances, or omissions? |
| **authority / consent** | Who may choose participation, disclosure, representation, intervention, exit, or the system's claimed end? |

### 2.3 Individual motive and emergent effect are separate planes

Four propositions can be true at once:

1. an actor intends care;
2. the method uses concealment or leverage;
3. other people redirect the method in ways the initiator did not control; and
4. the resulting group effect helps someone while leaving authority or accountability defective.

Accordingly, this ledger never infers “the class wanted” from aggregate behavior unless a collective decision is directly established. It distinguishes:

- **actor purpose** — the end an identifiable person says or behaviorally shows they seek;
- **local participation** — what a person actually does within the field;
- **coordination mechanism** — why several actions align;
- **emergent result** — what the group produces whether or not anyone selected that exact result; and
- **retrospective interpretation** — how a focalizer or later ledger explains the pattern.

### 2.4 Two recurring causal circuits

The punitive circuit reaches its clearest form in V04–V05:

```text
latent grievance or status threat
        ↓
deniable initiating harm stays below the sympathy threshold
        ↓
the target's visible resistance becomes the salient disturbance
        ↓
status-linked framing recodes disturbance as fault
        ↓
bystanders gain a low-cost moral license to align
        ↓
distributed punishment makes reply more costly
        ↓
reply is treated as confirmation that the target is the problem
```

Target replacement can restart the circuit without reforming it. When Konno becomes vulnerable, prior resentment and Hinami's condemnation license the same structure against her.

The maintenance circuit is different:

```text
someone detects strain or missing capacity
        ↓
a facilitator translates, schedules, mediates, or creates a lower-risk role
        ↓
ordinary participation resumes
        ↓
the group regains function and avoids permanent exclusion
        ↓
unaddressed responsibility may become less visible
        ↓
maintenance labor is assumed again at the next rupture
```

Maintenance is therefore neither trivial nor automatically reparative. It can preserve people and projects while also absorbing consequences that responsible actors have not answered.

### 2.5 Controlling non-equivalences

- atmosphere is not mood;
- popularity is not total control;
- status is not moral authority;
- visibility is not causal primacy;
- silence is not agreement;
- laughter is not free consent;
- conformity is not shared motive;
- a rumor need not be true to change the field;
- a role need not be spontaneous to become owned;
- formal office is not the same as informal centrality;
- participation is not equal ownership of a group end;
- coordination is not consensus;
- conflict cessation is not justice;
- re-entry is not restitution;
- facilitation is not neutrality;
- resistance is not social invulnerability;
- distributed interpretation is not distributed consent; and
- institutional absence is not proof that no institution or adult exists, only that none is shown doing the required work in the admitted scenes.

## 5. Developmental reconstruction by system phase

### 5.1 V01 — local atmosphere, status permission, and reputational repair

V01 first makes `空気` analytically useful in the home-economics vote. A joking majority frame is not neutral play: it makes a preferred answer about Tomozaki's humor cheap and Tama's refusal costly. Hinami does not issue a formal order. As a high-status actor, she changes which interpretation is safe by converting Nakamura's loss and rejection into acceptable shared humor. The same people can then respond differently without anyone explicitly announcing a new norm.

The mechanism is local and relational:

| Component | V01 evidence |
|---|---|
| **stored status** | Hinami and Nakamura can introduce or survive material that would be riskier from a marginal speaker. |
| **norm cue** | a joke, glance, majority performance, or high-status reframing indicates which response will be socially legible. |
| **variable susceptibility** | Tama resists, Yuzu bends more readily, Mimimi actively smooths, and Tomozaki initially calculates exposure. |
| **costly resistance** | Tomozaki and Yuzu oppose Erika while frightened and lower in leverage. |
| **aftercare** | Hinami later reframes Atafami seriousness and affiliation so the confrontation does not fossilize into a hostile reputation. |

The Erika confrontation prevents a deterministic reading. Erika can frame earnest game commitment as contemptible and followers can align, but Tomozaki's direct objection and Yuzu's support alter the event. Their success is not broad confidence or status equality; it is evidence that values plus available expression can sometimes outweigh expected cost.

The aftermath also introduces path dependence. Tomozaki expects catastrophic exclusion, but curiosity and Hinami's active reframing soften the result. Nakamura's position survives, Yuzu's gaming becomes intelligible through her interest in him, and awkward relations remain repairable. Reputations are therefore **stored priors that can be revised**, not fixed labels and not simple summaries of merit.

### 5.2 V02 — school-scale legitimacy, audience framing, and rumor

The student-council election moves the model from a small conversation to a school audience. Hinami enters with accumulated recognition, trust, academic and athletic credibility, issue knowledge, and Mizusawa's recommendation. Mimimi possesses charisma and live energy but not the same stored institutional legitimacy. Copying Hinami's campaign on Hinami's terms would reproduce the status gap.

Tomozaki changes the comparison field through `別の土俵を作る`: the staged Siri interruption disrupts the formal register, captures attention, and makes improvisation the salient competence. Mimimi's response produces a real local audience effect. The final 456–131 defeat shows the limit. A momentary reframe can reveal capacity without erasing accumulated credibility or reorganizing the whole electorate.

This yields a layered model:

| Layer | Function | V02 result |
|---|---|---|
| **reputation stock** | supplies default trust and role fitness | strongly favors Hinami |
| **event frame** | decides which competence is salient now | Tomozaki shifts it toward Mimimi |
| **performance** | tests capacity inside the temporary frame | Mimimi succeeds locally |
| **institutional decision** | aggregates many priors and responses | Hinami wins decisively |
| **post-result meaning** | differs by actor and relation | effort, defeat, value, and belonging do not align automatically |

The Hinami/Mizusawa rumor adds an information rule. Its truth is denied, but it still changes Tomozaki's attention and creates a teasing opportunity for Hinami. A rumor is socially causal when people anticipate consequences around it; causal force does not make its content true.

### 5.3 V03 — the camp as information engineering with residual agency

The summer/camp operation is the first sustained group strategy around other people's relationship choices. Tomozaki, Hinami, Mimimi, and Mizusawa want Yuzu and Nakamura to progress. They diagnose Nakamura's status problem: he must learn what Shimane is doing without appearing to accept peer permission to switch romantic targets. Takei's position outside the teasing/status game becomes useful, so the group manufactures a seemingly accidental Twitter discovery and conceals Mimimi's relay.

The operation has no single ethical label:

- care for friends is genuine;
- information is deliberately staged;
- Takei is used without informed participation;
- Nakamura's response costs are modeled rather than ignored;
- the group removes an obstacle but cannot produce his choice;
- an unplanned river accident matters more than part of the formal plan; and
- the final outing is small, chosen, and valued rather than the confession planners might imagine as “success.”

`空気` here changes through planned and accidental micro-events: pairings, delayed departures, bath banter, teasing, the accident, and Nakamura's new privacy command. This disconfirms the idea that only a single popular mastermind can move a field. Skilled actors, peripheral actors, material accidents, and target resistance all contribute.

Takei is especially important to the social-system ledger. His low calibration and unusual status do not make him useless. They can insulate information from a prestige contest, supply spontaneous action, and generate outcomes planners cannot safely imitate. Competence is plural and field-dependent.

### 5.4 V04 — burden allocation, coalition power, and target substitution

V04 shows that atmosphere allocates work before it allocates overt punishment. Konno's posture, labels, group position, and supporters make Hirabayashi's refusal of tournament captaincy expensive without requiring an explicit command. Yuzu initially refuses the same role because accepting only to absorb pressure would repeat an unwanted pattern; she later accepts from an owned reason to help Hirabayashi. Identical visible compliance can therefore hide coercion or agency.

Nakamura's absence provides a natural experiment in informal centrality. The apparently unified male group decomposes into individual preferences when its center is gone. When a central person is present, others need not have identical motives to behave coherently; centrality changes the expression cost of difference.

Tomozaki's tournament coalition is a real advance and a warning. He coordinates Yuzu, Hinami, Mizusawa, inferred Konno motives, status threat, and a claim about Nakamura's preferences. The plan activates latent motivations and produces participation and celebration. Konno does not authorize the motivational frame constructed around her. The local success also changes the next field: Yuzu and Nakamura's relationship forecloses one status-bearing possibility, and pressure moves toward lower-risk targets.

The substitution sequence is:

| Stage | Pressure-bearing actor | Why this target is comparatively available | What the class sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| initial burden | Hirabayashi | low status, low expected resistance, limited coalition | reluctance and assigned responsibility more than the coercive setup |
| redirected hostility | Hirabayashi | Konno can preserve deniability and sympathy control | Tama's defense becomes increasingly conspicuous |
| resistant replacement | Tama | she intervenes publicly and will not accept the frame | repeated conflict and disturbance |
| later reverse target | Konno | public overreach, accumulated resentment, high-status fall, Hinami's cue | a wrongdoer apparently receiving deserved return harm |

The volume ends before the full reverse cycle, but it establishes path dependence, accumulation, substitution, and feedback. It also separates causal explanation from moral entitlement. Hinami can explain why Hirabayashi became vulnerable while still recognizing that Konno is wrong; her conditional-aid rule nevertheless risks withholding help from the person least able to display safe self-directed action.

### 5.5 V05 — the punishment machine, moral license, and an incomplete stopping point

V05 supplies the corpus's most complete social-system experiment. Konno keeps harassment deniable and below the sympathy threshold. Tama's direct protests are public. Classmates increasingly experience the protest rather than the initiating conduct as the recurring disruption. Avoidance then approaches participation because the class can preserve peace by treating the resistant target as the problem.

Mizusawa names the legitimating move: if Tama is framed as unable to read the room, attack can be redescribed as **punishment**. The label matters because it converts private irritation into moral permission. Bystanders do not need Konno's original motive. They need a socially acceptable reason why joining, laughing, avoiding, or failing to intervene is no longer blameworthy.

Tomozaki's training changes Tama's reception rather than her governing judgment. The first local success is ethically costly: she stops protesting damage to her desk and property, reducing visible disturbance while bearing more harm. Later, the small-stature routine and broader recipient knowledge create `一貫した隙`, a stable and safe-to-recognize opening. The class can interpret her as a person rather than an unvarying rejection of them.

Hinami then intentionally exploits Akiyama's grievance, Konno's fashion/status sensitivity, relational cues around Yuzu and Nakamura, and accumulated class resentment. Several causal details remain open: the source-facing reading does not identify who damaged the lead case or provide Tomozaki every recruitment conversation. What is directly supported is that Hinami manages cues, publicly exposes Konno's romantic vulnerability, and continues after Konno is already defeated.

Once Konno pushes Akiyama and refuses apology, the crowd becomes partly self-propelling. Former allies, sports-group students, and other classmates turn tears, desk-kicking, mockery, and Konno's own language into “justice.” Hinami created conditions and supplied a powerful cue; she did not individually perform every later act or retain complete control of the crowd.

Tama stops the reverse persecution from the position of the person with the strongest plausible revenge claim. The intervention succeeds because two things now coincide:

1. her moral rule remains stable—collective return harm is wrong; and
2. her acquired public legibility lets the class hear the rule without coding it as another rejection of everyone present.

This result must be decomposed:

| Dimension | V05 boundary |
|---|---|
| immediate harassment of Tama | stops |
| reverse persecution of Konno | stops after Tama's intervention |
| Tama's class reception | materially improves |
| Konno's ordinary peer participation | later resumes, aided by Yuzu |
| acknowledgment of harm | not demonstrated |
| apology to Tama | not demonstrated |
| restitution for the destroyed strap | not demonstrated |
| Akiyama's full participation/account | unresolved |
| formal institutional response | not demonstrated |
| general anti-bullying rule or future procedure | not demonstrated |

The correct state is **cessation plus re-entry, without completed justice**.

Yuzu is central to that distinction. She repairs the strap, monitors Hinami, follows the fallen Konno when others do not, and helps her regain ordinary peer life. This is valuable maintenance and a barrier against permanent expulsion. It also permits the class to function without forcing responsible actors to complete acknowledgment or restitution.

### 5.6 V06–V07 — political peace, public roles, translation, and distributed production

V06 immediately rejects the claim that V05 settled the system. Konno and Akiyama can perform enough outward friendship for their group to continue while criticizing one another privately. Yuzu mediates because she believes she can. The class enters festival production with workable political peace and unresolved trust.

Three new public-system capacities emerge.

First, reputation becomes deliberately cultivable through Tomozaki's Instagram assignment. Photographs communicate affiliation, invitations create new casual access, and repeated participation produces a more legible group position. Consent is divisible: Tama gives direct capture/upload permission; a group knowingly poses; Kikuchi accepts a two-shot but refuses publication and is obeyed; Mimimi and Yuzu are partly maneuvered without full purpose disclosure. Public visibility can create belonging and memory while also exposing people to hidden optimization.

Second, formal committee roles give coordination a repeatable structure. Yuzu becomes overall festival committee chair. Tomozaki prepares proposals, recruits credible intermediaries, structures a vote, and translates Kikuchi's private work into a form the class can evaluate. The manga café and play do not succeed because one leader controls the class. They succeed because proposal form, institutional procedure, status-bearing voices, recipient consent, and specialized labor align.

Third, the festival demonstrates that a public project can widen inclusion without requiring identical social style. Kikuchi accepts authorship and class participation but fails when she tries to imitate universal Hinami-like brightness. Tachibana assumes rehearsal coordination; Yuzu secures rooms and moves work; Mimimi repairs Tomozaki's comedy freeze through shared timing; Tama performs an authored role; male peers handle styling and ordinary circulation; technical workers carry production. Selected habitat and distributed role fit outperform assimilation to one ideal type.

Festival success does not retroactively close the V05 case. Konno's useful dialogue suggestion is real prosocial participation, not apology. Akiyama's ordinary presence is not an account. Tama's successful role is evidence of expanded range, not proof that future disagreement carries no risk. Yuzu's visible competence does not demonstrate that maintenance cost has become limitless or fairly allocated.

### 5.7 V08–V09 — group centrality, owned affiliation, overload, withdrawal, and repair

V08 separates group membership from an assigned status metric. Hinami sets a medium goal: Tomozaki should become the center of a recognizable group of at least four. Tomozaki accepts the curriculum and can perform the task, but the volume does not show that centrality itself is his desired end. The self-search alliance with Mimimi, Takei, and Tama is owned because its activity answers a question he personally has; it also happens to satisfy the metric.

The distinction matters because group systems can be entered for different reasons:

| Form | Origin | Current ownership |
|---|---|---|
| “Tomozaki group” centrality metric | Hinami's curriculum | behaviorally accepted; terminally unproven |
| self-search alliance | Tomozaki's vocational uncertainty plus peers' interests | purpose substantially owned |
| Atafami gathering | pre-existing game affinity and nanashi status | strongly owned participation |
| school friendship routines | accumulated contact and shared history | later defended as constitutive ties, not mere level markers |

The Atafami gathering also demonstrates status plurality. At school Tomozaki's reputation was historically marginal; in the game community nanashi is an obvious center. That standing produces praise, access, professional models, and a setting Hinami initially enters through protected identity. No single school hierarchy exhausts a person's social position.

Group abundance creates allocation pressure. Work, Tama's family outing, game gatherings, self-search activity, and ordinary peer contact displace time with Kikuchi. The causal issue is not that groups contaminate a pure couple. It is that belonging requires time, disclosure, and priority governance.

In V09 Tomozaki responds by trying to erase the network: he cancels an outing, exits group chats, abandons the centrality target, and removes a Mimimi-linked object. The abandonment gives strong evidence that **central status as such was not his owned end**. His grief gives equally strong evidence that the particular relationships are owned.

Network repair comes from differentiated actors rather than collective unanimity. Yuzu and Nakamura initiate one conversation; Mizusawa supplies a relationship model; Mimimi detects self-amputation, confronts Tomozaki, and tells Kikuchi; Kikuchi rejects a smaller substitute person. The system does not order Tomozaki back into every group. It makes visible that leaving all ties is a destructive response to a narrower governance failure.

### 5.8 V10 — distributed care, fit by heterogeneity, and intervention overreach

The V10 birthday trip is the strongest completed case of a distributed intervention. No participant possesses a total model of Hinami. Its power comes from heterogeneity:

| Contributor or subsystem | Distinct contribution |
|---|---|
| **Tomozaki** | initiates the project, supplies game knowledge, seeks dialogue, and connects the parts |
| **Yuzu** | turns diffuse concern into invitations, timing, logistics, welcome, and low-risk relational transitions |
| **Mimimi and Tama** | contribute remembered tastes, food, humor, and shared history rather than diagnosis |
| **Mizusawa** | negotiates professional/game resources and helps structure opportunities |
| **Kikuchi** | enters the network, observes, supports, and deliberately makes room for a private conversation |
| **Nakamura and Takei** | supply ordinary belonging, unguarded energy, and group continuity |
| **Endo/game network** | creates a bespoke game and makes Tomozaki's professional resource socially consequential |
| **teachers, rivals, staff, owners, and other beneficiaries** | provide a distributed record of effects Hinami cannot reduce to the focal group's claim alone |
| **family video** | introduces an intimate causal narrative whose informational fit the group has not established |

The trip creates real joy. Strangers, rides, Found, food, and messages elicit reactions beyond Hinami's ordinary public range. The result is not evidence that one “true self” has been extracted. It is evidence that varied settings make varied response possible.

The same intervention crosses authority lines. Tomozaki gives private Hinami information to Mizusawa without permission; the group designs an emotionally consequential event around a person who did not jointly select it as treatment; and the final video remains unseen evidence at the V10 boundary. Beneficial effects do not settle whether every input was proportionate or authorized.

### 5.9 V11 — rupture, institutional absence, substitution, and the prospective party

The mother's video breaks the intervention's implied contract because it interprets Hinami's life through a triumphal adversity-to-success narrative. Hinami accepts that the party was enjoyable and that `日南葵` produced valuable effects. She rejects the group's informational and normative standing to infer from those effects that her standards or underlying self are correct.

The rupture becomes institutional. Hinami stops attending school, does not perform her expected student-council handoff, and becomes unreachable through ordinary calls. The source shows retained skill in private game training, so the absence is not evidence that all competence vanished. It is a failure of reason and participation in a particular life system.

Third-year course allocation then increases structural distance:

- Tomozaki, Kikuchi, Mizusawa, and Mimimi share regular humanities;
- Nakamura, Yuzu, Tama, and Takei enter science; and
- Hinami enters the advanced course in a separate building with a different gate and routine.

Administrative placement does not cause the crisis. It removes incidental contact, shared handoffs, and low-stakes opportunities through which earlier groups often repaired themselves.

The peers' response splits rather than disappearing. Yuzu tries to reconnect people and carries disproportionate guilt for initiating the video. Nakamura refuses participation partly to reject the claim that Yuzu alone caused the result; he does not declare the friendship necessarily over. Mimimi and others take on the student-council event, preserving an institutional output without requiring Hinami's performance. Mimimi's live tournament commentary creates value not reducible to imitation of Hinami.

The final party proposal is a structural advance over the lone-hero model. Kikuchi names the limits of a Tomozaki/Kikuchi dyad and asks for Mimimi, Mizusawa, Yuzu, Tama, and other important people. The proposal recognizes distributed expertise:

| Perspective | System knowledge potentially added |
|---|---|
| Mimimi / Haruka | what an admired model does to an admirer's own route |
| Mizusawa | loneliness, performance, and the risk of imagined recognition |
| Yuzu | affiliation, logistics, re-entry, and maintenance cost |
| Tama | principled refusal and the limit of retaliation in another's name |
| Kikuchi | motive, narrative form, multiple agents, and interpretive exposure |
| Tomozaki | games, coaching history, direct disclosure, and failed unique-warrant rescue |

The advance remains prospective. The party is not yet assembled; information is uneven; Hinami has not authorized representation, delivery, intervention, or participation; no withdrawal rule exists; and neither school return nor ordinary contact occurs. **Distribution corrects interpretive monopoly. It does not manufacture consent.**

## 6. Cross-volume system models

### 6.1 `空気` as a cost-and-permission field

The corpus-wide formulation supported through V11 is:

> `空気` is a locally coordinated field of expectations, legitimacy, salience, and response costs. It changes which acts are available, visible, interpretable, or punishable without determining every participant's motive or eliminating resistance.

Its properties accumulate across the main chain:

| Property | First strong demonstration | Later qualification |
|---|---|---|
| **locality** | V01 home-economics and Erika scenes | V02 shows school-scale coordination without making it universal |
| **status asymmetry** | Hinami changes permissible humor; Erika frames earnestness | V04 central-actor absence reveals that followers retain latent preferences |
| **reframing** | Hinami repairs reputation after conflict | V02 Tomozaki can change salience but not erase stored credibility |
| **contestability** | Tomozaki, Yuzu, and Tama resist | resistance can succeed while remaining costly and needing allies |
| **path dependence** | V01 aftermath changes later reputations | V04 success contributes to a new vulnerable-target field |
| **substitution** | V04 Hirabayashi → Tama | V05 Konno becomes the reverse authorized target |
| **accumulation** | repeated conflict hardens Tama-as-disruption | later ordinary contact can slowly change the interpretive prior |
| **distributed enforcement** | Erika's followers and the V05 class | no single actor must order each sanction once moral license exists |
| **accidental change** | V03 Takei/river event | planning competence never closes material or resistant contingency |
| **maintenance dependence** | Yuzu's repeated repair work | function can resume while accountability remains absent |

The model does not explain every action. Private values, attraction, ambition, family history, game mastery, economic constraints, and institutional rules retain independent causal force.

### 6.2 Status, reputation, and unequal norm enforcement

Status is best tracked as a bundle rather than one rank:

| Status resource | What it changes | Representative evidence |
|---|---|---|
| accumulated competence reputation | whose proposal or reassurance receives prior trust | Hinami in V02 election and later school roles |
| group centrality | whether individual preferences cohere or become openly expressible | Nakamura's absence in V04; Konno's followers |
| interpretive authority | whose joke or condemnation changes the permissible frame | Hinami in V01 and V05 |
| institutional office | access to rooms, schedules, audiences, and continuity duties | Yuzu's festival chair role; student council in V11 |
| affinity expertise | status in a field separate from school popularity | nanashi in Atafami gatherings |
| relational standing | ability to make a request receivable or stop retaliation | Tama as harmed party; Kikuchi as Tomozaki's partner |
| visible vulnerability / public character | predictability that reduces perceived threat | Takei, Hinami's cheese enthusiasm, Tama's V05 routine |

High status lowers some costs but raises others. Hinami can mobilize audiences and becomes the object on whom others build credible routes. Konno can coerce below the sympathy threshold but is vulnerable to a spectacular fall once her legitimacy breaks. Tomozaki's nanashi status supplies professional access and later becomes an overclaimed rescue warrant. Status is affordance, dependency, and exposure—not moral credit.

Norm enforcement is unequal because identical acts enter different reputational priors. Tama's direct preference can be heard as disruption while Takei's excess is familiar comedy. Konno can use indirection while Tama's reply is visible. Later Tama's acquired legibility makes a similarly firm moral objection receivable. The content does not change alone; the system through which it is heard changes.

### 6.3 Information, rumor, secrecy, and public meaning

| Information event | Route | System effect | Epistemic/authority limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hinami/Mizusawa dating rumor (V02) | peer rumor, then Hinami's correction | changes Tomozaki's attention and permits teasing | effect does not prove content or romance |
| camp Twitter discovery (V03) | engineered relay through Takei, evidence removed | lowers Nakamura's status cost for learning and acting | care coexists with deception; Takei lacks informed role knowledge |
| Konno/Akiyama grievance and cues (V05) | partial reports, observed resentment, planned prompts | helps produce public overreach and crowd realignment | exact lead-case actor and all recruitment details remain open |
| Nakamura “other girl” concern (V06.5) | rumor plus indirect verification | activates Yuzu's insecurity and Konno's group expertise | concurrent story; surveillance and reassurance coexist |
| Instagram and group images (V06) | selected public traces | communicate affiliation and change reputation | capture, purpose disclosure, and publication permission are divisible |
| Rena notification (V08) | private conduct becomes visible accidentally | reveals incomplete couple disclosure and wider group-contact risk | no affair follows from the message alone |
| Hinami birthday testimony (V10) | curated messages from many prior contacts | gives distributed evidence of consequence and gratitude | effect cannot prove Hinami's whole self or governing values correct |
| family video (V10–V11) | Yuzu/Nakamura family contact; group screening | inserts an intimate success narrative and precipitates rupture | group lacks full history and cannot infer authorization from affection |
| Haruka inquiry (V11) | covert contact and selective disclosure | increases causal reconstruction and intervention capacity | Hinami does not know or authorize the channel |
| Kikuchi's fiction and party proposal (V11) | personal reports transformed into public-facing narrative | reaches Tomozaki and offers distributed interpretation | fiction is authored hypothesis; Hinami's representation consent absent |

Information has at least four independent dimensions: truth, route, timing, and authority. A true report can be unauthorized; a false rumor can be causal; a reader can know formation evidence no actor knows; and a group can hold many fragments without possessing a shared or complete record.

### 6.4 Inclusion, exclusion, re-entry, and selected habitat

| Case | Exclusion or marginality | Re-entry / alternative belonging | What remains unresolved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tomozaki V01 | marginal school status and anticipatory withdrawal | trained access, costly defense, curiosity, mixed peer inclusion | competence precedes secure self-concept; belonging is contextual |
| Mimimi V02 | identity threat within comparison to Hinami, not literal group expulsion | Tama's non-comparative recognition and return to track | rank/self-worth coupling is not permanently cured |
| Tama V04–V05 | class recodes resistance as disruption and increases avoidance | acquired legibility, coalition support, moral intervention, ordinary participation | victim-borne adaptation cost and class accountability remain |
| Konno V05–V06 | status collapse turns former center into authorized target | Yuzu's accompaniment and later ordinary/festival participation | apology, restitution, trust, and durable conduct remain open |
| Kikuchi V06–V07 | broad-group translation strain and attempted universal assimilation | selected literary habitat plus bounded public authorship | selected habitat is not a command to abandon all wider contact |
| Rena retrospective | exclusion from appearance and niche-interest groups | older game community and sexual-status access | substitution creates new boundary harm rather than secure belonging |
| Hinami V11 | withdrawal from school, peers, and council after value collapse | no re-entry before boundary; game contact remains possible | participation, desired end, support terms, and institutional return open |

Re-entry is a distinct social good. It prevents permanent expulsion and can restore ordinary human contact. It does not erase the question of what happened, who bears loss, or what prevents recurrence.

### 6.5 Facilitation and translation roles

Facilitation is specialized work, not generic niceness.

| Actor | Recurrent system function | Risk or limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Hinami** | frame setting, reputational repair, coalition activation, public competence, strategic modeling | can convert insight into control or punitive license; her centrality creates dependence |
| **Tomozaki** | diagnosis, strategy, stakeholder mapping, translation, cross-group recruitment, task allocation | can hide purpose, narrow success criteria, and treat a helpful system result as authority |
| **Yuzu** | logistics, emotional smoothing, bridge work, re-entry, repair initiation, public coordination | invisible labor becomes assumed; guilt and absorber burden lack a durable limit |
| **Mimimi** | atmosphere repair, improvisation, attention capture, humor, crisis performance, audience translation | authentic skill can hide exhaustion and invite automatic emergency assignment |
| **Mizusawa** | status reading, pacing, recipient translation, negotiation, correction, role differentiation | insight can become unlicensed information access or imagined recognition |
| **Tama** | principled refusal, moral stopping, non-comparative recognition, legitimacy as harmed party | directness is not always receivable; courage can be made to carry the institution's missing duty |
| **Kikuchi** | perspective enlargement, motive modeling, narrative translation, authorial integration | accuracy can expose persons; public form can outpace consent |
| **Takei** | unguarded entry, safe practice, status-insulated relay, accidental reconfiguration | low calibration can create risk; usefulness does not imply informed participation |
| **Nakamura** | male-group centrality, rough boundary setting, loyalty signals | abrasive communication and pride can make coordination covert |
| **Konno** | fine-grained hierarchy reading, group mobilization, later practical aid | social acuity supports both help and deniable harm; prosocial acts are not restitution |

No facilitator is neutral. Changing form or lowering participation cost changes the field. Ethical evaluation depends on target authority, purpose disclosure, collateral burden, and stopping conditions—not only on whether facilitation “works.”

### 6.6 Public roles and institutional capacity

The series distinguishes informal influence from formal role without treating either as unreal.

| Institutional setting | Formal structure | Informal dependency | Demonstrated capacity | Missing safeguard or limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| student-council election (V02) | school-wide vote and campaign roles | stored reputation, recommendation legitimacy, audience expectations | public comparison and issue presentation | fair procedure does not equal equal starting social capital |
| sports tournament (V04) | captaincy, teams, class competition | Konno's hierarchy and Yuzu/Nakamura relations | shared activity and class celebration | burden allocation can be coerced; downstream target substitution ungoverned |
| culture festival (V06–V07) | committee chairs, rooms, votes, rehearsal, performance | Tomozaki's translation, Hinami's intervention, Yuzu's maintenance | distributed authorship and recovery | prior accountability remains outside project success |
| Atafami scene (V08 onward) | gatherings, tournaments, commentary, sponsorship, professional roles | nanashi prestige, creator/player networks, follower visibility | alternative belonging and vocational access | rank/status can become identity proof; interpersonal safety is uneven |
| student council and third-year school (V11) | office, event, courses, buildings, handoff expectations | Hinami as admired model; Mimimi and peers as substitutes | peers preserve an event under absence | no demonstrated crisis handoff, workload rule, or return pathway |

The absence of a shown teacher or formal disciplinary response in the V04–V06 harassment arc is analytically material. The burden of detection, adaptation, stopping, and re-entry falls on students. This does not prove adults never exist or never act elsewhere. It establishes that the source-facing resolution shown is informal and peer-borne.

### 6.7 Cessation, repair, justice, and ordinary maintenance

These states must be recorded separately:

| State | Minimum evidence | Tomozaki example |
|---|---|---|
| **cessation** | the immediate harmful act or open conflict stops | harassment and reverse persecution stop in V05 |
| **functional coexistence** | people can share space or project work again | Konno/Akiyama group and class function in V06–V07 |
| **re-entry** | a fallen/excluded person resumes ordinary participation | Yuzu helps Konno return to her group |
| **acknowledgment** | responsible actor names conduct and responsibility | not demonstrated for Konno→Tama or Akiyama's full role |
| **apology** | recipient-directed recognition and regret | not demonstrated in the controlling class case |
| **restitution** | material or symbolic loss is addressed by an accountable party | destroyed strap is repaired by Yuzu, not shown restituted by its destroyer |
| **trust repair** | altered practice makes future reliance reasonable | not demonstrated across Konno/Akiyama/Tama |
| **norm/institution repair** | group or institution adopts a rule/procedure against recurrence | not demonstrated |

Ordinary maintenance—meals, jokes, rehearsals, photographs, group chats, errands, committee work, travel, shared games, and routine school presence—keeps affiliation real. Missing routines are equally diagnostic: unread messages, abandoned chats, course separation, missed school, and absent handoff remove low-cost repair channels. Ordinary activity can coexist with unresolved injury; that is why its return is evidence of function, not proof of justice.

### 6.8 Distributed intervention: what plurality changes and what it cannot

| Intervention | Distribution gain | Residual authority problem | Current outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| V03 camp | several actors model status, information, timing, and opportunity | concealment and use of Takei; targets did not choose the plan | obstacle reduced; small chosen outing occurs |
| V05 Team Tomozaki | multiple recipient-specific capacities replace one copied curriculum | secret recording; victim carries adaptation cost | Tama gains legibility and stops crowd harm |
| V06–V07 festival | author, translator, chairs, performers, coordinators, and peers share production | overload and uneven consent/publicity remain | successful public work and recovery |
| V09 relationship/network repair | peers supply different corrections and refuse group erasure | no complete future allocation rule | chosen ties and couple continue |
| V10 birthday trip | ordinary memories and specialized resources exceed any single diagnosis | privacy, informational fit, and subject-selected remedy remain incomplete | joy and disclosure, followed by video rupture |
| V11 proposed story party | plurality can contest Tomozaki/Kikuchi interpretive monopoly | subject consent, membership, information sharing, delivery, exit, and stopping all open | project formation only |

The longitudinal revision is precise: **distributed competence is usually more capable and less epistemically narrow than lone-hero action; it is not self-authorizing.**

### 7.2 Unknowns that remain causally material

- who damaged Konno's lead case;
- the full content and sequence of Hinami's recruitment of Akiyama;
- the exact degree of each bystander's knowledge during covert harassment;
- whether any unshown adult or formal school process addressed the V04–V06 harm;

## 9. Counterevidence and rival readings

| Rival reading | Evidence that makes it attractive | Counterevidence and current judgment |
|---|---|---|
| **“The series teaches submission to the room.”** | Social technique, reputation, and recipient fit repeatedly improve outcomes. | Costly refusal, recipient-owned adaptation, selected habitat, and moral stopping are equally central. **REJECT as total thesis.** |
| **“Tama's purity alone defeats the class.”** | Her judgment stops reverse persecution. | Earlier directness was recoded as disruption; coalition support and acquired legibility make the same core receivable. **REVISE to stable judgment plus changed interface and standing.** |
| **“Konno is the whole bullying system.”** | She initiates and manages sustained harm. | Followers, bystanders, stored irritation, Akiyama, Hinami, and the later reverse crowd reproduce the mechanism. **REJECT singular-cause version without reducing Konno's agency.** |
| **“Hinami simply loses control in revenge.”** | Strong attachment and anger are visible. | She calculates cues and continues after Tomozaki attempts to stop escalation. **DOWNGRADE loss-of-control account; preserve affect as end, discipline as method.** |
| **“The crowd's attack is justice because Konno harmed Tama.”** | Konno's conduct is serious and public refusal follows. | Unlimited return harm repeats the same authorization mechanism; Tama explicitly stops it. **REJECT.** |
| **“Yuzu's reintegration work excuses everyone.”** | Her labor prevents permanent exclusion and restores function. | The next volume shows cold war and no accountability. **REJECT; preserve maintenance as real good.** |
| **“Festival harmony proves the class matured.”** | Former antagonists cooperate and the production succeeds. | Cooperation is ordinary function, not demonstrated acknowledgment or safeguard; Yuzu still absorbs maintenance. **REJECT closure, preserve broadened capacity.** |
| **“Kikuchi fails socially because she refuses development.”** | Her Hinami-like rehearsal leadership disrupts coordination. | She chose strenuous change toward a universal ideal; selected authorship and affinity produce stronger participation. **REJECT deficit-only model.** |
| **“Tomozaki's later group proves Hinami's centrality goal was right.”** | He becomes an organizer and retains many ties. | He abandons central status as an end while defending particular relationships. **REVISE: assigned form helped create access; owned affiliation diverged from the metric.** |
| **“The V10 group saves Hinami until one accidental bad video.”** | Most activities produce pleasure and the messages demonstrate gratitude. | The event was never jointly selected as remedy; privacy and fit were already incomplete; the video exposes rather than originates the authority problem. **REJECT.** |
| **“Hinami's withdrawal betrays people who relied on her.”** | Haruka and council systems suffer meaningful consequences. | Dependency is real, but it does not create a duty to perform a disowned self indefinitely. **OPEN on responsibilities of handoff; reject ownership of her recovery.** |
| **“The V11 party is safer because everyone participates.”** | Different perspectives can contest hero and author monopoly. | Members have not agreed, Hinami has not consented, information is uneven, and no stopping rule exists. **OPEN project; reject safety-by-number.** |

The controlling synthesis is not that all intervention is suspect. The corpus repeatedly values facilitation, translation, ordinary maintenance, and distributed competence. Its harder question is whether a group can preserve these capacities while making purpose, authority, burden, exit, and accountability explicit.

## 12. Open questions and explicit negative constraints

### 12.1 Open questions at the V11 boundary

#### Class harm and accountability

- Do Konno or Akiyama ever acknowledge their specific conduct, apologize, or make restitution?
- Does Tama receive any choice in what repair would count for her?
- Has the class acquired a durable norm against covert harassment and reverse persecution, or only a memory of one stopped event?
- Would the acquired interpretive prior protect Tama during a new high-cost disagreement?
- Did any teacher or formal institution learn enough to create a safeguard?

#### Facilitation and burden

- Can Yuzu refuse repair or logistics work without losing standing or harming relationships she values?
- Can Mimimi use performance and atmosphere-repair skill without becoming the default emergency substitute?
- Can Tomozaki coordinate without hiding purpose or treating organizational competence as authority?
- Can Kikuchi translate persons into fiction without making exposure the price of inclusion?
- Can Tama's moral stopping power be supported so that resistance does not substitute for missing institutional duty?

#### Hinami absence and the peer party

- Who has legitimate authority to define the party's membership, information pool, medium, delivery, and stopping rule?
- Will invited peers actually join, disagree, withhold information, or refuse the premise?
- Will Hinami be asked before representation or delivery, and will refusal remain usable?
- Can student-council and school functions be redistributed without making Hinami's return a condition of institutional normality?
- Can the group distinguish a handoff obligation from a demand that Hinami resume the public role?
- What ordinary, low-stakes route—if any—remains available after course separation and school absence?

#### Status and belonging

- Does Tomozaki's professional-game status become durable community membership or another single proof dependency?
- Can selected habitat and cross-group participation remain complementary rather than competing ideals?
- Does Rena acquire a non-exploitative belonging structure or durable boundary rule?
- How do third-year course structures change friendship maintenance after the immediate crisis?

## 13. Specialist promotion and synthesis-gate effect

### 13.1 Promotion decision

**A dedicated social-atmosphere and group-systems specialist is mandatory.**

The architecture already assigns this responsibility, and the reconstructed evidence confirms the threshold independently:

- the same mechanisms recur from V01 through V11;
- later volumes materially revise early `空気`, status, and repair claims;
- the central V04–V06 class system cannot be owned by a Konno, Tama, Hinami, Tomozaki, or Yuzu monograph alone;
- festival, romance-network, game-community, and V10–V11 intervention cases test the mechanism outside bullying;
- individual motive and emergent result repeatedly diverge;
- the cessation/justice/re-entry distinction has downstream ethical and literary consequences; and
- the V11 party requires group-level analysis beyond dyads.

The specialist should live under `../06 Specialist Synthesis/` and own the literary argument about how fields allocate permission, how reputation stores interpretation, how punishment becomes distributed, how facilitation creates access and hidden burden, and how groups can increase capacity without becoming sovereign over persons.

The specialist must:

1. begin from SOC-C01–SOC-C22 rather than recapping plots;
2. distinguish description, causal reconstruction, and ethical judgment;
