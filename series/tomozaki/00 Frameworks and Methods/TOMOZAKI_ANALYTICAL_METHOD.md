# Bottom-Tier Character Tomozaki — analytical method

## 1. Responsibility

This document governs source-facing analysis of the locked Japanese light-novel corpus for *Bottom-Tier Character Tomozaki* (`弱キャラ友崎くん`). Its responsibility is methodological: it defines how observations become claims, how volume-by-volume state is frozen and revised, and when recurring questions deserve their own canonical artifacts.

It is **not** a plot summary, character monograph, romance ranking, thematic conclusion, or whole-series verdict. Those responsibilities belong downstream and must be earned by source evidence.

## 2. Current source and authority boundary

The current semantic anchor is the audited Japanese-language EPUB set dated 2026-08-29: numbered main Volumes 01-11 plus side-story Volumes 06.5 and 08.5. Volume 07 is the acquired Special Edition witness. The exact source boundary is recorded in `../01 Source Lock and Inventory/TOMOZAKI_SOURCE_LOCK_AND_INVENTORY.md`.

- Git stores interpretation, analytical state, and routing.
- The governed Drive evidence plane stores primary EPUBs and the audit manifest.
- Normalized English filenames are locators, not translations and not semantic authority.
- Anime, manga, official translations, interviews, reception material, and other supplements are separate witnesses unless a later source-lock revision explicitly integrates them.

Never fill a gap in the locked source with remembered canon, a wiki, adaptation knowledge, fandom consensus, or general model knowledge while presenting the result as source-derived.

## 3. Evidence and epistemic classes

Every durable claim should be recoverable to one or more source-bearing observations. Use these working classes:

- **DIRECT** — explicitly stated or unambiguously shown by the current source boundary.
- **CORROBORATED INFERENCE** — not stated verbatim, but supported by multiple observations, perspectives, or consequences with weak counterevidence.
- **INTERPRETIVE HYPOTHESIS** — a plausible model that organizes evidence but remains meaningfully contestable.
- **PREDICTION** — a prospective expectation frozen before later unread evidence is consulted.
- **VALUE JUDGMENT** — an explicit normative evaluation resting on reconstructed descriptive evidence.
- **OPEN / UNRESOLVED** — evidence is insufficient, contradictory, viewpoint-bound, or dependent on unread material.

A narrator's explicit thought is DIRECT evidence that the narrator thought it; it is not automatically DIRECT evidence that the thought is objectively correct. Likewise, a confident social theory stated by a character remains a character-held model until the text independently supports it.

## 4. Prospective sequential reading

The numbered main volumes use a **prospective freeze**.

For each VNN:

1. begin from the frozen analytical state after VNN-1;
2. record important open questions, expectations, and uncertainty before consulting VNN;
3. read VNN as the new source increment;
4. separate confirmation, extension, complication, revision, and falsification of earlier claims;
5. write the VNN deep reading;
6. freeze its bounded state before advancing to VNN+1.

Later evidence may revise the current model. It may not silently rewrite what an earlier reader reasonably knew. Earlier predictions remain historical analytical evidence even when they fail.

A volume deep reading is not a chapter transcript. Select evidence because it changes an analytical state.

## 5. Focalization, self-theory, and epistemic distance

This series requires unusually careful separation between **focalized self-description** and independently demonstrated character state.

For any important claim, ask:

- Who perceives, narrates, remembers, or asserts this?
- What information is available to that person at this volume boundary?
- Is the statement a description of behavior, a self-explanation, a theory of another person, or a theory of social life?
- Does later behavior corroborate it?
- Does another character independently corroborate it?
- Could emotion, status anxiety, aspiration, embarrassment, strategic framing, limited information, or rhetorical purpose explain the account?

Do not flatten the protagonist's explicit metaphors or theories into omniscient narration. Do not flatten another character's coaching language into authorial law. The fact that a model is useful to a character does not establish that it is exhaustive or normatively correct.

## 6. Learned behavior is not automatically inauthentic

The analysis must not assume that deliberate practice, imitation, self-monitoring, or socially learned technique is inherently fake.

Distinguish at least:

- externally prescribed behavior;
- consciously practiced technique;
- instrumental compliance;
- habit formation;
- internalized competence;
- value adoption;
- self-authored choice;
- strategic self-presentation;
- concealment or masking;
- behavior that remains alienated from the actor's own values.

A behavior can begin as a tactic and later become ordinary competence without becoming deception. Conversely, fluency does not prove endorsement. Treat authenticity as an evidence question about authorship, motive, felt ownership, and recipient context rather than a binary trait.

## 7. Character state before trait generalization

For important characters, reconstruct **state** before claiming stable traits. Separate:

- stable tendency;
- developmental state;
- role effect;
- recipient-conditioned behavior;
- group-context effect;
- crisis or stress effect;
- deliberate performance;
- genuine revision.

Do not force every contradiction into hidden consistency. A contradiction may represent development, competing goals, self-deception, different recipient effects, hypocrisy, uncertainty, or an unresolved model.

## 8. Competence, confidence, status, self-concept, and agency

Do not collapse social development into a single improvement axis. Track independently when evidence warrants:

- **competence** — demonstrated ability to perform a social or practical task;
- **confidence** — expected ability or willingness to act under uncertainty;
- **status** — position in a peer group or social hierarchy;
- **self-concept** — how the character describes or evaluates themselves;
- **agency** — ability to choose, refuse, set ends, revise goals, and bear the costs of action;
- **value change** — what the character comes to care about or reject.

These can move in different directions. Improved technique does not necessarily imply higher self-worth; increased status does not necessarily imply greater self-authorship; a chosen refusal can represent development even when it lowers short-term social success.

## 9. Coaching, assignments, rules, and self-authorship

Guidance and structured practice should be reconstructed before being morally classified.

For any coaching or assignment relationship, track:

- who defines the goal;
- who selects the method;
- whether refusal is practically available;
- whether consent is broad, specific, ongoing, or assumed;
- what information each party controls;
- whether success criteria change;
- whether the learner internalizes, modifies, rejects, or exceeds the framework;
- whether dependence grows or shrinks;
- whether disagreement produces negotiation, coercion, withdrawal, or revision.

Do not presume that effective guidance is therefore benevolent, manipulative, emancipatory, or oppressive. Those are conclusions requiring evidence across state and consequence.

## 10. Games, rankings, optimization, and rule metaphors

Game language and optimization metaphors are analytically important but must be treated as **models used by characters**, not as automatic statements of the series' ontology.

Track separately:

- explicit game/rank terminology;
- strategic analogies used to understand social situations;
- what the analogy illuminates;
- what it obscures;
- whether characters revise or abandon it;
- domains where optimization succeeds;
- domains where relationships, values, ambiguity, or irreducible personhood resist optimization.

Avoid the inverse error of assuming that strategic reasoning is inherently dehumanizing. The question is how the model is used, what it predicts, what it misses, and how its user treats other people within and beyond the model.

## 11. Relationships are directional and stateful

Relationship analysis must be dyadic or network-aware rather than a list of pairings.

For A→B and B→A, track as evidence warrants:

- desired relationship and expectations;
- affection, attraction, admiration, rivalry, obligation, dependency, or resentment;
- trust and disclosure;
- information asymmetry;
- mentorship or coaching;
- status and leverage;
- reciprocity;
- conflict and repair;
- boundaries and refusal;
- forms of address and register;
- what changes across volume states.

Do not infer reciprocity from one-sided feeling, or romance from narrative prominence. Friendship, rivalry, mentorship, attraction, dependency, and group affiliation may overlap without being interchangeable.

## 12. Peer networks, status, and group norms

Track social ecology rather than treating each interaction as isolated.

Where material, distinguish:

- explicit group membership;
- informal centrality;
- reputation;
- gatekeeping;
- alliance and mediation roles;
- public versus private behavior;
- status signals;
- norm enforcement;
- who can violate a norm without equivalent cost;
- who interprets whom for the group;
- how one relationship alters another.

A character's social position should be inferred from repeated network effects, not merely from labels such as popular, normie, loner, or gamer.

## 13. Ordinary life as evidence

Small-scale behavior is necessary to reconstruct people without reducing them to plot functions or social-strategy abstractions.

Record mundane evidence when it bears on the model: hobbies, gaming habits, food, clothing, commuting, study, work, family routines, purchases, gifts, humor, irritation, avoidance, rest, texting, planning, punctuality, physical comfort, and low-stakes interactions.

Ordinary-life evidence should not become trivia accumulation. Promote it when it clarifies values, competence, attachment, self-presentation, recipient effects, or state transitions.

## 14. Japanese wording, register, and translation control

The Japanese source is the semantic anchor for wording-sensitive claims.

Record original wording when interpretation depends on:

- forms of address;
- politeness/register shifts;
- self-reference;
- evaluative labels;
- social categories;
- game terminology;
- ambiguity, hedging, or rhetorical force;
- emotionally charged or relationship-defining lines.

A later translation may be used as a convenience witness only if its role is stated. Do not silently substitute translated wording for a Japanese-language claim.

## 15. Side-story volumes 06.5 and 08.5

Volumes 06.5 and 08.5 are inside the locked available source inventory but **outside the simple integer-numbered prospective chain**.

Before integrating each side-story volume:

1. establish its publication and diegetic placement relative to the frozen main-volume state;
2. identify focalizers and whether individual stories look backward, sideways, or forward;
3. route each observation to the main-volume state it can legitimately inform;
4. record whether it confirms, extends, complicates, or revises the current model;
5. never rewrite predictions that were frozen before the side-story witness was consulted.

The reading order decision belongs in the current state/source-routing artifacts, not in undocumented analyst convenience.

## 16. Claim revision vocabulary

When later evidence tests a prior claim, prefer explicit revision states:

- **PRESERVE** — materially unchanged;
- **STRENGTHEN** — same claim, stronger evidence or wider support;
- **REVISE** — core model survives but wording, scope, or causal account changes;
- **DOWNGRADE** — claim remains possible but confidence or scope falls;
- **REJECT** — evidence materially contradicts it;
- **OPEN** — adjudication remains insufficient.

This vocabulary describes analytical state, not moral approval.

## 17. Minimum numbered-volume deep-reading responsibilities

Each numbered deep reading should cover, as evidence warrants:

- exact source witness and volume identity;
- event/scene structure and focalization;
- character-state changes and contradictions;
- social learning, tactics, habit, and internalization;
- competence/confidence/status/self-concept/agency changes;
- relationship and peer-network changes;
- coaching, consent, refusal, and self-authorship questions;
- self-presentation and authenticity evidence;
- game/rank/optimization metaphors and their limits;
- ordinary-life evidence;
- distinctive Japanese wording/register where interpretation depends on it;
- DIRECT evidence versus inference/hypothesis/value judgment;
- rival readings and counterevidence;
- revision status of important prior claims;
- bounded predictions/open questions for the next unread main volume.

## 18. Promotion thresholds for new artifacts

Create a new canonical artifact only when it owns a recurring retrieval/revision responsibility that existing files no longer serve reliably.

Examples:

- a longitudinal ledger after the same question recurs across multiple volume freezes;
- a character monograph after enough longitudinal evidence exists to model stable tendencies, state effects, contradictions, relationships, and abstentions;
- a specialist synthesis when a recurring problem cannot be maintained cleanly inside general ledgers;
- a full-series synthesis only after the relevant source boundary is actually covered.

Do not create directories merely to advertise future ambitions.

## 19. Comparative and ethical frameworks

PACTRIH, cross-series simulation, shipping comparison, political/ethical mapping, and other higher-order frameworks are **downstream**.

Before applying them:

- establish the relevant Tomozaki source boundary;
- reconstruct the character/relationship/social-system model first;
- distinguish descriptive reconstruction from normative judgment;
- preserve uncertainty and state dependence;
- avoid treating protagonist status, popularity, romantic outcome, or narrative reward as moral evidence.

A comparative framework may expose new questions, but it must not substitute for source reading.

## 20. Falsification and rival-reading rule

For any thesis important enough to recur, ask what evidence would make it weaker.

A durable analysis should be able to name:

- supporting evidence;
- counterevidence;
- alternative causal explanations;
- missing evidence;
- source boundaries that could change the result.

When evidence supports multiple readings, keep them live until one becomes materially stronger. Precision about uncertainty is preferable to premature closure.

## 21. Bootstrap prohibition

This method establishes how analysis will be done. It does not authorize substantive conclusions by itself.

The first canonical interpretive artifact under this root should be the Volume 01 deep reading. No whole-series character model, relationship verdict, ethical score, romance conclusion, or thematic synthesis is established merely by creating this scaffold.
