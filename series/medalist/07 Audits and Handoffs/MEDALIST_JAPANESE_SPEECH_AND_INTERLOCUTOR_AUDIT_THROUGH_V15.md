# Japanese speech and interlocutor audit through V15

This audit owns the retrievable printed-language sample and its interlocutor contrasts. The [independent source-fidelity audit](MEDALIST_INDEPENDENT_SOURCE_FIDELITY_AND_CORRECTION_AUDIT.md) owns all correction decisions, source/visual counts, adversarial tests and publication acceptance. The current Inori/Tsukasa monographs, Hikaru–Yodaka supporting study and coach–pupil study receive bounded conclusions from this audit; no permanent parallel speech ledger is created.

The sample has24 diagnostic examples in17conversation contexts. Multiple excerpts from one exchange remain one context; they do not count as24independent character rules or new principal clusters. It was selected after reading the existing corpus, so this is staged reassessment, not blind sampling. Additional early/middle/junior original checks corroborate particular contrasts without establishing a complete frequency distribution. All examples are original Japanese print at adequate resolution. Creator language comments and voice-actor/adaptation material are separated; no acoustic pitch, timbre, intonation or delivery is inferred.

The input/current-version distinction follows f81 and the independent audit’s correction checkpoint. The entrusted-role noun is役目, not the frozen transcription役割. V02SP3 is the cold-term naming; V04 revisits it. Hikaru’s V13 strongest-self rival judgment is private and has no depicted recipient; actual communicated recognition belongs to the earlier thicket promise. These corrections are governed by the independent audit and ledger addenda, not new literary IDs in this home.
## Findings with explicit exceptions

**Inori: directness and grammatical politeness are distinct.** Her first ambitious request addresses 司先生 and ends 手伝ってくれますか (SE01). Her late refusal of a less demanding role ends 必要なんです (SE07). Both are direct assertions of a wish within respectful coaching relations. Her V06 question to the same teacher, 先生も信じてる？ (SE03), uses plain contracted grammar: a categorical claim that she always uses polite grammar to her coach fails. She uses polite negative しません while promising Iruka that she will not make excuses (SE04), so grammatical politeness does not imply yielding. To Nozomi in V15 she uses family address and plain endings, 大丈夫だよ and 優勝するからさ (SE06). This is an interlocutor contrast demonstrated in the sample, not a frequency census.

Inori's early わたし and later 私 represent the same first-person expression, *watashi*. Their spelling alone does not show a personality change. Her V15 press statement uses formal 思っています and repayment/burden vocabulary (SE05). It is shown as an interview relayed to Tsukasa; Kanayumi's accompanying interpretation must remain a separate adult inference. Inori's later conversation with Tsukasa is her own speech, and the printed entrusted-role noun is **役目**, not 役割 (SE07, V15 occurrence 0185). The source supports the role/duty reading while requiring a transcription erratum in the frozen V15 reading and selective term repair downstream.

**Tsukasa: respectful address often coexists with casual grammar.** The original chapter-one trial gives the child 君は十分偉いよ before skill assessment (SE10). Chapter two adds emphatic いのりさん address (SE11). FB02 says the editor found the first chapter slightly frightening and the author adjusted the speech and began using the child's name with さん (FC06). The production account supports a change of presentation; it does not mean kindness first came into existence in chapter two.

Tsukasa uses 俺, あなた/name-san, contractions and plain prohibitions to his pupil (SE12–SE13). His later request 約束させてほしい also mixes respectful address with plain grammar (SE15). To senior colleague Sonidori he uses 鳩鳥先生 and 知っています (SE16). To Yodaka in a disagreement he combines 俺/貴方 with です/ます explanation (SE14). Name honorification, grammatical formality and care must therefore be tracked independently. “Polite to his pupils throughout and rough only alone” in the current form specialist is overbroad if it means grammatical politeness. A bounded repair is: “Tsukasa often preserves respectful forms of address to pupils, while mixing them with plain grammar, 俺 and contractions; formal grammatical speech also occurs with seniors and adversaries.” Each clause has a located example; it is not a behavioral rule or a claim of constant usage.

**Hikaru: public and mentor-facing formality can carry confrontation.** In her first peer conversation she uses 君, 私 and plain explanations such as 滑ってるんだよ (SE17). When she knows the name, she uses いのりちゃん and plain 嬉しいよ (SE18). Her お母さんいない statement to Inori (SE19) establishes a reported absence; it does not establish its cause. Her remembered nighttime exchange with Yodaka includes 気づいたんです and a shift toward a plain declaration of wanting skating of her own (SE20). Her later formal 不当だと思います contests the fairness of a result (SE21). A challenge to a mentor is compatible with polite grammar. Public 誇りに思っています, 大好きです and 証明します are explicitly public self-presentation (SE22), distinct from the adjacent private fear and interior rival judgment.

The actual V13 occurrence 0038 has Inori directly address Hikaru with 光ちゃんだけには and say she did not want Hikaru to see the earlier skating (SE08). This provides communicated evidence for Hikaru's particular importance to Inori. It cannot turn Hikaru's separate suitability thought into something communicated. The independent audit owns the V13 rival-attribution correction; this overlap adds no independent cluster to its count.

**Contrast speakers: avoid pronoun stereotypes.** Yodaka's technical conversation uses 僕, 君 and familiar 慎一郎くん (SE23), alongside plain analytic speech. It does not require a stereotyped coarse-male register to be forceful. Iruka's お前 and 言うな (SE24) form a rougher immediate exhortation before Inori's polite promise. One scene does not establish generalized hostility, a universal coaching doctrine, or a habitual address distribution.

**Lexical recurrence and regional scope.** 役目 in Inori's late speech is her interpretation of an entrusted role; it is neither Tsukasa's V13 代わり nor Kanayumi's 人生の犠牲. A semantic relation among these terms must preserve the changed noun and speaker. The V01 occurrence 0212 creator aside labels Miku's speech as Mikawa rather than Nagoya and comments that Inori uses little dialect and Tsukasa uses some. That is editorial language commentary, not a dialogue census. The diagnostic sample does not independently establish a regional-form frequency or global dialect rule. Particles such as よ, さ and contracted -てる/-ちゃ appear across interlocutors and cannot alone establish an exclusive idiolect. No acoustic pitch, timbre, intonation or voice-actor delivery is inferred from balloons, typography or fanbook actor commentary.

## Additional corroboration and limits

Early V02 pupil-facing plain instruction and formal counterspeech to Yodaka distinguish respect from morphology. V04 Inori’s選べる to Ryoka means capacity to choose her coach secured by strength, in a secret exchange Tsukasa does not hear. V05 Tsukasa’s俺 with politeます to Kago accompanies the child’s name+さん while the child is absent. V08 Hikaru politely reports her coach’s doctrine to Tsukasa then shifts to a plain unreceived mutter; remembered Kameganya formsええ/したる/や are actually printed in one Suzu-directed scene, not a blanket dialect rule inferred from location.

V09 Tsukasa’s plain argumentative rebuttal coexists with asking leave to speak loudly and polite athlete-address狼嵜選手さん. His自傷行為 account is self-history and ethical wording, not a clinical diagnosis. Hikaru’s polite accusatory content and uncaptioned tears remain different channels. V10’s interview role-play changes address/register before returning to peer conviction;僕/私 in a remembered voice montage changes grammatical owner without creating a simultaneous live Yodaka utterance. Gori’sもしかして and Tsukasa’s conditionalのなら carry epistemic limits:later face-reading confirmation cannot retroactively prove the early formation hypothesis.

V12 Hikaru’s courteous nickname negotiation and formal refusal to belittle her past coexist with a bow; deference does not prove unresisting submission. Instrumental all-fake speech to Rio and solitary naming of home do not establish one wholly false personality. V15 Inori’s plain mother-facing reassurance precedes polite大丈夫です/食べました to Tsukasa; Nozomi hears the film-erasure hypothetical before he arrives. Reader knowledge cannot fill that reception gap.
