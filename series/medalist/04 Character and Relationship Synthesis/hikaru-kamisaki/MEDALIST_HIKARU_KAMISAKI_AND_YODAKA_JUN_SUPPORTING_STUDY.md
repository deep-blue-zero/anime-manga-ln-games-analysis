# Hikaru Kamisaki and Yodaka Jun: the prodigy from inside and the champion who needed no coach

## Scope and status

This is a supporting study, narrower than a protagonist monograph, and should not be used as one. It treats two people as one subject because the work does: Hikaru Kamisaki, the rival of Inori Yuitsuka, and Yodaka Jun, who coached Hikaru in secret and stopped on the night of her second national junior title.

Its evidence ends earlier than the project's boundary. Neither of them appears in continuity after Volume 13. In Volume 14 Hikaru is present only as what Inori means to reach (`MD-EV-V14-0010`), and in Volume 15 Yodaka is what Tsukasa Akeuraji, as a boy, once saw on a screen (`MD-EV-V15-0042`). Both claims were preserved unchanged across those volumes and advanced afterwards only by supplements (`MD-CL-0003@14`, `MD-CL-0016@14`). What follows is therefore a statement through V13, with creator-layer additions marked. The ledger holds both studies at a bounded, provisional scope (`MD-RD-0067`, `MD-RD-0061`, `MD-RD-0078`).

Principal sources: the readings of [V12](../../02%20Sequential%20Readings/MEDALIST_V12_DEEP_READING.md) and [V13](../../02%20Sequential%20Readings/MEDALIST_V13_DEEP_READING.md), the two [fanbook](../../02%20Sequential%20Readings/MEDALIST_FB01_PARATEXT_AUDIT.md) [audits](../../02%20Sequential%20Readings/MEDALIST_FB02_PARATEXT_AUDIT.md), and the [claims ledger](../../03%20Longitudinal%20Ledgers/MEDALIST_CLAIMS_PREDICTIONS_AND_REVISIONS_LEDGER.md).

## Thesis

For seven volumes Hikaru Kamisaki is drawn from outside, as a condition other children live under (`MD-CL-0023@1` to `@4`). Volumes 8 to 13 then give her an inside, and it is not the inside of a self-sufficient genius. By her own account and by scenes shown directly, she is a child who was placed by adults, who secured her place in each household by working out its rules, and who was unafraid in competition because she stood behind Yodaka Jun (`MD-CL-0003@11`, `@12`).

Yodaka is the opposite case. By the testimony of Kyōboku Yutaka, a former coach of his, he corrected himself, planned his own career, and left every teacher within a year (`MD-CL-0016@9`). His coaching is that formation applied to a child. Its last step, by his own statement, is that the skater lets her coaches go. So the arrangement ended in the way its logic required and not in the way its wording had threatened: Yodaka withdrew when Hikaru won in her own manner, not when she failed (`MD-CL-0016@12`).

What Hikaru has in Yodaka's place is what his method leaves out: adults who stay, and Inori Yuitsuka, the rival she says keeps her from being alone. Whether Hikaru's independence is a departure from Yodaka or his manner continued is not decided by the page, and this study does not decide it.

## Seen from outside (V01–V07)

The first meeting sets an order that the later volumes keep. Hikaru Kamisaki befriends Inori Yuitsuka, then a beginner, before either knows who the other is, strikes Sonidori Rio when he insults Inori, skates with her, and declines to teach her (`MD-EV-V01-0012`, `MD-EV-V01-0016`, `MD-RL-0013`). Hikaru is pleased, by her own later remark, that Inori did not recognize her (`MD-KN-0005`). In the same scene Hikaru reports long hours, private help, a sense that it is not enough, and no mother (`MD-EV-V01-0017`). Volume 2 supplies the facts behind that: an adopted orphan in a wealthy house, a program sheet that names Sonidori Shin'ichirō, and Yodaka Jun, a retired Olympic champion, who coaches and choreographs her and sends her to a late lesson after her event (`MD-EV-V02-0024`, `MD-EV-V02-0027`, `MD-CS-0029`, `MD-IN-0014`, `MD-RT-0007`). Rio's memory in Volume 3 shows the method: Yodaka jumps once and tells Hikaru to remember (`MD-EV-V03-0053`).

Hikaru's warmth toward Inori in these volumes is conduct without stated meaning. On the day of Inori's sixth-grade test Hikaru knows her at once and makes meeting at the All-Japan a promise (`MD-EV-V04-0039`, `MD-CS-0058`). Inori later infers that Hikaru had believed she would qualify (`MD-KN-0044`). The ledger notes that a seeded skater could have named no other meeting, so the belief is Inori's inference and not confirmed (`MD-RT-0024`).

Hikaru's own words in this period are never about a rival. Abroad for ballet, she says she wants to be the one who works the magic and to know a craft at its highest standard (`MD-EV-V05-0006`, `MD-CS-0064`). She studies Yodaka's old programs in secret because she wants everything he saw and not only what he gives (`MD-EV-V07-0018`, `MD-CS-0129`). The one moment that seems to concern Inori is uncaptioned: at a shared practice Hikaru stops over Inori after a fall, asks whether she is all right, and skates away as Inori says her name (`MD-EV-V06-0046`, `MD-CS-0108`). The claims ledger kept four explanations open (`MD-CL-0003@5`). Later Hikaru crosses a room barefoot to tell Inori she has been waiting (`MD-EV-V07-0041`), which fixes her direction and does not explain the turning away (`MD-RT-0043`).

Meanwhile the field organizes itself round Hikaru: skaters discount their rankings, raise their layouts, and call their cohort by her name (`MD-CL-0023@1`). Yodaka states that this is intended (`MD-EV-V06-0038`). Whether Hikaru then knew his design is not shown (`MD-KN-0059`).

## Hikaru's terms (V08–V09)

Volume 8 reverses the picture. Hikaru Kamisaki tells Sonidori Rio that one fall in the nightly run-through ends her skating, that competition is mild beside it, and that she has no use for a self that fails (`MD-EV-V08-0038`, `MD-EV-V08-0039`, `MD-CS-0164`, `MD-RT-0046`). Hikaru, whom other children cry over, is under a condition harsher than any she imposes (`MD-CL-0023@4`). That explains her ease better than temperament does, though not finally; Volume 13 gives a second explanation in her own voice.

Three acts of hers in that volume matter here. Days before the event, skating alone at night, she asks Tsukasa Akeuraji whether his second job is a sacrifice for Inori Yuitsuka, and reports Yodaka Jun's teaching that victory requires one (`MD-EV-V08-0032`, `MD-EV-V08-0037`). Having watched Inori's quadruple, Hikaru asks to open with a quadruple toe loop untried on that ice; Sonidori Shin'ichirō advises against it and Yodaka, reached late by telephone, allows it and raises her last jump (`MD-EV-V08-0033`). And she says, of the piece Yodaka once reserved to himself, that she will challenge him (`MD-EV-V08-0035`). She wins with 128.98 (`MD-PERF-0079`). Whether surpassing Yodaka is escape or obedience could not be said then (`MD-CL-0003@7`) and is addressed, not closed, by Volume 13.

The rain scene of Volume 9 is the first time Hikaru speaks of Inori, and she speaks to Tsukasa. She credits Inori's step sequence over her own, says she wanted Inori to medal, calls Inori strong because a little abnormal, and blames Tsukasa's refusal of sacrifice for the lost medal (`MD-EV-V09-0045`). She accepts Tsukasa's answer that sacrifices cannot be weighed on one scale and condemns her own sneer (`MD-EV-V09-0047`). Then she states her position. She knows that a shadow falls on someone when she shines. She will not do anything so sly as ask forgiveness for it, and supposes Yodaka felt the same. If Inori loses again in a year she will not doubt Yodaka (`MD-EV-V09-0048`, `MD-CL-0023@5`, `MD-MOT-0013`).

Two cautions attach. The statement is general; nothing shows that Hikaru connects it with Ahiru Miku, who collapsed after her and during whose program she walked out without expression (`MD-EV-V09-0004`). And Hikaru weeps twice without a caption; that Tsukasa's words struck her own nightly rule fits the timing and is an inference (`MD-CS-0191`).

## The inside (V10–V12)

**A body.** At the summer camp Hikaru Kamisaki has lower-back pain. Okazaki Iruka, an older skater, sees it, lends her a support, and tells her she is taking skating lightly; Sonidori Shin'ichirō, by Hikaru's own remark, has not noticed, and Yodaka Jun is not shown to know (`MD-EV-V10-0043`, `MD-RL-0238`, `MD-KN-0103`). Hikaru rests, skips her block by seeded entry, and later thanks Iruka for an injury caught early (`MD-EV-V11-0008`, `MD-EV-V13-0002`). Riley Fox afterwards gives the injury a cause: Hikaru over-practised under her club-mates' eyes because winning on less work than theirs would have felt like guilt. Hikaru does not deny it and refuses to be ashamed of it (`MD-EV-V12-0022`, `MD-KN-0119`).

**A misreading.** At the same camp Hikaru asks Inori Yuitsuka on what grounds Inori believes she will win, hears that Inori cannot forgive a self that fails on the ice, and concludes that Inori is Yodaka's likeness and that Tsukasa Akeuraji is her own kind (`MD-EV-V10-0040`, `MD-EV-V10-0041`, `MD-CS-0218`, `MD-CL-0025@5`). In Volume 9 Hikaru had described Inori in terms that fitted herself. The reassignment may mean that Hikaru had found she was not what she was told to become. It may mean only that she was glad of the rival she wanted. The claim kept both (`MD-CL-0003@9`). By Volume 12 the reading has hardened into appetite: Yodaka bores of Inori, Hikaru decides, because he thinks himself without character and Inori is his copy, and Hikaru wants the medal as something taken from that copy (`MD-EV-V12-0026`). That account of Yodaka is Hikaru's inference.

**A transfer.** Hikaru leaves the club that fronted for her and moves alone to Tokyo, to Riley Fox's club, under the same concealment (`MD-EV-V11-0048`, `MD-EV-V12-0003`, `MD-IN-0120`). The page gives four reasons and ranks none: Inori's growth, time lost to rest, Yodaka's own history of changing clubs, and a belief that she harms those round her, the latest instance being Sonidori's collapse from overwork, which she overhears attributed to pupils she attracted (`MD-CS-0251`, `MD-KN-0118`). She tells Sonidori Rio the decision was hers and not Yodaka's (`MD-EV-V12-0005`).

**The contract.** In Tokyo Yodaka states the terms. They began with the small Hikaru's plea to be taken to the Olympics and his question whether she could live his road. She follows that road; she wins gold at every competition; the moment she leaves it he stops being her coach; a further condition attaches to the run-through (`MD-EV-V12-0019`, `MD-EV-V12-0020`). Its sanction takes nothing from her but him. She asks whether keeping it is enough, and is told that the one she must fight is herself.

**An act, and a home.** To be let go, Hikaru tells Rio that her six years in his house were a performance: she saw that she must not be turned away, copied the manner of his mother, Ava, and hid what Yodaka had already taught her (`MD-EV-V12-0006`, `MD-CS-0252`). The work surrounds this with reasons to weigh it both ways. A memory shown directly has the small Hikaru noting that in the house she came from holding back pleased, that here a child should be childlike, and that she must not get it wrong (`MD-EV-V12-0013`). Sonidori had guessed as much (`MD-EV-V10-0035`). Against it, Rio cites years no one staged, says he can tell her laughs apart, and asks whether she knows which self is the act (`MD-EV-V12-0007`, `MD-CL-0021@6`). Alone on the train Hikaru calls the house her home and says she learned feeling there (`MD-EV-V12-0014`, `MD-CS-0258`). At her new school she is called cheerful and likes it (`MD-EV-V12-0017`).

The bounded statement is double. Hikaru adapted on purpose from the first day; what she built by adapting was real to her (`MD-CL-0003@11`). The adaptive habit has enough independent support to be called a disposition: two households, a peer's remark that she takes care not to overawe (`MD-EV-V10-0039`), her reading of the new club, and in Volume 13 her recognition of laughter put on for adults. The claim that the manner was false does not.

On the same train Hikaru revises the belief that drove her out. Had she stayed she would have gone on thinking she only hurts people; she resolves to be a light of hope and not one that casts shadows (`MD-EV-V12-0015`, `MD-CL-0023@8`). In November she falls in competition for the first time, by forcing a jump that was opening, and scores 71.10 (`MD-PERF-0103`). That night she tells herself that Yodaka is watching and she need only obey. Yodaka is elsewhere, refusing to ease her layout and saying a loss ends it. Hikaru does not hear this (`MD-EV-V12-0038`, `MD-KN-0121`).

## Volume 13: what Hikaru takes back and what she takes

The night Inori Yuitsuka is eliminated, Hikaru Kamisaki waits in the cold, follows her, and finds her hiding in a thicket (`MD-EV-V13-0007`, `MD-VF-0113`). Hikaru sees that Inori's laughter had been for the adults' sake and that this is like herself. So Inori was never Yodaka Jun's likeness (`MD-EV-V13-0008`). What replaces the reading is plainer: Hikaru likes Inori because Inori's tenacity keeps her from being alone (`MD-EV-V13-0009`, `MD-CS-0274`). Hikaru promises to skate the next day for Inori.

To Riley Fox that night Hikaru says more. The music was the piece for becoming Yodaka. Praise never felt hers, because she had only done the jumps he would do. She has never shown skating of her own. She was unafraid because she hid behind Yodaka, and she fears he will stop showing her the road (`MD-EV-V13-0018`, `MD-CS-0276`). This is self-report, given once, to an adult she is asking for help; it is also the most exact account of her ease that the series offers.

The free skate enacts it. Hikaru opens in Yodaka's manner, stops at centre ice, and dances her own part; Tsukasa Akeuraji reads the change as line to curve (`MD-EV-V13-0017`, `MD-VF-0114`, `MD-SK-0081`). She lands a quadruple Lutz, a quadruple toe loop in combination and a quadruple Salchow, each with the raised arms she has kept since she began and which, by Tsukasa's analysis, steady her rotation (`MD-EV-V13-0019`, `MD-SK-0080`). She finds pleasure in choosing her own jumps, rejects in herself the keeping of weakness as a bond, and decides that on the ice she is allowed to be the strongest (`MD-EV-V13-0023`, `MD-CS-0278`). Free skate 156.54, total 227.64, first by more than thirty-six points (`MD-PERF-0108`, `MD-COMP-0016`). To the press she says she will show by winning that the life she chooses is the brightest (`MD-EV-V13-0032`).

The same pages give Hikaru's origin. Yodaka was sent to her by adults as a skating teacher. He refused the name of teacher, told her that her interest was compliance with the adults' wishes and that his skating would never make her happy, and left her free to choose. Hikaru chose him because he named her distress and looked happier on the ice than anyone (`MD-EV-V13-0015`, `MD-EV-V13-0016`, `MD-RT-0091`). A profile page, at a creator's weight, adds that she was orphaned, passed among relatives and institutions, was adopted at five into the house whose name she bears, and is twelve (`MD-EV-V13-0033`). The ledger does not fix the order of that house, the plea of Volume 12, and her arrival at the Sonidori house (`MD-TM-0100`, `MD-TM-0107`). The ledger's V12 projection and the V13 reading call her thirteen; the profile gives twelve. The difference is not resolved here.

## Yodaka Jun: four kinds of statement

**What is shown.** Yodaka Jun cushions a fall in a stairwell and, in the same scene, tells Inori Yuitsuka, then a beginner, that she will never beat Hikaru Kamisaki (`MD-EV-V02-0026`, `MD-EV-V02-0028`). He wounds Sonidori Rio, who calls him a runaway from skating (`MD-EV-V03-0046`). He skates at night better than when he competed, smokes, teaches a backflip by one demonstration, and hands Tsukasa Akeuraji the means to return to performing (`MD-EV-V06-0032`, `MD-EV-V06-0036`). He decides Hikaru's layouts by telephone from elsewhere (`MD-IN-0084`). He names Inori's quadruple before he is told (`MD-EV-V07-0040`), praises Inori's step sequence aloud, and is gone before her score (`MD-EV-V09-0029`, `MD-EV-V09-0036`). He still uses a towel Inori gave him (`MD-EV-V08-0026`).

**What others say.** Sonidori Shin'ichirō: Yodaka could not live off the ice after retiring at twenty; the offer to coach came with private ice; Yodaka asked by letter for secrecy and for Hikaru's daily care; and Sonidori keeps the secret by choice, to protect both (`MD-EV-V06-0027`, `MD-EV-V06-0033`). This is a friend's hearsay, and the sentence giving the main reason for secrecy is cut off. Kyōboku Yutaka: Yodaka corrected himself without video, saw his own career from above, needed no coach, and left before the season ended; the talent needed no adult and the child did, and Kyōboku did not say so (`MD-EV-V10-0034`, `MD-RL-0241`). Takamine Shō, who once taught Yodaka: seven years of compulsory victory is too long, because growth is loss (`MD-EV-V10-0002`, `MD-RL-0223`). Riley Fox: she owes her Olympic title to a rule changed after the sport lost Yodaka, and shelters the arrangement as repayment; Hikaru doubts that is all (`MD-EV-V12-0018`, `MD-RL-0288`). Leonid Sorokin, Yodaka's old choreographer: takes Hikaru's side, tells her she is the one who led a confined dancer out, and urges Yodaka in vain to ease her layout (`MD-EV-V08-0035`, `MD-RL-0181`, `MD-RL-0298`). Tsukasa: a man who tried to live outside the ice and was shut back in (`MD-EV-V06-0033`).

**What Yodaka says of himself.** Very little, and all of it as doctrine or record. On the night ice of Volume 6: ability not proven within the limited time is the same as none; he contracted to coach Hikaru because she had the aptitude; she will win gold at every competition for life as he did; there is an absolute (`MD-EV-V06-0035`, `MD-EV-V06-0038`, `MD-EV-V06-0039`). In the aquarium, to the objection that it is too long: he could (`MD-EV-V10-0002`). In Volume 12: the clauses of the contract, and contempt for Tsukasa and Inori, which sits oddly with his reactions a year earlier and may be for Hikaru's benefit (`MD-RT-0087`). In Volume 13: his reason for stopping.

**What is not known.** Why Yodaka retired; a press conference asks and the panel cuts away (`MD-EV-V06-0035`). Who approached him; the adults of Hikaru's house sent him, by a bonus scene and her memory, and no one is named (`MD-EV-V02-0047`, `MD-IN-0058`). The principal reason for secrecy. His family, on which three colleagues are emphatic and uninformative (`MD-EV-V10-0035`). Whether he knew what Hikaru's body was paying. Whether he wished to be surpassed. Where he is now, and whether he skates.

## The ending, in three parts

On the night of Hikaru Kamisaki's title Yodaka Jun debriefs her on two technical decisions, tells her to finish the present choreography, and then says that from that day he stops coaching her (`MD-EV-V13-0028`). The V13 reading keeps three things apart, and so does this study.

*What Yodaka says.* He is not angry and forbids Hikaru nothing; they are different people. He tried many teachers' ideas, and in the end they were ornament beside the ice. Only what he drew out himself was needed for his last gold. So he let his coaches go, and while he guides Hikaru she cannot go further along the road he walked (`MD-EV-V13-0028`, `MD-EV-V13-0029`, `MD-KN-0128`).

*What Hikaru infers.* That this is why Yodaka hid his coaching: so that a role he does not himself acknowledge could be made never to have existed, and so that he could leave like this. It is her inference. It fits years of concealment better than any reason given before, and he does not confirm it (`MD-CS-0281`, `MD-RT-0090`).

*What remains unknown.* Whether the stated reason is the whole of it. A wish not to be needed, or not to be surpassed, would produce the same act, and the page gives no ground to choose (`MD-CL-0016@12`). Whether the ending is final is not shown (`MD-IN-0132`).

Two further facts frame it without explaining it. A memory returns to Hikaru of Yodaka's first lesson, before boots: he carried her across an empty rink so that the cold struck her skin, said that this was his impulse (衝動) toward the ice, and told her to find her own (`MD-EV-V13-0029`). Yodaka therefore named the cost and the terminus at the start. And he skates one last model, goes out, does not return, and leaves his boots (`MD-EV-V13-0030`, `MD-VF-0117`). On the volume's last pages Hikaru is holding a boot (`MD-EV-V13-0051`); an under-jacket aside, at lower weight, has her keeping them for Yodaka and Riley Fox looking for a way to hold him (`MD-EV-V13-0055`).

The earlier reading of the contract was wrong in a specific way. At Volume 12 it expected withdrawal as a sanction for failure. The withdrawal came as fulfilment, on a win (`MD-RT-0089`, `MD-RV-0212`). Yodaka's response to Hikaru's unilateral change of program was a technical review and no reproach (`MD-CL-0024@7`).

## The rivalry in both directions (MD-PAIR-0005)

**Hikaru toward Inori.** Hikaru Kamisaki has taken Inori Yuitsuka's skating repeatedly: a quadruple after seeing Inori's, Takamine Shō as a teacher to match the step sequence in which Inori beat her, and a novice career ended on the combination that opened Inori's free skate a year before (`MD-RL-0186`, `MD-SK-0066`, `MD-EV-V12-0025`). Two of Hikaru's readings were wrong. She doubted that Inori would come at her in earnest, when Inori was at that hour swearing never to lose again (`MD-KN-0090`). And she took Inori for Yodaka Jun's likeness, which she withdrew herself, in thought and then to Riley Fox (`MD-RT-0092`, `MD-KN-0127`). A third stands unexamined: Hikaru thinks Inori strong because protected, which is the thing Inori cannot forgive in herself (`MD-EV-V12-0015`). At the boundary Hikaru likes Inori for herself, needs her so as not to be alone, and has told her, hand outstretched, that only Hikaru is fit to be Inori's rival (`MD-RL-0300`, `MD-VF-0116`). Hikaru's wish to defeat Inori is not withdrawn.

**Inori toward Hikaru.** Gratitude and aim from the first day (`MD-RL-0014`); chagrin in place of delight after the lost medal (`MD-RL-0234`); and in Volume 11 an idealization. In the leader's chair Inori checks a wish that others fail with the thought that Hikaru would never have it (`MD-EV-V11-0025`, `MD-RL-0266`). That is more than the page shows of Hikaru, who owned her shadow and declined to apologize, and it is not corrected on the page. What is corrected is Inori's judgment of herself: she apologizes in the thicket for having presumed to be Hikaru's rival, and is answered the next day with a summons (`MD-EV-V13-0008`, `MD-EV-V13-0025`). Inori calls Hikaru the light of hope (`MD-RL-0301`), the phrase Hikaru had chosen for herself alone on a train; Inori is not shown to know that.

**At the boundary.** Hikaru and Inori have not met since that free skate. Inori's vow in the warm-up in Beijing includes fighting Hikaru (`MD-EV-V14-0010`). Inori is not shown to know the conversation under the bridge, the contract, or that Yodaka has left; the last has had an occasion to travel and is not shown doing so (`MD-KN-0091`, `MD-KN-0116`, `MD-KN-0129`, `MD-KN-0142`).

## Those who stay, and the limit

What stands against the contract, the V12 reading observed, is not argument but people who offer what it omits. None opposes Yodaka Jun. Sonidori Shin'ichirō could not hold Hikaru Kamisaki and said so; he arranged her leaving from a hospital bed, chose her school, and came to tell her that a spin had improved, at which she was overjoyed (`MD-RL-0242`, `MD-RL-0278`, `MD-RL-0279`). His wife, Ava, told Hikaru she is their daughter whether or not she skates (`MD-RL-0280`). Sonidori Rio refused Hikaru's account of herself and renamed his own aim: to be someone who is not a shadow beside her (`MD-RL-0277`). Rio is the one person who claims to know Hikaru unperformed, and he has been left behind; his claim is untested since (`MD-RL-0276`).

Riley Fox is the fourth, and after Volume 13 the adult responsible. Her position on risk is new to the series' range (`MD-CL-0024@7`). She permitted Hikaru, a growing skater with a recent back injury, three quadruples, once, on an agreement that quadruples will be limited for a time when Hikaru's growth stops, because a skater will not accept talk of peaking until the wish to challenge has been discharged (`MD-EV-V13-0031`, `MD-SK-0082`, `MD-IN-0133`). Riley Fox then promised Sonidori, the former guardian, to protect Hikaru (`MD-RL-0307`). The limit has not yet applied, so the position is stated and not tested.

Sonidori's testimony at that meeting is the strongest counterweight to the volume's own account of Hikaru's independence. Hikaru's free skate, Sonidori says, was the most like Yodaka's of any she has given, though she was trying to move away from Yodaka; the stands said of her what they used to say of Yodaka, that such heat is a last flame; and Sonidori watched Yodaka, his friend, make that heat by burning what Yodaka held dear (`MD-CS-0283`, `MD-RL-0309`). `MD-CL-0025@8` records that neither coach's creed was vindicated by the result. In Volume 15 Tsukasa Akeuraji recognizes the same heat in Inori Yuitsuka and names its cost (`MD-RL-0362`, `MD-CL-0025@9`). Yodaka does not appear; the recognition is Tsukasa's.

## Creator-layer context (fanbooks)

Everything in this section is the author's conception as reported by an editorial writer, dated October 2024 and September 2025 and expressly revisable, or bonus fiction. None of it is a story page, and no trait above rests on it.

Fanbook 01, written before Volumes 12 and 13, says that Hikaru Kamisaki is modelled on an animal of the pack that keeps its rank, minds her position in every group, never defies her two adults, and does not think Yodaka Jun always right (`MD-EV-FB01-0013`). It says that Yodaka smokes partly as self-harm, so that he could not return to competition once he had resolved to stop; that his telephone holds one contact and it is not Hikaru's; and that a pendant Hikaru was looking for in Volume 1 had been thrown away by Yodaka, who regretted it and searched too (`MD-EV-FB01-0014`). The design side thus agrees with the disclosures of Volumes 12 and 13 (`MD-CL-0003@13`, `MD-RT-0117`).

Fanbook 02 describes, for the period before Hikaru's transfer, days arranged round a midnight lesson two or three nights a week, a fear above all of failing to produce at it, daytime practice left undirected on Yodaka's policy, and no friends at school (`MD-EV-FB02-0006`). Of Yodaka it says that he skates alone most nights because he loves skating and owns a room in which he keeps his worn-out boots, which the book likens to a grave (`MD-EV-FB02-0007`). The author says that the rain scene of Volume 9 was meant as the moment Hikaru's smiling mask breaks, that the accusation Hikaru speaks there was first written for Yodaka, and that the idea of growing strong by offering something up is a beautiful composition and not necessarily right (`MD-EV-FB02-0026`, `MD-CL-0025@10`).

None of this explains the boots Yodaka left in Volume 13. It establishes that, in the author's conception, boots are what this man keeps and buries, and that a man who bars his own return is consistent with one who removes himself from a pupil. The page that would connect either to his reason does not exist (`MD-CL-0016@13`, `@14`).

## Open questions

These are carried unchanged from the readings (`MD-OQ-V13-03`, `MD-OQ-V13-04`, `MD-OQ-V15-07`).

1. **Yodaka Jun's motive.** Open beyond his stated reason. Evidence: his own account to someone other than Hikaru Kamisaki; a completed statement of why the coaching was hidden; his conduct if Hikaru loses, or surpasses his record.
2. **Where Yodaka is.** Not given; whether he still skates is not said. Evidence: any reappearance, and what Riley Fox does to keep him.
3. **Hikaru's own impulse.** Yodaka told her to find one; the page has not said what it is. That Inori Yuitsuka keeps her from being alone is a statement about a person, not about the ice. Evidence: a program conceived without Yodaka's model; what Hikaru says skating is for.
4. **The limit on quadruples.** Agreed and not yet applied. Evidence: Hikaru's layouts once her growth stops, and whether she keeps the agreement against her own wish to challenge.
5. **What Hikaru's independence amounts to.** She left Yodaka's manner in form; Sonidori Shin'ichirō, as a witness, says she reproduced its heat. Evidence: how people who knew Yodaka's skating read Hikaru's later performances; whether she is still unafraid without him behind her; what she does after a fall.

## Boundary, omissions and what this study does not claim

The endpoint is `MD-FR-FB02`: the six-minute warm-up before the free skate of the Junior Grand Prix Final, with the result unknown. For Hikaru Kamisaki and Yodaka Jun the effective story endpoint is the close of Volume 13. Nothing after Score 61 is stated or guessed, and no forecast is made of a reunion, a return or a meeting.

Excluded: the anime, English editions, external rules, and the parody chapters. Creator-layer material is confined to the section so headed and to items flagged in the text (Hikaru's early history and age, from a profile; the boots kept, from an aside).

Untested: Hikaru's conduct without Yodaka; Rio's claim to know her; Riley Fox's limit; Inori's idealization of Hikaru; every motive of Yodaka's. The study does not call Yodaka cold or damaged; the V06 reading held both, and later evidence adds an act and no interior. It does not call Hikaru's manner false. It does not say why Yodaka left.
