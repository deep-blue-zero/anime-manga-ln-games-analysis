# Tsukasa Akeuraji — a provisional monograph

This study concerns one character, Tsukasa Akeuraji (`MD-CHAR-0001`), through the combined boundary `MD-FR-FB02`. The story stands in the six-minute warm-up before the free skate of the Junior Grand Prix Final. Its result is not known and nothing here guesses at it. The evidence is the fifteen frozen readings, the two fanbook audits and the seven ledgers, chiefly the [claims ledger](../../03%20Longitudinal%20Ledgers/MEDALIST_CLAIMS_PREDICTIONS_AND_REVISIONS_LEDGER.md) and the [character ledger](../../03%20Longitudinal%20Ledgers/MEDALIST_CHARACTER_DEVELOPMENT_AND_READINESS_LEDGER.md). The study paraphrases throughout and reproduces no dialogue.

## 1. Thesis

Tsukasa Akeuraji is a man who learned in childhood that to want something is to cost someone, and that a person who costs is judged. That remembered history gives a supported account of his difficulty asking for help and his need to repay it. Three features of his coaching meet within that structure; the origin of his perceptual faculty remains unresolved.

The first is his eye. His self-teaching depended on watching, and the faculty is real: he predicts a movement from a posture, reasons from the difference, and reproduces what he has seen once. Outside witnesses attest it from Volume 2 to Volume 12 (`MD-SK-0013`, `MD-SK-0039`, `MD-RT-0088`). That history does not by itself establish how the faculty arose; the later hawk’s-eye comparison is testimony and his own inference (`MD-EV-V10-0034`–`0036`), not proof that his household produced it.

The second is his practice of deciding. Having been steered as a boy by adults who meant well, he explains, sets out options, and declines to overrule a child's stated purpose. The corpus evaluates this favourably, with reasoned bodily restrictions in V03, V04 and V13 and a stated reservation at V15 (`MD-CL-0007@12`). These restrictions do not amount to overruling her purpose.

The third is a creed with a hole in it. He tells children that sacrifice does not make anyone strong and that he will exact none. He has never applied that to himself. He gives everything, counts each of Inori Yuitsuka's losses as his failure, and pares himself afterwards. In Volume 15 he sees this and says it: while denying sacrifice, he had put his faith in his own (`MD-EV-V15-0045`, `MD-CL-0010@16`, `MD-CL-0025@10`).

The argument of this study is that these are one structure and that Volume 15 names it without yet testing it. His practice of helping and the burden he placed on Inori share a supported relation to that history; his perceptual strength enters the structure without a proved childhood origin. A readable face, total labour and a refusal to forbid help explain Inori’s trust and her reading of care as debt. This is an interpretation of conduct, memory and adult appraisal, not an exclusive genealogy of every strength or of her trust. The change he states in the stairwell is aimed at the premise and not only at the words. Whether it holds is the one test outstanding, and the boundary falls before it (`MD-RD-0073`).

## 2. How the understanding moved

Three claims carry Tsukasa Akeuraji, and their versions record a reading that was repeatedly too early.

`MD-CL-0010` began as an open question: what does his failed aspiration do in his coaching? It stayed unresolved through five versions, because his denial that he coaches to soothe regret was neither refuted nor confirmed. At version 6 the history under the regret was told, and the question became whether a purpose he calls his own, and ties to Inori Yuitsuka's trust, can be held apart from her. Versions 8 and 9 recorded a stated answer, that his work is no sacrifice whatever the result. Version 10 recorded his first admission of loss, and version 12 that he had staked his pride as a coach on her gold. Version 13 judged that his declaration on the bench removed the premise of that stake. Version 15 withdrew the judgement: the stake was restated on the afternoon of the final and answered only that night. Version 16 adds the author's prior statement of intent and no evidence.

`MD-CL-0004` moved from practicable cues (version 1), to a perceptual skill attested from outside (2), to a disclosed method of allocation, logging and layout (5), to devices found when instruction stops arriving (9, 10), to listening first and a story in place of reassurance (11), to the analysis of feeling (12). Version 13 records the method criticized at its root.

`MD-CL-0025` began as a contest between two coaches, Tsukasa and Yodaka Jun, over certainty and proof (1), gained a second axis in what effort is (3), was redrawn as a likeness (5), was decided on the scoreboard and dissolved when Yodaka left (8), and ends with Tsukasa revising his own creed (9, 10).

Across all three, Tsukasa's statements ran ahead of his conduct, and the reading several times took a statement for a settlement. That is the main caution this study carries into its own conclusions.

Four features of this sequence matter for the argument.

**The tellings change colour.** In V01 the history is a failure that disqualifies him from teaching (`MD-CS-0001`). In V04 it is first wasted years and then, by the volume's end, an ally (`MD-CS-0049`, `MD-CS-0057`). In V06 it is the lack of a verdict. In V07 it is clinging on by changing the dream's shape (`MD-CS-0128`). In V09 Kōhei supplies the terms of a staked proof, and Tsukasa draws from it a doctrine: sacrifice is the self-harm of someone blinded by inferiority, and one can pay everything and lose (`MD-EV-V09-0046`). In V10 it is a weapon he never used (`MD-CS-0214`). In V13 it is a story of kind adults, a wrong verdict and an obedient boy. In V15 the same episode is retold with nothing judged in advance, and the facts move: the prohibition was advice with no rule behind it, he hid, they would have helped, they apologized, and the verdict that he was in the way was his own (`MD-RT-0106`). Nothing in the V13 account was false. It had been sorted. The V15 reading takes this retelling as the volume's demonstration of its own rule against sorting people into guilty and innocent (`MD-CL-0031@1`).

**Each telling has an audience.** Inori hears the V13 version. Kago hears the house. Of the V15 material Inori is told one sentence, that Tsukasa and his brother tried to erase their father's suffering in his place (`MD-KN-0149`). She is not shown to know why his last championship was his last (`MD-KN-0150`).

**Witnessed past and self-account differ in weight.** Kōhei's memory (V09), Hitomi's account to Rio (`MD-EV-V04-0027`) and Kago's statements (V05, V15) are testimony by others. The house, the circle and the motive for skating are Tsukasa's own recollection, and Kanayumi in the same volume warns that memory is edited each time it is recalled (`MD-EV-V15-0022`). The corpus holds them as HIGH that he remembers so, and no higher.

**The lateness is itself evidence.** Tsukasa does not tell Inori that the rink of V12 was his own (`MD-EV-V12-0030`). He worked three years for the Kago family without saying that he skated. A creator aside in V07 has him flinch at a question about his teacher, Takamine (`MD-EV-V07-0017`). The reticence is on the page in unrelated settings before V15 explains it.

## 4. What the house explains, and what it does not

The V15 reading states that much of what the ledgers had recorded as Tsukasa Akeuraji's character follows from that room without strain. The inference is strong where the conduct was recorded independently, before the cause was known.

**Money, help and debt.** In V01 he refuses Kago Kōichi's funding and resists returning to the household as a burden (`MD-CS-0016`, `MD-RL-0018`). In V07 he buys a harness for the club with his own money, and in V08 he is found working nights on cracked ribs to pay for it and is ashamed to be seen (`MD-CS-0121`, `MD-CS-0144`). In V15 the younger man will skate only on what he earns, calls asking his parents unfilial, refuses a reduced fee, and regrets his one request the moment it is declined (`MD-CS-0321`, `MD-CS-0323`). When help comes his thought is that it must be repaid with results. This is one pattern in four volumes, shown in conduct and not only in report. `MD-CL-0017@11` reads it as the obstacle under a second pattern: he asks freely for technical knowledge and has never asked about himself.

**The verdict he wanted and the verdict he gave.** The boy of V06 wanted one thing, to be told whether he had talent, as permission to go on. What he says to the crying Inori Yuitsuka in V01, before he has assessed her skating, is that she is already remarkable (`MD-EV-V01-0007`, `MD-RT-0031`). In V07 Inori gives him, half asleep and not knowing its weight, the sentence he lacked: that he was born to skate (`MD-EV-V07-0016`, `MD-RT-0040`). The reading in V15 adds that his reason for skating at all, worth without usefulness, is his father's sentence turned over. That last step is the reading's inference; the page gives the two sentences and does not join them.

**Self-concept.** On the evening of the short program, turned away from the press zone, he feels that he has no place except as a coach (`MD-EV-V15-0015`, `MD-CS-0318`). One thought on one evening abroad is a state, not a trait. It has support elsewhere. Hitomi told Rio in V04 that Tsukasa had no record and a need to make the ice his place (`MD-CS-0053`). In V05 he defines his work as invisible and wholly contained in Inori's performance (`MD-CS-0070`). In V06 he offers the coach's seat to Hitomi and has to be pushed into it (`MD-CS-0092`). In V12 he cannot acknowledge himself as a coach until Inori wins (`MD-CS-0266`). Against it stand his statements that coaching is his dream (V06) and that he likes his life (`MD-EV-V15-0033`), and his open joy at the boards in V14 (`MD-CS-0309`). The fair summary is that he values the work as his own and has had nowhere else to stand. The stairwell promise addresses the second half.

**Motives.** He has given four first-person accounts of why he coaches. In V01 he denies that it compensates for regret (`MD-RT-0003`). In V06 it is his dream and his use of his possibility, conditioned on being entrusted by Inori. In V13 it is his dream and no substitute, and he resolved on it before he met her, so that no one would be left as he was (`MD-EV-V13-0043`, `0044`). In V15 he says he wanted to save a child who could not step into that world, and assents, with the word probably, to Kago's reading that he wanted to become the adult his boyhood self had wished for (`MD-EV-V15-0039`, `0045`). These need not conflict. The last is the first to name a need of his own, and it is partly another man's diagnosis. The reading keeps one limit. In his own memory the boy wished for someone to take him into that world. In Kago's reading he wished for an adult who never breaks. The first is what he did in V01. The second is what he has tried to be since (`MD-RT-0109`).

**Fears.** Those shown are of misleading a pupil without credentials (V01, V04); for Inori's body (he could hardly stand on hearing of her growing pains and hid it on the telephone, `MD-CS-0202`); of costing Hitomi her career (`MD-RL-0357`); and of being consoled, which he feels as shame (`MD-CS-0190`). A fear of blame is alleged against him by Hikaru in the rain in V09; in V15 he remembers the charge and grants that he knew the feeling (`MD-EV-V15-0049`).

**What the house does not explain.** It does not account for his eye, which Jakuzure hedged as a product of ice dance (`MD-EV-V02-0041`) and which he later takes for a faculty shared with Yodaka (`MD-CS-0214`); that identification rests on another man's metaphor and one jump (`MD-CL-0010@10`). It does not explain his exuberance or his delight in his pupils. And the corpus has nothing on his family now beyond a sentence that there is no home to go back to (`MD-EV-V15-0030`, `MD-OQ-V15-05`).

## 5. Method

The reconstruction contract asks that a coach's technical diagnosis, pedagogy, perception of the athlete's needs, decisions and the athlete's responses be kept apart. They separate cleanly in this case, and Tsukasa Akeuraji's reliability differs sharply between them.

### 5.1 Technical diagnosis

His diagnostic record is the strongest part of the evidence. In V02 Jakuzure, a coach from a rival club, watches him work and names the skill: he predicts the motion from the posture and reasons from the difference (`MD-SK-0013`). In V04 he skates a program after one viewing, and a hostile Rio judges his skating to be Yodaka's image in everything but the jumps (`MD-EV-V04-0025`). In V06 he improves a singles take-off within a session by watching Yodaka and Sonidori, two Olympic medalists (`MD-SK-0039`). In V10 he traces Inori Yuitsuka's failing triple Lutz to a take-off edge laid too deep to draw the free leg through, a fault produced by the very depth of edge he had trained, and refuses a flatter edge as a shortcut that would have to be unlearned (`MD-SK-0064`, `MD-CL-0004@9`). In V12 Riley Fox confirms the reproducing gift (`MD-RT-0088`).

The errors are instructive. His first diagnosis of the double Axel, that Inori lacked strength, was wrong for months and was overturned by her own evidence (`MD-SK-0020`, `MD-CS-0047`). He did not see the shin injury of V03, which Kanayumi's trained eye is later said to have noticed beforehand (`MD-RT-0073`). His forecast of the field in V08 was wrong about Suzu, the one skater he tried to predict (`MD-CL-0004@7`). In V15, watching Daria's short program, he judges that he would change her music or layout; Kyōboku, who knew her reason, backed her, and Daria won the segment (`MD-EV-V15-0004`, `0005`; `MD-CL-0024@9`). His eye is for bodies in motion. On what a skater means to do, he knows only what he has been told.

### 5.2 Allocation of training: edges and steps first

His first strategic choice was made in V01 and has been the most consequential. With two months and a double jump not ready, he and Inori chose foundation and a spin over the jump (`MD-SK-0004`–`0007`, `MD-CL-0008@1`). In V03, with the same doubles as three rivals and fewer than a fourth, she outscored all four on a step sequence a judge called the best in the field, and lost to harder content that was also clean (`MD-CL-0008@3`; `MD-SK-0015`, `MD-SK-0016`). In V06 the method is stated by others: practice weighted to skating and steps by a standard carried from ice dance, converted into execution marks on every element (`MD-SK-0035`). In V07 the jump specialist Uobuchi attributes her readiness for harder jumps to that training (`MD-SK-0041`, `MD-RT-0039`). In V09 her step sequence is called at the top level, by an older skater's account the first for a novice in eleven years, while both leaders receive level two (`MD-SK-0057`). In V14 it is level four again (`MD-SK-0085`). In V15 Baek Horang's coach, with no interest in crediting him, reads Inori's margin over Baek Horang from a step-sequence level and spin marks and calls Tsukasa a strategist (`MD-SK-0089`, `MD-CL-0008@14`).

The costs are on the page too. Gori judged in V06 that the time spent on completeness left two required triples untrained, and Rio said he could not bear the method daily (`MD-RL-0137`, `MD-EV-V06-0007`). The jump Inori skipped as a novice became compulsory in the junior short program (`MD-SK-0063`). Component marks are seldom printed, so the mechanism is largely the work's own attribution. And on the day Baek Horang's coach credits him, Inori is overtaken by Daria, whose step sequence is also level four.

### 5.3 Teaching jumps without being a jumper

He holds a beginner's badge in singles and says so without defence (`MD-CS-0049`). His answer to that limit is the pattern `MD-CL-0017` tracks: he borrows. He asks Jakuzure for method in V02, invites Nachi for jumps in V04, and in V07 brings in Uobuchi, a harness specialist, buys the same tool, learns it by watching and then under instruction, and drives overnight to reach Uobuchi after his own fall with the pole has cracked his ribs and cost Inori the Lutz (`MD-SK-0041`, `MD-SK-0042`, `MD-SK-0044`). What he adds himself at the decisive moment is not mechanics. He tells Inori that wanting success is not what produces it and that how finely she feels is the key to control; then he asks her to teach him what a quadruple feels like and to jump once more for a word (`MD-EV-V07-0027`). The claims ledger treats this as the centre of his method in his own words (`MD-CL-0004@6`). In V10 the same indirection recovers the Lutz: when instruction stops arriving he sets a contest in which he must learn the jump beside her, and she lands it clean (`MD-SK-0064`, `MD-RL-0227`). He lands a double Lutz within the hour (`MD-SK-0065`).

### 5.4 Regime after growth, and feeling taken apart

Inori's growing pains change the method. From V11 attempts at a new element are rationed, each failure is examined before the next, rest days and conditioning are part of the event, and a new combination enters the layout only on three landings in three under Kōhei's cautious test (`MD-SK-0067`, `MD-SK-0068`, `MD-SK-0072`). Tsukasa states that reducing needless sacrifice is itself effort (`MD-CS-0227`). He also divides the adults' functions: Kanayumi, the trainer he obtained by kneeling until she agreed; Kōhei, a second coach who examines new elements; Hitomi as head coach; and himself in the part of never braking (`MD-IN-0114`, `MD-CS-0240`). That last assignment is a decision about himself that matters later.

In V14 the habit of analysis reaches expression. Told that Inori's Presentation mark has room, he does not send her to her own memories. He takes the bodily signs of a feeling apart, enlarges them for distance, builds them into the choreography, demonstrates beside her and studies Hitomi and Kōhei as they dance the piece (`MD-SK-0084`, `MD-EV-V14-0026`, `0027`). He states an aim that comes from his history: to learn how far instruction can pass the wall people call sense, since he was once told he had none (`MD-CS-0306`, `MD-CL-0010@14`). He concedes that the method depends partly on the skater's sense, and its specific effect cannot be isolated in the printed mark.

### 5.5 Pedagogy: what lands and what does not

His teaching manner is explanation: options laid out, a scoring reason for each refusal, analogies, video, a pupil who completes the reasoning herself (`MD-CL-0004@3`). It works while Inori is listening. Three times it is shown not to arrive. At the V10 camp she hears analysis as correction and hurries (`MD-RL-0226`). In V11 his praise falls flat while Kanayumi's identical judgement registers, and Inori realizes at a competition that rules he had explained since February had never gone in (`MD-EV-V11-0009`, `0024`). On the platform in V13 he gives her every reassurance she needs, and she hears nothing after his first sentence (`MD-EV-V13-0039`).

What works when explanation fails is repeated across the pair’s recorded work from Volume 2 through Volume 14: a frame instead of a statement. An off-ice rehearsal and a permission to fall in V02 (`MD-CS-0021`). A frozen lake and a release from tomorrow's outcome in V07 (`MD-RL-0145`). A game in V10. An image of breathing a hall's air in V11 (`MD-RL-0259`). On the bench in V13, a story about himself in place of a second attempt at reassurance (`MD-CL-0004@11`). The step he lacked was supplied by Gori: listen before speaking. He applied it within the day, stopped defending himself in mid-sentence, and let her finish (`MD-RL-0315`). In V14 he explains his own face the moment she misreads it, unprompted (`MD-EV-V14-0004`).

### 5.6 Perception of her needs

Here his record is mixed, and the mixture has a shape. He is quick on what Inori does and slow on what she believes. He sees in V02 that she blames herself as hard as she strives (`MD-RL-0038`). He sees within a day at the V10 camp that she is treating him as someone to show results to (`MD-CS-0210`). He hears in V11 that her assent is empty and stops the session (`MD-CS-0238`). He names her self-punishment to himself on the platform (`MD-CS-0291`).

Against that, he did not know for five volumes why she hurried in V04. He has never been told that she believed she had ended his own skating; that form of the belief, on the page since V09, is neither voiced to him nor withdrawn (`MD-KN-0147`). In V13 he first read her deceit about school as growing autonomy and let weeks pass (`MD-CS-0286`). In V15 he learned from Kanayumi, and only then from Inori, what Inori carries. Kanayumi's explanation is that the obstacle was his own conduct: what Inori read in his face was true (`MD-EV-V15-0021`). His difficulty was not inattention. He was the thing being read.

### 5.7 Decisions

`MD-CL-0007` evaluates his decisions by whether he listens, offers practicable options and tells the truth about uncertainty, separately from whether Inori wins. The record supports a favourable evaluation with marked exceptions. He orders rest against her wish in V03 and states that catching up fastest is not the aim (`MD-CS-0039`). He caps her volume in V04 on health grounds. He declines an escalation at the boards and gives the scoring reason (`MD-CS-0036`). He will not relocate her in V11 as a proof of seriousness and will not answer an offer without her and her family (`MD-CS-0245`). In V13 he states the one prohibition of the series, on missed school and unregulated volume, where her wish had become harm to her body (`MD-CL-0007@11`).

The exceptions run toward risk, and each time at her appeal or on his own ambition. He built a qualifier for gold alone (`MD-CS-0098`). He admitted an unlogged quadruple on a far thinner test than the practice record he had relied on a month before (`MD-SK-0045`). In V08 he reasoned his way to the safe layout and reversed it when she asked to be believed in once more (`MD-CS-0167`). He left her coachless on an impulse in V03 and criticized himself for it (`MD-CS-0035`). Absolute promises recur beside his creed that nothing on the ice is absolute: V01, V05, V06 and the afternoon of V15 (`MD-CS-0014`, `MD-CS-0080`, `MD-EV-V06-0011`, `MD-EV-V15-0010`).

### 5.8 Her responses

Inori takes his method into herself. She derives a rule from his demonstration and uses his cue under fatigue (`MD-RL-0046`, `MD-RL-0206`). She asks him to set out both options as he taught her before she chooses (`MD-RL-0149`). She names him as the one who taught her to decide only when convinced (`MD-RL-0270`). She also overrides his plan in mid-program in V09, hides her practice in V10, discounts his praise in V11, deceives him and rages at him in V13, and in V15 tells him that being told she need not strain is what she hates (`MD-EV-V15-0048`). Her independence is partly his product, and it is turned on him. That is consistent with the method's stated aim and is not evidence of its failure.

### 5.9 The criticism of Volume 15

Kanayumi's criticism reaches the root. In December Gori had taught him that an adult's face is a reward or a punishment to a child, and he took it as an instruction to govern his face. He proposes in V15 to apply it harder. Kanayumi answers that concealment suited a younger child; this one sees for herself; what she sees is true; to hide a fact from someone who knows it is to lie, and the lie will fail. She calls his way of coaching service with his whole strength and suggests he change it. She adds that the same readable face is why a child who probes adults could trust him at all, and calls it his greatest strength (`MD-EV-V15-0021`, `0023`; `MD-RT-0105`).

He accepts it, and the stairwell shows the acceptance in four acts. He asks a direct question. He listens to the end. He abandons the message he had prepared, and tested on Kanayumi by telephone, when he sees that it would overrule Inori. He checks twice whether he was understood, with December in mind (`MD-CL-0004@13`). What he offers is a commitment about his own conduct in place of a reassurance about hers.

## 6. The creed against sacrifice, and its holder

From V06 the series sets Tsukasa Akeuraji against Yodaka Jun, the champion who made him skate. Yodaka holds that worth is what is proven within the limit and that one grows strong by what one gives up. Tsukasa answers that there is no absolute on the ice and, to Yodaka's pupil Hikaru Kamisaki, that his own work is no sacrifice and that he will not make Inori Yuitsuka pay one (`MD-EV-V08-0037`). In the rain in V09 he grounds the creed in his own history: he paid everything and lost (`MD-RT-0059`).

The corpus has never taken this at face value. `MD-CL-0025@1` noted that the two men share a premise about proof: Tsukasa teaches that strength must be proven to be increased and calls a medal absolutely necessary. `MD-CL-0010@8` noted that he said his work was no sacrifice while hiding cracked ribs and a night job. In V11 he measures Inori by Riley Fox's first season and calls fourth place his own second failure (`MD-EV-V11-0045`). In V12 he ties his pride to Inori's gold. In V15 he vows that a third failure is unthinkable. Hikaru's objection in V09, that his doctrine protects him from asking of a child what broke him, was never answered directly (`MD-CL-0010@9`).

Volume 15 turns the argument on its spokesman. He held the creed for children and practised its opposite on himself (`MD-RT-0112`). Having seen it, he does not then convert Inori. He recognizes in her the heat he first saw in Yodaka on a screen, says that it is one with great suffering, and declines to call it a mistake. What he keeps is narrower than what he preached: that suffering should be chosen, and should be one's own (`MD-CL-0025@9`, `MD-RL-0362`). Yodaka does not appear; the recognition is Tsukasa's alone, and Yodaka's reason for leaving coaching in V13 remains unknown beyond what he stated.

## 7. Relations

**Inori Yuitsuka.** Only what bears on Tsukasa Akeuraji is given here. The bench of V13 was real. He told her his history and declared that coaching her is his dream and stands in for nothing. She repeated it accurately. Each then misread it. He took his own words for a pledge that settled the matter. She took them as a part entrusted to her, and was glad for that reason (`MD-RT-0104`, `MD-CL-0027@7`, `MD-CL-0029@6`, `MD-RV-0259`, `MD-RV-0261`). The mechanism between them is established: she reads adults' faces and takes the cause on herself (`MD-CL-0028@5`). The hypothesis that this capacity was formed under hardship in her early years is not adopted, and nothing here relies on it. Three statements in V15 must stay distinct. Kanayumi's phrase, that Inori skates at the sacrifice of his life, is Kanayumi's inference. Inori's own statement to him is narrower and dated by her to middle school: it concerns her mother Nozomi's and his present labour and distress. And it goes further than any adult guessed: she will not be relieved of the weight (`MD-EV-V15-0017`, `0048`). His address to her is itself a piece of method. He uses her given name with a polite suffix, いのりさん, even in urgent instruction, where a coach might drop it (V01 reading; `MD-EV-V03-0007`).

**Hitomi Takamine.** His former partner and now his head coach. She corrects him throughout: his loud gestures, his impulse in V03, his attempt to give away the coach's seat (`MD-RL-0040`, `MD-RL-0054`, `MD-RL-0138`). Her diagnosis in V04 is that he moves for others and cannot move for himself (`MD-EV-V04-0010`). In their competitive years she told him that half his frantic work only hurt him and asked at whom it was aimed (`MD-RL-0316`). V15 discloses his fear of costing her a career and her embrace on the night he believes he did (`MD-RL-0356`, `MD-RL-0357`). What she now thinks of that night is not given.

**Kago Kōichi and Meiko.** The relation ran through four stages: employer of a schoolboy, a couple who could not sponsor him, a household that took him in, and a bereaved man who pressed him toward the ice show (`MD-RT-0108`). In V05 Kago releases him from the debt in words. In V15 Kago reverses it, counting what he failed to do for the boy as his own debt and asking to carry the regret (`MD-RL-0086`, `MD-RL-0352`). Kago's rule, no trial of good and evil, is what lets Tsukasa tell his childhood without marking anyone (`MD-EV-V15-0034`). Meiko taught him, at twenty-three, that a problem shared may be solved at no one's expense (`MD-RL-0350`). She died on the night of his failure (`MD-RL-0351`).

**Takamine Shō.** As Tsukasa's teacher, Takamine gave him the doctrine that answers his own case years before he could use it: supporters take part in the sport and are not offerings to a skater; resolve to burden someone, or give up properly; do not let skating make your life unhappy (`MD-EV-V15-0026`, `MD-RL-0348`). Tsukasa refused the loan and the reduced fee (`MD-RL-0349`).

**Kōhei and Juna.** Kōhei keeps Tsukasa's history and, from V10, is the colleague who brakes: he demands conditions of success and sets the test (`MD-RL-0201`, `MD-RL-0256`). In V07 Tsukasa heard his caution and decided the other way (`MD-RL-0158`). Juna taught him by ridicule and is, years later, a source of advice on teaching expression (`MD-RL-0335`, `MD-RL-0336`).

**Gori and Kanayumi.** Two colleagues correct Tsukasa on the same subject a month apart, and the second bounds the first. Gori: a coach's face is a reward or a punishment; listen first (`MD-RL-0314`). Kanayumi: governing the face is now a lie; talk without concealment; do not compare sufferings; tell someone your history (`MD-RL-0346`). He protests each time and acts on it within hours (`MD-RL-0315`, `MD-RL-0347`).

**Jakuzure and Rio.** Jakuzure named Tsukasa's eye, told him not to hurry, covered for him and rebuked him (`MD-RL-0036`, `MD-RL-0052`). Rio arrived contemptuous of a coach with no record and left calling him his own (`MD-RL-0061`, `MD-RL-0071`). What Tsukasa gave Rio was recognition and demonstration; the technical advice was not new to the boy (`MD-SK-0026`).

**Yodaka Jun.** The origin of Tsukasa's skating and his declared opponent from V02. In V06 Yodaka judged that Tsukasa knew his ability was rare, denied him the word talent, and offered him a way back to performing (`MD-RL-0116`). Tsukasa took from Yodaka both the wish to show worth without usefulness and the belief that no adult is needed (`MD-RL-0362`). By V12 Yodaka's stated view of him is contempt, which may be a pose (`MD-RL-0292`).

## 8. Body, ordinary behaviour and speech

Tsukasa Akeuraji is tall; the circle's advice that tall skaters cannot jump was aimed at him, and a margin note in the author's voice corrects it (`MD-EV-V15-0036`). He treats his body as an instrument at others' disposal. He takes a fall unbroken so that a pole will not strike Inori Yuitsuka, asks first whether she is hurt, minimizes cracked ribs, hoists her again within the week and works nights on them (`MD-CS-0123`, `MD-CS-0131`, `MD-CS-0144`). In Yokohama he had a back that would not mend while he worked nights, and passed a test only once he could sleep (`MD-SK-0091`). In V11 he is visibly tired (`MD-CS-0227`). He carries Inori at the lake in V07 and up twenty floors in V15. His stamina is shown as large and as spent without accounting.

Ordinary behaviour is recorded mostly in comic registers and is weighed so. He is exuberant at the boards and has to be told by Hitomi that his gestures are loud; Kanayumi checks him for romping with Inori abroad (`MD-RL-0040`, `MD-CS-0229`). He praises so freely that his praise loses value (`MD-EV-V11-0009`). He is mortified by a replay of his own celebration (`MD-CS-0309`). He lives in Kago's house and does its laundry (`MD-EV-V02-0032`). He is curt and self-deprecating before Juna, a clubmate who became a performer (`MD-CS-0140`).

On speech the readings record address and register, never sound. He keeps courteous address to a child under pressure; with Kago his self-reference and refusals are blunter (V01 reading). The V14 side chapter gives the manner an origin at a lower weight, a vow made against Juna's example. A fanbook gives an external origin, noted in section 11.

Courteous address and grammatical politeness differ. To Inori he uses 俺 and いのりさん with plain instructions and contractions; to Yodaka he can use 貴方 with formal accountable clauses (`MD-EV-V02-0028`, `MD-EV-V06-0037`). The V14 remembered club anecdote mixes slang with polite morphology and frames equality as a vow, not a perfectly achieved trait. The [speech/interlocutor audit](../../07%20Audits%20and%20Handoffs/MEDALIST_JAPANESE_SPEECH_AND_INTERLOCUTOR_AUDIT_THROUGH_V15.md) supplies the located contrasts, creator-layer presentation change and counterexamples. No acoustic delivery or operational rule follows.

## 9. How the manga frames him

**The face.** The series' motif of seeing and being seen (`MD-MOT-0005`) settles on Tsukasa Akeuraji's face from V10. Gori guesses that Inori Yuitsuka moves by adults' faces; Tsukasa worries in V11 that his dismay showed; in V13 a face is called reward or punishment; in V14 Inori misreads it and he explains; in V15 Kanayumi calls it both the instrument of the burden and his greatest strength. The page that sets the stake draws it as dark close-ups of his eyes, with Inori small behind him, looking (`MD-VF-0136`).

**Hands and carrying.** Contact marks the shared undertaking from the first fist bump (`MD-MOT-0001`). The drawstrings of his hood become Inori's tether before a skate; she grips them in V05 and he throws them to her in V08 (`MD-VF-0044`, `MD-RL-0169`). The harness is contact through a tool that injures the one who holds. In V15 carrying is made literal before it is discussed, and it is contact she asks to end (`MD-VF-0144`).

**The maze and the eye above.** `MD-MOT-0014` draws a skater running through a maze and a coach looking down. In V10 Tsukasa places himself inside the walls with a self-made map, having had no guide. In V15 Kanayumi extends the figure: supporters stand in a maze of their own and need a third party's sight (`MD-VF-0139`). The motif states his limit exactly. He can see Inori; he could not see himself.

**Black and white.** The jacket of V15 shows him alone, hands clasped at his mouth, under a crow's wing and claws (`MD-VF-0133`). `MD-MOT-0017` gathers the volume's crows, Kago's sorting into black and white, a single white bird, a costume that turns from black to red and not to white, and the closing captions of a boy with a ruler under crows and a girl among doves. The cover puts him under the sign of accusation. The volume's answer is not acquittal. It is the original colour, which includes small faults (`MD-EV-V15-0034`).

**Devices of withholding.** His father's speech is a blur until no one is to be judged (`MD-VF-0140`). His wish to skate is a page of search queries (`MD-VF-0141`). The confession ends on two pages of a lake without figures (`MD-VF-0143`). The form delays the reader’s access to the remembered speech until it returns legibly; this is a disclosure interval, not a measurement of elapsed story time.

## 10. One day in Beijing: the tension the boundary leaves open

Four acts fall within one afternoon and night, and they do not agree.

In the afternoon, with the title in reach, Tsukasa Akeuraji vows that the gold he twice failed to deliver will be delivered and that a third failure of his responsibility is unthinkable. Inori Yuitsuka stands behind him and watches (`MD-EV-V15-0010`). He has kept such a tally before: a complete defeat in V09, a second failure in V11, a pride withheld in V12.

In the evening he is told what that conduct does. He first proposes to hide it better, then calls his own thought that Inori minds too much a cowardly phrase, then sees that having said the words in December let him treat the matter as closed (`MD-EV-V15-0024`). With Kago he agrees that taking the whole blame was how he stopped thinking. Late that night he formulates it: he had put his faith in his own sacrifice (`MD-EV-V15-0045`).

Next he carries Inori up twenty floors on his back over her protest, lists his reasons, and tells her that one must prepare to excess for gold (`MD-EV-V15-0047`). The page does not comment.

Then, on the landing, he listens, sets his prepared message aside, declines to deny her wish to carry, and promises three things: to audition for the ice show again some day, to remain her coach with all his strength, and not to be unhappy if she does not win. He says his problem is his own to solve, that if she wins for his life she will be living his life, and asks her to choose only the suffering she can call her own, binding himself to the same and to asking for help (`MD-EV-V15-0050`, `MD-CS-0332`). She agrees after a pause, is overjoyed that she will see him skate, and withdraws nothing. He does not know whether it reached her (`MD-EV-V15-0051`).

Two readings of the carrying are available, and the evidence does not choose. It may be the old conduct persisting an hour after the insight: devotion expressed through his body, against her stated wish, with gold as the reason. Or it may be an ordinary precaution by a coach whose skater must sleep and jump a quadruple in the morning, with three lifts out of order. Both fit the page. What tells slightly toward the first is that she protests and he overrides, and that his stated justification repeats the afternoon's logic. What tells toward the second is that the reasons are sound and that he sets her down when she asks.

The reasons for caution about the promise are specific. He has made first-person declarations before, in V06, V08 and V13, and each coexisted with contrary conduct. He was taught this lesson at twenty and at twenty-three by people he loved, acted on it once, and went back (V15 reading). The promise is for some day. The diagnosis is partly Kago's, and Tsukasa's assent carries the word probably. The reasons for taking it seriously are also specific. For the first time he alters a premise and not a sentence: a life of his own on the ice would remove the fact that his fulfilment rests on her results, which no statement could remove while it stayed true. He has a cause for the pattern, in his own memory. He has told his history to one person and asked another for help about himself, which `MD-CL-0017@11` records as new. And he tested his plan on a third party and heard its risk.

What would count as the change holding, on evidence the series could supply:

- after a result that is not gold, conduct that differs from V09, V11 and V13: no tally of his failures, no paring of food, sleep or rest, no managed face, and plain talk with her about what he feels;
- an unprompted request for help about himself;
- a concrete step toward the audition, or an honest account to her of why not yet;
- a change in the total labour Kanayumi named, visible in how his work is divided.

What would count against it:

- a third failure counted and carried as before, especially if she is shown reading it;
- concealment resumed, or the promise treated as settled because it was said, which is the error he diagnosed in himself;
- the audition dropped without a word to her the first time it competes with her schedule.

A win would discriminate little. `MD-CL-0010@2` observed long ago that shared success cannot separate his interest from hers, and the promise not to be unhappy can only be tested by the case it names. A further question sits beside this one and is Inori's, not Tsukasa's: whether her wish to carry is changed by a promise that removes one of its premises. `MD-OQ-V15-02` and `MD-OQ-V15-04` hold both open. This study does not forecast either.

## 11. Creator-layer context from the fanbooks

Everything in this section is the author's conception as reported by an editorial writer, dated October 2024 (Fanbook 01) or September 2025 (Fanbook 02) and expressly liable to change. None of it is a story page, and no trait above rests on it.

**Fanbook 01.** Tsukasa Akeuraji's data sheet gives him as the second of four brothers, with both parents living; the character ledger carries an erratum to that effect, and V15's story pages agree, with one elder and two younger brothers (`MD-EV-FB01-0009`). The same box dates his muscle training to once dropping his partner. A box on the rink clerk Sekoma says that Sekoma helps Inori Yuitsuka out of regret for a poor boy who came constantly and for whom he did nothing (`MD-EV-FB01-0020`). The box does not name the boy. That the boy is Tsukasa is an inference of the audit, supported by V15's scene at that rink's counter and stated by no source (`MD-KN-0156`, `MD-RT-0115`). A box on the family of Kago Kōichi reports that the series was first conceived around Inori and Kago's daughter Yō, and that Tsukasa, the adult coach, was created after an editor asked what the adults were doing (`MD-EV-FB01-0012`, `MD-RT-0116`). This is a statement of design. It bears on why the work examines adult responsibility so closely and on nothing in his psychology. One box says Meiko saw at once that the boy skated and that the family backed him quietly. V15 has Kago say that they first learned of it in Yokohama, and Meiko say only that she had guessed he liked it (`MD-EV-FB01-0010`, `MD-KN-0155`). The conflict is one of degree. The story governs, and the difference is kept.

**Fanbook 02.** In a conversation printed before the chapters of V15 were collected, the author says that Tsukasa, whatever he says, gave up the skater's road and made himself forward-looking by force for Inori's sake; that he has not truly accepted his past self; and that the series is to be his journey toward coming to like himself (`MD-EV-FB02-0030`, `MD-KN-0159`, `MD-RT-0118`). This corroborates as intent what the pages of V15 show. It is not an event, it cannot certify that the pages realize it, and it is not extended here past the boundary: it says nothing about what he does next. The author also says that the boy wished Yodaka Jun would be his coach and would tell him whether he had talent, and that hearing something of the kind at last in V06 he felt it had come too late (`MD-EV-FB02-0023`, `MD-KN-0158`). V15's story page leaves the wished-for someone unnamed, and this study does the same in its own voice. The author says Inori's remark at the lake in V07 was built as an affirmation of his having been born, apart from skill or record (`MD-EV-FB02-0024`, `MD-RT-0120`); a collaborator finds the same remark cruel. Of the rain scene of V09 the author says the creed of sacrifice is a beautiful composition and not necessarily right (`MD-EV-FB02-0026`, `MD-RT-0119`). A feature on his daily life in the period of V05 to V09 has him living at Kago's, caring for Yō, taking night traffic work when short, holding back from more work at Kago's firm as presuming too much, and cutting his sleep, which the box itself calls not good (`MD-EV-FB02-0004`). That agrees with story-page conduct in V07, V08 and V11 and adds no new kind of fact. A sketchbook note gives him a wish to repay Inori (`MD-EV-FB02-0028`). His courteous address is traced to an editor's finding that Tsukasa as drawn in the first chapter was a little frightening. And a boxed timeline says he left the hobby circle because relations worsened. V15 shows concealment, a reproach, an apology the next day and a courteous leave-taking. The timeline is the kind of sorted summary that V15 takes apart, and the story governs.

## 12. Why no operational reconstruction model is offered

The reconstruction contract separates a monograph from an operational model and says that the first does not establish readiness for the second. A model would need time-indexed, knowledge-conditioned rules that exclude plausible responses, with counterexamples, negative constraints and a real test for each. That is not available here, for four reasons.

First, the boundary falls between a stated change and its first test. Any rule about how Tsukasa Akeuraji responds to Inori Yuitsuka's failure would have to choose between the conduct of V09 to V15's afternoon and the conduct he promised at night. The evidence cannot choose, and a rule that covered both would exclude nothing.

Second, his conduct is strongly conditioned on what he knows, and that changed more in one evening than in the previous six volumes. Rules fitted to the man who did not know what she carried do not transfer to the man who does.

Third, several regularities are tied to interlocutor and stakes and have few independent instances: his blunter manner with Kago, his deference to seniors, his conduct toward Hikaru. Several panels of one scene are one context.

Fourth, the readiness rows in force assess the monograph as bounded and provisional and promote no rule (`MD-RD-0073`, `MD-RD-0078`). This study follows them. It offers no rule records and no global identifier.

## 13. Boundary, omissions and what this study does not claim

**Endpoint.** Japanese collected volumes 1 to 15, Scores 1 to 61, to the warm-up before the free skate of the Junior Grand Prix Final in December of Inori Yuitsuka's first middle-school year, with Official Fanbooks 01 and 02 as audited supplements. The short program is complete. The free skate, the result and everything after are outside the evidence.

**Excluded.** The animated adaptation and its cast; English editions; advertisements and previews; any chapter after Score 61; external rulebooks. A preview in the V14 witness that named a different short-program order is contradicted by story pages and is not used.

**Creator layer.** Section 11 in full; the creator page softening the circle's part; the V07 gag that first named Tsukasa Akeuraji's brothers; the comic side chapter of V14, which is story-adjacent fiction told in a frame. The V15 claim that his reason for skating answers his father's sentence is the reading's inference.

**Untested or unknown.** Tsukasa's conduct after any result. Whether the audition is pursued. His family at present. His reason for stopping beyond his words. What Hitomi thinks of their last championship. What Yodaka thinks of him now, and why Yodaka left coaching. Whether the faculty he believes he shares with Yodaka is what he takes it to be, and whether it can be taught. Whether Inori's burden is altered.

**Not claimed.** That Tsukasa is cured, or that he is relapsing. That Inori's early life was one of hardship. That Kanayumi's formulation is Inori's. That the film ending of V14 describes the pair. That any competition result confirms or refutes his worth as a coach. That his refusal to forbid is right; the corpus evaluates it favourably and records his own reservation.
