# Medalist — manga form and the representation of performance

## What this study is, and what it rests on

Version 1.0 was drafted from descriptions without original-page inspection, as the release audit records. Version 1.1 incorporates the [independent source-fidelity audit](../07%20Audits%20and%20Handoffs/MEDALIST_INDEPENDENT_SOURCE_FIDELITY_AND_CORRECTION_AUDIT.md): 27 selected original-page sequences across V01–V15 were actually viewed at adequate resolution, with additional semantic pages. Its detailed inventory distinguishes new direct verification from inherited feature records. The study still draws on `MD-VF-0001`–`MD-VF-0145`, `MD-MOT-0001`–`MD-MOT-0017`, the fifteen frozen readings and `MD-CL-0012@16`; claims outside the inspected windows remain recorded descriptions. Individual page geometry and source order were inspected. Japanese facing-page binding geometry and physical page-turn timing are not generally verified; the two owner-authorized English illustration cross-checks remain auxiliary.

Three limits govern what follows. First, locators are image occurrences in a volume's witness, in archive or spine order, not printed page numbers. Image order was verified for every volume; which pages face each other was not. Facing geometry is recorded as verified for four pairs in the run (two in V05, one each in V07 and V08), and a V14 title image is recorded as reading as one composition across two occurrences (`MD-VF-0123`). The locators of three of the four pairs coincide with next-volume advertisements (`MD-EV-V05-0044`, `MD-EV-V07-0044`, `MD-EV-V08-0045`). The only verified story spread is the roster of fifteen skaters in V05 (`MD-VF-0038`, `MD-EV-V05-0013`). No double-page effect is claimed for anything else; where a reading says "page turn", this study reports the wording and does not certify the binding. Second, the ledger grades what is drawn HIGH and what it does to a reader MODERATE; effects are the project's inferences. Third, nothing here concerns any animation, any translation, or how a line sounds.

## The argument

The manga does not try to make a still page move. It splits a performance into channels and gives each channel its own drawn form: what the body does, what the officials record, what a watcher understands, and what the skater is thinking of. Its meanings come from three operations on those channels. It keeps a device constant and changes whose knowledge the device stands for. It runs some channels while withholding another. And, from the sixth volume on, it cuts a program with the causes of each element, so that a skate is read as an argument and not as a spectacle. `MD-CL-0012@2` first stated the principle as the assignment of knowledge by form; the later versions extend it and never retract it.

The strongest rival account is simpler. Sports manga explain rules to lay readers and delay results for suspense, and all of this could be convention. The ledger carries that alternative in its own rows (`MD-VF-0001`, `MD-VF-0009`, `MD-VF-0018`). What tells against convention alone is reuse with a changed owner: the same tracker records a collapse and then a clean skate; the same annotated-skate form passes from Inori Yuitsuka to Hikaru Kamisaki and back; the same ending is repeated with the speakers exchanged. A convention would not need to be exact in that way. The claim remains an inference at moderate confidence.

## 1. A program laid over pages

**The official record has its own picture.** V02 introduces a captioned row of squares, one per element, with a printed legend, filled for added credit, reduced credit, review or error, and stamped for an invalid element (`MD-VF-0009`). The reading's account is that it separates the body's act, the panel's record and the hopes of those at the boards. Its first hard use is Ryoka Miketa's clean jump ruled invalid: a category event, not a fall. V03 adds a ladder of jump names ranked by value and a ranking board ruled with a line called a wall (`MD-VF-0018`). V05 adds base-value labels at take-off, a ten-square row under each of four interleaved skates, printed boards of element codes beside faces, and counters of elements remaining (`MD-VF-0040`, `MD-VF-0041`). V11 runs a seven-box row under each short program so that identical layouts are seen filled differently (`MD-VF-0097`). V14 draws the panel itself as people, with pens over mark sheets and a finger over a keypad (`MD-VF-0125`).

**The same device serves opposite outcomes.** In V08 a full-page triple Axel is followed by a row whose first square stays grey: landed and doubted (`MD-VF-0070`). In V12 Hikaru Kamisaki's first fall is a judge's hand on a button and one black square (`MD-VF-0109`). In V13 Inori Yuitsuka's short program is told almost wholly by its record: a row marked for a miss and a fall, a step checklist with a turn struck out in each cluster, and a standings board on which her line slides down (`MD-VF-0112`, `MD-EV-V13-0004`, `MD-EV-V13-0006`). In V14 the same row fills box by box with no gap (`MD-VF-0127`, `MD-VF-0129`; `MD-CL-0012@14`). The form reports the collapse and gives no cause for it. Neither do the readings, and this study supplies none.

**The program is cut with its reasons.** V06 gives one free skate of Inori's some forty pages and interrupts it with a lay viewer, an explainer, a boy in the stands, rival coaches and Tsukasa Akeuraji's interior, each adding one step of the case for the layout (`MD-VF-0045`). V08 applies the form to Hikaru (`MD-VF-0072`). In V09 the interruptions are Inori's own secrets and bargains (`MD-VF-0080`). In V13 Hikaru's free skate runs about fifty pages, cut with her origin, a night's talk and a technical lecture in a rival coach's voice, over a twelve-box tracker (`MD-VF-0114`, `MD-VF-0115`). In V14 four rivals' programs become worked examples of the scoring criteria (`MD-VF-0124`).

**Contrasting cases.** Where nothing official is at stake the apparatus is absent: Tsukasa's own skate in V04 is five nearly wordless pages with no tracker (`MD-VF-0031`), and Yodaka Jun's remembered program in V10 is framed as one coach's testimony (`MD-VF-0091`). The record can also replace the performance. Inori's first international title is a headline, a calendar and a broadcast graphic (`MD-VF-0108`), and her V11 free skate is not drawn (`MD-VF-0103`). The tracker is therefore the manga's way of drawing adjudication, not skating, and it can stand alone. All values, levels and calls are in-story statements; `MD-CL-0013@17` keeps that boundary.

## 2. Motion and rotation in still images

**Multiple figures.** A chain of one skater's figures along a path appears three times in V02: under remembered instruction, before spectators who cannot find the preparation, and beneath a close-up of Tsukasa Akeuraji's eyes (`MD-VF-0012`). The reading calls this its best evidence for the governing claim: the device is constant and its owner changes. Later chains include a ten-figure step strip (`MD-VF-0031`), two consecutive full pages for one three-jump pass (`MD-VF-0047`), and a full-page jump as a chain in V05 (`MD-VF-0043`). The ledger's caution is explicit: figure counts are not frame counts, and a jump's name or rotations cannot be recovered by counting positions (`MD-VF-0003`, `MD-VF-0006`).

**A line before a body.** In V07 the first unaided quadruple is drawn as a blade's arc over a night horizon and then as a full-page landing seen from below (`MD-VF-0063`).

**Distance and silence.** The decisive jump of a V04 test is a page of impact lettering, then a still wide panel with no sound marks, then a landing (`MD-VF-0033`). V13 shows an opened jump in a wide shot (`MD-VF-0112`); V14 shows a combination from under the roof (`MD-VF-0127`).

**A jump not drawn.** The last fall of the V13 short program is a sound over two spectators' backs (`MD-VF-0112`). A V11 fall is reported and not drawn (`MD-MOT-0006`, V11 row).

**A change of state inside a jump.** In V15 a costume's colour turns from black to red inside a double Axel, with a transformed face over an ink burst (`MD-VF-0134`, `MD-EV-V15-0005`). The V15 reading describes this as crossing a page turn. The ledger records it across occurrences 0020–0024 in a volume with no verified geometry. The sequence is established; the binding is not. The same volume ends on a full-page jump with a second image at its apex (`MD-VF-0145`).

**Tone.** V02 sets Inori Yuitsuka's white ground, with melody written as sound-lettering, against Hikaru Kamisaki's black ground and slit light (`MD-VF-0010`, `MD-VF-0013`). The ledger forbids reading that as moral coding. V03 slows to wide strips of one gesture each when the text speaks of slowness (`MD-VF-0022`).

**Contrasting cases.** Off-ice movement receives the same grammar: a run-through on asphalt (`MD-VF-0021`), a jump in a park (`MD-VF-0027`), a dancer's turns on a floor (`MD-VF-0032`). The machinery marks an act as skating regardless of where it happens.

## 3. Who watches

Nearly every performance is given an owner of the view. The watcher varies, and the variation is the point.

**Spectators who teach.** V05 places an informed fan, Sekoma, beside Kago Kōichi, who has paid without understanding (`MD-EV-V05-0017`, `MD-VF-0039`). In V06 Kago, the lay viewer, sees ease before the expert names it (`MD-MOT-0002`, V06 row). In V14 the teacher is Kamogawa Kōhei and the pupil is Kago's daughter Yō, who was a lost bystander in V05; the lesson answers her belief that she lacks a sense for art, and one spectator's face is used as a gauge of each program (`MD-VF-0124`, `MD-EV-V14-0014`). The V14 reading records that the lesson has a declared interest behind it and that a counter-voice is left unrefuted (`MD-CL-0030@2`).

**Coaches, the hall and screens.** Tsukasa Akeuraji's eye is drawn as an instrument in V02 (`MD-VF-0012`). In V15 Baek Horang's coach reconstructs Inori Yuitsuka's strategy from the live protocol (`MD-EV-V15-0002`). V08 draws attention as a ring of silhouettes with lit eyes pressing on two small figures (`MD-VF-0067`), then as applause thinning to black under commentary that has no speaker (`MD-VF-0074`). Tsukasa as a boy at a television and Yodaka Jun on the ice share one panel in V06 (`MD-VF-0052`); in V14 Inori sees Tsukasa's celebration on the arena screen (`MD-VF-0131`).

**One look, two addressees.** In V13 a wide shot seats Inori directly behind the judges, so that Hikaru Kamisaki's gaze, taught as technique, is also a look at Inori (`MD-VF-0116`).

**Inori behind Tsukasa.** In V15 Tsukasa's resolve to win is a page of dark close-ups, ending with Inori small behind him, looking (`MD-VF-0136`, `MD-EV-V15-0010`). The page shows a stake being set and being seen. It does not show what Inori concluded.

**Contrasting cases.** A test in V04 has no audience at all (`MD-MOT-0002`, V04 row). Ryoka Miketa in V04 sees the same skating and is unmoved because she counts jumps (`MD-MOT-0005`, V04 row). In V09 the watcher Inori most wanted is an empty chair (`MD-VF-0083`). Watching is not uniformly a good in the record: by V12 it is a cause of injury and a ground of obedience (`MD-MOT-0005`, V12 row).

## 4. Diagrams and didactic pages

The early diagrams explain the sport: food as a shorthand for two training plans in V01 (`MD-VF-0007`), cards and a star chart of values in V05 (`MD-VF-0039`), collectible cards and a rhythm game's full combo in V06 (`MD-VF-0046`), a landing diagram with quarter lines in V08 (`MD-VF-0070`), a board-game track with a falling status bar in V11 (`MD-VF-0096`).

The later diagrams explain persons and institutions. V10 draws two doctrines of coaching as one maze, from inside and from the air (`MD-VF-0090`). V13 draws a failed explanation as a diagram of a sentence that snagged and words that bounced off (`MD-VF-0118`). V14 gives a dream an administrative diagram, a staircase of years and a pyramid built from a ranking table (`MD-VF-0122`), teaches a shared "ruler of beauty" with three plain shapes, and decomposes a punch into stance, shoulder and breath so that feeling is a set of teachable parts (`MD-VF-0124`, `MD-VF-0128`). V15 extends the maze to hold the supporters as well (`MD-VF-0139`) and closes on Tsukasa Akeuraji as a boy with a long ruler (`MD-VF-0145`).

The direction of travel is the finding. The apparatus that made scoring legible is turned on what cannot be scored, and V15 marks the limit in its own image: the ruler of V14 measured programs, and the ruler of V15 measured Tsukasa as a boy (`MD-MOT-0002`, V15 row; `MD-CL-0030@2`).

**Contrasting cases.** Some diagrams are attributed opinion and must not be read as data: two bar charts of landing rates in V06 are Gori Seiji's estimate (`MD-VF-0055`). Some images are metaphors and not lessons: a small figure stooping for one bright thing in V03 (`MD-VF-0023`), a knight's sword labelled with a jump (`MD-VF-0030`, marked isolated).

## 5. Films, memories and doubles inside a program

**Memory as intrusion.** A dark borderless panel repeating an adult's sentence breaks into effort for two different children in V03 (`MD-VF-0020`). In V09 a page of jagged lettering round a wild face is stopped by one remembered sentence in a plain panel (`MD-VF-0082`). In V14 the previous month's fall is breaking glass on the approach to the same jump, and the landing answers it (`MD-VF-0127`).

**A film laid over people.** V08 stages the story behind Hikaru Kamisaki's music as its own sequence and lays it over two people without saying which is which; the casting comes later from Hikaru's choreographer (`MD-VF-0071`, `MD-VF-0073`). The allegory that follows is Tsukasa Akeuraji's reading.

**A program cut with an origin.** In V13 Hikaru's movements are overlaid on a younger Yodaka Jun's, and the program turns on a full stop at centre ice with a diagram of line against curve (`MD-VF-0114`).

**A double.** Inori Yuitsuka's V14 short program is built on a film of a boy and a ghost with his face. The pages show a dark inverse of Inori joined at the blade over black water, Tsukasa drawn as the ghost, the film's scenes continuous with the home rink and the arena, and a last pose over her own reflection (`MD-VF-0126`, `MD-VF-0129`, `MD-VF-0130`). The ledger states the limit: the application of the film's ending beyond the program is not made by the page. V15 confirms the caution. Inori tells her mother, Nozomi, that she does not find that ending happy and would not mind vanishing into the gift, and then calls it only a film (`MD-EV-V15-0046`; `MD-MOT-0016`, V15 row).

**A film that lends its iconography.** In V15 the revenge film behind Karasuba Daria's program is told in framed captions that merge with Daria's own (`MD-VF-0135`). Some ninety image occurrences on, Kago Kōichi's account of blame is drawn in the same film's imagery, gavels and a dressmaker's dummy on a scaffold (`MD-VF-0142`).

**Contrasting case.** Inori's first competitive program in V02 is drawn as continuous and bright with no interior cut (`MD-VF-0010`). The embedded story is a later method.

## 6. Withheld and delayed information

**The skate withheld.** V05, V08 and V12 each end before Inori Yuitsuka skates (`MD-VF-0044`, `MD-VF-0075`, `MD-VF-0111`). V08 repeats V05's ending with the speakers exchanged.

**The result withheld.** V06 gives Inori's win as a printed sheet and turns to the skater who came fourteenth; there is no celebration panel (`MD-VF-0049`). V09 cuts from an ovation to a podium whose centre figure alone, Hikaru Kamisaki, is named, and delivers Inori's number in the next chapter; the reading records that it misidentified a figure there itself (`MD-VF-0083`, `MD-EV-V09-0037`, `MD-EV-V09-0039`). V11 gives a placing as a caption (`MD-VF-0103`). In V13 Inori's final place is not printed (`MD-EV-V13-0006`), and what the result is to Inori is first said aloud by her, in a thicket at night (`MD-CL-0012@13`, `MD-EV-V13-0008`).

**Speech withheld.** The speech of Tsukasa Akeuraji's father is printed as illegible marks at its first recall in V15 and in clear type fifty-two page positions later (occurrences 0076→0128, with fifty-one intervening pages) (`MD-VF-0140`, `MD-EV-V15-0020`, `MD-EV-V15-0035`). A sentence Karasuba Daria begins at the end of V14 is left unfinished (`MD-EV-V14-0031`); V15 supplies what lay behind it as an apology (`MD-EV-V15-0011`).

**Captions and owners withheld.** Meiko's death is one room shown twice (`MD-VF-0042`; the death is inferred from the image). Tsukasa's lowered face is seen by Inori through a van window with no caption (`MD-VF-0060`). Yodaka Jun's departure is boots set down and a caption that he did not return (`MD-VF-0117`); Yodaka's motive beyond his stated reason is not given and is kept open here. Captions run over Inori's and Tsukasa's faces without settled attribution in V02 (`MD-VF-0015`), and Inori's creed and a young Yodaka's share one balloon in V10 (`MD-VF-0092`).

**Time withheld.** A year passes in two black bands (`MD-VF-0025`); eight months pass between two panels of one girl (`MD-VF-0085`); V10 counts backward from that cut with a pictogram (`MD-VF-0086`).

**Contrasting cases.** The manga also over-informs. V04 cross-cuts two rooms so that the reader learns one fact from both sides and sees what each party does not know (`MD-VF-0028`). V03 plants a hole in the ice forty pages before it injures Inori, so that the injury owes nothing to her skating (`MD-VF-0019`). Withholding is thus one setting of a general control over who knows what. A reader is sometimes placed with the hall, sometimes above both parties.

A caution from the creator layer applies to pacing. A jacket note in V06 says volume-final chapters are cut to a fixed length (`MD-EV-V06-0053`), and the V09 acknowledgements report twenty pages cut from two chapters (`MD-EV-V09-0052`). Abruptness at a volume's end is weaker evidence of design than abruptness elsewhere.

## 7. Endings, covers and jackets

Endings fall into three recorded kinds. The declaration or opening pose before an unskated program (V05, V08, V12). The paired image: two result boxes in V06 (`MD-VF-0054`), two matched portraits in V07 (`MD-VF-0065`), two small children in V10 (`MD-VF-0094`). And the definition: V13 closes with one sentence in captions over three girls, then a boot held in two hands and a party in an airport (`MD-VF-0120`, `MD-EV-V13-0051`). V15 does none of these. It stops on a jump in a warm-up, after captions over a ruler, crows, doves, a nest in thorns and a hand raised to light (`MD-VF-0145`, `MD-EV-V15-0052`). An ending in the air states nothing about an outcome, and none is known.

Emphasis is not settlement. In V13 a mended ride lights up behind Inori Yuitsuka at a vow (`MD-VF-0119`). The V15 reading lists that scene among its echoes as recalled as a doubt, and the project withdrew its V13 judgment that the declaration was a durable answer (`MD-RV-0259`, `MD-RV-0261`).

Jackets are classed as cover paratext that identifies no event. Their sequence reads as an index of whose volume it is: paired reaching hands (`MD-EV-V01-0041`), the two coaches, Tsukasa Akeuraji and Yodaka Jun (`MD-EV-V06-0055`), Inori and Hikaru Kamisaki (`MD-EV-V07-0047`), Inori alone for the first time (`MD-EV-V09-0056`), Inori and Tsukasa under a bird of prey (`MD-EV-V10-0057`), two rivals and a blade without Inori (`MD-EV-V13-0056`), Tsukasa and Inori running side by side (`MD-EV-V14-0042`), Tsukasa alone under a crow's wing (`MD-VF-0133`, `MD-EV-V15-0057`). The V13 and V15 jackets match volumes whose pages go largely to someone other than Inori. That correlation is this study's inference.

## 8. The comic register

Gag drawing is used inside the main register, for three jobs.

It compresses distress without diagnosing it: a frozen program becomes a "loading" image in V01 (`MD-VF-0004`), and haste in V06 is a child drawn steaming (`MD-VF-0055`).

It closes or reopens serious matter. A training arc is declared complete on the page where it begins (`MD-VF-0056`). A kneeling gag set up in V11's first chapter returns in its last as an argument that abasement burdens the person knelt to (`MD-VF-0095`). V14 replays in comic key Tsukasa Akeuraji's misread face, which was V13's crisis (`MD-VF-0121`). In V15 a page of self-accusation, with a verdict lettered across Tsukasa's eyes, is broken by a shoulder throw (`MD-VF-0138`).

It carries facts at lower weight. Four-panel strips and under-jacket comics hold ages, family details and small events with authorial glosses. The readings class them as bonus fiction or creator asides, note that gag chronology is loose, and decline one parody date outright (`MD-EV-V02-0050`, `MD-EV-V07-0043`, `MD-EV-V08-0047`, `MD-EV-V15-0055`). Explainer pages rank the jumps for readers or distinguish the sport's disciplines (`MD-EV-V05-0042`, `MD-EV-V15-0053`).

**Contrasting case.** Comedy does not always release. Inori Yuitsuka and Yagi Yūna each supply themselves an imagined coach, drawn comically in Inori's case and privately in Yūna's (`MD-VF-0035`).

### Which motifs organize the work

Four do, on three tests: presence in nearly every volume, a recorded change of meaning in most, and a tie to the formal method.

**Seeing and being seen** (`MD-MOT-0005`) is the method turned into subject. A form that gives each view an owner has made looking its theme. It is recorded in every volume from V02 and changes in each.

**Hands, contact and carrying** (`MD-MOT-0001`) is the only motif in all fifteen volumes. Its direction reverses: contact begins as what an adult gives a child and ends, in the V15 stairwell, as a load Inori asks to put down (`MD-VF-0144`).

**The verdict that returns, with appraisal behind it** (`MD-MOT-0007`, `MD-MOT-0002`, `MD-MOT-0017`). These are one line at three scales: the official mark, the sentence an adult leaves in a child, and in V15 the colours by which persons are sorted. The work defends the first within its bounds and refuses the last. The colour scheme is one volume old and may prove local.

**Falling and getting up** (`MD-MOT-0006`) supplies V13's closing definition and V15's naming of guilt as the obstacle (`MD-CL-0019@12`).

Three organize an arc and not the work. Cards and chairs (`MD-MOT-0009`) are the vocabulary of strategy from V05 to V11 and thin afterwards. The lake, water and the double (`MD-MOT-0011`, `MD-MOT-0016`) organize Inori's inner place from V07, while the ledger keeps noting that the landings happen indoors. Light and shadow (`MD-MOT-0013`) belongs to Hikaru's arc and has no entry after V13.

The rest are incidental as visual motifs, whatever their thematic weight: food, night and morning, the maze, confinement and the two verbal motifs. Responsibility after helping (`MD-MOT-0004`) is a central theme that the visual ledger barely develops; its home is elsewhere.

The counter-reading is that the ranking reflects the ledger's habits, since a motif registered early collects more rows. Later volumes would discriminate: if the black-and-white figure and the double persist while cards do not return, the ranking holds.

## Written Japanese

This section draws only on the readings' "Written Japanese" sections. It concerns printed words. It establishes nothing about how any line sounds, and each of those sections says so.

**Words that change mouths.** The readings track a small vocabulary that moves between speakers, and the movement is where the argument is carried.

- 証明, proof: Yodaka Jun's in V06; Tsukasa Akeuraji's own measure in V08, and that volume's jacket line; Inori Yuitsuka's, twice, in V09; in V13 a chapter title joins it to being in earnest.
- 本気, being in earnest: in V11 what a pointless payment would prove, and a word used to Inori by a pupil of Riley Fox; in V13 Tsukasa's word twice on a bench and the word in Inori's thought when she cuts school.
- 絶対, absolute: Tsukasa's in V01 and V04; denied in quotation marks by Sekoma, a bystander, in V05; denied by Tsukasa and affirmed by Yodaka in V06; by V08 Inori's for intention and wish, and Tsukasa's only in the negative; Inori's again in V14 of an Olympic stage.
- 犠牲, sacrifice: in V08 given to Hikaru Kamisaki as Yodaka's teaching, and negated by Tsukasa; in V09 set beside self-harm and a sense of inferiority; in V11 qualified as needless. In V15 it appears in Kanayumi Miho's phrase about Tsukasa's life, in a sentence of Tsukasa's teacher that replaces sacrifices with participants, and in Tsukasa's caption that he denied it and believed in his own. Kanayumi's phrase is Kanayumi's. Inori's own words to Tsukasa, as that section lists them, are a word for being rewarded, said of everyone if she wins, and 役目, an entrusted role or duty, for what she thought she had been given (original V15 occurrence 0185; frozen transcription corrected by erratum).
- 代わり, a substitute: negated in Tsukasa's V13 declaration, beside a word for dream that he moves from what he lost to what he has. V13 treated this as an answer. V15 withdrew that judgment (`MD-RV-0259`, `MD-RV-0261`): Inori heard the words accurately and took them as a role entrusted to her. The vocabulary shows the misreading on its face, since Tsukasa's noun in V13 and Inori's in V15 are different nouns.
- 図々しい, presumptuous: Karasuba Daria's word for Inori in V14, which Inori does not know and asks about; turned by Daria on herself in V15, with a related noun in Tsukasa's memory of himself as a boy.
- 価値, worth: in Kamogawa Kōhei's creed in V09 with talent and use; in V15 twice in the speech of Tsukasa's father beside productivity, and then Tsukasa's own word as a boy, stripped of usefulness.
- The cold feeling: in V13 Tsukasa's word for Inori's new state is the term first named in V02’s bonus chapter and revisited in the V04 farewell.

Smaller transfers are recorded as well: a verb of choosing that is a potential in V04, a plain past in V09 and a passive in V10; and a phrase for a light of hope used by each girl of the other in V13.

**Chapter titles.** Titles are figures and sometimes literal facts. V03–V04 run from hating the night to morning coming, and the last is literally six in the morning. V06 uses a word for signal fire written with the character that begins Hikaru's family name; V12 opens on light and shadow, the first character being her given name. V13 has proof of being in earnest, and a mermaid and a blade. V14 reverts to plain event-and-segment titles. V15 uses a set phrase for glossy black hair that is also a crow's feather; a pastor and a lamb, where the name of Yō, Kago Kōichi's daughter, contains the character for sheep; and midway on a spiral, over a stairwell. Personal names follow an animal convention.

**Address.** In V02 Tsukasa's formal second-person pronoun is used twice with opposite force, to dignify Inori, a child, and to hold Yodaka, a superior, to account. In V03 Sonidori Rio's word for father carries a gloss meaning this person. In V04 Rio's change is measured by address, from a bare title to Tsukasa's full name and then a possessive. Tsukasa often preserves respectful forms of address to pupils while mixing them with plain grammar, 俺 and contractions; formal grammatical speech also occurs with seniors and adversaries. The [speech/interlocutor audit](../07%20Audits%20and%20Handoffs/MEDALIST_JAPANESE_SPEECH_AND_INTERLOCUTOR_AUDIT_THROUGH_V15.md) records these contrasts and exceptions. Tsukasa's V05 declaration joins his private first-person pronoun to the respectful second person he has always given Inori; Inori's V08 answer copies its construction and exchanges subject and object. Regional forms mark the fields. Shirone is given no gendered caption, and the reading uses none.

## Paratext as form

The project classed every non-story component, and the classes set the weight.

- **Editorial pages** introduce athletes and coaches with a recap from V03 on (`MD-EV-V03-0002`, `MD-EV-V15-0001`). They are used for fields, and their errors are recorded (`MD-EV-V08-0001`, `MD-EV-V10-0001`).
- **Profiles** are documentary paratext: attributed fields, not tests of behaviour (`MD-EV-V01-0033`, `MD-EV-V13-0033`).
- **Four-panel strips, sketch pages and under-jacket comics** are bonus fiction or creator asides. One under-jacket page confirms a pairing the story only implies (`MD-VF-0035`).
- **Author's pages** speak in the author's voice. V07 places three colour pages before the events they follow and accounts for an omitted scene on a pencil page (`MD-VF-0059`, `MD-EV-V07-0009`).
- **Colophons** are production paratext. From V10 to V15 the digital colophon's date precedes the print colophon's; the pattern is recorded and unresolved (`MD-EV-V10-0055`, `MD-EV-V15-0054`).
- **Next-volume previews** are marketing and are excluded as evidence from V02 on. The V14 preview states a short-program result that V15's story pages contradict; the story governs (`MD-EV-V14-0039`, `MD-EV-V15-0009`).
- **The "Exhibition" parody**, in V05, V12 and V14, recasts the cast in a fantasy genre. It is classed as parody, not continuity, and entered in the visual ledger as the work playing with its own motifs (`MD-EV-V05-0043`, `MD-EV-V12-0043`, `MD-EV-V14-0035`; `MD-VF-0111`, `MD-VF-0132`). The third instalment stages a meeting the story withheld and changes no knowledge state.
- **A sample of another series** is bound into the V01 and V14 witnesses. Both ranges were excluded.
- **The two fanbooks** add no feature or motif record. Two remarks are kept as context at the reported-creator layer: that the V09 rain scene was drawn so that a mask would be seen to break (`MD-EV-FB02-0026`), and an assistant's remark on the series' nights (`MD-EV-FB02-0032`).

The formal point is that the volume is built in registers of declining authority, and the manga uses the lower ones on purpose: to play its ideas for laughs, to stage what the story withholds, and to place facts where they bind less.

## Boundary, omissions and what this study does not claim

The endpoint is `MD-FR-FB02`: V01–V15, Scores 1–61, with two audited fanbooks. The story stands in the warm-up before the free skate of the Junior Grand Prix Final. The short program is complete. The result of the final is not known and nothing is said about it. The series is ongoing and every conclusion is provisional.

Excluded: any animation; any translation; anything after Score 61; external rules or protocols; the two bound-in samples; all next-volume previews.

Not claimed: that any page was seen by this author; that any two pages face each other beyond the pairs the ledger verified; that a drawn sequence measures rotation, speed, timing or sound; that colour pages were assessed beyond their recorded descriptions; that a reader responds as the ledger infers; that the author intended any effect, except where a creator statement is cited at its own layer. The creator-layer points here are the V06 note on chapter length, the V09 acknowledgements, the V07 author's page, the V05 under-jacket page and the two fanbook remarks.

Untested: whether the motif ranking survives later volumes; whether the black-and-white figure persists; whether the tracker's reuse across V13 and V14 was designed as a pair.

Two terms sometimes used for this material were not found in the corpus and are not used: a title for Inori Yuitsuka's V14 short program, and any reference to mascot explainers.
