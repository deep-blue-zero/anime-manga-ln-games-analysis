# Skating, competition and expression through MD-FR-FB02

## Scope, channels and the rule about rules

This study collects what the manga establishes about the sport as Inori Yuitsuka practises it: what she has skated and scored, how her technique was built and where it has failed, what her rivals can do, what bodies have cost, how the story says the sport is scored and governed, and how it treats artistry. Its principal source is the [skating ledger](../03%20Longitudinal%20Ledgers/MEDALIST_SKATING_TRAINING_AND_COMPETITION_LEDGER.md), read with the skating section of each volume reading and with claims `MD-CL-0005@14`, `MD-CL-0008@14`, `MD-CL-0013@17`, `MD-CL-0022@12`, `MD-CL-0024@9` and `MD-CL-0030@2`.

Four channels are kept apart, as the ledger keeps them. **Depicted** means a performance drawn on the page. **Called** means an official level, call, score or place printed on the page. **Attributed** means an appraisal by a named character. **Inferred** means this study's or a reading's reasoning. A drawn landing is not a credited element until a call says so, and a coach's appraisal is not a call.

No rulebook of any real federation and no medical source was admitted to this corpus; the ledger's external-reference table has no rows at any freeze. Every rule below is a statement made inside the story or, where marked, a fanbook's own summary. Whether any of them matches the real sport was not checked, and nothing here says that it does or does not. The work's own notices, a season marker on its early scoring (`MD-SK-0007`) and a statement that its skating history is fiction (V11 reading), are recorded as its statements about itself.

The story stands in the six-minute warm-up before the free skate of the Junior Grand Prix Final. Nothing of that free skate is known.

## The first decision and what it bought

Tsukasa Akeuraji, Inori Yuitsuka's coach, came from ice dance. His first allocation of her training was to skating, edges, steps and a spin before difficult jumps. In V01 he laid the trade before her, a double jump first or polish first, and she chose the foundation (`MD-EV-V01-0025`, `MD-SK-0004`). When her double was still falling four days before her first event, Hitomi Takamine argued for reliable singles, and the plan was rebuilt round a flying sit spin (`MD-EV-V01-0029`, `MD-SK-0007`). By Sonidori Rio's account in V06 the pattern persisted: practice weighted to skating and steps, with a small share for jumps (`MD-EV-V06-0007`).

`MD-CL-0008` has followed this decision through fourteen versions. Its record is best stated as purchases and failures.

**What it bought.** At Inori's first event the spin earned credit with a variation she added herself (`MD-SK-0009`). At Kyoto she outscored Shishidō Seira, Kurosawa Miho, Oguma Ritsuki and Kitora Kanna, who had the same doubles or more, and a judge called Inori's step sequence the best in the field (`MD-PERF-0023`, `MD-EV-V03-0027`). At the block Inori won by 1.17 with a clean program whose hardest jump was a triple loop, against Yagi Yūna's triple Lutz and triple loop with two errors; the mechanism named on the page is execution marks pursued on every element (`MD-SK-0035`). Uobuchi Kakeru, a freelance jump coach, then said that Inori's precision and rotation speed, which he attributed to Tsukasa's training, were what a quadruple needs (`MD-SK-0041`, `MD-EV-V07-0023`). That appraisal changed what the foundation was credited with: the precondition of difficulty, and no longer only a substitute for it. At the national novice championship Inori received a level-4 step sequence, by an older skater's account the first for a novice in eleven years, while Hikaru Kamisaki and Kamoto Suzu, the gold and silver medalists, received level 2 (`MD-SK-0057`, `MD-EV-V09-0039`). At the Final Baek Horang's coach, reading a live protocol, attributed Inori's 0.02 margin over Baek Horang to a step sequence at level 4 against level 3 and to very high execution marks on spins, against a harder jump layout with a second-half combination (`MD-SK-0089`, `MD-EV-V15-0002`). `MD-CL-0008@14` treats this as the first confirmation by someone with no interest in giving it.

**What it failed to buy.** At Kyoto quality did not make up for Yamato Ema's content, two levels harder and also clean (`MD-PERF-0025`). After the block a rival coach called the triple Lutz the condition for a national podium, and Inori had neither it nor the flip (`MD-SK-0037`). At the nationals the foundation gave her speed, a stable quadruple and the top step level, and she was fourth. In the junior class both of Inori's distinctive assets were removed by stated rule: the short program bars women's quadruples and prescribes the jumps, and the free skate has no step sequence (`MD-SK-0063`, `MD-SK-0069`, `MD-SK-0071`). The claim's V11 formulation, that the foundation is a necessary condition at this level and no longer a sufficient one, has not been withdrawn. There is also a cost inside the foundation. By Tsukasa's diagnosis Inori's triple Lutz failed because the take-off edge was laid too deep to draw the free leg through, a fault produced by the edge control that was her advantage (`MD-SK-0064`).

**Where it did not hold.** At the All-Japan Junior the step sequence was called at level 2 with a fault in each cluster (`MD-SK-0079`). The page gives no reason. A month later it was level 4 again, with each listed turn checked in both directions (`MD-SK-0085`).

The strongest counterweight to the outside confirmation is in the same segment. Karasuba Daria, who overtook Inori that day, also had a level-4 step sequence (`MD-PERF-0117`). The foundation explains a margin of 0.02 over Baek Horang, by her coach's reading. It does not explain a deficit of 0.33 to Daria.

## Jumps in order of acquisition

Practice acquisition, layout admission and official calls require separate evidence. Tsukasa Akeuraji’s stated model is to land once, raise the success rate until the jump can enter a layout, then develop combinations or GOE (`MD-SK-0038`; original V06 occurrence 0182). The third stage is not an official call. The pages omit some acquisitions and give no stable rate for most jumps; the four analytical channels remain distinct.

**Singles and doubles.** Her first competition credited singles through the loop, Lutz and flip, with the Axel fallen (`MD-PERF-0011`). One double loop was landed in practice in V02 (`MD-PERF-0017`); two doubles were credited at Kyoto, the Salchow's acquisition being elided (`MD-SK-0014`); after an elided year she had all five doubles below the Axel in competition (`MD-SK-0018`).

**The double Axel.** It was the stated barrier to the grade-6 badge. Tsukasa first attributed its absence to strength; an off-ice landing showed the strength was there, and he retracted the diagnosis (`MD-EV-V04-0005`, `MD-EV-V04-0006`, `MD-SK-0020`). It was landed on the second day of a camp after two technical interventions, and which one mattered is not isolated (`MD-SK-0021`).

**Triple Salchow, loop and toe loop.** The triple Salchow came the same day as the double Axel, which the story explains by a stated nearness of rotation speeds (`MD-SK-0022`). A triple loop was acquired in the next two months; how is not shown (`MD-SK-0034`). All three were credited at the block, where the closing combination rested on a perfect landing record in Tsukasa's practice logs, an induction that rival coaches judged extreme (`MD-SK-0036`, `MD-EV-V06-0013`).

**Triple flip and triple Lutz.** Both were first landed in one session with a harness operated by Uobuchi Kakeru, a jump coach, then without it (`MD-PERF-0062`). The harness is said in-story to correct the axis and not to add flight (`MD-SK-0041`). Days later, after Tsukasa was injured operating one, Inori's triple Lutz came out as a single (`MD-EV-V07-0011`). Uobuchi attributed this to an unconscious release of the body's tightening before take-off; Inori stated a fear of falling and named its sources (`MD-SK-0042`, `MD-EV-V07-0025`). The diagnosis is Uobuchi's.

**The quadruple Salchow.** Uobuchi proposed it in place of the Lutz, on stated values of 9.7 against 5.9. It was built with a harness, a higher arm swing and attention redirected to fine sensation, against early openings attributed to fear; the story states that a mistimed landing can break the leg (`MD-SK-0043`). It was landed three times on the page in practice (`MD-PERF-0064`, `MD-PERF-0066`, `MD-PERF-0071`). The decision to use it rested on no log, which the ledger calls thin beside the V06 warrant (`MD-SK-0045`). In competition two were credited, one with a double toe loop attached (`MD-SK-0055`).

**The Lutz relearned.** Inori practised it alone and kept this from Tsukasa. It was admitted on a warm-up tally of four in five, placed third behind two quadruples, and fallen (`MD-SK-0056`). She fell on it at the selection meeting, landed one in practice at a camp (`MD-PERF-0091`), landed it in Bangkok's short program, fell in that event's free skate by report, fell at the All-Japan Junior, and landed it at the Final (`MD-SK-0070`, `MD-SK-0085`). Before a panel, by depiction or report, that is two landed and four fallen.

**Triple flip and triple toe loop.** The combination was admitted on three landings in three under a test set by the cautious Kamogawa Kōhei (`MD-SK-0068`). Its second jump was called a quarter short in Bangkok; the flip opened to a single in Yokohama from a new head-down entry; it was credited at the Final. The flip's take-off edge is a separate recorded weakness, marked on Inori's V09 protocol and called in Bangkok's free skate by report.

**The triple Axel.** It has never been shown in Inori's practice. She attempted it once, alone in mid-program at the national novice championship, on a cue Tsukasa had given for the double; it was landed without a fall, reviewed and downgraded (`MD-SK-0058`). Older skaters judged that it cost the podium. The V09 reading notes that this is consistent with the stated rules and cannot be computed from the page.

**Since growth.** The quadruple could not be trained during Inori's growing pains (`MD-SK-0062`). One was landed in Bangkok's free skate by report, one is credited by headline at Event 7, and one is in the layout settled for the free skate of the Final (`MD-SK-0092`). None has been drawn in competition since V09.

## Steps, spins and execution marks

**Step sequence.** Inori Yuitsuka's calls run: base level at Kyoto; level 2 at the grade-5 event; level 3 at the block; level 4 at the national novice championship and in Bangkok; level 2 in Yokohama; level 4 at the Final, with a printed base value of 3.90. The in-story criteria are counted features: variety of difficult turns and steps, clusters on each foot, both rotational directions, and use of the upper body (`MD-EV-V09-0028`). Two things limit the advantage. Hikaru Kamisaki's sequence went from level 2 to level 4 in a season with Takamine Shō, Tsukasa Akeuraji's own teacher (`MD-SK-0073`). And the element does not exist in the junior free skate.

**Spins.** The flying sit spin was the platform of Inori's first plan (`MD-SK-0006`). A level-1 flying sit spin is already credited at Kyoto (`MD-PERF-0023`); the second Meikō Cup protocol lists FCSp2, LSp3 and CCoSp4 (`MD-PERF-0027`). In Yokohama a level-4 spin is the one element the record shows credited. At the Final all three are level 4, and Baek Horang's coach names spins as where the gap was made.

**Execution marks as strategy.** The stated rule at V06 is that each step of the mark adds a tenth of base value, that marks above plus three require three compulsory criteria, and that the highest and lowest judges are dropped. Inori's block program pursued the mark on every element in place of added difficulty, which an observer calls a senior's strategy (`MD-SK-0035`). On entering the junior class Tsukasa named a ceiling of plus three to be raised, beginning with height (`MD-SK-0061`). In Bangkok the strategy was everyone's: skaters with identical jump content were eight points apart (`MD-SK-0069`). At the Final Inori's seven printed base values sum to 32.13, so 8.77 of her technical mark came from execution; that subtraction is the V14 reading's inference, and the protocol is not printed in full.

## The rival frontier

**Hikaru Kamisaki.** Her content has been ahead of Inori Yuitsuka's at every freeze. At the national novice championship she opened with a quadruple toe loop never tried on that ice and closed with a triple Axel, Euler and triple Salchow, for 128.98, with a step sequence at level 2 (`MD-PERF-0079`, `MD-SK-0052`). A year later her short program used a triple Axel as the first jump of the combination, which the stated rules permit, and a level-4 step sequence; she fell on a triple Lutz, by her own account after forcing a jump that was opening, and scored 71.10 (`MD-SK-0074`, `MD-SK-0075`). Her free skate the next day held a quadruple Lutz, a quadruple toe loop in a three-jump combination, a quadruple Salchow, two triple Axels and a triple Lutz and triple loop, without a fall, for 156.54 (`MD-PERF-0108`).

Tsukasa Akeuraji attributes these jumps to a self-developed raised-arm variation, in which one arm grips the other hand and pulls upward with the rotation, thinning the axis as a harness would; he says Hikaru has kept it in every competition from early on and that the rules reward it little (`MD-SK-0080`, `MD-EV-V13-0020`). This is Tsukasa's reading and not a call. On it rests the V13 observation that Inori's distinction and Hikaru's are each an accumulated habit.

**Kamoto Suzu.** She is called an all-rounder. At the national novice championship she kept a triple Axel in her layout after falling on it at practice and landed it under review, for 110.55 with a step sequence at level 2 (`MD-PERF-0074`, `MD-SK-0051`).

**Okazaki Iruka.** She is the measure of the junior field. Her Bangkok short program had the same three jumping passes as Inori's and scored 73.83 against 65.28; Tsukasa locates the difference in height, entries, bonus placement, ornament after landings and carriage (`MD-PERF-0098`). This is Iruka's last junior season.

**The four foreign finalists.** V14 gives each a constraint and a way of meeting it. Robin Chen has a tradition: a ballet piece with fixed choreography, and a step sequence that covered a step-out (`MD-EV-V14-0013`). Belinda Bergamasco has a limit: a reduced layout after a leg injury and a strategy rebuilt on the component mark (`MD-EV-V14-0015`). Mia Ocelot has two coaches, one of them her uncle Liam Ocelot, who disagree in public about whether she should be coached to the rules or to the crowd (`MD-EV-V14-0018`). Baek Horang has a conflict inside the criteria, between a style that stills the upper body and a criterion that wants it used, solved by design (`MD-EV-V14-0019`).

**Karasuba Daria.** She leads at the boundary with 71.59, on a triple Lutz, a double Axel, a level-4 step sequence and a second-half combination (`MD-PERF-0117`). Tsukasa had judged Daria out of form all season.

Inori holds one card the short program did not admit. Tsukasa reasons that the result is the sum of two segments and that the quadruple has until now had to win back what the short program lost (`MD-EV-V15-0009`). The gap is 0.33.

## Body and risk

The body enters the record as stated pain, reported injury and adult rules about volume. No diagnosis is made here beyond what characters say.

**Shin pain (V03).** After the Kyoto program Inori Yuitsuka disclosed, on being asked, two days of shin pain trained through. A first-aider named possible shin splints; rest and strength work followed (`MD-SK-0017`, `MD-EV-V03-0032`). The terms are the characters'. A year later Tsukasa Akeuraji refused to add practice volume on this ground (`MD-SK-0020`).

**Growing pains and regulated volume (V09–V11).** Eight months after the nationals Inori is taller, has knee pain and is recovering jumps (`MD-SK-0060`). In V10 the pain is in both knees in the evening, no injury is found, and the quadruple cannot be trained (`MD-SK-0062`). In V11 Kanayumi Miho, a medical trainer, and Tsukasa reorganize the work: taping, rest days, a daily quota of attempts at a new element, and analysis after a failure in place of immediate repetition (`MD-SK-0067`, `MD-EV-V11-0010`).

**Over-training (V13).** After her elimination Inori trained against that regime: school missed for ice time, a cracked blade, a day of fever. Tsukasa, who had never overruled her, told her that her volume must be regulated while she grows (`MD-SK-0083`, `MD-EV-V13-0034`, `MD-EV-V13-0035`). Whether she complied is not shown.

**Eight centimetres (V14).** Inori states her height as 146 centimetres, eight more than in the summer of her sixth school year. Kamogawa Kōhei reports that growing pains and a changing body limited her practice through the season; none is shown at the Final (`MD-SK-0087`, `MD-EV-V14-0008`). The night before the free skate she is carried upstairs to spare her legs (`MD-SK-0092`).

**Hikaru's back.** Hikaru Kamisaki's lower-back pain in practice is noticed by Okazaki Iruka and not by Sonidori Shin'ichirō, her coach of record (`MD-SK-0066`, `MD-EV-V10-0043`). Hikaru rests and returns. Riley Fox, her new head coach, attributes the injury to over-practice under club-mates' eyes; Hikaru does not dispute it (`MD-EV-V12-0022`). Hikaru reports no pain after her short program.

**Riley Fox's agreement.** Riley Fox permitted Hikaru three quadruples once, on an agreement that quadruples will be limited for a time when Hikaru's growth stops. Riley Fox's stated reasoning is that a skater will not accept talk of peaking until the wish to challenge has been discharged. A margin note says such limits depend on the skater (`MD-SK-0082`, `MD-EV-V13-0031`). The limit has not yet applied.

**Iruka's fracture.** Iruka went down in a competition warm-up; the fall and its cause are not shown (`MD-SK-0077`). V13 reports a compound fracture, three months, and withdrawal from the Final (`MD-EV-V13-0036`). In V15 Karasuba Daria counts the cost in the sport's stated currency: up to 850 ranking points from this Final and the world junior championships together, a senior debut without the record that buys entries, and an Olympics three years away that leans on both; she adds that jumps are easily lost in these years (`MD-EV-V15-0012`). `MD-CL-0022@12` retains this formulation.

**Coaches' positions.** `MD-CL-0024@9` collects a range, not a doctrine. Gori Seiji builds his practice on preventing injury and calls the belief that growth takes jumps away half fact and half curse (`MD-EV-V10-0025`); his own skater, Iruka, is the one injured. Kōhei declines a quadruple for Ahiru Miku, a light young skater, saying he does not know its risk (`MD-SK-0046`). Yodaka Jun, coaching Hikaru in secret, bound her to a rule that one fall in a nightly run-through was terminal (`MD-EV-V08-0038`). Riley Fox permits the extreme layout once and attaches a limit. Kyōboku Yutaka argues against Daria's device and then backs it. Tsukasa defines himself as the adult who does not brake, arranges for others who will (`MD-EV-V11-0033`), and draws bodily limits in V03, V04 and V13. The recorded post-skate responses often absorb departures, while risks are fenced beforehand by tests, quotas and agreements. That observed pattern does not establish that no adult ever punishes a risk: Yodaka’s reported nightly sanction remains a counterpressure, whose enforcement is not shown in that scene. A rule, not a person, sanctioned Inori in November.

## Scoring and institutions, as the story states them

All of the following is in-story statement. None was checked against any external document.

**Badges and classes.** Badge grade decides which events a child may enter: grade 5 for Novice B, grade 6 for Novice A and junior, grade 7 for senior eligibility. The double Axel is the barrier to girls' grade 6. Novice A covers ages eleven and twelve, junior thirteen to eighteen, and the class year turns in July. The Kyoto field showed that a badge grade sorts skaters into an event without describing their level (V03 reading).

**Novice A.** Ten elements; Lutz, flip and loop each required; an Axel-type jump compulsory; the last two jumping passes multiplied by 1.1; a fall deducted at 0.50; three grades of under-rotation. The compulsory Axel forces a low-value double on most skaters, so a triple Axel frees a pass (`MD-SK-0050`). The three credited triple Axels at the nationals belonged to the three medalists, Hikaru Kamisaki, Kamoto Suzu and Koara Ako (`MD-SK-0059`).

**The junior short program.** Three jumping passes: a required solo jump that rotates yearly and is the Lutz this season, a solo double Axel, and a combination. Women may not attempt quadruples in it (`MD-SK-0063`). A triple Axel may open the combination (`MD-SK-0074`). A missed combination cannot be attached to a later jump, because the remaining jumps are required solo jumps (`MD-SK-0078`, `MD-EV-V13-0004`). That rule is the only stated mechanism in Inori Yuitsuka's November result: one opened jump cost a whole pass with no remedy.

**The cut.** From junior level only the top twenty-four of the short program skate the free (`MD-EV-V13-0006`). The event is said to govern entry to the senior national championships and selection for the world junior championships. What the elimination cost Inori in either is not stated.

**The series and the Final.** Seven events; at most two per skater; points of fifteen, thirteen, eleven, nine and seven by place; six women reach the Final. The result is the sum of two segments, and a second-half combination in the short program carries a one-tenth bonus.

**World ranking and Olympic selection.** V14 teaches a second channel of standing. Ranking points are awarded by the grade of an event and the result, over three years; every place at this Final earns points. Inori is 141st with 432 points, beside finalists holding 475 to 2,194 (`MD-EV-V14-0009`). One Olympic place goes to the national champion before the Games; up to three exist; for the others the ranking weighs heavily, with conditions that vary by cycle. The senior age rule is unchanged this cycle, so her first eligible Games are seven years on (`MD-EV-V14-0003`). `MD-CL-0005@14` reads these volumes as showing result and standing parting in both directions: a series finalist cut from her national championship on a short program 17.32 below her Bangkok score, then second at the Final a month later with the same list of elements, while ranked 141st.

**The component mark.** A score is the technical mark plus a component mark of three parts: Composition with five listed criteria, Presentation with three, Skating Skills with five (`MD-SK-0086`, `MD-EV-V14-0014`). Kamogawa Kōhei, watching as a spectator, works it on four programs. Whether a five-beat piece is skated in five is a matter of Composition and of knowledge. Looseness on an unstable surface is Skating Skills, a component with technical criteria and no base value. A design can reconcile a style with a criterion it offends. And a crowd-pleasing program is marked down because long passages on two feet do not use the skill the sport is rooted in. The lesson is candid that Presentation needs the live experience and is where a hall and a panel can disagree. The page shows that divergence once: the loudest ovation of the first three and the lowest printed component total, 27.12 (`MD-EV-V14-0017`).

**The mixed zone.** After skating, athletes pass a mixed zone in which cameras precede writers, in an order said to follow sponsors' support, and coaches may be asked for comment (`MD-EV-V15-0014`). Kyōboku Yutaka, the coach of the leader Karasuba Daria, is interviewed. Tsukasa Akeuraji is told he is not needed.

## Expression

The series treats artistry as something built, and it lets the contrary view speak.

**Built from the start.** In V01 Inori Yuitsuka feared looking foolish while dancing, and Tsukasa Akeuraji danced as a model for her to imitate (`MD-SK-0004`). At Kyoto she derived from his demonstration a slowed arm led from the shoulder (`MD-SK-0016`). At the V04 camp Shirone Kohaku, a ballet instructor, taught posture as a daily habit, and Inori applied ballet hands in her badge test (`MD-SK-0023`, `MD-EV-V04-0032`). The effect on any mark is unknown.

**The film program.** Inori's junior short program is set to music from a film about a timid boy who trains with his own double, with martial-arts choreography (`MD-EV-V11-0019`). In Bangkok she judged two rivals' stage presence far beyond her own, and her free skate's choreographic sequence scored poorly by report; Tsukasa and Riley Fox both attributed that to too little rehearsal with music across a full rink, which Tsukasa called conjecture (`MD-SK-0071`). In Yokohama the hall clapped in time before the program fell apart (`MD-EV-V13-0003`). In Beijing its story is told and she places herself in it: the first jump as the scene in which the boy earns his place, Tsukasa as the ghost that strengthens him, the last jump as the part she must complete by her own strength (`MD-EV-V14-0023`, `MD-EV-V14-0028`, `MD-EV-V14-0029`). The work does not apply the film's ending, in which the helper withdraws, to Inori and Tsukasa; in V15 she says she does not find that ending happy (`MD-EV-V15-0046`).

**Feeling calculated in reverse.** After Bangkok Hitomi Takamine located the room in Inori's component mark in Presentation. Tsukasa answered with a drill he named as emotional expression calculated in reverse: the gestures a body makes under a feeling are taken apart, exaggerated for distance and built into the choreography (`MD-SK-0084`, `MD-EV-V14-0026`). He concedes that success depends partly on the skater's sense. The measurable result is one number: a component total of 30.36, the highest printed component total among the skaters then marked, 0.14 above the next printed value; Baek Horang’s component breakdown is absent. The three components are not printed separately, so the gain cannot be assigned to Presentation.

**Hikaru's inherited music.** Hikaru Kamisaki's novice free skate used the piece with which Yodaka Jun won a world junior title, assigned by him (`MD-EV-V08-0035`). At the All-Japan Junior she skated the same music with choreography revised overnight: a part danced in Yodaka's manner, a full stop, and then her own. She had resolved to leave what she calls the miniature garden, a word from her program's title (`MD-EV-V13-0013`, `MD-EV-V13-0017`, `MD-SK-0081`). Appraisals divide. Leonid Sorokin, her choreographer, calls it a change for the worse; Sonidori Shin'ichirō calls it the performance most like Yodaka's (`MD-EV-V13-0027`, `MD-EV-V13-0031`). The panel gave it the largest margin the series has printed.

**Daria's costume and verdict.** Karasuba Daria's short program is set to a film piece whose title means a guilty verdict, in a self-made costume that turns from black to red inside a jump. The device had been failing through the season and stiffening her movement. Kyōboku Yutaka, her coach, advised dropping it; she refused, and he backed her (`MD-SK-0090`, `MD-EV-V15-0005`). She gives the program's meaning herself: a verdict on those who did not see her friend Iruka's suffering, and on herself (`MD-EV-V15-0007`).

**The doctrine and its counter-voices.** `MD-CL-0030@2` holds, at moderate confidence, that V14 advances a doctrine: taste is personal; a panel therefore needs a shared ruler; the component mark is that ruler; most of it can be read by knowledge and technique; and because no young skater can meet it alone, the solidarity of skater, coach and choreographer appears in the mark (`MD-EV-V14-0020`, `MD-EV-V14-0032`). It is the series' fullest statement of why a coach from ice dance matters to a jumper.

The work bounds it in three ways. First, its spokesman, Kamogawa Kōhei, has an interest: he is a former ice dancer whose taste coincides with the criteria, and the page says so. Second, the contrary view is voiced and not refuted. Liam Ocelot, an Olympic champion and Mia Ocelot's uncle, holds that a skater should charm the world and change the rules, and cites Yodaka as someone who did; the hall is on his side (`MD-EV-V14-0018`). Third, V15 supplies a counter-case. Kyōboku was in his own day a skater attached to original expression. He backed a device the criteria do not ask for, against his own advice and against what Tsukasa would have done in his place, and Daria won the segment (`MD-EV-V15-0004`). V15 also turns the ruler against a different use in its last captions, a ruler put in Tsukasa's hand as a boy so that he would stay ordinary (`MD-EV-V15-0052`, `MD-MOT-0017`).

Daria and Inori, the two skaters at the top of the short program, both had level-4 step sequences, and both teams made their programs jointly. Daria's win therefore does not refute the doctrine of joint making. It tells only against the preference for design that satisfies criteria over a skater's own device, and it is one skate.

## What the corpus cannot say

1. **Why Inori Yuitsuka's short program collapsed in November.** The page states a mechanism for the first jump only, a new head-down entry, and a rule that made the error irreparable. It gives no cause for the Lutz, the step sequence or the double Axel. The V13 reading notes that the faults were not confined to jumps, and stops there; V14 says the cause is still not stated (`MD-SK-0085`). This study supplies none.
2. **Unprinted scores.** Inori's Bangkok free skate and total; every score at Event 7; her final place in Yokohama; the parts of Daria's 71.59 and Baek's 71.24; every component mark before V14.
3. **The three components of 30.36.** Any statement that the drill raised Presentation, or that Inori's edges were marked in Skating Skills, is inference.
4. **Anything about the free skate of the Final.** The start order, Inori's layout beyond the inclusion of a quadruple Salchow, and the result lie outside the boundary. A preview page in the V14 volume stated a different outcome for the short program than V15's story pages show; the story governs.
5. **Rates.** No landing rate is established for any of Inori's jumps. A warm-up tally of four in five, a test of three in three and Tsukasa Akeuraji's practice logs are the only counts given.
6. **Real-world applicability.** Whether the stated values, levels, class ages, selection rules or ranking points correspond to any real season was not checked. The medical terms are the characters'.

## Fanbook data as labelled supplement

Both official fanbooks are audited supplements at their own source layer. Neither is an external rule reference.

Fanbook 01 reconstructs four programs already in the record, two at the first Meikō Cup and two at the West Japan meet, with start orders, element lists and scores that agree with the manga (`MD-EV-FB01-0026`), and carries a primer on classes and grades that is the fanbook's own summary (`MD-EV-FB01-0002`). Fanbook 02 prints a fifteen-place table and seven element lists for the Chūbu block, Inori Yuitsuka's among them with every element credited and a step sequence at level 3 (`MD-EV-FB02-0022`). It prints a fifteen-place table and three element lists for the national novice championship (`MD-EV-FB02-0025`). That table supplies final ranks the manga did not print: fifth 105.43, sixth 102.44, seventh 102.03, eighth 101.10, a ninth-place score of 87.26 that the manga never printed, then 85.88, 81.02, 80.07, 79.55, 74.67 and 74.32. These ranks are fanbook data.

A fanbook table has Okazaki Iruka winning the series Final at sixteen (`MD-EV-FB02-0027`); the manga says only that she won last season's women's final. A creator statement that Karasuba Daria will not change a layout in order to win agrees with V15 and is reported conception (`MD-EV-FB02-0020`). No conclusion here rests on fanbook data alone.

## Boundary, omissions and what this study does not claim

The endpoint is `MD-FR-FB02`: Japanese collected volumes 1 to 15, Scores 1 to 61, with the two fanbooks. Inori Yuitsuka is second after the short program of the Junior Grand Prix Final, and the warm-up for the free skate is in progress. The series is ongoing and every statement here is provisional.

Excluded: the anime, any translation, anything after Score 61, advertisements and previews, and parody chapters. Creator-layer and fanbook material is used only in the section so labelled.

Untested: every in-story rule against any real rulebook; every physical or medical statement against any source; the comparability of scores across panels, which a table compiled by Tsukasa Akeuraji assumes (`MD-COMP-0009`); and the base-value reconstruction of Inori's V09 program, which depends on two values the page does not print.

This study does not claim that the foundation will decide the Final, that Inori's quadruple is reliable, that the component doctrine is the author's settled view, or that any adult's position on risk is endorsed by the work. It does not explain the November collapse.
