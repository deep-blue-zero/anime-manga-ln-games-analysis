# Adult doctrines, institutions and the ethics of burden

## Argument and method

This study follows one question through fifteen volumes and two supplements: what a child's skating costs, who pays, and what the adults who pay say they are owed. Its sources are the [institutions ledger](../03%20Longitudinal%20Ledgers/MEDALIST_INSTITUTIONS_RESOURCES_AND_AUTHORITY_LEDGER.md), the [claims ledger](../03%20Longitudinal%20Ledgers/MEDALIST_CLAIMS_PREDICTIONS_AND_REVISIONS_LEDGER.md) and the readings they index. It executes the comparative study marked ready as `MD-RD-0044`, extended by `MD-RD-0076` and bounded by `MD-RD-0077`/`0078`.

The argument has four steps. Skating in this story is paid for in several currencies, and only some of them are money. The gates a child must pass are held by parents, coaches, rules and chance. The adults hold some fifteen distinguishable doctrines about risk and sacrifice; results on the ice do not sort them, and the work does not crown one. What it does instead is trace doctrines to their holders' pasts, let children answer them, and arrive in the last admitted volume at a position about who may decide and how fault is to be handled, not at a rule about how much a child may carry. That position is stated late, by adults, to an adult, Tsukasa Akeuraji. At the boundary Inori Yuitsuka, the one child to whom it is finally put, has agreed after a pause and withdrawn nothing.

The owner's instruction for this domain is to analyse opportunities, constraints, obligations, welfare, merit claims and competing goals without forcing an ethical verdict. Three kinds of statement are therefore kept apart: what a story page shows; what the claims ledger evaluates, with its named criterion; and what only a creator statement says. Where the work declines to judge, the study does not judge for it.

**Evaluative criteria in force.** Five claims here are marked EVALUATION. `MD-CL-0011@16` uses a care criterion that includes the recipient's options and boundaries. `MD-CL-0007@12` judges listening, practicable options and truthful uncertainty apart from winning, and from V05 tracks whether Tsukasa overrules Inori's stated wish. `MD-CL-0009@3` asks whether Ryoka Miketa's voiced wish to progress is respected, with the stake of her coach, Nachi Mario, distinguished. `MD-CL-0006@3` asks whether a child has a viable choice without threatened exclusion. `MD-CL-0018@2` asks whether an adult's affirmation of a child is conditional on performance. The institutions ledger adds a standing rule: success cannot itself absolve a decision. The other claims used (`0014`, `0022`, `0024`, `0025`, `0026`, `0030`, `0031`) are INFERENCE and carry no verdict.

## Resources: what skating costs here, and who bears it

The ledger gives almost no amounts, and the study gives none. What the work supplies is a list of scarcities and of the people who absorb each.

**Ice.** Hired ice is scarce from the first volume: sessions that may run past one in the morning by Tsukasa Akeuraji's account (`MD-IN-0003`), a 4:30 a.m. slot that is the only one Nachi Mario's one-pupil club can get (`MD-IN-0008`), private slots drawn by lot even for national qualifiers (`MD-IN-0055`). Several Nagoya clubs share one rink; Riley Fox's Tokyo club owns its own and, by her account, costs a family less in total (`MD-IN-0115`). Geography decides more than effort at two points: a prefecture with no rink, from which Kumano Mimasashi drives Umitsuki Waka to Nagoya and weighs ice time against the parents' load (`MD-IN-0047`), and a provincial rink so empty that Ahiru Miku had it to herself daily until it closed and ended her career (`MD-IN-0071`, `MD-IN-0093`). A fanbook bonus manga, at FB-FICTION weight, sets out a small club's week: hired ice on two evenings and one dawn, public sessions otherwise (`MD-IN-0155`).

**Fees and wages.** Lesson fees are quantified once: for Tsukasa as a late starter, daily ice-dance lessons cost about a young full-time worker's monthly pay; he could afford one a week, and three jobs then removed his practice time (`MD-IN-0152`). Nachi's one-pupil club runs at the margin, so her livelihood rests on Ryoka Miketa's attendance (`MD-IN-0013`). In Okazaki Iruka's household fees and care come apart: her parents pay for her skating and, by her own account on a story page, wound her and treat the payment as cancelling it (`MD-IN-0117`; `MD-EV-V11-0036`).

**Unpaid adult labour.** Ryoka's mother sews sequins through the night (`MD-IN-0008`). Nozomi Yuitsuka, the mother of Inori Yuitsuka, grants permission and equipment and travels (`MD-IN-0002`, `MD-IN-0023`); a bonus manga shows her driving Inori from school to the rink every day (`MD-EV-FB01-0022`). When Nozomi returns to full-time work she stops travelling and Mika Yuitsuka, Inori's adult sister, escorts (`MD-IN-0108`). Gori Seiji's wife chaperones Iruka, whose parents are kept at a distance (`MD-IN-0117`). Tsukasa, as assistant coach, buys a jump harness himself and pays for it by directing traffic at night while injured (`MD-IN-0068`, `MD-IN-0086`). Sonidori Shin'ichirō, a head coach, collapses from overwork after Hikaru Kamisaki's fame multiplied his pupils (`MD-IN-0122`).

**Informal and in-kind support.** Sekoma, the rink clerk, admits the seven-year-old Inori by privately paying her entry and fearing for his job (`MD-IN-0007`). Kago Kōichi's household gives the young Tsukasa work, a room, a borrowed tablet and clothes; in Yokohama it could not sponsor him and took him in as live-in help, which gave him sleep and a passed test (`MD-IN-0009`, `MD-IN-0018`, `MD-IN-0153`). Expertise is borrowed across rival clubs: Nachi as jump coach and the ballet teacher Shirone Kohaku for a camp (`MD-IN-0033`), and Kanayumi Miho, a medical trainer obtained as a shared appointment, at Tsukasa's insistence, by lending him to another club for months (`MD-IN-0104`, `MD-IN-0114`).

**The earned economy.** Beside this runs support that follows results. A regional title brings designated practice, judges' comments and an early place in the queue of Uobuchi Kakeru, a travelling specialist, and Tsukasa names that as the medal's real reward (`MD-IN-0055`, `MD-IN-0056`, `MD-IN-0065`). Federation designation brings practice slots, subsidies for competing abroad, a camp and a uniform to those chosen (`MD-IN-0042`, `MD-IN-0096`, `MD-IN-0103`). Sponsorship is conditional at every appearance: a national medal as the credential with which Tsukasa, as a late starter, could ask for it, and unpaid (`MD-IN-0094`); Iruka's contract reopened by one fracture (`MD-IN-0136`); a press zone ordered by sponsors' support (`MD-IN-0146`). From V14 the world ranking adds a cumulative table on which Inori stands 141st (`MD-IN-0139`), and V15 has Karasuba Daria explain it as a lifeline: one injury before two events costs Iruka up to 850 points, a senior debut with a record behind it, and standing for an Olympics three years on (`MD-IN-0148`).

**A late starter's poverty.** Tsukasa's history is the one case followed from end to end, and it changes as it is told. V01 gives self-teaching from fourteen and work that paid fees (`MD-IN-0001`). V13 gives a hobby circle that shared ice free and forbade jumps as too tall, too late and too poor (`MD-IN-0137`). V15 gives the household behind both: his father driven from his work, his elder brother Hajime's examination as the family's last stronghold, neighbours who judged them, and Tsukasa as a boy, who learned that wanting costs money and did not say what he wanted (`MD-IN-0150`; `MD-CS-0326`). The ledger's summary of the ice-dance years is exact: the barrier was money and his refusal of help, not rejection (`MD-IN-0152`).

**What the evaluation says.** `MD-CL-0011` began as the claim that beginnings are enabled by distributed, sometimes hidden care which can also intrude, conceal and oblige. By @6 it distinguishes unrequited care from an earned economy and notes that the two are not opposed on the page: the second is how the first kind of adult gets a child what she needs. By @14 the spectators' lesson joins them: a component mark is not raised by a skater alone, so the earned number contains the unearned help (`MD-EV-V14-0020`; `MD-CL-0030@2`). At @15 the evaluation of the adults is favourable where they share a burden without asking for return, and unfavourable only in the sense the work itself allows, that causes can be named: they steered a child, or did not look. Its counterevidence stands. Sekoma's help began with a frightening threat, Kago's early gifts were unsolicited and resisted, and those helped keep trying to pay (`MD-CL-0011@5`).

The children pay in a different currency. Standing conferred by designation is felt by Inori as a debt that a result can default on (`MD-IN-0134`). That is the subject of the fifth section.

**The children who leave.** Four exits are shown, and their layers differ.

*Mika.* On story pages her skating ended with a fracture in jump practice, as Inori remembers it (`MD-EV-V06-0047`; `MD-CS-0116`), and she describes herself without bitterness as someone who stopped before junior (`MD-CL-0014@7`). That she stopped to lighten her mother's load, and that Nozomi and Inori misread her tears, is a creator box only (`MD-EV-FB01-0007`; `MD-KN-0154`). Inori is not shown to know it.

*Ahiru Miku.* Her rink closes and her career ends on a bench at a national event (`MD-IN-0093`). Her reason is on a story page: everyone would have to strain very hard for her, and she could not put her dream first (`MD-EV-V09-0011`). Whom she meant to spare, and the author's comment that it is not a matter resolve about skating can settle, are FB-CREATOR (`MD-EV-FB02-0016`).

*Tsukasa as a boy.* He was not refused. He could not ask (`MD-EV-V15-0020`/`0036`).

*Iruka.* A fracture removes Iruka, the series favourite, from a championship, a final and a season; the warm-up is not stopped for her (`MD-IN-0127`, `MD-IN-0136`). She says she will not stop (`MD-EV-V13-0050`). The cause of the accident is not shown.

The gates that end careers in this story are seldom prohibitions. They are a closing rink, a fee, a body, a rule about age, and a child's own reckoning of what she costs.

### What the series does with this field

**It does not let results sort the doctrines.** The claims ledger records this at each test. At the novice nationals Yodaka's regime, the heaviest, scores highest, Suzu, the unburdened skater, is second and in tears, and Inori ends by choosing to carry Tsukasa's standing (`MD-CL-0026@1`). Miku's careful plan and Inori's daring one fail on the same day (`MD-CL-0024@5`). The dated contest between Tsukasa and Yodaka is decided on the scoreboard and dissolved the same night (`MD-CL-0025@8`).

**It gives doctrines causes.** Kanayumi says of Tsukasa's policy that it seems made out of his own past (`MD-EV-V15-0022`), and the volume shows the making: his father's sentence that a man who only costs money should vanish, and a son who built a coach who costs no one anything (`MD-EV-V15-0035`/`0039`). On story pages the same holds for Yodaka, whose rule is his own career; for Kōhei, whose creed about talent comes from the failure of his friend Tsukasa (`MD-EV-V09-0009`); for Kago, whose rule comes from a bereavement; and for Nozomi Yuitsuka, Inori's mother. For Kameganya, Gori and Riley Fox the biography is creator-layer only (`MD-EV-FB01-0019`, `MD-EV-FB02-0014`/`0018`). That the work treats doctrines generally as products of their holders' histories is this study's collation. The work states it of one man, Tsukasa.

**It lets children answer.** Hikaru argues Yodaka's creed against Tsukasa, who refuses it, concedes Tsukasa's answer and keeps the creed (`MD-EV-V09-0047`/`0048`). She corrects Riley Fox and receives an apology. Daria refuses Kyōboku's advice with a reason and he changes side. Inori tells Tsukasa that reassurance drains her (`MD-EV-V15-0048`). The one finalist whose own wish the page does not give is Mia Ocelot, whose two coaches, her uncle Liam Ocelot and one the page leaves unnamed, quarrel over her in public (`MD-EV-V14-0018`; `MD-IN-0142`).

**It turns the leading doctrine on its holder.** `MD-CL-0025@9` records that Tsukasa held the creed against sacrifice for children and not for himself, and that he says so. The author's reported comment that growing strong by offering something up is a beautiful composition and not necessarily right is FB-CREATOR corroboration (`MD-EV-FB02-0026`; `MD-RT-0119`), not the ground of the claim. A wider pattern is visible and should be held loosely: the adults who protect children from burden tend to do it by spending themselves (`MD-IN-0086`, `MD-IN-0122`). The work comments on this in one case only.

**Where it arrives is a rule about deciding.** `MD-CL-0024@9` notes that two coaches in V15, Kyōboku and Tsukasa, reach one position from opposite sides: each defers to a reason that concerns who the skater means to be. What Tsukasa keeps of his creed is narrower than before: that suffering should be chosen and should be one's own (`MD-EV-V15-0050`). He distinguishes forbidding a harm to Inori's body, which he did at V13, from overruling a purpose (`MD-CL-0007@12`). That evaluation is favourable with a reservation, and the reservation is his own: he sees in Inori the heat he once saw in Yodaka and says it is one with great suffering (`MD-EV-V15-0049`; `MD-OQ-V15-04`).

**Where the evidence is thin.** Several doctrines rest on one scene (Kameganya, Meiko, Nachi since V04) or on Tsukasa's memory of his teacher (Takamine in V15). Yodaka has not appeared since he left. The convergence of V15 is one volume old and untested by a result. No doctrine has been shown meeting an injury it caused; the two serious injuries shown have no stated cause.

## Burden and debt

`MD-CL-0026@9` counts four responses by a child to the cost of being supported.

**To stop.** Ahiru Miku, on a story page (`MD-EV-V09-0011`). Mika Yuitsuka, by a creator box only (`MD-EV-FB01-0007`).

**To hide.** Tsukasa Akeuraji as a boy practised at another rink so as not to trouble the adults who helped him, and concluded that he was in the way (`MD-EV-V15-0037`/`0038`). Inori Yuitsuka practises a jump in secret because Tsukasa was hurt on it (`MD-EV-V09-0023`), evades a founding rule by feigned illness (`MD-IN-0135`), and tells Tsukasa brightly that there is nothing to discuss (`MD-EV-V15-0047`).

**To pay, with results or with the body.** Umitsuki Waka, eliminated at a regional event, grieves in terms of what the driving cost her coach, Kumano Mimasashi (`MD-CS-0073`). ## Blame

`MD-CL-0031@1` registers a position, at MODERATE-HIGH, from one volume: to find the causes of a harm is necessary and is not the same as sentencing a person; sorting those involved into guilty and innocent stops thought; taking the whole blame oneself is one form of that sorting, and declaring everyone kind is another; and suffering is not to be made small by comparison or by the excuse that no one knew.

**The speakers.** The claim counts four and calls them independent. Kago Kōichi states it from his own hunt for a culprit after the death of his wife, Meiko, which stopped when it reached his small daughter, Yō (`MD-EV-V15-0034`). Kanayumi Miho states its corollaries (`MD-EV-V15-0019`/`0023`). Karasuba Daria reaches it by skating a verdict of guilt and then recognizing that she had filed Inori Yuitsuka under a word and stopped looking (`MD-EV-V15-0007`/`0013`). Tsukasa Akeuraji applies it to himself and Inori: a failure has causes, a cause is not a crime, and neither of them needs punishing (`MD-EV-V15-0051`). The first three stand on separate grounds: a bereavement, a professional's observation, a program. Tsukasa has heard all three that evening. His statement is an application and not a fourth independent witness.

**The retelling.** The volume enacts the rule on its own earlier material. At V13 Tsukasa told the hobby circle as kind adults who forbade and a boy who obeyed (`MD-EV-V13-0042`). At V15, promised that no one will be marked black or white, he tells it again and the facts move. The prohibition was advice with no rule behind it. He hid his practice. They came to watch, bandaged him, reproached and praised him, and apologized next day. He left on his own conclusion that he was in the way (`MD-EV-V15-0036`/`0037`/`0038`). Nothing in the first account was false; it had been sorted. Kago's account of the mechanism is the careful one: adults steered a child by a force he could not resist, in good will and without noticing, and their failed forecast was then counted as his own choice (`MD-EV-V15-0040`). A fanbook timeline summarizes the episode as relations worsening; that is the sorted account again, and the story governs (`MD-EV-FB02-0030`).

**Daria's verdict and its retraction.** Her program condemns the adults who failed her friend Okazaki Iruka, and herself for not having seen it. She had extended the verdict to Inori. She bows, explains what Iruka has lost, calls herself the presumptuous one, and then alerts Kanayumi to Inori's words (`MD-EV-V15-0011`/`0012`/`0016`). What she retracts is her treatment of a bystander. Her anger at those who failed Iruka is not retracted.

**The limits.** The position does not excuse: the same voices name what adults did to a child. The claim's own counterevidence is kept. It is one volume. The program that is a verdict wins the segment with 71.59. Inori is not shown to accept that she needs no punishment; the observation row for Tsukasa's sentence notes that its effect on her burden is not shown. A rival reading is that this is a therapeutic episode for one character and not a position of the work; against it stand three unprompted speakers and the retelling.

**Design context.** A fanbook box reports that the series was first conceived with a child, Yō, as Inori's supporter and that Tsukasa, the adult coach, was created after an editor asked what the adults were doing (`MD-EV-FB01-0012`; `MD-RT-0116`). These are statements of design at FB-CREATOR weight and are evidence for no event.
