# Volume 3 — the time that cannot be recovered

Volume 2 left open what Inori would do with a result she did not want. Volume 3 gives her one. At the West Japan meet she skates the best program of her life, with every element credited, and loses to a girl whose jumps she cannot yet attempt. The loss is not a failure of nerve or of execution. It is a measurement of years: her rival has been on the ice since she was five. On the train home, with a sprained ankle and a silver medal she was not present to receive, Inori says the thing the first two volumes had been circling. The time she lost by starting late can never be recovered, and she will never make that a reason to give up.

The reading's argument is that this volume replaces catching up with a different project. Until now both Inori and her coach have treated her late start as a debt to be repaid by working harder and faster. Kyoto shows that the debt cannot be repaid, and shows in Ema what repaying it by force does to a body. What Inori and Tsukasa decide instead is to stop hurrying: no winter competitions, a year of building. The volume then jumps that year and shows what it bought. Its last chapter introduces a boy for whom the same arithmetic runs the other way, an early starter with every advantage who believes he is already finished, and ends with Inori making a new promise in anger that sits uneasily with the one she made on the train.

## The unwanted result

`MD-OQ-V02-01` asked how Inori would respond to a result she did not want, and what her coach would then say. Volume 3 answers both, once.

The loss is constructed to exclude the easy explanations. She does not fall in the program. She is not robbed: the manga shows Ema's content, element by element, and it is harder at almost every jump. She does not collapse under pressure after a morning that offered every excuse, with lost skates, an absent coach and a 23-point wall. A caption gives the verdict without softening it. What remains when excuses are removed is the gap itself, and the volume names its unit: years.

Her response on the train has three parts and each one is a refusal of something she had done before. She does not deny the gap, which she had half done by assuming Ema was near her. She does not blame herself, though self-blame had been her first move in every earlier setback. And she does not promise to outwork it, which had been her creed as recently as V02's "私は出遅れているんだから人よりも頑張って追いつかないと". She treats the lost time as a fixed fact, declines to count it as a reason to stop, and gives up being frantic about it. She will keep believing she can win "as the self who started late".

This is the opposite of resignation, and the difference is checkable. Resignation would lower the aim; she keeps it. What changes is the timetable and the mood. `MD-CL-0019@1` registers the response at the strength the text allows: one defeat, one statement, and then a year the manga mostly does not show. The year's outcome is consistent with it. She passes four grades, wins, and by her coach's account has become strong against being unable. But the outcome was produced off the page, and the volume's last chapter gives the vow its first real test and she bends it. Provoked by Rio, she sets herself a deadline (this year) and a target borrowed from someone else's examination (a triple and double combination, which is the boys' requirement, before Rio lands it). Whether that is the old haste returning in the service of loyalty, or a new ambition a stronger body can now carry, the volume does not decide. It has made the question exact.

## Three decisions and an apology

`MD-CL-0007@2` held that Tsukasa's certainty had acquired a boundary, and noted that the hard case, his conduct around a loss, was untested. `MD-CL-0010@2` asked what his judgment would do if Inori's good and his own contest with Yodaka pulled apart. Volume 3 supplies four pieces of evidence.

**He ran.** The decision to fetch the skates himself was generous and wrong. It left an eleven-year-old without a coach at her second competition, depended on a train he did not control, and by the author's own aside was not even the fastest option. The volume does not hide this behind the rescue. He doubts it within minutes of returning, in terms of responsibility. Jakuzure says it to his face. Hitomi says it severely enough that he travels back to apologize. The impulse is of a piece with V02's pledge, a man whose first instinct is to spend himself, and the volume shows what that instinct costs when the pupil needs presence more than sacrifice.

**He said no.** When Inori proposes to raise her difficulty at the boards, he refuses and explains, and the explanation is about scoring and not about her limits. He anticipates that she will wonder why he does not simply say "let's try". This is the mirror image of 名港杯, where she added difficulty without asking and he was moved. The difference is that here she asked first, and that he had a reason he could state. She still hears him say she can win. She tests the claim against his face and accepts it.

**He ordered rest.** This is the first clear case in the series where what Inori wants and what is good for her separate. She wants to train through the last week of the holidays because she is behind. He tells her to stop, tells her why, and holds to it. The fused-interest worry of `MD-CL-0010@2` predicted that a coach who had staked himself on beating Yodaka's pupil might push. He does the reverse, and five days later states the principle: the aim is not to catch the leaders quickly. That is direct evidence against the worry, in the one situation so far that could have confirmed it.

**He apologized.** After the defeat he does not console, re-guarantee or look for a cause outside himself. He says the coaching side was lacking and that preventing injuries and accidents is his to think about. He had not known about the shin pain. She had been training on it for two days and had filed it under muscle soreness. Whether a more experienced coach would have caught it is not something the text settles, but he treats it as his.

Taken together these revise both claims. On `MD-CL-0007`: his "絶対" has not disappeared (he says it of getting her to the start), but it now attaches to his own effort or to a reasoned plan, and it coexists with a readiness to say he was wrong. On `MD-CL-0010`: the feared divergence arrived in a mild form and he chose her welfare. The claim stays open for a different reason. The entanglement now runs in both directions. In V02 he fought an adult for her possibility. In V03 she fights a boy for his reputation, and stakes a jump on it. The pair's interests are more fused than before, and the party now at risk of overreaching on the other's behalf is the child.

The year plan is also the first major decision the two are said to have made together ("いのりさんと話し合って"). The train scene is what made it possible. A coach cannot hold a frantic child out of competition for a winter; he can do it with one who has just said she will stop being frantic.

## Ema, and what a detour is worth

Ema is the volume's most carefully built figure and she is built as a comparison. The prologue supplies the terms: genius for results in an instant, 秀才 for results over years. Her story then takes the second term apart.

Her history is Inori's present, run earlier and worse. She fell behind for a reason outside her control, tried to close the gap by volume, and injured herself repeatedly. At eight or nine she stopped eating to stop growing. Her coach's instruction was to rest, and she did not manage it until she understood why. The grade-1 test, which children at her club pass in passing, she failed twice. What the volume shows on the ice is what came out the other side: jumps no other entrant has, landed as a matter of course. Jakuzure's account of the mechanism is plain. The detour was not wasted. The strength and experience built while she could not do what the others did became the base everything else stands on.

Three things follow for the larger argument. First, the first-aid room and the flashback are placed back to back on purpose. Inori is told to rest for shin pain on one page; Ema's instruction to rest is shown a few pages later. Ema is the demonstration of what Inori's creed of catching up by effort would have done to her, and of what the alternative yields. Tsukasa says as much: hearing the story gave him confidence in the decision to wait.

Second, Ema and her coach are a second coach-and-pupil pair with the same shape as the first, and the manga marks it. Jakuzure's "whatever height you grow to, I will make you a fine skater" is the same kind of promise as Tsukasa's "whoever you turn out to be", made to a child whose body, not her nerve, was the uncertain thing. He, too, refuses the credit. The differences matter as much. Ema's detour happened inside the strongest club in the country, with a head coach who suggested moving her on. That suggestion can be read as concern, as he frames it, or as a strong club's way of shedding a slow child; the text gives his words and Jakuzure's objection and no verdict. `MD-CL-0020@1` registers the comparison and keeps that ambiguity.

Third, Ema's view of herself resists both labels. She is not proud and not modest. Hard jumps are "only natural" given how much she did. That sentence quietly removes the mystery from talent in her case, and it is also, read once more, the sentence of a child who has learned to treat an enormous amount of work as nothing special. The volume does not press that second reading and neither does this one.

What Inori takes from watching is the image at 0119: herself picking up shining things one at a time while someone far ahead carries a full basket. It is the same recognition she had before Hikaru in V02, with one change. Hikaru's distance was a marvel. Ema's is an amount, and Inori can see what it is made of.

## Believing by decision

V02 ended with the coach resolving that confidence is not something others give. V03 shows the pupil reaching self-belief by the opposite road, and says so in her own words.

Alone outside the arena, she does not discover that she is good. She is explicit that she knows her no-good self in detail and hates it. What she decides is which self to stand with: the one her coach believed in. The belief is borrowed, and she knows it is borrowed, and she chooses it. The passage is also the fullest account so far of where the no-good self came from: a child for whom a place to fail was prepared before she tried. That phrase gathers up the mother's forecast in V01, the adults' comparisons in V02, and the mother's own instant "I knew this would happen" on the morning of the lost skates, which the mother then visibly overrides.

`MD-CL-0015@1` described a recurrent pattern in which Inori defines herself by a deficit and acts from it, and declined to call it a trait. Volume 3 adds instances on both sides. The self-blame still arrives first: "私また…", "こんな私で". So does the haste, and now with a bodily cost, since she trained through pain and thought only of building muscle faster. Against that, she now has moves that interrupt it. She postpones self-reproach because it would stop her skating. She supplies her own encouragement. In the program she turns "I do not have the muscle for this" into "so I can still improve" inside one caption. A year on, her coach thinks failure no longer shakes her. The fair summary is that the content of the pattern has not changed and her handling of it has. `MD-CL-0015@2` says that, and continues to withhold the word trait: the evidence is still almost entirely from evaluative settings, and the last chapter shows the old urgency can be re-lit from outside.

There is a detail in this sequence that belongs to the relationship. She saw him tremble in front of Yodaka. V02 gave his fear from the inside; V03 confirms she read it from the outside and has kept it. Part of what she borrows from him is not his confidence but the sight of him contradicting someone while afraid.

## Rio, or the other arithmetic

Score 11 turns the volume's question round. Inori started late with nothing behind her and believes she can win. Rio started early with an Olympic medalist for a father and coach, shares a house with the best novice in the country, lands the jump Inori cannot, and believes he is finished.

The text gives enough to see the structure and not enough to be sure of the person; he has one chapter. Three things are shown. He is praised only by reference to someone else, as his father's son or his father's pupil. He knows that the praise Hikaru's success reflects onto his father is unearned, because he knows who really coaches her, and so the compliments he receives are doubly hollow. And he has failed the grade-6 test at least four times, a fact the admiring bystanders evidently do not know. "サラブレッドとしてもう終わってんの" is the conclusion he has drawn. Yodaka's sentence to him, that he is the one in Hikaru's way, has lodged in him the way the sentence to Inori lodged in her, and the manga uses the identical device for both: the dark panel breaking into a moment of something good.

Tsukasa's response is the series' recognition scene played for a child with the opposite problem. In V01 he told a girl nobody rated that she was already admirable. Here he tells a boy everybody rates for the wrong reasons what, specifically, is his. He can do this because of the skill Jakuzure named: he sees that the jump is not the father's. Rio's panic at being praised is comic and precise. He has no way of receiving a compliment that is actually about him.

`MD-CL-0001@2` distinguished enabling from selecting recognition and asked for a case where selection misfires. Rio is that case. Being selected by birth has given him access to everything and a standing judgment that he must already be good, and the judgment has become the thing he fails. The claim is revised to say that the selecting regime burdens as well as privileges, at least here. `MD-CL-0021@1` registers Rio himself at low to moderate confidence.

His challenge to Inori is the credential argument at its most reasonable. He does not sneer at Tsukasa. He asks what the man did, whether he can perform what he teaches, and whether it is wise to stake a single career on someone with no record. He even concedes that rare coaches have a gift purely for teaching. Everything V01 and V02 established about Tsukasa says the argument is wrong in this instance. Nothing says it is wrong in general, and Inori does not rebut it. She overrides it ("ごり押し", as the caption has it), and then proposes to settle it by results. Rio's private reaction is the truest line in the chapter: he wishes he could talk like that.

## Yodaka, a little further

The volume adds four facts and no explanation. The colour page states that his coaching of Hikaru is not public. He coaches by demonstration: he jumps, at night, and says "remember it". Asked by Rio what he is coaching her for, he does not answer. Accused of having run away from skating, he reacts as to a blow, and replies by wounding the child.

Each of these fits more than one account. A champion who retired at twenty and still jumps alone at night for one pupil has not left skating in any ordinary sense; "ran away" may be a boy's cruelty or may touch something true about why he does not appear. The secrecy protects someone, and the text does not say whom. His cruelty to Rio could be contempt, or impatience with a child he thinks is wasting himself on a misreading, or simple retaliation. `MD-CL-0016@2` records the pattern and keeps the motive open. The chapter title, 夜が嫌い, is not attached to a speaker within these pages. It may be Rio's, whose nights contain that rink.

One thing can be said about his effect. His two spoken verdicts on children have both become intrusive thoughts in their targets. Tsukasa called a medalist's denial a curse in V02. V03 shows that this was an accurate description of what it does.

**In-story rule and procedure statements.** Senior All-Japan qualification runs through block competitions and the West or East Japan championship. The pre-skate practice here is five minutes for grade 1 and six for novice. A skater must begin within thirty seconds of being called. A grade-1 program has six elements; a spiral is not a scored element at that grade but can raise component marks. The component score is assessed under five headings, of which only three (skating skills, performance, interpretation) are marked at beginner and grade 1. Jump value rises in the order toe loop, Salchow, loop, flip, Lutz, Axel within each number of rotations, shown as a ladder without figures. Quality marks depend, among other things, on a correct-edge take-off and on flow through the landing. Grade 6 is required of girls for Novice A, grade 4 of boys; the boys' grade-6 test requires a triple and double combination; the double Axel is the barrier to girls' grade 6. Rio adds that grade 6 and a double Axel would not clear the All-Japan Novice qualifier, which is his claim and not a caption's.

**Depicted performance.** Inori at Kyoto: both doubles landed in the practice; a fall before the music; single Axel, double Salchow, double loop, Lutz and Axel sequence, step sequence at the base level and a flying sit spin at level 1 with a difficult broken-leg variation, six credit marks in six; a fall into ice damage after the final pose. Ema: double Lutz, double loop, single Axel, double flip and double toe loop, step sequence, flying camel spin, six in six. Kanna: Lutz and double toe loop with unusual height on the second jump, a solo double toe loop. Ritsuki: double Salchow left alone by the hole, double loop, double toe loop and Axel as a replacement. Miho: double toe loop and single toe loop, a held spiral. Seira: a double Salchow shown. A year later Inori's grade-5 program lists eleven elements including all five doubles below the Axel, a three-jump combination and three levelled spins. In practice her double Axel is rotated and falls. Rio lands a double Axel.

**Attributed appraisal.** Judges on Kanna's height and force; a judge on Inori's step sequence as the best in the field; Jakuzure on her stability after four months; Miho and Seira disagreeing about what is impressive; Tsukasa on Rio's jump as a long type unlike his father's; Hitomi on Tsukasa's year; Tsukasa on Inori having grown strong against "can't".

**Analytical inference.** `MD-CL-0008@2` credited the foundation-first strategy with a spin that earned credit. Kyoto extends it. With the same two doubles as three of the four skaters she beat, and fewer than the fourth, Inori outscored all of them; the manga attributes the difference to a step sequence it calls a weapon produced by that strategy and to credit on every element. The same result shows the strategy's ceiling. Quality does not make up for content two levels harder, and Ema had both. The coach's refusal to escalate was right about what could be gained on the day and could not have changed the outcome.

`MD-CL-0005@2` separated practice, warm-up and competition. Kyoto adds that a badge grade is weak evidence of level. The grade-1 field contained a first-time competitor from speed skating scoring 23, a second-grader with a double toe loop, and a child with a double Lutz who had failed the grade's test twice. Category by badge sorted them into one event; it did not describe them.

Physical state: a probable ankle sprain from the post-program fall, reported healed five days later; shin pain named by a first-aider as possible shin splints, treated, with strength training to prevent recurrence. These are in-story statements by a first-aider and a coach. No medical claim is made here beyond what they say.

## Written Japanese

The Kyoto field is distinguished by speech as much as by drawing. Seira and Miho speak Hiroshima forms (じゃ, けえ, ちょる, ウチ), Seira loudly and Miho in a mutter. Ritsuki speaks polite です and ます forms as a weapon, and drops them to curse under her breath. Ema and Jakuzure speak Kyoto and Osaka Kansai. Kanna barely speaks. The effect is that regional strength, the chapter's title, is audible on the page in the only way a page allows.

Rio's speech carries his situation. He calls Yodaka クソジジイ and あのジジイ. He says 親父 where the furigana-style gloss beside it reads この人 or あの人, "this person", so that the word for father is printed with distance over it. He addresses Inori as アンタ and then あんた. His "できないんだよ" does not say what cannot be done; it could be her aim, or his own test, and the page lets both stand.

Sonidori's register is the formal opposite of Yodaka's. He introduces himself with と申します, apologizes with お詫び申し上げます, and says 明浦路先生. In V02 the gold medalist asked "キミは何者?". In V03 the silver medalist gets the name right, which the volume has by then established as something even Hitomi and Inori cannot do.

Inori's key lines are marked typographically. "私たちは" is printed with emphasis dots on the plural. Her assertions to Rio end in もん, a child's insisting particle, and the caption names the mode as ごり押し. On the train her register is the plain polite speech she always uses to her coach, and the sentence is built on まま: as the self who started late, unchanged. The mother's "ため息禁止" is said to herself. None of this establishes how any line sounds.

## Manga form

**The jump ladder and the wall.** Score 9 introduces a second diagram beside the element row: two columns of jump names ranked by value, on which each skater's content is marked. It lets a reader with no skating knowledge see at a glance that Ema's markers sit above everyone's. The ranking board with a ruled line and the label "wall" does the same for scores. The volume teaches its own scoring so that the defeat can be understood as fair.

**A planted hazard.** The hole Kanna leaves at 0056 is noted by onlookers, catches Ritsuki at 0059, and takes Inori down at 0097 after a clean program. It is a rare case in this series of a physical cause set up across forty pages, and it is placed so that the injury owes nothing to her skating. A pencil gag afterwards has Kanna filling it in.

**The intruding panel.** Yodaka's sentence returns as a dark, borderless panel at 0025 and 0035 for Inori and at 0179 for Rio, each time cutting into a moment of effort or pleasure. The repetition of the device across two children is the volume's way of showing that the two sentences are the same kind of act.

**Off-ice as on-ice.** Her run-through on asphalt at 0034–0041 is drawn with the full grammar of a performance: sequential figures, hand close-ups, a final pose. The watcher at the fence, later identified as a rival club's trainer, gives it an audience. The scene is hers alone and the form treats it as skating.

**Slowness.** When her legs fail, pages 0086–0087 break into wide horizontal strips with a single gesture each. The text is about slow movement staying in the eye and the layout does what it says.

**The gleaner.** At 0119 Inori's understanding of Ema is an image: a small figure stooping for one bright thing while a distant one carries many. It is the only extended visual metaphor in the volume and it is hers.

**The train.** Page 0136 gives her vow a full page with no background but the window light, seated, the bandaged foot in view. The composition is still. Compare the reflection page of V02, all motion and large lettering: the earlier resolve was a discovery, this one is a decision.

**Echoes.** The mother's cheek-slap at 0018 and the daughter's at 0025. The drawstrings, gripped in V01 and V02, tied into a bow at 0069. The fist bump, which Rio's question about changing coach is printed over at 0185. The empty second step of the podium at 0130 and the medal hung on a train at 0138.

**Time.** A year passes in two black bands and a caption. The elision is itself a formal choice with a cost for analysis: the period in which the train vow was either kept or not is the period the reader does not see.
