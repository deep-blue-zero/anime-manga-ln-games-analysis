# Volume 1 — making a beginning possible

Volume 1 turns two apparently disqualified beginners into an athlete and a coach. It does this through particular acts of recognition, instruction and support, rather than by demonstrating that conviction alone defeats time or money. Inori passes a beginner badge test and learns to sustain a program; Tsukasa discovers that his skating can help someone else develop. Neither has competed with this partnership when the main story ends. The final flying sit spin offers a credible next step within the depicted coaching account, while the repeated promise of victory remains untested.

This is a reading of the entire admitted V01 witness. Its bonus fiction materially changes the account of who enables those beginnings. The end state therefore includes those disclosures, while keeping their story times and evidential kinds distinct. ## Developments in disclosure order

Score 1 first gives a language of difficult technique, apparently effortless beauty and collective cost, then moves from Tsukasa's All-Japan appearance to a two-years-later failed show audition. His history starts skating at fourteen, outside the opportunities offered to younger entrants, with work financing self-directed practice and later ice-dance instruction. The retrospective account makes his failure a history of constrained access rather than proof that he never possessed skating ability. Kago offers support, but Tsukasa fears accepting money will change their relationship. This refusal precedes his attempt to help a child whose own access depends on adults. (`MD-EV-V01-0001`–`0002`.)

The first encounter with Inori is ethically mixed and comic. Tsukasa pursues a presumed nonpaying child; her escape ends in a hazardous leap, and he catches her. A scolding adult becomes a protective one without erasing the risk his pursuit helped create. Worms appear to be her currency with the rink clerk. Her heavily annotated manual then discloses concentrated learning hidden under her mother's ban. She plans to become good enough before asking permission, so imagined competence functions as a condition for being allowed to want something. Her school difficulties and peers' refusals explain that expectation without establishing a diagnosis or universal incapacity. (`0003`–`0004`.)

Hitomi offers Tsukasa an assistant role; he doubts that a person without the right record can responsibly teach. Inori's mother also treats prior experience as predictive: the elder daughter's painful skating history and Inori's school struggles seem to foretell failure. The trial separates these arguments from what Inori actually does. Tsukasa recognizes her effort before evaluating the skill, then helps turn self-taught movement into controllable propulsion. The child's pleasure does not settle the mother's concerns, but it gives the disagreement a concrete object beyond adult forecasts. Inori finally voices her wish, and Tsukasa promises an All-Japan route. Her mother consents hesitantly; boots and lessons make that permission effective. (`0005`–`0009`.)

Score 2 tests the new partnership against formal evaluation and social comparison. Inori chooses this month's beginner test over waiting. At the venue, she meets a girl who offers friendship before either recognizes the other's sporting status. Rio's ridicule disrupts her composure, and the friend intervenes. Before the test, Tsukasa redirects Inori's attention with a small finger task, a familiar-rink frame and her own worm-based coping reference. The depicted success is specific: she performs the required patterns and receives a pass. It is not evidence that she will never freeze under pressure. On the train, her mother adds a middle-school endpoint to permission. (`0010`–`0014`.)

Back at the rink, other parents disparage the late-starting child and her novice coach. Their words are superimposed over images of the smiling coaches: the visual arrangement gives form to Inori's feared judgment, not evidence that those coaches uttered the insults. The friend challenges the adults and is then welcomed as the famous Hikaru. This immediate reversal makes prestige a mechanism of recognition; there is no depicted parental apology or durable change of values. Skating together brings friendship, admiration and competition into the same relation. Hikaru's extraordinary jump is shown, but its exact element and rotations are not identified here. Inori wants to match and eventually beat her. (`0015`–`0016`.)

Hikaru's self-report changes what “genius” can mean to Inori: extensive practice, private coaching, night ice and many people's help coexist with a feeling of insufficiency. Hikaru cannot teach the beginner simply because she can perform, and she directs Inori back to her coach. Inori then explicitly asks Tsukasa to help her become world number one and win gold. Tsukasa welcomes that aspiration and gives it an immediate smaller target. The child's earlier wish for permission becomes a communicated demand for ambitious assistance; it does not become a fully informed consent to every future cost. (`0017`–`0018`.)

Score 3 moves from permission and ambition to a timed training decision. A flashback discloses the mother's limit to Tsukasa only after Inori has learned it. The 名港杯 in two months offers a beginner section and a possible medal distinct from Hikaru's expected novice territory. Inori chooses winning that section as the next goal. Her first program run contains a landed single Salchow but stops because she forgets what comes next. When she says she fears looking foolish while dancing, Tsukasa performs with her. His size attracts attention rather than hiding her, but the demonstration gives her something absorbing to imitate. Subsequent practice is joyful and sustained. (`0019`–`0021`.)

Ryoka initially offers attention and companionship, then makes friendship conditional on Inori rejecting adult reliance and proving herself alone. Her landed double Salchow turns “independence” into a competitive challenge. The rhetoric is not the practical source of her ability: Nachi's instruction, booked ice, her mother's costume labor and early-morning attendance enable it. Nachi also keeps her at beginner grade despite her request to advance, pursuing a win and fearing she might leave after defeat. His later gratitude and tears complicate a wholly cynical motive, without cancelling that exercise of authority. (`0022`–`0024`, `0027`–`0028`.)

Tsukasa explains a double-first strategy versus polishing the whole program first through cake and taiyaki. Inori tries to infer which answer he wants; he tells her to choose rather than read his mind. She chooses foundation and dancing because she wants beauty like his as well as victory. That is a reasoned choice within mentor admiration, not proof she has become independent of it. His assurance that either path will lead to victory conflicts with the uncertainties he has just explained. With four days left, her double still falls, although she now resumes the program. Hitomi challenges his estimate and recommends reliable elements. (`0025`–`0026`, `0029`.)

A twisting dog gives Tsukasa a new movement image. He changes the instruction from upward jumping to folding the axis leg, and Inori executes a flying sit spin. The final plan uses a strength produced by the foundation work rather than simply catching up to Ryoka's double. Tsukasa's board makes the plan intelligible through relative base values; Hitomi predicts consistency and possible extra credit. The last mainline page renews the promise to win. It supplies neither a judged program nor a competition outcome. (`0030`–`0032`.)

The rear stories then revise access and support. At seven, Inori repeatedly visits the rink alone, unable to obtain parental permission; Sekoma frightens her away before offering a worm arrangement and paying her entrance himself. The first encounter's apparent barter now becomes disguised adult subsidy. Tsukasa's sushi story reveals the low pay, side work and equipment cost behind his coaching, the Kago household's earlier extensive support, and his return there. He explicitly denies choosing coaching merely from lack of talent or to soothe regret. Kago explains support through effort and affection rather than skating success alone. These are consequential additional contexts, with self-report distinguished from demonstrated motive. (`0035`–`0037`.)

## Recognition, teaching and the ethical limits of rescue

The volume repeatedly asks who gets to decide that a beginning is still possible. Inori's mother and Tsukasa initially mistake imperfect credentials or present difficulty for an adequate forecast of the future. Their reasons differ: her remembered responsibility for a hurt child and his fear of misleading a pupil deserve attention. Yet both can close an opportunity before its subject has spoken. The trial matters because it interrupts that substitution of prediction for participation. It produces an observable response to instruction, then makes Inori's spoken wish harder to dismiss. `MD-CL-0001@1` and `MD-CL-0002@1` retain that distinction between recognition, capability and access.

“君は十分偉いよ” at image 0044 recognizes effort and worth before the skill demonstration. This placement matters: dignity is not presented solely as the reward for being useful to a coach. The subsequent weight/posture instruction is equally important because affirmation alone would leave the child with the same practical limit. The early intervention combines listening with a usable change, while admitting Tsukasa's inexperienced mistakes, including the helmet reminder and his initially poor grasp of why disclosure to a parent is frightening. His own history supports empathy but does not guarantee accurate judgment of this child. `MD-CL-0004@1` treats that as situated pedagogy, not a universal coaching method.

Written address helps differentiate the relations. Inori's early “先生” assumes a role before Tsukasa has accepted it; later “司先生” accompanies an actual partnership. His “いのりさん” during urgent instruction maintains courteous address even under pressure. With Kago, his more confrontational self-reference and refusal contrast with his accommodation of a pupil. Nachi's rough exchanges with Ryoka contrast with his formal apology to other adults. These differences support interlocutor-conditioned conduct; typography and punctuation do not disclose acoustic pitch, timbre or actor delivery. The dialect aside specifically labels Miku's speech **Mikawa**, so geographic setting alone would be an unreliable basis for calling all speech Nagoya dialect.

The ethical standard here is whether care expands a child's practicable choices while listening to her reasons, disclosing material uncertainty and keeping adult stakes distinguishable. Tsukasa's invitation to choose and instruction against guessing his preference meet part of that standard. His guaranteed-victory language does not meet the uncertainty part. Its warmth can reduce paralysis, and the flying sit solution shows real work behind the reassurance; neither makes the promise truthful as a prediction of a contest. Nachi's withholding of the next badge similarly constrains an expressed choice for his competitive aim. No result is available that could excuse or condemn either method through success alone. `MD-CL-0007@1` and `MD-CL-0009@1` keep those evaluations bounded.

Rescue is also distributed and intrusive. Sekoma's subsidy enables learning but conceals a workaround and begins with a frightening threat. Kago's support relieves scarcity but includes unasked-for payments, disclosure of private poverty and pressure to accept help. The comedy permits gratitude and boundary conflict to coexist. Tsukasa's return to the household is a change in his resources, not proof that his anxiety about dependence was baseless. His denial of projection is important counterevidence, especially his statement that even a successful audition would have led him to coaching. It remains an account of himself, not independent certification that every promise to Inori is free of his own history. `MD-CL-0010@1`–`0011@1` preserve both sides.

## Rivalry, confidence and artistic learning

Hikaru and Ryoka offer sharply different terms of peer recognition. Hikaru's initial invitation comes before reputation; she later protects Inori from disparagement, welcomes an equal-age rival and admits the help behind her ability. She also draws a limit around what she can teach. Inori's admiration therefore has a cooperative object even when it becomes a desire to win. By contrast, Ryoka converts attention into a threat of abandonment if Inori remains adult-dependent. That pressure is directed friendship behavior, not a diagnosis or a verdict on all of Ryoka's relationships. Her wish for a first friend, eagerness to improve and pleasure in challenging Inori complicate the episode. `MD-CL-0003@1` and `MD-CL-0006@1` require both directions of these relationships.

Inori's changes are context-specific. She can move fast during escape and skating while describing herself as poor at ordinary school work; she can pass the beginner patterns while freezing in her first complete program. A landed jump and the capacity to continue choreography are separate achievements. Likewise, returning to choreography after the fallen double is a meaningful change from the initial rehearsal, but it is not mastery of the double. A global transformation from “incapable” to “confident” would erase the situations that make development legible. `MD-CL-0002@1` and `MD-CL-0005@1` support a narrower progression in asking for help, choosing and recovering within practiced tasks.

Tsukasa's dance demonstration does more than reassure. Sequential figures, broad arm/torso gestures, skate details and the child's changing attention allow the reader to share a discovery: the conspicuous adult can skate with an expressive authority his own account of failure has obscured. Inori wants to become admirable in this way. Her artistic aim is therefore evidenced by what she says and subsequently practices, not merely inferred from a beautiful drawing. The mentor supplies an image to work toward; the relationship shapes her preference without making it empty compliance.

The cake/taiyaki comparison first makes training time a genuine allocation problem. Inori chooses to polish movement, and the deadline exposes its cost: the double is still unreliable. The final spin offers a third construction assembled from developed strengths. This resolves the local apparent opposition between technique and dancing more convincingly than an assertion that both are always compatible: program polish makes a difficult spin strategy possible in the coach's account. Its future competitive adequacy remains open. `MD-CL-0008@1` distinguishes that explanatory inference from the board's scoring statements.

## Manga form, memory and attributed expertise

The volume's changes in panel density and scale distinguish explaining, doing and appraising. Technical diagrams isolate edges, weight distribution, test patterns and program slots; expanded skating figures then give movement an expressive and spatial presence that the diagrams do not exhaust. Close-ups of eyes, hands and blades bind attention to a consequential action. The initial freeze is compressed into a comic loading image, while the final flying spin stretches anticipation over approach, airborne folding, landing, turning and exit. The depicted sequence supports a training outcome, but its stylized phases cannot be treated as calibrated frames for a rotation or speed measurement. `MD-CL-0012@1` connects the formal and technical homes without collapsing them.

The parent-gossip montage is a particularly important control case. Remembered or imagined judgments appear alongside smiling faces of the coaches, so reading the words as those coaches' present speech would invert the relationship. Later, wordless paired skating and mirrored reaching gestures let aspiration and trust appear bodily. This contrast shows why silent passages and composition must be inspected alongside language. Claims about original spread geometry remain withheld because this package does not verify it.

The opening and final promise echo one another: an adult and child reach toward a shared future, while the title “genius” becomes a recognition they hope training can elicit. The color cover's posed hands belong to this paratextual arrangement, not to an extra event in story chronology. Short Program 1 and Short Program 2 additionally echo responsibility after helping a dependent person: both adults invoke an obligation extending beyond one gift. The analogy is useful, but the child at a rink and an adult deciding where to live do not have equivalent options. That difference limits an otherwise tempting unified rescue thesis.

Skating expertise is carefully attributed. The badge pass is an actual represented assessment outcome. The mother's endpoint, parents' age assumptions, Nachi's credential strategy and coaches' scoring explanations are other kinds of institutional speech. At image 0208, the printed note identifies **2019–2020 rules**; the board contrasts hypothetical base totals **4.78** and **5.18**, with spin values **1.1** and **1.7**. They are not official awarded scores. The claims of more than 90 percent success for Ryoka's combination and Inori's spin are not an inspected trial dataset. No external JSF/ISU or medical document was admitted, so no assertion of current real-world correctness, safety or rule compliance is made. `MD-CL-0013@1` preserves that boundary.

The creator's jacket note expresses a wish to portray competitors' effort and the appeal of those supporting them; the credits name supervision and reporting assistance. Those production statements are compatible with this reading, but do not authenticate every technical explanation or settle a character's motive. Profiles establish attributed fields, including Tsukasa's full name, without making blood type, birthday or height a personality model. Gags remain comedy; Inori's fried-shrimp dream is a fear representation, not actual abandonment. These limits are necessary uses of the supplemental architecture inside an ordinary manga transaction.
