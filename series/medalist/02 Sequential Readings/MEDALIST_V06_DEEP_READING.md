# Volume 6 — proof

Volume 5 stopped on a sentence: today I will make you a gold medalist. Volume 6 opens by showing what the sentence was made of, lets it come true by 1.17 points, and then spends three chapters asking whether it should have been said.

The answer to the first question is unusually complete. The words were chosen. The coach prayed that their force would only push her forward. The layout behind them was the product of four volumes of unglamorous skating practice, converted into marks by a rule the volume stops to explain, and it closed on a jump that his own records showed she had never missed. The win is the first clean skate of the competition.

Then the volume turns on its own result. A rival coach who has just lost to it calls the strategy a long-term mistake and the medal a noose. The girls it beat can all do a jump the winner cannot. And the champion whose verdict has hung over the story since Volume 2 arrives in person, skates better than he did when he was competing, says in so many words what he is doing with Hikaru and why, and tells Tsukasa that an ability which is not proven in the time allowed is the same as no ability at all.

The reading's argument is that Volume 6 is where the work's two accounts of talent meet and are found to share a premise. Yodaka holds that worth is what is proven within the limit. Tsukasa denies the verdict and has just built a season on the same logic: to become strong, he has taught his pupil, one must prove strength. What separates them is narrower and more interesting than belief against cynicism. It is whether a result can be made certain, and who is entitled to say in advance that it cannot be reached. The volume sets a date on which that will be tested and ends two weeks short of it.

## What the sentence rested on

`MD-CL-0007@5` held the evaluation of "today I will make you a gold medalist" until the page showed what it was attached to. `MD-OQ-V05-02` asked whether that was a plan he could state or a promise. It was both, and the volume documents each part.

It was deliberate. He is choosing the words as she skates back to him, and what he prays is the most self-aware thing he has thought about his own speech: that their force be only the force that moves her forward. He knows that the same words can crush. In Volume 1 he promised a result to a mother without visible calculation. Here he uses a strong sentence as an instrument and fears it.

It was conditional in a way the Volume 5 page hid. Before the declaration he says that he alone is not enough and that her resolve is what matters; after it she asks whether he believes it and he says he does; she answers "leave it to me" and he answers "I leave it to you". The grammar of the last page of Volume 5 made him the agent. The exchange that follows hands the agency back.

It rested on evidence. The layout turned her one distinctive asset into marks through a rule, and its riskiest element closed on a jump with a perfect record across every condition he could isolate. Gori, who does not know this, names what such a layout needs, a conviction of complete success, and the flashback supplies it as a spreadsheet. The certainty is an induction from practice. It is the best kind available and it is still a wager; the volume marks the moment with his own inner shout of "land it".

And it had a purpose beyond itself, which he gives to Sonidori and then to her. Qualifying would not do. First place buys what fifth does not: designated practice, a specialist's time, the expectations of people who can help. He judged that without them the month before the All-Japan could not be used.

This changes the claim. The Volume 1 promise and this declaration share a surface and little else. What survives of the original concern is narrower and should be kept. The criterion since version 1 has been that a win cannot validate a promise. The win does not validate this one. What the page shows is that the promise was reasoned, tested, feared and meant as a means. It also shows what it cost and risked. He built a layout that left no margin when a safer one would have qualified her, and the professionals watching say so before they know it worked. He let a twelve-year-old carry "gold or nothing" onto the ice. And the reward he bought with it is a month in which she must learn two triples under the eyes of everyone who now expects them. Gori's image for that is a noose. Tsukasa's is a passport. Both are on the page and the All-Japan will not settle which was right, since a good result there would be the same kind of evidence the criterion excludes.

Volume 6 also sorts the word that has followed him. He says "absolutely" twice in this volume: the promise he absolutely cannot break, and the medal that was absolutely necessary now. Yodaka says there is an absolute, meaning a result. Tsukasa, to his face, says there is none on the ice. The two uses are consistent. His absolutes attach to what he commits and what he judges necessary. He denies them of outcomes. `MD-CL-0007@6` is reformulated on that distinction, with the evaluation no longer held and no longer simple.

## The low cards

`MD-CL-0008` has followed the foundation-first strategy from a food metaphor in Volume 1 through a second place and its stated ceiling in Volume 3 to a caution in Volume 5, when the three highest scores printed all carried errors and harder content. Volume 6 gives the strategy its mechanism, its result and its bill.

The mechanism is stated three times from three positions. Sekoma gives the rule: execution marks scale the value of what one already has. Rio gives the cause: an ice dancer's standard for skating, and a practice allocation that is mostly skating. A rival assistant coach gives the appraisal: the best skating in the event. The first two are testimony from people on her side. The third is not. Sonidori, who predicted it, concedes more: her program is what his own pupil's plan had aimed at.

The result is the narrowest possible confirmation. A clean program with a triple loop as its hardest jump beat a program with a triple Lutz and triple loop and two errors by 1.17. On the stated values that is what one would expect to be close. It does not show that completeness beats difficulty. It shows that at this event, on this day, a perfect execution of lower content edged an imperfect execution of higher. Had Yūna kept her feet once more the order would have reversed, and the volume lets her coach think so.

The bill is presented by Gori and is not disputed by anyone. The qualifiers who lost to her can land the jump that is called the absolute condition for a national podium. She has neither it nor the flip. The time that made her clean was time not spent on them. Rio adds a limit of a different kind: the method works on a child who can bear it, and he, who has every other advantage, could not.

What is disputed is foresight. Gori thinks the coach overlooked the cost. The coach says he saw at the camp that those two jumps could not be ready by the block whatever he did, and chose to win with what she had so as to buy help for the month after. On the page his account is the better supported: the plan is consistent with his refusal in Volume 4 to add training volume, and the help arrives on the last page. But "according to plan" is his description of a plan whose second half has not happened. `MD-CL-0008@5` records the strategy as vindicated at the regional level by a hair, explained, and placed by the work itself under a stated national ceiling.

`MD-CL-0004@5` gathers what the volume adds about how he coaches: the allocation of practice, the testing of instruction on his own body, the logs and their analysis, strategy as part of the work, and the reconstructive eye now shown learning a singles take-off from Yodaka in one session while two Olympic medalists watch. Peers want his method on the night he wins. The same volume supplies the two strongest objections it has yet faced.

## Passport and noose

The designated practice is the first time the work has shown, from inside, what selection gives. A rink obtained by lottery, the block's judges offering comments, a handful of skaters and the whole ice. Inori is there because she proved something, and she has been taught to understand it that way.

`MD-CL-0001` has described recognition in this work as enabling, selecting, burdening, and at Volume 5 as comparative. `MD-CL-0011` has tracked enabling care, and at Volume 5 found its mark in adults who decline repayment. Volume 6 sets a second economy beside that one. Support at this level is earned. It follows results, and the coach who has embodied the enabling kind since the first chapter now works the selecting system on purpose, for a child it would never have selected. He calls the specialist's visit a reward.

The volume does not present this as a fall. It is how the institution runs, and his use of it is skilful. But three things on its pages keep the earlier account from being simply superseded. The assistant coach who refused the small Inori did so because a parent wanted a professional to say "impossible", and he obliged: that was the selecting system too, at its entrance. The adolescent Tsukasa asked for exactly one thing, a verdict on his talent, as permission to go on, and nobody gave it; what he later gave Inori unasked was that verdict. And the children who leave the block uncalled-for share a wish that someone would know how hard they work. Proof opens doors. Most of the people in the volume are on the other side of them.

Gori's noose belongs here. Expectation is what Tsukasa set out to obtain, in his own words to Sonidori, and expectation is what Gori thinks will choke her. They are describing one thing. The volume gives early evidence for each: a specialist on his way, and a child skating a whole session without a pause because she has seen the gap.

## Two coaches at night

Score 22 resolves more of the work's open matter than any chapter so far, and it does so through people saying things. That calls for some care about who says what.

**Yodaka, in his own words.** He contracted to coach Hikaru because she had the aptitude for his road. The road is his own record repeated: gold at every competition entered, for life. It is possible because he did it, by ability and by correct choices. He knows what the top requires and they do not. There is an absolute. Ability not proven within the limited time is equivalent to none. A capable man who chose backstage work has thrown his possibility away. `MD-CL-0016` has carried "the same road", his purpose and his secrecy as unresolved since Volume 2. The first two are now stated by him. His verdict on Inori in Volume 2 follows from them as a corollary. It was never an estimate of her. No one is to beat Hikaru, so no one can.

**Yodaka, by Sonidori's report.** He could not live off the ice after retiring at twenty; the offer to coach came with private ice for himself; the arrangement began for Sonidori with a courteous letter from Yodaka asking for secrecy and for the child's daily care. Sonidori keeps the secret by choice, to protect Hikaru and to protect the skating. This is hearsay from a friend and is marked on the page as what Sonidori was told. Who made the offer to Yodaka is not said. The main reason for secrecy is the sentence Yodaka interrupts.

**Yodaka, as shown.** He skates better than he did as a champion. He teaches Tsukasa a backflip the way he is said to teach Hikaru, by doing it once and pointing. He smokes. He asks about Tsukasa's career, which is the first interest he has shown in another adult. He produces, unasked, the means for Tsukasa to return to performing. He laughs once.

What kind of figure this adds up to should not be forced. A reading in which he is the story's cold antagonist has his contempt for a child's dream and his programme for another child's whole life. A reading in which he is a damaged man who can only exist on ice, and who offers the one kind of respect he understands, has Sonidori's testimony, the video, and the concession about good coaches. The page supports both and has him say nothing about himself beyond his record. Rio's taunt in Volume 3, that he ran away from skating, is answered by a friend's account that he could not leave it. Why he retired is asked at a press conference and the panel cuts away.

**Tsukasa.** `MD-CL-0010` has asked since Volume 1 what his failed aspiration does in his coaching and has stayed open through five versions. Volume 6 gives its history without remainder. He taught himself by imitating one man from a screen, on rental skates. What he lacked and wanted was someone to confirm what he had. He wanted a judgment of his talent as leave to continue, and believed nobody wished him on the ice. He began in earnest at twenty and competed for two seasons. The Volume 4 inference that he built his skating by eye from the skater he admired is now narrated. The Volume 1 scene in which he told a crying child that she was already remarkable is what he had needed said to him.

It also gives, for the first time, his own claim on the work. Hitomi said in Volume 4 that he moves for others and cannot move for himself. In Volume 5 he called his work invisible and wholly contained in his pupil's performance. On the night ice everything he does is still for Inori, by his own account: he falls on borrowed blades in order to learn a take-off she needs. But offered a way back to performing by the man he copied, he calls coaching his dream and his way of using his possibility. That is a first-person sentence. It is conditioned on her ("as long as I am entrusted"), and the same evening he had to be pushed into the coach's seat and sat appalled at the head of a table. `MD-CL-0010@6` records the mechanism as disclosed and the question changed. It is no longer what his regret does. It is whether a purpose he calls his own, and makes conditional on a child's trust, is one he can hold when she no longer needs him.

Yodaka's remark that Tsukasa knows his ability is rare cuts against the picture this ledger has carried since Volume 2, in which the gift had to be pointed out to him. Either he has learned it since, as the "ally" speech of Volume 4 suggests, or Yodaka infers too much from a calm face. It is one observer's inference and is held at that.

**The pair.** He began at twenty; the other retired at twenty. One tried to leave the ice and was shut back in; the other tried to get onto it and was not wanted. Tsukasa calls them exact opposites, and the page sets the ages side by side. `MD-CL-0025@1` registers what the confrontation makes of this. The work now has two stated creeds about certainty and proof, held by two coaches, with a date for their test. They do not differ on whether proof matters. Tsukasa teaches that it does and has just used it. They differ on whether an outcome can be guaranteed and on whether anyone may rule a child out beforehand. Yodaka's "you may retract it" returns, across four volumes, the word Tsukasa demanded of him in a stairwell.

## Inori after the medal

`MD-OQ-V05-01` asked what she would skate and how it would be scored. Ten elements, ten credited, 81.20, first. `MD-CL-0005@5` notes what kind of fact that is: the winner of the event is, by the field's own measure of level, its least equipped qualifier, and her rivals know it by the following week.

Three things about her are new.

The first is a memory. As a small child she dreamed repeatedly of the kiss-and-cry and prayed for the dream to stop, because waking was grief. This is the longing before the story, in her words, and it dates the deficit that `MD-CL-0015` has tracked to before she could have called it one. Her definition is exact: a skater was someone allowed to sit there.

The second is the family history behind the prohibition. Her sister's skating ended with a fracture in jump practice. Her mother once took her to a club hoping to hear that it could not be done, and was told so. `MD-CL-0014@3` revises the mother accordingly. The Volume 1 obstruction had a specific injury behind it and took the form of seeking a professional's refusal. Her Volume 3 reflex at a sprain, "I knew this happens", was memory. None of it is excused by the page or blamed; she is shown now with one friend among the parents and a celebration dinner.

The third is what winning did. `MD-CL-0015@4` said that whether her driver was appetite or the old shame re-measured could not be told before she had skated and been scored. She has now been scored first. Her response within a week is to conclude that the gap to Hikaru is real, to skate a session without stopping, and to say that even if it is impossible she wants two more jumps in order to fight her. Satisfaction is brief and is mostly her coach's. This is the pattern's fifth consecutive volume, under defeat, steady progress, pressure and victory alike. That is enough to call it a standing way of conducting herself: she measures against a gap and moves toward it. What it rests on is still not shown to be one thing. The object has narrowed from "being behind" to a person. The tone at the table is want, stated beside a clear admission of the odds. And the nonstop session is the first conduct since the train in Volume 3 that looks like the haste she renounced there.

`MD-CL-0019@4` takes that up. Her coach's answer to it is the same in kind as in Volume 4. He does not add volume. He changes the inputs, with a specialist, and tells her the arithmetic. Whether the vow holds is now a question about two weeks.

Her habit of committing herself aloud before people above her recurs for the fourth time, to Iruka, and for the second time in defence of someone she loves. It was her coach in Volume 3; it is her sister here. `MD-CL-0002@6` records that, and two smaller acts that belong with it: at the boards she turned his declaration into a question about his belief before she accepted it, and she counted her own revolutions through the final spin.

What she kept from him in Volume 4 she still keeps.

## The field afterwards

**Yūna.** `MD-CL-0022@2` registered her speech against the late starter as the missing fifth answer to unrecoverable time and noted that the volume ended before answering it. The answer comes from her, and it is a retreat from the speech. Beaten by the late starter, she says that chasing is what she is good at. One line after a defeat is not a disposition. As a state event it reverses the grievance: the position she called helpless she now calls her skill.

Two further facts qualify what Volume 5 inferred about her. A clubmate tells her she was the reason that clubmate did not quit, so the child who felt unlooked-at has been watched closely by someone all along. And her profile states that she is secretly in love with her coach. `MD-CL-0001@5` read her wish for "the strength to make him turn round" as evidence that recognition has a comparative side. That reading stands. The wish also has a colouring the story pages did not show and the profile supplies. The profile is an editorial layer. It is recorded as such and the claim's second addition is narrowed.

**Sonidori.** `MD-CL-0024@1` asked what an adult does when a child's gamble fails. Yūna's did, by 1.17 points, on a plan she broke. His response has no reproach in it. She is not alone; he will build the layout the jump needs; they will go to win. Privately he had felt a little regret, and he does not pass it on. The same volume shows him as the keeper of someone else's secret, uneasy that he will be thought to take another man's credit, and as the friend who wanted a stranger to see what he was protecting. `MD-CL-0024@2` adds his case and Tsukasa's, which is the range's new extreme: a maximal gamble originated by the coach.

**The prodigy's condition.** `MD-CL-0023@1` held that Volume 5 treated a prodigy as a condition other children live under, and named her presence at a later event as its test. She is present at the practice. One triple Axel, and the qualifiers respond by jumping their hardest jump in turn, and the champion by skating until she steams. The escalation follows her into the room. The volume also discloses that the condition is designed. An adult intends that no one in her generation shall win while she competes. Whether she knows this is not shown.

**Hikaru.** `MD-CL-0003@4` had her warmth and one statement of ambition. Volume 6 gives her no words but "are you all right?". She waves across the ice. Later she stops over Inori after a fall, looks, and skates away while Inori is saying her name; she leaves the session early. The gag pages show her gracious and easily pleased among clubmates who are afraid to approach her. The on-ice moment is the first thing she has done toward Inori that is not plainly warm, and it has no caption. Disappointment, concern, discomfort at being seen to hover, and an ordinary wish to get on with her session all fit. It is recorded and not read.

## Written Japanese

The declaration's companion sentences are the ones Volume 5 withheld. 「俺はあなたを勝たせる自信がある でも俺だけじゃだめだ」 keeps the causative, to make her win, and at once limits it. His prayer uses a wishing form, 〜になってくれ, addressed to his own words. Her question is 「先生も信じてる?」, with も: you too. The exchange that follows is two forms of one verb, 任せて and 任せる.

## Manga form

**The skate as an argument.** Her program runs for forty pages and is seldom shown alone. It is interrupted by a lay viewer, an explainer, a boy in the stands, three sets of rival coaches, a flashback and her own coach's interior, each adding one step of an explanation. The performance and the case for it arrive together.

**Game images.** Volume 5's cards become collectible cards with rarity and levels, a draw machine, and at the end a rhythm game's "full combo". The judges' criteria appear as a ticking checklist beside her face. The device translates a scoring system for a reader who does not know it and marks the strategy as a way of playing.

**A result as a document.** Her score is a number over two stunned faces. Her rank is a printed sheet on the next page, and the same page turns to the skater who came fourteenth. The win has no celebration panel of its own.

**Final-rank captions.** Through Score 21 each child is labelled with her final place and score as she appears, so the standings are assembled from departures.

**The boy and the man.** Tsukasa's adolescence is drawn in black gutters and opens from an inverted title image. When Yodaka skates, one panel splits the boy at the television and the adult on the ice in the same posture.

**The interruption.** Sonidori's explanation of the secrecy is cut off in mid-sentence by a full page of the man it concerns, standing behind the listener.

**Paired captions.** Score 22 ends on two result boxes side by side, a first place and a withdrawal, over two faces.

**Echoes.** The fist bump. The drawstrings in her hands. "Retract". A television and a child watching it, now for the third time. The jacket, which for the first time shows the two coaches and not the pupil.

A creator note on the jacket flap says that the last chapter of each volume is cut to fit a fixed page count and the first chapter of the next is expansive by reaction. That fits Score 20's long skate and Score 23's brisk close, and is a caution against reading pace as emphasis at volume ends.
