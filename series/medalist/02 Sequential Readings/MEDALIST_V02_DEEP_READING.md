# Volume 2 — permission to fall, and who gets to forecast

Volume 1 ended on a promise of victory that nothing had tested. Volume 2 tests it, and the test turns on a change in what the coach promises. Minutes before Inori's first competition, after a warm-up in which everything she could normally do went wrong, Tsukasa stops promising a win and promises something he can actually deliver: that he will teach her again from the start however many times it takes. She falls on her first jump, gets up, finishes the program, adds a difficulty nobody planned, and wins the beginner section with 12.64. The volume then spends its second half measuring how small that win is. Hikaru scores 97.23 two categories up. An Olympic champion tells Inori to her face that she will never beat Hikaru. Tsukasa answers him, and in answering binds his own lifetime to her goal.

The argument of this reading is that Volume 2 is about forecasting: who is entitled to say what a child will become, on what evidence, and what the forecast does to her. Inori's mother, a judge, a rival's coach, the rival herself and a gold medalist all issue forecasts. The volume keeps showing that the ones made before watching are the ones that go wrong. It does not show that Tsukasa's own forecast, the largest of all, is right.

## Developments in disclosure order

**Score 4 opens in memory.** A very small Inori is held by her mother while her elder sister competes, and asks whether her sister will fall. The mother answers that she is strong when it counts and "絶対大丈夫". The sister lands, hiding with beauty all the times she had fallen before, and Inori's narration states the question the volume then works on: how does one become a person on whose uncertain future others will stake their expectation. This is a recollection, in colour, with ornate caption frames. It establishes that the mother who refused skating in V01 once watched a daughter compete with open confidence. (`MD-EV-V02-0001`.)

The competition morning names the sister, 結束実叶 (Mika), home from study in Canada to watch. The mother has stayed up adjusting the costume, has a headache, admits she "got carried away because it was fun", and sends the others ahead. Inori thinks her mother is cheerful because Mika is back. Mika corrects her: it is because Inori is skating. A large panel holds Inori's surprised face. The misattribution matters because it shows what Inori still expects of her mother's attention at the start of the volume. (`0002`.)

At the venue she is number 12, last in the second practice group; start order was drawn by lot at the previous day's opening ceremony, and her mother reports that she drew it rigid with fear of going first. Ryoka passes, clicks her tongue and says nothing. A caption explains that judges begin assessing skating skill during the practice period, and a judge thinks that number 12 is the best skater in the group. Then the warm-up collapses. She falls on an Axel, falls again, and fails the flying sit spin that her coach notes she has never failed before. (`0003`–`0004`.)

Tsukasa's response is practical and ordered: check for pain, name a plausible cause (a toe pick catching; unfamiliar ice), move to an off-ice timing check, rehearse the image of success. The mother then asks him to change every jump to something easy. Her reasons are specific. The event is too early. A first competition is remembered for life. Inori has finally found something to pour herself into, and "スケートまで失敗の思い出を作らせないでください". He answers with facts about what she reliably lands and says the planned content is not a gamble. Inori overhears and blames herself: because she cannot do things, adults are troubled, and starting skating changed nothing. (`0005`.)

What follows is the volume's first hinge. She sees herself reflected, in costume and skates, and reverses the sentence: "変われないんじゃない 変えてないだけだ". From outside she already is a skater. She stops her mother, says she wants to do winning and losing in skating because she wants to be a medalist as an athlete, says she knows hard things fail and that winning is not guaranteed, and asks to be watched. Her interior line is the sharpest in the volume: it was not skating that made her sister admirable, it was her sister; so "私がスケートを特別にするんだ". She then apologizes to her coach for telling her mother about the athlete aim before they had planned to. (`0006`.)

Tsukasa's reply replaces the V01 promise. She need not fear falling. If everything fails he will teach it again from the beginning, hundreds of times. If her mother is not persuaded today he will keep teaching until she is recognized. She may be weak under pressure and easily nervous: "どんな貴方でも目標まで導く為に俺がいる". The last thing he says is to go all out. (`0007`.)

The program, to Holst's "Jupiter", lasts about a minute by the scoreboard clock. The single Axel falls. She rises smiling and resumes. A panel device now appears that the series will reuse: a row of squares, one per element, marked for added credit, reduced credit, under review or error. Her row reads reduced, under review, added, added, added: fall on the Axel; a single loop held with a deliberate landing check; a single Lutz and single Axel sequence; a single flip; the flying sit spin. A judge registers speed and stroke length unusual for the grade and credits the ice-dance background of the club's head coach. In the spin she changes position into a broken-leg variation. Tsukasa had mentioned it once and deferred it until after the competition; she inserts it herself. His narration is that after shaking and falling she was thinking about winning to the very end. (`0008`–`0009`, `0011`–`0012`.)

In the stands the mother gives her own account of the last several years. Other adults had praised Mika and pitied Inori, and blamed the mother's return to work. "出来ないって言い続けてきたの いのりの為なんかじゃない… 自分が傷つくのが怖かったんだ". She had come to believe the outside evaluations she hated. This is the mother's self-report in one scene, corroborated by a short flashback of the remarks. (`0010`.)

Afterwards Inori tells her coach she attempted the variation on her own because he had said falling was allowed, and apologizes. They bump fists. Her mother asks what kind of medalist she means to be. "オリンピック". "わかった." She holds her, says she watched, apologizes for leaving her to work so hard alone and unpraised, and says she will become a mother who can support the dream. The announcer reads 12.64, first place for now. (`0013`–`0014`.)

**Score 5 belongs to Ryoka.** Nachi's send-off discloses that Ryoka is the only pupil in her club and that private ice has rarely been possible. Ryoka skates eighteenth and last. Her Axel and Lutz draw added credit, and Nachi's thought supplies history: at her previous club Ryoka was treated as a problem child for her temper, and her feel for rhythm stood out. Then the planned double Salchow and single Axel combination comes apart. The double lands; the Axel never follows. Nachi's plan is immediate and sound: add the Axel to the final flip. Ryoka cannot retrieve it. Her interior fills with "やり直したい", over fragments of earlier scoldings and a child offering a hand, and she simply does the combination again, cleanly. A judge notes it is the cleanest of the day as he marks it invalid. The caption states the rule: a beginner program's first jumps must be four different kinds. She scores 11.17 and finishes third. (`0016`–`0018`.)

The corridor scene changes how V01's Ryoka reads. Nachi does not punish; she asks, almost gently, whether the recovery came to mind. Ryoka answers in polite speech, which the page labels in large type as out of character, and then drops into a self-reference she does not normally use: "オレはみんなみたいに大人を味方にできんから…". She wanted to become someone who could manage alone because nobody was on her side. People dislike her, she no longer knows whom to dislike back, and if her character were different she would still be friends with Inori. Nachi's reply affirms the temperament as the source of the skating, and then gives her creed: losing makes you think you were wrong, but "強い方が正しいんだ", so next time they win and prove it. Ryoka says this is not how adults talk. (`0019`.)

Inori comes to find her. She does not ask for an apology or mention the condition Ryoka set. She says practising together was fun and asks whether Ryoka will skate with her again. Ryoka throws herself at her. On the podium Inori, first, holds out a hand to Ryoka, third; Sakurai Hime of Ryoka's former club is second. The profiles that follow give Ryoka's family history and Nachi's past as a nationally ranked junior, and repeat that the club has one pupil and marginal running costs. (`0020`–`0021`.)

**Score 6 turns to the adults.** A flashback conversation gives Tsukasa's origin. In middle school he chanced on a television replay of an Olympic gold medalist, knew none of the rules, could not forget it for days, and went to a rink. The medalist is 夜鷹純 (Yodaka Jun), who retired at twenty; the rival on that podium was 鴗鳥 (Sonidori). Tsukasa adds that he never reached the world that man was in. In the stands, television crews have appeared for the novice event because Hikaru is skating. Rio turns up, a novice-boys entrant who does not recognize Inori; a caption identifies his father, 鴗鳥慎一郎, as head coach of Hikaru's club and a former national-team skater. (`0022`–`0023`.)

Hikaru's free skate to "Danse macabre" is drawn in a different register from Inori's: black grounds, slit lighting, a face that changes across three narrowing strips. A triple Lutz and triple loop combination arrives with no visible preparation; spectators cannot say when she set up. Tsukasa reads the program sheet. It lists grade 6, and the same name, Sonidori, as coach and choreographer. He feels something is off and wonders whether the choreography is really Sonidori's. She scores 97.23 and wins; the first skater had 78.44. Inori's response is a definition: a person is called a genius when they make you want, strongly, to watch them try again. Then a doubt: what was Hikaru thinking when she said she hoped they would compete together soon. (`0024`–`0025`.)

She runs to find Hikaru and instead collides with a tall man at the top of a staircase, slips, and is caught by his fall. A run of gags follows (she pours barley tea on the unconscious man; her coach slips in it). When the man pushes his wet hair back, Inori says he looks like Yodaka Jun, and he says he would, being him. He remembers Tsukasa: he was at the All-Japan the day Tsukasa and his partner failed a lift spectacularly, and the performance stayed with him enough to recall the face. He has sent Hikaru home for a late-night lesson. He is her coach. Tsukasa concludes that the choreography was his. (`0026`–`0027`.)

Inori asks him to carry a message: today she understood again how far away Hikaru is; thank you for saying you wanted to compete with me; I will not stop working so that I can beat you, so wait. He asks her grade. Beginner. "一生かけようが君が光に勝てる事はないよ." She knows too little, about herself and about this world. A black panel carries one balloon: "撤回してください". Tsukasa, who a page earlier could barely believe this man remembered him, tells him that he is the one who knows too little, that a medalist's denial of a child's possibility is a curse, and to weigh his words. Yodaka's answer reframes everything: he is Hikaru's coach; who is Tsukasa; for this child to beat Hikaru means Tsukasa beating him. Three pages slow to heartbeats. Tsukasa's thought is "人生ふたつぶんの勇気の力か". He says they will win, that it does not matter who Yodaka is or who he himself is, and that for as long as Inori needs him he will spend his own lifetime taking her to victory. "貴方に未来は決められない." (`0028`–`0029`.)

**Short Program 3** is the same night, outside the venue. Inori asks to compete again before the summer ends, in the grade-1 slot, once she passes a badge test that has already been applied for. She explains without being asked. She was truly happy to win. But the cold feeling she carried before skating, "このままじゃ嫌だ", might melt away in that happiness, and that frightened her a little; she wants to keep the cold feeling as carefully as the glad one. Tsukasa finds a meet in Kyoto whose entry closes the day after the test. He tells her that whatever anyone says she is already a figure skater. Then he admits what he had wanted to say: they have not yet properly celebrated. They shout and he lifts her overhead until her family's car arrives. (`0030`.)

**Score 7** begins with Tsukasa dreaming Yodaka's question and waking to declare, to the laundry, that he will become the best coach in the world. The target is the West Japan elementary and middle school meet in Kyoto, a region he calls the strongest at present. Past grade-1 winners average in the eighteens; he sets a target in the twenties, eight above her 名港杯 score, reachable with two kinds of double jump. Inori reports that she landed a double by herself on Sunday. Shown, it falls. He brings a tablet borrowed from Kago and a three-segment model of the body in rotation; the video shows her legs arriving late. The attempts get worse. He suggests stopping; she refuses, because she wanted the double before the 名港杯 and girls her age can already do it as a matter of course. (`0032`–`0034`.)

His answer is that time spent unable to do something is not wasted, and his narration gives an assessment of her that the volume has by now earned: her drive is powerful, and she tends to blame herself with the same force. Then he notices that every word applies to him. He has been hurrying too, out of fear that he is not far enough ahead of her to take her to the world. "俺が先に変わらなければ." He decides that he is being raised by her as she is by him. (`0035`.)

On badge-test day she calms herself by finding earthworms, on her own initiative, and has learned not to bring them to her coach. Three people from Kyoto's 蓮華茶FSC arrive: 鹿本すず, last year's All-Japan Novice B silver medalist; 大和絵馬, a fourth-grader taking the same grade-1 test; and their assistant coach 蛇崩遊大, twenty-eight. Inori charges a flock of pigeons to rescue the silently crying Ema and is mobbed herself. Both girls pass. Ema's single Salchow looks to Inori unhurried, and oddly like Hikaru's jumping. (`0036`–`0038`.)

Tsukasa then asks Jakuzure to teach him how to coach jumps. Hitomi hauls him aside: strong clubs earn their methods, 蓮華茶 is the strongest club in the country, and one does not ask. He says that is exactly why he is asking, because an athlete's career happens once. Jakuzure agrees at once; he thinks knowledge should be shared, and he recognizes the feeling. Ema is among the first pupils he has coached from before beginner grade, he wants her talent acknowledged, and he does not want to watch her give up one step short. She will enter the same Kyoto meet in the same slot. (`0039`.)

The lesson does not go as either expects. Jakuzure watches Inori's program, learns she has skated for not quite four months, and finds little to correct. He has Ema jump a double Salchow, cleanly, and asks for a comment. A dark page runs one set of captions across both faces, about wanting to erase the self that cannot do it, and ends on "見るんだ". Inori lands a double loop. ## Permission to fall

`MD-CL-0007@1` held that Tsukasa supported Inori's choices while his absolute promises of victory hid the uncertainty he himself acknowledged, and `MD-OQ-V01-02` asked what that reassurance would do when a result went badly. Volume 2 does not supply a bad result. It supplies something more specific: at the moment of greatest risk, he withdraws the guarantee himself.

The sequence is tight (0019–0029). The warm-up removes the factual basis for confidence, since even the element that had never failed fails. The mother proposes the protective answer: lower the difficulty so nothing can go wrong. Tsukasa refuses on the evidence, but when Inori comes to him shaking and afraid of ruining everything, he does not repeat that she will win. He removes the cost of failure. What he commits to is his own conduct: re-teaching, continuing, staying. That is a promise whose truth depends on him, where "we will win" depends on judges and rivals.

Its effect is visible and she names it. The unplanned broken-leg variation is the riskiest thing in her program, added by a child who had just fallen. Afterwards she attributes it to his words and reports it at once. V01 worried that a guarantee might make setbacks harder to disclose. Here, a permission makes an unauthorized risk easy to disclose. This is one occasion, and she succeeded; a failed variation would have been the harder test.

The revision should not be overstated. Within the same volume he tells Yodaka "俺たちは勝ちます" and tells Inori she will "絶対" have the double by Kyoto. The absolute register has not gone; it has acquired a boundary. He speaks in certainties when defending her possibility against an adult who denies it, and when projecting over months. He speaks in permissions when she is about to step on the ice. Whether that boundary is a stable feature of his coaching or the product of one frightening warm-up cannot be decided from a single competition. `MD-CL-0007@2` records the revision and keeps the question open; the publisher's own Volume 3 slogan about there being no "absolute" on the ice is marketing and is not used as evidence.

## The mother's reversal, and what the judge could not see

In V01 the mother's refusal rested on a forecast drawn from the elder daughter's history and Inori's school difficulties, and `MD-CL-0001@1` treated the trial as the point where participation interrupted prediction. Volume 2 gives the forecast a source and an owner. By her own account it was built from other adults' comparisons, and its function was to protect her, not Inori. The prologue is the necessary counterweight: she once stood in an arena certain that a daughter would not fall. The certainty she extended to Mika and withheld from Inori was never a neutral reading of the two children.

Three things keep this from being a simple conversion. First, her request to simplify the jumps is not foolish. A first competition can mark a child, the warm-up had gone badly, and she says plainly that she does not see why the score needs to be high. She is arguing for a different good, enjoyment without contest, and Inori has to say out loud that contest is what she wants. Second, the reversal comes after she has watched her daughter fall and stand up; it is produced by evidence, like every durable change of judgment in this volume. Third, her commitment is spoken in the flush of a win. The middle-school endpoint she set in V01 is not mentioned. An Olympic aim cannot coexist with stopping at middle school, and she says "わかった" to the aim, so the endpoint has very probably lapsed; it has not been withdrawn in words, and the costs of the new commitment have not yet been presented to her. `MD-CL-0014@1` registers the mother's account at that strength.

The judge's appraisal is a small, exact irony. He sees speed and deep edges unusual for a beginner and explains them by the club's head coach having come from ice dance. He is not wrong about the lineage. But the coach who built those edges is standing beside her, unnamed on any sheet he has, with a history the reader knows. Recognition reaches Inori through her skating before it reaches the person who taught it. The same structure returns at larger scale with Hikaru, whose sheet names one coach while another did the work, there by concealment and here by obscurity.

## Losing, and two ways of being told you are right

Ryoka's defeat is the volume's clearest case against reading results as verdicts on ability. Her double Salchow lands twice. By the judge's remark the repeated combination is the cleanest of the day. She loses on a rule about repetition and on a recovery she had been taught and had not practised. `MD-CL-0005@1` distinguished technical execution, task continuity and felt confidence in Inori; Ryoka supplies the mirror case. The skater with the harder jump and the claimed nine-in-ten success rate fails her combination at the one attempt that counted, and the skater who failed everything in the warm-up delivers. Practice reliability, warm-up form and competition execution are three different quantities in this volume, for both girls.

Her collapse is not about the jump. What floods in is every earlier occasion of being the child who got it wrong, and the wish to redo all of it. The repeated combination is that wish enacted, at the cost of the result. Then she tells Nachi where the V01 demand came from. `MD-CL-0006@1` evaluated her condition on friendship, that Inori stop relying on adults, as narrowing Inori's options while her own skating depended on adults. That evaluation of the act stands. Its explanation changes: she is describing a strategy formed by a child who, in her own telling, could not get adults on her side, and who decided to need no one. She now calls it a mistake and connects it to losing Inori. The condition is not formally retracted; it is abandoned, by a self-account that makes it intelligible. Inori's repair asks for nothing and restores the friendship without either girl having to concede the argument about adults.

Nachi's conduct after the loss answers part of `MD-OQ-V01-03`. In V01 she kept Ryoka at beginner grade to win and feared that a defeat would cost her the pupil. The defeat came anyway, through no fault of the grade, and the pupil did not leave. The disclosed stake is larger than V01 showed: one pupil, costs barely covered. Against that pressure her first move is to refuse Ryoka's self-condemnation. That is care, and it is care for the trait other adults punished.

The words she uses deserve their own evaluation, which `MD-CL-0018@1` registers. "The strong are right; win and you become right" affirms a child against the people who called her a problem. It also makes her rightness depend on a scoreboard that has just gone against her. Set beside Tsukasa's "どんな貴方でも" from the previous chapter, the contrast is exact: one adult tells a child she is acceptable whatever happens on the ice, the other tells a child that what happens on the ice will prove she was acceptable all along. Both are said with love. The volume does not rank them, and Ryoka's own reply, that Nachi does not talk like an adult, is affectionate, not an objection. It is one scene. Whether the creed hardens into pressure or stays a rallying cry between two people who enjoy a fight is for later evidence.

## Hikaru's distance, Yodaka's verdict, Tsukasa's answer

Inori's reaction to Hikaru's score is worth separating from the reaction the scene could have had. She does not despair and she does not minimize. She revises her idea of genius toward the audience's side (wanting to see the attempt again), questions her friend's motive for the first time, and composes a message that contains thanks, an admission of distance and a refusal to stop. `MD-CL-0003@1` described a friendship that preceded status. In V02 only Inori's side of it is shown. Hikaru appears on the ice and waving with a trophy; what she thinks of Inori now is not on the page.

Yodaka's verdict is the antithesis of the mechanism V01 built. `MD-CL-0001@1` said that beginnings become possible when recognition is joined to instruction and access, and that credentials misdescribe capacity. Yodaka asks one question, her grade, and issues a lifetime forecast. Short Program 4 shows the same habit five years earlier and gives its premise: he judges a child's prospects before seeing her, on reported circumstances, and names luck as the fundamental talent. Then he sees her and reverses within a page. So even the volume's spokesman for pre-judgment is moved by watching. The difference is what he watches for. V01's recognition followed a child's effort and asked what teaching could add. Yodaka's recognition identifies someone the world will find "whatever I do". One enables; the other selects. `MD-CL-0001@2` now distinguishes the two, and it has to concede something to the second: the V01 ledger listed "talent recognition and patronage select exceptional individuals" as a live alternative, and Hikaru's path, an adopted child with a household's resources and a champion's private coaching, is that alternative shown working.

How much can be said about Yodaka himself is limited. He appears in one confrontation and one nine-page memory. In both he is curt, bored on his own account, and exact about status. He also catches a falling child with his body, remembers an obscure ice-dance couple's failed lift as memorable, and says, without explanation, that the girl will follow the same road he did. What that road is (early fame, a short career, isolation, something else) the text does not say. Reading him as contemptuous is supported by his words to Inori. Reading him as issuing a warning in a cruel form is not excluded by anything shown. `MD-CL-0016@1` registers the structural point and leaves his motive unresolved.

Tsukasa's answer is the largest development in his line. `MD-CL-0010@1` asked how far his own failed aspiration shapes his coaching and kept the question open against his sincere denial in V01's bonus story. Volume 2 adds three facts. The man who made him a skater is the man now telling his pupil she has no future. That man recasts her rivalry as a contest between the two coaches and asks who Tsukasa is, which is the question his whole history has failed to answer. And Tsukasa responds by pledging his lifetime and then, alone, by resolving to become the best coach in the world.

It would be easy to call this projection confirmed. The evidence is more discriminating than that. He demanded the retraction before Yodaka made it about him; the first defence was of her. His pledge is conditioned on her need ("いのりさんが必要とする限り"). His own gloss on his courage, two lives' worth, says that acting for her gave him what he lacked for himself, which is an account of borrowing strength from a responsibility, not of using a child to settle a score. On the other side, from this night his ambition and hers point at the same target, his idol's defeat, and "俺たちは勝ちます" is spoken in the first person plural. From here on the two interests are fused, and a fused interest looks identical to devotion until the day the two come apart. That day has not come. `MD-CL-0010@2` is reformulated accordingly and stays open: the question is no longer whether he coaches to soothe a regret, which he has denied and which nothing here contradicts, but what happens to his judgment if her good and his contest with Yodaka ever diverge.

## The coach who had to be told

Score 7 is built on a symmetry that the page at 0173 makes literal. Inori cannot land a double and hates the self that cannot. Tsukasa cannot believe in his coaching and hides that from her. He counsels patience and hears himself. The volume then gives him what he gave her in V01: someone who watches before judging.

Jakuzure's appraisal is the first independent, expert description of what Tsukasa is good at. `MD-CL-0004@1` inferred a responsive pedagogy from outcomes and noted that nobody had isolated its cause. Here a coach from the country's strongest club tests him on a pupil he has never seen and reports that he cannot match the resolution of the analysis. The mechanism is described by Tsukasa in his own words: prediction from posture, comparison, attention to the gap. Jakuzure's explanation of where it came from, the trajectory-reading that ice dance demands, is offered with repeated hedges and should be held at that strength. It does connect to something Yodaka said for a different purpose, that he remembered a failed lift, and to the former teacher's verdict that only time was missing. Three separate witnesses now attest that the late starter was good; none of them is Tsukasa.

That is the tension in his resolution at 0172. He concludes that confidence is not something others give, in the same breath as feeling ashamed that he could not see his own ability until another person named it. The resolution is sincere and it is also, on its face, not how the confidence arrived. A reader can take it as the right lesson drawn late, or as a man converting a gift into a duty because gifts are hard for him to hold; V01's bonus story showed exactly that difficulty with Kago's help, and a gag here shows him insisting on paying rent. `MD-CL-0017@1` keeps both readings and names the test: whether the self-trust holds when nobody is there to confirm it.

Hitomi's objection should not be lost in the comedy. She is right about the norm. Methods are a club's capital and one does not ask a rival for them. The scene does not prove the norm false; it shows one coach who chooses to share, for reasons he states, and who then announces he intends to beat them anyway. Cooperation and rivalry between the adults are presented as compatible in the same way V01 presented them between Inori and Hikaru.

## The cold feeling

Three times in two volumes Inori has described herself by a deficit and then acted from it. In V01 she hid her practice and planned to be good enough before asking. In the V02 corridor she concludes that her inability burdens adults. In Short Program 3 she names the thing directly: a cold feeling that she does not want to stay as she is, older than skating, which she is afraid joy will dissolve. In Score 7 it shows as haste, a refusal to stop falling, and the measuring of herself against "girls my age".

Her coach's assessment (strong drive, equal self-blame) is his inference, stated as narration. It fits these scenes, and it fits them from two sources, her own words and his observation. `MD-CL-0015@1` therefore registers a recurrent pattern across four situations, at moderate confidence, and stops short of calling it a trait. Three cautions apply. All four situations involve performance and evaluation; how she behaves where nothing is being assessed is barely shown. The pattern is reported mostly in her own or her coach's voice. And the volume itself shows the counter-movement: she reframes the corridor thought within two pages, accepts the idea that time spent failing has value, and by the badge test manages her nerves with a method she chose. What can be said is that she treats dissatisfaction as fuel and knows she does. What cannot yet be said is how she fares when the fuel has nothing to burn.

Her wish to keep the cold feeling is also the first time she theorizes her own motivation, and she does it to her coach unprompted. That belongs with the other evidence for `MD-CL-0002@2`: speaking against her mother's protection, altering her program without leave, proposing her own next competition, declining his suggestion to stop, reading the video for herself. V01's alternative, that her choices were compliance with an admired adult, has less room now. Several of these acts run against what an adult had planned. They remain acts inside a relationship of strong admiration, and she apologizes for two of them.

## Written Japanese

Address and register carry several of this volume's turns. Tsukasa's "貴方" appears twice with deliberate weight and opposite force: glossed あなた and offered to Inori in "どんな貴方でも", then turned on Yodaka with emphasis marks in "「知らなすぎる」のは貴方の方だ". The same formal pronoun dignifies a child and holds a superior to account. Yodaka shifts between 君 and キミ for both of them and uses 僕 of himself; the katakana form lands on the challenge "キミは何者?". When the scene replays in Tsukasa's dream the lettering reverts to 君, a reminder that the dream is his reconstruction.

Ryoka's speech is Mikawa dialect as V01's aside specified (とる, けん, おらん, だわ). Her profile says her habitual self-reference is ミケ. In the corridor she first speaks in polite forms, which the page flags as 敬語 in display type, and then says オレ, marked with emphasis dots. Both departures occur at the point of greatest shame. The profile's history, a child raised by her father alone until six and formerly under another surname, sits next to a drawing of a boyish small child; the page offers this without comment and the reading does not build on it beyond noting that the self-reference has a history.

Mika calls her sister のんちゃん. The household's attendants call the adopted child 光様 and the visitor 夜鷹様; Hikaru in a gag strip calls him コーチ. ## Manga form

**The element row.** The square device (first at 0035) lets a silent sequence carry a running official result without a commentator. It separates three things the reader would otherwise merge: what the body did, what the panel of judges recorded, and what the people at the boards hoped. It also makes Ryoka's disaster legible as a category event, a stamp across an element, where a fall would have read as a physical one.

**Three uses of the stroboscopic chain.** Multiple exposures of one skater along a path appear at 0039 for Inori, 0099 for Hikaru and 0165 for Ema, and mean different things each time. Inori's chain is tied to remembered instruction and shows a jump being assembled from its preparation. Hikaru's chain shows the opposite, a jump whose preparation the watchers cannot find. Ema's chain runs beneath a close-up of Tsukasa's eyes and shows a jump as he perceives it. The device is constant; whose knowledge it represents changes. This is the volume's best evidence for `MD-CL-0012`.

**Tonal assignment.** Inori's program is white ground, onomatopoeic melody written into the air, overlapping borderless figures. Hikaru's is black ground and slit light, with a face that hardens across three strips. Yodaka's pages in both his scenes are the darkest in the book. The contrast is the volume's shorthand for two regimes of skating; it should not be read as a moral coding of the children, since Hikaru's only speech in the volume is cheerful.

**Reflection and reaching.** At 0024–0027 a full-height reflected figure and the largest lettering in the chapter carry Inori's reversal, followed by a foreshortened hand toward her mother's silhouette. Hands recur: the fist bump, an adult hand reaching toward the final pose, Nachi's hands around Ryoka's wrist, the offered hand on the podium. `MD-MOT-0001` noted in V01 that touch is not inherently care; here every instance is offered and accepted, including the one that repairs the friendship V01's coercive grip had damaged.

**Seeing.** "私を見てて", a judge assessing from the practice period, a mother who "did not see" her daughter, a coach whose gift is an eye, a child with a beast's gaze, and "見るんだ" across two faces. The repetition is dense enough to register as a motif (`MD-MOT-0005`) and its point is consistent: in this volume, judgments made after looking are the ones that hold.

**The audience children.** At 0056, as Inori bows, two panels show a toddler clapping on a parent's lap and a small child holding a soft toy. The prologue showed a small Inori holding a soft toy in her mother's arms, watching her sister. The echo answers the prologue's question visually, without a caption: she is now the one being watched by a child. The reading holds this at moderate confidence because the link is compositional.

**Silence.** Page 0050 is wordless: a corridor, Hitomi stern, Tsukasa bowed, Inori watching from a distance, inserted into the middle of the spin. No caption fixes when it happened. It reads as what she had seen of the adults' trouble on her account and as the thing she is answering by adding the variation, and it is left as an inference.
