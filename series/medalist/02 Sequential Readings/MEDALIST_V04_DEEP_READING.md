# Volume 4 — goodbye and thank you

Volume 3 ended with a boy telling Inori that her coach had no record and that she should find another. Volume 4 puts the record on the table. Asked his singles grade in a bathhouse by a child who already suspects the answer, Tsukasa says it plainly: beginner. The volume then declines to argue the point. It answers in three other ways. He assembles the expertise he lacks, from a rival club and from a ballet studio. He skates, and the one child who has watched an Olympic champion train in private says the skating is that champion's. And both of the children in his charge do the thing the boy said could not be done.

Under that plot the volume is about what to make of years one would rather not have had. Inori says she will have to tell the self she hated "goodbye and thank you". Tsukasa, who that same week called his self-taught years wasted, says the effort of the self he hated turned out to be on his side. Rio, who has every advantage and so "nothing to blame", finds out whose life he has been measuring himself against. Ryoka meets a four-year gap she made herself and decides to win until the choice was right. The reading's argument is that the volume sets these four answers side by side and prefers none of them openly, though it gives the first two the last word.

It also settles, for now, the question Volume 3 left: whether Inori's vow to Rio would undo her vow on the train. It does not. The reason is partly hers and partly her coach's, and one part of it he does not know.

## Developments in disclosure order

**The prologue** is spoken by a male "we". Before saying a dream aloud, we check again and again that the road to it exists and that we have not strayed from it, before someone can deny it. If possibility means walking a road others have walked, what was in front of the person who made the road, and how much courage did his first step take? The images are a figure at a desk at night, hands tracing a circle on ice, and Tsukasa with a black-gloved hand entering the frame. The voice is most naturally Tsukasa's and the road-maker most naturally Yodaka; neither is named. (`MD-EV-V04-0001`.)

**Score 12** resumes in the minute after Inori's vow. She will not tell her coach what the quarrel was about, and returns only to make a request in form: she absolutely wants to pass grade 6 this summer, learn the triple and double, and enter this year's All-Japan Novice. Left with Rio, Tsukasa asks what got him into skating. "My parent was an Olympian, so obviously I was made to." (`0003`.)

An hour later Inori's father drives her back for something she dropped, and Tsukasa asks him for ten minutes. In a park at night he tells her that at the present pace the double Axel and the grade-6 test are all this year will hold, and that the triple and double and the All-Japan Novice are, honestly, difficult. Her thought: it is the first time he has told her she might not be able to. There are two ways to go faster. One is more practice, and he rules that out on his own authority, because her volume is already at capacity and more would bring the shin pain back. The other is to change the method, which risks a method that is working: reaching grade 5 in a year is not normal, and it happened because of her effort and because the method suits her. Then he asks what happened with Rio and says they will think about it together. (`0004`.)

She does not tell him. What she says instead is that she has grown four centimetres, that her school fitness test was among the best in her year, that she has gone from "a child with nothing" to one praised for sport. She could once do things only on ice and can now do them on land. She has changed, and whatever the method she wants to believe in her ability. Then she runs across the park and lands a double Axel on the ground. (`0005`.)

Tsukasa's reasoning is given step by step. On ice the skates supply speed, so a jump is easier there than on land. He had assumed the missing double Axel was a matter of strength and taken the long view. If she can do it on land, the body is ready and the cause is technique, which changes the outlook. "I was only looking at the ice." He withdraws what he said, using the word he once demanded of Yodaka, and names the plan: four days at the camp in Fukui. Whether she is in time, he thinks, depends on him. (`0006`.)

The Lux summer camp is at Tsuruga. Each child has filled in a sheet of goals. Inori's is crowded; Rio's is blank. Tsukasa's first measure is a guest: Nachi, Ryoka's coach, who once landed a quadruple Salchow in practice. His stated reason is that the same advice lands differently from a different mouth. He has also been negotiating something for the last day. A caption explains the novice selection camp held each year in Nagano, the gateway through which recent Olympians passed, open to twelve-year-olds at grade 6 by the March deadline. Inori missed it for lack of grade and by age can never attend. Rio goes every year. (`0007`–`0008`.)

Nachi's drill is to jump along a straight lane. Inori lands far outside it and sees why it helps: she had been filming herself and could never tell from the recording what to fix, and now she can see by how much she strays. Tsukasa proposes that the three coaches chase the children's jumps round a circuit, and wears the other two out. Nachi calls him relentlessly positive. Hitomi corrects her: he can be very forward-looking for someone else, and when it is about himself he turns backward and cannot move, past the reach of anyone's words. (`0009`–`0010`.)

That night, in the bath, Tsukasa offers to think through the blank sheet with Rio. Rio says he has a goal and does not want to say it to anyone. Tsukasa says he knows the feeling; he too once hid his aims for fear of being thought too big or too small. Rio answers that it is unfair for only him to be made to speak, and asks the question he has prepared: what is the coach's own singles badge grade? He has checked the published results and found no trace of the name. Tsukasa says, "Beginner." In the girls' room at the same moment Ryoka is asking Inori when her coach passed grade 7. Rio says Inori would be disappointed if she knew. Tsukasa says he has already told her. (`0011`–`0012`.)

Under the futon Inori tells Ryoka something she says is a secret from her coach. If she keeps failing grade 6, people will blame him and ask why she does not change teachers. That is why the unlanded double Axel is so bitter. She wants people to say she is growing fast, and to show she will not stop here. "司先生を選べる強い選手になりたいんだ" — she wants to be a skater strong enough to be able to choose him. In the bath Rio closes the subject: if the coach knows the gap and still means to beat Hikaru there is nothing to discuss, and in any case he came only to get away, and expects his own growth to stop where it is. (`0012`–`0013`.)

Alone, Tsukasa remembers. A younger Hitomi is telling him that the beginner test can be taken without a club and that it is remarkable to have got so far alone. He answers that he piled up so little; that what a coach would have solved in an hour took him years; that it was wasted, that he was desperate and merely present on the ice, that he wishes he had met a teacher sooner. The memory holds a self-teaching manual in his hands and cuts to the small crying Inori of Volume 1 with hers. He bursts out of the bath and runs along a ridge under the stars shouting at the brat who asked knowing the answer. He knew this would be pointed out one day; that was why he hesitated to coach; he settled all of it "that day" and does not want it dug up. He falls off a small cliff and, lying in the grass, swears that he will make Rio jump the triple and double, and Inori too. He adds that he could never think himself above Sonidori. (`0014`–`0015`.)

By morning he is cheerful. He tells Inori she falls because she lands on the inside edge. A caption says an Axel is compulsory in a competition layout, and that the triple Axel Hikaru jumps is a miracle few women have landed; a fantasy panel makes the double Axel the one sword Inori will always carry. She lands it. (`0016`.)

**Score 13** opens on the second evening. Inori has moved straight on to triples without celebrating, and her coach is holding back because she is. Rio, to his own annoyance, is being pulled along by her. His run-through is fluent, and onlookers recall that he was eighth at the All-Japan Novice two years ago. He tries a triple Salchow and falls. Tsukasa praises the attempt. Inori, at the boards, has worked out what the double Axel taught her: do not turn, do not make it turn, only become the axis. A caption notes that a double Axel rotates almost as fast as a triple. She lands a triple Salchow. Tsukasa asks whether he may celebrate now. Not yet, she says, and gives him back his own rule: a jump is finished when it is set to the music. (`0018`–`0020`.)

Rio leaves the rink. A flashback shows a small wind-blown girl arriving at his house and himself, small, leading her round the ice and telling her to ask him anything, because he is the silver medalist's son. She says she will work to be as good as he is. Then she skates away beside a tall man in a long coat. (`0021`.)

Tsukasa finds him in the car park with taped and bruised feet. Rio's account of himself is the volume's sharpest. He will not make the podium; four straight novice titles are gone; at eleven he has no triple; he goes to Nagano and is never seeded. This is all he amounts to from the best position there is. Other people can say they lacked money or time or a coach. "俺は全部あるから何のせいにもできない." Tsukasa asks, "Are you aiming at Yodaka Jun?" Everything Rio listed is one man's career: the triple Lutz and triple loop and the triple Axel in Novice B, a seed every year, four titles. A sealed memory opens. He once asked Yodaka for the same lesson Hikaru gets, and was told, "You're in the way." His father explained that "Jun-kun" coaches Hikaru under a special promise and asked him to make do with his father, and the boy insisted he preferred his father anyway. Tsukasa adds that Rio's habits on the ice resemble Yodaka's more than Sonidori's. Rio's conclusion is his own: he has been bound by that man, and what he admired was an illusion that man built. (`0022`–`0024`.)

Tsukasa carries him back inside and announces that he will skate Rio's program once. He has seen it once. Hitomi says he can generally remember a thing from one viewing. What follows is five nearly wordless pages. Children marvel at his crossovers. Rio's reaction is not what he expected to feel: everything in this man's skating except the jumps is the image of Yodaka Jun. (`0025`.)

**Score 14** continues the program in Inori's voice: a blade leaning so deep it flashes the ceiling lights, long limbs drawing circles, "my teacher I am proud of". Rio revises upward: in skating alone, might he be better than Yodaka? At the place where Rio fell, Tsukasa jumps a single Axel, thinking that he cannot really jump, and then supplies the choreography that would have followed, by guessing what Sonidori would have set. Hitomi tells Rio who he is looking at. When they met, Tsukasa was a skater with no record at all. His hold on the rink was beyond anyone's; he seemed to be crying out with his whole body that he would make the ice his place. Starting late, he never gave up improving, and paired with her across a gap of more than ten years he reached the All-Japan. That is not something a talented person does, she says. It is something truly remarkable that he did. (`0026`–`0027`.)

Afterwards the children fill the holes their jumps left in the ice. Ryoka is unimpressed, since he jumped only once. Rio comes and works beside Tsukasa without a word. At six the next morning he is waiting, having guessed the coach would be first. He addresses him by his family name, correctly, hands him the sheet with one goal written on it, a triple and double, and bows. Tsukasa cries. A truck backfires in the car park and a young person in a headband gets out: the guest he had been negotiating for. (`0028`–`0030`.)

**Score 15** is a ballet lesson. The guest is 白根琥珀, twenty-one, an instructor from a Nagoya studio and a winner of domestic competitions. Rio and Hikaru, it emerges, have ballet at their club every other day. Ryoka tried it once and quit after two days. Inori has never done it and is the worst in the room. She learns by staring at Rio. Ryoka says aloud that it is boring and pointless, the teacher's nerve fails, and Tsukasa, hidden behind a door so as not to distract anyone, supplies the reason in one word: posture. Shirone rises, springs, turns three times in the air and lands with the arms exactly where they began. The explanation that follows is about what daily repetition is for. A frightened or dejected body loses its line. If the body has been taught the right shape until it is habit, the heart can be free and the shape will hold. (`0031`–`0032`.)

Ryoka's thought is that by not choosing this four years ago she has made a four-year gap, and that it feels like looking down a very long road. Inori's, beside her, is relief: she can start piling it up from now. Back on the ice a diagram weighs where to put the double Axel in a program (earlier for stamina, later for a ten percent bonus), and she skates it to music. Everything grade 6 requires is in place. (`0033`–`0034`.)

Then three triples in four pages. Ryoka decides that since the past cannot be returned to she will win and make her choice have been right, and lands a triple Salchow. Rio, with his coach's "take challenges lightly" in his head, lands a triple toe loop and double toe loop: his first triple, and in combination. Ryoka adds a double toe loop to hers at once. Inori asks to try, and a caption reports that she could not do it straight away. The tally is printed like a results board. First, Rio. Second, Ryoka. Third, Inori, just before the session ended. (`0035`.)

At the barbecue on the last evening Rio tells Inori that since he landed it first, what he said still stands: Akeuraji-sensei is great, and he is "mine". She is outraged. Tsukasa makes her release a worm she has brought, and asks why she likes them. Because finding one is exciting, she says; and, under her breath, because with this creature she was able to skate. (`0036`–`0037`.)

He tells her that soon she will no longer be the Inori who started late. At grade 6 she will have caught up with the children who began at five. She says that makes her a little lonely. She hated who she was before she met him. Because she was behind, and had the cold feeling that she could not stay as she was, and was sad that she was not a child who skated, she was able to work so hard. When she reaches grade 6 the cold feeling's job will be over, and she will have to say goodbye and thank you to the self she hated. He says he is surprised because he understands it so well. Today he found that the efforts of the self he hated had come round to his side, and he ought to be thankful too. Then he refuses her premise. The Inori before skating was not no good. The Inori he first met and the Inori now have been the same hard-working, admirable Inori throughout. She says that being unable used to be sad, and now she is excited to see what shines on the far side of it; she can believe she will get up when she falls; she loves these days. (`0038`.)

A month later the grade-6 test is held at the rink where she took her first badge test. Outside it she meets Hikaru, who is delighted, taller, and unsurprised: "やっぱり?" Inori says she can finally compete with her and is sorry to have kept her waiting. Hikaru says, at the All-Japan, and makes it a promise. (`0039`.)

The test has three parts and all three must be passed that day, or this year's Novice A is gone: elements, a short program, a free skate. Rio is sitting it too, for the sixth time. His father thanks Tsukasa again with a bow that mortifies him. Inori looks at her hands at the spot where, in Volume 1, her coach played a finger game to stop them shaking. They are still. She passes the elements, the double Axel on a second asking. She passes the short program with ballet hands. Rio skates his with the borrowed phrase in his head, and grins without reserve for the first time in the series while his father clenches a fist. Before the free skate, which has no announcement and no applause and one failure in which means failing the grade, Inori's nerve wavers. Tsukasa holds out his drawstrings. Nothing here can send them back to the start, he says. After this test there is only forward. Be confident in what has been piled up. (`0040`–`0042`.)

The double Axel is drawn as a full page, then a still wide panel with the sound gone, then a landing. "One at a time, one at a time." A checklist ticks down the page. In the middle of it a strip shows a child's sneakers on asphalt, and a full page a playground at dusk with one small figure on a wall. While the results are read she is suddenly terrified, and Rio nudges her and says there is no need: she was good. He passes, with an examiner's remark on careful skating that shows real practice. She passes. A black band: entry to the Chūbu block competition, the All-Japan Novice A qualifier, is fixed. The last page gathers the children she has met, and Hikaru at a moonlit window. (`0043`–`0045`.)

The profile of Sonidori that follows contains one sentence that bears on everything above. What Tsukasa taught Rio at the camp was the same as what his father had told him many times. Since recognizing his slump, Rio had been unable to take his father's words. (`0046`.)

## The two vows

`MD-CL-0019@1` held that Inori had accepted the unrecoverable gap and renounced haste, and then bent that resolution with a vow made in anger. `MD-OQ-V03-01` asked what would govern her pace, and `MD-OQ-V03-02` how her coach would handle a vow made for his sake. The volume answers in the night park and it is worth being exact about who does what.

Tsukasa does four things. He tells her the truth as he sees it, including that the goal may be out of reach; her own thought marks this as new. He removes the dangerous option himself, on grounds of her health, before she can choose it. He names the cost of the remaining option. And when she shows him something he had not known, he says he was looking in the wrong place and takes his judgment back. None of this is reassurance. It is the opposite of the Volume 1 promise, and it works better: the plan that follows is a change of method, with other people's knowledge brought in, and no increase in load.

Inori does two things. She gives a reason for her ambition that is true and is not the reason. And she supplies evidence. The reason she gives, that she has changed and wants to trust what she has become, is borne out by the rest of the volume. The reason she withholds, and tells Ryoka, is that she is afraid her failures will be charged to him. The withholding is new. In every earlier volume she disclosed to him at once: the unplanned variation, the cold feeling, the vow on the train. Here she judges that this is something he should not carry. Whether that is tact, or the first thing she has kept from him because it is about him, the text leaves open; it calls it a secret and moves on.

What happens to the vow's letter is comic and deliberate. She said she would land a triple and double before Rio. She lands hers third of three. Nothing follows from it. Rio declares that his landing first proves the coach's greatness, which was the whole point of her wager, and claims the coach as his own. Her stake is paid by the wrong child and she is left stamping. The volume has let the deadline stand, since this is her last eligible year, and let the rivalry dissolve.

The train vow, then, holds through this volume, on the evidence of what does not happen: no overtraining, no recurrence of the shin pain, no distress at being third, and relief on meeting a discipline she must start from zero. `MD-CL-0019@2` records that, with its limit. She was not asked to wait. The body cooperated, the jump came, the test was passed at the first attempt. A setback inside this same deadline is the case still unseen.

## Beginner

The bathhouse exchange is built so that the credential question is asked by the person best placed to ask it and answered without defence. Rio has done research. He means to follow the answer with "can you raise an athlete above yourself?" He never gets to, because the answer is lower than he prepared for.

`MD-CL-0010` has carried, since Volume 1, the question of how Tsukasa's own failed aspiration shapes his coaching, and Volume 1's bonus story had him deny that he coached to soothe a regret. Volume 4 gives the regret in his own words and gives it a function. He thinks of his self-taught years as waste, wishes he had met a teacher sooner, and the memory cuts directly from the manual in his hands to the manual in a crying child's. This is the series stating outright what Volume 1 only staged: he recognized Inori because he had been her. That is compatible with his denial. He does not coach to feel better about the past; the past is why he could see her. It is also why he hesitated. He knew that a coach with a beginner's badge would be told so one day.

Three things are then set against the badge, and they are different kinds of evidence.

The first is Hitomi's testimony, the fullest account of him by anyone who knew him as an athlete. It makes two claims. He had nothing when they met and reached the national championships beside a partner ten years his senior in experience; and what drove him was a need to make the ice the place where he belonged. She adds, earlier and separately, that he can act for others and is paralysed for himself. Both are consistent with everything shown since Volume 1: the refusal of Kago's help, the courage "for two lives" before Yodaka, the resolution about confidence that he reached only after someone else named his gift.

The second is Rio's eye. He has watched Yodaka skate at night for years and has no wish to flatter anyone. His judgment that the coach's skating is Yodaka's image, and may exceed it apart from the jumps, is the most surprising testimony in the volume and the hardest to discount. It also explains something. Tsukasa began skating because of a televised performance by Yodaka, taught himself, and has the faculty Jakuzure identified, of seeing a movement and reconstructing it. The volume shows that faculty twice more, when he memorizes Rio's program in one viewing and when he fills in choreography he was never shown. The plain inference is that he built his skating by eye from the man he admired. The text does not say so, and the reading holds it as an inference.

The third is results, and here the volume is careful in a way that should be kept. The profile page states that what he told Rio was what Rio's father had told him many times. He added no technique. What he supplied was a different voice, attention to what was Rio's own, and the demonstration that someone with no record could skate like that. The camp's other gains came from Nachi's drill and Shirone's lesson, which he arranged. `MD-CL-0004@4` therefore describes his pedagogy at this boundary as a diagnostic eye joined to a willingness to assemble other people's knowledge, and `MD-CL-0017@3` notes that the weakness Volume 2 exposed, needing others to tell him things, has become a method he uses on purpose.

His private answer to the badge is a vow shouted at night with the word "absolutely" in it. It is directed at what he will do, it is made alone, and it is hedged with the admission that he is no Sonidori. What he says to his pupil before her test is the third version of his pre-performance speech, and the progression is the clearest line of development he has: we will win; you may fall and I will teach you again; nothing here can send us back, be confident in what you have built. The last makes a claim about the past, which is the only thing a coach can guarantee.

## Having everything

`MD-CL-0021@1` registered Rio at low confidence from one chapter. Volume 4 gives him a volume, and the picture sharpens without contradicting the first.

His own diagnosis is that advantages remove excuses. Others can attribute a shortfall to circumstance; he cannot. The argument sounds like self-knowledge and Tsukasa's question shows it is not. Rio has not been measuring himself against boys his age. He has been measuring himself against the novice career of one man, and has concluded from falling short of an Olympic champion's record at eleven that he is finished. He did not know this was his standard until it was named. The source is a private injury: he was the first person to teach Hikaru anything on ice, she promised to become as good as he was, and she was taken up by someone who then told him he was in the way.

Two cautions apply. The account comes in a rush, in one night, to one sympathetic adult, and the boy's reaction is to swing at once to the conclusion that his admiration was an illusion. An eleven-year-old's first statement of his own case is evidence of how it looks to him that night. And the change that follows is quick. Within thirty-six hours he writes his goal and bows; within three days he lands the combination; within a month he passes a test he had failed five times. The volume attributes this to recognition and to seeing the coach skate. The profile adds that the technical content was not new. So the most that can be said is that something other than instruction was what he lacked, and that being seen accurately by an adult with no stake in his pedigree was at least part of it.

What the volume does with his relation to Inori is economical. In Volume 1 he told her not to speak to him. Here, as the results are read, he tells her she was good. The profile explains the earlier contempt as the pride of someone born inside the sport and wary of newcomers, which fits, and is editorial. Their rivalry over the coach is played for comedy and has a point: the argument he made in Volume 3, that a coach without a record is a gamble, he now refutes himself by wanting the same coach.

Sonidori is the volume's quietest figure and its most consistent. He bows to a junior instructor three times across two volumes. He told his small son to make do with him. He stands at the boards with his arms folded while the boy passes at last, and the page gives him one clenched fist. The profile says he will talk for four hours about the people who helped him. A man who could not get his own advice heard by his own child, and who found someone else to say it, is doing in his way what Tsukasa did by inviting Nachi.

## Goodbye and thank you

`MD-CL-0015` has tracked a pattern in which Inori describes herself by a deficit and acts from it. In Volume 2 she feared that joy would dissolve the cold feeling and wanted to keep it. In Volume 3 she chose, against a self she said she hated, the self her coach believed in. Volume 4 gives the pattern an ending in prospect, and gives it in her words.

What she says by the sparklers has three parts. The feeling had a function: it is why she could work. The function has a term: it ends when she is no longer behind. And the self that carried it is owed thanks as well as a farewell. This is a child describing a motive as a phase, a year after describing it as something to be preserved. Her account of the present is different in kind. She is drawn by what lies beyond a difficulty where she used to be pushed by shame at it.

The evidence beyond self-report is modest and points the same way. Her hands do not shake where they shook in Volume 1. Shown a discipline in which she is the worst in the room, she is relieved. Beaten to a goal she had staked her pride on, she pouts. The free-skate test visits the child on the playground wall in a wordless page and moves on. Against this: the secret motive is fear on someone else's behalf, which is a deficit of a kind; and she is terrified while the results are read. The fair statement is that by her own account and on the visible signs the driver is changing from avoidance to appetite, and that the change has been tested only in a season where everything went well. `MD-CL-0015@3` says so and names the test, which is what moves her once she is no longer late.

Tsukasa's reply is the best thing he says in four volumes, and it has two halves that should not be merged. He agrees that a hated past can turn out to have been useful, speaking from that day's experience of skating in front of the children. And he denies that her past self was ever what she says it was. The first half meets her where she is. The second repeats, across the whole distance she has come, what he said before he had seen her skate at all.

## Four answers to lost time

Read together, the volume's four principal children and adults each face time that cannot be had back, and each answers differently.

Inori accepts the loss, starts from where she is, and thanks the self that lost it. Tsukasa called his loss waste on Tuesday and an ally by Friday; the change came from using what the wasted years had built in front of people who needed it. Rio has no lost time and is the most stuck, because without a deficit there is nothing between him and the verdict. Ryoka's gap is one she chose, and her answer is Nachi's creed from Volume 2 spoken in her own voice: win, and the choice will have been right.

`MD-CL-0022@1` registers this as a designed comparison. The volume does not rank the answers in words. Its sympathies are legible in what it does with them. Inori's and Tsukasa's are given the long quiet scene. Ryoka's produces a triple Salchow on the next page and is left unexamined, as Nachi's version was in Volume 2. Rio's is shown to rest on a mistake about what he was comparing himself to. The comparison is the point, and it is why this volume, in which almost nothing is won, reads as the series' fullest statement so far of what it thinks effort is for.

## Yodaka, Sonidori and a promise

Two facts are added and one question is sharpened. Sonidori, in Rio's memory, calls Yodaka by a familiar form of his given name and says he coaches Hikaru under a special promise. Yodaka told a small boy who asked to be taught that he was in the way, years before he told him so again. And by Tsukasa's recital, Yodaka's novice record is the template Rio has been failing to match, and by Tsukasa's phrase Hikaru is "following the same trajectory", which is very nearly what Yodaka himself thought on first seeing her.

The promise explains the secrecy at one remove and explains nothing about its content or its parties. The repeated "in the way" shows a settled attitude toward one child and does not show its ground. `MD-CL-0016@3` records the additions and keeps motive, purpose and "the same road" open.

Hikaru is on the page for five pages and they matter. She knows Inori at once, is plainly glad, seems to have expected that Inori would be taking this grade about now, and turns a hope into an appointment. `MD-CL-0003` has said since Volume 2 that the friendship was evidenced from one side only. That is no longer true. Her direction is observed, it is warm, and it treats Inori as someone she will meet as a competitor. What she thinks beyond that is still unshown, and the gags that show her at home, killing a cockroach bare-handed and chattering about her coach, are comic.

**In-story rule and procedure statements.** The novice selection camp in Nagano takes a few twelve-year-olds per prefecture at grade 6 by a March deadline. An Axel-type jump is compulsory in a competition layout. A double Axel rotates nearly as fast as a triple Salchow or toe loop. Jumps in the second half of a program earn a ten percent bonus. GOE runs from +5 to −5 in eleven steps. Grade 7 confers senior eligibility, grade 6 Novice A and junior, grade 5 Novice B. The grade-6 test has an elements part including a solo double Axel, a short program of two minutes fifty and a free skate of three minutes thirty; in the free skate one failure fails the grade. Novice skaters may enter twice in each of classes A and B. Boys' grade 6 requires a triple and double. The beginner test can be taken without club membership.

**Depicted performance.** Off the ice: Inori lands a double Axel on the ground; Shirone performs three turns in the air from standing and lands in position. At the camp: Inori's double Axel landed on day two after falls; a triple Salchow the same evening; a triple and double of unstated type at the end of day three; the double Axel set to music. Rio: a clean double Lutz, double toe loop and double loop combination in a run-through; a fallen triple Salchow; a landed triple toe loop and double toe loop. Ryoka: a triple Salchow, then a triple Salchow and double toe loop. Tsukasa: Rio's program without its jumps, with strong crossovers, a step sequence in which he accelerates, a single Axel and a flying camel spin. At the test: Inori's double Axel in the free skate, with a double Lutz and double toe loop, flying sit spin, double Salchow and step sequence ticked off; Rio's jump in the short program landed with a long run-out.

**Attributed appraisal.** Tsukasa on Inori's landing edge; Nachi on centrifugal pull and the straight lane; Tsukasa on Rio's level and on his resemblance to Yodaka in habit; Rio on Tsukasa's skating; Hitomi on Tsukasa's memory and history; Shirone on posture; Ryoka on jumps as the only measure she trusts; examiners' remarks.

**Analytical inference.** The route to the double Axel was a diagnosis corrected by evidence. The coach had attributed the failure to strength and planned for months. An off-ice landing showed the strength was there. Two interventions followed, both about the path and the axis and neither about power, and the jump came within two days. The triple came the same day because, as a caption says, the rotation speed is nearly the same; the manga presents this as a known phenomenon and the reading takes it as the manga's account. All of these are single depicted landings in practice. The test is the only setting in this volume where an outcome is certified, and there the double Axel needed a second attempt in one part and was clean in another.

Physical state: no pain is reported in this volume. The coach's refusal to add practice volume cites the earlier shin pain as the reason.

## Written Japanese

The volume's turning points are marked by forms of address. Rio's passage from 先生 as a formality, through silence, to 明浦路先生 with emphasis dots at six in the morning is the measure of his change, and it echoes his father, who alone among the adults gets the name right. By the barbecue he has added the possessive: 俺の明浦路先生. Inori's comic "オレの司" a few scenes earlier borrows a rough male pronoun for the same claim.

Tsukasa's speech splits by audience as it has since Volume 1. To his pupils he is unfailingly polite, さん to both, including the boy who has just humiliated him. Alone on the ridge he says クソガキ and the plain volitional 跳ばす three times. The contrast is the scene's joke and its evidence: the anger is real and is kept from the child.

His "初級だよ" is two words and a soft sentence-final particle. Nothing in it apologizes. Inori's secret is framed with 内緒なんだけど, and its key verb is 選べる, the potential form: not "choose" but "be able to choose". Her sparkler speech is built on a run of から clauses, each giving a reason she could work, before the でも that turns it. Hikaru's "やっぱり?" is one word that presupposes she had been expecting something.

Shirone speaks a breezy casual register with ッス endings and uses 俺, and the manga gives no gendered caption; the reading follows the page and uses no pronoun beyond the name. Ryoka's Mikawa forms continue (もんで, わからんかった, おる). Sonidori's "純くん" for Yodaka is the only familiar address anyone uses of him. None of this establishes how any line sounds.

## Manga form

**Two rooms, one fact.** The disclosure of the coach's grade is cut between the men's bath and the girls' futons, question in one and question in the other, so that the same fact is exposed from both sides at once and the two answers ("I have already told her"; "this is a secret from him") are set against each other. It is the volume's most carefully edited sequence and it is how the reader learns that the pupil knew and the coach does not know.

**A program without words.** Tsukasa's skate runs across the end of one chapter and the start of the next, mostly silent, with overlapping exposures, a ten-figure step strip, and at its first climax a single image in which his figure and Yodaka's dark one share a diagonal. The chapter break falls on Rio's realization. The second half is given to Inori's narration and then to Hitomi's history, so that the same performance is seen by a sceptic, a devotee and a witness in turn.

**The floor.** Shirone's three turns are drawn with the full machinery the series uses for jumps on ice. The page makes the lesson's point before the dialogue does.

**Silence in the test.** The free-skate double Axel is a page of impact lettering, then a wide still panel from behind with no sound marks, then the landing. The test itself is described as having no announcements and no applause, and the layout removes sound at the decisive moment.

**The child on the wall.** In the middle of the checklist a strip shows sneakers on asphalt and the next page a playground at dusk with a small seated figure, and then the bright rink. No caption explains it. It is the farewell she spoke of a month earlier, done as an image.

**Titles.** The four chapter titles run from Volume 3's "I hate the night" through "howling at the night" and "dance the night" to "morning comes", and the last is not a metaphor: it is six in the morning when Rio hands over his sheet. A possible motif of night and morning is registered and held until it recurs outside this arc.

**Echoes.** The drawstrings, gripped by her in three volumes, are offered by him. The finger game of Volume 1 is recalled at the same spot by hands that no longer shake. The manual in Tsukasa's hands and the manual in the child's. The sword labelled with a jump's name. The hole-filling after practice, a volume after a hole put her in a first-aid room.
