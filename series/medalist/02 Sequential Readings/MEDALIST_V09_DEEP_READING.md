# Volume 9 — the right to aim

Volume 8 ended with a pupil telling her coach that this time she would make him the coach of a gold medalist. Volume 9 is the skate, and she does not. She lands two quadruple Salchows, falls on a triple Lutz, earns the first level-four step sequence a novice has been given in eleven years, changes the rest of the program without telling anyone, and ends on a triple Axel that she has never been shown attempting. She does not fall. It is called short by half a turn. She is fourth, by half a point.

The author's own note calls Volumes 5 to 9 one arc. It closes on a defeat, and the volume is careful about whose defeat it is. The plan her coach built held: the base value of what she attempted was higher than the champion's. What cost her the podium was the jump she added herself. The reason she added it is the volume's central disclosure. She believes that by becoming his pupil she ended his dream, and she has been skating to make that worth it. He has spent three volumes telling other people, a patron, a champion, a rival's child, that he gave up nothing. He has not told her, and she has not told him.

The reading's argument is that Volume 9 answers the question the arc has been asking about recognition and load, and that the answer is his: a person is not made only of what others want from her, and may aim at first place even if no one expects it. The volume then shows, in the same night, that she has not been able to take it. In public she thanks him and says she is not downcast. In a hotel bed she tells her sister that anything but first was unbearable and swears never to lose again. A rival comes to him in the rain to say that his kindness cost his pupil a medal. Eight months later the federation names her a designated skater, and the promise he made in the first volume is kept in a form neither of them chose.

## What happened on the ice

`MD-OQ-V08-01` asked for the changed layout, the calls, the score and the place. All four are now on the page, and they separate cleanly into what was planned and what was not.

The change her coach agreed to was narrower than Volume 8's last page suggested. The quadruple was doubled, once in combination, on the ground that her Salchow keeps its speed. A triple Lutz replaced a double, at her proposal and on her condition, after a short tally in the warm-up. The step sequence was aimed at the top level. The stated purpose was to approach the champion's base value and win the rest on quality. By the printed sums the plan did what it was meant to do. Two elements in, she led on base value. `MD-CL-0007@8` called the decision divided between a reasoned bet and a judgment set aside. On this evidence it was a bet with a warrant, thinner than the one at the block and still a warrant, and it was hers as much as his.

The fall on the Lutz was foreseeable from the layout's own structure. The rule that the hardest jumps go first put a fine-technique jump third, on legs emptied by two quadruples. He knew it was the dividing line. That she attempted it at all was her doing, and the motive she gave a clubmate was repair: it was the jump he was hurt on.

Everything after the step sequence was unplanned. She spent the three-jump combination on a loop that was meant to stand alone, which left only an Axel, and made it a triple. Nothing in nine volumes shows her practising one. In Volume 7 she listed it as a distant wall. The jump stayed upright and was valued as a double with a penalty.

The older skaters' account of the margin is specific, and the reading adopts it with their own caveat. The downgrade cost the podium. The planned ending would, on their reckoning, have kept it. The timing of both errors lowered the component marks, which they offer as a guess. So `MD-CL-0008@7`'s question about what the foundation is worth at this level has an answer in two parts. The skating base produced the event's fastest skater, a quadruple that could carry a second jump, and a step sequence beyond both leaders. It did not produce a win, and the shortfall was not in the base. `MD-CL-0005@7` gains its cleanest case: a higher base value than the champion's and a score nineteen points lower; a jump the audience took for landed and the panel did not.

One further fact bounds any credit. The bronze medalist's triple Axel came from the same specialist who gave Inori her triples and her quadruple. He is pleased for both.

## I ended his dream

`MD-CL-0002` has tracked since Volume 4 something she kept from her coach for his sake. In Volume 4 she told a friend that she hurried so that he would not be blamed, and wanted to be strong enough to be able to choose him. Volumes 7 and 8 added guilt at his injury and a vow to make him a gold medalist's coach. Volume 9 gives what was underneath, in her own interior voice, at the worst possible moment: she believes that she ended his skating.

The belief has a source the page names. She pieced it together from what people around her said: that a man who skates like that had staked everything, and stopped on the day he took her on. It is not a fantasy. In the first volume he was auditioning for ice shows and gave that up to coach.

What she does not have is his side. To his patron he said that his work is invisible and that her performance is all of it. To Yodaka, offered a way back, he called coaching his dream and his way of using what he has. To Hikaru he said that he had never thought of it as a sacrifice and did it for his own happiness. Each of those was said to someone else. `MD-CL-0027@1` registers this as the pair's central asymmetry at the close of the arc: she is skating to redeem a loss he says he did not suffer, and neither has said so to the other. He knows part of it. He overheard the Lutz secret and let her keep it. Whether he knows the rest is not shown.

The asymmetry does work in the plot. Both of her departures from the plan trace to it by her own account, the Lutz to his injury and the Axel to his dream. It also explains the form of her gratitude in the playground, which is thanks for having been made into someone others expect things of, and her private thought months later that if she had not lost they might have stayed together.

His answer in the playground is aimed at a neighbouring target and may be read as reaching this one. She is not only what is evaluated; nobody is made only of what others want; she may aim without anyone's expectation. That removes the ground on which a debt to him could stand. It does not mention the debt, because she has not told him of it. Her last look in that scene is drawn without a smile, and the reading leaves it there.

## Two modes in one jump

`MD-CL-0015@7` distinguished two ways she moves toward a gap and asked which would govern her skate. The answer is both, and the volume is exact about the order.

Through the first eight elements she is in the attentive mode under conditions that would excuse the other. The lake routine runs twice inside thirty seconds for two quadruples. After the fall she is on the music with the right face, and reports, calmly, that the knock has put feeling back in her legs. In the step sequence her thought is about loving a thing.

The decision to change the ending is made in the other mode: must, cannot otherwise, punishment if she fails. The approach to the Axel is the page of compulsion from Niigata over again. What interrupts it is her coach's sentence from that morning, supplied by her own memory, and then her own resolution from Volume 4 not to grow on the cold feeling alone. The jump itself is attempted on a technical cue he gave her two years earlier. So the frantic state chose the jump, and the attentive state executed it. That is a finer result than either "she panicked" or "she rose to it", and `MD-CL-0015@8` records it so.

After the result the two modes separate by audience. To her coach she says she is not downcast and is happy to have been called a genius. To her sister she wails that only first would do. The second is not presented as the truth behind a false first: her pleasure in standing is consistent with everything since Volume 5. But the composure was partial, and it was for him. `MD-CL-0019@5` asked how she would meet a real setback inside her last eligible year. ## What he says a person is

Three statements by Tsukasa in this volume belong together, and the third is new.

Before her skate he does not promise and does not demand. He tells her that she has changed outcomes many times, that nothing is fixed, and that the colour of the medal is hers to choose. After it he does not mention that she overrode the plan twice. His first words are thanks for being allowed to watch. `MD-CL-0024@4` had no case of a child's unilateral risk that failed. This is one, and the adult's response is gratitude. His private response is a verdict on himself: complete defeat, and shame that she is again the one consoling him.

In the playground he says what he thinks a person is. Talent, diligence and luck are real and are not the whole of her. No one is made only of what others want. The right to aim does not wait on anyone's expectation. `MD-CL-0001` has described recognition in this work as the thing that enables. The man who has done most of the enabling now says that it is not what worth rests on. He includes himself in the sentence, and Volume 6 showed the boy he was, wanting a verdict on his talent as permission to go on. It also sits oddly with his own strategy, which for three volumes has been to win standing so that support will follow, and with the last page, where standing arrives and he presents it to her as the fulfilment of his first promise. The reading does not call that a contradiction. He holds that proof is how the system works and that it is not what a person is for. The volume shows how hard that is to hand to a thirteen-year-old.

Under the bridge he gives the doctrine its ground. Volume 8 had him tell Hikaru that he would not make Inori pay a sacrifice. Here he says why, and it is autobiography. Sacrifice is what someone who feels inferior reaches for first, a way of hurting oneself that looks like commitment. He paid everything and lost. One can lose having paid and win without paying. `MD-CL-0010@8` asked whether his purpose could be held apart from his pupil's result. On the night of her defeat he does hold it: the result is a "complete defeat" and the doctrine does not move. `MD-CL-0025@3` gains his version of "no absolute on the ice", applied this time to effort.

What he does next is leave. The volume gives one line for it, in her narration: he went to study for her junior career. Where, with whom and for how long are not said. The man whose weakness in Volume 2 was needing others to tell him things, and whose method by Volume 4 was to borrow them, goes away to learn. `MD-CL-0017@6` records the step and that its content is unknown. It also records what the step costs. For some months his pupil, who believes she drove him from skating, believes that her loss drove him from her.

## Hikaru under the bridge

`MD-CL-0003` has waited four volumes for Hikaru to say something about Inori. She says a great deal, to Inori's coach, at night, having gone out in the rain to find him.

She credits Inori without reserve for the one thing she herself tried and missed. She wanted Inori to win a medal, and is angry that she did not. She describes her: strong because a little abnormal, able to throw everything away for skating. That is a likeness to herself as Volume 8 disclosed her, and she seems to mean it as praise. And she is worried that Inori, who looked composed at the ceremony, will not come for her seriously again. The turning away in Volume 6 and the waiting in Volume 7 resolve into this. She wants a rival of her own kind, and she thinks Inori's coach is the obstacle.

Her charges against him are her coach's doctrine at full strength, and they are not weak. His refusal to exact sacrifice has now been followed by a lost medal. His own career ended in failure, and she asks whether that was for want of nerve. His protectiveness may be fear of blame. The last echoes a remark made in Niigata about adult caution. He does not answer these on the merits. He answers that sacrifices cannot be weighed on one scale, and she accepts that, condemns her own sneer, and discloses a gratitude to him for Rio.

Then she states her position, and it settles two things `MD-CL-0023` and `MD-CL-0016` had open. She knows that her shining casts a shadow on others. The prodigy is aware of her cost. She will not seek forgiveness for it, and attributes the same stance to Yodaka. And her first reflex, when Tsukasa attacks sacrifice, is to ask whether he is insulting her coach. The girl who said in Volume 8 that she would challenge Yodaka also defends him. She sets a test with a date: Inori's result in junior, a year on, will decide for her between the two men.

She cries twice in the scene. The page does not say why. A reading in which his words struck at her own condition, since if sacrifice is self-harm then her nightly rule is that, is consistent with the timing and is an inference.

## Miku and the limits of the careful way

`MD-CL-0022@4` counted a closing rink among the work's limits on time. Volume 9 follows it to the bench.

Her coach chose the careful way in Volume 7. He declined the quadruple and competed on completeness. It failed for a reason outside its terms: the skater before her. That is one half of a pairing the volume draws without comment. Kōhei's care did not protect Miku from the air Hikaru left. Tsukasa's daring did not protect Inori from her own addition. Neither doctrine is vindicated by the day.

Her reason for stopping, given late and in her own words, is the other half. It was not only the rink. Continuing would have required everyone round her to strain, and between that and her dream she could not choose the dream. She marks this as her own view. Inori hears it and defines herself against it. She chose. `MD-CL-0026@1` set three coaches' positions on what a child should carry. The two girls now hold the two answers to a prior question, whether a child may let others strain for her dream at all. The work shows the one who said no with two adults telling her she owes them nothing, and the one who said yes carrying a debt nobody asked her to pay.

Kōhei's creed, that talent has value only when someone has been able to use it, is Yodaka's sentence about proof arrived at through grief for a friend. It is the third coach to hold some version of it. Against all three the volume sets a spectator's remark, that a sport which cannot call such children geniuses is a strange one, and Tsukasa's sentence in the playground.

The same memory completes his history from outside. `MD-CL-0010@8` had his own account of staking everything on one championship. A clubmate who stood on that podium now supplies the rest: he came from Nagoya a beginner, led an elite partner within a year, needed the medal as a credential for sponsorship before his money ran out, and failed at a point he had never missed in practice. His peers thought his talent sufficient. The Volume 6 logic by which a regional title was a passport to support was first applied to himself, and it did not work.

## The designated skater

The time skip is the second in the series and is handled the same way: a caption with an age and a class, and losses mentioned in passing. She has grown; her knee hurts; she is getting jumps back. A former rival's coach is running her sessions.
