# Official Fanbook 02 — paratext audit

The second official fanbook was published on the same day as Volume 14. It does for the cast of Volumes 5 to 9 what the first did for Volumes 1 to 4, and it goes further in one direction: the author is reported at length on how three episodes were made, pages of her working sketchbook are reproduced, and a conversation first printed in August 2025 has her say where she means to take the coach.

That last item is the reason this audit matters to the state frozen at Volume 15. The interviews behind this book were given in September 2025, after Volume 13 and before the chapters collected in Volume 15 were published. In them the author says that the coach has not accepted his past self and that the series will be, for him, a journey toward coming to like himself. Volume 15 is the volume in which he tells that past and finds that he had believed in his own sacrifice. The supplement therefore corroborates, as intent stated beforehand, the reading this project reached from the story pages afterwards. It reveals no event. It is a statement of direction, and this audit uses it only to say what the author intended and never to say what will happen.

As with the first book, creator statements here are reported by an editorial writer and labelled as her conception at the time. Where they differ in colour from a later story page, the story page governs.

## Entering state and exposure

The entering state is `MD-FR-FB01`: mainline complete through Score 61, one supplement audited, seven ledgers at version 1.15, claim versions as listed there. This book was published before Volume 15 and could expose nothing ahead of the project. The risk again ran the other way, and one further risk is specific to this book: an author's statement of where a character is going invites being read as a forecast. It is entered below as intent already partly enacted, and the open questions of `MD-FR-V15` are left exactly as they stand.

## What the book adds

### The author on the coach

In the conversation the author is asked what becomes of him. Her answer, reported here in paraphrase, is that whatever he says he is a man who gave up the skater's road and made himself forward-looking by force, for his pupil's sake; that in the true sense he has not accepted his past self; that when he has, and must decide for himself what to do, what skating has been in his life should come into view; and that the series will be, for him, a journey toward coming to like himself. She adds that the story will accelerate, with the heroine's last growth and the run-up to the Olympics. (`MD-EV-FB02-0030`.)

Read against Volume 15 this is exact. There he is told that his conduct, and not his words, is what his pupil reads. He traces his rule of costing no one anything to his father. He concludes that he had put his faith in his own sacrifice, and he promises to audition for the ice show again. A volume earlier his pupil skated a film that ends on a hero able to love himself. The author's sentence names the arc those pages draw.

Three limits. It is a statement of design and not a page. It was made before the pages and cannot certify that they realize it; the project's claim rests on the pages. And it must not be extended forward: nothing in it says what he does after the freeze.

The same conversation gives the origin of his manner. Her editor found the coach of the first chapter a little frightening; softening his speech and having him address the child by name with a polite suffix from the second chapter made him who he is, a man who keeps the courtesies of ordinary life in a profession that often does not. Of his line that she must not try to read his wishes and that he will support whichever road she chooses, she says she thought hard and concluded that to push up what the child has decided is real coaching. That is the criterion this project has tracked since Volume 1, in the author's words. (`0030`.)

Earlier in the book, in her account of the night scene of Volume 6, she says what he had wanted. From boyhood he had wished that the champion would become his coach, and would tell him whether he had talent. Hearing it at last from the man himself, he is glad and feels that it has come too late; that night, she says, he parted from the boy who admired him. Volume 15 has him remember a wish for someone to take him into that world and leaves the someone unnamed. The author names him. (`0023`.)

A boxed timeline of his life agrees with Volume 15 on every dated point: four brothers, the footage in middle school, the patrons in high school, ice dance at twenty, live-in work in Yokohama, a last championship at twenty-four. It differs in colour on one. It says he left the hobby circle because relations worsened. Volume 15 shows concealment, a reproach, an apology the next day and a courteous leave-taking. The summary is the kind of sorted account that Volume 15 itself takes apart, and the story governs. (`0030`.)

His "daily life" feature, for the period of Volumes 5 to 9, adds present-day particulars. He lives at his patron's house and his day turns on the daughter's care. Parents at the club call him the partner with whom the head coach's career ended, and some say she would have placed higher with another. He takes night traffic-control jobs when short of money and holds back from working more at his patron's firm as presuming too much. He cuts his sleep to keep everything going, and the box says in so many words that this is not good. (`0004`.)

A page of the author's sketchbook from the same period lists among his motives a wish to repay the child for having given his own skating a new road. (`0028`.)

### The author on sacrifice

Her account of the rain scene of Volume 9 states the position the series takes on its central dispute. The idea that one grows strong because one offers something up is, she says, a beautiful composition and not necessarily right in the real world. She had so much to say about it that the storyboard filled with the word and went dead, and she was sent back to what the series is. She adds two facts about the scene. The verdict the prodigy speaks there was first written for her coach, who would not, the author decided, trouble to come and say it. And the scene was meant as the moment the prodigy's smiling mask breaks, a side she had not yet shown the heroine. (`0026`.)

This is the author, in September 2025, declining the creed of sacrifice in her own voice, while the chapters in which the coach discovers that creed in himself were still to be collected. It supports the project's reading of Volumes 9, 13 and 15 as an argument against that creed and not a celebration of it.

Her account of the lakeside night of Volume 7 says that what she set out to draw was the coach hearing from the child a sentence that saves him: an affirmation of his having been born at all, apart from character, skill or record. Volume 15 discloses a father who told him that a man who only costs money has no worth in living. The line of Volume 7 answers that sentence eight volumes before the reader hears it. In the conversation the voice actor, and not the author, observes that the same line can look cruel to a reader who knows what the coach gave up. (`0024`, `0030`.)

### The heroine

Her daily life is recorded for the same period: an untidy room that her mother puts in order once a month, the daily drive from school, friends at the club, a clubmate who is jealous when she is with others. (`0003`.)

Two sketchbook pages reproduce drafts of her interior for the free skate of Volume 9. They are drafts and not what the page printed. In them she fears being called a fool for attempting a quadruple; she will not go back to being a no-good child, because the fact of a medal exists; and she has not yet answered a single expectation of the people who were wounded together with her, whom she wants to make glad that they protected her. Volume 15 has her date her sense of cost to middle school. The drafts show that the author had the structure of repayment in her at eleven. The heroine's own dating stands as her statement; the motif is older in the author's hand. (`0029`.)

The author says in the conversation that the heroine is too much in earnest about competition for stories of skating for pleasure to fit easily around her, and that she does not hold that only ascetic striving has worth. (`0030`.)

### The prodigy and her coach

Two "daily life" features describe the hidden regime from inside, at a creator's weight. Her days are arranged around a midnight lesson of about an hour, two or three nights a week. Her daytime practice is left undirected on his policy, so that she will think it out herself. What she fears most is failing to produce at his lesson, and she is tense from the morning. She has no friends at school and stands apart at her club. (`0006`.)

He is nocturnal. He skates alone on hired ice most nights, and in his head when there is none, because, the box says, he loves figure skating. He owns three flats: one to live in, one to train in, and one in which he keeps his worn-out boots, a room like a grave that he seldom enters. His pupil holds a key to the second and has never used it. (`0007`.)

In Volume 13 he ended his coaching and left his boots on a bench. The supplement does not explain that act. It does establish, as the author's conception, that boots are what this man keeps and buries.

### The injured favourite, her friend's family, and the mother

The creator box on Iruka gives her home: wealthy, outwardly happy, with parents who cannot attend to another's feelings and who quarrel and blame her. The heroine's sister spoke to her at their shared rink, saw that the child was straining and that her mother was odd, and drew her into a club that was a world of children. The heroine's mother also sensed it, had the child to the house and smoothed things when the other mother was about to make trouble. The sister's sudden retirement parted them. The coach and his wife who later took her in are liked by her parents, are privately furious at them and bear with them for her sake. (`0012`.)

Two things follow for the project's ledgers. The mother who opposed her own daughter's skating was, years before, the adult who quietly sheltered another family's child. And by the first fanbook the sister's retirement was itself an act to spare that mother. The friend whom Volume 13 shows waiting to be rescued a second time was left by a girl who was giving something up. None of the three is shown to know the others' part.

A strip adds that an assistant coach of the sister's old club once turned the heroine's application away because her mother said it was out of the question. (`0010`.)

### A girl who stopped

The box on the skater whose career ends in Volume 9 gives her reason at a creator's weight. Her mother is dead and her father, a lorry driver, raises her alone. Skating filled an absence and widened the distance between them. As she grew she asked whether she could keep him working without rest, judged a professional path impossible, and resolved to stop cleanly when her rink closed. To the objection that a gifted child would be taken up by a strong club, the author answers that for this girl it is not a matter that resolve about skating can settle. (`0016`.)

With the sister of the first fanbook this makes two children, in the author's conception, who stop so as not to cost a parent. Volume 15 shows a third who could not begin, and a fourth who means to go on and pay.

### Other creator statements

A senior head coach is given a history of not being understood and a consequent purpose of lessening the number of people without a place. The coach who taught the heroine's coach about an adult's face is a former schoolteacher who resigned from overwork. The Olympic champion who runs the prodigy's new club is said to repeat foolish behaviour to assert a freedom her childhood lacked, and to perform her whole life. The heroine's second coach has a complex toward the junior who overtook him within a year, which drives his study of teaching. The senior who taught by ridicule clowns because he does not know how to talk to people. The skater who leads the final at Volume 15 makes her own costumes and would not change a layout to win; her coach prizes that and is troubled by it. (`0010`, `0014`, `0017`, `0018`, `0020`.)

The last of these was written before the chapters of Volume 15 and agrees with them.

### Documentary layers

The story guide prints element lists for seven programs at the heroine's regional qualifier and three at the national novice championship, and complete fifteen-place result tables for both. Every score the manga printed is matched. Places the manga did not print are fanbook data. (`0022`, `0025`.)

A table sets the story's events against Winter Olympic years without calendar years, under a statement that the story's world is a little in the future and differs from the real one. It gives relative ages and intervals consistent with the manga's own: the coach is fifteen years older than the heroine; his last championship was at twenty-four; the champion's gold came sixteen years before the heroine entered middle school. It states that the favourite won the series final at sixteen. One internal inconsistency in a supporting character's ages is noted in the ledger and not resolved. The project's rule that no calendar year of the story is inferred is confirmed by the publisher's own statement. (`0027`.)
